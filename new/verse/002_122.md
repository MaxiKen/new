## Sūrah al-Baqarah 2:122

> O Children of Israel! Remember My favours upon you and how I honoured you above the others.

**Expanded Commentary**

**The Refrain Returns**

The verse is a return, and the return is its meaning. Seventy-five verses earlier, the sūrah opened its address to this community with nearly the same sentence: *O Children of Israel! Remember all the favours I granted you and how I honoured you above the others* (2:47). The two verses are twins in wording and function, and their placement marks the architecture of the recital's longest movement: the address to Israel begins at verse 40 with the covenant-call, states the twin commands of remembrance and preference at verse 47, files a hundred verses of record — the charter, the witnessed bond, the contradiction in Madina's wars, the trade, the covered hearts, the rejected Messenger, the demands, the never-pleased condition — and closes at verse 122 with the twin commands repeated word for word. The structure is an inclusio, the rhetorical frame that seals a section by restating its opening, and the sealing is deliberate: the movement that began with *remember* ends with *remember*, so that everything filed between the two stands enclosed inside the command. The repetition also does the work only repetition can do. The first hearing came before the record; the second comes after. The community addressed at verse 47 had been asked to remember before the recital enumerated what was to be remembered; the community addressed at verse 122 hears the same command with the enumeration complete — every favor re-filed, every breach documented, every excuse answered. The refrain's return is therefore not redundancy but summation: the same two clauses, now carrying a hundred verses of evidence inside them.

**Remember: The Act the Recital Demands**

The command at the verse's head — *remember My favours* — names the religious act the whole movement has treated as foundational. The favor is comprehensive in its wording: *niʿmatī*, My favor, singular and possessive, gathering the sea and the manna and the rock's springs and the raised dead and the pardoned calf and the sent messengers into one held benefit — the recital's own filing of each station is the favor's itemization, and the command asks that the items be held together as a single gift from a single Giver. Memory in the recital's usage is not recall but recognition: the past favor acknowledged as favor, from Allah, owed a response. The movement has documented the failure-mode of this memory at every scale — the favors answered with the calf, the covenant cast at every station, the blessings covered by the very word the recital used for disbelief — and the verse's command at the section's close restates the demand that was refused: whatever the record shows, the obligation of memory stands, and stands unmet or met only by the remnant the recital kept excepting. For the hearing community, the command transfers in full. The sūrah's second-person method — *remember My favor upon you* — has never been confined to its historical addressees; every community that has received revelation has received the favor, and the verse's placement at a section-close marks it as the recital's standing imperative to every section's survivors: hold the gifts as gifts, from the Giver, on record.

**Honoured Above the Others**

The second clause restates the preference whose meaning the movement has spent a hundred verses correcting: *how I honoured you above the others*. The honoring — *tafḍīl*, the preferment — is stated at verse 47 and repeated here without qualification, because the qualification is the record between the two statements. The preferment was real: messengers from within the community, Scripture in its custody, favors no contemporary people received in such number. And the preferment's meaning was operational, not ontological — election to a covenant whose terms the recital quoted in full (83), a bond witnessed (84), and a practice that violated both. The misuse the movement documented ran in three directions, and the refrain's return closes all three: the preferment read as exemption — the numbered days of the Fire (80), the death-wish challenge's exclusive paradise (94); read as monopoly — the *none will enter except us* of verse 111, classified there as desires; and read as identity-sufficiency — *we believe only in what was sent down to us* (91), answered by the killing of their own prophets. The honoring was a trust with tasks: worship none but Allah, kindness to the four rings, the goodly word, the prayer and alms — and the second hearing of the clause, after the tasks' documented refusal, carries the correction inside the repetition. The verse does not withdraw the honor; the recital nowhere says the favors were taken back. It re-files the honor at the close of the evidence, which leaves it standing in its only sustainable sense: a historical preferment for a covenant's service, held by a people the service defined, and lost wherever the service was refused — the remnant excepted, as always, the few whose honoring and whose honoring-keeping were one fact.

**Why the Section Closes With Praise Rather Than Verdict**

The movement's final address to Israel is not a sentence. After the record — the distortions, the murders, the covered hearts, the never-pleased condition — the recital's last direct words to the community are the two clauses of verse 47 returned: remember, and you were honored. The choice is theologically exact, and its exactness has three faces. Toward the addressed, the refrain keeps the relationship's original grammar in force to the last word: the speaker is still the Benefactor, the addressees still the benefited, and the command to remember presumes that remembrance remains possible — the door the whole movement's warnings were built to keep open. Toward the remnant, the refrain is vindication: the honored-above-others clause was true of them in its operative sense, and the section closes with the truth restated over the exceptions the recital never stopped filing. Toward the hearing community — the Muslims of Madina and every reciter after — the close is instruction in how a divine record ends: not with the defendants' condemnation but with the gifts' restatement, so that the heirs of the covenant receive the section's whole content in its final form — the favors real, the tasks binding, the failures documented, the memory commanded. The verdict-language the movement used freely is left inside the frame; what stands at the frame's edge is the favor. The structure says what the recital has said at every station: judgment belongs to Allah's file, and the file's cover page, front and back, is the command to remember the gift.

**The Command at the Hinge**

The verse stands at a hinge of the sūrah's whole argument, and the hinge-function explains its brevity. The Israel-movement closes; the recital is about to turn back behind Israel to the covenant's earlier root — the father from whom both the honored lineage and the recital's own community's direction of prayer descend, whose story the coming verses will tell in the sūrah's longest continuous narrative of a prophet. At the hinge, the refrain does the work hinges do: it gathers the closing section's meaning into its most compact form — favor remembered, honor held in trust — and hands the gathered meaning forward. The two clauses are exactly what the next movement needs as its premise: a community is about to be shown that the covenant's true line runs through submission rather than lineage, and the demonstration will proceed from the favors and the honor this verse restates. Nothing in the refrain is wasted and nothing is added; the recital compresses a hundred verses into the sentence it began with, and the compression is the section's final lesson in the memory it commands. To remember the favor as the recital has now made it memorable — itemized, breached, pardoned, restated — is to hold the only inheritance the movement recognizes: not a status to claim but a gift to carry, with the tasks the gift was given for, into whatever section of the recital comes next.

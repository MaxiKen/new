## Sūrah al-Isrāʾ 17:3

> **˹O˺ descendants of those We carried with Noah ˹in the Ark˺! He was indeed a grateful servant.”**

**Expanded Commentary**

**Descendants Of Those Carried With Noah**

Dhurriyyata man ḥamalnā maʿa Nūḥ — O progeny, descendants of those We carried with Noah. Dhurriyyah from dh-r-r means progeny, descendants, offspring. Man ḥamalnā maʿa Nūḥ, those We carried with Noah in Ark, meaning believers who were saved with Noah from flood, as 11:40 says *We said, carry in it of each kind two, and your family, and those who believed*. The address dhurriyyata man ḥamalnā maʿa Nūḥ is to Children of Israel, reminding them that they are descendants of those saved with Noah, not of those drowned. Al-Ṭabarī explains that this is to remind them of Allah's favor in saving their ancestors, so they should be grateful and not take trustee besides Him. The phrase is vocative, O descendants, calling them to remember origin. The brackets ˹O˺ and ˹in the Ark˺ in translation clarify vocative and context: they are descendants of those carried with Noah in Ark.

**He Was Indeed A Grateful Servant**

Innahu kāna ʿabdan shakūran — indeed he was a grateful servant. Huwa refers to Noah, Nūḥ. ʿAbdan, servant, same word used for Muḥammad in verse 1, ʿabdihi, His servant, showing that highest honor is servanthood. Shakūran, grateful, thankful, intensive form indicating abundant gratitude. Al-Ṭabarī says Noah was grateful servant because he thanked Allah for blessings, obeyed commands, and was patient in calling his people for 950 years. Al-Qurṭubī notes that shakūr is one who shows gratitude with heart, tongue, and limbs, abundantly. Noah's gratitude is mentioned here to set example for his descendants, Children of Israel, who are being told not to take trustee besides Allah and who will be warned about two corruptions. If ancestor Noah was grateful servant, descendants should be grateful too.

**Why Mention Noah After Moses**

Verse 2 mentioned Moses given Scripture as guide for Children of Israel, commanded not to take trustee besides Allah. Verse 3 adds O descendants of those We carried with Noah, He was indeed grateful servant. The connection is that Children of Israel are descendants of those saved with Noah, and Noah was grateful servant, so they should follow his gratitude, not corruption. Al-Rāzī explains that mentioning Noah after Moses shows that Israelite lineage goes back to those saved with Noah, not only to Jacob, and that gratitude is ancient prophetic quality. The sūrah's theme of gratitude appears: Noah was shakūr, grateful, while many humans are hasty and ungrateful, as verse 11 will say human prays for evil as for good and is ever hasty, and as previous chapter said most are ungrateful.

**The Significance Of Being Carried With Noah**

Being carried with Noah in Ark is great favor: saved from flood that drowned all disbelievers. Al-Ṭabarī says those carried with Noah were believers, few, who were saved because they believed and followed Noah. Their descendants, Children of Israel, are therefore descendants of saved believers, not of drowned. This should make them grateful and obedient, not corrupt and arrogant. The phrase ḥamalnā, We carried, indicates Allah's action in saving, not their own power. As with Night Journey where Allah took servant by night, here Allah carried believers with Noah. Both are acts of divine carrying that show power and mercy. The verse therefore reminds Israelites that their existence is due to divine carrying and saving, so they should not take trustee besides Him.

**Grateful Servant As Model**

ʿAbdan shakūran, grateful servant, is model for all servants. Noah is described as grateful servant in Qur'an in several places, as in 17:3 and implied in 11:48. His gratitude included thanking Allah after being saved, as 23:28 says *When you and those with you are settled in Ark, say Praise be to Allah who saved us from wrongdoing people* (23:28). His gratitude also included obedience in building Ark despite mockery, patience in calling people, and trust in Allah. Al-Zamakhsharī notes that shakūr is intensive, indicating Noah was constantly grateful, not occasionally. For Children of Israel who were given Torah as guide and commanded not to take trustee besides Allah, Noah's example of constant gratitude is relevant: they should be grateful for Torah and for being descendants of saved, not corrupt.

**Linguistic Note On Dhurriyyah And Shakūr**

Dhurriyyah from dh-r-r means progeny, descendants, from same root as dharr, small ants, indicating many small offspring. Man ḥamalnā, those We carried, from ḥ-m-l, to carry, same root as ḥaml, carrying, as in cattle bearing loads. Maʿa Nūḥ, with Noah. Innahu kāna ʿabdan shakūran, indeed he was grateful servant, with innahu for emphasis, kāna for past, ʿabdan for servant, shakūran intensive form of shākir, grateful, from sh-k-r, gratitude. The verse's structure — O descendants of those We carried with Noah, indeed he was grateful servant — with vocative and statement, calls Israelites to remember ancestry and to follow ancestor's gratitude, preparing for warning about two corruptions that will come because they did not follow gratitude.

**How This Verse Connects To Sūrah's Themes**

Sūrah al-Isrā' themes include Night Journey, Israelite history, Qur'an guiding to most upright, and moral code. This verse connects Night Journey to Israelite history via Noah, showing that all prophetic history is linked: Noah saved, Moses given Scripture, Muḥammad taken by night to Jerusalem, all part of one divine plan. The mention of grateful servant also connects to verse 9 which says Qur'an gives good news to believers who do good that they will have mighty reward, and to verse 19 about striving for Hereafter appreciated. Grateful servanthood leads to mighty reward and appreciated striving. The verse therefore is not only historical note but moral call: be grateful servant like Noah, not corrupt like those who will cause corruption twice.

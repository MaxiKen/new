## Sūrah al-Shuʿarāʾ 26:202

> which will take them by surprise when they least expect ˹it˺.

**Expanded Commentary**

**The Consequence Arrives Outside Their Schedule**

The punishment does not arrive according to the timetable of the people who rejected it. It takes them by surprise when they least expect it. This is the opposite of their earlier demand that punishment be brought quickly and visibly. They treated delay as proof that the warning was empty, then discovered that delay had never meant cancellation.

Surprise here does not mean that Allah's judgment is random. The time is known to Allah even when it is hidden from the people. The shock belongs to those who built their plans on the assumption that tomorrow would resemble today. Their ignorance of the hour makes their false security collapse, not the divine decree become uncertain.

**Unawareness Is a Moral Condition**

The people are unaware because they have trained themselves not to attend to the warning. A person can hear words without allowing them to become knowledge that changes behavior. He can make the sermon familiar, the threat amusing, and the promise of judgment remote. In that state, unawareness is not innocent lack of information; it is the result of repeated neglect.

Quran 7:187 describes the Hour as something that will take people by surprise, and 21:40 likewise presents it as arriving suddenly. These passages place the emphasis on human unpreparedness. Allah has warned, but people have mistaken the absence of immediate consequences for evidence that no consequence exists.

**A Hidden Deadline Exposes False Control**

People often plan responsibly by using calendars, contracts, and forecasts. The verse does not condemn planning. It condemns the illusion that planning gives a person control over the moment when accountability arrives. A household can arrange tomorrow's meals and still be unable to command the hour of judgment.

The hidden deadline also limits arrogance. A ruler may believe that institutions will protect him, a trader may believe that wealth will keep him secure, and a denier may believe that he can ask for proof later. Surprise strips away those imagined guarantees. It does not take away what was truly done; it takes away the fantasy that the account can be postponed by human preference.

**Preparedness Is the Proper Response to Uncertainty**

Because the time is concealed, the correct response is readiness rather than speculation. People should not waste their lives trying to calculate what Allah has kept hidden. They should make their conduct fit the truth that judgment can arrive before their plans are complete.

Readiness is practical. Keep obligations clear, repair wrongs quickly, and do not make repentance dependent on a future mood. A person who has already chosen honesty is not made helpless by suddenness. The warning has done its work before the event if it has taught the servant to live without treating delay as permission.

The commentators also discuss the grammar behind the sudden arrival. Some understand the approaching subject to be the punishment mentioned immediately before; others connect the feminine form with an implied Hour, the final event whose arrival is hidden. The difference does not weaken the warning. Whether the focus is the punishment of a people or the Hour itself, the point is that an event known to Allah can overtake a people who have treated uncertainty as security.

This distinction helps the reader avoid a shallow demand for dates. The Quran speaks about suddenness to cultivate readiness, not to provide a schedule that can be used for speculation. A person who spends his attention trying to calculate the hidden moment may still leave his obligations unrepaired. Knowledge of the date would not improve a heart that refuses to act; readiness begins with accepting that the date is not ours to control.

Unawareness can also spread through institutions. A company may keep operating because no inspection has yet exposed its fraud. A government may assume that public anger will remain quiet. A household may repeat a harmful custom because no visible consequence has arrived. The quiet period can make a whole group inattentive. The verse interrupts that group confidence and says that the absence of surprise so far is not a promise that surprise will never come.

A prepared person is not one who lives in panic. He is one who refuses to build his peace on the assumption that accountability is far away. He plans for ordinary life while keeping the account before Allah, and he welcomes each day as an opportunity to correct rather than as evidence that correction can wait. Suddenness is frightening only to the person who has made delay his protection.

The concealed hour also gives ordinary duties their seriousness. A person may imagine that only dramatic acts matter before Allah, yet sudden judgment can find him in the middle of a small choice: a promise kept or broken, a payment made or withheld, a word used to defend truth or conceal harm. Readiness is formed through these repeated decisions, not through a single performance at the end.

The verse should not make people abandon their responsibilities or live as though every moment were the last in a way that harms dependents. It teaches a balanced vigilance. Plan, work, build, and care for others, but do not let planning become a claim that Allah owes you the future. Every ordinary plan remains under His knowledge, and every day is an opportunity to make the next day morally safer.

The people who are surprised are those who least expect the event because they have trained themselves to expect only continuation. They think the present order will protect them indefinitely. Revelation interrupts that assumption. The world continues by Allah's permission, not by a promise that the world will never be interrupted. A prepared servant welcomes time without worshiping it.

## Sūrah al-Baqarah 2:279

> If you do not, then beware of a war with Allah and His Messenger! But if you repent, you may retain your principal—neither inflicting nor suffering harm.

**Expanded Commentary**

**The Warning Stated**

The sentence addresses those who do not comply with the command and warns them of a war from God and His Messenger, and the community's teachers read the clause as the most severe warning that the Book addresses to the community on any matter of property. The passage has required the believers to give up what remains of the practice, and the sentence here states the consequence of the refusal.

The wording places the matter within the relations of war, so that the person who persists is described as one whom the community's authority may confront. The community's scholars noted the form of the phrase, since the instruction concerns the conduct of the community as a body rather than the private fault of an individual.

The verse then states the rule for those who turn back, which is the retention of the principal without the addition, and the sentence closes with the statement about the wrong that is neither inflicted nor suffered. The community's teachers read the two clauses together as the account of the outcome for those who return.

**The Reports About the Phrase**

The commentators transmitted the explanations of the early teachers about the warning, and one of them described the state of the person who consumes the practice on the day of the rising. The material shows the severity that the community attached to the subject, and the teachers used the reports when they explained the verse.

The community's scholars also discussed the meaning of the phrase as the statement that the person has placed himself in the position of an enemy of God and His Messenger. The reading supplies the sense of the warning without the need for a further act.

The teachers of the community drew from the reports the lesson that the matter belonged to the gravest of the offences that the Book treats, since the passage names the two sources of authority in its warning. The sentence in this place supplies the statement from which the community's law on the subject was built.

**The Authority and the Practice**

The community's jurists discussed the application of the warning, and some of them held that the authority of the community is charged with compelling those who persist in the practice to give up their gains, and with fighting them if they refuse. The material shows the manner in which the passage was understood by the judges of the community.

The scholars also noted that the warning belongs to the dealings that the community conducts among its members, so that the instruction has a public as well as a private dimension. The Book's treatment of the matters of property elsewhere places them within the responsibilities of the community's order.

The teachers drew from the passage the lesson that the wealth of the community is governed by rules that its authority may enforce. The sentence in this place supplies the ground on which the community's practice rested.

**The Principal Retained**

The sentence states that those who turn back may keep the principal of their wealth, and the community's teachers read the clause as the mercy that accompanies the warning. The commentators explained that the addition that the debt had carried was removed while the amount that had been lent remained the right of the lender.

The scholars of the community noted the balance that the clause preserves, since the debtor is released from the increase and the creditor is not deprived of the property that was his. The passage's treatment of the subject was read by the jurists as a body of instruction that attends to both parties.

The wording also supplied the community with the framework of its law about the debts that had been contracted under the earlier practice, and the judges of the community applied the clause to the cases that came before them. The sentence therefore completes the instruction with the rule that settled the accounts of the city.

**The Wrong Named**

The sentence closes with the statement about the wronging and the wronged, and the community's teachers explained the phrase as the description of the relation that the rule establishes between the two parties. The creditor who demands the addition wrongs the debtor, and the debtor who withholds the principal wrongs the creditor.

The community's scholars read the clause as the standard by which the dealings of the believers are measured, so that neither party is permitted to exceed the right that the law has established. The Book's concern for fairness in the matters of property runs through the passage, and the sentence supplies its statement.

The teachers also noted the placement of the clause at the close of the instruction, since the rule is presented together with the reason for it. The community's preachers used the phrase when they spoke about the dealings of the believers with one another.

**The Place in the Passage**

The sentence stands between the command to give up the practice and the instruction about the debtors who are in difficulty, and the community's teachers read the arrangement as the passage's movement from the warning to the mercy. The Book has required the community to abandon the practice and has named the consequence of the refusal, and the verses that follow treat the cases of those who cannot pay.

The community's scholars observed that the passage treats the subject of wealth from the side of the creditor and the debtor in turn, so that the whole of the relationship is addressed. The sentence in this place supplies the rule for the claims that the earlier dealings had created.

The verses that follow commend the creditor who grants the respite and who releases the debt, so that the passage closes with the generosity that the Book has been recommending throughout. The sentence and the verses together form the community's law on the subject.

**What the Verse Establishes**

The sentence establishes the warning addressed to those who persist in the practice, the severity that the phrase carries, the retention of the principal by those who turn back, and the statement about the wrong that is neither inflicted nor suffered. The community's teachers used the verse when they set out the law of the practice and when they described the responsibilities of the community's authority, and the sūrah's next verse turns to the debtor who is in difficulty, so that the warning is followed by the instruction that completes the treatment of the subject.

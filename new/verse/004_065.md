## Sūrah al-Nisāʾ 4:65

> But no! By your Lord, they will never be ˹true˺ believers until they accept you ˹O Prophet˺ as the judge in their disputes, and find no resistance within themselves against your decision and submit wholeheartedly.

### Expanded Commentary

**Submission in the place of dispute**

The verse swears that true belief requires accepting the Messenger as judge in disputes, feeling no inward resistance to his decision, and submitting wholeheartedly. This is a demanding definition because outward compliance can conceal resentment. Faith is tested when the ruling limits one’s own interest or favours a weaker claimant.

**The three stages of acceptance**

First comes referral: the dispute is brought to the revealed standard rather than settled by power. Then comes inward acceptance, the struggle against the feeling that one deserves an exception. Finally comes submission in action. The stages do not mean that a believer experiences no emotion; they mean that emotion does not become rebellion. One can ask about evidence and application without refusing truth once it is clear.

**The law protects equality**

Prophetic arbitration mattered because it placed influential and ordinary people under one rule. The famous reports of the Prophet rejecting intercession for a noble offender illustrate that justice cannot be adjusted according to lineage. The verse therefore supports rule of law, but with a sacred source and a moral demand that the heart also accept the outcome.

After the Prophet, disputes are referred to the sources and qualified scholarship that faithfully interprets them. No later authority is infallible. The verse calls for humility before truth, not blind submission to every person who claims to represent it.

**Submission is tested by self-interest**

It is easy to praise a judgment that benefits us. The deeper test is whether we accept the same standard when it limits our property, status, or family. This does not prohibit asking for evidence or seeking review; it prohibits making personal preference the final court.

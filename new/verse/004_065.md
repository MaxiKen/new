## Sūrah al-Nisāʾ 4:65

> But no! By your Lord, they will never be ˹true˺ believers until they accept you ˹O Prophet˺ as the judge in their disputes, and find no resistance within themselves against your decision and submit wholeheartedly.

**Expanded Commentary**

**By Your Lord, They Will Never Be Believers Until**

The oath *fa lā wa rabbika lā yuʾminūna ḥattā yuḥakkimūka fī mā shajara baynahum* — but no, by your Lord, they will never be true believers until they accept you as judge in their disputes — follows the promise that those who wronged themselves and came seeking forgiveness with the Messenger’s prayer would find God ever Accepting (4:64). The oath *wa rabbika* — by your Lord — personalizes the guarantee to the Prophet ﷺ; the Lord who sent messengers to be obeyed by His leave is invoked as witness that faith remains incomplete until judgment is accepted from the sent one. *Yuḥakkimūka* — to make you judge, to seek your arbitration — was read as referral in any dispute — *mā shajara baynahum* — what sprouts, what tangles between them — whether about property, marriage, or alliance. The sūrah has just said to refer disputes to Allah and His Messenger as best and fairest outcome (4:59) and to reject false judges though commanded (4:60); this verse makes that referral the measure of faith itself. Claim — *yazʿumūna that they believe* (4:60) — is not enough; the test is whether the claimant, when entangled, makes the Prophet ﷺ the judge.

**Disputes That Sprout Between People**

The phrase *fī mā shajara baynahum* — in what has sprouted between them — with *shajara* evoking a tree that branches and entangles, was explained as any quarrel that grows between people where rights are tangled — the same quarrels the sūrah has regulated: a mother’s third or sixth (4:11), a wife’s quarter or eighth (4:12), a dowry left or reclaimed (4:20–21), a trade by mutual consent or falsehood (4:29), or a split where mediators are appointed (4:35). The sūrah has shown that those quarrels can be resolved by returning trusts to rightful owners and judging with fairness as a noble exhortation from the All-Hearing, All-Seeing (4:58). Here the exemplar of that fairness is the Prophet ﷺ himself. Early reports placed the verse after a dispute between a Companion and a Jew where the Jew accepted the Prophet’s judgment and the hypocrite sought another, and after an incident where a Companion expected favour and did not receive it; the general rule was read as covering every *shajara* where faith and desire pull against each other. To accept him as judge in that tangle is to accept that the limits — *ḥudūd* — he clarified are the standard, and that the clarity God wills (4:26) is the clarity he brings.

**No Resistance Within Themselves**

The second condition *thumma lā yajidū fī anfusihim ḥarajan mimmā qaḍayta* — and find no resistance within themselves against your decision — moves from outer referral to inner acceptance. *Ḥaraj* — tightness, constriction, resistance — was read as the reluctance that remains after the mouth says I accept but the chest tightens against the share given. The sūrah has diagnosed that tightness elsewhere: craving what God gave some over others (4:32), begrudging the people for bounty (4:54), and promoting stinginess (4:37). To find no *ḥaraj* is to be free of that constriction, to assent where the judgment gives a wife her quarter or gives a daughter her half, as God appointed. The verse was stressed as requiring not only outward compliance — coming to the Messenger — but inward ease. The hypocrites who turn away stubbornly (4:61) and then come swearing goodwill after disaster (4:62) show outward movement without inner *sharḥ* — openness; this verse demands the opposite: referral with an open chest. The Qur’an elsewhere says that God expands the chest for guidance, and that expansion is the counterpart to the constriction removed here. The sūrah has said that God suffices as Guardian and Helper (4:45) and that He multiplies an atom’s weight (4:40); to find no resistance is to trust that the judgment that seems to reduce one’s share will be multiplied elsewhere.

**Submit Wholeheartedly**

The third condition *wa yusallimū taslīmā* — and submit wholeheartedly — was read as full surrender, verbal noun doubled — *taslīmā* — to stress total submission without reservation. *Taslīm* was explained as peace in submission, the opposite of the *ṣudūd* — barring — that marks the hypocrite (4:61). The sūrah has shown submission in action: avoiding major sins to be admitted to honour (4:31), asking God for bounty rather than craving another’s (4:32), returning trusts and judging with fairness (4:58). Here submission is the inner counterpart to those outer acts where the dispute is not about a small provision but about a right the self resists yielding. The doubling *yusallimū taslīmā* was heard as requiring submission that is not partial or conditional — not *I submit if the judgment favours me* — but submission that remains even when it fails to favour. That wholeness is why the verse is an oath. Faith is not complete until the judgment of the sent one is accepted, felt as ease, and lived as surrender, which is also why the sūrah will say that had they done what they were advised, it would have been better and more reassuring and would have brought great reward and guidance to the straight path (4:66–68).

**A Measure of True Belief**

Placed between the open door of coming after wronging oneself to find God ever Accepting (4:64) and the reminder that had they obeyed a hard command few would have done so but doing what was advised would have been better (4:66), this oath defines the threshold of true belief. It is not a demand for perfection from the weak — the sūrah has said He wills to lighten because humans were created weak (4:28) and to cover lesser faults where major sins are avoided (4:31) — but a demand for sincerity where judgment is given. The community that was taught to appoint mediators where a split is feared (4:35) and to refer disputes upward (4:59) is here told that the referral must be without inward *ḥaraj* and with whole *taslīm*. The Prophet ﷺ as judge in tangled disputes is the living form of the sūrah’s ethic of limits. To accept his judgment without resistance is to live as one who believes that not an atom’s weight is wronged, that good is multiplied many times, and that the One who is All-Hearing, All-Seeing will see the surrender. The oath by your Lord makes that acceptance not a recommendation but the line between claim and faith, between professing belief in what was revealed and actually coming to it when the dispute sprouts.

## Sūrah an-Nūr 24:62

> The ˹true˺ believers are only those who believe in Allah and His Messenger, and when they are with him on a public matter, they do not leave without his permission. Indeed, those who ask your permission ˹O Prophet˺ are the ones who ˹truly˺ believe in Allah and His Messenger. So when they ask your permission for a private matter, grant permission to whoever you wish and ask Allah’s forgiveness for them. Surely Allah is All-Forgiving, Most Merciful.

**Expanded Commentary**

**Constitutional Definition of the Faithful Community: innamā al-muʾminūn**

The divine discourse frames political discipline and organizational solidarity as essential hallmarks of genuine faith: *innamā al-muʾminūna alladhīna āmanū bi-Allāhi wa-rasūlihī wa-idhā kānū maʿahū ʿalā amrin jāmiʿin lam yadhhabū ḥattā yastaʾdhinūh*. The restrictive particle *innamā* establishes an uncompromising constitutional boundary around authentic belief.

True faith (*īmān*) is not confined to private personal piety, nocturnal prayers, or individual asceticism. It requires a profound commitment to the collective welfare, security, and administrative order of the believing community. An individual who claims to believe in God and His Messenger while exhibiting anarchic individualism, defying communal leadership, or deserting public assemblies during critical crises has failed the foundational test of faith. Faith is validated in the crucible of collective responsibility, where personal convenience is subordinated to the strategic needs of the community, uniting hearts and hands in selfless service to the divine cause.

**The Concept of Collective Affairs: amr jāmiʿ**

The phrase *amrin jāmiʿ* (a collective affair / public matter) carries vast jurisprudential and political scope across Islamic governance. Classical commentators, including al-Ṭabarī, al-Qurṭubī, and al-Rāzī, defined *amr jāmiʿ* as any public endeavor that gathers the community together for collective action, mutual consultation, strategic mobilization, or communal survival.

This includes mandatory military campaigns—such as the digging of the trench at the Battle of the Khandaq or the expedition to Tabūk—as well as the Friday congregational sermon (*Jumuʿah*), emergency political councils, defensive preparations, and critical judicial deliberations. In such high-stakes assemblies, the physical presence and unified solidarity of every member are vital. A sudden, disorganized departure of individuals weakens collective morale, creates security vulnerabilities, and disrupts the execution of communal strategies, threatening the survival of the entire state, spreading confusion among the ranks.

**Procedural Discipline: Asking Leave as an Article of Faith**

The verse makes seeking the leader's permission (*istiʾdhān*) before departure a direct test of religious authenticity: *inna alladhīna yastaʾdhinūnaka ulāʾika alladhīna yuʾminūna bi-Allāhi wa-rasūlih*. Asking permission is not an empty bureaucratic formality; it is a profound declaration of allegiance and respect for legitimate authority.

When an individual needs to leave a collective gathering due to urgent private circumstances—such as caring for a sick child, repairing a compromised dwelling, or attending to sudden physical distress—they do not sneak away silently or walk out in defiance. They approach the Prophet ﷺ with humility, present their genuine excuse, and await his authoritative judgment. This protocol ensures that leadership retains complete operational awareness of communal capacity, prevents chaotic desertion, and reinforces the chain of command, creating an orderly, resilient civic body capable of coordinated action across any emergency.

**Prophetic Prerogative and Executive Discretion: fa-ʾdhan li-man shiʾta minhum**

The divine law grants the Prophet ﷺ absolute executive discretion in evaluating requests for departure: *fa-idhā istaʾdhanūka li-baʿḍi shaʾnihim fa-ʾdhan li-man shiʾta minhum*. The clause *fa-ʾdhan li-man shiʾta* (grant permission to whoever you wish) establishes that granting leave is not an automatic, unconditional right that citizens can demand.

The leader must evaluate each request in the light of broader public interest (*al-maṣlaḥah al-ʿāmmah*). If the private excuse is genuine and the community's defensive situation permits their temporary absence, permission is granted with compassionate understanding. However, if the public danger is extreme and the individual's presence is irreplaceable, the leader holds the constitutional authority to deny permission, requiring the individual to remain at their post. This balance between executive authority and situational flexibility prevents both autocratic rigidity and undisciplined desertion, preserving public safety while respecting human needs.

**The Pastoral Intercession: wa-staghfir lahumu Allāh**

The verse introduces an extraordinarily tender pastoral duty for the leader when granting leave: *wa-staghfir lahumu Allāh*, and ask Allah's forgiveness for them. The command to seek divine forgiveness for departing individuals reveals the delicate spiritual tension inherent in public service.

Even when an individual's private excuse is legitimate and permission is lawfully granted, departing from a collective effort on behalf of God's cause involves prioritizing personal concerns over communal duty. Such departure naturally leaves a lingering sense of spiritual deficiency or regret in the heart of a sincere believer. By commanding the Prophet ﷺ to make intercessory supplication for them, the divine law cleanses their conscience, heals their sense of regret, and wraps their departure in prophetic compassion, ensuring that those who leave for legitimate domestic reasons remain spiritually connected to the community's collective endeavor, surrounded by divine favor.

**Synthesizing Military Discipline and Personal Compassion**

The concluding divine names provide the theological foundation for this balanced civic order: *inna Allāha ghafūrun raḥīm*, surely Allah is All-Forgiving, Most Merciful.

Islamic governance does not function as a heartless military machine that crushes human individuality and ignores legitimate family distress; nor does it function as an undisciplined, chaotic democracy where individuals abandon their posts at will. Verse 62 synthesizes rigorous constitutional discipline with deep, empathetic pastoral care. By combining procedural order (*istiʾdhān*), executive leadership (*fa-ʾdhan*), and divine pardon (*wa-staghfir lahum*), the Qur'an creates a mature, cohesive, and loving society where citizens are united in shared purpose, anchored in mutual trust, and sustained by divine mercy across all collective trials.

This balance remains an enduring model for every healthy institution and community. When leaders combine clear executive decision-making with genuine compassion for their followers' private challenges, they inspire unshakeable loyalty and high morale. Conversely, when citizens respect institutional processes and communicate openly with leadership rather than deserting their duties, the organization achieves operational excellence without succumbing to bureaucratic alienation or internal fragmentation.

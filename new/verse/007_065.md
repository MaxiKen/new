## Sūrah al-Aʿrāf 7:65

> And to the people of ’Âd We sent their brother Hûd. He said, “O my people! Worship Allah—you have no other god except Him. Will you not then fear Him?”

**Expanded Commentary**

**The People of ʿĀd and Their Messenger**

The verse begins the second of the sūrah's punishment accounts, and it names the people of ʿĀd and their messenger Hūd. The Book tells the story of this people in several passages, and the accounts run through the sūrah of Hūd (11:50–60), the sūrah of the Poets (26:123–139), the sūrah of the Sand Dunes (46:21–25), and the sūrah of the Moon (54:18–21). The verse in front of us introduces them in the same form that the previous account used for Noah.

The tradition identifies ʿĀd as a people of southern Arabia, and the sūrah of the Dawn names their city as Iram of the pillars and describes its like as not having been created in the lands (89:6–7). The audience of the sūrah knew the traditions of this people, and the Book's account in front of us assumes that knowledge. The narrative states the message and the response rather than the geography.

**The Brother Sent to His People**

The verse describes the messenger as the brother of the people, and the term establishes the kinship that the sūrah's narratives rely upon. The same word is used of Ṣāliḥ among Thamūd and of Shuʿayb among the people of Midian, and the sūrah of Hūd uses it in the opening of the ʿĀd account (11:50). The messengers are drawn from the communities they address, and the Book presents the choice as part of the mercy of the arrangement.

The description of the messenger as a brother also sets up the refusal that follows. The leaders who answer him in the next verse accuse him of folly and falsehood, and the charge is brought against a man of their own people rather than against a stranger. The sūrah's later account of the people of Lot and the people of Shuʿayb uses the same opening, and the frame is fixed across the series.

**The Call to Worship God Alone**

The message of Hūd is the message of Noah, and the verse repeats the words of the earlier account: worship God, for you have no god besides Him. The sūrah of Hūd reports the same call in its own account (11:50), and the sūrah of the Poets opens the ʿĀd narrative with the question of whether the people will fear God (26:123). The verse in front of us states the call in the form that the sūrah's readers have now met three times, and the repetition is the point.

The Book presents the unity of the message as the mark of the messengers, and the sūrah of the Prophets states that every messenger before the Prophet received the same revelation that there is no god but God (21:25). The call in front of us supplies the second instance in the sūrah's series, and the peoples of Ṣāliḥ and Shuʿayb receive it in their turn. What differs between the narratives is the community and its response, not the message.

**The Question About Fear**

The verse closes with the question of whether the people will fear God, and the question adds a note that the earlier account did not have in the same words. The sūrah of the Poets opens the ʿĀd narrative with the same question (26:123), and the sūrah's later verses attach the call to the fear of the punishment that follows the refusal. The question in front of us is therefore a warning in the form of an appeal.

The Book's use of the fear of God as the beginning of right conduct appears in its instructions to the community throughout, and the sūrah's opening verses have placed the fear of the reckoning at the centre of their account of the last things. The question in front of us states the disposition that the people lack, and the narrative that follows shows what its absence produced.

**The Pattern of the Second Account**

The verse repeats the structure of the first account with the names changed, and the repetition is one of the sūrah's deliberate effects. The call is the same, the address to the people is the same, and the answer of the leaders that follows is different only in its wording. The Book's arrangement of the narratives in series makes the history a single argument, and the verse in front of us supplies the first element of the second version.

The tradition transmits the account of ʿĀd in more than one form, and the sūrah of the Sand Dunes reports the messenger's warning to a people dwelling in the sand hills and his reminder of the favours they had received. The verse in front of us states the call in the fewest words, and the later verses of the sūrah's account will add the reminders of what the people had been given.

**Where the Verse Sits**

The verse stands sixty-fifth in the chapter, and it begins the account of ʿĀd immediately after the conclusion of the account of Noah. The verses that follow report the answer of the leaders, the reply of the messenger, and the reminder of the favours that the people had received (7:66–69). This verse supplies the opening of the second narrative in the series.

The reader who has seen the first community destroyed now meets the second with the same message. The messenger is a brother of the people, the call is the worship of the one God, and the question about fear is put in the same form; and the sūrah's next verse records the answer that the leaders of the people gave.

**The Transition to the Next Verse**

The next verse reports that the disbelieving leaders of the people said that they considered the messenger foolish and counted him among the liars (7:66). The call in front of us is followed by the answer of the notables, and the reply of the messenger comes in the verse after that.

The two verses together repeat the exchange of the first account. The messenger calls the people to the worship of God alone and asks whether they will fear Him, and the leaders answer with the two charges of folly and falsehood; and the sūrah's next verses state the messenger's response and the reminder he gives his people.

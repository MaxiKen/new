## Sūrah al-Nisāʾ 4:84

> So fight in the cause of Allah ˹O Prophet˺. You are accountable for none but yourself. And motivate the believers ˹to fight˺, so perhaps Allah will curb the disbelievers’ might. And Allah is far superior in might and in punishment.

### Expanded Commentary

**The Prophet’s personal responsibility**

The Prophet is commanded to fight in Allah’s cause and told that he is accountable only for himself, while being urged to motivate believers. This does not isolate him from the community; it clarifies that he cannot force their courage or answer for their choices. A leader must fulfil his own duty and invite others, not make their hesitation an excuse for abandoning his mission.

**Curbing power, not seeking domination**

The aim is to curb the strength of those who attack the community. It is not a call to pursue war for its own sake. The surrounding passages on treaties, peace, and protection set boundaries. Military force is a means of stopping aggression and protecting the vulnerable, not a spiritual identity.

Allah is stronger in might and punishment, so the Prophet’s confidence is not based on numbers. This can console a small community, but it also restrains it: victory remains God’s gift, and those who fight must not become intoxicated by temporary power.

**Leadership begins with personal duty**

A leader cannot force sincerity in followers, but can make the moral standard visible through his own conduct. He must also encourage without manipulating, explain the cause, and protect those who cannot fight. Strength is measured by the justice it restrains itself to serve.

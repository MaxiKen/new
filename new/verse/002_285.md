## Sūrah al-Baqarah 2:285

> The Messenger ˹firmly˺ believes in what has been revealed to him from his Lord, and so do the believers. They ˹all˺ believe in Allah, His angels, His Books, and His messengers. ˹They proclaim,˺ “We make no distinction between any of His messengers.” And they say, “We hear and obey. ˹We seek˺ Your forgiveness, our Lord! And to You ˹alone˺ is the final return.”

**Expanded Commentary**

**The Confession Stated**

The sentence states that the messenger believes in what has been revealed to him from his Lord and that the believers share his faith, and the community's teachers read the verse as the summary of the creed that the sūrah has set out. The Book has treated the guidance, the law, the history of the earlier communities, and the account, and the sentence here gathers the faith of the community in a single statement.

The wording places the messenger first, so that the community's faith is presented as the acceptance of what he received. The teachers noted the arrangement, since the verse joins the leader of the community and its members in the same confession.

The Book's method of summarizing its teaching in a statement of faith appears in other passages, and the community's scholars gathered the instances when they explained the verse. The sentence in this place supplies the confession that closes the sūrah.

**The Articles Named**

The sentence names the objects of the faith, which are God, His angels, His Books, and His messengers, and the community's teachers read the list as the account of what the believers accept. The Book has treated each of the articles in the passages that precede, and the verse here gathers them into a single confession.

The commentators noted the order of the list, since the faith in God comes first and the acceptance of the messengers follows the acceptance of what was sent to them. The community's scholars used the arrangement when they set out the creed of the community.

The scholars also observed that the sentence joins the Books and the messengers together, so that the community's faith is presented as the acceptance of the revelation and of those who carried it. The passage's treatment of the earlier communities and their prophets was read with the verse.

**The Distinction Refused**

The sentence records the words of the believers that they make no distinction between any of the messengers, and the community's teachers read the clause as the statement of the attitude that the faith requires. The Book has treated the preference among the messengers in an earlier passage, and the two verses were read together by the commentators.

The wording distinguishes the confession of the community from the conduct of the earlier peoples, who accepted one messenger and rejected another. The teachers drew from the clause the lesson that the faith of the community is complete in its acceptance of the whole line of those who were sent.

The community's scholars also discussed the relation between the clause and the passage about the preference among the prophets, and they explained the two as the statement of the gifts that differ and the acceptance that does not. The sentence in this place supplies the confession that the community repeated.

**The Obedience Stated**

The sentence records the words of the believers that they hear and obey, and the community's teachers read the clause as the statement of the disposition that accompanies the faith. The Book has required the obedience of the community throughout the sūrah, and the verse here supplies the words in which it is expressed.

The commentators explained the phrase as the acceptance of the command together with the readiness to perform it, and they noted the connection with the instructions that the sūrah has set out. The community's scholars read the confession as the answer that the believers give to the revelation.

The teachers also observed that the phrase stands between the statement of the faith and the petition that follows, so that the obedience and the request for forgiveness belong to one another. The sentence in this place presents the conduct of the community in the words that the Book places in its mouth.

**The Petition Named**

The sentence closes with the request for the forgiveness of the Lord and with the statement that the return is to Him, and the community's teachers read the two clauses as the completion of the confession. The Book's treatment of the forgiveness appears throughout the sūrah, and the verse here supplies the petition that accompanies the community's faith.

The scholars of the community noted the phrase about the return, since the confession ends where the account of the earlier verse began. The teachers read the arrangement as the statement that the faith of the community is directed toward the meeting with its Lord.

The wording also places the petition within the statement that the community repeats, so that the request for forgiveness belongs to its confession rather than to a private moment. The community's preachers used the verse when they taught the words that gather the faith.

**The Standing of the Verse**

The sentence is among the passages that the community recites together with the verse that follows it, and the reports of the community describe the standing of the two. The teachers read the pair as the seal of the sūrah, since the confession of the faith and the supplication that accompanies it close the long chapter.

The reports preserve the sayings of the Prophet ﷺ about the two verses, and the material describes the gift that the community received. The community's scholars transmitted the accounts and used them when they encouraged the recitation of the passage.

The verse's placement at the end of the sūrah gives it its function, since the chapter that began with the description of the Book and its guidance closes with the confession of those who accepted it. The teachers of the community read the arrangement as the statement of the sūrah's purpose.

**What the Verse Establishes**

The sentence establishes the faith of the messenger and of the believers, the articles that the community accepts, the refusal to make a distinction among the messengers, the obedience that accompanies the confession, and the petition for forgiveness with which the verse closes. The community's teachers used the verse when they set out the creed of the community and when they taught the words that summarize the faith, and the sūrah's final verse supplies the supplication that completes the passage.

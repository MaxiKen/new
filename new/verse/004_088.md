## Sūrah al-Nisāʾ 4:88

> Why are you ˹believers˺ divided into two groups regarding the hypocrites while Allah allowed them to regress ˹to disbelief˺ because of their misdeeds? Do you wish to guide those left by Allah to stray? And whoever Allah leaves to stray, you will never find for them a way.

**Expanded Commentary**

**Why Are You Divided Into Two Groups Regarding the Hypocrites**

The question *fa mā lakum fī al-munāfiqīna fiʾatayni* — why are you believers divided into two groups regarding the hypocrites — addresses the community after the diagnosis that some among you lag (4:72) and some turn away stubbornly from the Book and Messenger (4:61) while claiming *we hear and obey* by day and plotting by night (4:81). *Fiʾatayni* — two groups, two factions — was explained as a split among Companions after the expedition to Uḥud, where hypocrites lagged and believers differed over whether they were still counted as believers to be helped or as a group to be treated as having regressed. The sūrah has shown the hypocrites’ many signs — seeking false judges though commanded to reject them (4:60), publicising security or fear without referral (4:83), and saying to one who offers peace *you are no believer* for gain (4:94) — and the believers’ instruction to refer disputes to God and Messenger as best outcome (4:59). To be *fiʾatayni* about them is to disagree about the very case the sūrah has been clarifying, as though the limits about trust, greeting, and fighting were not yet clear enough to judge.

**While Allah Allowed Them to Regress Because of Misdeeds**

The clause *wa Allāhu arkasa hum bi mā kasabū* — while Allah allowed them to regress to disbelief because of what they earned — was read as divine recompense that inverts progress. *Arkasa* — to turn over, to make regress, to return to the start — was explained as turning them back into disbelief after they had outwardly moved forward, because of *bi mā kasabū* — because of what they earned — the same phrase that ties disaster to hands (4:62) and evil from the self (4:79). The sūrah has tied their earning to many acts: seeking *ṭāghūt* (4:60), distorting *rāʿinā* (4:46), fabricating lies as blatant sin (4:50), believing in *jibt* (4:51), and envying for bounty (4:54). To earn those is to earn regression, because the path God made clear (4:26) and to which He would have guided had they done what was advised (4:68) was left, and the far straying Satan desires (4:60) was followed. The sūrah has said that God is sufficient as Guide and that He guides to the straight path whom He wills; *arkasa* shows the opposite dispensation: those who earn deviation are allowed to regress, as the sūrah earlier said *“Allah has condemned them for disbelief, so they do not believe except a few”* (4:46).

**Do You Wish to Guide Those Left to Stray**

The question *a turīdūna an tahdū man aḍalla Allāhu* — do you wish to guide those God has left to stray — was read as correction of the faction that wanted to treat the regressed as still guided. *Man aḍalla Allāhu* — those God has left to stray — was explained as those whom He allowed to regress after they chose what earns regression; the sūrah has warned that whoever He leaves to stray, you will never find a way for them (4:88, 4:143). To wish to guide them as though the choice had not been made is to dispute the divine recompense itself. The sūrah has shown that guidance is favour from God known to Him (4:70) and that whoever obeys is with the blessed (4:69); favour and guidance follow obedience, not claim. To seek to guide as believers those whose earning was to seek false judges and to lag at disaster as if no bond existed (4:73) is to confuse the community’s *mawaddah* — bond — with approval. The sūrah elsewhere says that God guides not those who do wrong after clear signs, and *arkasa* is the term for that wrong’s end where guidance is withdrawn.

**Whoever He Leaves to Stray, You Will Never Find a Way**

The closing *wa man yuḍlili Allāhu fa lan tajida lahu sabīlā* — and whoever Allah leaves to stray, you will never find for them a way — was read as sealing the case. *Sabīl* — way — was explained as the path God wills to make clear (4:26), the noble ways of those before, and the straight path (4:68). To be left without *sabīl* is to be without the straight path that would have been granted had they done what was advised, and without the referral that is best outcome (4:59). The sūrah will repeat this principle where hypocrites are concerned, showing that where God allows regression, human *tawfīq* cannot force guidance. The phrase *lan tajida* — you will never find — was addressed to the Prophet ﷺ and to any believer, as though to say look for a way for them and you will not find one, because the sūrah has already shown that the distributor of bounty and guidance is not the envious who would not give a speck (4:53) but the One who never wrongs by an atom. To seek a way for those He has left to stray is therefore to seek *sabīl* where He has closed it, while the sūrah’s open *sabīl* — trade by mutual consent (4:29), greeting with better (4:86), and fighting for the oppressed (4:75) — remains for those who have not regressed.

**A Verse That Steadies the Community’s Judgment**

Placed after the gathering on the Day about which there is no doubt and whose word is more truthful than God’s (4:87), this verse steadies judgment before the next verses describe who may be taken as allies and who must be left where they are allies of a treaty people or opposed to fighting (4:89–90). The community divided into two groups about hypocrites is told that division itself is mistaken where God has already allowed regression because of what was earned; the hypocrites are not a middle group to be guided as though they were steadfast, because the sūrah has shown their many signs — night-plotting, publicising without referral, seeking *ṭāghūt* — and their recompense is regression. For the weak for whom burdens were lightened, the verse is also lightening: you need not guide those God has left to stray; you need only be clear about regressed because of misdeeds, so that the bond *you are from one another* (4:25) is not used to excuse the one who treats it as if it were none. The sūrah therefore teaches the community to judge as God judges — by what was earned — and to keep its two groups as one in that judgment, so that the straight path remains clear.

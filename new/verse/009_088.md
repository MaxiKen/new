## Sūrah al-Tawbah 9:88

> But the Messenger and the believers with him strived with their wealth and their lives. They will have all the best, and it is they who will be successful.

**Expanded Commentary**

**The Messenger Leads Through Sacrifice**

The verse places the Messenger and the believers who remain with him on the side of striving. Leadership is not presented as a command issued from safety while others bear every cost. The Messenger spends wealth, strength, time, and personal security alongside the people. His example gives the command credibility because he embodies what he asks from others.

This does not mean that every leader must perform the same physical act as every follower. It means that leadership cannot be built on permanent exemption from sacrifice. A leader who asks others to endure what he will never share loses moral authority. A leader who shares the burden can correct, organize, and decide with a clearer understanding of what the people are carrying.

**Wealth and Life Form a Complete Offering**

The verse again joins wealth and life. A person may give resources from a distance, or give bodily effort while lacking money. The two forms together express the whole human capacity. The believer asks what Allah has placed in his hand and offers that capacity without claiming that one type of sacrifice makes all other duties unnecessary.

The phrase also prevents a purely symbolic faith. Belief has a body and a budget. It appears in how people spend, travel, care, teach, and protect. A servant can be poor and still spend himself in service. A wealthy servant can offer equipment, relief, and time. The key is that neither hides behind the form he prefers while refusing the form the situation requires.

**The Best Includes Both Worlds**

The promise of “all the best” is broad. Exegetes have understood it to include good things in this life and the reward of good deeds in the Hereafter. Sincere striving can produce a good reputation, useful example, stronger community, and inward dignity, but its greatest return remains with Allah.

This promise does not guarantee that every effort will receive immediate public success. A believer may strive and still face loss, criticism, or death. The good may appear as faith preserved, a wrong resisted, or a later generation strengthened. Allah's measure includes effects hidden from the servant. The best is not always the outcome he first requested.

**Success Is Defined by Faithful Action**

The verse calls them successful because they acted in obedience. Success is not reduced to defeating an opponent or acquiring wealth. It is reaching the result Allah approves, whether the world recognizes it or not. This definition allows the servant to continue after a setback without declaring the effort meaningless.

It also prevents pride after victory. If success belongs to Allah's judgment, the servant cannot use it as proof that he is superior to every person who stayed behind. He gives thanks, examines his intention, and remembers the people whose hidden service supported the result. A successful act remains safe when gratitude follows it.

**The Community Is Built by Shared Examples**

The Messenger's striving and the believers' striving are mentioned together. This gives the community a shared identity. People do not merely admire a leader's sacrifice; they join the work according to ability. The example travels through families, neighbors, donors, teachers, and protectors until service becomes a culture rather than a performance by a few heroes.

A community should therefore celebrate faithful conduct without turning servants into idols. Public examples can encourage people, but the example must point to Allah. The best leader makes others capable of serving, and the best follower serves without waiting for a leader to see him. Shared effort multiplies good beyond any one person's strength.

**Good Things Must Be Used for Good**

If Allah grants success, the believer treats the good things as trusts. Reputation becomes a chance to teach, wealth becomes a chance to relieve need, and strength becomes a chance to protect. A gift that is kept for the ego can become a new test even after an earlier sacrifice was accepted.

The verse therefore calls for continued effort after the visible struggle. Do not return to selfish comfort once the crisis ends. Keep the best of the result moving toward Allah's cause. Success is complete when it produces more obedience, more mercy, and more people able to serve.

The Messenger's example also protects followers from a false division between spiritual and practical work. Wealth can support a cause, but bodies must carry responsibilities that money cannot perform. Teaching can guide action, but action is needed when people require protection. The believers' success comes from joining these forms rather than ranking one servant's gift against another's. A person who gives money should not belittle the person who gives labor, and the person who labors should not despise the resources that make the labor possible. Allah gathers the different sacrifices into a good that exceeds any single contribution.

Good things also include the good name that sincere service leaves behind. A community may remember a person who spent wealth, protected others, or accepted hardship without demanding praise. Such remembrance is not the final reward, but it can encourage later people to follow. The servant should not seek reputation as his aim, yet he need not reject every good result Allah gives to faithful work. He uses reputation to direct people back to Allah and keeps the heart humble. Prosperity in this world is safe when it remains a consequence of obedience rather than a replacement for it.

Their success is safe when every good thing remains a trust and not a new object of pride.

Allah sees every sincere contribution.

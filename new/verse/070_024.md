## Sūrah al-Maʿārij 70:24

> and who give the rightful share of their wealth

**Expanded Commentary**

**Wealth Contains Others' Rights**

Wa-fī amwālihim ḥaqqun — "in their wealth, a right." Fī locates others' claims inside "my" money — ownership qualified from within. Ḥaqq means right, due, entitlement — not gift, tip, or favor. The poor own a share before asking; the rich hold it in trust. Such indwelling-right revolutionizes property: wealth is partnership, God the senior partner assigning shares. Withholding ḥaqq is thus misappropriation — keeping trust funds. Readers should audit holdings: which portion is others'? The verse presumes recognition — the righteous know wealth's dual ownership. What fī declares, fingers must deliver. Wealth wholly "mine" is wealth misunderstood. Others' rights reside in your accounts; release them.

Maʿlūm — "known, determined, acknowledged." The due is specified — fixed portion, known rate, not vague generosity. Maʿlūm transforms charity from mood to obligation: calculable, claimable, auditable. Such fixity protects both parties — givers know duty's limit, receivers know claim's measure. Vague benevolence fluctuates with feeling; maʿlūm endures past moods. Readers should fix their giving: calculate, schedule, deliver. What is known gets paid; what is felt gets forgotten. The adjective also dignifies receivers — claiming a known right, not begging a favor. Fixity is mercy's formality. Know your due; pay it duly.

**Zakāh or Beyond**

Scope divides exegetes: obligatory zakāh (Qatādah, al-Rāzī, al-Ṭabarī's majority) or zakāh-plus-voluntary (al-Rāzī, al-Ṭabarī also allow)? Zakāh fits maʿlūm — fixed rate, known due. Voluntary fits "in their wealth" — generosity beyond minimum. Grounds for majority: maʿlūm's fixity matches zakāh's nisab-rates. Grounds for widening: the righteous exceed minimums habitually. Strong reading: zakāh certainly, ṣadaqah additionally — obligation floored, generosity ceiled never. Both agree on essence — wealth shared duly. Readers should pay zakāh meticulously, then give beyond cheerfully. Minimums mandatory, maximums absent. What law fixes, love exceeds.

Right precedes request. Ḥaqq maʿlūm exists before beggars beg — ownership independent of asking. Such precedence dignifies: receivers claim, not plead. The verse's order (duty stated verse 24, recipients named verse 25) enacts this — obligation first, beneficiaries after. Giving thus answers God before need: duty-driven, not pity-driven. Readers should give by calendar, not by encounter — dues disbursed systematically. Encounter-based giving fluctuates; calendar-based endures. What God assigned, schedules deliver. The poor's ownership antedates their petition. Pay dues owing, not alms felt.

**Direct Cure for Manūʿ**

Verse 21 diagnosed withholding (manūʿ); verse 24 prescribes giving (ḥaqq). Symptom then treatment — exact correspondence. Manūʿ clutched good's touch; ḥaqq releases it. The sūrah's medicine matches disease molecule-for-molecule. Such precision proves clinical intent: the duty-list is prescription-pad, each duty dosing a symptom. Readers should take verse 24 as verse 21's antidote personally: grudging confessed, giving commenced. Diagnose by withholding-instances; treat by giving-acts. What manūʿ sealed, ḥaqq unseals. Prescription filled, syndrome fades. Give per verse 24; cease per verse 21.

Hoarded wealth rots; shared wealth roots. Barakah (blessing-growth) follows circulation: spent wealth multiplies (in heaven's accounts, often earth's). The hoarder's vessels (verse 18's awʿā) sealed fortune into fuel; the giver's hands convert fortune into eternity. Such economics invert earth's: saving secures temporarily, spending secures eternally. Readers should invest accordingly: portfolios split between vaults and charity. What stays, burns; what goes, grows. Fortune's nature is flow — dam it, and it stagnates; channel it, and it blesses. Share wealth; keep blessing.

**Due Paid Delivers Both Parties**

Giver purified, receiver dignified — ḥaqq delivers doubly. Tazkiyah (purification-growth) names zakāh's effect: wealth cleansed, soul grown. Givers shed miserliness; receivers meet needs honorably. Such mutuality makes charity communion, not condescension. Both parties gain; neither grovels. Readers should give in this spirit: purifying self, honoring fellow. What dues deliver exceeds money — dignity, purity, brotherhood. Pay dues; free both. The transaction blesses giver most — purification's profit. Give doubly-blessed.

**Shares Have Names**

The due has destinees: kin, orphans, the needy, the traveler, beggars — the Book elsewhere itemizes wealth's rightful claimants (2:177). Names prevent abstraction: ḥaqq maʿlūm wears faces. Kin first — blood's claim nearest. Orphans next — the fatherless fathered by community. Then need, travel, asking — each station specified. Such listing guides disbursement: dues distributed across categories, none monopolized. Readers should map their giving onto names: which orphan? what traveler? Named shares get paid; vague benevolence evaporates. What the list particularizes, conscience must deliver. Learn the names; release the shares. Wealth's rights walk on feet — find them.

**The Due Date Is Death**

Zakāh recurs annually; procrastination tempts perpetually. "Tomorrow" is manūʿ's accomplice — dues delayed become dues denied. The verse's maʿlūm (known) implies scheduled: known amounts, known times. Pay promptly, pay yearly, pay early. Death ends deposits but not debts — unpaid dues testify against hoarders. Such urgency pastors planners: calculate now, disburse soon. What schedules command, excuses cannot cancel. Readers should calendarize charity: annual zakāh-date fixed, monthly ṣadaqah-rhythm kept. Due dates discipline generosity. Meet heaven's deadlines before your own expires. Pay dated dues; enter dateless bliss.

Money examines before it enriches. Every fortune arrives as trial: acknowledged due paid, or manūʿ confessed? The test precedes the comfort — accounts credited, conscience debited till charity clears. Such examination dignifies wealth: fortune is stewardship exam, not ownership prize. Failures fill Laẓā's summons (verse 18's hoarders); passes fill gardens. Readers should receive income as question paper: dues calculated? shares released? What wealth tests, giving answers. The examination repeats per payday — no final passing till death. Treat each deposit as interrogation: whose is this portion? Answer with disbursement. Tested givers become trusted receivers — of eternity.

**Beggar and Deprived Named Next**

Duty stated; recipients follow. Verse 25 will name them — beggar and deprived. Obligation first, beneficiaries after: the sūrah orders ethics rightly. Readers should carry dues toward their destined hands. What follows will identify wealth's rightful claimants. The next verse names the beggar and the deprived as due-recipients.

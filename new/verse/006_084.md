## Sūrah al-Anʿām 6:84

> And We blessed him with Isaac and Jacob. We guided them all as We previously guided Noah and those among his descendants: David, Solomon, Job, Joseph, Moses, and Aaron. This is how We reward the good-doers.

**Expanded Commentary**

**The Gift of Descendants**

And We blessed him with Isaac and Jacob. The sentence follows the account of Abraham's trial with the announcement of what he received, and the Book pairs the two elsewhere in the same order. The sūrah of the Ranged records his prayer for righteous offspring (37:100), and the sūrah of the Prophets records its answer, saying that God blessed him with Isaac as a son and Jacob as a grandson, as an additional favour, and made all of them righteous (21:72). The addition of the words as an additional favour is the Book's way of saying that the gift exceeded what was asked.

The two names matter for the sūrah's audience as well as for Abraham. The tribes of Israel traced themselves to Jacob, and the Book elsewhere tells the Prophet that the believers make no distinction among the prophets and that they believe in what was revealed to Abraham, Ishmael, Isaac, Jacob, and their descendants (2:136; 3:84). The line that begins here is the line the sūrah will follow through the next two verses.

**"We Guided Them All"**

We guided them all as We previously guided Noah. The sentence places guidance rather than lineage at the centre of the gift, and it names Noah as the standard by which the others are measured for a reason that the next verse makes explicit: the men listed here are described as being among his descendants. The sūrah of Iron states the arrangement directly when it says that God sent Noah and Abraham and reserved prophethood and revelation for their descendants (57:26).

The Book's list of the guided in this verse includes Job, who does not belong to the Israelite line, alongside David, Solomon, Joseph, Moses, and Aaron. The point of the catalogue is that guidance is God's gift to those He chooses rather than an inheritance that runs through a single family, and the sūrah states that principle in the sentence that closes the verse.

**The Reward Attached to Good Conduct**

This is how We reward the good-doers. The sentence names the reward and the conduct it answers, and the reward in context is the guidance just described. The Book repeats the connection in several places: those who struggle in God's cause will be guided along His ways (29:69), and Noah is described as utterly grateful for God's favours, with the result that God chose him and guided him to the straight path (16:121). Guidance, in the Book's usage, is both the gift that starts a person on the road and the reward that follows the conduct it produces.

The sūrah of Consultation states the same principle in a form that ties it to the whole line of prophets, saying that God has ordained the way He decreed for Noah, for Abraham, Moses, and Jesus, and that He chooses for Himself whoever wills and guides to Himself whoever turns to Him (42:13). The catalogue in this verse is a list of people who did what that verse describes, and the reward they received is the guidance the sūrah says belongs to God.

**The Character of the Men Named**

The list in this verse moves through the great figures of the Book's prophetic history, and each one carries the story the Book tells about him. David and Solomon received kingship and judgement; Job endured a trial that the Book holds up as an example of patience; Joseph passed through slavery and imprisonment to authority; Moses and Aaron were sent to the tyrant of Egypt. The Book mentions them here without detail because the audience knew their stories, and the shared element in those stories is what the verse is naming.

That shared element is the guidance of God, given to each of them in the circumstances of his own life. The Book's habit of listing the prophets in groups is not a genealogical exercise. It is a demonstration that the same gift has been given across different centuries, lands, and conditions, and that the pattern does not change with the circumstances.

**Why the List Matters to the Makkans**

The sūrah's audience had claimed Abraham and used the claim to argue that their religion was the religion of their fathers. The catalogue in this verse and the two that follow it widens the frame: the prophets who carried the same message include men from outside the Arabian peninsula and outside the audience's lineage, and the Book had already said that God chose Adam, Noah, the family of Abraham, and the family of ʿImrān above all people of their time (3:33).

The list therefore functions as an answer to the claim of exclusive descent. Among the figures named in this verse are men from every branch of the tradition that the audience would have recognised, and the point of naming them together is that they were all guided by the same Lord, with the same message, and were all rewarded for the same thing.

**The Transition Into the Next Verse**

The sūrah continues the list immediately in the next verse with Zachariah, John, Jesus, and Elias, and then in the verse after it with Ishmael, Elisha, Jonah, and Lot. The catalogue is arranged in three groups, and the group in this verse begins with Abraham's sons and continues through the great names of the Israelite tradition.

The three verses together set the stage for the statement in the sūrah that follows them, which says that the guidance given to all these men is God's guidance, given to whoever He wills of His servants, and that if they had associated others with Him their deeds would have been wasted (6:88). The list has been assembled, and the sūrah now states what it demonstrates.

## Sūrah al-Aʿrāf 7:74

> Remember when He made you successors after ’Âd and established you in the land—˹and˺ you built palaces on its plains and carved homes into mountains. So remember Allah’s favours, and do not go about spreading corruption in the land.”

**Expanded Commentary**

**The Reminder of the Succession**

The verse continues the speech of Ṣāliḥ to his people, and it reminds them that God made them successors after the people of ʿĀd. The sūrah has used the same reminder in the mouth of Hūd, who told his people that they had been made successors after the people of Noah (7:69), and the sequence of the two verses states the chain of the replacements. The Book's use of the term for the peoples that took the place of the destroyed communities appears in the sūrah of the Cattle, where the audience is told that God has made them successors in the earth (6:165).

The reminder carries the argument that the sūrah is making. The people of Thamūd occupied the land and the wealth of a community that had been removed for its denial, and the messenger in front of us states the origin of what they possessed. The Book's narratives use the same reminder with the peoples that followed the destroyed communities, and the verse in front of us supplies the third instance in the series.

**The Settlement in the Land**

The verse states that God settled the people in the land, and the settlement is then specified by the kinds of building that it supported. The Book's descriptions of the destroyed peoples' habitation include the details of their construction, and the sūrah of the Rocky Tract states that the people of the stone valley carved dwellings out of the mountains in safety (15:82), while the sūrah of the Poets reports the messenger asking his people how they persist in carving out homes in the mountains with skill (26:149). The verse in front of us names the two kinds of building in a single sentence.

The description of the buildings serves the reminder of the favours, and the Book's accounts of the peoples that were given such advantages state that the advantages did not exempt them from the reckoning. The sūrah of the Dawn describes the people of Iram with their pillars and the people of Thamūd who carved out the rocks in the valley (89:7–9), and the passage then states that the peoples transgressed in the lands and multiplied corruption. The verse in front of us supplies the reminder in the messenger's own words.

**The Acknowledgment of the Dwellings**

The Book treats the remains of these dwellings as a sign that the audience knew, and the sūrah of the Rocky Tract states that the people of the valley were destroyed while their carved homes remained. The tradition records that the Prophet and his companions passed the ruins of Thamūd on the way to the expedition to Tabūk, and that he commanded them not to enter the dwellings of those who had wronged themselves except in a state of weeping, so that what had befallen the people would not befall them (al-Bukhārī 3380; Muslim 2980). The verse in front of us names the dwellings, and the tradition records the encounter with what was left of them.

The mention of the buildings in the verse therefore addresses a people whose monuments stood in the sight of the sūrah's first audience, and the sūrah's reader is directed to look at what the buildings did not protect. The Book's later references to the sites of the destroyed peoples serve the same purpose, and the passage in front of us states the buildings as evidence rather than as ornament.

**The Command to Remember the Boons**

The verse turns from the description of the buildings to the command to remember the boons of God, and the reminder is the purpose of the enumeration. The sūrah's earlier account of ʿĀd used the same command after naming the increase in stature and the inheritance of the land (7:69), and the sūrah of the Hūd's version of Thamūd's story reports the messenger recounting the favours of God among his people. This verse places the command after the list, and the order states that the favours are the evidence.

The Book's habit of connecting gratitude to the right use of what has been given appears throughout its addresses, and the sūrah's opening section has connected the enjoyment of the earth to the accounting that follows. The command in front of us states the response that the favours required, and the narrative that follows records the community's refusal.

**The Prohibition of Corruption**

The verse closes by forbidding the people to behave wickedly on the earth and to work corruption, and the phrase repeats the sūrah's earlier prohibition addressed to the community after the establishment of order (7:56). The Book uses the term for the conduct that spoils the arrangements of life, and the sūrah's account of the people of Shuʿayb will use the same term in the mouth of the messenger who warns against dishonest measures. The verse here states the prohibition after the reminder, and the two together state the messenger's request in full.

The Book's pairing of the command to remember the favours with the prohibition of corruption appears in its addresses to the communities, and the sūrah's series of narratives presents the peoples that disregarded both. The present verse supplies the third instance of the appeal, and the account that follows states the answer that the community gave.

**Where the Verse Sits**

The verse stands seventy-fourth in the chapter, and it follows the presentation of the sign and precedes the answer of the leaders. The verses that follow report the exchange between the arrogant and the believers among the people and the killing of the she-camel (7:75–77), and the account closes with the destruction of the community. The verse under discussion supplies the reminder that the third narrative contains.

The reader who has heard the call and seen the sign now reads the reminder of what the people had been given. The land, the castles, and the carved homes are named, the command to remember is issued, and corruption is forbidden; and the sūrah's next verses state the response of the leaders of Thamūd.

**The Transition to the Next Verse**

The next verse reports that the arrogant leaders asked the believers among the weak whether they knew that Ṣāliḥ had been sent by his Lord, and that the believers answered that they believed in what he had been sent with (7:75). The reminder of the favours in front of us is followed by the account of the division within the community, and the exchange shows which of the two groups the leaders addressed and how the believers answered.

The two verses together state the position of the third community before its destruction. The messenger reminds the people of what they have been given and forbids them to spoil it, and the leaders turn to the believers with a question that reveals their own refusal; and the sūrah's following verses record the killing of the sign and the end of the people.

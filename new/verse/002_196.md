## Sūrah al-Baqarah 2:196

> Complete the pilgrimage and minor pilgrimage for Allah. But if prevented ˹from proceeding˺, then ˹offer˺ whatever sacrificial animals you can afford. And do not shave your heads until the sacrificial animal reaches its destination. But if any of you is ill or has a scalp ailment ˹requiring shaving˺, then compensate either by fasting, charity, or a sacrificial offering. In times of peace, you may combine the pilgrimage and minor pilgrimage then make the sacrificial offering you can afford. Whoever cannot afford that ˹offering˺, let them fast three days during pilgrimage and seven after returning ˹home˺—completing ten. These offerings are for those who do not live near the Sacred House. And be mindful of Allah, and know that Allah is severe in punishment.

**Expanded Commentary**

**The Command to Complete the Rites**

The verse commands the community to complete the greater and the lesser pilgrimage for God, and it then sets out what is required of a person who is prevented from proceeding. The sentence opens a section on the pilgrimage, and the community's scholars read it as the foundation of the rulings on the journey and its interruption.

The command to complete carries the sense of finishing what has been begun, and the commentators used the wording to discuss the status of the lesser pilgrimage. What the reader receives is an instruction about the fulfilment of a rite, and the sentence then provides for the circumstances in which the fulfilment is delayed.

**The Status of the Pilgrimages**

The community's scholars state that the greater pilgrimage is one of the obligations of the religion and is required of those who are able, while the lesser pilgrimage is generally held to be a voluntary act. Some held that the command in this verse makes both obligatory, and others read the instruction as a requirement to finish whatever a person has begun.

The two readings are reflected in the community's practice, and the discussion shows how the verse was used in the development of the law. What the reader is given is an instruction whose scope was debated among the community's earliest scholars, and the text's wording supports the emphasis on completion rather than on the initial undertaking.

**The Offering for One Who Is Prevented**

The sentence states that a person who is prevented from completing the rites should offer whatever sacrificial animal he can afford, and it forbids shaving the head until the offering reaches its place. The commentators explained the situation as one in which a pilgrim is stopped by an obstacle or an illness before the rites are finished.

The community's jurists derived the rules for the person who is detained from the sentence, and they connected them with the events of Ḥudaybiyah. What the reader receives is a picture of a worship that provides for the interruption as well as the completion, and the sentence's provisions show that the Book's legislation takes account of what a traveller may meet.

**The Provision for Illness**

The sentence then permits a person who is ill or who suffers from a scalp ailment to shave and to compensate by fasting, by charity, or by an offering. The community's scholars explained the three alternatives as the compensation required for the relief that the person has taken.

The ruling appears again in the sūrah's treatment of the pilgrimage elsewhere in the Book, and the community's jurists derived from it the range of penalties available for the violations of the sacred state. The reader carries away a statement that the Book's legislation offers a means of repair rather than leaving the offender without recourse, and the sentence's three options show the flexibility the law provides.

**The Two Forms of the Journey**

The sentence provides for the pilgrim who joins the lesser pilgrimage to the greater in a single journey, and it requires an offering from him that is within his means, or a fast of three days during the pilgrimage and seven after the return. The community's jurists built the rules for the combined journey on these words, and they explained the ten days as the alternative for a person who cannot furnish an offering.

The provision is limited to those whose families do not live near the sanctuary, and the community's scholars explained the restriction by the circumstances of the journey. What the reader is given is a detailed account of the different ways in which the pilgrimages may be performed, and the sentence shows the Book's attention to the practical conditions of the community's worship.

**Reverence and the Warning**

The sentence closes with the command to be mindful of God and the statement that He is severe in punishment, and the community's scholars read the ending as the counterpart of the provisions that preceded it. The Book's legislation on the pilgrimage is accompanied by warnings as well as by permissions, and the reader is shown that the rites are not to be treated lightly.

The pair of clauses gives the sentence its balance, and the community's teachers used the ending when they spoke about the seriousness of the journey. The sentence leaves him with a statement about the standing of the rites in the Book's scheme, and the passage continues with the months in which the pilgrimage is performed.

**The Place of the Verse in the Section**

The sentence stands between the command to spend in the way of God and the rules of the pilgrimage, and it connects the community's property with its worship. The verses that follow describe the months, the conduct required of the pilgrim, and the remembrance that accompanies the rites, and the reader is carried from the general obligation to the details of its performance.

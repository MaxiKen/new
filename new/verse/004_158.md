## Sūrah al-Nisāʾ 4:158

> Rather, Allah raised him up to Himself. And Allah is Almighty, AllWise.

**Expanded Commentary**

**Rather God Raised Him to Himself**

The verse states *bal rafaʿahu Allāhu ilayhi* — rather, nay, but God raised him, lifted him, up to Him, to Himself. *Bal* — rather — was explained as correction to *they killed him not nor crucified, it was made to appear* (4:157), where boasting *we killed the Messiah, son of Mary, messenger of God* was negated; *rather* His raising is the truth. *Rafaʿahu Allāhu ilayhi* — God raised him to Him — was explained as God lifting Jesus alive to Himself, as He raised the Mount over them for covenant (4:154) — raising Mount as warning, raising Jesus as honour — the sūrah’s two raisings: one over breakers to frighten, one of the messenger to preserve. *Ilayhi* — to Him, to Himself — was read as to His presence, to the heavens, as the sūrah elsewhere says *“He raised him to Himself”* and the sūrah says *O Jesus, I will take you and raise you to Me”* (3:55). Early reports noted that Jesus was raised while his likeness was cast on another; the *shubbiha lahum* — made to appear — is explained by *rafaʿahu* — raised. Scripture has shown that God suffices as Trustee (4:132) and bears witness with His knowledge (4:166); raising the messenger is His trusteeship executed, as He can remove humanity and bring others (4:133) yet here removes the messenger from killers and brings him to Himself, preserving the line of revelation. The phrase was linked to *“Gardens for those who believe and do good”* (4:122) — raising is the Gardens’ prototype: honour of nearness.

**And God Is Almighty, All-Wise**

The seal *wa kāna Allāhu ʿAzīzan Ḥakīmā* — And God is Almighty, All-Powerful, All-Wise, was ever — was explained with *ʿAzīz* — Almighty, Mighty, invincible — the One who can raise against killers, as He is *Qadīr* — Most Capable to remove humanity (4:133) and *Qadīr* to pardon though capable to punish (4:149) — here *ʿAzīz* is capability to protect messenger where boasters claim killing. *Ḥakīm* — All-Wise — was read as wisdom that made likeness to appear to boasters while raising, that let disputants remain in doubt following assumption (4:157), and that will make his future testimony decisive. *ʿAzīz Ḥakīm* was also the seal for messengers delivering good news and warnings so humanity have no excuse after messengers (4:165), showing that raising Jesus preserves the messenger lineage until the final messenger — *“We have sent revelation to you as We sent to Noah and prophets after him”* (4:163) — all sent by the Almighty, All-Wise. Where the sūrah says *all honour belongs to Him entirely* (4:139) sought by taking disbelievers as allies, Jesus raised to Him is the honour given to the messenger whose *ʿizzah* boasters thought they terminated.

**A Verse That Follows Not Killed With Raised**

Placed after thunderbolt, calf, pardon, Mount, gate, Sabbath, firm covenant, then breaking, rejecting, killing prophets, claiming *ghulf*, denying, slandering Mary, boasting killing Messiah not killed nor crucified, it was made to appear, disputants in doubt, no knowledge but assumption, certainly not killed (4:153–157), this verse follows *not killed* with *raised*. The sūrah has shown that *“to Him belongs whatever is in heavens and earth”* (4:126, 4:131) — raising to Him is raising within His ownership; His heavens are the *maḥīṭ* — encompassing (4:126) — that can receive. The sūrah has shown that *“He does not wrong even an atom”* (4:40) — to raise the messenger whom they wrongly boasted killing is not wronging the atom but preserving it. The sūrah that warns *most secret talks have no good except charity, kindness, reconciliation* (4:114) sees boasting murder as the worst secret talk made loud boast; raising is its rebuttal as public honour.

**Raising as the Sūrah’s Reply to Visible Demand**

The raising *rafaʿahu Allāhu ilayhi* was linked to the initial demand — *bring down a book from heaven* (4:153) and *show God visible* — the demand for visible spectacle: those who demanded visible God and then took visible calf are now shown that the visible messenger they boasted visible killing was made invisible by raising — spectacle’s opposite. Where Moses’ people saw the Mount raised over them as warning for covenant, the Messiah’s people who boasted murder are shown that God raises whom He wills. The verse was read with *“We have sent revelation to you as We sent to Noah…”* (4:163) — revelation continues, messengers are many, raising one does not end mission; the final reminder — *“there are messengers We told you and others We did not, and to Moses He spoke directly”* (4:164) — shows His *kalām* reaches directly and His raising reaches powerfully. For the weak this is an anchor: where the Messiah was wronged by boasters while being God’s Word through Mary and spirit from Him (4:171) not too proud to be servant (4:172), His raising shows the servant honoured though boasters claimed killing.

**A Short Verse That Anchors Witness to Come**

Though only *“Rather God raised him to Himself, He is Almighty, All-Wise,”* the verse anchors the witness to come: the raised Messiah will be believed in by many before the end, and will stand as witness on the Day. The sūrah that bears witness to what He sent with His knowledge and angels bear witness (4:166) makes that future testimony part of its proof. The next passage will turn from history of the Book’s People to law general: forbidden foods, usury, and unjust wealth — painful punishment for disbelievers among them, but great reward for those grounded in knowledge who believe in what was revealed to you and before. That reward is the *ajran ʿaẓīmā* — great reward — He keeps for the grounded in knowledge (4:162).

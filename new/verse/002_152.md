## Sūrah al-Baqarah 2:152

> remember Me; I will remember you. And thank Me, and never be ungrateful.

**Expanded Commentary**

**The Sentence the Blessings Were Leading To**

The unfinished sentence of the previous verse completes itself here, and the completion is an imperative: since We sent you a messenger of your own stock, reciting, purifying, teaching — *remember Me*. The exegetes read the connection exactly that way: God sent you a messenger, so remember Him. The whole inventory of favour — qiblah, House, Book, wisdom, unprecedented knowledge — issues in a command of four words in Arabic, and the command is answered in the same breath by a promise of four more: *I will remember you*. The reciprocity is the verse's structure and its theology at once. Human remembrance is an act — invoked, performed, repeatable; divine remembrance is an event of another order, and the verse stakes the believer's entire standing on the exchange. No other command in the qiblah passage received an answer clause; this one does, because remembrance is the one act whose return transforms the actor's case rather than merely pleasing the Commander.

**What Remembrance Is, on Both Sides**

The early commentators fixed the terms of the exchange. A saying attributed widely among them states the round: God remembers whoever remembers Him, increases whoever is grateful to Him, and punishes whoever disbelieves in Him. On the human side, remembrance is performed through praising God and through acts of obedience — the tongue's dhikr and the limbs' dhikr, which the tradition never separates, since praise without obedience is noise and obedience without praise is machinery. On the divine side, God's remembrance of the servant is His forgiveness, His protection, and — in the language the reports use — His mention of the servant among those near Him. The asymmetry is the point: the servant remembers in time, with a tongue and a will; God remembers with the consequences of eternity attached. The verse's second command, gratitude (*shukr*), extends the same logic to blessings received: gratitude is remembrance with its object specified — the Giver held in mind at the moment of the gift — and its opposite, *kufrān*, ungratefulness, is not mere rudeness but the covering-over of a known favour, the same root the Qur'an uses for disbelief itself.

**The Ḥadīth of Nearness**

The reciprocity of this verse has its fullest exposition in a ḥadīth qudsī — a sacred report in which God is quoted, distinct from Qur'an — preserved in Ṣaḥīḥ al-Bukhārī (7405) from Abu Hurayrah: Allah says, *I am as My servant thinks of Me, and I am with him when he remembers Me. If he remembers Me in himself, I remember him in Myself; if he remembers Me in a gathering, I remember him in a gathering better than it. If he draws near to Me a handspan, I draw near to him an arm's length; if he draws near an arm's length, I draw near two; and if he comes to Me walking, I come to him running.* Every clause escalates in God's favor: the return is always larger than the act, the divine movement always faster than the human one. The report reads like an unfolding of "remember Me, I will remember you" — and its final image, the servant walking and God running to meet him, is the verse's economy stated as parable. Remembrance begun is remembrance answered before it finishes.

**Gathered Remembrance**

The ḥadīth's middle clause — remembrance in a gathering answered by remembrance in a better gathering — received its own elaboration in a report in Ṣaḥīḥ Muslim (2699a): when people assemble in one of the houses of Allah, reciting the Book of God and studying it among themselves, tranquility descends upon them, mercy covers them, the angels surround them, and Allah mentions them among those who are with Him. The four gifts map onto the verse's promise with social precision: the remembered individual of 2:152 becomes the remembered assembly, and the divine remembrance takes visible form — stillness, covering mercy, encircling angels, mention in the highest company. This is why the verse became one of the Qur'anic foundations of methodical remembrance (*dhikr Allāh*) as a practiced discipline: the promise of reciprocity is unconditional in form, and the practice traditions built on it — individual, congregational, litanic — are attempts to hold the human side of the exchange constant so that the divine side, which the texts describe as unfailing, is never missed.

**The Mirror Image: Neglect Answered by Neglect**

The exchange has a negative counterpart that the Qur'an states with the same symmetry: of the hypocrites — *they neglected Allah, so He neglected them* (9:67). The construction is identical to this verse's, reversed: as remembrance is answered by remembrance, forgetting is answered by forgetting. The mirror shows what is actually at stake in the command. Divine neglect is not anger in the human sense but withdrawal — the absence of the mention, the protection, the forgiveness that remembrance draws down; the soul left to the company it chose. The sūrah of Repentance places the sentence after describing people who encourage evil, forbid good, and clutch their wealth, so the neglect named there has a social texture: communities that forget God in their public life are forgotten in theirs. Read beside 2:152, the two verses form the complete covenant of remembrance — the exchange offered and the exchange declined — and the offer's terms explain the urgency of the double command: remember Me, and thank Me, and never be ungrateful. Three imperatives for one act, because the act is the hinge on which the whole relationship turns.

**The Hearts That Settle**

The verse's promise has a documented effect on the human side as well: remembrance is not only answered in heaven but calmed on earth. The sūrah of the Thunder states it as a law of the heart — *"those who believe and whose hearts find comfort in the remembrance of Allah. Surely in the remembrance of Allah do hearts find comfort"* (13:28) — and the sūrah of the Spider ranks the practice above the prayer that contains it: *"The remembrance of Allah is ˹an˺ even greater ˹deterrent˺"* (29:45), greater even than prayer's power to restrain from indecency, because it is the prayer's animating core. The comfort and the restraint are two faces of the same reciprocity: the heart held in God's presence by remembrance is simultaneously soothed and governed. This is the verse's full circle — remember Me, and the remembering itself begins to answer you, quieting the heart that performs it, before the divine remembrance answers it in forgiveness, in mention among the angels, in the gathering better than any gathering on earth.

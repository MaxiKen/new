## Sūrah al-Naḥl 16:28

> **Those whose souls the angels seize while they wrong themselves will then offer ˹full˺ submission ˹and say falsely,˺ “We did not do any evil.” ˹The angels will say,˺ “No! Surely Allah fully knows what you used to do.**

**Expanded Commentary**

**Souls Seized While Wronging Themselves**

Al-ladhīna tatawaffāhum al-malā'ikatu ẓālimī anfusihim — those whom angels take in death while they are wronging themselves. Tatawaffā from w-f-y means to take fully, to cause to die, to take soul. Al-malā'ikah, angels, are agents of death, as in 32:11: *Say, the angel of death who is entrusted with you will take you* (32:11). Ẓālimī anfusihim means wronging themselves, wrongdoers to themselves, because sin harms self first. Al-Ṭabarī explains that wronging self here means persisting in shirk, denial, and opposition to messengers, thereby harming own souls that will face punishment. The phrase indicates that at moment of death, they are still in state of wrongdoing, not having repented. Angels come to take souls while they are in that state, making death itself a moment of exposure. The same phrase appears in 4:97 for those who wronged themselves by staying among disbelievers without emigrating when able.

**Offering Submission At Last Moment**

Fa-alqaw al-salama — then they will offer submission, peace, surrender. Alqaw al-salama means to throw down submission, to offer peace, to surrender. In this context, it means when they see angels and realize death and punishment, they will surrender and claim innocence. Al-Qurṭubī explains that alqaw al-salama here means they will offer submission and claim they were not doing evil, as continuation says: mā kunnā naʿmalu min sū' — we were not doing any evil. This is false claim to escape. Al-Rāzī notes that this submission is not sincere repentance but desperate attempt to avoid consequence when consequence is already present. The Qur'an describes similar deathbed claims in 6:23: *By Allah, our Lord, we were not polytheists* (6:23), and in 63:10: *My Lord, if only You would delay me for brief term so I would give charity* (63:10). All are too late.

**We Did Not Do Any Evil — A False Defense**

Mā kunnā naʿmalu min sū' — we were not doing any evil. Sū' means evil, bad deed, sin. They deny wrongdoing at moment of death, hoping denial will help. Al-Ṭabarī says they deny that they were associating partners, calling revelation fables, plotting, opposing believers. They claim they were not doing evil, meaning they did not consider their deeds evil or they pretend they did not do. This denial is both self-deception and attempted deception of angels. It shows that arrogance persists even at death: instead of admitting fault, they deny. The phrase min sū' with min indicates any evil, even small, they deny all. This is contrast to believers who at death are described as ṭayyibīn, good, pure, acknowledging.

**No, Allah Knows What You Did**

Balā inna Allāha ʿalīmun bi-mā kuntum taʿmalūn — No, indeed Allah knows what you used to do. Balā is strong negation of their denial: no, not as you claim. Inna Allāha ʿalīmun, surely Allah is Knowing of what you used to do. The response is attributed to angels or to Allah via angels, as tafsīr differs. Some say angels respond, some say Allah responds, some say those given knowledge respond. In any case, message is that denial fails because Allah knows. This echoes verses 19 and 23: Allah knows what you conceal and reveal. At death, hidden deeds are known. The phrase bi-mā kuntum taʿmalūn, what you used to do, indicates ongoing deeds, habitual wrongdoing, not single mistake. Kuntum taʿmalūn implies continuity: you used to do, you persisted.

**Why This Scene Is Placed Here**

After describing worldly collapse of plotters and Resurrection humiliation, the sūrah now describes death scene, which is intermediate between world and Resurrection. It shows that humiliation begins at death, not only on Day. Those who plotted and called revelation fables will at death try to surrender and deny, but angels will reject denial. This prepares for verse 29: *Enter gates of Hell to abide therein*. The sequence is: plotting in world, building collapse, death with false submission, angels' rebuttal, entry to Hell, then contrast with righteous. The scene also teaches that submission must be in world before death, not at death when seen. The Qur'an says *But repentance is not for those who do evil until when death comes to one of them, he says, indeed I have repented now* (4:18). True submission is before seeing angels.

**Linguistic Note On Tawaffā And Salam**

Tawaffā from w-f-y means to take fully, to receive in full, used for death because soul is taken fully. Ẓālimī anfusihim, wrongers of themselves, from ẓ-l-m, to wrong, to put thing in wrong place. Alqaw from l-q-y means to throw, cast, offer. Salama from s-l-m means peace, submission, surrender, from same root as islām. In this context, al-salam means submission, surrender, offering peace to angels to avoid punishment. Mā kunnā naʿmalu min sū' — we were not doing evil — uses mā for negation, kunnā for past continuous, naʿmalu for doing. Balā is particle for negating negative statement, meaning yes indeed, no, contrary to what you say. The verse ends without closing quote in translation file, but meaning is complete: Allah knows what you used to do, so denial fails.

**The Difference Between Two Death Scenes**

This verse describes death of wrongdoers, while verse 32 will describe death of virtuous: *those whose souls angels take while they are virtuous, saying Peace be upon you*. The two death scenes are contrasted deliberately. Wrongdoers are taken while wronging themselves, offer false submission, claim no evil, and are told Allah knows what they did, then commanded to enter Hell. Virtuous are taken while virtuous, greeted with peace, told to enter Paradise for what they did. The contrast shows that death is not uniform; it reflects life. Al-Qurṭubī notes that angels take souls in manner that matches deeds: harshly for wrongdoers, gently for righteous. The phrase ẓālimī anfusihim vs ṭayyibīn captures this: one group wronged selves, other purified selves. The lesson is that preparation for death is preparation in life.

**Why False Claim Of No Evil Fails**

Claiming *we did not do any evil* fails for three reasons. First, Allah knows, as verse says. Second, angels have recorded deeds, as 82:10-12 says *Indeed over you are keepers, noble writers who know what you do* (82:10-12). Third, limbs will testify, as 36:65 says *Today We seal their mouths and their hands speak to Us and their feet bear witness to what they used to earn* (36:65). The false claim therefore is futile. It also reveals self-deception: they truly believed their deeds were not evil because their hearts were in denial and arrogant, as verse 22 said. Their denial at death is continuation of denial in life. The angels' response *No, Allah knows* breaks self-deception and establishes truth before punishment.

**A Lesson On Sincerity Before Death**

The verse teaches that sincerity must be before death. Once angels arrive, claims of innocence are not accepted. True repentance is in world, when unseen, not at death when seen. The Qur'an says *Until when death comes to one of them, he says My Lord send me back, that I might do righteousness in what I left behind. No, it is a word he speaks* (23:99-100). The time for doing good is now, while soul is still in body and choice remains. The verse therefore urges listener not to delay repentance, not to be among those who wrong themselves and then offer false submission at last moment. The door of repentance is open now, as verse 18 said Allah is Forgiving, Merciful, but it closes at death.

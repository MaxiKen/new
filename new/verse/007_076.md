## Sūrah al-Aʿrāf 7:76

> The arrogant said, “We surely reject what you believe in.”

**Expanded Commentary**

**The Leaders' Refusal**

The verse reports the answer of the arrogant leaders of Thamūd, and the answer is a declaration that they do not believe in what the believers have accepted. The sentence matches the confession of the believers in the previous verse and states the opposite of it, and the two verses together set the community's division in the clearest form. The sūrah's earlier accounts reported the leaders' charges against the messengers, and the answer in front of us contains no charge; it states the refusal alone.

The form of the refusal is a statement about belief rather than about argument, and the Book's accounts of the elite's answers frequently take the same form. The sūrah of Sabaʾ reports the elite of each society saying that they reject what the messenger has been sent with (34:34), and the sūrah of the Ornament reports the same people answering that they found their forefathers on a way and are following in their footsteps (43:23). The verse in front of us states the refusal in the shortest words that the sūrah's narratives contain.

**The Two Confessions Set Against Each Other**

The verse sets the leaders' unbelief against the believers' faith in the same words, and the parallel states the nature of the division. The believers had said that they believed in what the messenger had been sent with, and the leaders say that they do not believe in what the believers believe. The two sentences describe the same message from two directions, and the sūrah's reader is shown that the difference lies in the people and not in what was presented to them.

The Book's use of the same pairing appears in its account of the two groups at the opening of the sūrah of the Cow, where the believers are described by what they accept and the disbelievers by their refusal (2:3–6), and in the sūrah of the Cave, where the companions of the garden and the believer state their positions in contrast. The verse in front of us supplies the pairing in the narrative of Thamūd, and the account proceeds to the deed that followed the refusal.

**The Elite's Consistency**

The leaders who speak in front of us appear throughout the sūrah's narratives, and their position is stated in the same terms each time. They are the ones who answer the messengers in the earlier accounts, they ask the believers the question that reveals their own view, and they state their refusal in front of us. The Book's treatment of the elite as the group that answers for the community appears in its accounts of the peoples throughout, and the sūrah of the Cattle states that God has placed in every city the great ones who conspire in it (6:123).

The Book's descriptions of the elite's refusal include the note that their standing in the world was the source of their confidence, and the sūrah's passage on the Heights reports its people asking the arrogant whether the believers they had dismissed were not the ones who had entered the garden (7:49). The verse in front of us supplies the refusal that the sūrah's later passage answers, and the narrative that follows shows what the refusal produced.

**The Refusal and the Sign**

The leaders' declaration comes after the sign has been given, and the Book's accounts of the communities state that the signs do not compel belief. The sūrah of Fussilat reports that the people of Thamūd were shown the guidance and chose blindness over it (41:17), and the sūrah of the Sun states that the messenger warned them about the camel and they denied him and hamstrung her. This verse states the refusal while the sign is still alive among them.

The Book's teaching about the relation between signs and belief appears in several passages, and the sūrah of the Cattle reports the audience's demand for a sign and the answer that the signs belong to God. The verse here supplies the declaration of unbelief in the presence of the proof, and the account that follows states what the people did with the sign itself.

**The Community's Answer and Its Consequences**

The refusal in front of us is the community's answer to the message, and the sūrah's narratives state the consequences of the answer in each case. The Book's accounts of the peoples that were destroyed present the destruction as the outcome of the refusal rather than of any defect in the signs, and the sūrah's introduction has stated that the communities that rejected the messengers were destroyed. The present verse supplies the third community's formal rejection, and the account that follows carries the narrative to its end.

The Book's treatment of the refusal as a settled position appears in its accounts of the audiences that asked for the punishment to be brought, and the sūrah's next verse reports the leaders' community carrying out the act that the warning had forbidden. The verse under discussion states the words, and the sūrah's following verses state the deed.

**Where the Verse Sits**

The verse stands seventy-sixth in the chapter, and it answers the believers' confession in the previous verse. The verse that follows reports the hamstringing of the she-camel and the community's demand that the messenger bring the punishment (7:77), and the account closes with the destruction of the people (7:78). Here the verse supplies the leaders' answer in the exchange inside the third community.

The reader who heard the believers state their faith now hears the leaders state their refusal. The two answers are set against each other, and the account proceeds to the act that followed; and the sūrah's next verses report the killing of the sign and the demand that the messenger produce what he had warned about.

**The Transition to the Next Verse**

The next verse states that the people hamstrung the she-camel and insolently defied the command of their Lord, and that they demanded that the messenger bring upon them what he had threatened if he was among those sent (7:77). The refusal in front of us is followed by the deed, and the account then reports the destruction of the community and the messenger's words as he turned away from them.

The two verses together state the end of the third community's probation. The leaders declare their unbelief, the people destroy the sign that had been given to them, and they demand the punishment; and the sūrah's following verses record the arrival of what they asked for.

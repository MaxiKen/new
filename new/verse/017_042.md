## Sūrah al-Isrāʾ 17:42

> **Say, ˹O Prophet,˺ “Had there been other gods besides Him—as they claim—then they would have certainly sought a way to ˹challenge˺ the Lord of the Throne.”**

**Expanded Commentary**

**Had There Been Other Gods Besides Him As They Claim**

Qul law kāna maʿahu ālihatun kamā yaqūlūna — say, if there were with Him gods as they say, claim. Qul from q-w-l, to say, command to Prophet. Law kāna, if there were, hypothetical, had there been. Maʿahu, with Him, with Allah, alongside Him. Ālihatun from a-l-h, gods, deities, plural of ilāh, god. Kamā yaqūlūna, as they say, claim, as they claim, from q-w-l, to say. Al-Ṭabarī explains that say, O Prophet, had there been other gods besides Him, with Him, as they claim, pagans claim there are other gods, angels as daughters, idols as gods, etc., then what would happen? Al-Qurṭubī says law kāna maʿahu ālihatun is hypothetical to refute polytheism, if there were other gods with Allah as they claim, then...

**They Would Have Sought Way To Challenge Lord Of Throne**

Idhan la-ibtaghaw ilā dhī al-ʿarshi sabīlan — then they would have sought to possessor of Throne a way, to challenge. Idhan, then, in that case. La-ibtaghaw from b-gh-y, to seek, desire, as ibtaghā seeking bounty, seeking way. Ilā dhī al-ʿarshi, to possessor of Throne, to Lord of Throne, from dh-w, possessor, and ʿ-r-sh, Throne, mighty Throne, as Lord of Throne, as previous chapter said Lord of Throne. Sabīlan from s-b-l, way, path, means to challenge, to reach, to dispute, to seek way to overthrow, challenge. Al-Ṭabarī explains that then they, other gods, would have certainly sought a way to challenge Lord of Throne, to dispute His sovereignty, to seek way to His Throne, to overthrow, as gods would compete, as kings compete for throne, so if multiple gods, they would seek way to challenge Lord of Throne, leading to chaos, as Qur'an says in 21:22 *Had there been in heavens and earth gods besides Allah, they would have been ruined* (21:22), and 23:91 *Allah has not taken son, nor is there with Him any god, otherwise each god would have taken what he created and some would have sought to overcome others* (23:91).

**Why This Argument Refutes Other Gods**

Why this argument? Because if there were multiple gods, they would compete for Throne, seek way to challenge Lord of Throne, leading to conflict, corruption, not order. The fact that heavens and earth are ordered, night and day signs, provision, etc., shows one Lord, not multiple competing. The argument is rational: multiple sovereigns would seek to dominate, seek way to Throne, leading to chaos, but universe ordered, so only one God, Lord of Throne. The phrase Lord of Throne, dhī al-ʿarshi, indicates supreme sovereignty, Throne is greatest creation, symbol of kingship, so seeking way to Lord of Throne means seeking to challenge supreme sovereignty.

**Connection To Outrageous Claim And Varying Signs**

Verse 40 said Has Lord favoured you with sons and taken angels as daughters? Outrageous claim. Verse 41 varied signs in Qur'an so they may be mindful but drives farther away. Verse 42 says had there been other gods besides Him as they claim they would have sought way to challenge Lord of Throne, refuting other gods. The connection is that outrageous claim that angels are daughters and other gods exist is refuted by rational argument: if other gods, they would seek way to challenge Lord of Throne, leading to chaos, but universe ordered, so no other gods, claim outrageous. Varying signs includes this rational argument, but disbelievers increase in aversion.

**Link To Glorified Highly Exalted Above What They Claim**

Next verse 43 says Glorified and Highly Exalted is He above what they claim, tasbīh, glorification, exaltation, above claims of other gods, daughters, etc. So verse 42 argument that other gods would seek way to Throne leads to glorification above claims, as Allah exalted above such.

**Linguistic Note On Alihah And Ibtigha And Dhu Al-Arsh**

Qul from q-w-l, to say. Law kāna, if there were, hypothetical. Maʿahu, with Him. Ālihah from a-l-h, gods, plural of ilāh. Kamā yaqūlūna, as they say. Idhan, then. Ibtaghaw from b-gh-y, to seek, desire, from ibtaghā. Dhī from dh-w, possessor. ʿArsh from ʿ-r-sh, Throne. Sabīl from s-b-l, way, path. The verse's structure — say if there were with Him gods as they say, then they would have sought to Lord of Throne a way — with hypothetical if other gods as they claim, then consequence seeking way to challenge Lord of Throne, shows refutation of polytheism via rational argument of competing sovereigns seeking Throne.

**How This Verse Teaches Monotheism Via Rational Argument**

This verse teaches monotheism via rational argument: had there been other gods besides Him as they claim, they would have sought way to challenge Lord of Throne, leading to conflict, chaos, but universe ordered, signs varied, provision, etc., so only one God, Lord of Throne, no other gods. Al-Qurṭubī says believer should understand that if multiple gods, they would compete, seek way to Throne, universe would be ruined, but it is ordered, so only one God, Lord of Throne, Glorified Highly Exalted above what they claim. The practical lesson is to affirm monotheism, Allah alone, no other gods, no daughters, no sons, no partners, as wisdom, most upright, and to glorify Him above claims, as next verse says.

**A Reflection On Lord Of Throne**

Dhī al-ʿarshi — possessor of Throne, Lord of Throne — indicates supreme sovereignty, kingship, as Throne is greatest creation, symbol of dominion, as Qur'an says Lord of Throne, Mighty Throne. If other gods existed, they would seek way to Lord of Throne, to challenge, to overthrow, as worldly kings compete for throne, leading to war, chaos, but heavens and earth ordered, night and day signs, provision abundant or limited with awareness, etc., show one Sovereign, Lord of Throne, no competitor. The believer who reflects will say Glorified Highly Exalted is He above what they claim, will worship none but Him, as Lord decreed, will follow wisdom revealed, most upright, leading to appreciated striving, mighty reward.

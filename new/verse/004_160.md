## Sūrah al-Nisāʾ 4:160

> We forbade the Jews certain foods that had been lawful to them for their wrongdoing, and for hindering many from the Way of Allah,

**Expanded Commentary**

**We Forbade the Jews Certain Foods For Their Wrongdoing**

The verse states *fa bi ẓulmin mina alladhīna hādū ḥarramnā ʿalayhim ṭayyibātin uḥillat lahum* — so for wrongdoing, injustice, from those who became Jews, became *hādū*, We forbade them good things, pure foods, that had been lawful for them. *Bi ẓulmin mina alladhīna hādū* — for wrong, injustice, from those who are Jews — was explained as the same *ẓulm* that struck them with thunderbolt for demand greater than a book (4:153) — *because of their wrongdoing* the Mount was raised (4:154), covenant broken, signs rejected, prophets killed without right, hearts claimed *ghulf* sealed (4:155), Mary slandered with mighty *buhtān* (4:156), boasted killing Messiah not killed (4:157). *Ḥarramnā ʿalayhim ṭayyibātin uḥillat lahum* — We forbade them good things that had been permitted — was explained as foods once *ḥalāl* made *ḥarām* as discipline, as Levitical prohibitions of certain fats and meats that remain in Jewish law — the sūrah elsewhere says *“every food was lawful to Children of Israel except what Israel forbade himself before Torah”* (3:93) — here additional forbidding is for *ẓulm*. *Ṭayyibāt* — good, pure, wholesome — was read as the good that was forbidding is not evil in itself — the foods are *ṭayyib* — pure — but forbidden as recompense, as the sūrah says *“whoever commits evil will be rewarded accordingly”* (4:123) — here reward is forbidding of *ṭayyibāt*. The sūrah has shown that *“We forbade certain foods for wrongdoing”* is counterpart to *“We forgave them after calf yet gave Moses clear proof”* (4:153): forgiveness for calf did not cancel consequence for ongoing wrong.

**And For Hindering Many From the Way of God**

The addition *wa bi ṣaddihim ʿan sabīli Allāhi kathīran* — and for their hindering, blocking, turning away many from the way, the path of God — was explained with *ṣadd* — to hinder, to bar, to turn away — the same *ṣadd* that hypocrites *turn away stubbornly* — *yaṣuddūna ṣudūdā* (4:61) — when told come to revelations and Messenger, and that the sūrah’s noble ways He wishes to make clear (4:26) are blocked where hindering occurs. *ʿAn sabīli Allāhi* — from God’s way, path — was read as the believers’ path (4:115) that was not to be left, the way of Abraham pure (4:125), the straight path He guides to through mercy and grace (4:175). *Kathīran* — many, much — was explained as many hindered: they hinder others by rejecting signs, killing prophets, claiming hearts are *ghulf*, demanding visible God, taking calf, and by legal forbidding that remains; the sūrah later says *“those who disbelieve and hinder from God’s way have certainly gone far astray”* (4:167) — far straying is hindering’s fruit. Early reports linked hindering to rabbis who concealed description of the Prophet ﷺ in Torah to keep followers, and to usury that consumes wealth unjustly (4:161) — hindering by economic oppression as well as doctrinal concealment. The sūrah has shown that *“We have sent revelation to you as We sent to Noah…”* (4:163) — all accepted without distinction (4:152) — hindering is the opposite: to block that acceptance.

**A Verse That Follows Boast Corrected With Law For Hindering**

Placed after the history of the People of the Book demanded a book from heaven, demanded visible God, were struck, took calf yet forgiven, Mount raised, firm covenant taken, then breaking, rejecting, killing, claiming *ghulf* sealed, denying, slandering Mary with mighty *buhtān*, boasting killing Messiah not killed but made to appear, raised, belief before death and witness on the Day (4:153–159), this verse follows boast corrected with law for hindering: for wrongdoing and hindering many from God’s way, We forbade them *ṭayyibāt* that had been lawful. The sūrah has shown that association is far straying (4:116) and defying the Messenger after clarity is left to what he chose (4:115); hindering many from God’s way is that far straying made social — not only straying self but blocking others, as Satan orders cutting and altering (4:119) to alter creation he blocks God’s creation. The forbidding of *ṭayyibāt* was read as the sūrah’s measure: as the emigrant finds many havens and abundance (4:100) — *saʿah* — the hinderer finds *ṭayyibāt* removed — narrowing where he narrowed others’ way. Where *“He wills to lighten burdens because humanity is weak”* (4:28) — lightening is giving *ṭayyibāt* — hindering many removes lightness.

**Good Things Forbidden as Measure, Not as Origin**

The phrase *ṭayyibātin uḥillat lahum* — good things that had been lawful for them — was explained to show that the foods are *ṭayyib* — good, pure — not foul; forbidding is measure, not origin. The sūrah elsewhere says lawful beyond marriage limits is lawful as long as sought with wealth in lawful marriage (4:24); the *ṭayyibāt* here are lawful in principle yet forbidden as recompense for *ẓulm*. Classical notes added that this forbidding is distinct from Israel’s self-forbidding before Torah (3:93) and that the final Book makes lawful *ṭayyibāt* again except what was forbidden as discipline, showing that the Qur’an’s *ḥalāl* restores where the Tawrāh’s discipline had narrowed, as the Prophet ﷺ was sent to remove burdens — *“he releases them from heavy burdens and yokes”* (7:157). The sūrah has said *“God wishes to lighten burdens”* (4:28) — the final lightening is not for those who persist in *ẓulm* and large *ṣadd*, but for those who believe in what was revealed to you and before (4:162).

**A Short Verse That Anchors Discipline in Hindering**

Though only *“We forbade Jews certain good foods that had been lawful for wrongdoing and for hindering many from God’s way,”* the verse anchors discipline in hindering many. Immediately the next verses add two more *ẓulms* — taking interest despite prohibition and consuming people’s wealth unjustly — painful punishment prepared for disbelievers among them, but those well-grounded in knowledge who believe in what was revealed to you and before, especially those who establish prayer and pay alms — to them great reward (4:160–162). The pattern — forbidding *ṭayyibāt* for *ẓulm* and *ṣadd* succeeded — shows that economic hindering — interest, unjust consumption — is the material counterpart of doctrinal hindering — concealing description, demanding visible God. For the weak this is an opening: where the hinderer’s *ṭayyibāt* were removed, the well-grounded who believe in what was revealed to you and before are promised great reward — the *ṭayyibāt* restored in Gardens where promise is true, as the emigrant’s many havens were restored after hindering.

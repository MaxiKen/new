## Sūrah al-Nisāʾ 4:160

> We forbade the Jews certain foods that had been lawful to them for their wrongdoing, and for hindering many from the Way of Allah,

### Expanded Commentary

**Restrictions as consequences of wrongdoing**

The verse says certain foods were forbidden to the Jews because of their wrongdoing and their hindering people from Allah’s way. The Qur’an elsewhere describes some restrictions as punishment for disobedience, not as a statement that the foods were intrinsically evil from creation. Divine law may impose a burden in response to a community’s history while later revelation changes the dispensation.

**Law and moral conduct**

The mention of hindering others from guidance shows that ritual restrictions cannot be separated from public ethics. A community that claims strict observance while blocking truth and exploiting the vulnerable has misunderstood the purpose of law. Conversely, a later easing of a rule is not permission to despise the earlier command; it reflects divine wisdom in different communities.

Muslims should be cautious about using this verse to condemn Jews as a whole. It speaks of wrongdoing among those addressed and is followed by recognition of knowledgeable, faithful people. The proper lesson is self-examination: do our practices draw people toward God or make us obstacles through hypocrisy?

**Punishment and legal history**

The verse encourages readers to distinguish a temporary restriction imposed for wrongdoing from a universal judgment about a people or a food. Legal history contains discipline, mercy, and changed dispensations. Muslims should therefore avoid contempt for earlier communities while taking the warning about obstructing guidance seriously in their own institutions.

## Sūrah al-Baqarah 2:242

> This is how Allah makes His revelations clear to you, so perhaps you will understand.

**Expanded Commentary**

**The Sentence Stated**

The verse states that God makes His signs clear to the community so that they may understand, and the sentence closes the section of the sūrah that has treated the rules about the household. The Book has set out its provisions and now states the purpose of the instruction.

The wording connects the legislation with the understanding of those who receive it, and the community's teachers read the sentence as the statement that the rules have been given in order to be comprehended. Readers are shown that the passage's commands are addressed to a community that is expected to reflect on them, and the sentence supplies the disposition in which the whole section is received.

**The Sign as Instruction**

The commentators explained the phrase rendered as signs as a reference to the rulings that the passage has set out, and the community's scholars noted that the term covers both the verses of the Book and the evidence they contain. The sentence's use of the term places the rules about marriage and divorce among the signs that the Book supplies.

The Book's practice of describing its revelations as signs appears throughout the sūrah, and the community's teachers used the phrase when they spoke about the whole of the guidance. Readers are given the account of a closing that gathers the provisions of the section under a single description, and the sentence supplies the statement about their source.

**The Purpose Named**

The sentence names understanding as the purpose of the clarification, and the community's teachers read the phrase as the statement that the rules are intended to be applied with comprehension rather than with the mere performance of a form. The wording supplies the goal that accompanies the legislation.

The Book's treatment of the subject elsewhere attaches the language of understanding and reflection to its instructions, and the community's scholars gathered the instances. The passage shows that the section's rules about the household are presented as material for study, and the sentence supplies the frame in which the community receives them.

**The Place in the Sūrah**

The sentence stands at the end of the section on the household and before the passage that treats the community's conflicts and its losses, and the community's teachers read the arrangement as a transition. The Book has completed its treatment of the family and now turns to the community's wider affairs.

The wording of the closing connects the section with the verses that precede it, and the community's scholars noted that the same formula appears at the ends of other passages in the sūrah. The account describes a division of the material that places the household within the larger body of the sūrah's guidance, and the sentence supplies the closing statement.

**The Reflection Expected**

The sentence states that the community is expected to understand, and the community's teachers read the clause as the statement about the seriousness with which the legislations are to be studied. The Book's practice of expecting comprehension from its community appears throughout its instructions.

The wording also carries the implication that the obedience of the community is to be accompanied by the knowledge of the reasons that the Book supplies, and the community's scholars used the sentence when they encouraged the study of the law. The passage shows that the legislation of the sūrah is presented together with the reasons and the purposes that accompany it, and the sentence supplies the statement.

**The Passage Ahead**

The verses that follow treat the community that left its homes in fear of death and the accounts of the followers of the earlier prophets, and the reader is carried from the rules of the household to the narratives that follow. The Book's arrangement of its subjects places the legislation beside the history of the communities that preceded it.

The community's teachers read the transition as the statement that the guidance of the sūrah addresses the conduct of the community in the world as well as its life at home. The passage closes with the statement about the signs and the understanding, and the passage continues with the account of those who left their homes.

**The Understanding the Signs Require**

The sentence closes the section on the household with a statement about the purpose of the revelation, and the community's teachers read the formula as the instruction that the rules are given to be understood rather than merely repeated. The Book attaches the language of understanding to its legislation at several points, and the passages together present the law as a subject of study.

The community's scholars built their method upon the sentence when they examined the reasons and purposes of the rulings. The teachers of the early generations discussed the causes of the revelations and the situations the passages addressed, so that the community's practice rests upon comprehension rather than upon the mere imitation of a form.

The placement of the formula at this point in the sūrah is deliberate. The household has been treated at length, and the sentence gathers the material under the description of the signs that are made clear, so that the reader leaves the section with the commands and with the reason for which they were given.

**What the Verse Establishes**

The sentence establishes that the signs are made clear, that the clarification has the understanding of the community as its purpose, and that the legislation of the section belongs to the guidance the Book supplies. The community's teachers used the verse when they spoke about the study of the law, and the reader is shown that the provisions about the household are presented for comprehension as well as for observance.

The verse supplied the community with the warrant for the study of the law, and the teachers used it when they encouraged their students to seek the meaning of what they memorised. The clause also prepares the reader for the accounts that follow, since the narratives that come after the rules are presented as part of the material that the community is to understand.

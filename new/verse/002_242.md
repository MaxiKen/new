## Sūrah al-Baqarah 2:242

> This is how Allah makes His revelations clear to you, so perhaps you will understand.

**Expanded Commentary**

**The Seal on the Code**

One sentence closes the longest continuous legislation in the Qur'an. The family code — marriage-gates, ʿiddahs, ransoms, nursing, custody, widows, provisions — ends not with a new ruling but with a statement about the rulings: *This is how Allah makes His revelations clear to you*. The demonstrative *this* — kadhālika, thus, in this manner — has a specific referent the exegetes mark: the clarity just witnessed, the clarity of everything from 2:221 onward, is itself the thing being pointed at. The verse is the code's seal and its self-description in one: God has made the signs clear, the clearing has taken exactly this form — case by case, clause by clause, right against right — and the form is named so that the reader knows what kind of book he is holding. A legislation that ends by describing its own method is a legislation that intends its method to be recognized, retained, and extended by the minds it has just trained.

**The Refrain Across the Book**

This sentence is one instance of the Qur'an's most characteristic closing-refrain, and the pattern's repetitions define its function. The same formula closes the garden-parable later in this very sūrah — *This is how Allah makes His revelations clear to you, so perhaps you will reflect* (2:266) — and appears in the sūrah of the Table's legislation (5:89), the sūrah of Light's household rules (24:61), and the sūrah of ʿImrān's counsel (3:103). Each instance stands at the end of a unit of instruction, and each does the same double work: it names what has just happened — divine clarification, not human drafting — and it states the clarification's purpose in a single verb of the mind. The recurrence converts the individual verses into a signature: wherever the refrain appears, a legislative or parabolic unit has completed and the Book is marking the completion. The reader who has learned to hear the formula can find the seams of the Qur'an's architecture by ear.

**Understanding and Reflecting**

The refrain's final verb varies, and the variation is precise. Here the purpose-clause is *so perhaps you will understand* — taʿqilūn, from ʿaql, the intellect's binding-and-comprehending. At 2:266 it is *so perhaps you will reflect* — tatafakkarūn; at the wine-and-gambling verse the same reflection-verb closed a pair of legislations (2:219). The exegetes note the fit of each verb to its unit: reflection suits the parable and the weighing of two worlds; understanding — ʿaql, the root that carries both reason and restraint — suits a code of binding rules whose clauses interlock, qualify, and limit one another. The family legislation just completed is precisely a structure to be ʿaqled: held together in the mind, its exceptions grasped as exceptions, its general sentence (2:241) recognized as the summary of its cases. The verse's verb is thus a claim about the code's intelligibility — it can be understood, it was drafted to be understood, and its understanding is the stated purpose of its clarity.

**Clarity as the Method of Mercy**

The verse names God as the clarifier — *Allah makes His revelations clear* — and the naming carries the code's whole self-understanding. Nothing in the preceding legislation was left deliberately obscure: the ambiguous middle-prayer, the withheld night, the unseen hour belong to a different divine pedagogy; here the signs are mubayyanāt, made plain, spelled out in cases and numbers and named parties. The exegetes connect this clarity to the code's burdens — a legislation of counts and waiting-periods and scaled provisions asks real patience, and the clarity is what makes the asking legitimate: the obligated party is never trapped by an unreadable rule. The formula also answers the code's internal complaints: the questions the sūrah has been fielding — they ask you concerning wine, orphans, the new moons — were met not with mysteries but with rulings, and the refrain states the pattern as a policy. God's dealing with this community, in its law, is dealing by clarification; the perhaps of the purpose-clause leaves the understanding free, but leaves it without excuse.

The clarity named here has a social dimension the code has just demonstrated. A law that assigns rights to widows, scales provisions by capacity, forbids secret commitments, and forbids the child's use as a weapon is a law that can be argued about in the open — every party to a domestic dispute can read his own position in it. Obscure legislation serves the powerful, who can afford interpreters; plain legislation arms the weak, who carry the text itself. The widow counting her months, the divorced woman owed her provision, the guardian auditing his own intentions — each has the clarified sign in hand, and the verse's seal tells them the plainness was deliberate: the signs were made clear *to you*, the plural including the code's every beneficiary, not only its jurists.

**The Hinge Between the Two Halves**

The verse's position is structural as well as rhetorical. Behind it, the sūrah's great legislative block — qiblah, fasting, pilgrimage, war, family — has run nearly unbroken from verse 183. Ahead, the sūrah's narrative mode resumes: the stories of the fleeing thousands, of the requested king, of the tested army, which carry the sūrah toward its second half. The one-sentence verse is the hinge between them, and hinges of this kind do interpretive work: the legislation just sealed is the community's law, and the narratives about to begin are the community's memory — law and memory, the two materials of a formed people, joined by a sentence that describes the first as made clear and the purpose of the clarity as understanding. The commentators read the transition as deliberate encouragement: the code trained the mind in God's legislative wisdom; the stories will train it in God's historical wisdom, and the same refrain-family will close them too. The verse asks nothing new of the reader — it only marks the moment where everything asked so far has been made plain, and waits to see if the understanding arrives.

## Sūrah al-Baqarah 2:169

> He only incites you to commit evil and indecency, and to claim against Allah what you do not know.

**Expanded Commentary**

**The Enemy's Full Job Description**

The previous verse declared Satan an enemy; this verse publishes his methods, and the list is exhaustive in its brevity — *he only incites you* — with "only" doing doctrinal work: there is no portion of his program that is benign, no counsel he gives that carries a grain of good. The three items named cover the field of human failure completely. Evil — *sūʾ*, the bad act in general, whatever harms its doer. Indecency — *faḥshāʾ*, the shameful in particular, the sin that exceeds evil's ordinary measure and offends the public sense of decency. And saying about God what one does not know — speech concerning the Divine undertaken without warrant. The sequence ascends in gravity: from act to shameful act to false theology, and the climax reveals the verse's real subject. Satan's campaign against human beings is not primarily a campaign of temptation toward pleasures; it is a campaign of corruption through unauthorized religious speech — and the food prohibitions the tribes invented, which the previous verse answered, are the case in point.

**Faḥshāʾ and Its Counter-Command**

The second item has its own vocabulary history in the Qur'an. That Satan commands indecency is stated again in this sūrah's verse on charity — *"The Devil threatens you with ˹the prospect of˺ poverty and bids you to the shameful deed ˹of stinginess˺"* (2:268), where even miserliness is classified inside faḥshāʾ — and in the sūrah of the Light: *"Whoever follows Satan's footsteps, then ˹let them know that˺ he surely bids ˹all to˺ immorality and wickedness"* (24:21). Against these stands the Qur'an's counter-command, stated with matching insistence: God forbids indecency, wickedness, and aggression (16:90); the prayer itself restrains from indecency and wickedness (29:45). The two lists confront each other as programs: what Satan bids, God and the prayer forbid, and the human being stands between the two commands with no neutral ground. The breadth of faḥshāʾ — covering sexual shame in its most common usage but extending to every act of excessive foulness, including the stinginess that abandons the needy — shows that indecency in the Qur'an's sense is a category of excess: the sin that goes beyond disobedience into ugliness, the act that degrades the actor in other eyes as well as in God's.

**The Worst Command: Speech About God Without Knowledge**

The third item is the verse's summit, and its placement last is an argument. To say about God what one does not know is to legislate in His name without His word — to declare permitted what He forbade, forbidden what He permitted, or to describe Him in terms no revelation supplied. Al-Ṭabarī connected this clause directly to the sacrificial customs of the Arabs that the sūrah of the Table dismantles item by item: the specially released she-camel, the one vow-protected, the one whose newborn was reserved — none of which God ever ordained — *"But the disbelievers just fabricate lies about Allah, and most of them lack understanding"* (5:103). The two verses name the same crime from two sides: the fabricated ruling is the lie about God, and its engine is the saying-without-knowing. The severity of the classification is intelligible: murder destroys a body, indecency degrades a person, but false theology corrupts the religion itself — every generation downstream inherits the invented prohibition as divine, and the lie acquires the authority it counterfeited. This is why the sin the verse ranks highest is committed most confidently: the tribes that forbade their cattle did so in God's name, with reverence, certain they were pious.

**The Three Zones of the Human Person**

Read structurally, the three incitements map onto the three zones in which a human being can fail. Evil operates in the zone of appetite — the act the desire pulls toward. Indecency operates in the zone of social shame — the act that exceeds appetite's privacy and wounds the community's moral sense. Saying about God without knowledge operates in the zone of creed — the word that misrepresents heaven itself. The mapping shows the campaign's completeness: Satan does not need a person to fail in all three zones, because a failure anchored in any one gives him a foothold in the others. The appetite-corrupted person eventually justifies himself; the shame-corrupted person eventually redefines decency; and the creed-corrupted person — the verse's climactic case — eventually commands the other two zones in God's name. The footsteps imagery of the previous verse is thus completed: the tracks lead through all three zones, and the walker who follows long enough will be led to speak about God what he does not know, which is where the path terminates and where the enemy's real work — not the temptation of individuals but the falsification of religion — is done.

**Knowledge as the Only Defense**

The verse names its own antidote in the wording of the third item. The sin is not saying about God what is false — a person can utter a false statement about God in honest ignorance and stand under the general hope of forgiveness; the sin is saying *what you do not know*, the speech that proceeds without knowledge whether or not it happens to land near the truth. The criterion is thus epistemic: religious claims require a warrant, and the only warrant the Qur'an recognizes is what God has actually sent down. The believer's defense against the footsteps is not stronger willpower but better information — knowing what revelation permits before adding prohibitions, knowing what it describes before describing God further. This is why the food verses that follow this one are structured as they are: a universal permission to eat of the lawful and good (2:168), the exposure of Satan's program (2:169), the ancestral refusal of revelation named as the obstacle (2:170), and then the revealed list itself — short, explicit, four items — closing the space where unauthorized speech operates. The verse condemns the method; the coming legislation removes the occasion, because where God has spoken plainly, saying about Him what one does not know has nowhere left to stand.

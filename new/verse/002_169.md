## Sūrah al-Baqarah 2:169

> He only incites you to commit evil and indecency, and to claim against Allah what you do not know.

**Expanded Commentary**

**What the Enemy Commands**

The verse states what Satan commands, and the sentence follows the description of him as a manifest enemy in the previous verse. The Book names three things: evil, indecency, and the attribution to God of what a person does not know.

The structure of the sentence is a contrast with the command that precedes it. The reader has just been told to eat what God has made lawful; he is now told what the enemy of humanity instructs, and the two instructions are presented as the alternatives between which a person chooses.

**Evil and Indecency**

The first two terms describe wrongdoing and shameful conduct, and the Book uses the second frequently for acts that the community would be ashamed to acknowledge. The pairing covers both the harm that a person does and the conduct that degrades him, and the community's teachers read the two as a description of the field in which temptation operates.

The Book states elsewhere that God and the prayer forbid indecency, and the sūrah's later passages repeat the term among the things that the Book commands. The verse's placement of the two terms at the head of the list shows the reader what the command of the enemy leads to, and the community's scholars noted that the wording describes an instruction rather than a suggestion.

The Book uses the first term for the harm that a person commits and the second for acts that shame the one who performs them, and it treats both as things that the community is to avoid without qualification. The community’s teachers noted that the second word appears in the sūrah’s later commands and among the things the Book forbids to the believers, and they read the two terms here as a summary of the field in which a person is tempted. The sentence is therefore general in its scope, and the specific practices that follow in the passage are understood as instances of what the verse has named.

**Speaking About God Without Knowledge**

The third item is the most serious in the sentence, and it concerns statements about God that a person makes without the knowledge to support them. The Book returns to this offence in several places, and the sūrah's following verses describe people who follow the practice of their ancestors and refuse the revelation that has come to them.

The commentators connected this part of the sentence with the prohibitions that communities invented for themselves, since a person who declares something forbidden or permitted on his own authority is claiming a knowledge that belongs to the lawgiver. What the verse establishes is that the offence is not only the act that follows from a false ruling but the assumption of the authority to make the ruling.

**The Occasion in the Following Verses**

The sūrah's next verses deal with people who answer the invitation to follow what God has revealed by saying that they follow what they found their ancestors practising. The commentators connected the passage with the subject of food and with the practices of the tribes that had forbidden certain things, and the present verse's third item names the offence that such practice involves.

The connection explains the placement of the sentence. The Book has just forbidden the following of Satan's footsteps and has described him as an enemy; it now names the content of what he commands. The reader who continues into the next verses sees the practice described in concrete form, and the passage reads as a single argument about the source of a community's standards.

**Al-Ṭabarī's Reading**

The commentators connected the phrase about indecency with the passages in which the Book names the animals that the idolaters had declared forbidden, and they cited the verse in which God states that He has not appointed such categories and that those who disbelieve invent lies about Him.

The reading shows how the community's teachers applied the verse to the practices of their own time. What they drew from it is that a prohibition invented by a community is not a lesser matter than an act of wrongdoing, since it places a human decision in the position that belongs to the law of God, and the sentence treats it accordingly.

**The Command and the Counter-Command**

The passage sets two commands side by side, and the reader is given the authority behind each. One command comes with the provision of what is lawful and good, and the other comes from a being the Book has just identified as an enemy whose enmity is open.

The contrast is the sūrah's method of presenting the choice, and it does not obscure the attractiveness of the second command. What the verse shows is that the guidance of the Book comes with a provision attached, while the instruction of the enemy produces harm and the presumption of knowledge, and the reader is left to compare the two.

The Book’s presentation of the two commands gives the reader the means to judge what is asked of him, since one comes from the Lord of the creation and the other from a being whose enmity has been announced. The community’s teachers drew from the pairing a lesson about the way harmful practices present themselves, since what the verse describes is not offered as evil but as custom. The reader is shown that the source of a practice is what determines its worth, and the passage leaves him with the comparison and with the choice it implies.

**What the Verse Establishes**

The sentence establishes the content of the enemy's instruction and the character of the harm it does, and it places the assumption of knowledge about God at the head of the offences it names. The reader is given a standard for judging the practices that a community inherits, and the passage continues with the account of those who appeal to their ancestors. The two verses together address the same practice, and the sūrah uses the pair to show how a community comes to follow a standard that no one has authorised.

The sentence establishes the content of the enmity that the Book has described and names the three things the enemy of humanity wants his hearers to do. The community’s teachers read the list as a picture of how a person is drawn away from obedience, beginning with conduct and ending with statements about God that have no basis. What the reader receives is a description that applies to the practices the following verses describe, and the passage continues with the words of those who answer the invitation to follow the revelation with an appeal to their ancestors.

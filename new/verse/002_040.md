## Sūrah al-Baqarah 2:40

> O children of Israel! Remember My favours upon you. Fulfil your covenant and I will fulfil Mine, and stand in awe of Me ˹alone˺.

**Expanded Commentary**

**A People Addressed by Name, a History Invoked**

“O Children of Israel! Remember My favours upon you. Fulfil your covenant and I will fulfil Mine, and stand in awe of Me alone” (2:40). The sūrah, after the Adam narrative and the paired promise and warning (2:38–39), turns to a living audience in Madinah — the Children of Israel — by name and history. The vocative is not accusation first, but remembrance. Before command comes memory: remember niʿmah — favour — upon you.

Isrāʾīl is Yaʿqūb, and “children” gathers twelve tribes and centuries. To be addressed as children of a prophet is to be reminded both of lineage and of responsibility borne under that lineage. The reminder is affectionate and demanding at once: the past bestowal obliges present fidelity.

**“My Favours Upon You” — Which Favours?**

The source lists three overlapping referents without forcing exclusivity:

- Deliverance from Pharaoh and the sea (2:49–50) and the subsequent gifts of shade, manna, quails, and land — the immediate Exodus favours still in communal memory.
- Placement of prophets among them and sending of books — Torah and prophetic succession — an honor of guidance no other people held at that scale.
- The coming of Islam itself, since the address falls among Jews around Madinah: the Qur’an arriving as confirmation is itself a favour to them if received.

The word niʿmah is also read in the broadest sense — creation, provision, faith itself — as in 2:47 and 2:122 where the favour is recalled again. The recency of a favour deepens obligation, but the oldest favours — life, hearing, guidance — already suffice.

**“Fulfil Your Covenant and I Will Fulfil Mine” — Reciprocity Without Symmetry**

“Fulfil your covenant and I will fulfil Mine” — awfū bi-ʿahdī ūfi bi-ʿahdikum (2:40). The covenant (ʿahd) is glossed as gratitude and fidelity on the people’s side and forgiveness and reward on God’s side; or as the specific mīthāq of 5:12 — twelve leaders raised, covenant taken —; or as faithfulness to what they find “inscribed with them in Torah and Gospel” (7:157) predicting the coming Prophet, whom al-Qurṭubī sees here. All share one structure: God’s fulfilment follows theirs. The divine “I will fulfil” does not make God needy; it makes promise reliable. Human fulfilling is loyalty; divine fulfilling is largesse.

The covenant here also echoes the earlier breach described: “those who violate Allah’s covenant after it has been affirmed” (2:27). That description fits any breaker, but this verse names a people who affirmed with particular clarity. The warning there becomes invitation here: what was broken can be kept.

**“And Stand in Awe of Me Alone” — The Fear That Frees**

“and stand in awe of Me alone” — wa iyyāya farhabūn (2:40). Rahbah is awe that trembles and trusts. The exclusive “Me alone” is added in translation to capture the emphatic iyyāya: fear Me, not Pharaoh, not patrons who buy signs (2:41), not crowds whose rejection might shame. Awe of God loosens awe of men, which is why the same root will return as reverence in the next verse: “reverence Me” (2:41) and “be mindful of Me” (2:41). To fear rightly is to be freed rightly.

**Why Israel Is Addressed Next**

The sūrah’s logic turns from universal history (Adam) to a particular history that embodies the universal rule about signs: a people given signs abundantly, whose response will model for all what fidelity and evasion look like. The particularity is not tribalism; it is case study. What is said to them will soon be said to all through the same duties: believe the confirming revelation, do not sell signs, do not confound truth, establish prayer, give alms, bow with those who bow (2:41–43). A community that lived longest with revelation is the most apt mirror.

**Remembering as Practice, Not Sentiment**

Udhkurū — remember! — in Qur’anic idiom is not nostalgia. It is to keep present. The Children of Israel are told to remember favour not to bask but to fulfil. Memory here is covenantal: to recall deliverance is to consent to be delivered still — from self-worship, price-selling, and truth-concealing.

**Living the Verse Beyond Its First Address**

Even for those not of Israel by lineage, the verse trains a three-step return:

- Remember favour concretely: name a deliverance you have known.
- Fulfil the human side of covenant in the smallest next obedience that is due.
- Transfer awe from the many feared things to the One alone worthy of awe.

The promise stands: human fulfilling will be met, without delay in divine meaning, by divine fulfilling.

**What Not to Reduce**

This verse does not name every term of the covenant as a list; commentators differ because the Qur’an names it broadly to gather all forms of faithfulness under one call. To reduce ʿahd to one clause — only ritual or only belief in prediction — narrows what the verse intentionally leaves wide: fulfil the covenant, all of it as you know it, and the divine part will be fulfilled beyond measure.

## Sūrah al-Qiyāmah 75:31

> This denier neither believed nor prayed,

**Expanded Commentary**

**From the Deathbed to a Case Study**

The sūrah has followed one death from the collarbones to the driving, and now it names the kind of person whose death that was. The shift to the third person is significant: the previous scene was a particular deathbed, and this verse generalises it into a type. Anyone who matches the description should recognise himself in it. The Qur'an uses this device repeatedly — a concrete scene, then the category it instantiates — because a particular death can be contemplated with detachment, whereas a description one might fit cannot. The sūrah is moving from reporting to diagnosis.

**Two Failures, Not One**

The charge has two halves, and both are needed. *He did not affirm the truth*, and *he did not pray*. The first is the inward act — accepting the message as true, trusting the Messenger, assenting to what the revelation said about the Day. The second is the outward act — the worship that belongs to the One whose truth has been accepted. The verse thereby excludes half-measures in both directions. A person who prays while denying the Day has not done the first; a person who concedes the Day in words and never turns to his Lord has not done the second. The sūrah has already insisted that unbelief is a matter of what a person wants, and here it adds that what a person wants shows up in what he does with his body at the appointed times.

**A Second Reading of the First Verb**

Some commentators took the first verb in its other sense and read the verse as *he did not give in charity*, since the root that means to affirm the truth also means to give alms — the connection being that almsgiving is the verification of faith. The grounds for this reading are strong. The Qur'an uses the root in both senses, and the parallel passage in Sūrah al-Muddaththir names exactly these two failures as the self-description of the people in the Fire: *“They will reply, ‘We were not of those who prayed, nor did we feed the poor.’”* (74:43–44). On that reading the verse pairs worship of God with care for His creatures, which is the pairing the Qur'an uses constantly. The reading is coherent and well supported; the majority held the first sense on the ground that the sūrah's context is denial rather than stinginess, and that is the stronger reading here, though the two converge in practice, since a person who affirms the truth gives. The convergence is visible in the Qur'an's own definitions. Righteousness is described as believing, *“and give charity out of ˹their˺ cherished wealth to relatives, orphans, the poor, ˹needy˺ travellers, beggars, and for freeing captives; who establish prayer, pay alms-tax, and keep the pledges they make”* (2:177). Belief, worship, and giving appear in one sentence because the Qur'an does not treat them as separate departments.

**The Confession of the Inhabitants of the Fire**

The parallel in al-Muddaththir is worth following, because it supplies the full catalogue that this verse abbreviates. The inhabitants of the Fire are asked what put them there, and they answer with four items: *“We were not of those who prayed, nor did we feed the poor. We used to indulge ˹in falsehood˺ along with others, and deny the Day of Judgment, until the inevitable came to us.”* (74:43–47). Neglect of worship, neglect of the poor, absorption in the talk of the crowd, and denial of the Day — and then the arrival of the certainty that ends the argument. The sūrah's two failures are the first two items in that list, and the order is the same in both passages.

**Why Denial Takes This Shape**

It is worth asking why disbelief should be described through worship and charity rather than through doctrine. The answer is that the Qur'an does not treat the Day of Judgment as a hypothesis to be evaluated. It treats it as a claim that, if true, reorganises a life: a person who believes he will be asked about what he owns gives, and a person who believes he will stand before his Lord prays. Where those two are absent, the belief is not present either, whatever the mouth says. The sūrah has already located unbelief in the will; here it locates the evidence of that unbelief in the two acts that a living faith produces as a matter of course. The opposite profile is drawn just as concretely, and it is drawn in terms of what a person is not distracted from: *“by men who are not distracted—either by buying or selling—from Allah’s remembrance, or performing prayer, or paying alms-tax.”* (24:37). The difference between the two types is not intelligence, temperament, or opportunity. It is what each has allowed to occupy him, and the occupation is visible long before the deathbed.

**A Profile to Be Checked, Not a Curse**

The verse is addressed to the living, and its purpose is diagnostic rather than merely condemnatory. A person can examine the two items honestly and find out where he stands, which is the use the Qur'an intends for every description of the doomed. Nothing in the wording suggests that these failures are beyond repair before death; the sūrah has said the opposite, holding out forgiveness while breath remains (39:53). What the verse rules out is the comfort of thinking that a person can neglect both worship and his obligations to others and still be counted among those who affirmed the truth. The next verse will add the disposition behind the neglect. What this verse establishes on its own is that the reckoning turns on two ordinary things rather than on anything exotic: a person's assent to the truth and his attendance on his Lord. No one will be asked at the driving whether he had mastered an argument. The question will be about what he did with the two items named here, and the Qur'an has stated the question in advance so that no one arrives surprised by it.

## Sūrah al-Nisāʾ 4:151

> they are indeed the true disbelievers. And We have prepared for the disbelievers a humiliating punishment.

**Expanded Commentary**

**They Are Indeed the True Disbelievers**

The verse completes *ulāʾika humu al-kāfirūna ḥaqqan* — they are — those who deny God and messengers and wish to distinguish between Him and His messengers saying *we believe in some and disbelieve in some desiring a way between* (4:150) — indeed, truly, the disbelievers in truth, the true disbelievers. *Ḥaqqan* — truly, in truth, as right, as due — was explained as the Book’s verdict after judgment by what He shows (4:105) that distinguishing God from messengers is not compromise but *kufr* in full, as *“whoever denies God, angels, Books, messengers, Last Day has gone far astray”* (4:136). *Humu al-kāfirūna* — they are the disbelievers — was read with emphasis: not merely among disbelievers, but they are them, for the sūrah has shown that taking disbelievers as allies instead of believers gives Him clear warrant against yourselves (4:144) and sitting where revelations are denied makes you like them (4:140); to make distinction between God and messengers is that like-ness made creed, so the verdict is *ḥaqq* — true — as God’s promise is *ḥaqq* (4:122) and Book was sent *bi al-ḥaqq* with truth (4:105). The sūrah has said *“they do not believe except few”* where hearts were sealed for disbelief (4:155); here *ḥaqqan* marks those few who believe as distinct from those truly disbelievers. Early reports added that hypocrisy’s *mudhabdhab* between neither these nor those (4:143) is not neutral: to desire a way between distinguishing is *ḥaqqan* disbelief.

**And We Have Prepared for Disbelievers a Humiliating Punishment**

The preparation *wa aʿtadnā li al-kāfirīna ʿadhāban muhīnā* — and We have prepared, made ready, for disbelievers a humiliating, degrading, debasing punishment — was explained with *aʿtadnā* — prepared, as the humiliating punishment was prepared for disbelievers where prayer was guarded under arms (4:102: *We have prepared humiliating punishment for disbelievers*), and for those given a portion who hide bounty (4:37: *humiliating punishment for disbelievers*), and will be for those who oppose the Messenger (4:115: *humiliating punishment* implicit). *ʿAdhāban muhīnā* — humiliating punishment — was read as humiliation corresponding to the *ʿizzah* — honour — they sought among disbelievers as allies (4:139) where *all honour belongs to Him entirely* — the honour sought among the wrong company becomes humiliation prepared. The sūrah has shown that the intentions to deceive God while He outwits (4:142) and to deceive the Prophet while grace protects (4:113) end in humiliation where deception’s honour is inverted. Where the sūrah says *“do not take disbelievers as allies instead of believers, would you give God clear proof against yourselves?”* (4:144) — *sulṭān mubīn* — preparation of humiliating punishment is the proof made sanction, because proof against yourselves was made clear by distinguishing.

**A Verse That Completes Distinction With Verdict**

Placed after *those who deny God and messengers wishing to distinguish saying we believe in some and disbelieve in others desiring a way between* (4:150), this verse completes distinction with verdict: they are indeed true disbelievers, We have prepared for disbelievers humiliating punishment. The sūrah has shown that belief was commanded as comprehensive — *have faith in God, His Messenger, Book He revealed to His Messenger and Scriptures before* (4:136) — steadfast, not selective, as the Way of Abraham pure (4:125) was not selective among associates; to be selective among messengers is to undo that comprehensive faith. The sūrah has shown that those who believe and do good are in Gardens true where promise is *ḥaqq* (4:122), while those who disbelieve are in Hell without escape (4:121); humiliating punishment is the disbeliever’s analog to honour sought. The phrase *aʿtadnā* — prepared — was linked to *“We have prepared humiliating punishment for disbelievers”* where guarded prayer stands (4:102): as prayer under arms guards against the enemy who wishes you neglect for a sweep (4:102), humiliating punishment guards against the theology that wishes you accept half the messengers and reject half as compromise. Elsewhere the Book ties that guard to *“Gardens under rivers where they abide forever”* (4:122) — comprehensive faith is the Gardens’ price, distinction its loss, and the comparison steadies the choice.

**Ḥaqqan Disbelief After Ḥaqqan Promise**

The word *ḥaqqan* — truly — for disbelievers was contrasted with *ḥaqqan* for promise: *waʿda Allāhi ḥaqqan* — promise ever true (4:122). The sūrah has used *ḥaqq* for Book — *bi al-ḥaqq* — and for promise and for verdict; truth is one. To call them *kāfirūna ḥaqqan* is to say the judgment that they are disbelievers is not by wish — *neither by your wishes nor those of People of the Book* (4:123) — but by truth, as the sūrah says *whoever commits evil will be rewarded accordingly* (4:123) — distinction is evil rewarded accordingly where honour was sought. Where the sūrah says *“whose word more truthful than God’s?”* (4:122) — none — *ḥaqqan* here is His word: they are true disbelievers. For the weak this is clarifying: where settlement between spouses is best (4:128) and settlement is desired, compromise between God and messengers is never best; it is *ḥaqqan* disbelief whose preparation is humiliating.

**A Short Verse That Anchors Creed Before Its Opposite**

Though only *“they are indeed true disbelievers, We have prepared for disbelievers humiliating punishment,”* the verse anchors creed before its opposite — the subsequent verse will describe believers who accept all messengers receiving rewards (4:152). The two verses together frame the choice after distinction was described: desire a way between distinguishing and be counted true disbeliever with humiliating preparation, or accept all without rejecting any and be given rewards with forgiveness and mercy. The passage after that will turn to the People of the Book demanding a book from heaven (4:153).

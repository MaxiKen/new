## Sūrah al-Baqarah 2:185

> Ramaḍân is the month in which the Quran was revealed as a guide for humanity with clear proofs of guidance and the standard ˹to distinguish between right and wrong˺. So whoever is present this month, let them fast. But whoever is ill or on a journey, then ˹let them fast˺ an equal number of days ˹after Ramaḍân˺. Allah intends ease for you, not hardship, so that you may complete the prescribed period and proclaim the greatness of Allah for guiding you, and perhaps you will be grateful.

**Expanded Commentary**

**The Month Named**

The verse names the month of Ramadan as the one in which the Quran was revealed, and it states that the fast is to be observed by those who are present in it. The sentence supplies the reason why the obligation is attached to this month rather than to another.

The connection between the fast and the revelation is the subject of the sentence, and the community's teachers read the passage as a statement about honour rather than about cause. The month is distinguished because of what God sent down in it, and the fast is the community's response to the distinction.

**The Quran as Guidance**

The sentence describes the revelation as guidance for humanity, as clear proofs of guidance, and as the criterion, and the three descriptions appear together in the sūrah's treatment of the Book. The community's scholars explained the first as the general direction the revelation supplies, the second as the evidence that accompanies it, and the third as the standard by which right and wrong are distinguished.

The Book's language in this sentence connects the month of the fast with the purpose of the community's existence, and the reader is shown why the fast and the revelation belong together. What the reader receives is a statement that the guidance is available, and the community's practice of reciting the Book in the month preserves the connection the verse makes.

**The Obligation and Its Repetition**

The sentence repeats the provision for those who are ill or travelling, and it states that they should fast an equal number of days after the month. The repetition of the concession shows its importance, and the community's scholars noted that the rules of the fast are stated twice within a few verses.

The Book's method of restating a provision in a new context gives the reader the assurance that the regulation is settled. What the sentence establishes is that the obligation and the concession belong to the same law, and the community's jurists derived the rules of making up missed days from the two statements together.

**Ease Rather Than Hardship**

The verse states that God intends ease for the community and not hardship, and the community's scholars used the clause as a principle in the interpretation of the rules of the fast. They applied it to the concessions and to the cases in which the practice creates a difficulty that the law does not require.

The Book's use of the same idea in other passages about the law shows that the principle is general. What the reader receives is a statement about the purpose of the legislation that governs the month, and the community's teachers read the sentence as a guide for the cases the texts do not name explicitly.

**Completing the Number and Magnifying God**

The sentence names two outcomes of the practice: that the community may complete the prescribed period and that it may proclaim the greatness of God for having guided it. The community's scholars connected the second with the takbīr that is recited at the end of the month and on the way to the festival prayer.

The Book's account of the practice therefore extends beyond the days of the fast to the completion and the expressions that accompany it. What the sentence establishes is that the practice has a conclusion, and the reader is shown that the community's celebration belongs to the observance of the month rather than being an addition to it.

**The Reports About the Month**

The reports describe the month as a time of particular blessing, and they preserve the Prophet's statement that when it comes the gates of the Garden are opened and the gates of the Fire are closed and the satans are fettered. The community's teachers gathered the reports on the month with the verse's account of the revelation, and they treated the month as the season of the Book.

The tradition also describes the community's practice of reciting the whole of the Book during the month and of giving charity in it, and the two practices are connected with the verse's pairing of guidance and fasting. What the reader is given is a month whose observance is defined by the revelation that was sent down in it, and the passage continues with the instruction to call upon God.

**What the Verse Establishes**

The sentence establishes the month, its distinction, the obligation to fast in it, the concession for those who cannot, the intention of the lawgiver, and the completion for which the community works. The reader is given the full account of the practice in a single verse, and the passage that follows turns to the nearness of God and to the response He gives to those who call on Him.

## Sūrah al-Nisāʾ 4:114

> There is no good in most of their secret talks—except those encouraging charity, kindness, or reconciliation between people. And whoever does this seeking Allah’s pleasure, We will grant them a great reward.

**Expanded Commentary**

**No Good in Most Secret Talks**

The verse states *lā khayra fī kathīrin min najwāhum* — there is no good in much, in most, of their secret talks, private counsels. *Najwā* — secret talk, whispering, private counsel — was explained as the night plotting just exposed: *yubayyitūna mā lā yarḍā mina al-qawli* — harbouring by night what displeases Him (4:108) — and advocating for self-wrongers (4:107) and throwing sin on the innocent (4:112). *Lā khayra* — no good — was read as no benefit, no virtue, no reward, as the sūrah has said of those who hide from people while God is with them where they plot (4:108) and of advocates who dispute in this life but will find no disputant on the Day (4:109). *Kathīrin* — much, many — was stressed: most secret talks are not good, though some are, so that secrecy itself is not condemned, only its content. The sūrah has shown that reports are to be referred to the Messenger and those in authority for validation (4:83) and that verification before fighting is required (4:94); secret talk that bypasses referral and verification is the opposite. Classical notes added that the *hum* — their — refers to the deceitful group and by extension any private counsel not turned to good; the generality widens the rule beyond the theft case to every confidentiality that hides deceit.

**Except Three — Charity, Kindness, Reconciliation**

The exception *illā man amara bi ṣadaqatin aw maʿrūfin aw iṣlāḥin bayna al-nāsi* — except those, except him who commands, encourages, charity, what is known as good, or reconciliation between people — was explained as the three goods that make secrecy good. *Ṣadaqah* — charity, alms — was read as giving from what God provided, the opposite of devouring orphans’ wealth and of withholding bounty (4:37–40), and as the blood-money waived charitably (4:92) and dowry graciously adjusted (4:4). *Maʿrūf* — kindness, what is known, recognised as good — was explained as every act the sound heart knows as decent: fair speech, trust returned (4:58), verification, being sure before fighting, judging by what God shows. *Iṣlāḥ bayna al-nāsi* — reconciliation, setting right between people — was read as the sūrah’s own procedure where a split is feared — appoint a mediator from his family and hers (4:35), and as settlement where a wife fears indifference (4:128). To *amara* — to command, to encourage, to enjoin — was stressed: the good is not merely doing charity or kindness privately, but commanding it, making the secret talk itself a counsel toward it. Where night plotting commanded throwing sin on the innocent, this exception commands the inverse.

**Whoever Does This Seeking God’s Pleasure — Great Reward**

The promise *wa man yafʿal dhālika ibtighāʾa marḍāti Allāhi fa sawfa nuʾtīhi ajran ʿaẓīmā* — and whoever does this seeking God’s pleasure, favour, approval, We will grant them a great, mighty reward — was explained with two conditions: doing the commanded good — *yafʿal* — and seeking thereby God’s pleasure — *ibtighāʾa marḍāti Allāhi* — not reputation. *Marḍāt* — pleasure, approval — was read as the same seeking that distinguishes true striving with wealth and lives (4:95) from seeking fleeting gain by denying peace (4:94) and from spending to show off without belief (4:38). *Fa sawfa nuʾtīhi ajran ʿaẓīmā* — We will grant great reward — was explained as the same *ajr ʿaẓīm* promised to the striver above the sitter (4:95–96) and to the one who holds fast after repentance (4:146); the reward for commanding charity, kindness, reconciliation in secret is therefore ranked with striving. The *sawfa* — will, soon — was read as certainty, as the emigrant’s reward already secured where death overtakes (4:100). Early reports illustrated *iṣlāḥ* with a man who reconciles two disputants by private counsel where public speech would shame, achieving without humiliating preparation for disbelievers (4:102) a peace that is itself worship.

**A Verse That Generalises the Theft Case Into an Ethic of Speech**

Placed after the Prophet’s preservation by grace and teaching (4:113), this verse generalises the theft case into an ethic of speech. The Book and wisdom taught him what he did not know — the hidden moving of armour, the night counsel — and by that teaching the community learns that most private counsels are not good unless they encourage the three. The sūrah has shown that *“God wishes to lighten burdens”* (4:28), that purification is eased with earth (4:43), that prayer is shortened on travel (4:101), and that precaution remains even when arms are laid aside (4:102); here speech is eased: private talk is not burdened with prohibition, only guided — make it about charity, kindness, reconciliation, and it is great reward. The sūrah has also warned that to follow desires is to deviate greatly (4:27) and that Satan commands altering creation and deluding with hopes (4:119–20); secret talk that deludes is Satan’s *najwā* (58:10), while secret talk that reconciles is God’s. For the weak the sūrah lightens, this is a practical lightening: you need not be present at every dispute, but if you whisper, whisper toward giving, kindness, mending.

**The Great Reward That Makes Secrecy Worship**

The closing *ajran ʿaẓīmā* — great reward — was explained as making secrecy itself worship where it would otherwise be suspicion. The sūrah that opened from a single soul, that commanded mindfulness of ties and family (4:1), that multiplied a good deed many times over (4:40), here multiplies a private word that mends people into a mighty wage. The contrast with the double burden — *buhtān and manifest sin* — for throwing on the innocent (4:112) is exact: there private talk earned two burdens, here it earns great reward, according as intention was *marḍāt Allāh* or concealment from people. The next verse will set the boundary of that intention: whoever opposes the Messenger after guidance is clear and follows a path other than the believers will be left to what he chose and burned — what an evil end (4:115), for he chose the *najwā* that had no good.

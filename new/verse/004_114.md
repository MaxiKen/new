## Sūrah al-Nisāʾ 4:114

> There is no good in most of their secret talks—except those encouraging charity, kindness, or reconciliation between people. And whoever does this seeking Allah’s pleasure, We will grant them a great reward.

### Expanded Commentary

**Not every private conversation is harmless**

Most secret talks contain no good, the verse says, except those that encourage charity, recognized kindness, or reconciliation. Secrecy can protect a vulnerable person and allow mediation without humiliation, but it can also shelter plotting, gossip, and manipulation. The moral value lies in what the conversation serves.

**Three constructive exceptions**

Charity includes urging material support and sharing resources. *Maʿrūf* includes every sound act recognized by revelation and decent custom. Reconciliation involves restoring relationships without concealing serious danger or forcing a victim back into harm. These are examples of speech that may need privacy precisely because public exposure would make them harder.

Whoever does this seeking Allah’s pleasure receives a great reward. The intention directs the conversation away from reputation. A mediator should not create drama so that others praise his peacemaking; he should quietly make justice and repair possible.

**Private speech can heal**

A quiet conversation that arranges food for a neighbour, reconciles spouses, or encourages a debt relief may be more valuable than public moral commentary. The verse gives such speech a high place because it serves people rather than the speaker’s reputation. Its reward belongs to the one who seeks Allah’s pleasure, not the one who seeks to be seen as kind.

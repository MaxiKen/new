## Sūrah al-Isrāʾ 17:29

> **Do not be so tight-fisted, for you will be blameworthy; nor so openhanded, for you will end up in poverty.**

**Expanded Commentary**

**Do Not Be Tight-Fisted**

Wa-lā tajʿal yadaka maghlūlatan ilā ʿunuqika — and do not make your hand chained to your neck. Wa-lā, and not, prohibition. Tajʿal from j-ʿ-l, to make, set up, as verse 22 lā tajʿal maʿa Allāhi ilāhan, do not make with Allah other god, and verse 12 jaʿalnā night and day signs. Yadaka, your hand, from y-d, hand, limb. Maghlūlatan from gh-l-l, chained, bound, shackled, fettered, from ghalla, to chain, bind, fetter, from ghull, chain, fetter, shackle, collar. Ilā ʿunuqika, to your neck, to your own neck, from ʿ-n-q, neck, as verse 13 bound destiny to neck, ṭā'ir bound to neck, and verse 29 tight-fisted metaphor chained hand to neck. Al-Ṭabarī explains that do not make hand chained to neck means do not be tight-fisted, stingy, miserly, withholding, as if hand bound, chained to neck cannot give, cannot extend to give due to relatives, poor, traveller, metaphor for stinginess, tight-fistedness, withholding. Al-Qurṭubī says this is metaphor for withholding, not giving due, being stingy, miserly, tight-fisted, which is blameworthy.

**Blameworthy If Tight-Fisted**

Fa-taqʿuda malūman — then you will sit blameworthy, end up blameworthy. Fa-, then, so, consequence. Taqʿuda from q-ʿ-d, to sit, end up, remain, become, to sit, as sitting indicates ending, remaining in state, as verse 22 fa-taqʿuda madhmūman makhdhūlan, you will sit condemned abandoned, and verse 29 similar. Malūman from l-w-m, blameworthy, blamed, from lāma, to blame, from malām, blame, and malūm, blamed, blameworthy, one blamed. Al-Ṭabarī says if you are tight-fisted, withholding when you should give due to relatives, poor, traveller, you will end up blameworthy, blamed by people and by Allah, because you withheld due, right, and people blame miser. Al-Rāzī says malūman indicates blame for stinginess, miserliness, as people blame tight-fisted who does not give due, and Allah blames, as withholding due is disobedience, ungrateful.

**Nor Openhanded To Poverty**

Wa-lā tabsuṭhā kulla al-basṭi fa-taqʿuda malūman maḥsūran — and do not extend it all extension, then you will sit blameworthy, in poverty, regretful, depleted. Wa-lā, and not, prohibition second extreme. Tabsuṭhā from b-s-ṭ, to extend, open, spread, from basaṭa, to extend, open, spread, as hand extended for giving, as yabsuṭu al-rizqa, extends provision. Kulla al-basṭi, all extension, complete openness, total extension, from k-l-l and b-s-ṭ, all, every, extension, openness, meaning fully openhanded, extravagant, spending all, wasteful. Fa-taqʿuda, then you will sit, end up, remain. Malūman maḥsūran, blameworthy, depleted, in poverty, regretful, from l-w-m and ḥ-s-r, blameworthy and maḥsūr, depleted, in poverty, regretful, unable, from ḥasara, to be depleted, regretful, to be in poverty, from ḥasrah, regret, poverty, from maḥsūr, depleted, regretful, in poverty, unable to give. Al-Ṭabarī says do not be so openhanded, extend hand fully, spending all wastefully, extravagantly, beyond due, beyond moderation, so that you end up in poverty, depleted, regretful, unable to give, blameworthy for waste. Al-Qurṭubī says this is prohibition of being openhanded to point of poverty, spending all, wasteful, tabdhīr, which makes one brother to devils, and leads to maḥsūr, depleted, regretful, in poverty.

**Why Moderation Commanded Between Extremes**

Why moderation commanded? Because both extremes blameworthy and harmful: tight-fisted leads to blameworthy, malūman, as withholding due, right, miserly, and openhanded to poverty leads to blameworthy and maḥsūr, depleted, regretful, in poverty, unable, wasteful, brother to devils. The most upright is moderation, middle path, wasat, between tight-fisted and openhanded, giving due to relatives, poor, traveller, not spending wastefully, not withholding, not spending all to poverty, but balanced. This is part of wisdom, most upright guidance, that Qur'an guides to, as verse 9 says guides to most upright. Previous chapter said wealth and children adornment, but Hereafter better, and provision in due measure, not all extended, so moderation in spending is wisdom, most upright, balanced.

**Connection To Giving Due And Wasteful Brothers To Devils And Kind Word**

Verse 26 said give to close relatives their due, poor, traveller, and do not spend wastefully, tabdhīr. Verse 27 wasteful brothers to devils, Devil ever ungrateful. Verse 28 if you must turn down due to lack while hoping for bounty, give kind word, gentle. Verse 29 do not be tight-fisted nor openhanded. The connection is that giving due is commanded, wasteful prohibited, tight-fisted prohibited, openhanded to poverty prohibited, kind word when lack, all together teach moderation, most upright spending, balanced. Tight-fisted is like not giving due, withholding right, openhanded is like wasteful, tabdhīr, brother to devils, extravagance. Moderation is gratitude, not ungratefulness, middle path.

**Link To Abundant Or Limited Provision And All-Aware All-Seeing**

Verse 30 will say Lord gives abundant or limited provisions to whoever He wills, All-Aware All-Seeing of servants. Verse 29 teaches moderation regardless of abundant or limited: if abundant provision, do not be openhanded to poverty, wasteful, extravagant; if limited provision, do not be tight-fisted withholding due, but give due moderately and give kind word if lack means while hoping for bounty. The provision abundant or limited is test, by will, with awareness and sight, what is best, as test of gratitude and patience, so moderation required in both states, abundant and limited, to be most upright.

**Linguistic Note On Maghlulah And Basṭ And Mahsur And Malum**

Tajʿal from j-ʿ-l, to make. Yad from y-d, hand. Maghlūlah from gh-l-l, chained, bound, from ghalla, to chain, bind, from ghull, chain. ʿUnuq from ʿ-n-q, neck. Taqʿuda from q-ʿ-d, to sit, end up, remain, from qaʿada. Malūm from l-w-m, blamed, blameworthy, from lāma, to blame. Tabsuṭ from b-s-ṭ, to extend, open, from basaṭa. Basṭ from b-s-ṭ, extension, openness. Kull from k-l-l, all, every. Maḥsūr from ḥ-s-r, depleted, in poverty, regretful, from ḥasara, to deplete, regret, from ḥasrah, regret. The verse's structure — do not make hand chained to neck then you sit blameworthy, and do not extend it all extension then you sit blameworthy depleted — with two prohibitions and two consequences, both sitting, teaches moderation, avoiding both extremes, tight-fisted and openhanded, which both lead to blameworthy, one also to poverty, depleted, regretful.

**How This Verse Teaches Moderation In Spending As Most Upright**

This verse teaches moderation in spending as most upright, wisdom: do not be tight-fisted, chained hand to neck, withholding due, for you will be blameworthy, nor openhanded, fully extended, wasteful, extravagant, spending all, for you will end up in poverty, depleted, regretful, blameworthy. Al-Qurṭubī says believer should be moderate in spending, give due to relatives, poor, traveller, not waste, not withhold, not spend all to poverty, but middle path, balanced, as most upright, wisdom. The practical lesson is to check spending: are you tight-fisted withholding due, right, when you have means? Then you will be blameworthy. Are you openhanded spending all wastefully, extravagantly, beyond due? Then you will end up in poverty, depleted, regretful, blameworthy, brother to devils. Be moderate, give due, avoid waste, give kind word when lack, hope for bounty.

**A Reflection On Sitting Blameworthy And Depleted**

Fa-taqʿuda malūman, you will sit blameworthy, and fa-taqʿuda malūman maḥsūran, you will sit blameworthy depleted — sitting indicates remaining in state, not fleeting, stuck, as verse 22 condemned abandoned also sitting, fa-taqʿuda madhmūman makhdhūlan. Tight-fisted sits blameworthy, openhanded sits blameworthy depleted, in poverty, regretful, unable. Moderation avoids both sitting states, leads to appreciated striving, mighty reward, not blame, not poverty, not regret. The believer who reflects will avoid tight-fisted and openhanded, be moderate, give due, avoid waste, give kind word when lack while hoping for Lord's bounty, as part of most upright guidance that Qur'an guides to, wisdom, leading to mighty reward and avoiding painful punishment.

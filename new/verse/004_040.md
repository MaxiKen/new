## Sūrah al-Nisāʾ 4:40

> Indeed, Allah never wrongs ˹anyone˺—even by an atom’s weight. And if it is a good deed, He will multiply it many times over and will give a great reward out of His grace.

**Expanded Commentary**

**Not Even by an Atom’s Weight**

The assurance *inna Allāha lā yaẓlimu mithqāla dharratin* — indeed, Allah never wrongs even by an atom’s weight — follows the question what harm would belief and donation have done (4:39) and answers it with a measure smaller than sight. *Dharratih* — atom, speck, smallest mote seen in a sunbeam — was read as the least conceivable quantum of weight. To say God does not wrong by that weight is to say justice at the smallest scale is exact. The sūrah has been precise about small amounts before — whether inheritance is little or much (4:7), a small provision for bystanders (4:8), provision from orphan wealth without waste (4:5–6), trade by mutual consent where consent must be genuine (4:29). Here precision becomes a divine attribute. The One who appointed fractions — half, quarter, eighth, sixth, third, two-thirds — after debts and bequests (4:11–12), who required four witnesses before sanction (4:15), and who halved penalty where appropriate (4:25) is now said to weigh at a resolution finer than any human scale. The Qur’an elsewhere says *“whoever does an atom’s weight of good will see it, and whoever does an atom’s weight of evil will see it”* (99:7–8), and this verse is the premise that makes that seeing just. Nothing is rounded away, neither a hidden donation nor a concealed bounty.

**If It Is a Good Deed, He Will Multiply It**

The second clause *wa in taku ḥasanatan yuḍāʿifhā* — and if it is a good deed, He will multiply it many times over — reverses the economy of withholding. Stinginess and ostentation (4:37–38) were both calculations that giving loses; this verse says giving gains, not one-to-one but multiplied. *Yuḍāʿif* — to double, to make many — was read as at least tenfold, as elsewhere *“whoever brings a good deed will have ten like it”* (6:160), and in other passages as seven hundredfold or without count where God wills, showing that multiplication is not a fixed rate but a generosity. The sūrah has already promised that avoiding major sins leads to covering lesser ones and an honourable entry (4:31) and that belief and donation from what was provided would have harmed no one (4:39). Multiplication explains why. A small provision offered with kind speech (4:8), a fair dowry given graciously (4:4), a just division witnessed (4:6), or a quiet trade done with genuine *tarāḍ* — mutual content (4:29) — each may seem modest, but the One who weighs atoms multiplies them. That multiplication is not a metaphor for market profit; it is the market of the next life where the currency is grace and the auditor is the One who never wrongs by even an atom.

**And Will Give a Great Reward Out of His Grace**

The addition *wa yuʾti min ladunhu ajran ʿaẓīmā* — and will give a great reward out of His grace — separates multiplication from grace. Multiplication is recompense for the deed; the great reward — *ajr ʿaẓīm* — is from *ladunhu* — from His presence, out of His grace. The sūrah elsewhere calls that great reward Gardens under which rivers flow for those who keep limits (4:13) and an honourable entry for those who avoid major sins (4:31). Here it is promised for the atom’s weight of good that He multiplies, showing that even the smallest good, once multiplied, is then further crowned with a gift beyond calculation. The pairing matters because it answers two fears at once: the fear that a small good will be ignored, and the fear that the great reward is unreachable for the weak. The first is answered by not an atom’s weight is wronged; the second is answered by grace that gives beyond multiplication. The weak for whom the sūrah wills to lighten burdens (4:28) and to turn in mercy (4:26–27) are precisely those who can offer only an atom’s weight, and the verse tells them that weight is enough to set multiplication and grace in motion.

**A Verse That Secures Trust in Division and Donation**

Placed where it is, this verse secures trust at the two points where trust is most tested: division and donation. Division of estates and trade requires confidence that a sixth or eighth will be counted exactly and that mutual consent will be honoured; the atom’s weight that is never wronged is that confidence. Donation to relatives, orphans, neighbours, and travellers (4:36) requires confidence that what is given from what was provided (4:39) will not be lost; multiplication and a great reward out of grace is that confidence. The sūrah has warned that deliberate devouring and killing will be met with Fire that is easy for God (4:30) and that stinginess concealing bounty and spending for show with Satan as associate (4:37–38) lead to humiliation and evil company. This verse is the other side of that economy, where the One who finds that is easy also finds it easy to multiply. The same ease that burns the deliberate overstep multiplies theAtom’s weight of good, because both proceed from perfect knowledge. The Name that closed the previous verse — *Allah has perfect knowledge of them* (4:39) — becomes here the guarantee that knowledge is not idle: what is known at the smallest scale is weighed justly and then exceeded in generosity.

**Justice That Is Exact and Grace That Exceeds**

The sūrah’s balance between justice and grace is stated here in a single sentence. Justice is exact — not even an atom’s weight of wrong — so that the wronged — orphan, widow, heir, neighbour — can trust that no portion is rounded away. Grace exceeds — multiplication and a great reward from His presence — so that the doer of good, even with a single sincere *ḥasanah*, can hope for more than his deed’s flat value. That pairing reflects the divine Names that have closed this section: All-Knowing, All-Wise (4:26), All-Knowing, Most Forbearing (4:12), Witness over all things (4:33), and ever Merciful to you (4:29). Knowledge ensures the atom is not missed; wisdom determines its multiplication; witness secures it; mercy adds the great reward. For the weak created weak (4:28), that pairing is the lightest of burdens: keep the major limits (4:31), believe and donate from what was provided (4:39), and know that even the smallest good is never wronged but is multiplied and crowned. The next verses will bring witnesses from every community and the Messenger as witness (4:41); what those witnesses will confirm is exactly this — that not an atom’s weight was lost, and that what was done for God was multiplied many times over and met with a great reward from His grace.

## Sūrah al-Nisāʾ 4:40

> Indeed, Allah never wrongs ˹anyone˺—even by an atom’s weight. And if it is a good deed, He will multiply it many times over and will give a great reward out of His grace.

### Expanded Commentary

**Absolute justice in the smallest amount**

Allah does not wrong even the weight of a particle. The image makes the principle concrete: no deed, however tiny, is omitted or undervalued. Human courts overlook evidence, memories fail, and social status bends judgment. Divine justice has none of those weaknesses. The smallest injury to a dependent and the smallest act of kindness are both present in the final account.

**Increase is grace, not a wage owed to God**

When Allah multiplies a good deed, the increase is described as coming from His bounty. This preserves two truths: the good act genuinely matters, and the reward exceeds what the person could claim by merit alone. The believer therefore acts carefully without becoming proud. One kind word, one protected inheritance, or one refusal to join a rumour may be enlarged beyond public measurement.

**A foundation for law and hope**

The verse comforts victims who think their loss is too small to matter and restrains wrongdoers who think a minor appropriation is invisible. It also protects the poor person’s contribution. Charity is not valued by its market price alone; a small gift given from limited means may carry an immense moral weight.

Justice and mercy meet here. Allah does not diminish the good and does not overlook the wrong, yet He may multiply good beyond exact equivalence. The person who fears that the scale is against him should repent and repair, while the person who thinks himself righteous should remember that every hidden motive is also weighed. The chapter’s family laws rest on this confidence: no right entrusted to human beings disappears from the knowledge of God.

**The smallest right still belongs to someone**

A tiny sum taken from a child, an uncredited hour of labour, or a kind act ignored by the public may seem insignificant. The verse makes such estimates unsafe. Justice counts the scale of the amount, but God also knows the vulnerability of the person affected and the motive of the person who acted.

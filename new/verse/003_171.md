## Sūrah Āli ʿImrān 3:171

> They are joyful for receiving Allah’s grace and bounty, and that Allah does not deny the reward of the believers.

**Expanded Commentary**

**The Continuation of the Description**

The verse continues the sentence about those who were killed in the way of God, and it adds a further element to the state described in the two verses before it. The men are said to be rejoicing in a blessing and a bounty from God, and the sentence closes with a statement about the reward of the believers in general.

The passage has been building a single picture across three verses: alive with their Lord, provided for, glad, aware of those who remain, and free of fear and grief. The verse before the reader supplies the ground of their joy and the assurance that the reward of such people is not lost.

**Blessing and Bounty Distinguished**

The two words in the sentence are not synonyms. The first describes a good condition that a person is given, and the second describes favour that goes beyond what is due, the generosity of a giver who is not paying a debt.

The pair is used in the Book at the close of several passages about divine giving, and the sūrah had already used the second term when it described God as possessed of bounty toward the believers. The men who died are said to rejoice in both, and the sentence therefore presents their state as one that combines a settled condition with an ongoing generosity.

**The Negation of Loss**

The sentence states that God neglects not the reward of the believers, and the wording denies the possibility that the reward is either lost or reduced. The Book uses the same formula elsewhere: one passage declares that God does not discount the reward of those who do good, and the sūrah about the Repentance repeats the phrasing at the end of a list of the hardships the believers endure.

The community needed the sentence. The arguments it had been hearing suggested that the men who died had thrown their lives away, and the verse answers the suggestion by denying the premise of any wasted deed. The reward is stated to be in the keeping of a God who does not allow it to disappear.

**Why the Assurance Is Placed Here**

The assurance stands at the end of the account of the martyrs because the sūrah has been dealing with a community that had reason to feel its sacrifices were lost. The Book does not require the community to treat the loss as insignificant; it requires it to trust the account that will be settled later. The dead had left households behind, and the survivors had been told by their own critics that the losses had been avoidable.

The Book's answer does not dispute the losses; it asserts the accounting. The sentence places the reward in a register that the community could not see, and it tells the believers that nothing they had given in the way described in the passage had been forgotten.

**The State Between Death and Resurrection**

The passage's description of men who are alive, glad, and aware of those still living gave the community's teachers a great deal of material, and the tradition used these verses in its teaching about the state of the soul after death and before the resurrection. The Book provides the fact in a few words and leaves the stages unstated.

The sūrah of the Cow states that those killed in God's cause are alive, adding that the community does not perceive it. The two passages together establish the reality of a state that the living cannot observe, and the tradition's discussions of the subject begin from them.

**The Report About the Slain of Badr**

The commentary tradition records that after the battle of Badr the Prophet addressed the enemy's dead and asked whether they had found what their Lord had promised them to be true, and that when he was asked why he spoke to the dead, he replied that they could not hear him any better than the dead could.

The report is part of the tradition's evidence for the awareness of the departed, and it is placed alongside these verses in the commentaries. What the verse before the reader establishes is the state of the believers who died in God's cause, and the reports belong to the same subject in the community's teaching.

**Reflections on Time and the Departed**

The commentators noticed that the passage describes the people who have died as aware of those who have not yet joined them, and they discussed how such awareness relates to the sequence of events as the living experience it. Time as measured by the living may not be the measure of the state the verses describe.

The Book does not settle such questions, and the tradition's discussions of them have remained the work of scholars rather than a matter of doctrine. The verses state what the community needed to know, and they leave the rest, which is a pattern that the Book follows consistently in its descriptions of the unseen.

**What the Verse Establishes for the Believers**

The sentence's last clause speaks of the believers in general, and it draws the community reading the passage into the same assurance that has been given about its dead. What is not neglected for the martyrs is not neglected for anyone who follows their path.

The community is thus given a statement it can rely on in every situation in which its efforts seem to have produced nothing. The verses that follow describe the community's answer to a call made while its wounds were still fresh, and the assurance stated here is part of the ground on which that answer was given. The verse does not promise visible results; it promises an account that will be complete, and the sūrah's next verses show the community what response it was expected to make after the fighting had ended.

## Sūrah al-Naḥl 16:26

> **Indeed, those before them had plotted, but Allah struck at the ˹very˺ foundation of their structure, so the roof collapsed on top of them, and the torment came upon them from where they did not expect.**

**Expanded Commentary**

**Plotting That Seems Secure**

Qad makara al-ladhīna min qablihim — indeed those before them had plotted. Makara means to plot, scheme, plan secretly with cunning, often to deceive or harm. Al-ladhīna min qablihim refers to previous nations who plotted against their messengers, like people of Nūḥ, ʿĀd, Thamūd, Pharaoh. Their plotting included mocking messengers, calling revelation fables, building structures of power to oppose truth. Al-Ṭabarī explains that makr here includes both ideological plotting, like saying revelation is fables, and physical plotting, like building strongholds to resist. The verse says qad, indeed, emphasizing that this is not hypothetical; it happened. Those before them plotted, just as Makkans plot now. The past is precedent. The sūrah uses past to warn present: you are not first to plot, and you will not be first to escape consequence.

**Striking At The Foundation**

Fa-atā Allāhu bunyānahum min al-qawāʿid — but Allah struck at their building from its foundations. Bunyān means structure, building, edifice, both literal and metaphorical: physical towers, palaces, and also systems of power, ideology, and social order built on falsehood. Qawāʿid means foundations, bases, supports. Al-Ṭabarī says Allah came upon their building from foundations, meaning He destroyed it from its base, causing total collapse. The image is vivid: a building that looks strong because roof and walls stand, but foundations are undermined, so whole collapses. Al-Rāzī explains that foundation here can mean literal foundations of buildings they built to defy, or metaphorical foundations of their plotting, their arguments and power structures. Allah strikes at root, not only branches. The Makkans who built their economy on idol worship and their pride on ancestry had built a bunyān; this verse says Allah can strike its qawāʿid. The same root q-w-ʿ-d appears for foundations of Kaʿbah that Ibrāhīm raised, showing that foundations can be for good or evil.

**Roof Collapsing From Above**

Fa-kharra ʿalayhim al-saqfu min fawqihim — so the roof collapsed upon them from above them. Sakhkhara, roof, ceiling, covering. Kharra means to fall down, collapse. Min fawqihim, from above them, emphasizes that collapse came from direction they felt safest, from above, where roof should protect. Instead of protecting, roof becomes instrument of punishment. Al-Zamakhsharī notes the irony: they built roof to shelter themselves from truth, but roof fell on them. The image also conveys suddenness: a roof collapse is not gradual; it is immediate, crushing. The verse says al-saqfu, the roof, definite, indicating the roof of their own building, not another. Their own construction becomes their destruction. This is divine justice: the means they built for security becomes means of ruin. The same pattern appears in stories of nations destroyed by what they trusted: Pharaoh drowned in sea he crossed, Qārūn swallowed by earth whose treasures he hoarded.

**Torment From Where They Did Not Expect**

Wa-atāhum al-ʿadhābu min ḥaythu lā yashʿurūn — and torment came upon them from where they did not perceive. ʿAdhāb means torment, punishment. Min ḥaythu lā yashʿurūn, from where they did not expect, did not perceive, matches verse 21's lā yashʿurūn, they do not perceive when they will be raised. Their lack of perception is part of punishment. Al-Ṭabarī explains that punishment came from direction they did not anticipate, in manner they did not expect, at time they did not expect. This could be earthquake, flood, wind, or sudden humiliation. The phrase emphasizes that plotting, however clever, cannot cover all directions. Plotters guard front, but punishment comes from below, above, or inside. The verse teaches that security is only with Allah; those who plot against His messengers feel secure in their bunyān, but true security is not in building but in obedience.

**A Warning To Makkans And All Plotters**

The verse is addressed to Makkans who plotted against Prophet ﷺ, who met in Dār al-Nadwah to decide to imprison, kill, or expel him, as 8:30 says: *And when those who disbelieved plotted against you to restrain you or kill you or expel you* (8:30). Their plotting was secret, but Allah knew, as verse 19 and 23 said He knows what they conceal. The verse says those before you plotted and their building collapsed; yours can too. Al-Qurṭubī notes that this is both warning and consolation: warning to plotters that their foundations are vulnerable, consolation to believers that plotting against truth ultimately fails. The verse's placement after discussion of bearing burdens of those misled shows that plotters not only bear own burdens but also cause collapse of those who follow them. Their bunyān includes followers who trusted their roof.

**Linguistic Note On Makr And Bunyān**

Makara from m-k-r means to plot with subtlety, often with deceptive cunning. In Qur'an, makr is attributed to both humans and Allah, but Allah's makr is just counter-plotting that defeats human plotting, not deceptive in blameworthy sense. Bunyān from b-n-y means building, structure. Qawāʿid from q-ʿ-d means foundations, bases, also used for women past childbearing, those who sit, showing root sense of sitting, being fixed. Saqf from s-q-f means roof, covering. Kharra from kh-r-r means to fall with noise, to collapse. The verse's rhythm — fa-atā Allāhu bunyānahum min al-qawāʿidi fa-kharra ʿalayhim al-saqfu min fawqihim — repeats min and fawq, emphasizing from below and from above, foundations and roof, total collapse from all sides. The verse ends with from where they did not perceive, leaving listener with sense of vulnerability: you cannot guard all directions; only Allah can protect. This final phrase also links to earlier description of idols as dead who do not perceive, showing that plotters share their idols' lack of perception. They do not perceive where punishment comes from, just as idols do not perceive when they will be raised. Both are blind to reality because they turned away from signs.

**How Foundation Imagery Teaches Humility**

Foundation imagery teaches that what is hidden matters more than what is visible. A building's beauty is in walls and roof, but its survival depends on foundations unseen. Similarly, a person's apparent success may rest on hidden foundations of gratitude or ingratitude. If foundations are shirk and arrogance, collapse is inevitable even if walls look strong. The sūrah therefore invites self-examination: what are my foundations? Are they tawḥīd and gratitude, which Allah establishes firmly, or are they plotting and denial, which Allah can strike? The answer determines whether roof will shelter or crush.

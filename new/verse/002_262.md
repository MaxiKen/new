## Sūrah al-Baqarah 2:262

> Those who spend their wealth in the cause of Allah and do not follow their charity with reminders of their generosity or hurtful words—they will get their reward from their Lord, and there will be no fear for them, nor will they grieve.

**Expanded Commentary**

**The Condition Stated**

The sentence names the reward of those who spend their wealth in the cause of God and then do not follow what they have given with reminders of their generosity or with words that wound. The verse supplies the condition that accompanies the act, and the community's teachers read it as the statement that the manner of the giving belongs to the giving itself.

The sūrah has already treated the spending that is offered for the sake of God, and the sentence here describes the quality that preserves it. The Book's method in the passage is to present the act and then to name what protects it, so that the community learns to attend to the whole of the matter.

The wording places the reminder and the injury after the gift, and the teachers noted the choice. The harm of the two is done to a person who has already received the help, and the sentence names the offence that follows the benefit.

**The Two Faults Named**

The commentators explained the first fault as the reminder that a giver delivers to the one who received from him, whether it is spoken openly or conveyed by the manner of his conduct. The second is the word or the act that causes pain to the person who was given to, and the teachers described the shame that such conduct produces in someone whose need has been exposed.

The community's scholars noted that the sentence joins the two, since the reminder of the gift and the injury that accompanies it belong to the same disposition. A person who regards his gift as a favour to be acknowledged has already treated the one who received it as his debtor.

The Book elsewhere warns against the conduct that breaks the heart of the poor, and the passages that treat the subject were gathered by the commentators when they explained the sentence. The duty of the giver therefore includes the protection of the dignity of the one who receives.

**The Reward Named**

The sentence states that those who give in the manner it describes will have their reward with their Lord, and the community's teachers read the phrase as the assurance that the promise is kept for them. The wording places the reward in the keeping of God rather than in the hands of the recipients, so that the giver does not look to people for the requital of his gift.

The Book's treatment of the subject elsewhere promises the increase of what is spent, and the sentence here states the outcome for the givers whose conduct has preserved the act. The two statements were read together by the community's scholars, so that the promise of the increase and the promise of the reward were understood as one.

The teachers also drew attention to the order of the sentence, since the reward is named for those who have completed the giving with the manner the verse requires. The condition is not an addition to the act; it is part of what makes the act what it is.

**The Fear Removed**

The sentence continues with the statement that no fear will come upon them and that they will not grieve, and the community's scholars explained the pair as the promise that accompanies the people of the Garden throughout the Book. The wording appears in the descriptions of the reward elsewhere, and the teachers regarded the repetition as the confirmation of the giver's standing.

The first clause concerns what lies ahead, and the second concerns what has passed, so that the two together describe a condition that is complete. The community's preachers used the formula when they spoke about the state of the believers in the life to come.

The phrase also answers the anxiety that the giver may feel about the property he has parted with. The fear that poverty follows the gift and the grief over what has been spent are both removed, and the sentence leaves the community with the assurance that accompanies the act.

**The Teaching of the Passage**

The sentence supplies the standard that the verses that follow develop, and the community's teachers read the whole passage as a single instruction on the manner of charity. The reminders of the gift, the injury, and the display of generosity are treated in the verses that come after, so that the sentence here states the principle in advance of the details.

The scholars of the community drew from the passage the rule that the acceptance of a gift is injured by the conduct of the giver as much as by the gift itself. The poor who are given what they need and then reminded of it have been harmed, and the Book does not treat the act as a benefit to its giver.

The teachers also observed that the passage is addressed to a community whose giving was part of its shared life, so that the warning concerns the relations of its members rather than a practice of the wealthy alone. The sentence therefore taught the manners of the community as well as the law of its charity.

**The Teachers' Use of the Sentence**

The preachers of the community returned to the sentence when they described the conduct that destroys the reward of a gift, and the reports describe their attention to the manner of the giver. The material shows the care with which the early community treated the feelings of those who lived in need.

The community's teachers also used the verse when they set out the duties of the wealthy toward those who served them, since the reminder of a favour is the temptation that accompanies the possession of property. The sentence supplied them with the reminder that the words of the giver are written.

The instruction in the passage that follows takes the same subject further with a comparison, and the community's scholars read the two together. The sentence in this place therefore stands at the head of the treatment of the manner of giving, and the verses that follow supply its illustration.

**What the Verse Establishes**

The sentence establishes the reward of those who give without the reminder of their generosity and without the word that wounds, the assurance that accompanies them, and the standard by which the manner of the giving is judged. The community's teachers used the verse when they taught the conduct of charity, and the scholars of the community placed it among the instructions that protect the dignity of the poor.

The passage continues with the comparison that treats the same subject under another figure, so that the reader is given the promise of the reward and the warning of what destroys it in the verses that follow one another.

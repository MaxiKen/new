## Sūrah al-Nisāʾ 4:86

> And when you are greeted, respond with a better greeting or at least similarly. Surely Allah is a ˹vigilant˺ Reckoner of all things.

### Expanded Commentary

**Peace must be answered with peace**

When greeted, believers must respond with something better or at least equivalent. The rule begins with the ordinary greeting of peace, but its ethics reach further: acknowledge the other person, return goodwill, and do not use religious identity as an excuse for coldness. A better response may be longer, warmer, or more generous, while a minimal equivalent remains the legal floor.

**Courtesy and security**

In a community marked by conflict, a greeting can communicate that the person is not being treated as an enemy. That does not mean every claim of peace overrides evidence of attack, but it prevents suspicion from becoming the default. The believer listens to the greeting and judges conduct by due process rather than by prejudice.

Allah is a Reckoner of all things. The exchange may seem too small to matter, yet the Qur’an places it beside laws of war and governance. Justice is formed in small acts of recognition as well as in major decisions.

**Greeting establishes a public ethic**

Returning peace is a small act of refusing to make hostility the default. It can be offered while legitimate boundaries remain in place. A community that answers courtesy with courtesy makes later truth-telling and negotiation easier because the other person has not been reduced to an enemy before the conversation begins.

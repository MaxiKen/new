## Sūrah al-Nisāʾ 4:86

> And when you are greeted, respond with a better greeting or at least similarly. Surely Allah is a ˹vigilant˺ Reckoner of all things.

**Expanded Commentary**

**Respond With a Better Greeting or At Least Similarly**

The verse commands *wa idhā ḥuyyītum bi taḥiyyatin fa ḥayyū bi aḥsana minhā aw ruddūhā* — and when you are greeted, respond with a better greeting or at least similarly. *Taḥiyyah* — greeting, gift of life — was explained as *al-salām* — peace — the word the sūrah will soon name where those who offer peace are not to be called *you are no believer* (4:94). The command to answer with better — *aḥsana minhā* — or to return it equally — *aw ruddūhā* — was read as ethic for all greetings among believers, and by extension for any kindness. The sūrah has commanded *iḥsān* to parents, relatives, orphans, poor, neighbours, companions, travellers, and those in possession (4:36), and has warned that interceding for good brings a share in reward (4:85). To answer a greeting with better is the smallest *iḥsān* that keeps the larger ones alive; it is intercession for good at the level of a word. The community that was told to take precautions and go forth (4:71), to reflect on the Qur’an that has no inconsistency (4:82), and to refer news of security or fear to those with sound judgment (4:83) is here told that the first sound of meeting is peace, and its reply should be more peace. Early authorities noted that the better greeting was exemplified as *wa ʿalaykum al-salām wa raḥmatu Allāh* for *al-salām ʿalaykum*, and at least the same for where equality is required.

**Greeting as Peace After Battle and Before**

The placement matters. The sūrah has moved through fighting — believers fight for God, disbelievers for the Devil whose schemes are weak (4:76), those who sell this life for the next fight whether martyred or victorious (4:74), and the oppressed cry for a guardian and helper (4:75). In that field, greetings can be mistaken for stratagem, as the next verses warn not to say to one who offers peace *you are no believer* seeking fleeting gain (4:94). This verse establishes the rule before that warning: when a greeting of peace is offered, answer it with better or same. The rule applies to the battlefield where a combatant may offer *salām* and to the town where neighbours meet. The sūrah elsewhere says *“do not kill yourselves, surely Allah is ever Merciful to you”* (4:29), and greeting with peace is the opposite of killing; it is the invitation to keep the bond that the lagger treated as if it were none (4:73). To answer peace with more peace is therefore the opposite of the night-plotters who spend the night contradicting *we obey* (4:81). One adds peace, the other plots harm; the verse chooses which night the tongue should keep.

**At Least Similarly — The Minimum That Protects**

The option *aw ruddūhā* — or at least similarly — was explained as the minimum that prevents greeting from becoming a hierarchy. To return the same — *wa ʿalaykum al-salām* for *al-salām ʿalaykum* — was read as obligatory where better is recommended, so that no one is left without answer. The sūrah has used similar minima elsewhere: the atom’s weight not wronged (4:40), the thread not wronged (4:49, 4:77), and the intercessor’s share for good (4:85) — each ensures that the small is counted. To answer at least similarly ensures the small greeting is counted. The ethic was extended to those in possession — bondspeople — and to neighbours near and distant (4:36); a greeting is due to each without regard to status, because *you are from one another* (4:25) and the sūrah opened with *from a single soul* (4:1). To withhold a greeting or to answer with less would be the stinginess that conceals bounty (4:37) at the scale of a word, while to answer with better is the donation from what was provided (4:39) at the same scale. The sūrah’s measure of *naṣīb* — share — for intercession (4:85) thus includes the share of peace returned.

**Surely Allah Is a Vigilant Reckoner of All Things**

The closing *inna Allāha kāna ʿalā kulli shayʾin ḥasībā* — surely Allah is a vigilant Reckoner of all things — matches the smallness of the act with the greatness of the watcher. *Ḥasīb* — reckoner, guardian, sufficient — was explained as counting the greeting and the reply as deeds, not missing the better — *aḥsan* — nor the equal — *radd* — as intercession’s share is not missed (4:85). The sūrah has used *ḥasīb* for the orphan’s handover — *sufficient is God as Reckoner* (4:6) — and for the watch over all things where intercession’s share is weighed (4:85); here it is the watch over greetings. That continuity shows greetings are not courtesies outside law; they are entries in the ledger that also records trusts and judgments, and that ledger is kept with the same sufficiency that multiplies an atom’s weight. The One who is All-Hearing, All-Seeing over trusts and judgments (4:58) is the Reckoner overpeace offered and peace returned. To answer with better is therefore not only kindness to the greeter; it is reckoning before the Reckoner, where even a better peace is multiplied.

**A Verse That Holds the Community’s Tongue**

Placed between the share for intercession (4:85) and the gathering on the Day about which there is no doubt (4:87), this verse holds the community’s tongue at its first word. The sūrah has diagnosed distorted words — *we hear and disobey* veiled as obedience (4:46), *rāʿinā* played with tongues (4:46), *we obey* spent the night contradicting (4:81) — and has prescribed straight speech — *qawlan sadīdā* (4:9), *qawlan balīghā* that reaches souls (4:63). Greeting with peace and answering with better is the daily practice of that straightness where speech is most frequent. A community that learns to answer *al-salām* with *aḥsan* will be the community that does not say to the one who offers peace *you are no believer* for fleeting gain (4:94), that does not call absence at disaster a blessing (4:72), and that does not wish after gain as if no bond existed (4:73). For the weak for whom burdens were lightened (4:28), the command is also light: when greeted, add a word. That added word, counted as *ḥasīb* and weighted as an atom, is the least burden that still joins the great reward, and it is the sound of the straight path walked.

## Sūrah al-Baqarah 2:237

> And if you divorce them before consummating the marriage but after deciding on a dowry, pay half of the dowry, unless the wife graciously waives it or the husband graciously pays in full. Graciousness is closer to righteousness. And do not forget kindness among yourselves. Surely Allah is All-Seeing of what you do.

**Expanded Commentary**

**The Case of the Fixed Dowry**

The verse addresses the case of a divorce that occurs before the marriage has been consummated but after the dowry has been fixed, and it requires the payment of half of what was agreed. The sentence follows the provision for the marriage in which no dowry was named and supplies the rule for the case in which the amount has been settled.

The Book's concern is the fairness of the arrangement when the marriage ends before it has begun, and the community's teachers read the sentence as the measure that divides the loss between the two parties. Readers are shown that the legislation attends to the terms the parties have agreed and to the stage the arrangement has reached.

**The Half Stated**

The sentence names half of the dowry as the amount due, and the community's jurists built the rule on the wording. The commentators explained the reason for the division, since the woman has been released from the agreement and the man has not completed the marriage.

The Book's treatment of the subject elsewhere supplies the rule for the case in which the consummation has taken place, and the community's scholars gathered the passages when they set out the law. Readers are given the account of a ruling that apportions the consequences of the separation between the parties, and the sentence supplies the proportion that the community applied.

**The Exception Named**

The sentence states that the woman may forgo her share or the husband may forgo the amount that would otherwise be returned, and the community's teachers read the clause as the permission that accompanies the rule. The wording names both parties, and the commentators noted that the forgiveness of either is a good that the Book commends.

The Book's treatment of generosity elsewhere presents the remission of a debt as a virtue, and the community's scholars connected the passages. The passage shows that the legislation leaves room for the parties to act beyond what is required of them, and the sentence supplies the permission that makes the arrangement flexible.

**The Recommendation of Forgiveness**

The sentence states that the forgiveness is closer to the mindfulness of God, and the community's scholars read the clause as the recommendation that accompanies the ruling. The Book's practice of attaching a commendation to its provisions appears throughout the sūrah.

The wording places the virtue within the framework of the community's obligations rather than outside them, and the community's teachers used the sentence when they spoke about the conduct of the parties after a separation. The passage shows that the Book's legislation on the household joins the requirement of fairness with the encouragement of generosity, and the sentence supplies both together.

**The Instruction to Remember**

The sentence instructs the community not to forget the generous conduct among themselves, and the community's teachers read the clause as the reminder that accompanies the rule. The commentators explained the phrase as the statement that the community's dealings should be conducted with the remembrance of what is fitting.

The Book's practice of reminding its community of the conduct it commends appears throughout its legislation, and the community's scholars used the clause when they discussed the arrangements that follow a separation. The account describes an instruction that concerns the manner as well as the substance of the settlement, and the sentence supplies the standard.

**The Closing Name**

The sentence closes with the statement that God sees what the community does, and the community's scholars read the pair as the ground of the instruction the passage has given. The same closing appears at the end of the rules about the widow's waiting period, and the community's teachers read the repetition as the statement that the conduct of the household is observed.

The Book's use of the phrase gives the reader the disposition in which the arrangements are conducted, and the passage's concern is the fairness of the community's dealings at every stage of a marriage. The passage closes with the rules about the dowry and with the reminder that accompanies them.

**The Division and the Generosity**

The half that the sentence names divides the consequences of the separation between the two parties, and the jurists built the rule on the wording together with the case in which the marriage has been consummated, where the whole of the dowry is owed. The teachers explained the proportion as the balance between the agreement that was made and the marriage that never took place.

The sentence also opens the way for either party to give up what is due, and the commentators noted that the generosity of the husband is mentioned before that of the wife in the wording. The Book commends the remission of a debt elsewhere, and the community's teachers read the clause as the invitation to a conduct that exceeds the requirement.

The instruction not to forget the gracious dealing among the community closes the passage, and the attribute of sight at its end places the arrangements of the former spouses before God. The teachers drew the lesson that the law of the household is observed as much in the manner of the settlement as in its substance, and the community's practice followed the standard.

**What the Verse Establishes**

The sentence establishes the payment of half of the dowry in the case it names, the permission for either party to forgo what is due, the recommendation of generosity, the instruction not to forget the generous conduct, and the knowledge of God that accompanies the ruling. The community's teachers used the verse when they set out the law, and the reader is shown that the Book's legislation on the household attends to fairness and to generosity together.

The community's teachers used the sentence when they taught the conduct of the parties after a separation, and the passage supplied the measure of what is owed together with the encouragement of what is given freely. The verse's closing statement about the divine sight accompanies the instruction, so that the private settlement of a broken engagement belongs to the account that every person will meet.

## Sūrah al-Baqarah 2:189

> They ask you ˹O Prophet˺ about the phases of the moon. Say, “They are a means for people to determine time and pilgrimage.” Righteousness is not in entering your houses from the back doors. Rather, righteousness is to be mindful ˹of Allah˺. So enter your homes through their ˹proper˺ doors, and be mindful of Allah so you may be successful.

**Expanded Commentary**

**The Question About the Moon**

The verse reports a question about the phases of the moon and gives the answer that they are markers of time for people and for the pilgrimage. The question is one of the inquiries the community put to the Prophet ﷺ, and the Book supplies the answer in a form that also corrects a second practice.

The sentence treats the subject as a matter of practical use rather than of speculation about the motions of the heavenly bodies, and the community's scholars noted the direction of the answer. What the reader receives is a statement about the calendar by which the community organises its worship and its affairs.

**The Phases and the Calendar**

The commentators explained that the term refers to the crescents that mark the beginning of the lunar months rather than to the astronomical new moon, which is the point at which the moon is invisible. The sighting of the crescent begins the month, and the community's scholars derived the rules for the beginning of the fast and of the festival from the observation.

The Book's answer states the purpose of the phases, which is the determination of time and the fixing of the pilgrimage. What the reader receives is a statement about the calendar's function, and the community's practice of sighting the moon in the month of the fast follows directly from the arrangement the verse describes.

**The Other Practice Corrected**

The second half of the sentence addresses the practice of entering houses from the rear, and the commentators report that some of the community would avoid the front doors when returning from a journey and would enter over a wall. The practice was connected with a state of ritual consecration or with a custom the community had inherited.

The Book's answer states that righteousness does not consist in such a practice and instructs the community to enter by the proper doors. The sentence's structure resembles the earlier statement about the direction of prayer, since both passages address the tendency to treat a customary practice as the substance of the religion, and the reader is shown the same correction applied twice.

**The Term for Righteousness**

The sentence uses the term that appeared in the longer description of the righteous a few verses earlier, and the community's teachers connected the two passages. What the Book calls righteousness consists of belief, worship, generosity, and endurance, and the sentence in this place states that neither the rear doors nor any other custom takes its place.

The answer therefore redirects the questioners from a detail to the substance. What the sentence establishes is that a practice of the community's own devising cannot carry the weight that belongs to the Book's account of a religious life, and the reader is shown how the Book replies to inquiries about the mechanics of a ritual.

**The Instruction to Be Mindful**

The sentence closes with the command to be mindful of God and the statement that the community may thereby prosper, and the ending echoes the purpose clauses that appear throughout the sūrah's legal passages. The community's scholars read the ending as the response that belongs to the correction the sentence has given.

The word for prosperity carries the sense of success and of attaining what is sought, and the Book attaches it to the conduct it commends. What the reader receives is the statement that the correction of a practice and the awareness of God belong together, and the passage continues with the instructions about conflict and restraint.

**The Reports About the Practice**

The commentators preserved the reports about the custom the verse corrected, and they connected it with the community of Madinah and with the practices of the Arabs before the revelation. The reports describe the practice in different ways, and the community's scholars noted the variety while agreeing on the sense of the correction.

What the passage establishes is that the Book addressed the customs of the community it was forming. The reader is shown a practice that the community had taken as a duty and that the Book reclassified, and the sentence's brevity indicates that the correction was complete once the purpose of the practice had been named.

**The Verse's Place in the Sūrah**

The sentence stands at the end of the section on the fast and the rules of worship and at the beginning of the passage on fighting, and its subject is the organisation of the community's time. The Book has been describing the disciplines of the month and the arrangements that accompany them, and it now states the function of the calendar by which those disciplines are observed. From the ordering of a family's meals and inheritance, the section turns to the defence of the community, and the command that follows is addressed to those under attack.

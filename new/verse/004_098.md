## Sūrah al-Nisāʾ 4:98

> Except helpless men, women, and children who cannot afford a way out—

### Expanded Commentary

**The exception for genuine incapacity**

Men, women, and children who cannot find a way out are exempted. Their weakness is recognized without contempt. A child cannot make political arrangements; a captive, disabled person, or impoverished family may have no route of escape. Divine command measures capacity rather than demanding a performance that circumstances make impossible.

The listing of men, women, and children counters the tendency to imagine migration as only a male political act. Families and the vulnerable are part of the moral account. Those with means have a duty to create safe routes for those without them, not to judge them from a position of security.

This verse also teaches hope in action. An inability to leave now does not become an excuse for those who could later act; circumstances must be reassessed honestly. Allah knows the difference between a closed door and an unwillingness to walk toward an open one.

**The unable are not forgotten**

Children, captives, the disabled, and the impoverished must not be judged by a duty they had no means to perform. The community’s obligation is to protect them from the oppressor and from the harshness of people who mistake privilege for moral superiority. Allah measures capacity with perfect knowledge.

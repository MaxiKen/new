## Sūrah al-Baqarah 2:208

> O believers! Enter into Islam wholeheartedly and do not follow Satan’s footsteps. Surely he is your sworn enemy.

**Expanded Commentary**

**An Address to the Believers**

The verse addresses the believers and commands them to enter into the religion wholly, and it forbids the following of the steps of Satan. The sentence follows the two portraits of the previous verses and turns to the community that has accepted the message.

The Book's practice of issuing a general command after describing two types of conduct gives the reader the instruction that the passage has prepared. What the reader receives is a command addressed to the community as a whole, and the sentence's second clause supplies the warning that accompanies it.

**The Meaning of the Term**

The commentators discussed the term rendered as peace or submission, and they noted that the word's plain sense concerns wholeness and the ending of division. Many explained it as the religion itself, while others read it as obedience, and the community's teachers connected the reading with the command to enter into the religion completely.

The Book's use of the vocabulary of peace supports both applications, since the religion is presented as the means by which a person is at peace with his Lord. What the sentence establishes is that the community is to embrace what it has received without reservation, and the community's scholars read the instruction as a statement about the completeness of the response.

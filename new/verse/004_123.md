## Sūrah al-Nisāʾ 4:123

> ˹Divine grace is˺ neither by your wishes nor those of the People of the Book! Whoever commits evil will be rewarded accordingly, and they will find no protector or helper besides Allah.

### Expanded Commentary

**Salvation is not a slogan of belonging**

Neither the wishes of Muslims nor those of the People of the Book determine divine grace. The verse rejects the claim that a community name automatically guarantees safety. Whoever commits evil will be recompensed accordingly and will find no protector besides Allah.

The statement is not denial of mercy or intercession; it is denial of entitlement. No group can bargain with God by saying its ancestry, label, or religious history exempts its members from accountability. The verse should make believers more humble, not more eager to condemn others.

Justice and grace remain together. Evil has a consequence, while repentance and divine pardon remain possible before death. The person who relies on a name is exposed; the person who combines faith, repair, and hope has a sounder relationship to mercy.

**No community owns forgiveness**

The verse makes religious identity a beginning of responsibility, not a substitute for it. A Muslim cannot demand safety merely by name, and a People of the Book cannot rely on ancestry. The question is how each person responds to truth, wrongdoing, repentance, and the rights of others.

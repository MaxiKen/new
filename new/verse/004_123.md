## Sūrah al-Nisāʾ 4:123

> ˹Divine grace is˺ neither by your wishes nor those of the People of the Book! Whoever commits evil will be rewarded accordingly, and they will find no protector or helper besides Allah.

**Expanded Commentary**

**Neither by Your Wishes Nor Those of the People of the Book**

The verse opens *laysa bi amāniyyikum wa lā amāniy ahla al-kitābi* — Divine grace — matter, recompense — is neither by your wishes nor wishes of the People of the Book. *Amāniy* — wishes, fancies, empty hopes — was explained as the *tamannī* — wishing — already unmasked as Satan’s delusion — *yummanīhim* (4:119–120) — and as the human wish that grace be by claim. The sūrah has shown wishes that mislead — those who wish you deviate greatly (4:27), who wish you alike in disbelief (4:89), who wish you neglect weapons for a sweep (4:102) — here it says neither set of wishes governs divine grace. *Laysa bi amāniyyikum* — not by your, Muslims’, wishes — was read as addressing the companions who might count on belief alone while committing evil, and *wa lā amāniyyi ahla al-kitābi* — nor by People of the Book’s wishes — as addressing Jews and Christians who claimed *“we are God’s children”* or *“none enters Paradise except our group.”* The sūrah has said that the People of the Book demanded a written revelation from heaven and were struck for wrongdoing (4:153) and that some Jews distort words (4:46); both groups’ wishes are set aside. Early reports linked the verse to mutual boasting between Muslims and People of the Book, where each claimed Paradise by belonging; the verse removed belonging as claim and installed deed and promise as measure.

**Whoever Commits Evil Will Be Rewarded Accordingly**

The rule *man yaʿmal sūʾan yujza bihī* — whoever commits, does, evil will be rewarded, recompensed accordingly, with it — was explained as the ledger law already given — *whoever earns sin earns only upon himself* (4:111) and *whoever earns evil or sin then throws on innocent bears slander* (4:112) — now as recompense law for all. *Sūʾ* — evil — was read broadly: associating, falling to Satan’s promises, hidden night plotting, devouring wealth, killing a believer, throwing sin. *Yujza bihī* — will be recompensed with it, rewarded accordingly — was explained as exact recompense — *jazāʾ* — the same root for the intentional killer’s *jazāʾ* is Hell (4:93) and for the believer’s *jazāʾ* is Gardens (4:122). The sūrah has said *“God does not wrong even an atom; if it is good He multiplies many times”* (4:40) — punishment is according, reward is multiplied — so *yujza bihī* is the atom-measured side. The pronoun *bihī* — with it, thereby — was read as equivalence: the evil’s own weight as recompense, not another’s, not less than atom. The Book’s promise being true (4:122) and whose word more truthful (4:122) was linked here: to say *yujza bihī* is the Most Truthful’s word about consequence, not a wish.

**And They Will Find No Protector or Helper Besides God**

The closure *wa lā yajid lahū min dūni Allāhi waliyyan wa lā naṣīrā* — and they will find for themselves no protector, guardian, nor helper besides God — was explained with *walī* and *naṣīr* — the same pair the oppressed cry for from grace (4:75) and that God alone suffices as (4:45), and that the self-wronger’s advocate cannot provide on the Day (4:109). *Min dūni Allāhi* — besides God, apart from Him, short of Him — was read as the failure of every alternative guardian: clan, People of the Book status, female gods invoked (4:117), Satan the rebellious guardian (4:119) whose promise is delusion, whose abode is Hell without *maḥīṣ* (4:121). To find no *walī* and no *naṣīr* where evil is committed and not sought away (4:110) is the law’s complement to *“whoever seeks forgiveness finds Him All-Forgiving, Most Merciful”* — the only *walī* and *naṣīr* besides Him that could rescue is none. The sūrah has warned *“do not take disbelievers as allies instead of believers”* (4:139, 4:144) and *“do not take them as allies unless they emigrate”* (4:89); here the ally beside God is declared absent where evil is choice.

**A Verse That Follows Two Gardens With the Rule of Wishes**

Placed after Gardens forever with rivers under, God’s promise ever true (4:122), this verse follows true promise with the rule that neither wishes govern grace. The sūrah has shown Satan promises and deludes with empty hopes (4:120) — *amāniy* — and contrasts it with God’s promise true; this verse names *amāniy* again as the false measure the community might substitute — *our wishes, their wishes*. The sūrah that promised great reward for commanding charity, kindness, reconciliation seeking God’s pleasure (4:114) had framed *ibtighāʾ marḍāt Allāh* as the valid seeking, not wishful claim. *Laysa bi amāniyyikum* was therefore read as the hinge where the theology of 4:116 — *He forgives what is less than association for whom He wills* — meets practice: will is not wish. For the weak the verse is clarifying: to hope for forgiveness as helpless (4:99) and to seek it after evil (4:110) is not wish alone but act of seeking; to commit evil expecting belonging to protect is wish, and it will be *yujza bihī*.

**Not by Claim but by Recompense — With No Walī Besides Him**

The construction *laysa bi amāniyyikum* — not by wishes — followed by *man yaʿmal sūʾan yujza bihī* was read as substitution of claim by recompense. The sūrah has said that to Allah belongs whatever is in heavens and earth and He is Fully Aware (4:126, 4:131–132), and that He is sufficient as Trustee (4:132); recompense *bihī* is therefore not by your computation but by His awareness, yet measured exact as not wronging an atom. The closing *no walī nor naṣīr besides Him* generalises the particular question *who will be their *wakīl* on the Day?* (4:109) and *who will advocate before God?* (4:109) — answer: none besides Him. The verse generalises the theft case’s lesson where advocates for self-wrongers found no advocate on the Day; every evil, by whomever, Muslim or Person of the Book, finds no protector besides Him, only the recompense of the act unless forgiveness is sought and found. The next verse will set the opposite face: those who do good, male or female, with faith will enter Paradise, not wronged even by a speck (4:124).

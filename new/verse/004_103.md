## Sūrah al-Nisāʾ 4:103

> When the prayers are over, remember Allah—whether you are standing, sitting, or lying down. But when you are secure, establish regular prayers. Indeed, performing prayers is a duty on the believers at the appointed times.

### Expanded Commentary

**Remember Allah in every posture**

After prayer, believers are told to remember Allah standing, sitting, and lying down. The phrase covers the moments in which normal prayer has ended or cannot be performed in its complete form. Dhikr is not confined to one bodily position or one emotional state. A frightened soldier, a sick person, and a resting traveller can remain connected to God.

**Security restores regular order**

When danger passes, regular prayer is established. The concession is not a replacement for the ordinary form once safety returns. The verse therefore balances flexibility with discipline. A person should not turn an emergency arrangement into a permanent neglect of the appointed prayers.

Prayer at appointed times is a duty, not merely a beneficial habit. The times structure the day around remembrance and prevent worship from being postponed until the heart happens to feel ready. Yet the preceding concessions show that the duty is designed for real human life, including fear, travel, and illness.

**Remembrance after crisis**

The return to regular prayer after danger teaches that emergency spirituality should mature into ordinary discipline. A person may remember God in every posture, but when stability returns he should not remain permanently disordered. Appointed times restore a community’s rhythm after fear has scattered it.

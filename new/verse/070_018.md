## Sūrah al-Maʿārij 70:18

> and gathered and hoarded ˹wealth˺.

**Expanded Commentary**

**Wealth Gathered, Mercy Scattered**

Jamaʿa fa-awʿā — "gathered and stored." Jamaʿa piles up; awʿā stows away — accumulation plus retention, earning plus embalming. Wiʿāʾ, the noun behind the verb, means vessel, container: the hoarder turns wealth into contents sealed from circulation. Gathering serves life; hoarding serves fear — fear of poverty, of future, of dependence on God. The fa- links piling to storing as habit: gathered, therefore stowed; earned, therefore entombed. Such pairing indicts not possession but posture — hands open to receive, closed to release. Wealth's purpose is flow: provision through the rich to the poor. Hoarding dams the flow, flooding the hoarder while parching neighbors. Readers audit their vessels: does wealth pass through toward need, or pool behind fear? Containers confess. What you store against God, Laẓā counts as fuel.

**The Twin Verse in Sūrah 104**

Sūrah 104 opens on the same figure: the slandering backbiter who amasses wealth and tallies it (104:1-2). Twin portraits, twin dooms — gathering-plus-counting there, gathering-plus-storing here. The pairing instructs: hoarding's sociology (slander guards wealth; wealth funds pride) meets hoarding's eschatology (Laẓā summons both). Counting deserves note — the 104 hoarder numbers his piles, affection quantified. Numbers replace neighbors; tallies replace ties. Together the verses anatomize miserliness whole: mouth wounding (slander), hands clutching (hoarding), heart counting (tallying). Laẓā claims the complete figure — back turned on truth (verse 17), hands closed to poor (verse 18), tongue loose on fellows (104). Readers should hold both portraits together: wealth's disease infects speech, fists, and arithmetic alike. The cure is one — open hands. Give, and Laẓā's summons never names you.

**Hoarding Means Withholding Zakāh**

Exegetes specify the crime: refusing the obligatory alms, zakāh — the poor's due portion in the rich's wealth. Hoarding here is not mere saving but withholding what God assigned others. The poor own a share of every fortune; the hoarder expropriates it. A blistering tableau elsewhere details the wage: hoarded gold and silver heated in hellfire, branding foreheads, sides, and backs — "taste what you hoarded" (9:34-35). Branding fits hoarding part by part: foreheads that frowned at beggars, sides turned away, backs presented. Such fittingness preaches proportion — members that withheld get marked. Readers should translate doctrine to dirhams: zakāh calculated, zakāh paid, conscience clear. The verse threatens not wealth but theft-from-God. What He earmarked for the poor was never yours. Withhold it, and Laẓā collects with interest.

**Containers Against the Day**

Awʿiyah — vessels, storages — multiply in vain. Vaults, accounts, portfolios: containers against contingency. Yet the Day opens all vessels, audits all stores, voids all hedging. What containers kept from the poor, fire claims from the keeper. Futility crowns the verse: stored wealth saves no storer. The auction of verses 11-14 already proved it — children, clans, worlds tendered and refused. Hoards join the refused lots. Such economics invert earth's: saving secures here, spending secures there. The wise transpose currencies — earthly stores into heavenly credit via charity. Fools maximize the wrong account, arriving rich in voided coin. Readers choose containers daily: vaults that Laẓā opens, or charities that mercy keeps. One storage survives the Day — what was given. Vaults burn with their keepers; alms await them.

**Gathering Is Not the Sin — Clutching Is**

Balance guards the verse against misuse. Earning is lawful, provision praiseworthy, wealth itself a potential blessing — the Book honors lawful acquisition and commands seeking bounty. Jamaʿa alone never damns; fa-awʿā damns — gathering plus sealing, earning plus embalming. The sin is clutching: receiving without releasing, holding without heeding. Such precision protects merchants and farmers while warning misers. Hands may fill; they must also pour. The prophets owned, traded, and inherited — yet their stores flowed. Readers should neither romanticize poverty nor demonize profit; the verse targets neither. It targets the sealed vessel, the stopped flow, the dammed mercy. Earn freely, spend dutifully, and Laẓā's summons passes over. Wealth is water: life in motion, rot in stagnation. Keep it moving toward need.

**Backs and Bank Accounts Together**

Verses 17-18 pair creed with deed: backs turned (belief's desertion), hands closed (mercy's refusal). Laẓā's company holds both defects — wrong facing, wrong fisting. The pairing completes the portrait: truth declined, poor denied. Neither defect alone tells the whole story; together they convict the whole person. Such pairing recurs across the Book — belief-plus-deeds as salvation's twin wings. Right facing without open hands limps; open hands without right facing wanders. Laẓā summons those lacking both. Readers audit both wings: face toward truth? hands toward poor? The verses stage judgment's two questions in order. Answer each rightly, and the fire's call never sounds. The sūrah will soon list the opposite company — praying, giving, believing (verses 22-35). Two guest-lists: Laẓā's and the Garden's. Choose yours by posture and purse.

**Vessels Sealed, Hearts Sealed**

Awʿā seals vessels; sealing vessels seals hearts. The hoarder's wiʿāʾ (container) and qalb (heart) harden together — closed fists, closed sympathies. Wealth dammed stops mercy flowing: the sealed vault teaches the sealed breast. Such is miserliness's physiology — avarice as cardiac condition. Giving reverses both sealings at once: opened hands open hearts, outflows restore circulation. The Book's charity-therapy works medically — spending cures clutching by exercising release. Each gift loosens the next; generosity compounds like interest. Readers should treat giving as cardiology: prescribe regular doses, increase gradually, monitor warmth returning. Hoards harden; alms soften. What awʿā closed, ṣadaqah reopens — vessels first, then hearts. Laẓā claims the sealed; mercy claims the opened. Break one seal today, and both begin healing.

**Human Nature Gets Diagnosed Next**

Back-turners summoned, hoarders claimed — now the sūrah diagnoses the disease beneath both. Verses 19-21 will anatomize anxious humanity: fretful in hardship, grudging in ease. Turning and hoarding prove symptoms; impatience is the syndrome. Readers should receive the coming diagnosis personally — the passage aims inward. Laẓā's fuel is not alien stuff but familiar frailty. The next verses expose the anxious heart that turns backs and seals vessels.

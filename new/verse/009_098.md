## Sūrah al-Tawbah 9:98

> And among the nomads are those who consider what they donate to be a loss and await your misfortune. May ill-fortune befall them! And Allah is All-Hearing, All-Knowing.

**Expanded Commentary**

**A Donation Can Feel Like Loss to a Selfish Heart**

Some nomads regard what they spend as a loss. The problem is not that giving reduces a balance on paper. The problem is the belief that nothing is gained through obedience. They see only the object leaving their hand and do not see gratitude, social repair, spiritual reward, or Allah's promise. Their account has become so narrow that charity appears irrational.

A believer also notices the cost of giving. He may need to plan, reduce a desire, or postpone a purchase. Faith does not require pretending that sacrifice has no material effect. It teaches the servant to place the material cost inside a larger account. What is lost from immediate possession may be gained as protection for a person, dignity for a family, or nearness to Allah.

**They Wait for the Believers to Fail**

Their hostility appears in the way they await a change in fortune for the believers. They hope that good circumstances will circle away from the community and become misfortune. This is more than caution about political events. It is emotional investment in the believers' setback.

The believer should examine whether he carries a smaller form of this waiting. Does another person's success make him secretly wish for reversal? Does he give support only while the group is strong? Loyalty is tested when the cause is vulnerable. A sincere servant may correct mistakes, but he does not enjoy the collapse of people who are trying to obey Allah.

**The Circle of Fortune Is Not the Circle of Truth**

The word for change in fortune evokes a circle or circuit. The hostile person imagines that power must turn, that today's success will become tomorrow's defeat, and that the community's good fortune is temporary enough to mock. Worldly conditions do change, but their movement does not decide the truth of revelation.

The believer knows that victory and loss can both be tests. Success can expose arrogance, and defeat can expose loyalty. He does not worship the circle of fortune. He prepares for change, thanks Allah in ease, and remains faithful in hardship. A person who waits only for the next reversal will never find a stable moral center.

**Allah Hears the Complaint Beneath the Gift**

The verse ends with Allah's hearing and knowledge. He hears the words spoken about the donation, the private resentment that accompanies it, and the hope that the believers will suffer. The giver may convince people that the gift was generous, but Allah knows whether it was a sacrifice or an accusation.

This knowledge comforts the sincere donor who is criticized for giving. It also warns the donor who uses charity to display superiority or to purchase influence. Allah hears both the public contribution and the private motive. The servant should give in a way that can survive divine knowledge, not merely human applause.

**Charity Reveals the Meaning of Ownership**

A person who sees everything as his own will call every obligation a loss. A person who sees himself as a trustee asks what the Owner wants done with the gift. Stewardship does not remove personal rights. It places those rights under a moral order. The owner can provide, save, and enjoy lawfully while recognizing that another person's need may have a claim.

This view makes giving less humiliating. The donor is not performing superiority over the poor; he is returning part of a trust to the purposes Allah established. The recipient is not receiving a favor from an absolute owner; he is being supported through a system of rights and mercy. Such understanding protects both sides from resentment.

**Ill-Fortune Is Not the Believer's Prayer**

The verse invokes ill-fortune upon those who await the community's misfortune. The believer should not turn this into a habit of cursing everyone who disagrees. He may ask Allah to protect people from the harm they intend and to guide them from hostility. The warning is directed at a settled desire for the failure of faith.

The servant therefore keeps his heart free from spite. He gives when giving is right, speaks truthfully, and trusts Allah with outcomes. If the community suffers, he repairs rather than rejoicing. If it succeeds, he remains humble. Allah hears the hidden wish, so the safest wish is for truth to prevail and for hostile hearts to return before their hostility becomes their final account.

A donor who regards charity as loss may still be invited toward a better account. Show how spending feeds a person, protects dignity, and trains the heart to recognize Allah's bounty. Do not flatter the donor or conceal the obligation, but do not assume that resentment can never change. The person waiting for misfortune should also be reminded that fortune is not a god. The believers may rise or fall in worldly terms, yet Allah's truth is not measured by the circle of events. The sincere community gives through ease and hardship, trusting that Allah hears the complaint and knows the sacrifice.

The believer can help such a person by showing that charity is not an indemnity imposed by an enemy. It is an act through which Allah purifies wealth and joins people in responsibility. The donor remains free to ask whether the funds are used properly, but he does not treat the duty itself as theft. He should also stop waiting for the community's collapse. Worldly reversals come to every group, and Allah can make a setback a lesson or a success a trial. The sound heart gives in hardship, hopes for the believers' good, and trusts Allah with the fortune that no human being controls.

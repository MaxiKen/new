## Sūrah Āli ʿImrān 3:42

> And ˹remember˺ when the angels said, “O Mary! Surely Allah has selected you, purified you, and chosen you over all women of the world.

**Expanded Commentary**

**Angels Speaking to a Woman in Her Own Right**

The verse reports a direct address: the angels come to Mary and speak to her about herself. Nothing precedes their words about a husband, a father, or a priest; the addressee is the woman, and the announcement concerns her own standing.

That directness is unusual in the literature of the ancient world and it is a pattern in this sūrah. The same narrative has opened with a mother's vow, continued through a child's acceptance and miraculous provision, and now arrives at an address from the angels to the woman herself. The Book is building a story in which a woman is not a step in a man's genealogy but the subject of the account.

**Three Verbs: Selected, Purified, Selected Again**

The announcement stacks three acts of God, and the middle one separates the other two. Mary is selected, purified, and then selected a second time over the women of the world. Commentators read the sequence as describing two stages of a life: a first election in which she was devoted to the service of the sanctuary and given sustenance that had no visible source, and a second election in which she was given the miracle of a child without a father.

The order tells the reader something about how the Book treats distinction. Purity comes between the two elections, which means the honour is never separated from the condition of the person honoured. A chosen servant is one who has been cleansed, and the cleansing is described as God's act rather than her achievement, which is how the Book keeps the credit with God while describing a life of extraordinary devotion.

**What "Purified" Means in This Sūrah**

Commentators explained the purification as purity of intention and worship, and also as protection from the defilement that contact with evil people brings. The Book's language of purification is used elsewhere for the household of the Prophet, where God states His intention to remove impurity and to purify its members completely (33:33).

The two senses are complementary rather than alternative. A person is purified in what she intends when she worships, and she is protected from what would stain her when she lives among others. Mary's life as the narrative presents it involves both: years of private devotion in a chamber, and later a public accusation that she answers with silence. The verse's verb covers the inner and the outer condition at once.

**"Over All Women of the World"**

The second selection is stated in the widest terms available. The same phrase, used of the chosen families in verse 33, was qualified there by the words *of their time*, and the qualification helps here as well: Mary is selected over the women of her era, and commentaries that placed Khadījah, Fāṭimah, and Āsiyah at the summit of believing women were not contradicting this verse.

The Prophet's own statements on the subject are recorded in several collections. Anas reports him saying that four women are sufficient for a person among the women of the world: Mary the daughter of ʿImrān, Khadījah, Fāṭimah, and Āsiyah the wife of Pharaoh (Sunan al-Tirmidhī 3878). Abū Mūsā reports him saying that many men reached perfection but that among women only Āsiyah and Mary attained it (Ṣaḥīḥ al-Bukhārī 3411). The reports do not cancel this verse; they show how the community understood it — as the elevation of a particular woman in a particular history, not as a ranking designed to diminish the other women named with her.

**The Narrative's Thread and How It Is Woven**

The verse opens with *and ˹remember˺ when*, a construction that continues the story rather than starting a new one. Commentators observed that the same construction resumes at verse 45 and again at verse 47, so that a sequence of angelic announcements is followed by Mary's reply in the later verse. The narrative is laid out across several sentences with the answer held back.

The arrangement suits the content. What is being narrated is not a single conversation but a series of addresses spread across years: the announcement of her selection, the command to worship, news of a son, her question, and the reply. Weaving them into consecutive verses means a reader meets the whole of her calling in one passage, and the shape of a life given to God appears at once rather than in pieces.

**The Child Who Was Dedicated Is Now Addressed**

Read against the earlier verses, the address here completes a transaction that began before Mary was born. Her mother offered what was in her womb for service, asked that the offering be accepted, named the child, and sought protection for her and her offspring from Satan. The narrative now shows the child receiving an announcement from angels in her own right.

The sequence is the sūrah's answer to a mother's anxiety. Every part of the vow that worried her is touched: the child was accepted graciously, raised well, provided for, protected, and now told by angels that God has chosen her. What a woman asked for in a private prayer in a household becomes the formal declaration of a chapter.

**A Woman Whose Honour Revelation Itself Defends**

The Book's treatment of Mary does something that the sūrah's historical setting made necessary. In the disputes of the Prophet's time, her reputation was a subject of accusation and jest, and the Qur'an answers by naming her among the righteous, describing her truthfulness, and preserving the record of her chastity.

This verse is part of that defence. An honour conferred by God and announced by angels cannot be removed by the talk of a street, and the Book states the honour in the strongest terms available to it. The community that recites the sūrah is being given a portrait of the woman whose son it also reveres, and the portrait's first element is the verdict of God on her.

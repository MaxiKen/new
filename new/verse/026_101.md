## Sūrah al-Shuʿarāʾ 26:101

> nor a close friend.

**Expanded Commentary**

**The Final Scene Removes Intimate Support**

The line completes the confession that the condemned have no intercessors, no close friend, and no loyal friend. The emphasis is not on ordinary loneliness after a social disappointment. It is on standing before God without a person whose closeness can bring relief, plead successfully, or share the burden of judgment. The friend who once cared about every concern is absent as a source of rescue.

This final absence exposes the limits of human attachment. Friendship can be one of life's greatest gifts, but it cannot become a substitute for God. A friend may comfort, advise, and help within worldly limits. No friend can carry another person's worship, erase deliberate association, or command forgiveness on the Day of judgment.

**Ṣadīq Ḥamīm Names More Than Companionship**

The Arabic expression behind loyal friend joins ṣadīq with ḥamīm. Ṣadīq indicates a close friend, while ḥamīm suggests someone so deeply concerned that he feels the other's pain and cares about what the other cares about. The phrase reaches beyond casual company. It describes the person one expects to stand near in the worst crisis.

That is why the absence is so severe. The condemned do not merely lack acquaintances. They lack the intimate companion who might be expected to share their distress or speak on their behalf. The phrase also exposes the false security of social closeness when that closeness has been built around idolatry and disobedience rather than truth.

**Friendship Cannot Replace Moral Responsibility**

A person may be loved by many people and still remain accountable for his own choices. Friends can influence a direction, but they cannot remove the duty to examine that direction. If a group encourages false worship, its closeness becomes a danger rather than a guarantee of safety. The warmth of companionship cannot turn an error into guidance.

This does not make friendship unimportant. It makes its proper purpose clearer. A good friend helps another return to God, warns against harm, and refuses to praise a destructive choice. A friend who only confirms desire may feel loyal while leading the other toward isolation from truth. The final absence of a loyal friend shows the cost of relationships that never challenged false allegiance.

**The Condemned Had Friends in the World**

The line does not mean that the people lived without human companions. They had families, communities, leaders, and associates. Their loss is that none of those relationships can function as a saving friend before God. Worldly companionship ends at the boundary where each soul must answer for what it served.

This distinction helps explain why the verse uses the final scene to expose false security. A person may look socially protected because influential people stand around him. Yet power and loyalty are temporary when they are not rooted in obedience to God. The crowd disperses, the leader cannot defend the follower, and the friend cannot enter the judgment in the friend's place.

**A Parallel Describes the Same Solitude**

Quran 40:18 warns of the approaching Day when wrongdoers have neither a close friend nor an intercessor whose word is accepted. That parallel confirms that the absence in this verse is a feature of final accountability, not a general condemnation of friendship. Human closeness remains valuable in the world, but it cannot become an independent channel of salvation.

The parallel also shows why the word close matters. The issue is not whether someone is present in the crowd. It is whether anyone has the authority and acceptance to help. A friend may stand nearby and still be unable to alter the verdict. Nearness in space cannot replace permission before God.

**False Worship Corrupts Friendship**

Idolatry can bind people together through shared rituals and inherited identity. That bond may feel strong because it gives members a common object of loyalty. Yet when the object fails, the friendship built around it has no power to repair the damage. Shared error becomes a shared accusation rather than a shared refuge.

The verse therefore asks what holds relationships together. If friendship depends on agreement at any cost, it will break when truth becomes unavoidable. If it is rooted in concern for God's pleasure, it can survive correction and even help a person repent. The final scene distinguishes loyal companionship from companionship that merely protected the same falsehood.

**The Absence Calls for Direct Reliance on God**

A believer should love friends deeply while knowing that the heart's ultimate dependence belongs to God. This is not emotional detachment. It is freedom from demanding that another human being provide what only the Lord can provide. Such freedom makes friendship more honest because it removes the impossible burden of being one another's savior.

A friend can be asked for advice, practical help, and prayer. The request remains a request to a servant, not worship of a power. Hope is directed to God, while gratitude is offered to the person who helps. This order protects both sides from disappointment turning into blame when human ability reaches its limit.

**The Final Lack of a Friend Is a Warning Now**

The condemned discover too late that their social world could not protect them. The living can learn the lesson before the final scene. Choose companions who strengthen truthful worship, become the kind of friend who warns with mercy, and do not make loyalty a reason to follow falsehood.

The short line carries a large warning. No friend, however intimate, can replace the Lord. The only secure companionship is the companionship that directs the heart back to God and remains within the limits He has set. When judgment arrives, what saves is not the number of friends but the soundness of the allegiance they helped form.

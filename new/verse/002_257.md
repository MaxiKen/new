## Sūrah al-Baqarah 2:257

> Allah is the Guardian of the believers—He brings them out of darkness and into light. As for the disbelievers, their guardians are false gods who lead them out of light and into darkness. It is they who will be the residents of the Fire. They will be there forever.

**Expanded Commentary**

**The Guardianship Named**

The sentence names God as the guardian of the believers, and the term the Book uses carries the senses of the ally, the protector, and the one who takes charge of another's affairs. The community's scholars explained that the guardianship combines the care that guides with the authority that defends, so that the believer is described as a person who has been taken in hand by his Lord.

The word appears throughout the Book in the discussions of allegiance and support, and the teachers gathered the passages when they set out the subject. A guardian in this sense is not a distant patron but the one who manages the affairs of the person who has placed himself in his care.

The sentence also implies the choice that the believer has made, since the guardianship follows the faith that the verse names. The community's teachers drew from the pairing the lesson that the relationship is established by the person's acceptance and maintained by the care of God.

**Light and Darkness**

The second clause describes the work of the guardianship as the bringing of the believers out of darkness and into the light, and the commentators read the figure as the movement from error to guidance. The Book uses the pair of images throughout, and the passages that the teachers collected present the light as the guidance that shows the path and the darkness as the confusion in which a person cannot find it.

The order of the movement is significant, since the sentence describes the exit from the darkness before the entry into the light. The community's scholars read the sequence as the account of the change that the guidance works in a person, so that the false is abandoned before the true is embraced.

The wording also describes the act as continuous rather than complete, and the teachers explained the form of the verb as the statement that the care of God accompanies the believer through the stages of his life. The reports on the verse describe the light as the guidance of the Book itself.

**The Two Processions**

The sentence then turns to those who disbelieve and describes their guardians as the false deities who lead them out of the light and into the darkness. The symmetry of the two clauses has been noted by the commentators, since each group is described with its guardian, its movement, and its destination.

The teachers explained the phrase about the false deities as the description of everything that a person takes as the object of his reliance in place of God, whether it is an idol, a claim to power, or the desires of his own soul. The Book elsewhere calls these objects by the same term that the sentence uses.

The movement of the second clause is the reverse of the first, and the community's scholars read the reversal as the statement about the direction of a life. The one who is led out of the light enters the darkness, and the sentence then names the destination that belongs to the path.

**The Destination Stated**

The sentence closes with the statement that those who disbelieve are the inhabitants of the Fire and that they will remain there, and the community's teachers read the clause as the completion of the description. The Book joins the two destinies in several passages, and the reports that describe them were gathered by the commentators when they treated the subject.

The wording states the outcome in the present tense, and the scholars explained the form as the statement of a condition that has already been established for those whose lives have taken that direction.

The sentence does not present the matter as a punishment that descends without warning. The light has been offered, and the passage's earlier verses have described the distinction that the community was given, so that the entry into the darkness is described as the result of a choice.

**The Use of the Image in the Book**

The community's teachers traced the imagery of light and darkness through the Book, and they found the same figure in the passages that speak of the revelation as the light that leads the believers. The verses that describe the Prophet ﷺ as a light and those that describe the Book as the light of guidance supplied the material for their explanations.

The commentators noted the difference between the light that is named in the sentence and the lights that belong to the created order, since the light of the verse is the guidance that reaches the heart. The distinction between the two was preserved in the community's teaching.

The sūrah's other passages on the same theme were read with the sentence, and the teachers used the combination when they spoke about the duty of the believer to seek the means of guidance that the Book supplies.

**The Promise for the Community**

The sentence has been read by the community's teachers as the promise that accompanies the believers in the difficulties of their life, and the reports describe the comfort that the passage brought to those who were weak in Makkah and Madinah. The guardianship is named for the community as a body as well as for the individual believer.

The wording also supplies the ground of confidence in the community's affairs. Its defense, its guidance, and its outcome are placed in the hand of the One who brought it out of the darkness, and the teachers used the clause when they encouraged the believers in the trials they met.

The present passage stands among the verses about giving and spending, and the guardianship named here accompanies the instructions that follow. The community's scholars read the sequence as the statement that the conduct the sūrah requires is the fruit of the guidance the sentence describes.

**What the Verse Establishes**

The sentence establishes the guardianship of God over the believers, the movement from darkness into light that the guardianship works, the parallel condition of those who disbelieve with the guardians who lead them away, and the destination that belongs to each path. The teachers used the verse when they set out the subject of allegiance, and the community's preachers returned to the two processions when they described the change that the faith works in a person.

The clause about the light has been read as the statement about the guidance of the Book, and the attributes of the surrounding verses supply the ground on which the guardianship rests. The reader leaves the sentence with the two paths set out plainly and with the promise that accompanies the first of them.

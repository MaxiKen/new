## Sūrah al-Aʿrāf 7:24

> Allah said, “Descend as enemies to each other. You will find in the earth a residence and provision for your appointed stay.”

**Expanded Commentary**

**The Command to Descend**

The verse reports the command given after the fall, that the pair and their adversary should descend with enmity between them. The sūrah of the Cow reports the same command with the same clause about mutual enmity, and it adds that the earth will be a dwelling and a provision for a time (2:36). The sūrah of Ṭā Hā reports the descent and the statement that some of them will be enemies to others (20:123). The three accounts agree on the elements of the command.

The command is addressed to more than the two human beings in the reading the commentary tradition prefers, since the adversary is included in the descent and the enmity is placed between the parties. The verse in front of us therefore states the terms on which the whole history of the human race will proceed, and the sūrah's warnings about the enemy are grounded in the conditions of that history.

**Enmity as the Standing Condition**

The verse describes the descent as a departure into mutual enmity, and the Book treats the condition as the framework within which the human being lives. The sūrah's instruction to the children of Adam later in the chapter says that the enemy and his tribe see them from where they cannot see him, and the verse that warns them against being deceived as their parents were deceived follows the same line (7:27). The enmity is not a passing quarrel but the standing relation between the human being and the tempter.

The Book's descriptions of the enemy's activity elsewhere state its limits as well as its reach. The sūrah of the Rocky Tract states that he has no authority over the sincere servants of God (15:42), the sūrah of the Bee states that he has no authority over those who believe and rely on their Lord (16:99), and the sūrah of the Night Journey states that he has no authority over the servants of God. The enmity in front of us is therefore the setting of a test rather than a state of helplessness.

**The Earth as a Dwelling**

The verse names the earth as the place of dwelling for the pair and their descendants, and the commentary tradition explains the term as a settled and established place. The sūrah of the Cattle uses the same word in its description of the resting place of human beings, and the sūrah of the Cow states that the earth will be a dwelling and a provision for a time (2:36). The verse in front of us states the dwelling before it states the provision.

The Book's treatment of the earth as the place of human life appears throughout its accounts of the created order, and the sūrah of the Heights itself has already described the establishment of people upon the earth and the means of livelihood placed in it (7:10). The descent in front of us places the pair in the world that the opening verses described, and the narrative connects the two passages.

**Provision for a Time**

The verse describes the provision of the earth as belonging to a limited period, and the Book repeats the qualification in its accounts of the present life. The sūrah of the Cow gives the same phrase in its account of the descent (2:36), and the sūrah of the Heights will state later that every community has a term that can neither be advanced nor delayed (7:34). The enjoyment of the earth is real, and it is bounded.

The commentary tradition places the phrase alongside the Book's many comparisons between the present life and the life to come, and the sūrah of the Family of Imran describes the love of the present life in its list of the things that people desire before it states that the reward of God is better. The verse in front of us supplies the reason for the comparison, since the provision is granted for the interval that ends at the appointed term.

**The Reading of the Fall in the Tradition**

The commentary tradition contains a reading of the descent that emphasizes its place in the divine arrangement. The fall brought the pair to the earth, where the human being was to be the deputy of God, and the tradition notes that the drawing near to God that follows the experience of distance is a part of the human vocation. Ibn ʿAṭāʾ Allāh of Alexandria expressed the thought in his aphorisms, saying that a disobedience which bequeaths humility and need is better than an obedience which bequeaths might and pride.

The Book's own statements about the purpose of human life on the earth support the reading, and the sūrah of the Cave states that God has made what is on the earth an adornment for it in order to test which of them is best in deeds (18:7). The verse in front of us states the descent and the provision, and the sūrah's remaining instructions are addressed to people whose term on the earth is running.

**Where the Verse Sits**

The verse stands twenty-fourth in the chapter, and it follows the confession of the pair and precedes the statement of the cycle of life, death, and resurrection. The narrative then turns to the instruction of the children of Adam about clothing and conduct, and the account of the first pair gives way to the address to their descendants. The command to descend in front of us is the hinge between the two.

The reader of the sūrah has been given the Garden, the boundary, the deception, the fall, the confession, and the descent. The chapter's teaching about the present life, its provisions, and its term follows from the scene in front of us, and the warnings about the enemy continue to apply to the people who live on the earth.

**The Transition to the Next Verse**

The next verse states that the people will live on the earth, die there, and be brought forth from it (7:25). The dwelling and the provision named in front of us are given their completion in the cycle of life and death and in the raising that ends it.

The two verses together state the whole span of human existence on the earth. The descent is followed by the dwelling, the dwelling by the death, and the death by the bringing forth, and the sūrah then addresses the children of Adam who live between the descent and the raising.

## Sūrah al-Anʿām 6:156

> You ˹pagans˺ can no longer say, “Scriptures were only revealed to two groups before us and we were unaware of their teachings.”

**Expanded Commentary**

**The Excuse the Verse Removes**

You pagans can no longer say, Scriptures were only revealed to two groups before us and we were unaware of their teachings. The verse names the excuse that the sūrah's audience had used for its position, and it states that the excuse is now closed. The two groups are the communities that possessed the earlier scriptures, and the audience's claim was that revelation had passed them by and that they had no access to what it contained.

The Book addresses the same claim in its descriptions of the mission of the Prophet. The sūrah of the Story says that God had not sent a warner to the audience's ancestors before the revelation came to them, and the sūrah of the Bee says that messengers had been sent to every community with the command to worship God and shun false gods (16:36). The sentence in front of us states that the excuse of ignorance cannot be offered by people who have received a Book in their own language.

**The Two Groups Named**

The Book refers to the earlier communities in several passages, and it acknowledges the scriptures they were given. The sūrah of the Cow mentions the believers, the Jews, the Christians, and the Sabians, and states that those who believe in God and the Last Day and do right will have their reward (2:62); the sūrah of the Table gives the same statement (5:69); and the sūrah of the Cattle itself has described the Book given to Moses and the guidance it contained (6:154). The verse in front of us refers to those communities as the recipients of the earlier revelations.

The audience's position in Makkah was not that of a people without contact with those communities. The Book appeals repeatedly to the knowledge of the People of the Book and describes their recognition of the Prophet's message (6:20; 2:146), and its argument in the passage on revelation relied on the presence of the earlier scriptures in the region. The claim that the audience had been unaware of those teachings was therefore an excuse rather than a description of its situation.

**The Language of the Revelation**

The Book's answer to the excuse is supplied by the revelation's own language, and the sūrahs describe the Qur'an as an Arabic scripture in several places. The sūrah of Joseph says that God sent the revelation down as an Arabic Qur'an so that the people might understand (12:2); the sūrah of the Ornament says that it has been made an Arabic Qur'an so that the audience might understand (43:3); and the sūrah of the Poets describes the revelation as coming in a clear Arabic tongue (26:195). The verse in front of us states that the excuse of unfamiliarity no longer applies.

The Book's treatment of the charge that the Prophet was taught by others uses the same argument. The sūrah of the Bee records the accusation and answers that the person the accusers have in mind speaks a foreign tongue while the revelation is in eloquent Arabic (16:103). The verses in front of us belong to that line of argument, and they state that the Book's arrival in the audience's own language settles the question.

**The Position of the Audience Before the Revelation**

The sentence states what the audience's position had been before the Book came, and it does not deny that the earlier scriptures were given to other communities. What it removes is the possibility of treating that history as an excuse. The audience had been shown the signs in the created order, had heard the preaching of the messenger, and had access to the traditions of the communities around it, and the Book's arrival placed the guidance within its reach.

The Book's statements about the responsibility of the communities addressed by revelation rest on the same arrangement. The sūrah of the Cattle states that God would never destroy a society while its people were unaware (6:131), and the verse in front of us states that the condition described there had been met for the audience: the warning had come to it in the form of a Book it could read and recite.

**What the Verse Says About the Earlier Scriptures**

The verse acknowledges the earlier scriptures and their recipients without making them the property of those communities alone. The guidance they contained belonged to the same line of provision as the Qur'an, and the sūrah has just described the Book of Moses as a guide and a mercy (6:154). The audience had been living beside the inheritors of those scriptures, and its claim to ignorance was a refusal to ask.

The sūrah's argument about the earlier revelations runs through the chapter. It appealed to the Book of Moses as evidence that God reveals to human beings (6:91), it described the restrictions imposed upon the Jewish communities (6:146), and it stated that the people who were given the earlier books recognise the Prophet's message. The verse in front of us states the conclusion the audience is to draw from all of it.

**The Transition to the Next Verse**

The next verse completes the passage with the declaration that there has now come from the Lord a clear proof, a guide, and a mercy, and with the question of who does greater wrong than those who reject the signs of God and turn away from them (6:157). The two verses belong together: the first states that the excuse has been removed, and the second states what the arrival of the Book means for the people who still refuse it.

The sūrah's closing section continues from there with the warning about the signs whose arrival is awaited and with the statement that belief will not benefit those who did not believe before (6:158). The verses in front of us supply the terms on which the audience's responsibility rests, and the reader is left with the Book in his hands and the claim that it has settled the matter.

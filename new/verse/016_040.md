## Sūrah al-Naḥl 16:40

> **If We ever will something ˹to exist˺, all We say is: “Be!” And it is!**

**Expanded Commentary**

**The Command Be And It Is**

Innamā qawlunā li-shay'in idhā aradnāhu an naqūla lahu kun fa-yakūn — our word for a thing when We will it is only to say to it Be and it is. Qawlunā, Our word, speech. Li-shay'in, for a thing, for something. Idhā aradnāhu, when We will it, wish it, intend it. An naqūla lahu kun, that We say to it Be. Fa-yakūn, and it is, becomes. Al-Ṭabarī explains that when Allah wills something to exist, whether resurrection, creation, provision, punishment, He only says to it kun, Be, and it is, without effort, without delay, without intermediary. The phrase innamā, only, emphasizes ease: Our word is only Be. This is same amr, command, that opened sūrah: command at hand, angels descend with spirit from command, stars subjected by command. Now command is explained: command is kun, Be. Al-Rāzī notes that kun fa-yakūn shows that Allah's will is executed by His word alone, without need for tools, time, or assistance, unlike human creation which requires effort and time.

**Why This Verse Comes After Resurrection**

Verses 38-39 said they swear Allah will not raise dead, but it is true promise to make clear what they differed and for disbelievers to know they were liars. Verse 40 says if We will something, We say Be and it is. The connection is that resurrection, which they think impossible, is easy for Allah: He only says Be and it is. If He could create heavens and earth, create humans from drop, create cattle, rain, crops, sea, mountains, all with kun, then resurrecting dead is even easier, as 30:27 says *It is He who originates creation then repeats it, and that is easier for Him* (30:27). The verse therefore answers denial of resurrection with ease of divine command. They think resurrection is difficult because they compare to human ability; Allah says for Us it is only Be and it is.

**The Power Of Kun**

Kun, Be, is imperative of kāna, to be, exist. It is shortest command in Arabic, two letters, kāf and nūn, yet it brings existence from non-existence. Al-Qurṭubī explains that kun is not that Allah speaks with sound like human, but that His will is executed immediately, without delay, as if He said Be. The phrase fa-yakūn, and it is, indicates immediate existence after command, without interval. Some commentators discuss whether kun is literal speech or metaphor for immediate execution; majority say it is literal speech that Allah speaks, but its nature is beyond human comprehension, as 42:51 says *It is not for any human that Allah should speak to him except by revelation or from behind veil or by sending messenger* (42:51). The point is not modality but ease and power: Allah's command does not need preparation.

**If We Ever Will Something**

Innamā qawlunā li-shay'in idhā aradnāhu — Our word for a thing when We will it. Shay'in, thing, is general, includes anything: resurrection, provision, punishment, guidance. Idhā aradnāhu, when We will it, indicates that will precedes command, and command executes will. Al-Zamakhsharī notes that aradnāhu, We willed it, shows that existence follows divine will, not random, not forced. The phrase li-shay'in, for a thing, indicates that thing is non-existent before will, then willed, then commanded Be, then exists. This sequence refutes those who think world is eternal without beginning; it had beginning by will and command. It also refutes those who think resurrection is impossible because thing no longer exists; if Allah willed it to exist first time from nothing, He can will it to exist second time from remains.

**A Verse That Summarizes Divine Power**

This verse summarizes divine power that has been theme since verse 3: He created heavens and earth, He created humans from drop, He created cattle, horses, rain, crops, night, day, sun, moon, stars, earth colors, sea, mountains, rivers, paths, landmarks, stars. All are by kun. The verse says Our word for a thing when We will it is only Be and it is, meaning all previous creations were by Be. This makes gratitude more profound: the same easy command that creates uncountable blessings also can resurrect you to ask about gratitude. The ease of Be also shows mercy: Allah can provide blessings easily, without hardship to Himself, yet He provides abundantly. The verse ends with exclamation in translation, indicating wonder: Be and it is, immediate, without delay.

**Linguistic Note On Qawl And Kun**

Qawlunā from q-w-l means our word, speech, saying. Li-shay'in, for a thing, from sh-y-ʾ, thing. Idhā aradnāhu, when We willed it, from r-w-d, to will, wish. An naqūla lahu, that We say to it, from q-w-l. Kun from k-w-n, to be, imperative Be. Fa-yakūn, and it is, from k-w-n, with fa- indicating immediate consequence. The verse's structure — Our word for a thing when We will it is only to say Be and it is — is concise, with innamā for exclusivity, only, emphasizing ease. The verse is often quoted to show divine power and to encourage reliance: if Allah wills something for you, it will be by Be, so trust His will, not your own planning alone. The brackets ˹to exist˺ in translation clarify that shay' refers to something to exist, not just any thing.


**A Lesson That Connects To Gratitude**

This verse, like all verses in Sūrat al-Niʿam, ultimately calls to gratitude. Whether it describes Gardens, death, waiting, consequences, or divine command, the purpose is to make the reader recognize blessings and respond with thanks. Al-Qurṭubī notes that every warning in this sūrah is also a blessing because it warns before punishment, giving chance to change. Every promise is also a blessing because it encourages good. The sūrah therefore weaves warning and promise, blessing and accountability, to cultivate a heart that is mindful, humble, and grateful. The reader who reflects on this verse with that mindset will find both knowledge and motivation to be among those who say goodness when asked what Lord revealed, and who are greeted with peace at death.

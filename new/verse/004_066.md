## Sūrah al-Nisāʾ 4:66

> If We had commanded them to sacrifice themselves or abandon their homes, none would have obeyed except for a few. Had they done what they were advised to do, it would have certainly been far better for them and more reassuring,

### Expanded Commentary

**The test of costly obedience**

Allah says that if believers had been commanded to sacrifice themselves or leave their homes, few would have obeyed. The statement exposes the gap between verbal allegiance and costly commitment. Most people imagine themselves brave until obedience threatens family, wealth, comfort, or status.

**“It would have been better”**

The verse does not glorify suffering for its own sake. It says that doing what they were advised would have been better and more strengthening. Obedience builds moral confidence: a person who acts on a difficult command discovers that fear did not deserve the authority he gave it. Communities also become more secure when members keep commitments rather than negotiating every duty around convenience.

The verse must not be used by unauthorized people to demand dangerous acts from others. The command discussed is divine and carried through legitimate prophetic authority. In contemporary life, costly obedience includes truth-telling, leaving unlawful income, protecting an abused dependent, and accepting a fair ruling against oneself.

The concluding assurance is that Allah knows capacity and does not command beyond it. The problem addressed is not an impossible burden but the tendency to resist even manageable duties because comfort has become a hidden idol.

**Cost reveals the seriousness of a command**

A person need not seek extraordinary suffering to prove faith. Ordinary costly obedience may include leaving an unlawful job, telling the truth about a relative, or protecting someone unpopular. The verse asks whether comfort has become so authoritative that even a reasonable duty feels intolerable.

## Sūrah al-Nisāʾ 4:66

> If We had commanded them to sacrifice themselves or abandon their homes, none would have obeyed except for a few. Had they done what they were advised to do, it would have certainly been far better for them and more reassuring,

**Expanded Commentary**

**A Hard Command They Would Not Have Borne**

The verse states *wa law annā katabnā ʿalayhim an iqtulū anfusakum aw ikhrujū min diyārikum mā faʿalūhu illā qalīlun minhum* — if We had commanded them to sacrifice themselves or abandon their homes, none would have obeyed except for a few. The sūrah has just set an oath that true belief requires accepting the Prophet ﷺ as judge without inward resistance and with whole submission (4:65). That oath is demanding, yet this verse says a still harder command — to kill themselves or to leave their homes — would have been obeyed by even fewer. *Anfusakum* — yourselves — was read as sacrificing their own lives, and *min diyārikum* — from your homes — as exodus, as the migrants endured when they left Makkah. The sūrah therefore measures the community against a harsher test and finds only a few would have passed, which is not condemnation of all but realism about weakness. The same weakness explained as *“humankind was created weak”* (4:28) and met with lightening (4:28) is shown here as historical fact: given a command that cut at life and home, most would have held back. The verse was linked to hypocrites who claimed belief yet turned from the Book and Messenger (4:60–61); the harder command exposes that softer claims of obedience already falter, so harder claims would have failed almost entirely.

**Had They Done What They Were Advised**

The alternative *wa law annahum faʿalū mā yūʿaẓūna bihi la kāna khayran lahum wa ashadda tathbītā* — had they done what they were advised to do, it would have been far better for them and more reassuring — names the better path that was actually asked. *Mā yūʿaẓūna bihi* — what they were advised, what they were exhorted to do — gathers the noble exhortations the sūrah has given: return trusts to rightful owners and judge with fairness (4:58), obey God and the Messenger and refer disputes (4:59), accept the Prophet ﷺ as judge without *ḥaraj* and with *taslīm* (4:65), avoid major sins (4:31), and trade by mutual consent (4:29). None of those require leaving homes or self-sacrifice; they require keeping limits about wealth and marriage, referral to the Book, and obedience where obedience is the purpose of sending (4:64). To have done that *would have been khayr* — better — in both worlds, and *ashadda tathbītā* — more reassuring, firmer in grounding. *Tathbīt* — to make firm, to reassure — was read as inner firmness, steadiness after the wavering that marks the hypocrite who lags (4:72) and the one who fears people as God should be feared (4:77). The sūrah elsewhere says *“if you avoid major sins We will cover lesser ones and admit you to honour”* (4:31); doing what was advised is that avoidance made active, and firmness is its inner reward.

**Better and More Firm Than a Harder Test**

The verse contrasts two goods: doing the harder hypothetical command and doing the actual advice. The hypothetical — sacrifice or exodus — would have been a great test, but the verse says few would have done it, so its good would have been rare. The actual advice — what they were exhorted to do, the Book’s limits and the Messenger’s judgment — was within reach, and doing it would have been *khayran lahum* — better for them — than failing the harder one, and *ashadda tathbītā* — more reassuring — than the anxiety that comes from claiming belief while seeking false judges (4:60). Early authorities noted that the sūrah’s actual commands are lightened because the human was created weak (4:28), and lightened law obeyed is better than heavy law ignored. The hypocrites sought honour through alliance with *ṭāghūt* and rejoiced when not present at disaster (4:72); to have done what was advised — return trusts, judge fairly, come to the Messenger after wronging themselves to find Him ever Accepting (4:64) — would have replaced that shallow relief with deep reassurance. The reassurance is not only moral but practical: a community that keeps shares and trusts has less to fear from disaster, and a heart that submits without resistance has less inner constriction.

**A Few Who Would Have Obeyed Even the Hard**

The phrase *illā qalīlun minhum* — except for a few — was read as the sincere among them, the same few acknowledged after condemnation for distorting words (4:46) and after the division where some believed in him (4:55). Those few would have obeyed even a command to sacrifice or emigrate, as the true migrants did and as the few who accepted the Prophet ﷺ as judge without *ḥaraj* do now. The verse does not name them to praise them as exclusive; it names them to show that obedience at the hard level exists, which makes obedience at the lighter level more shameful to refuse. If a few would have given life and home for God, then all can give a sixth to a mother or a dowry to a wife or a referral to the Book. The sūrah’s ethic is therefore scaled: the same willingness that would have left a home is asked to leave a claim that exceeds a limit. The one who would not leave a home when commanded would also not leave a false judge when commanded to reject him; the one who would have left would also return a trust. The verse therefore measures sincerity by readiness, and it tells the majority that the better and more firm path remains open without requiring the hardest test.

**Advice That Firms the Heart**

Placed after the oath of faith as submission (4:65) and before the promise of great reward and guidance to the straight path for doing what was advised (4:67–68), this verse is the sūrah’s pastoral diagnosis for wavering hearts. It acknowledges that a community learning to divide estates with *“whether little or much”* (4:7), to guard the orphan’s property with witnesses (4:6), and to fight in God’s cause for the oppressed (4:75) will find the commands heavy if measured against a still heavier command that only a few would bear. To have done what was advised — come to the Messenger, seek forgiveness, accept judgment, avoid major sins, trade by mutual consent — would have been better and more reassuring, not because the advised acts are small, but because they are the deeds that make the heart firm. The next verses will say that doing them brings great reward by grace and guidance to the straight path (4:67–68); this verse says they also bring *tathbīt* — firmness — now, where the hypocrite feels only the tug between claiming belief and seeking *ṭāghūt*. For the weak for whom burdens were lightened, the better and firmer path is not the imagined heroic sacrifice alone, but the daily exhortation lived, which steadies the step before the harder road is ever required.

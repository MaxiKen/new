## Sūrah al-Baqarah 2:166

> ˹Consider the Day˺ when those who misled others will disown their followers—when they face the torment—and the bonds that united them will be cut off.

**Expanded Commentary**

**The Scene Described**

The verse describes a moment in the hereafter when those who were followed disown those who followed them, and it states that the punishment will be visible to them. The sentence begins with a word that refers the reader to a time already mentioned, which is the moment of the punishment described in the previous verse.

The Book returns to this scene in several chapters, and the community's teachers read the passages together when they spoke about the accountability of leaders. What the sentence establishes at the outset is that the relationship between a leader and his followers does not survive into the hereafter in the form it had in this life.

**The Leaders and the Followers**

The commentators identified the two groups in several ways. Some took the first to be the leaders of idolatry and the second their adherents, others applied the description to the satans and the human beings who obeyed them, and others read the sentence as a statement about everyone who led or followed in the ways of error.

The readings do not conflict, since the Book's descriptions of the hereafter often apply to a class of people rather than to a single group. What the sentence states is that the relationship of following, whatever form it took, ends in a disavowal, and the reader is shown that the bond between a misleader and his followers is not what either party imagined it to be.

**The Meaning of Recourse Cut Off**

The phrase rendered as recourse comes from a word that means a rope or a means by which something is obtained, and the commentators explained that what will be severed is everything the two groups relied on. Some named the bonds of love and friendship and the ties of kinship that had united them in the world, and others named the deeds on which a person might have hoped to rely.

The image of a rope is used elsewhere in the Book for the means by which a person holds on to safety, and the sentence states that the rope is cut. What the passage establishes is that the relationships and the deeds that people treat as security in this life are not security in the next unless they are of the kind the Book commends, and the reader is left with the question of what he is holding.

**Disavowal in the Book**

The Book describes the disavowal between leaders and followers in other passages, and it states that on the Day of Resurrection the followers and the followed will curse one another. It also reports the words of Satan to those who followed him, in which he admits that the promise he made was empty and tells them that he has no power over them.

The verses in this passage belong to that group, and the community's teachers used them when they warned against following a person without regard for the truth. What the Book presents is a scene in which the authority of a leader evaporates at the moment when it might have been useful, and the reader is shown what the relationship was worth.

**The Isolation of the Individual**

The sentence describes a disavowal that takes place in the presence of the punishment, and the effect is to leave every person alone with his own account. The Book's teaching about the hereafter consistently removes the supports on which a person leans in this world, and this verse removes the support of the group.

The reader is therefore given a picture of the moment when a person discovers that the position he occupied in relation to others no longer helps him. What the sūrah supplies in this passage is not merely a description of the punishment but an account of how the relationships of this life are to be understood, and the verse asks the reader to assess the company he keeps.

The Book’s account of the hereafter repeatedly removes the supports on which people rely in this life, whether they are property, kinship, or the standing that a group confers. The sentence contributes the loss of the group, and the reader is shown a scene in which every person stands with his own record. The community’s teachers read the passage as a description of what awaits those who place their confidence in other human beings, and the verse leaves the reader to examine where his own confidence rests.

**The Verse's Connection to the Previous Sentence**

The verse follows a statement about the wrongdoers seeing that power belongs to God, and it supplies the scene in which that knowledge arrives. The Book has just described what the offenders will understand; the sentence here describes who else will be present and what will happen between them.

The sequence gives the passage its force, and the reader is carried from a general statement about divine authority to a specific account of human relations under its judgment. The community's teachers used the pair of verses when they discussed the responsibility of those who lead others astray, and the passage continues with the words the followers will speak.

**What the Verse Establishes**

The sentence establishes that the bonds of following are severed in the hereafter, that the means on which people rely are cut off, and that the punishment is seen by those it concerns. The reader is given a description that applies to any relationship in which one person leads another away from the truth, and the verse that follows records what the followers say when they see what has happened.

The sentence also supplies the community with a standard for the relationships it forms, since the Book describes the fate of those connections with such clarity. What the verse asks the reader to consider is whether the person he follows will be able to help him when the means of help have been removed. The community’s teachers used the passage in their warnings about those who lead others into error, and the sentence gives their teaching its ground.

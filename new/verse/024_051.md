## Sūrah an-Nūr 24:51

> The only response of the ˹true˺ believers, when they are called to Allah and His Messenger so he may judge between them, is to say, “We hear and obey.” It is they who will ˹truly˺ succeed.

**Expanded Commentary**

**The Exclusive Formula of Believing Conduct: innamā kāna qawla al-muʾminīn**

The divine discourse presents an immaculate portrait of authentic faith that stands in absolute contrast to the evasive cowardice of the hypocrites: *innamā kāna qawla al-muʾminīna idhā duʿū ilā Allāhi wa-rasūlihī li-yaḥkuma baynahum an yaqūlū samiʿnā wa-aṭaʿnā*. The restrictive particle *innamā* (only / exclusively) establishes a definitive boundary around genuine belief.

For the true believers, there are no alternative reactions, no hesitations, and no opportunistic deliberations when summoned to divine and prophetic arbitration. While the hypocrites calculated whether the verdict would favor their pockets (vv. 48–49), the believers possess a singular, instinctive reflex. The summons to God and His Messenger does not trigger panic, resentment, or legal maneuvers; it evokes immediate, joyful compliance. Faith is not an intellectual debate over whether divine law is advantageous in a particular dispute; it is the settled conviction that divine wisdom is inherently just, benevolent, and sovereign over all human affairs, demanding total trust and unwavering loyalty across every sphere of life.

**The Readiness of Submission: samiʿnā wa-aṭaʿnā**

The verbal response of the faithful encapsulates the entire ethos of Islam: *samiʿnā wa-aṭaʿnā*, we hear and we obey! This two-part formula represents the complete synthesis of cognitive reception and physical execution.

First, *samiʿnā* (we hear) is not passive auditory reception, but attentive, respectful, and unbiased listening that seeks to comprehend divine commands without prejudice or resistance. Second, *aṭaʿnā* (we obey) is instantaneous, active conformity of the will and limbs to the judicial ruling, executed with cheerful readiness. This response echoes the covenant of the righteous companions described in 2:285, in stark contrast to the rebellious defiance of ancient Israel recounted in 2:93 (*samiʿnā wa-ʿaṣaynā*—we hear and we disobey). For the believer, hearing and obeying occur as a unified spiritual reflex, closing the dangerous gap between hearing truth and executing duty, leaving no room for hypocrisy.

**The Dignified Demeanor Before Judicial Authority**

The phrase *li-yaḥkuma baynahum* (so he may judge between them) highlights the moral dignity of the believer in the courtroom. When an authentic Muslim enters the prophetic court, their primary desire is not personal victory or commercial profit, but the manifestation of objective truth and justice.

If the Prophet ﷺ rules in their favor, they praise God for vindicating their legitimate rights and remain humble; if the Prophet rules against them, ordering them to surrender disputed land or repay a debt, they accept the ruling with serenity and immediate compliance. They realize that losing a temporary worldly asset under divine justice is an infinitely lighter burden than carrying a stolen penny into the terrifying scrutiny of the Day of Judgment. Their submission before the bench is an act of high worship, transforming a civil litigation into a profound manifestation of monotheistic piety, honoring God's justice above mortal desires and temporary worldly wealth.

**Contrast Between the Believer and the Hypocrite**

Placing verse 51 immediately after verses 48–50 establishes a pedagogical masterpiece of moral polarity. In verse 48, when the hypocrites are summoned to court, they reflexively turn away in dread (*muʿriḍūn*); in verse 49, they run submissively only when the verdict guarantees them profit (*yaʾtū ilayhi mudhʿinīn*); in verse 50, their hearts are diagnosed with disease, doubt, and paranoia.

Verse 51 sweeps away this pathetic psychological instability with the steady, unshakeable integrity of the faithful. The believer does not divide court appearances into profitable or unprofitable occasions. Whether winning or losing, rich or poor, plaintiff or defendant, the believer's stance remains unalterably fixed upon *samiʿnā wa-aṭaʿnā*. This unwavering consistency exposes the hypocrite as an unstable opportunist and crowns the believer as a person of transcendent principle who cannot be bought by worldly advantage, standing as a pillar of truth.

**True Prosperity Defined: wa-ulāʾika humu al-mufliḥūn**

The verse culminates in an emphatic eschatological and worldly verdict: *wa-ulāʾika humu al-mufliḥūn*, and it is they who will truly succeed. The noun *mufliḥūn* derives from the root *f-l-ḥ*, which primarily denotes agricultural flourishing, cultivation, and harvesting abundance (*falāḥ*).

In Qur'anic usage, *falāḥ* is the ultimate, multidimensional prosperity that encompasses the salvation of the soul from eternal torment, admission into the eternal gardens of paradise, and spiritual tranquility in this mortal life. The syntax employs the demonstrative pronoun *ulāʾik* alongside the isolating pronoun *hum*, establishing that true success belongs exclusively to those who hear and obey divine arbitration, entering eternal life with unblemished records. The opportunist may imagine that evading the court and retaining stolen wealth is a clever financial triumph; the Qur'an exposes such illicit gains as poisoned fuel for hellfire, affirming that authentic enduring success belongs solely to those who bow before divine law, harvesting eternal peace, honor, and celestial joy.

**The Sociological Strength of an Obedient Community**

Beyond individual salvation, verse 51 provides the foundational blueprint for a stable, resilient, and just civilization. A society where citizens obey the law only when it serves their private self-interest will inevitably degenerate into corruption, social distrust, and tyranny.

Conversely, a community permeated by the spirit of *samiʿnā wa-aṭaʿnā* builds an invincible foundation of social cohesion and mutual responsibility. Legal disputes are resolved swiftly without lingering blood feuds, commercial contracts are honored with sacred fidelity, the weak and vulnerable are protected from predatory elites, and judicial rulings are respected without civil unrest. By establishing that obedience to divine justice is the highest civic and religious duty, the revelation creates a culture of mutual trust and collective righteousness, allowing justice and peace to flourish across generations, establishing a society grounded in transcendent truth.

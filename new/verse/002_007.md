## Sūrah al-Baqarah 2:7

> Allah has sealed their hearts and their hearing, and their sight is covered. They will suffer a tremendous punishment.

**Expanded Commentary**

**The Heart That Does the Knowing**

Modern readers bring a division to this verse that the Quran does not observe: the heart as the seat of feeling, the mind as the seat of thought. In the Quran, *qalb* is the organ of consciousness, understanding, and faith, and the proof is in the places the word appears. Serenity is *sent down upon the hearts of the believers so that they may increase even more in their faith* (48:4). Revelation is brought *to your heart by Allah’s Will* (2:97). Failure is located in the same place: *it is not the eyes that are blind, but it is the hearts in the chests that grow blind* (22:46).

The consequence for this verse is that a sealed heart is not a heart that has stopped feeling; it is a heart that has stopped understanding. The faculty being removed is comprehension, and the state described is cognitive before it is emotional. The most chilling description of the condition in the Quran makes exactly this point, listing the organs and then denying their function: *They have hearts they do not understand with, eyes they do not see with, and ears they do not hear with. They are like cattle. In fact, they are even less guided* (7:179).

**Three Faculties, Two Verbs**

The verse distributes its verbs carefully. Hearts and hearing are *sealed* — closed with a seal, the way a document is closed, so that nothing more goes in or out. Sight receives a different word: a *covering* lies over the eyes. A seal is permanent in its own logic; a covering is a thing placed over something that is still there.

The distribution also follows the order in which revelation arrives. Hearing is named before sight, because the Quran is something heard before it is something read, and the sūrah's argument throughout assumes an audience that has listened. The three faculties named are the complete set of instruments a person has for receiving a message: an inward faculty that judges, an ear that takes in, an eye that confirms. With all three disabled, nothing about the message can reach the person it was sent to, and that is what the verse says has happened.

**Cause or Consequence: The Dispute**

The argument this verse has generated turns on a single question: did the seal produce the refusal, or did the refusal produce the seal? Read the first way, faith is not a product of human will but of a prior divine decree, and the warning is a formality. Read the second way, the seal is the outcome of a settled rejection — spiritual loss incurred rather than inflicted.

The textual evidence for the second reading is strong, because the Quran rarely mentions sealing without naming the disposition it answers. *This is how Allah seals the hearts of those unwilling to know ˹the truth˺* (30:59) makes unwillingness the condition. *This is how Allah seals the heart of every arrogant tyrant* (40:35) does the same. Another sūrah states the sequence outright: *We turn their hearts and eyes away ˹from the truth˺ as they refused to believe at first* (6:110). And the most explicit account of the mechanism says the damage is self-inflicted: *In fact, their hearts have been stained by all ˹the evil˺ they used to commit* (83:14).

The first reading has its own evidence, and it is the verse under commentary: God is the subject of the verb. He seals. Any account that makes the seal purely a natural consequence has to accommodate that. What the two readings share is more important than what divides them — both hold that the state is real, that it is now fixed, and that no human being can reverse it. *Imagine if Allah were to take away your hearing or sight, or seal your hearts—who else other than Allah could restore it?* (6:46).

**Rust, Hardness, Disease**

The Quran does not use one metaphor for this condition but several, and the variety is itself informative. Hearts are stained by accumulated wrongdoing (83:14); they *became hardened like a rock or even harder* (2:74); a sickness sits in them and is allowed to increase (2:10); and here they are sealed. Each image describes something acquired rather than implanted, and the images form a sequence: repeated refusal leaves a residue, the residue hardens, the hardening becomes a disease, and the disease ends in closure.

The sūrah states the decisive step in the sequence a few lines on: *There is sickness in their hearts, and Allah ˹only˺ lets their sickness increase* (2:10). The increase follows the sickness; the sickness is already present. A reader who takes these metaphors together gets a psychology rather than a decree — a description of what sustained refusal does to the instrument that was meant to recognise the truth.

**The Excuse the Verse Removes**

A sealed heart is the most convenient defence available to someone who does not want to believe, and the Quran records the defence being offered. *They say, “Our hearts are unreceptive!” In fact, Allah has condemned them for their disbelief* (2:88). The reply does not dispute the description of their hearts; it disputes the use being made of it. The condition is real and it is not an excuse.

Two features of the verse protect that conclusion. First, the seal and the punishment are named separately: the seal is a loss already incurred, the punishment is a sentence still to come, and the text does not conflate them. A reading in which God removes the capacity and then punishes for its absence would make the second clause unjust, and the verse keeps them distinct precisely to avoid it. Second, the seal's placement after the warning in the previous verse means the person was addressed before the closure: the message arrived, was refused, and the refusal was allowed to become permanent.

**What a Reader Is Meant to Do With This**

The verse is not written for the sealed. It is written for anyone who might become so, which is everyone. The Quran's response to the possibility is not speculation about election but a prayer: *My Lord! Increase me in knowledge* (20:114). The opposite of a sealed heart is a heart that keeps asking to be opened.

The mechanism of the closure is stated in a way that makes the danger legible: *do not be like those who forgot Allah, so He made them forget themselves* (59:19). The forgetting is the person's; the consequence is God's; and the two are one process. What makes the warning sharp is that no one in this condition recognises it — the sūrah turns immediately to people who are certain they believe and are wrong about it.

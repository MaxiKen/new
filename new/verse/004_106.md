## Sūrah al-Nisāʾ 4:106

> And seek Allah’s forgiveness—indeed, Allah is All-Forgiving, Most Merciful.

**Expanded Commentary**

**And Seek God’s Forgiveness**

The verse states *wa astaghfiri Allāha* — and seek God’s forgiveness, ask Him to forgive, to cover and efface. *Istaghfara* — to seek *maghfirah* — was explained as requesting covering of fault and wiping of trace, as the sūrah has shown that even religious error before prayer is covered by purification with earth where water lacks (4:43) and that mistaken killing is covered by freeing or consecutive fasting as repentance (4:92). The command was addressed to the Prophet ﷺ directly after *“do not be an advocate for the deceitful”* (4:105), showing that even the thought of inclining to defend the deceitful, or the distress of having nearly done so, calls for seeking forgiveness. Through him the command reaches every judge and every believer: where judgment was to be by what God shows (4:105) and not by clan, the fear of having favoured tie over truth requires seeking cover. The sūrah has taught that if wrongdoers came after wronging themselves seeking forgiveness and the Messenger prayed for them they would find Him ever Accepting of Repentance (4:64), and that whoever does evil or wrongs themselves then seeks forgiveness will find Him All-Forgiving, Most Merciful (4:110). To *seek forgiveness* is therefore not an admission of having betrayed trust but the sūrah’s discipline of staying under cover while judging.

**Indeed God Is All-Forgiving, Most Merciful**

The seal *inna Allāha kāna Ghafūran Raḥīmā* — indeed God is, was ever, All-Forgiving, Most Merciful — was explained as reason for the command, not merely its afterthought. *Ghafūr* — All-Forgiving, much covering — and *Raḥīm* — Most Merciful, ever Merciful — have sealed the wages of strivers (4:96), the emigrant’s secured reward (4:100), and the hope for the helpless (4:99) with *ʿAfuww Ghafūr*, and the law of mistake (4:92); here they seal the judge’s recourse. *Kāna* — was, is ever — was read as timeless attribute: He was forgiving before the deception occurred, before advocacy was even considered. To name these two names right after prohibiting advocacy for the deceitful was explained as opening the door that advocacy would close: the deceitful themselves, if they abandon plotting by night what displeases Him (4:108) and seek forgiveness, will also find Him All-Forgiving, as the next verses make clear that the door is for all who commit evil then seek — *“whoever commits evil… then seeks God’s forgiveness will find Him”* (4:110). The *innā* — indeed — was read as assurance where shame might keep one from seeking: the judge who almost defended deceit and the deceitful who were defended both have the same recourse — to seek.

**A Short Verse Between Two Prohibitions**

The verse’s brevity — *“seek forgiveness, God is All-Forgiving, Most Merciful”* — placed between *“do not be advocate for the deceitful”* (4:105) and *“do not advocate for those who wrong themselves”* (4:107) was explained as deliberate interruption. The sūrah inserts seeking forgiveness between the two advocacy warnings so that judgment is not left as mere negation — do not defend — but as positive turn — seek cover. The pattern matches the sūrah’s earlier insertion: between asking *“was not God’s earth spacious?”* (4:97) and promising many havens (4:100) the sūrah inserted exception and hope for the helpless (4:98–99). Here between two warnings to advocates the sūrah inserts the remedy that corrects both advocate and wrongdoer. Classical notes added that the sequence shows that the advocate’s fault, if he inclined, is not equal to the deceitful’s treachery (4:107) in degree, but both need the same Names; the judge’s seeking is for inclination, the wrongdoer’s for act. The verse therefore holds the community that was divided into two groups about hypocrites (4:88) together under one covering: whichever group erred by advocating, the path back is seeking.

**Forgiveness as the Guarantor of Fair Judgment**

To command seeking forgiveness where the Book was sent to judge by what God shows (4:105) was read as guaranteeing fair judgment, not undermining it. A judge who must not be an advocate for the deceitful carries pressure from kin, from secret talks that are mostly not good (4:114), from the fear of humiliation where rain and illness already excuse laying aside weapons (4:102). Seeking forgiveness — *istighfār* — was explained as the inward counterpart to the outward precaution — *khudhū ḥidhrakum* — still taken even when arms are laid aside. Where vigilance guards prayer, seeking forgiveness guards judgment. The sūrah has said that the good is multiplied many times over by grace (4:40) and that God does not like the stingy who withhold bounty (4:37); the most withheld bounty in disputes is fairness, and seeking forgiveness restores it by recalling that the Judge over judges is All-Forgiving, whose forgiveness the advocate will himself need on the Day when advocacy will be before God (4:109). For the weak this is the lightest discipline: where terrain required guarding prayer (4:102), judging requires guarding intention by seeking cover, and the Coverer is ever Forgiving, ever Merciful.

**A Two-Word Command That Opens the Next Three Verses**

Though only two phrases, the command opens the next three verses: do not advocate for those who wrong themselves (4:107), they hide from people but cannot hide from God where they plot by night (4:108), and the advocates now pleading for them in this world will not plead for them on Resurrection Day (4:109). To seek forgiveness before that exposure is to move from hiding to disclosure. The sūrah that warns *“do not kill yourselves, God is ever Merciful”* (4:29) and that promises *“He wills to lighten burdens, you were created weak”* (4:28) here lightens the judge’s burden: where you feared to judge between kin and Jew, between host and emigrant, between treaty people and hostile, seek, and you will find cover, because the Book was sent in truth not to burden you beyond seeing, but to show you what to see, and the One who shows is All-Forgiving, Most Merciful. The next verse will apply that seeking to the advocates themselves: do not advocate for the self-wrongers, for He does not like the deceitful sinner, and seeking is still open (4:110).

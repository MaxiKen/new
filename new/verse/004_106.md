## Sūrah al-Nisāʾ 4:106

> And seek Allah’s forgiveness—indeed, Allah is All-Forgiving, Most Merciful.

### Expanded Commentary

**Seeking forgiveness after judgment**

The instruction to seek Allah’s forgiveness follows the warning against advocacy for deceit. Even a person who recognizes truth, judges fairly, and then regrets past weakness can return to God. The verse also places the Prophet’s human process under divine mercy: revelation protects him from injustice, while forgiveness remains the proper response to personal shortcomings.

Repentance in a legal context includes correction. If an innocent person was accused, the false claim must be withdrawn; if property was taken, it must be returned; if public words caused harm, the repair should reach those who heard them. Allah’s mercy is not a substitute for earthly restoration but the source of strength to undertake it.

**Forgiveness follows honest correction**

Seeking forgiveness should make a person more willing to admit error publicly when public error caused harm. A judge, teacher, or leader who corrects the record models the mercy requested in the verse. The promise of forgiveness is strongest when the sinner no longer protects the false story.

## Sūrah Āli ʿImrān 3:98

> Say, ˹O Prophet,˺ “O People of the Book! Why do you deny the revelations of Allah, when Allah is a Witness to what you do?”

**Expanded Commentary**

**A Rebuke Addressed by Name**

The verse instructs the Prophet to address the People of the Book directly and to ask them why they reject God's revelations. The address is courteous in form and severe in content, which is the Book's characteristic way of handling a community that holds a scripture.

The command to say the words places them in the mouth of the messenger rather than in a narrative about him, so that the reader hears the rebuke as speech rather than as a report. The Book uses the formula at several points in this sūrah, and the repetition builds the sense of an argument conducted in public, with the community listening to both sides.

**What Is Being Denied**

The phrase used for what they deny is the Book's ordinary term for God's signs, which covers both the verses of the revelation and the evidences that establish it. In this setting the two senses converge, since the revelation being rejected was itself claimed to be confirmed by what the People of the Book already possessed.

The commentators understood the denial as directed at the messenger and his message, which the Book treats as denial of the sender. That is why the question's form is a rebuke rather than a request for information: the answer is already known to the ones being asked, and the Book makes plain elsewhere that they recognise the messenger as they recognise their own children while a group among them hides what they know (2:146).

**The Witness Clause**

The sentence does not stop at the question. It adds that God is a witness to what they do, and the clause is what gives the verse its force. A person may deny something and be unknown to everyone but himself; the verse states that the record is being kept.

The Book closes its rebukes with statements of God's knowledge so often that the pattern is a feature of its style. The effect here is to remove the two consolations available to a person conducting such an argument: that the denial is private, and that its motive is hidden. What the community being addressed is told is that the matter is not between them and the messenger, and the sentence places the account of their conduct in the hands of the one whose revelations they reject. The next verse in the passage repeats the pattern with a different verb, and the pairing keeps the reader's attention on the conduct rather than on the denials themselves.

**Recognition and Concealment in the Sūrah**

The sūrah has been developing a distinction between not knowing and not acknowledging, and this verse depends on it. A community that had received the earlier scriptures was in a position to test the claim being made to it, and the Book's argument throughout the passage assumes the materials were in its hands.

That is why the Book's harshest descriptions are reserved for concealment. The sūrah says of a group among them that they mix the truth with falsehood and hide the truth knowingly (3:71), and another passage states that those who hide the clear proofs after they have been made clear for humanity will be condemned by God and by all who condemn (2:159). The verse above is the shortest form of the same charge, and it leaves the reader with a question that the accused cannot answer without either admitting the recognition or denying the record.

**Why the Book Asks Rather Than Declares**

The question form appears throughout the sūrah's dealings with the People of the Book, and its repetition is deliberate. Three separate verses in this chapter ask the same community why they do or deny something, with the formula held nearly constant and the subject changing.

A declaration would tell the reader what the verdict is; a question makes him supply it, and it does so in language he cannot dispute without denying what his own knowledge tells him. The Book's method here is that of a trial in which the accused is invited to speak, and the invitation is extended until the silence becomes the answer. What follows the question in each case is a statement of the stake involved, whether it is the truth of the signs, the straightness of the path, or the fate of the believers.

**The Community Being Addressed and Its Successors**

The verse names a community that existed in the Prophet's own time, and its abiding lesson concerns a type of conduct rather than a group of persons. A community that possesses a revelation is being asked why it denies a further revelation that its own book anticipated.

The question is addressed to the heirs of a message as much as to the people who first heard it. The sūrah has already warned this community about turning back after having received proof, and the same warning runs beneath the rebuke delivered to the earlier community, which is part of why the passage reads as a warning with two audiences. What the Book asks is not whether the recipients are learned but whether they are truthful with what they hold.

**The Verse in the Passage's Movement**

The passage has turned from the argument about Abraham and the House to a series of direct addresses, and this verse opens the sequence. The change of voice marks a change of subject.

The evidence has been presented, the claims about ancestry have been answered, and the obligations attached to the House have been stated. What remains is the response of the people who had been arguing, and the Book asks them about it to their faces. The verses that follow pursue the same interrogation from different angles, and the reader is brought through the dispute to the point where only the conduct of the parties is left to discuss.

## Sūrah al-Anʿām 6:160

> Whoever comes with a good deed will be rewarded tenfold. But whoever comes with a bad deed will be punished for only one. None will be wronged.

**Expanded Commentary**

**The Reward of the Good Deed**

Whoever comes with a good deed will be rewarded tenfold. The verse states the measure of the reward, and the Book's accounts of recompense attach a multiplication to the good deed in several places. The sūrah of the Cow compares the spending of wealth in the cause of God to a grain that sprouts into seven ears, each ear containing a hundred grains, and it states that God multiplies as He wills for whom He wills (2:261); the sūrah of the Women says that God never wrongs anyone by an atom's weight and that He multiplies the good deed and gives a great reward (4:40); and the sūrah of the Stories says that whoever comes with a good deed will be rewarded with what is better (28:84).

The multiplication is the Book's statement about the generosity of the reward, and it is stated alongside the exactness of the punishment. Both measures are given here in a single sentence, and the arrangement presents the accounting as more generous than it is strict.

**The Punishment of the Bad Deed**

But whoever comes with a bad deed will be punished for only one. The sentence states the limit of the punishment, and the Book's statements about justice repeat the same principle. The sūrah of the Women states that God wrongs no one by an atom's weight (4:40); the sūrah of Fussilat says that whoever does good does so for his own benefit and whoever does evil does so to his own loss, and that the Lord is never unjust to His servants (41:46); and the sūrah of the Cave describes the record of deeds presented on the day of judgement with the declaration that the Lord wrongs no one (18:49).

The Book's presentation of the punishment as measured and limited belongs to its account of God's justice. The multiplication attached to the good deed is not paralleled on the other side, and the sentence in front of us states the difference explicitly. The reader is left with the two measures stated side by side.

**None Will Be Wronged**

None will be wronged. The clause states the conclusion of the two measures, and the Book uses the same declaration at the end of its passages about the reckoning. The phrase appears in the sūrah of the Sand Dunes in the sentence about the ranking of the two groups, and the sūrah of the Cow uses it in its account of the day when no soul will be wronged in the least. The assurance is stated here in the shortest possible form.

The declaration answers a question that the sūrah's audience might have carried. The Book has described the destruction of the earlier communities, the punishment of the people who turned away from the signs, and the judgment that rests with God alone; the sentence in front of us states that the accounting will be exact, which is the assurance the declaration provides.

**What the Verse Says About Individual Responsibility**

The verse turns from the divisions of the communities to the deed of the individual, and the arrangement is deliberate. The previous verse left the judgment of the sects with God; the verse in front of us states what each person's own deed will produce. The community's history and the standing of its groups are not what a person is asked about, and the sentence states the arrangement in terms that leave no room for the appeal to a community.

The Book's addresses to the individual appear throughout the sūrah. It stated that every soul will be rewarded according to what it earned, and it described the assembled jinn and humans testifying against themselves. The same principle is stated here at the close of the chapter's argument, and the two measures it gives show how the reckoning will be conducted.

**What the Verse Says About the Measure of Deeds**

The sentence establishes that the accounting is conducted by measure rather than by comparison. The good deed is increased and the bad deed is answered by its own weight, and the relative standing of the people who performed them is not part of the calculation. The Book's descriptions of the day of judgment include the same principle, and the sūrah of the Cave's presentation of the record shows each person confronted with his own scroll.

The Book's teaching on this point appears in its instructions to the community as well. The sūrah of the Chambers states that the most noble among people in the sight of God is the most mindful, and the sūrah of the Night Journey states that each person's record will be brought out for him to read. The measure is stated here in a single sentence, and the reader is left with the two sides of it.

**The Place of the Verse in the Sūrah's Closing**

The verse stands between the statement about the divisions of the communities (6:159) and the declaration of the Prophet about the path to which he has been guided (6:161). The three verses form the sūrah's closing movement: the schisms are left to God, the individual's deed is measured, and the Prophet declares the guidance he has received and the worship he performs.

The verses that follow the declaration include the Prophet's statement that his prayer, his worship, his life, and his death belong to God (6:162), the declaration that God has no partner (6:163), and the final verse about the ranks in which people have been placed (6:165). What this sentence states is the measure that underlies the whole closing passage, and the reader is left with the accounting that the sūrah has been describing since its opening verses.

**The Transition to the Next Verse**

The next verse gives the Prophet's declaration of the guidance he has received and of the tradition he follows (6:161). The declaration states what the Prophet has received, and it answers the sūrah's account of the audience's religion with a statement of the religion he follows.

The two verses stand in contrast with each other. The audience is left with its divisions and its own accounting, and the Prophet is described as having been placed on a path with a direction and a lineage that reaches back to Abraham. The terms of the accountability are stated here, and the verse that follows states the content of the guidance.

## Sūrah an-Nūr 24:7

> and a fifth oath that Allah may condemn him if he is lying.

**Expanded Commentary**

**The Climactic Fifth Oath: The Seal of Conscience**

The procedural sequence of marital accusation reaches its decisive threshold in the fifth declaration: *wa-al-khāmisatu anna laʿnata Allāhi ʿalayhi in kāna mina al-kādhibīn*. The first four oaths fulfilled the evidentiary requirement, standing in place of four external witnesses. The fifth oath, however, introduces an entirely different dimension: self-imprecation. The husband explicitly calls down the divine curse upon his own soul if he has fabricated the accusation.

This structural separation between four declarative oaths and a fifth imprecatory oath transforms the proceeding from an ordinary legal dispute into an existential trial before the divine throne. In the four initial oaths, the man asserts his truthfulness; in the final oath, he voluntarily surrenders himself to divine retribution if he is lying. The fifth oath acts as the ultimate seal of conscience, demanding that the accuser stake his eternal destiny upon the truth of his charge, closing any psychological retreat. The soul of the accuser is placed directly on the scales of divine judgment.

**The Nature and Terror of Divine Condemnation (laʿnah)**

In the Qur'anic lexicon, the term *laʿnah* signifies far more than temporary anger or verbal rebuke; it denotes absolute expulsion and banishment from divine mercy. To be cursed by God is to be stripped of spiritual protection, abandoned in this worldly life, and consigned to everlasting torment in the Hereafter. A believer regards divine mercy as the singular hope for salvation.

To deliberately invoke the curse of God upon oneself is the most terrifying utterance a human being can formulate. Classical scholars observed that an individual of sound faith would far prefer enduring eighty lashes, public disgrace, or worldly imprisonment than uttering a phrase that invites total exile from God's grace. By installing *laʿnah* as the culmination of the husband's testimony, the divine law erects an impenetrable spiritual barrier against calculated deceit and frivolous litigation, elevating the sanctity of the courtroom to an encounter with cosmic consequences. No temporal gain can compensate for the forfeiture of divine grace.

**The Judicial Admonition Before the Fifth Oath**

The profound gravity of this final oath is reflected in the procedural safeguards instituted by the Prophet ﷺ. In authentic traditions, such as the narration in Ṣaḥīḥ al-Bukhārī 4748, the Prophet ﷺ instructed the judicial authority to intervene directly before the fifth declaration. The judge places a hand over the man's mouth or issues a stern verbal admonition: Pause, for this fifth oath brings the inescapable consequence of God's wrath, and the punishment of the Hereafter is far more severe than the shame of this world.

This required pause demonstrates the humane sensitivity embedded within Islamic jurisprudence. Even after an accuser has completed four formal oaths, the law offers a final moment of reflection, encouraging him to withdraw the charge if motivated by fleeting anger or unfounded suspicion. The legal system seeks truth, not the spiritual destruction of its citizens, providing every opportunity for conscience to halt irreversible calamity before the final threshold is crossed.

**The Legal Consequences of Completing the Husband's Oaths**

The formal utterance of the fifth oath triggers immediate legal consequences. First, the husband is officially shielded from the statutory punishment of eighty lashes decreed in 24:4 for unproven slander. His sworn imprecations legally satisfy his obligation of proof as an accusing spouse. Second, if the husband accompanied his accusation with a formal renunciation of paternity (*nafy al-walad*), the child is legally detached from his lineage.

The child is henceforth attributed exclusively to the mother, inheriting from her and her relatives, while the husband is freed from child maintenance obligations. However, the completion of the husband's five oaths does not automatically establish the wife's guilt in a manner that warrants her execution or flogging. Rather, it shifts the evidentiary burden entirely onto her shoulders, requiring her to respond through the procedure outlined in the succeeding verse, maintaining a dynamic procedural equilibrium between the rights of the spouses.

**The Irrevocable Dissolution of the Domestic Bond**

The completion of the husband's imprecation results in the immediate, permanent termination of the marriage. Classical jurists unanimously agreed that a marital union cannot endure once a husband has formally invoked the curse of God upon himself regarding his wife's chastity. The mutual trust and affection required to sustain a righteous home are irrevocably shattered.

Imām Mālik, Imām al-Shāfiʿī, and Imām Aḥmad maintained that the separation resulting from *liʿān* creates an eternal prohibition (*taḥrīm muʾabbad*). The couple can never remarry under any circumstances, even if both later retract their oaths or express mutual forgiveness. Abū Ḥanīfah held that the dissolution requires a formal decree from the judge, but concurred that the marital bond is irreparably severed. The permanence of the separation serves as a sobering reminder of the finality of marital slander, permanently guarding the home against toxic reconciliation and ongoing recrimination.

**Self-Imprecation as the Boundary of Human Adjudication**

This legislation illustrates the philosophical boundary of human judicial authority. Human courts operate strictly upon outward evidence, documentation, and credible testimony. When a dispute occurs in total privacy, beyond the reach of external witnesses, earthly adjudication reaches its limit. Rather than resorting to coercive torture, forced confessions, or invasive surveillance, Islamic law entrusts the final verdict to the Lord of the unseen.

By placing the outcome under the divine curse, the legal system acknowledges that while human judges may be deceived by convincing rhetoric, God cannot be misled. The accuser walks away from the courtroom knowing that if he spoke falsely, he carries an active divine curse upon his soul, ensuring that divine justice will inevitably overtake him in this life or the next. The judicial process preserves human dignity by refusing to intrude where God alone judges, leaving the unseen to the All-Knowing Creator.

## Sūrah al-Nisāʾ 4:54

> Or do they envy the people for Allah’s bounties? Indeed, We have given the descendants of Abraham the Book and wisdom, along with great authority.

**Expanded Commentary**

**Envy for Bounty Given**

The question *am yaḥsudūna al-nāsa ʿalā mā ātāhumu Allāhu min faḍlihi* — or do they envy the people for Allah’s bounties — diagnoses the motive behind belief in idols and the reassurance that polytheists are better guided (4:51), and behind withholding even a speck if they had kingdom (4:53). *Ḥasad* — envy — was read as wishing the bounty be removed from its recipient, not merely wishing for similar bounty. The sūrah has tracked *faḍl* — bounty — as the surplus God apportions — not to be craved in another’s hand but to be asked from Him (4:32), concealed by the stingy (4:37), spent for show without belief (4:38), and donated from what He provided without harm (4:39). Here the bounty envied is the Book, prophethood, and the following that the Prophet Muḥammad ﷺ and his community received, a bounty the sūrah has called being made clear and guided to noble ways (4:26) and being turned toward in grace (4:27). The question *do they envy* does not seek information; it exposes that the sellers’ trade of a portion for misguidance and their wish to make believers deviate were not from lack of knowledge but from resentment that bounty came to another. The Qur’an elsewhere says envy drove many of those given the Book to wish believers back to disbelief after the truth became clear, and this verse places that wish at its root.

**Indeed We Have Given the Descendants of Abraham**

The answer *fa qad ātaynā āla Ibrāhīma al-kitāba wa al-ḥikmata wa ātaynāhum mulkan ʿaẓīmā* — indeed, We have given the descendants of Abraham the Book and wisdom, along with great authority — was read as both history and present proof. *Āl Ibrāhīm* — family of Abraham — gathers prophets and kings from his line, to whom the sūrah says God gave *kitāb* — Book, revelation — *ḥikmah* — wisdom, understanding of the Book and sound judgment — and *mulk ʿaẓīm* — great authority, kingdom. Early authorities noted that among his descendants were Yūsuf with governance, Dāwūd with kingdom, Sulaymān with kingdom and wisdom, and many prophets whose Books formed the portions — *naṣīb* — those in Madinah had been given. The point is that bounty to a family of Abraham is not new; it is the established way God deals with that lineage. To envy the Prophet ﷺ — himself a descendant of Abraham through Ismāʿīl — for receiving Book and wisdom and a growing authority is to envy the same pattern that had already favoured their own ancestors. The verse was linked to Jewish envy that prophethood came to an Arab of Ismāʿīl’s line rather than remaining within Isḥāq’s line, but the type reaches any who envy a bounty because it came to another branch of the same human family that began from a single soul (4:1).

**Book and Wisdom and Great Authority Together**

The triad *kitāb wa ḥikmah wa mulk ʿaẓīm* was explained as the complete bounty that makes guidance effective. *Kitāb* is the inscribed limit — *ḥudūd* — the inheritance shares after debts (4:11–12), the marriage bars (4:22–23), the lawful field beyond them sought with wealth in chastity (4:24). *Ḥikmah* is the understanding that makes the Book live — the prophetic clarification that debts precede bequests, that four witnesses are required for *fāḥishah*, that earth purifies where water fails (4:43). *Mulk ʿaẓīm* is the authority that lets Book and wisdom be applied — the community, the judgment between people with fairness (4:58), and the obedience to those in authority within obedience to God and Messenger (4:59). The sūrah has shown all three in this community’s early life: the Book that makes things clear (4:26), the wisdom that guides to noble ways before (4:26), and the authority that returns trusts and judges with fairness. To have been given those three in the family of Abraham before is to know that the same three given now to this branch is not usurpation but continuity. The enviers’ claim that polytheists are better guided (4:51) is therefore not only false; it is ungrateful to the bounty their own family received in all three forms.

**Why Envy Follows the Speck**

The verse follows the rhetorical question about shares of kingdom where even a *naqīr* — speck on a date stone — would be withheld (4:53) because envy explains that withholding. If those given a portion of the Book had shares of dominion, they would not give a speck, and the reason is *ḥasad* — they begrudge the *faḍl* given to others. The sūrah has diagnosed envy as a social force before — *“do not crave what Allah gave some over others; ask Him for bounty”* (4:32) — and here it names its historical root. The cure the sūrah offered there — ask God for bounty — is the cure here as well. Instead of envying the people for what God gave, ask Him for what He gives, because the distributor is not the family that envies; it is the One who never wrongs by a *fatīl* (4:49) and who multiplies good many times over (4:40). The sūrah has also said God knows best who enemies are and is sufficient as Guardian and Helper (4:45), so the envious wish to see believers deviate (4:44) cannot remove the bounty it envies; it can only expose the envier.

**A Verse That Returns Bounty to Its Giver**

Read with the verses that frame it, this question and answer return bounty to its Giver. The bounty envied — Book, wisdom, kingdom — was given by *We* — divine giving — to the family of Abraham, as it is given now to this community. That giving is *faḍl* — surplus, bounty — the same word used for the bounty the stingy conceal (4:37) and for the bounty to be asked for (4:32). To envy the people for it is to dispute the distribution of the One who never wrongs even by an atom and who will give a great reward out of grace (4:40). The sūrah leaves the enviers with the reminder that great authority was already in their own lineage, so the present authority is not a theft from them but a continuation among *you are from one another* (4:25). The next verse will say that some believed in him while others turned away, and that Hell is sufficient as torment (4:55), showing where envy leads when it is followed; this verse shows where it comes from. Between the two, the community is invited to see that the proper response to another’s bounty is not to believe in idols and call disbelief better guided, nor to withhold a speck, but to recognize that Book, wisdom, and authority have always been God’s to give to descendants of Abraham, and to ask Him for their share of that bounty with belief, rather than to begrudge those who received it.

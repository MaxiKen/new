## Sūrah al-Nisāʾ 4:54

> Or do they envy the people for Allah’s bounties? Indeed, We have given the descendants of Abraham the Book and wisdom, along with great authority.

### Expanded Commentary

**Envy of a divine bounty**

The verse asks whether the opponents envy people for what Allah has given them. Envy here is connected to the previous passage: they do not merely disagree; they resent the distribution of guidance and authority. The descendants of Abraham had already received Scripture, wisdom, and a great kingdom, yet divine bounty was not exhausted by that history. God may grant revelation and honour to another branch of Abraham’s family without injustice to the first.

**Bounty cannot be monopolized by ancestry**

The reference to Abraham’s descendants confronts claims that prophethood or divine favour belongs permanently to one lineage. Scripture is a gift, not a hereditary estate whose borders humans can enforce against God. Likewise, Muslims must not assume that ancestry, ethnicity, or historical achievement guarantees continued guidance.

**Envy and gratitude**

A grateful person can recognize another’s blessing without feeling diminished by it. Envy interprets another’s success as one’s own loss and then searches for arguments to make the blessing illegitimate. Religious envy is especially corrosive because it turns the language of doctrine into a mask for wounded status.

The verse does not deny that communities may criticize injustice or ask for fair distribution. It asks them to distinguish principled concern from resentment of a blessing simply because it was given to someone else. Allah’s generosity is wider than human permission, and His choice is not subject to the jealous gatekeeping of creation.

**Bounty is not an ethnic possession**

The history of Abraham’s descendants shows that divine favour can appear in more than one branch and more than one age. No community can convert a past blessing into a permanent monopoly. Gratitude for inherited revelation should lead to recognition when God sends guidance through another people, not to resentment.

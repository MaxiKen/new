## Sūrah al-Nisāʾ 4:75

> And what is it with you? You do not fight in the cause of Allah and for oppressed men, women, and children who cry out, “Our Lord! Deliver us from this land of oppressors! Appoint for us a saviour; appoint for us a helper—all by Your grace.”

### Expanded Commentary

**The cry of the oppressed**

The verse asks why believers do not fight in Allah’s cause and for oppressed men, women, and children. Their cry is a prayer for rescue from a land of oppressors and for a protector and helper. The text gives the oppressed a voice before describing the duty of those with capacity to help; their humanity is not abstracted into a political slogan.

**Protection, not domination**

The cause is framed by deliverance from oppression, not acquisition of territory or revenge. The presence of women and children also emphasizes the duty to protect civilians and the innocent. Any military claim that ignores their safety while invoking this verse has contradicted its moral centre.

The verse challenges comfortable believers to ask whether distance has become an excuse for indifference. Help can include diplomacy, refuge, financial support, advocacy, documentation, and lawful defence, according to capacity and consequence. Not every person has the same means, but every person can refuse to normalize oppression.

**The prayer in the verse**

The oppressed ask Allah to appoint a guardian and helper, teaching that human aid is a form of divine mercy rather than a replacement for God. Those who help must therefore act with humility, not turn rescue into ownership. The person freed from oppression remains a person with rights, voice, and agency.

**The oppressed are subjects, not symbols**

Those who help an oppressed population must listen to its people, protect their agency, and avoid replacing one domination with another. The prayer for a helper is not a demand for a new owner. Rescue becomes faithful when it returns people to safety and the ability to govern their own lives.

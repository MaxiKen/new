## Sūrah al-Tawbah 9:69

> ˹You hypocrites are˺ like those ˹disbelievers˺ before you. They were far superior to you in might and more abundant in wealth and children. They enjoyed their share in this life. You have enjoyed your share, just as they did. And you have engaged in idle talk, just as they did. Their deeds have become void in this world and the Hereafter. And it is they who are the ˹true˺ losers.

**Expanded Commentary**

**Power Does Not Change the Moral Pattern**

The hypocrites are compared with earlier peoples who possessed greater might, wealth, and children. The comparison removes their excuse that their present behavior is justified by circumstance. The earlier people had more visible strength, yet strength did not save them from the consequences of rejecting guidance. The hypocrites have less power and still repeat the same moral pattern.

This is a warning against measuring truth by the scale of worldly success. A large army, prosperous family, or influential network can make a community feel secure. None of these proves that Allah approves its direction. The question is what power serves. If strength protects justice and gratitude, it can be a blessing. If it protects arrogance and idle talk, it becomes evidence against its owner.

**Enjoyment Is Not the Same as Gratitude**

The earlier people enjoyed their share, and the hypocrites enjoyed theirs. The verse does not condemn every lawful pleasure. It condemns enjoyment detached from remembrance, responsibility, and accountability. A person may receive food, family, comfort, and status, then act as though receiving them has released him from the duty to thank the Giver.

Enjoyment becomes dangerous when it teaches the heart that the world is all there is. The person begins to defend the lifestyle that produced the forgetfulness. He does not ask whether the wealth is lawful, whether the family is being guided, or whether the comfort is making him generous. He asks only how to keep the share growing. Gratitude measures the effect of a blessing; appetite measures only its size.

**Idle Talk Connects the Generations**

The verse repeats that the hypocrites engaged in idle talk as earlier peoples did. Speech is part of historical continuity. Communities inherit not only buildings and wealth but habits of mockery, gossip, distraction, and the use of public language to avoid truth. A generation can imitate a previous generation's errors while imagining that its technology makes it wiser.

Idle talk is not every light conversation. It is speech that consumes attention without serving truth, mercy, knowledge, or wholesome rest. It can be loud or polished, traditional or modern. The believer asks what his words produce. If they make evil attractive, truth embarrassing, and time meaningless, their entertainment is carrying a serious moral cost.

**Imitation Can Be a Hidden Form of Defeat**

Earlier commentators connected this verse to the danger of following previous communities step by step. Imitation is not wrong when it concerns useful knowledge or sound practice. The danger is copying a community's moral failures because those failures appear successful. People see wealth and imitate the habits that accompanied it, forgetting that the visible result may conceal a judgment.

The believer studies history to gain discernment, not to collect costumes. He asks why a people rose, what corrupted them, and which choices destroyed their trust. The same error can wear a new uniform. A community that laughs at the failures of the past while repeating their pride has learned only the names, not the lesson.

**Deeds Can Become Void Through Misplaced Purpose**

The verse says their deeds became void in this world and the Hereafter. This does not mean that every worldly benefit vanished immediately. Their roads, wealth, and achievements may remain for a time. It means that the deeds lost their moral value because they were not directed toward Allah and did not protect the people from the consequences of disbelief.

A deed can also be emptied by hypocrisy when its outward form is preserved but its purpose is rejected. The believer therefore asks what his work is building in the soul. Public success cannot replace sincerity, and social benefit cannot be used to declare rebellion righteous. Allah alone gives lasting worth to an action.

**The Real Loss Is a False Calculation**

The final judgment calls them the true losers. They may have thought the believers were losing by sacrificing comfort, while they themselves were winning through possession and influence. The verse reverses the calculation. Loss is not measured by how much a person spent in the world; it is measured by what he carried into the final account.

This perspective does not make worldly planning pointless. It tells the servant what planning is for. Build, earn, raise children, and enjoy lawful provision, but do not trade faith for the means. The best share is the one that leads to gratitude. The worst share is the one that makes a person repeat an old error while calling the repetition progress.

A society can repeat an old error while believing itself original. It may mock earlier peoples for worshipping power while worshipping influence, or condemn their vain speech while filling its own hours with distraction. The names change, but the moral structure remains. A believer studies prosperity with caution, asks what it is doing to worship, and refuses to copy the habits of a successful corrupt culture. He can learn useful skills from any people without inheriting their contempt for guidance. The final loss belongs to the person who has spent the temporary share so completely that nothing remains for the meeting with Allah.

The wise person therefore receives the pleasures of this life as a loan and uses them as preparation for the next. He does not reject family, wealth, strength, or recreation, but he asks what each one is making him. If comfort makes him generous and prayerful, he thanks Allah. If it makes him contemptuous of the needy or careless with time, he treats the comfort as a warning. The earlier nations are not mentioned to entertain the listener; they are mirrors. The mirror is useful only when a person is willing to see his own face in the pattern and change before the ending arrives.

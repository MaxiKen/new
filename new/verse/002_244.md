## Sūrah al-Baqarah 2:244

> Fight in the cause of Allah, and know that Allah is AllHearing, All-Knowing.

**Expanded Commentary**

**The Command to Strive**

The verse instructs the community to strive in the way of God and reminds them that God hears and knows. The sentence follows the account of those who left their homes in fear of death, and it supplies the instruction that the account was given to serve.

The Book's concern is the readiness of the community to defend what it has been given, and the community's teachers read the sentence as the command that answers the fear described in the previous verse. Readers are shown that the passage addresses the conduct of the community in the world, and the command supplies the standard by which its affairs are conducted.

**The Meaning of the Phrase**

The commentators explained the phrase rendered as striving in the way of God as a reference to the effort undertaken for the sake of God, and they discussed its application to struggle of every kind. The wording names the purpose by which the effort is measured, and the community's scholars used the phrase when they set out the law on the subject.

The Book's treatment of the subject elsewhere supplies the rules and the circumstances of the command, and the community's teachers gathered the passages. Readers are given the account of an instruction whose scope the commentaries discussed at length, and the sentence supplies the phrase from which the discussions begin.

**The Relation to the Account Before It**

The sentence follows the narrative of those who abandoned their homes, and the community's teachers read the two together, since the account shows the outcome of a departure that was undertaken out of fear. The instruction that follows supplies the conduct that the account was meant to encourage.

The Book's method of drawing its commands from the events it recounts appears in the passage, and the community's scholars noted the connection between the narrative and the instruction. The passage shows that the sūrah's accounts are given with a purpose that the instructions supply, and the sentence in this place makes that purpose explicit.

**The Names at the End**

The sentence closes with the description of God as hearing and knowing, and the community's scholars read the pair as the ground of the instruction. The attributes accompany the command as they accompany the sūrah's other legislation.

The community's teachers noted that the closing names address the two aspects of the effort the community undertakes, since the first concerns what is said in its cause and the second concerns what is intended. The passage shows that the command is given together with the statement that the One who gave it perceives both, and the sentence's ending supplies the assurance that the effort is not lost.

**The Reports and the Applications**

The commentaries record the occasions on which the command was applied and the circumstances of the community at the time, and the reports describe the struggles in which the early believers were engaged. The community's teachers used the material when they set out the law.

The reports also show the care with which the community maintained the obligation in the conditions it met, and the reader is given the account of a command that the community understood as the defence of what it had received. The community's scholars noted that the sentence places the effort within the framework the passage has described, and the sentence in this place supplies the instruction that the narrative introduced.

**The Passage's Concern**

The sentence stands among the verses that treat the community's conduct after the rules about the household, and it opens the passage on the community's struggle and its trust in God. The Book has completed its treatment of the family and now addresses the affairs of the community as a body.

The community's teachers read the following verses as the development of the theme, and the reader is shown how the sūrah moves from the household to the history of the earlier communities. The passage continues with the instruction about the loan that is given to God, and the sentence in this place supplies the command that opens the sequence.

**The Effort and Its Purpose**

The community's teachers discussed the scope of the effort the sentence commands, and many of them included the struggle of the person against his own inclination within it. The verse names the purpose by which the effort is measured, so that the deed is judged by the cause for which it is undertaken rather than by its visible scale.

The campaigns of the early years supply the setting of the instruction, and the reports describe the believers who left their trades and their families for the field. The jurists built the rules of the duty upon the sentence together with the passages that describe the conditions under which it becomes binding, and the community's later history kept the discussion alive.

The attributes at the close of the verse answer the anxiety of the one who wonders whether his effort is seen. The teachers noted the pair, since the first name concerns what the community says in the cause and the second concerns what it intends, so that no deed undertaken for the sake of God is lost with Him.

**What the Verse Establishes**

The sentence establishes the command to strive in the way of God, the purpose by which the effort is measured, and the knowledge of the One who gave the command. The community's teachers used the verse when they set out the law, and the reader is shown that the sūrah's guidance addresses the community's affairs beyond its households, joining its instructions about the family with its commands about its defence.

The community's scholars used the sentence when they set out the kinds of effort that the law requires, and the verse supplied the purpose by which those kinds are ordered. The instruction also follows the account of the people who fled their homes, so that the passage contrasts their departure with the readiness that the command asks of the believers.

## Sūrah an-Nūr 24:60

> As for elderly women past the age of marriage, there is no blame on them if they take off their ˹outer˺ garments, without revealing their adornments. But it is better for them if they avoid this ˹altogether˺. And Allah is All-Hearing, All-Knowing.

**Expanded Commentary**

**Legal Concessions for Advanced Age: al-qawāʿidu mina al-nisāʾ**

The revelation introduces a compassionate, nuanced legal concession (*rukhṣah*) regarding the dress code of elderly women: *wa-al-qawāʿidu mina al-nisāʾi allātī lā yarjūna nikāḥan fa-laysa ʿalayhinna junāḥun an yaḍaʿna thiyābahunn*. The term *al-qawāʿid* is the plural of *qāʿid*, which literally derives from *qaʿada* (to sit down).

In classical Arabic idiom, *al-qāʿid* refers to a woman of advanced years who has "sat down"—meaning she has ceased to experience menstruation, cannot bear children, and has retreated from the active arena of courtship and marital pursuit, resting after decades of domestic labor. Throughout the sūrah, Islamic law has established rigorous standards of modesty, including the command for women to draw their head-coverings (*khumur*) over their chests and wrap themselves in outer cloaks (*jalābīb*) when in the presence of non-mahram men (v. 31). Verse 60 demonstrates the extraordinary realism of divine legislation by relaxing these heavy sartorial requirements for elderly women, preventing physical discomfort while upholding moral boundaries, establishing a compassionate standard of ease.

**The Definition of Post-Marital Senescence: lā yarjūna nikāḥan**

The legal concession is governed by a precise qualifying clause: *allātī lā yarjūna nikāḥan*, who no longer anticipate or desire marriage. Classical jurists, including al-Ṭabarī, al-Qurṭubī, and al-Shāfiʿī, emphasized that this exemption is not determined merely by a fixed chronological birthday.

It is defined by biological and social realities: it applies to women of advanced age who, due to physical senescence, no longer experience sexual desire, nor do men experience sexual attraction toward them. If an elderly woman still retains physical allure, dresses in an enticing manner, or actively seeks marriage, the concession does not apply to her; she remains bound by the standard dress codes of younger women. The law carefully isolates the underlying effective cause (*al-ʿillah*): the presence or absence of sexual attraction and fitnah. Where romantic attraction has naturally subsided due to advanced age, the law grants compassionate ease.

**Removing Outer Garments Without Adornment: an yaḍaʿna thiyābahunna**

The scope of the concession is clearly delineated: *fa-laysa ʿalayhinna junāḥun an yaḍaʿna thiyābahunna*, there is no blame on them if they take off their outer garments. Classical exegetes were unanimous that *thiyāb* (garments) in this context does not mean stripping down to undergarments or exposing the primary awrah.

Authoritative companions such as ʿAbd Allāh ibn Masʿūd and ʿAbd Allāh ibn ʿAbbās clarified that it refers specifically to the heavy, cumbersome outer cloak (*al-ridāʾ* or *al-jilbāb*) that covers the body from head to toe. An elderly grandmother inside the home or in public may remove this heavy outer mantle, remaining comfortably clad in her headscarf (*khimār*) and standard modest domestic dress (*dirʿ*). In the scorching climate of the Arabian Peninsula, carrying heavy multi-layered cloaks imposed genuine physical fatigue on elderly, frail bodies. Removing the outer mantle provides immense physical relief without compromising basic decency, visual modesty, or moral propriety.

**The Strict Prohibition of Tabarruj: ghayra mutabarrijātin bi-zīnah**

The concession is immediately hedged by an absolute moral prohibition: *ghayra mutabarrijātin bi-zīnah*, without revealing their adornments / without displaying ornaments. The term *tabarruj* derives from *burj* (a towering fortress or celestial constellation), denoting ostentatious self-display, flaunting beauty, and drawing attention to one's body or embellishments.

Even though an elderly woman may remove her heavy outer cloak, she is strictly forbidden from doing so in a manner that displays hidden adornments—such as jewelry on the wrists, neck, or ankles, dyed hair, makeup, or alluring garments. The removal of the outer garment must be purely for physiological comfort and practical ease, entirely devoid of flirtatious intent or aesthetic vanity. If the domestic garments beneath the cloak are brightly colored, translucent, or heavily decorated, removing the cloak becomes unlawful, because the goal of the concession is physical relief, not the display of personal ornamentation.

**The Supererogatory Virtue of Continued Modesty: wa-an yastaʿfifna khayrun lahunn**

Having granted the legal concession, the Qur'an immediately elevates the moral horizon toward spiritual excellence (*al-iḥsān*): *wa-an yastaʿfifna khayrun lahunn*, but it is better for them if they avoid this altogether and remain modest.

Islamic jurisprudence frequently distinguishes between *al-rukhṣah* (the legal concession that permits ease) and *al-ʿazīmah* (the higher, rigorous standard of spiritual perfection). While an elderly woman incurs zero sin (*laysa ʿalayhinna junāḥ*) by removing her outer cloak, voluntarily choosing to retain full covering out of reverence for modesty is far more pleasing to God. Maintaining the complete veil even in extreme old age communicates a sublime, lifelong devotion to modesty that inspires younger generations of women. It serves as an unshakeable statement that modesty is not merely a defensive tactic against predatory glances, but an inherent, radiant ornament of the female soul before its Creator, reflecting inner dignity.

**Divine Surveillance of Intent and Speech: Allāhu Samīʿun ʿAlīm**

The verse concludes with two profound divine attributes: *wa-Allāhu samīʿun ʿalīm*, and Allah is All-Hearing, All-Knowing.

He is *al-Samīʿ*—All-Hearing of the verbal statements, sighs of relief, personal whispers, and conversations that take place within homes when garments are adjusted. He is *al-ʿAlīm*—All-Knowing of the deepest interior motives governing human conduct and sartorial choices. God knows whether an elderly woman removes her cloak out of genuine physical fatigue and age-related frailty, or whether she does so out of lingering vanity, flirtation, or desire for attention. Connecting the legislation to divine omniscience reminds every believer that external rules are ultimately monitored by an all-seeing Creator, encouraging sincere alignment between external practice and internal purity across every stage of mortal life, ensuring that piety remains untainted.

## Sūrah al-Anʿām 6:57

> Say, ˹O Prophet,˺ “Indeed, I stand on a clear proof from my Lord—yet you have denied it. That ˹torment˺ you seek to hasten is not within my power. It is only Allah Who decides ˹its time˺. He declares the truth. And He is the Best of Judges.”

**Expanded Commentary**

**A Claim Several Messengers Have Made**

The declaration that the speaker stands on a clear proof from his Lord is placed on the lips of several prophets in the Book. Hūd announced it twice to his people (11:28; 11:63), Shuʿayb said the same when he was accused of seeking his own advantage (11:88), and the Book describes the believers generally as those who stand on clear proof from their Lord (11:17). The statement is therefore not a personal boast on the part of one man but the standing of every messenger in relation to the message he was given.

The clear proof in this context is the Qur'an itself, together with the demonstrative arguments the sūrah has been building. The Prophet is not claiming a private experience that others cannot evaluate. He is pointing to something the audience also possessed and could examine, which is what makes the next clause so grave.

**"Yet You Have Denied It"**

The sentence states the situation without softening it. The proof stands and it has been denied, and the two facts are placed next to each other in a single breath. The Book's descriptions of denial nearly always have this structure, since what is being described is not a lack of evidence but a response to evidence that is present.

That is why the sūrah's earlier verses kept returning to the same complaint in different words, asking who could restore hearing, sight, and hearts if God withdrew them (6:46), and naming the wrongdoers as the ones who deny the signs (6:49). The denial in this verse is not the beginning of the story but a settled response recorded after a long argument.

**The Demand to Hasten What Cannot Be Hastened**

The verse then names the thing the Makkans had been pressing for: the punishment they were seeking to hasten. The Book records the styles of that demand in several places. They asked, if the message was true, for stones to rain down on them from the sky (8:32); they asked when the promise would come to pass if it was true (27:71); and the Book answers once that perhaps some of what they were seeking to hasten was close at hand (27:72). Elsewhere the Prophet is told that the wrongdoers have a share of torment like that of their predecessors and that they should not ask for it to be brought forward (51:59).

The request was not a sincere inquiry. It was a challenge issued in public, on the assumption that nothing would happen, and the Book treats the tone rather than the content. The answer given here removes the question from the Prophet's authority entirely, which is the point the audience most needed to hear.

**Judgment Belongs to God Alone**

The sentence states that it is Allah alone who decides the time, and the Book states the same principle for judgment generally. The sūrah itself says a few verses later that all authority belongs to God and to Him people will be returned (28:88), and it says elsewhere that no one shares in His rulings (12:40). The Book makes the point repeatedly because the Makkans' demand assumed that the messenger or his God could be manipulated into a display on request.

The verse's effect is to state a limit on the messenger's function, and the sūrah will state that limit again in different terms when it asks the Prophet to say that he follows only what is revealed to him (6:50). The man who delivers the warning has no control over the timetable of the thing he is warning about.

**"He Declares the Truth"**

The phrase is translated from a verb that can be read with a slight change of letters to mean that God decrees the truth, and some commentators preferred that reading in a passage about judgment. Either way, the sentence states that God's relation to the truth is active: He declares it, establishes it, and makes the outcome follow from it.

The Book uses the same verb to describe God's narration of events, as when it relates the accounts of the prophets to the Prophet himself, and it uses the noun for the truth across the whole of the message. The verse is claiming that the truth is not a matter of opinion that the audience is free to negotiate but a fact that God states about the world and the Hereafter.

**"The Best of Judges"**

The verse closes with a name that appears at the end of several passages. Shuʿayb told his people to be patient until God judges between them, calling Him the Best of Judges (7:87); the Prophet is told to follow what is revealed to him and to be patient until God passes judgment, with the same name attached (10:109); Noah used it when he appealed to his Lord about his son (11:45); and the eldest brother of Joseph used it when he refused to leave the land (12:80). The Book ends the whole recitation with the same question: is Allah not the most just of all judges (95:8).

Putting the name at the end of a verse about the punishment the Makkans were demanding means the answer to their challenge is already settled in principle. A judge who is the best of judges does not need assistance or prompting from the parties before him, and the timing He has chosen is not something the accused can schedule.

**What the Verse Establishes About the Prophet**

Two things are established about the Prophet in this sentence. He has a proof, which means his position is not arbitrary, and he has no power over the punishment, which means his position is not coercive. The middle of the verse holds those two facts together, and the next sentence draws the conclusion the audience should have drawn for themselves.

That conclusion is put in the form of a conditional statement about what would have happened if the timing had been left to the Prophet, and it is stated in the plainest words available.

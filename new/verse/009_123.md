## Sūrah al-Tawbah 9:123

> O believers! Fight the disbelievers around you and let them find firmness in you. And know that Allah is with those mindful ˹of Him˺.

**Expanded Commentary**

**The Command Addresses an Organized Community**

The verse speaks to believers as a community under the Messenger's leadership. Its command to fight is not a license for private violence or for harming neighbors because of identity. It concerns an organized conflict in which authority, cause, and moral limits matter. The believer reads the command alongside the prohibition of transgression and the duty to protect those not engaged in fighting.

This context is essential because zeal without discipline can turn a revealed command into a pretext for chaos. Legitimate defense and resistance to aggression require knowledge of who is responsible, what means are lawful, and whether the action serves justice. A person cannot appoint himself judge, commander, and executioner and then claim that the verse approves his anger.

The wording about those around the believers teaches strategic responsibility. A community should not chase distant glory while ignoring danger at its own door. It protects neighbors, families, and institutions from immediate aggression before making grand claims about remote problems.

The principle applies to moral reform as well. A person should confront the clear fault nearest to his own responsibility before performing outrage about the faults of strangers. He begins with his household, conduct, and community. Nearness creates a duty of attention, not permission to treat every nearby person as an enemy.

**Firmness Is Not Cruelty**

The believers are told to let opponents find firmness in them. Firmness means that aggression will not be rewarded and that the community will not collapse through fear. It does not mean uncontrolled rage, torture, humiliation, or delight in suffering. The surrounding teachings of the Quran place firmness inside justice and restraint.

A firm person can speak calmly and still refuse an unjust demand. A community can be merciful to the vulnerable while being unyielding toward an aggressor. The aim of firmness is deterrence and protection. When the enemy recognizes that wrongdoing will meet a lawful boundary, the need for further conflict may be reduced.

**Inner Faults Must Be Faced First**

The spiritual reading of the verse turns the command inward without erasing its public setting. The believer must fight the disbelief, arrogance, greed, and cowardice within himself. If he wants to correct others while protecting his own corruption, his firmness is likely to become hypocrisy.

This inward struggle does not mean tolerating external oppression. It means that public resistance must grow from a heart disciplined by worship. A person who has learned to restrain his own anger is more capable of using force only when necessary. A person who cannot govern himself should fear being entrusted with power over others.

**Taqwā Governs Strength**

The verse ends by saying that Allah is with those mindful of Him. Divine support is connected to taqwā, not to numbers, confidence, or loud declarations. Mindfulness governs the use of strength before, during, and after conflict. It asks whether the cause is just, whether the means are lawful, and whether the heart is seeking Allah or revenge.

Taqwā also gives courage. The believer does not depend solely on equipment or public approval. He uses means carefully while knowing that Allah controls outcomes. This reliance prevents both cowardice and arrogance: cowardice because Allah is with the faithful, and arrogance because victory remains Allah's gift.

A community that responds to every disagreement as warfare will become unjust and exhausted. The verse does not erase distinctions between debate, competition, treaty, and armed aggression. Firmness is measured by the situation. Sometimes it means defense, sometimes a clear legal boundary, and sometimes patient dialogue that prevents conflict.

Proportion protects the innocent and preserves moral credibility. It also keeps a community from being manipulated by people who need a permanent enemy to maintain influence. Leaders must explain the threat, the objective, and the limits. Believers obey lawful direction while asking whether the action remains within Allah's justice.



The purpose of firmness is not endless battle. If an aggressor knows that the community can protect itself, negotiation may become more honest. A weak community may be pressured into agreements that serve only the powerful. Strength, governed by taqwā, can create the conditions for peace by making exploitation less profitable.

The believer therefore asks for the kind of strength that protects and then knows when to stop. Allah's companionship is not proven by the amount of harm inflicted. It is shown by courage that remains obedient, restraint that remains principled, and a willingness to accept peace when justice allows it.

Firmness must therefore be accountable. After danger passes, the community reviews whether its response protected the innocent and served a just purpose. It does not glorify every act performed under the banner of defense. Taqwā requires truth about mistakes as well as courage before an aggressor. A believer can honor those who protected people while still condemning excess. Allah's companionship is sought through justice, and justice includes the willingness to correct one's own side.

The mention of nearness also warns against exporting conflict as a way to escape local reform. A believer may speak passionately about distant injustice while ignoring a neighbor's rights or his own dishonesty. Taqwā brings responsibility home. It asks the servant to protect those immediately under his care, correct his own anger, and support lawful justice before seeking grand recognition. When local conduct is sound, broader resistance has a more trustworthy foundation.

Strength is therefore a trust, not a license to become harsh in every disagreement. It serves justice, protects the vulnerable, and remains answerable to Allah when the conflict ends.

When strength is used in this disciplined way, it protects rather than corrupts the community. The believer asks Allah for courage to resist aggression and humility to stop when the duty is complete. He does not confuse victory with permission to transgress.

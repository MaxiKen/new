## Sūrah al-Baqarah 2:174

> Indeed, those who hide Allah’s revelations, trading them for a fleeting gain consume nothing but fire into their bellies. Allah will neither speak to them on the Day of Judgment, nor will He purify them. And they will suffer a painful punishment.

**Expanded Commentary**

**The Offence Described**

The verse names those who conceal what God has sent down of the Book and exchange it for a small price, and it states what they consume in the process. The sentence returns to a subject the sūrah has already treated, since the charge of hiding the revelation appeared earlier in the passage about the People of the Book.

The new element in the sentence is the commerce, since the concealment is described as a sale. What is being traded is the Book, and what is received is a payment that the verse calls paltry. The reader is shown that the offence the sūrah has been describing has a motive attached to it, and the motive is named as gain.

**Concealment as a Transaction**

The Book uses commercial language for the conduct of the communities that received earlier scriptures in several passages, and the sūrah has already described a group that bought error and error at the price of guidance. The image of a sale makes the choice visible, since a person who sells something receives a price and loses what he gave up.

What the sentence establishes is that the exchange was deliberate. A person who conceals what he has been given and takes something in return has weighed the two and preferred the second, and the verse's description of the payment as paltry states the Book's judgment on the transaction. The community's teachers read the phrase as an indication of the disproportion between what was forfeited and what was received, and the reader is invited to consider what the modern equivalent of such an exchange would be.

**"They Consume Nothing but Fire"**

The second half of the sentence states that those described consume nothing but fire in their bellies, and the image follows from the wealth they have taken. The Book's picture is of people who eat what they have earned, and what they have earned is described in terms of its true nature rather than its appearance.

The commentators connected the phrase with the parallel in another chapter, where those who consume the property of orphans unjustly are described as filling their bellies with fire. The image in both passages makes the same point, which is that what a person consumes in this life may be something other than what he supposes, and the sentence leaves the reader with the picture of an appetite that is fed on what destroys it.

**God's Silence on the Day of Judgment**

The sentence states that God will not speak to them on the Day of Judgment and will not purify them, and the community's scholars discussed how this is to be reconciled with the passages in which God questions people on that day. The Book states elsewhere that He will question all of them concerning what they did, and it also reports an address to the people of the Fire.

Al-Rāzī explained the sentence as describing the withholding of words of welcome rather than the absence of every kind of speech, and he compared it to the treatment of a person who is refused audience by a king. The meaning the commentators settled on is that those described will receive no address of favour, and the community's teachers read the phrase as a statement about the treatment that awaits them rather than about the mechanics of the day.

**Purification Withheld**

The second part of the statement is that God will not purify them, and the commentators explained the verb as covering the praise of a person and the acceptance of his deeds as well as the removal of faults. What the sentence describes is the absence of everything that purification implies, so that the offenders receive neither commendation nor correction.

The Book's use of the term elsewhere includes its own purification of those whom it wills, and the community's scholars noted the contrast between the two statements. What the reader receives is a picture of a condition from which the remedies available in this life have been removed, and the verse's closing words name the punishment that belongs to it.

**The Closing Punishment**

The sentence ends with the statement that a painful punishment awaits those described, and the phrase appears at the close of the sūrah's charges against the communities that mishandled their scriptures. The wording is brief and the reader is left to supply nothing, since the sentence has already described what the punishment consists of in the loss of God's address and of purification.

The community's teachers gathered the passage with the verses that follow, in which the same offenders are described as having bought error at the price of guidance. The two sentences belong to a single account, and the reader is shown the transaction and its consequence in the space of two verses.

**The Passage's Continuing Argument**

The sentence continues the sūrah's treatment of those who possess knowledge of the revelation and deal with it in a way that serves their interest, and it supplies the reason for the warning that follows. The Book has described the concealment of the truth, and it now describes what the concealers have taken in exchange. The reader is given the motive, the act, and the outcome, and the passage continues with a statement of what the offenders have surrendered.

**What the Verse Establishes in the Community**

The sentence establishes a standard for those who hold knowledge of any part of the revelation, and the community's teachers applied it to the scholars of their own time when they warned against teaching for the sake of gain. What the verse describes is not a single act but a disposition toward what has been received, and the reader is left with the question of what he would be willing to exchange for the truth he has been given.

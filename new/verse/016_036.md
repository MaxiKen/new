## Sūrah al-Naḥl 16:36

> **We surely sent a messenger to every community, saying, “Worship Allah and shun false gods.” But some of them were guided by Allah, while others were destined to stray. So travel throughout the land and see the fate of the deniers!**

**Expanded Commentary**

**A Messenger To Every Community**

Wa-laqad baʿathnā fī kulli ummatin rasūlan — and We surely sent in every community a messenger. Laqad emphasizes certainty, indeed, surely. Baʿathnā from b-ʿ-th means to send, raise, resurrect, used for sending messengers and for resurrection. Fī kulli ummatin, in every community, nation, people, indicates universality of messengership. No community left without warner, as 35:24 says *There is no community but that a warner has passed among them* (35:24). Al-Ṭabarī explains that ummah here means people, community, generation that shares time and place. Every ummah had messenger to establish proof. The message was same across all: ani iʿbudū Allāha wa-ijtanibū al-ṭāghūt — worship Allah and shun false gods, avoid ṭāghūt. Ṭāghūt from ṭ-gh-y means to exceed bounds, to transgress, and refers to anything worshipped besides Allah that exceeds limits, including idols, tyrants, devils. The core call is two parts: affirmation, worship Allah, and negation, shun ṭāghūt, exactly tawḥīd.

**Worship Allah And Shun False Gods**

Ani iʿbudū Allāha wa-ijtanibū al-ṭāghūt — that worship Allah and avoid false gods. Iʿbudū from ʿ-b-d means to worship, serve, be servant. Ijtanibū from j-n-b means to avoid, shun, keep away from side, indicating active avoidance, not passive. Al-Ṭabarī says ṭāghūt includes idols, devils, and any object of worship besides Allah. Al-Rāzī explains that worshipping Allah and shunning ṭāghūt are two sides of same coin: you cannot worship Allah truly while also worshipping ṭāghūt; you must do both. The phrase ani iʿbudū Allāha wa-ijtanibū al-ṭāghūt is summary of all messengers' message, from Nūḥ to Muḥammad ﷺ, as 21:25 says *We did not send any messenger before you except We revealed to him there is no god but Me, so worship Me* (21:25). The universality shows that tawḥīd is not new or particular to Arabs; it is original message to all peoples, and shirk is deviation.

**Some Guided, Some Destined To Stray**

Fa-minhum man hadā Allāhu wa-minhum man ḥaqqat ʿalayhi al-ḍalālah — but among them are those whom Allah guided and among them are those upon whom straying was justified, destined. Fa-minhum, among them, from communities. Man hadā Allāhu, whom Allah guided, meaning those who accepted message, reflected on blessings, humbled themselves. Man ḥaqqat ʿalayhi al-ḍalālah, upon whom straying was justified, meaning those who refused, were arrogant, called revelation fables, so misguidance became deserved. Al-Ṭabarī explains ḥaqqat ʿalayhi al-ḍalālah as misguidance became due, established, because they chose denial. Al-Rāzī notes that this does not mean Allah forced some to stray arbitrarily; it means guidance was shown to all via messengers, but some accepted and were guided further, while others refused and straying became deserved. The same idea appears in 2:26: *He misguides many thereby and guides many thereby, and He misguides not except the defiantly disobedient* (2:26). The verse preserves both divine will and human choice.

**Travel And See Fate Of Deniers**

Fa-sīrū fī al-arḍi fa-unẓurū kayfa kāna ʿāqibatu al-mukadhdhibīn — so travel through land and see how was end of deniers. Sīrū from s-y-r means to travel, journey. Fī al-arḍi, through land, earth. Unẓurū from n-ẓ-r means to look, see, consider. Kayfa kāna ʿāqibatu al-mukadhdhibīn, how was end, outcome, of those who denied. Al-Ṭabarī says this is command to consider ruins of destroyed nations as evidence: travel and see traces of ʿĀd, Thamūd, Pharaoh, etc., and see that their end was destruction because they denied messengers. Al-Qurṭubī notes that travel here can be physical travel to see ruins, or travel of reflection through history via stories. The purpose is to learn: denial leads to bad end, as verses 26-34 showed. The command to travel appears many times in Qur'an, as in 12:109 and 30:42, to make history a teacher.

**Why This Verse Comes After Excuse Using Divine Will**

Verse 35 said polytheists argued had Allah willed we would not have worshipped others, and that messengers' duty is only clear delivery. Verse 36 says We sent messenger to every community with same message, some guided, some strayed, so travel and see fate of deniers. The connection is that excuse using divine will fails because messengers were sent to every community to clarify, so people cannot claim they did not know. The fact that some were guided shows guidance is possible; the fact that some strayed shows straying is due to choice, not lack of messenger. Travel and see fate of deniers shows consequence of choosing straying. The verse therefore completes answer to deterministic excuse: messengers sent universally, guidance shown, outcomes differ based on response, and history shows end of deniers.

**Linguistic Note On Ummah And Ṭāghūt**

Ummah from a-m-m means community, group, from same root as imām, leader, and umm, mother, indicating group with common purpose. Rasūl from r-s-l means messenger, one sent. Iʿbudū from ʿ-b-d means worship, serve. Ijtanibū from j-n-b means avoid, shun, keep at side. Ṭāghūt from ṭ-gh-y means to transgress, exceed, and refers to anything worshipped beyond bounds, including idols, tyrants, devils. Hadā from h-d-y means to guide. Ḥaqqat from ḥ-q-q means to be true, justified, due. Ḍalālah from ḍ-l-l means straying, misguidance. Sīrū from s-y-r means travel. Unẓurū from n-ẓ-r means look, see. ʿĀqibah from ʿ-q-b means end, outcome, consequence. Mukadhdhibīn from k-dh-b means deniers, those who called truth lie. The verse's structure — universal sending, universal message, two outcomes, command to travel and see — moves from divine action to human response to evidence in history, guiding listener to choose guidance and avoid fate of deniers.


**A Lesson That Connects To Gratitude**

This verse, like all verses in Sūrat al-Niʿam, ultimately calls to gratitude. Whether it describes Gardens, death, waiting, consequences, or divine command, the purpose is to make the reader recognize blessings and respond with thanks. Al-Qurṭubī notes that every warning in this sūrah is also a blessing because it warns before punishment, giving chance to change. Every promise is also a blessing because it encourages good. The sūrah therefore weaves warning and promise, blessing and accountability, to cultivate a heart that is mindful, humble, and grateful. The reader who reflects on this verse with that mindset will find both knowledge and motivation to be among those who say goodness when asked what Lord revealed, and who are greeted with peace at death.

## Sūrah Āli ʿImrān 3:46

> And he will speak to people in ˹his˺ infancy and adulthood and will be one of the righteous.”

**Expanded Commentary**

**A Child Who Speaks, and Speaks as His Own Witness**

The verse closes the angelic announcement with a promise about the child's speech: he will speak to people in infancy and in adulthood. The infancy clause is the one that carries the sign, and the Book attaches it to a specific situation which the sūrah of Maryam narrates, where the child in the cradle answers the accusations being made against his mother.

The scene in that sūrah gives the miracle its purpose. A woman returns to her people with an infant and is met with the worst accusation a community could make of her; the Book records her response as a gesture, and then has the child speak for himself: he identifies himself as a servant of God, given scripture and prophethood, made a blessing wherever he goes, and commanded to prayer and alms for as long as he lives (19:30–31). The first recorded utterance of Jesus in the Qur'an is an argument in his mother's defence.

**"In Infancy and Adulthood" — the Two Stages as One Promise**

The promise names two stages and so covers a life rather than an episode. Infant speech would be a wonder on its own; a lifetime of speech after it makes the wonder a pattern, and the sūrah of the Table describes exactly that, when God reminds Jesus of His favour in supporting him so that he spoke to people in his infancy and in his maturity (5:110).

The pairing also answers a reader's suspicion. A single early miracle could be dismissed as a wonder story with no consequence; the Book presents the cradle speech and the adult ministry as two halves of one reliable account. What the child said at the beginning, the man confirmed in his teaching: that he was a servant sent to a people, that God is his Lord and theirs, and that the path he called them to is straight.

**What the Infant Speech Provided**

Read against the narrative setting, the sign does something that no later statement could have done. It answers the slander at the moment it was made, in the voice of the person whose existence was the accusation, before the community had any opportunity to build a case.

The Book's account leaves the crowd with nothing to say: the response to the child's speech is not recorded, and the sūrah of Maryam moves on. There is a kind of closure in that silence which the story's shape requires. An accusation about a birth was answered by the child born, and no further argument was available to the accusers.

**"And One of the Righteous" — the Company He Belongs To**

The verse ends by placing him among the righteous, the same phrase used of John in verse 39 and of the prophets listed elsewhere in the Book. In the sūrah of the Cattle, Zachariah, John, Jesus, and Elias are named together as men who were all of the righteous (6:85).

The phrase is unremarkable in itself and consequential in this sūrah, because it is the Book's ordinary description of prophets. The child announced by angels, honoured in two worlds and brought near to God, is described with the same words used of his cousin and of the prophets of Israel. Whatever else the sūrah's readers conclude about him, they are given the category he belongs to.

**The Tradition of Those Who Spoke in the Cradle**

The Prophet's own report limits the wonder to a very small number. Abū Hurayrah heard him say: *None spoke in cradle but three: Jesus, Juraij, and a child* — and the report goes on to tell the story of the second, a hermit wrongly accused by a woman and vindicated by the speech of her infant, and of the third, a baby who answered his mother's wishes with a prayer that he not be like a tyrant and, in another case, that he be like a woman falsely accused (Ṣaḥīḥ al-Bukhārī 3436).

The report does not diminish Jesus' sign; it places it inside a small category of divine interventions on behalf of the slandered. The two accompanying stories are both about false accusation and vindication, and the third involves a child who asks to be like a woman who is lied about rather than a man of power. The Book's tradition treats the cradle speech as evidence given by God for the innocent, which is precisely the role it plays in the sūrah of Maryam.

**A Life That Is a Sign From Beginning to End**

The verse's second clause, that he will speak to people in adulthood, is the claim on which the whole later ministry rests. A prophet's work is conducted in words — teaching, warning, correcting, calling — and the Book presents the adult Jesus doing exactly that: coming with clear proofs, declaring that he has been sent with wisdom to clarify what the people differed about, and calling them to God (43:63).

Taken as a whole, the promise describes a life whose speech is itself the sign. He spoke before he could, and he kept speaking until his time among them ended, and the two facts belong to one office. A reader who has followed the sūrah's narrative from a mother's vow to this verse has been shown the whole arc of a prophet's life in a single line.

**Where the Promise Leaves the Story**

The announcement is complete: word, name, honour, nearness, speech, righteousness. What has not been given is any account of how a child comes to a woman with no husband, and that is the question the narrative puts next, in her own voice.

The reader who has been told all this about a child not yet conceived now hears the mother ask how it can be.

## Sūrah Fāṭir 35:6

> Surely Satan is an enemy to you, so take him as an enemy. He only invites his followers to become inmates of the Blaze.

**Expanded Commentary**

**The Enemy Is Named Without Ambiguity**

The verse makes a direct identification: Satan is an enemy of human beings. An enemy is not simply a source of occasional inconvenience. He seeks the ruin of the one he opposes, and he does so with persistence. The Qur'an removes any romantic picture of Satan as a rebellious but harmless figure. His hostility is not a symbol of independence; it is a program aimed at separating people from their Lord.

The warning matters because the enemy is often concealed by familiarity. Temptation may arrive as a thought that feels self-generated, a desire dressed as freedom, or a social habit presented as normal. Satan's influence does not require a dramatic apparition. His success lies in making a destructive choice appear ordinary and a protective command appear burdensome. The believer begins by accepting the Qur'an's classification rather than negotiating with the tempter's image of himself.

**Taking Him as an Enemy Means Refusing His Direction**

The command to take Satan as an enemy is active. It means opposing his suggestions, rejecting his invitations, and choosing obedience to Allah when desire pulls elsewhere. One does not defeat an enemy by merely knowing that he exists. The Qur'an links the command to steadfastness, repentance, remembrance, and seeking refuge. When temptation strikes, 7:200 directs the servant to seek protection with Allah; awareness of the enemy must become a practiced response.

This stance does not require hatred of every person who falls into error. Satan's party includes those who follow his way, but the believer still calls the sinner toward return rather than treating human beings as demons. The enemy is the whisper that beautifies disobedience and the will that persists in it. Distinguishing between the person and the satanic direction allows correction without arrogance. The believer fights the influence first within the self and then resists the structures that spread it.

**His Invitations Work Through Desire and Distortion**

Satan invites rather than compels. That detail preserves human responsibility. He can suggest, decorate, promise, and provoke, but he cannot force a person to choose. In 4:119 he declares plans involving false hopes, altered boundaries, and commands that misuse creation. In 7:16–17 he describes lying in wait along the straight path. His method is broad because human weaknesses are varied: pride, fear, envy, appetite, despair, and the desire to be admired can all become doors.

The invitation is most dangerous when it borrows a true idea. Freedom can be turned into contempt for every command; mercy can be turned into permission to persist; courage can be turned into cruelty. The heart is not merely offered an act but a story that makes the act seem noble. Taking Satan as an enemy therefore requires learning how to test the story. Revelation supplies the measure, and worship trains the will to prefer truth over the attractive explanation.

**The Party Is Formed by Repeated Consent**

Satan has followers, but the verse does not suggest that they are born with an irreversible satanic identity. A party forms through allegiance. A person hears a temptation, excuses it, repeats it, and eventually defends the pattern as part of the self. Communities can follow the same course when falsehood becomes a shared interest and moral warnings are treated as threats. The word party thus includes both inward loyalty and outward cooperation.

The Qur'an describes Satan's later disavowal of those who followed him (14:22). That future rejection reveals the emptiness of the alliance. Satan does not love his followers or accept responsibility for their choices. He calls them toward ruin and abandons them when the consequences appear. The wise person recognizes this now, before the false friendship is exposed as hostility. Every invitation should be judged by its destination, not by the pleasure or status it offers at the beginning.

Resistance is built from small acts of vigilance. The servant names the temptation, remembers the consequence, and chooses the lawful alternative before the desire becomes a command. Keeping good company and filling the tongue with remembrance also deprive the whisper of privacy. These practices do not make the trial disappear; they make the believer ready to recognize it.

The enemy also exploits delay. He tells the sinner that a later repentance will be easier, though every repeated choice makes the habit stronger. The Qur'an's command brings the decision into the present. A person does not need to know every future temptation; he needs to refuse the allegiance that gives temptation authority now.

The enemy's presence is a reason for alertness, not an excuse for helplessness. Allah has made the path of resistance known and has promised help to those who seek it.

**The Destination Makes the Choice Serious**

The final image is the Blaze. Satan's call is not a harmless experiment in personal taste; it leads toward punishment. The Qur'an names the destination so that moral decisions are not reduced to temporary comfort. A temptation may promise relief, profit, or recognition, but the end of the road reveals what it was really offering. The Blaze is the truth about a path that seemed attractive only because its final distance was hidden.

This warning also places hope within the verse. If Satan invites and people can take him as an enemy, then his call is resistible. The believer is not asked to possess flawless instincts. He is asked to return, seek refuge, and refuse to turn an impulse into a covenant. Prayer, remembrance, honest companionship, and quick repentance weaken his access to the heart. The enemy remains present, but he is not sovereign. Allah's command gives the servant a clear allegiance and a place from which to fight.

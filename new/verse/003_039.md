## Sūrah Āli ʿImrān 3:39

> So the angels called out to him while he stood praying in the sanctuary, “Allah gives you good news of ˹the birth of˺ John who will confirm the Word of Allah and will be a great leader, chaste, and a prophet among the righteous.”

**Expanded Commentary**

**The Answer Comes While He Is Standing in Prayer**

The verse locates the announcement precisely: the angels called to him while he stood praying in the sanctuary. The reply to a request arrives during the act of worship, which is the position in which the sūrah has placed him and the position in which the Book often places an answered prayer.

The detail is not incidental to the narrative's meaning. Zachariah had asked for a good child in a place set apart for service, and the answer finds him still inside that service, still standing. Nothing in the account suggests he had gone home to arrange matters by his own effort. He had done the one thing in his power — pray — and the answer interrupts it.

**The Name Chosen Before the Birth**

The announcement does not merely promise a son; it gives him a name. In the fuller account the name is described as one never given to anyone before: *“O Zachariah! Indeed, We give you the good news of ˹the birth of˺ a son, whose name will be John—a name We have not given to anyone before.”* (19:7).

A name arriving before a birth is a signature of ownership. The child is not left to be named by his household according to custom, and the Book reserves this treatment for a small number of figures. The God who supplies the provision of a girl in a room also supplies the name of a child not yet conceived, and the two details belong to one claim about who is managing the narrative.

**"Confirming a Word From Allah"**

Among the qualities announced is that the child will confirm a word from God. The commentators understood this as a reference to Jesus, who is described in this sūrah as a word from God given to Mary, and they took the announcement to mean that John would be the one to affirm his truth — which is how the Gospel tradition also presents him.

The verb *ṣaddaqa*, to confirm or declare true, is the same word the Qur'an uses for its own relation to the earlier scriptures: the Book confirms what came before it (2:41), and the Book given to the Prophet is a confirmation of previous scriptures (5:48). John's function as the verse describes it is therefore the function the sūrah assigned to the Qur'an itself at its opening. Two revelations, generations apart, settled on the same act: to affirm what was genuine before them.

**Sayyid: What "Great Leader" Carries**

The word translated "great leader" is *sayyid*, and its range is wider than the translation suggests. The lexicographers gathered meanings under it: a man of learning and devotion, generous, self-controlled, temperate, dignified, able to master his own desires. Al-Ṭabarī and Ibn Kathīr record these explanations, and most of them describe states of character rather than rank.

This is significant because the child being promised is given a title of authority, and the Book fills the title with character. The fuller portrait of John in another sūrah shows what was meant: wisdom granted while he was still a child, purity and compassion from God, and kindness to his parents, with neither arrogance nor disobedience in him (19:12–14). A leader in this vocabulary is not a man who commands others but a man who commands himself.

**Ḥaṣūr: the Debate Over a Word Usually Rendered "Chaste"**

The word rendered "chaste" is *ḥaṣūr*, and it is the verse's most disputed term. Some early reports took it to mean a man who abstains from women altogether, and a few commentators linked that reading to the idea that John never married. Ibn Kathīr rejected this, arguing that what is meant is immunity from unlawful sexual relations rather than abstention from marriage, since the prophets married and an incapacity for lawful relations would be a defect rather than a virtue.

The dispute is a good example of how the Book's vocabulary is settled. When a word admits a narrow and a broad sense, the commentators weighed the readings against the rest of revelation and against the standing of the men described, and the broad sense survived. John is praised for purity in the widest meaning: a life governed by self-restraint, not a life deprived of what God made lawful.

**"A Prophet Among the Righteous"**

The announcement closes by naming the child's station: a prophet, and one counted among the righteous. The Book lists Zachariah, John, Jesus, and Elias together in one verse as men whom God guided and who were all of the righteous (6:85).

The phrase gives the promise its final shape. Zachariah had asked for offspring that was *good*; the answer names a prophet, places him among the righteous, and describes his character in four adjectives. The request was granted at a level far beyond its wording, which is the pattern the sūrah has established for prayer in this household.

**The Answer's Place in a Life of Service**

The verse's first words and its last words enclose a lifetime. A priest standing in prayer is told about a son not yet born, and the son is given a name, a mission, a character, and a destination in one sentence. The prayer asked for a good child; the answer describes a prophet who will confirm another prophet's mission.

For the reader, the closing note is the one worth keeping. The Book's account of John's family in another sūrah ends with the reason they were answered: *“Indeed, they used to race in doing good, and call upon Us with hope and fear, totally humbling themselves before Us.”* (21:90). The announcement above was not a reward for a single petition. It came to a household whose habit was to compete in good deeds and to ask with both hope and fear.

## Sūrah al-Nisāʾ 4:52

> It is they who have been condemned by Allah. And whoever is condemned by Allah will have no helper.

**Expanded Commentary**

**Condemned by Allah**

The verse states *ulāʾika alladhīna laʿanahumu Allāh* — it is they who have been condemned by Allah — referring to those described just before as given a portion of the Scriptures yet believing in idols and false gods and reassuring disbelievers they are better guided than believers (4:51). *Laʿn* — to condemn, to expel from mercy — was read as God’s declaration that the offender is removed from His special mercy, the mercy that elsewhere is promised as turning toward you and lightening burdens because you were created weak (4:26–28). The sūrah has used *laʿn* for those who distort words and say *“we hear and disobey”* (4:46) and for the threat before faces are wiped or the defiant condemned like Sabbath-breakers (4:47). Here condemnation is not a prediction but a status: they have been condemned. The reason gathers the sequence: they were given a portion of the Book, they traded it for misguidance and wished believers to deviate (4:44), they played with words to discredit faith (4:46), they were invited to believe in what confirms their own before a severe transformation (4:47), they were warned that *shirk* is not forgiven when held to death while what is below it may be forgiven for whomever God wills (4:48), yet they chose belief in *jibt* and *ṭāghūt* and told polytheists they were better guided. To be condemned after that series is not arbitrary; it is the name for persisting in the trade after the price and the alternative were made clear.

**Whoever Is Condemned Will Have No Helper**

The sentence *wa man yalʿani Allāhu fa lan tajida lahu naṣīrā* — and whoever is condemned by Allah will have no helper — universalizes the status. *Naṣīr* — helper, giver of victory — echoes *Allah is sufficient as a Guardian and sufficient as a Helper* (4:45), where the same root *n-ṣ-r* promised that God’s help suffices against enemies who trade the Book. Here the reversal is stated: when God condemns, no helper suffices. The *jibt* and *ṭāghūt* in whom they believed (4:51) will not help, the polytheists they reassured will not help, and the alliances they sought by saying the disbelievers are better guided will not help. The Qur’an elsewhere says that on the Day when a witness is brought from every community (4:41), those who denied and disobeyed will wish to be dust and will not be able to hide any statement from God (4:42); help at that Day is the help that matters, and the condemned will have none. The sūrah has already said that whoever takes Satan as associate has an evil associate (4:38) and that spending to be seen without belief pairs with that companionship; to have the evil associate as the only companion is to have no helper where help is needed.

**Condemnation After a Portion Given**

The placement emphasizes that condemnation follows knowledge, not ignorance. They were given a portion of the Book — *naṣīban min al-kitāb* — and therefore the path of *“worship Allah and associate none with Him, be kind to parents, relatives, orphans, poor, neighbours, companions, travellers, and those in your possession”* (4:36) was known to them, as was the ethic of trade by mutual consent (4:29) and the promise that not an atom’s weight is wronged (4:40). To believe in idols after that is not a mistake about small detail; it is a choice to give *īmān* to *ṭāghūt* after *īmān* was offered its proper object. That is why the sūrah treats the condemnation as God’s act — *laʿanahumu Allāh* — not merely as people’s dislike. The sūrah elsewhere says *“Allah has condemned them for their disbelief, so they do not believe except for a few”* (4:46), showing that condemnation hardens the heart that persists. Here the few who might still believe are not mentioned, because the focus is on the general rule: once condemnation as *laʿn* is declared, the condemned will find no helper to reverse it. The invitation to believe in what confirms their own before faces are wiped (4:47) was the open door before that declaration; after it, the door is the one God declares closed.

**No Helper Where Help Is Most Needed**

The phrase *lan tajida lahu naṣīrā* — you will never find for him a helper — was read as addressed to the Prophet ﷺ and to any listener, as though to say look for a helper for them and you will not find one. That search was imagined on the Day when witnesses are brought (4:41), when wealth that was concealed (4:37) and speech that was distorted (4:46) and idols that were believed (4:51) each fail to intercede. The sūrah has repeatedly contrasted two companionships: the evil associate — Satan as *qarīn* — and the sufficient Guardian and Helper — God Himself (4:45). To be condemned is to be left with the first and to lose the second. The sūrah will soon ask *“do they have shares of the kingdom? If so, they would not give a speck”* (4:53) and *“do they envy people for God’s bounties?”* (4:54), showing that those with no share of kingdom and with envy as motive cannot help even themselves, let alone another. The verse therefore answers the alliance the sellers sought when they reassured polytheists. They sought helpers among the powerful disbelievers; the verse says the condemned will have no helper at all, because the only Helper who suffices has condemned them.

**A Verse That Steadies the Believers’ Trust**

Between the exposure of belief in false gods (4:51) and the question about kingdom shares (4:53), this short verse steadies the believers’ trust. The community that was told to refer disputes to Allah and the Messenger (4:59) and to return trusts and judge with fairness (4:58) might fear that alliances of sellers of misguidance and polytheists will overwhelm them. The verse says the calculus is simpler: whoever God condemns will have no helper, and God knows best who enemies are and suffices as Guardian and Helper (4:45). The sūrah has already shown that God never wrongs even by an atom’s weight but multiplies good many times over (4:40), and that He will cover lesser faults for those who avoid major sins (4:31). Those promises remain for the believers who keep limits. For the condemned, the opposite promise holds: no helper will appear to cover the *shirk* that is not forgiven when held to death (4:48) or the fabricated lie against God that is sufficient as blatant sin (4:50). The verse’s brevity matches its function. After the detailed trade of a portion for *jibt* and the reassurance to disbelievers, a single sentence about condemnation and no helper tells the community where that trade ends, and whom to trust instead.

## Sūrah al-Nisāʾ 4:52

> It is they who have been condemned by Allah. And whoever is condemned by Allah will have no helper.

### Expanded Commentary

**Condemnation follows deliberate alliance with falsehood**

The verse declares that those people are condemned by Allah and that the one whom Allah condemns has no helper. The condemnation follows the conduct described: using revealed knowledge to support false worship and to mislead others. It is not a blanket sentence on an ethnicity or a religious population; the Qur’an repeatedly distinguishes among people of earlier scripture and praises those who believe and act uprightly.

**The loss of every substitute**

A person may seek protection through status, a patron, a majority, or a religious institution. Yet when the final judgment exposes the false alliance, none can serve as a helper. The verse echoes a central Qur’anic theme: created powers may provide temporary worldly support, but they cannot rescue a person from accountability.

This is also a warning to communities that make compromise with injustice their security strategy. A group may gain influence by flattering a tyrant or endorsing his falsehood, but the alliance does not make falsehood true. It only transfers the moral cost to those who helped circulate it.

The appropriate response is not to despair of anyone’s guidance but to refuse the alliance ourselves. Ask Allah for protection from the kind of knowledge that becomes a weapon against truth, and measure support by justice rather than by the apparent strength of the supporter.

**No patron replaces God**

The person who chooses a corrupt patron may appear protected for a season. The verse looks beyond that season to the absence of any helper who can answer for the patronage. A believer can accept legitimate worldly help without treating the helper as an ultimate protector or obeying him in injustice.

## Sūrah al-Nisāʾ 4:137

> Indeed, those who believed then disbelieved, then believed and again disbelieved—˹only˺ increasing in disbelief—Allah will neither forgive them nor guide them to the ˹Right˺ Way.

### Expanded Commentary

**Reversal as a settled pattern**

The verse describes people who believe, disbelieve, believe again, and then increase in disbelief. The sequence is not a condemnation of sincere struggle or a person who seeks answers and returns honestly. It describes repeated manipulation of faith—using belief when useful, abandoning it when inconvenient, and allowing each reversal to harden into greater denial.

Allah neither forgives nor guides them in this state because they have made instability a strategy rather than a struggle. The wording should make believers cautious about treating sacred commitment as a political costume. Faith may grow through questions, but it cannot survive deliberate cynicism forever.

The final judgment of a particular person remains with Allah, who knows the last state and the inward cause. The practical warning is to return quickly whenever doubt or sin appears instead of feeding it until return becomes unwanted.

**Doubt is not hypocrisy by itself**

A sincere believer may struggle, ask questions, and return. The condemned pattern is calculated alternation that uses belief as a tool and treats truth as disposable. Teachers should create spaces for honest questions so that uncertainty can move toward knowledge instead of being driven into concealment and resentment.

## Sūrah al-Nisāʾ 4:122

> And those who believe and do good, We will soon admit them into Gardens under which rivers flow, to stay there for ever and ever. Allah’s promise is ˹always˺ true. And whose word is more truthful than Allah’s?

### Expanded Commentary

**A promise anchored in truth**

Believers who do good will enter gardens eternally, and Allah’s promise is true. The verse follows the description of Satan’s delusion with a promise that can be trusted. Good action is not pointless effort; it participates in a future that God guarantees.

The rhetorical question about whose word is truer than Allah’s answers the uncertainty created by competing promises. Wealth, security, and status can fail, but divine promise cannot. The believer therefore acts without demanding immediate proof that every sacrifice has paid off.

The phrase “believe and do good” keeps hope connected to conduct. Faith is not an identity badge that excuses injustice, and deeds are not independent from trust in God. Together they form a life directed toward the gardens.

**The certainty of divine promise**

The promise of the gardens is stronger than any promise made by status or wealth. It does not remove work from the believer; it gives work a reliable horizon. When a good act appears to produce no worldly return, the believer remembers that Allah’s word is not measured by immediate visibility.

## Sūrah al-Nisāʾ 4:122

> And those who believe and do good, We will soon admit them into Gardens under which rivers flow, to stay there for ever and ever. Allah’s promise is ˹always˺ true. And whose word is more truthful than Allah’s?

**Expanded Commentary**

**Those Who Believe and Do Good — Into Gardens Forever**

The verse states *wa alladhīna āmanū wa ʿamilū al-ṣāliḥāti sa nudkhiluhum jannātin tajrī min taḥtihā al-anhāru khālidīna fīhā abadan* — and those who believe and do good, do righteous, We will soon admit them into Gardens — orchards, enclosed gardens — under which rivers flow, to stay there forever and ever — *abadan*. *Āmanū wa ʿamilū al-ṣāliḥāt* — faith and righteous deeds — was explained as the sūrah’s shorthand for the faithful: opposed to those who take Satan as guardian (4:119), who invoke others besides Him (4:117), who follow other than believers’ path (4:115), and who will have Hell without escape (4:121). *Sa nudkhiluhum* — We will soon admit, We will enter — was read as divine assurance that admission is from Us, as the emigrant’s reward falls upon God secure (4:100) and as forgiveness is found upon seeking (4:110). *Jannātin tajrī min taḥtihā al-anhāru* — Gardens under which rivers flow — was explained as the sūrah’s repeated promise: the same phrase as for those who believe and do good earlier (4:57) and as for the obedient limits-keeping (4:13). *Khālidīna fīhā abadan* — staying therein forever and ever — was read with double emphasis — *khālidīn* and *abadan* — unlike *khālidan* alone for the intentional killer (4:93) or the self-wronger’s Hell (4:97): for belief and good, abiding is forever and ever with no exit, while Hell’s abiding is also described without *maḥīṣ* (4:121), keeping the symmetry that each *maʾwā* is lasting in its direction.

**God’s Promise Is Always True**

The affirmation *waʿda Allāhi ḥaqqan* — God’s promise as truth, truly, as right — was explained as the contrast to Satan’s promise that is nothing but delusion (4:120). *Waʿd Allāh* — God’s promise — was read as His word that Gardens are for the faithful, that good is multiplied, that ranks, forgiveness, and mercy are from Him (4:96), that many havens are found (4:100), that prayer at fixed time is writ (4:103), that whoever seeks finds forgiveness (4:110). *Ḥaqqan* — truly, as truth, as right, as due — was explained as that the promise corresponds to reality, not wish, unlike empty wishes that claim grace by belonging (4:123). The sūrah has said that the Book was sent with truth — *bi al-ḥaqq* (4:105) — to judge by what He shows; to say His promise is *ḥaqq* is to say judgment by it will be true, as *“God does not wrong even an atom”* (4:40). Where Satan orders cutting and altering (4:119) promising honour, God’s promise to keep creation intact ends in Gardens whose rivers flow under, not cut away.

**And Whose Word Is More Truthful Than God’s?**

The question *wa man aṣdaqu mina Allāhi qīlā* — and whose word, whose speech, whose saying, is more truthful than God’s? — was explained as rhetorical negation: none is more truthful, as earlier *“who is more truthful than God in speech?”* (4:87) where gathering on the Day about which there is no doubt was affirmed. *Qīl* — word, saying — was read as speech that judges, as *“He will gather you on the Day — about which there is no doubt, and who is more truthful than God in speech?”* (4:87) and where *“God’s word is more truthful than any”* seals the same gathering for emigrants. The question was linked to night speech displeasing to Him (4:108) and to advocacy that disputes in this life but not before Him (4:109): all speech besides His — Satan’s promising, secret plotters’ whispering — is delusion or displeasing; His *qīl* is truth. Classical notes added that *qīl* — saying — matches *waʿd* — promise — as both are speech acts; to compare whose *qīl* is more truthful after calling *waʿd* *ḥaqq* is to say: if promise is truth, the Promiser’s every saying is truth, and there is no *ghurūr* in it. The verse generalises the particular case of slander thrown on the innocent (4:112): human speech can bear *buhtān*, God’s cannot.

**A Verse That Completes the Contrast of Two Maws**

Placed after Hell as home without escape (4:121), this verse completes the contrast of two *maʾwās* — refuges — the sūrah has built since the angels’ question about spacious earth (4:97). One branch — helpless hoped to be pardoned (4:99), emigrant finds many havens (4:100), traveller shortens and guards prayer (4:101–102), remembers in all postures (4:103), pursues without faltering for hope (4:104), judges by Book (4:105) — ends in Gardens forever whose promise is truth. The other — invoking others, following Satan, accepting his appointed share, cutting creation, hoping his empty hope (4:117–120) — ends in Hell without *maḥīṣ*. The sūrah that warns not to deviate by following desires (4:27) and not to follow other than believers’ path (4:115) here shows that the two paths end in two homes whose endurance was named with the same verb — *khālid* — yet opposite quality: one as mercy — *Ghafūr Raḥīm* — one as humiliation prepared (4:102). For the weak the verse is the lightest of weights: belief and good, which the sūrah elsewhere defines as faith in God, Messenger, Books, angels, Last Day without distinction (4:136, 4:152) together with doing what is maʿrūf — charity, kindness, reconciliation (4:114) — is tied to an always-true promise, while Satan’s promise is nothing but delusion.

**The Always-True Promise Against the Always-Delusive Promise**

The pairing *waʿda Allāhi ḥaqqan* versus *mā yaʿiduhumu al-shayṭānu illā ghurūrā* (4:120) was read as the sūrah’s ledger of speech: one promise true, one delusion. The question *man aṣdaqu mina Allāhi qīlā* answers the implicit question of the weak — whom to trust where advocacy is loud, where private talk is many, where the enemy is clear (4:101) and where judgment must be by what God shows (4:105): trust the Speaker whose promise is *ḥaqq* and whose *qīl* none exceeds in truth. The verse was also linked to *“God is sufficient as Witness”* (4:79) and *“God is sufficient as Trustee”* (4:132): His *qīl* suffices as witness, His *waʿd* suffices as trustee. The next verse will remove wishful trust in any group’s claim, so that the forever abode is tied not to wish but to belief, good, and the ever-true word.

## Sūrah an-Nūr 24:35

> Allah is the Light of the heavens and the earth. His light is like a niche in which there is a lamp, the lamp is in a crystal, the crystal is like a shining star, lit from ˹the oil of˺ a blessed olive tree, ˹located˺ neither to the east nor the west, whose oil would almost glow, even without being touched by fire. Light upon light! Allah guides whoever He wills to His light. And Allah sets forth parables for humanity. For Allah has ˹perfect˺ knowledge of all things.

**Expanded Commentary**

**The Supreme Ontological Declaration: Allāhu Nūru al-samāwāti wa-al-arḍ**

The revelation ascends to the absolute zenith of metaphysical contemplation: *Allāhu nūru al-samāwāti wa-al-arḍ*, Allah is the Light of the heavens and the earth. In classical Islamic ontology and philosophical theology, light (*al-nūr*) is defined as that which is manifest in itself and brings all other things into manifestation (*al-ẓāhiru bi-nafsih al-muẓhiru li-ghayrih*).

God is the supreme Light of the cosmos because He alone brought all created entities out of the abysmal darkness of non-existence into the radiant light of actualized being. Furthermore, He is the Light of the heavens and the earth because He illuminates the celestial realms with celestial hosts and angelic orders, while illuminating the terrestrial realm and human intellects with prophetic revelation, moral insight, and divine guidance. Without His continuous, sustaining ontological radiation, the entire architecture of creation would instantly dissolve into absolute void.

**The Anatomy of the Parable: Niche, Lamp, and Crystal**

The verse articulates an allegorical masterpiece describing the vessel of divine guidance: *mathalu nūrihī ka-mishkātin fīhā miṣbāḥ al-miṣbāḥu fī zujājah al-zujājatu ka-annahā kawkabun durrīy*. The *mishkāh* is a hollow, non-perforated niche set into a thick wall, functioning to gather, reflect, and amplify light rather than allowing it to dissipate into space. The *miṣbāḥ* is the luminous burning wick, and the *zujājah* is an exquisitely clear crystal enclosure that protects and refracts the flame like a radiant, pearlescent star (*kawkab durrīy*).

Classical authorities, tracing their insight to Ubayy ibn Kaʿb and ʿAbd Allāh ibn ʿAbbās, explained that this parable represents the light of divine guidance residing within the human being. The niche is the chest of the faithful servant; the transparent crystal glass is their pure, untainted heart; and the radiant lamp is the uncreated light of faith and the Qur'an burning within. The crystal heart gathers the divine light, radiating clarity into every thought, emotion, and bodily action.

**The Blessed Olive Tree: Neither of the East nor of the West**

The fuel sustaining this sacred lamp is described with cosmic reverence: *yūqadu min shajaratin mubārakatin zaytūnatin lā sharqiyyatin wa-lā gharbiyyah*. The olive tree is celebrated throughout the Mediterranean and Near Eastern worlds as a symbol of peace, healing, and enduring life. In this parable, the tree is blessed because it occupies a singular geographical and cosmic position.

Located on an elevated mountain summit or open plain, it is shaded by no obstacle; the rising sun strikes it from the east in the morning, and the setting sun bathes it from the west in the evening. Because it belongs exclusively neither to the east nor to the west, its fruit ripens to absolute perfection, producing oil of unparalleled clarity, golden purity, and effortless combustibility. Symbolically, this represents divine truth, which is neither provincial, sectarian, nor bound by temporal geography. Divine revelation is a universal blessing radiating impartially to all humanity.

**The Radiance of Innate Disposition: yakādu zaytuhā yuḍīʾ**

The text delivers an astonishing description of the fuel's inherent luminosity: *yakādu zaytuhā yuḍīʾu wa-law lam tamsashu nār*, its oil would well-nigh glow even if no fire had touched it. This represents the luminous reality of the human primordial disposition (*al-fiṭrah*). The human soul, created by God in a state of natural innocence, possesses an innate affinity for truth, goodness, and beauty.

Even before encountering the external text of revelation, the pure human conscience instinctively perceives the unity of God and recoils from injustice and shamelessness; its inner oil is so clear that it almost generates light on its own. When the transcendent fire of prophetic revelation touches this primed, receptive disposition, an instantaneous and blinding ignition occurs. The inner light of the soul recognizes the outer light of scripture, bursting into full spiritual consciousness.

**The Mystery of Light upon Light: Nūrun ʿalā Nūr**

The verse culminates in an exultant proclamation: *nūrun ʿalā nūr*, Light upon light! This is the supreme compounding of divine illumination. It is the light of the pristine human intellect united with the light of descended revelation; the light of internal faith confirmed by the light of righteous deeds; the light of the prophetic soul harmonized with the eternal speech of God.

This compounded light provides absolute spiritual clarity, guiding the believer safely through the treacherous moral swamps, dark doubts, and chaotic temptations of worldly existence. God guides to this supreme illumination whomever He wills, electing those souls whose hearts are emptied of arrogance and filled with sincere yearning for truth. The verse assures the seeker that divine illumination is never an accidental acquisition; it is a divine gift bestowed upon the humble.

**The Pedagogical Parables and Absolute Omniscience: Allāhu bi-kulli shayʾin ʿAlīm**

The verse concludes with an epistemological reflection: *wa-yaḍribu Allāhu al-amthāla lil-nāsi wa-Allāhu bi-kulli shayʾin ʿalīm*. God coins profound allegories to translate transcendent metaphysical realities into sensory forms that human minds can comprehend. Physical lamps, niches, and olive trees serve as ladders allowing the finite human intellect to ascend toward the contemplation of infinite divine majesty.

Sealing the verse with divine omniscience reminds humanity that the Creator who established the physical laws of optics, combustion, and botanical growth is the very same Lord who engineered the human soul and understands its ultimate spiritual thirst. He knows which hearts are ready to become shining stars of divine light, and He knows which hearts have chosen to remain in self-inflicted darkness. In the light of His perfect knowledge, the universe stands revealed as a luminous manifestation of divine wisdom.

## Sūrah al-Nisāʾ 4:96

> far superior ranks, forgiveness, and mercy from Him. And Allah is All-Forgiving, Most Merciful.

### Expanded Commentary

**Degrees, forgiveness, and mercy**

The greater reward includes superior ranks, forgiveness, and mercy. The order is instructive: striving is not only a path to honour; it is also a path through which sins are forgiven and divine mercy is received. The warrior or supporter must not become proud of sacrifice, because the most needed reward may be pardon for weakness and error.

**A promise within limits**

The verse speaks of striving in Allah’s cause, so its promise cannot be transferred to aggression, revenge, or criminal violence. Sincerity does not turn a false cause into a true one. The believer must examine the authority, purpose, means, and treatment of those not fighting.

The names All-Forgiving and Most Merciful keep the passage from glorifying human effort. Even exceptional service remains dependent on mercy. A community should therefore honour those who serve while praying for their protection and refusing to make them objects of idolatrous admiration.

**Reward is not militarized pride**

The mention of forgiveness and mercy keeps striving within worship. A person who serves must continue to repent, protect life, and resist the ego’s desire to be celebrated as a hero. The greatest rank can be lost if sacrifice becomes a reason to despise those who were not in the same position.

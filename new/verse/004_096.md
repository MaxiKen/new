## Sūrah al-Nisāʾ 4:96

> far superior ranks, forgiveness, and mercy from Him. And Allah is All-Forgiving, Most Merciful.

**Expanded Commentary**

**Far Superior Ranks From Him**

The phrase *darajātin minhu* — far superior ranks, degrees from Him — completes *“those who strive will receive a far better reward”* (4:95). *Darajāt* — ranks — was explained as levels of reward, stations of nearness, each above the other as the heavens are layered. The plural was stressed: not one degree but degrees, as the mighty wage — *ajran ʿaẓīman* — was said to be beyond a single step. The addition *minhu* — from Him — was read as source: these ranks are not market wages earned by measure alone but grace given, as every good is multiplied many times over by His grace (4:40) and as the great reward for doing what was advised is by grace (4:67). The sūrah has shown that those who obey God and the Messenger will be with the blessed — prophets, truthful, martyrs, righteous — honourable company (4:69), and that obedience is by His will where messengers are obeyed (4:64). The ranks from Him place the striver with the martyrs among that company where striving costs life, and with the truthful where striving costs wealth against stinginess (4:37). Early reports illustrated the breadth: between two ranks is like between heaven and earth, yet the text leaves the count unnamed, keeping the face toward the Giver rather than the number.

**And Forgiveness and Mercy**

The triad *darajātin… wa maghfiratan wa raḥmah* — ranks, forgiveness, and mercy — was explained as the content of the mighty wage. *Maghfirah* — forgiveness — and *raḥmah* — mercy — were read as the removal of fault and the bestowal of favour, the two that make Paradise admission possible. The sūrah has paired them where burdens are lightened because humanity is weak (4:28), where the angry violation of wealth is threatened yet avoidance of major sins brings absolution (4:31), where wrongdoers who come seeking forgiveness find Him ever Accepting of Repentance (4:64), and where repentance soon after error is pardoned (4:17–18). To place forgiveness among the striver’s wages shows that striving is not sinless; the one who goes forth still needs covering for haste, for the claim of peace denied, for the gain sought. The sūrah has just warned not to say to a bearer of peace “you are no believer” for fleeting gain and to remember you were as they were (4:94); forgiveness is the remainder that makes the rank secure. Mercy is the surplus beyond covering, as the sūrah said He multiplies a good deed and gives a great reward from grace (4:40). Forgiveness wipes, mercy lifts — together they make ranks more than wage.

**And God Is All-Forgiving, Most Merciful**

The seal *wa kāna Allāhu Ghafūran Raḥīmā* — And God is All-Forgiving, Most Merciful — repeats the same two names that seal the removal of mistake by fasting as repentance (4:92) and the reminder that you were as they were (4:94). *Ghafūr* — All-Forgiving — was explained as forgiving the striver’s faults where verification was imperfect yet sincere, and forgiving the sitter with excuse whose harm kept him home. *Raḥīm* — Most Merciful, ever Merciful — was read as the mercy that gave ranks from Him and that made Hijrah wide for the oppressed (4:100). The pairing after ranks shows that the whole elevation is inside mercy, not outside it. The sūrah has said *“God does not wrong even an atom, and if it is good He multiplies”* (4:40), and *“if you avoid major sins We absolve lesser”* (4:31): forgiveness and mercy are the mechanism of that multiplication where the good is striving with wealth and life. Where the intentional killer was sealed with anger, curse, and mighty preparation (4:93), the striver — and by inclusion the excused sitter promised *ḥusnā* (4:95) — is sealed with forgiveness and mercy.

**Degrees Are Gift, Not Contract**

The wording *minhu* was read by grammarians as partitive and honorific: ranks that are *from Him* — originating with Him, bestowed as gift, not bargained as wage. That matches the sūrah’s language where the great reward for doing what was advised is *by Our grace* (4:67), where the good deed multiplied is *from His grace* (4:40), and where guidance to the Straight Path is a gift after reward (4:68). To strive with wealth and life where the oppressed cry (4:75) is costly, yet the verse says the cost does not purchase rank as trade purchases goods by mutual consent (4:29); it receives rank as gift from the One who created from a single soul and spread humanity (4:1) and who wills to lighten burdens because humanity is weak (4:28). The striver’s wealth, given against stinginess (4:37) and hoarding (4:39), is therefore not a loan repaid with interest but an offering answered with degrees whose distance only the Giver measures. That is why the verse leaves the number unnamed: between two degrees is as between heaven and earth in reports, yet the text keeps the eye on source — *minhu* — rather than count — so that striving is not calculated as profit but trusted as bounty.

**A Short Verse That Holds the Sūrah’s Weight Together**

Though only *“ranks, forgiveness, and mercy from Him, and God is All-Forgiving, Most Merciful,”* the verse carries the sūrah’s weight. It follows the prohibition against consuming wealth unjustly (4:29) with the call to spend wealth for God where the oppressed cry (4:75); it follows the command to verify before fighting (4:94) with the assurance that verification’s owner gives ranks; it follows the distinction between sitting and striving (4:95) with the answer that the distinction is not enmity but mercy’s distribution. The sūrah opened by creation from a single soul and commanded mindfulness of ties (4:1), showed envy of God’s bounties as the disease (4:54), and taught that good and evil are from His decree while evil from the self as means (4:79); here the medicine is named as forgiveness and mercy from Him that make degrees real yet keep brotherhood. For the weak for whom burdens were lightened, the triad is the lightest yet highest: where life and wealth are given, He gives degree; where fault remains, He forgives; where nothing more can be offered, mercy carries. The next passage will turn from those who strive to those who claim oppression while remaining, whose excuse angels will interrogate (4:97).

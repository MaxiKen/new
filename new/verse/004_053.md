## Sūrah al-Nisāʾ 4:53

> Do they have control over shares of the kingdom? If so, they would not have given anyone so much as the speck on a date stone.

**Expanded Commentary**

**Do They Have Shares of the Kingdom**

The question *a lahu naṣībun min al-mulk fa idhan lā yuʾtūna al-nāsa naqīrā* — do they have control over shares of the kingdom? If so, they would not have given anyone so much as the speck on a date stone — follows the condemnation of those who believe in idols and declare polytheists better guided (4:51) and who have no helper once condemned (4:52). The sūrah now asks whether such people possess any share of dominion — *naṣīb min al-mulk* — that would justify their judgment about who is better guided or their claim to distribute favour. *Mulk* — kingdom, authority, dominion — in this sūrah has appeared as God’s appointment of heirs (4:33) and as the heritage to which He multiplies reward (4:40); to have a share of it would be to have a stake in apportioning guidance, provision, and intercession. The verse denies they have any. Their portion was a share of the Book — *naṣīban min al-kitāb* (4:44, 4:51) — which they traded for misguidance; they have no share of kingdom. The Qur’an elsewhere says that if people possessed the treasuries of mercy, they would withhold from fear of spending, because humanity is ever stingy; this verse is that truth applied to those who claim better guidance for disbelievers. If the keys were theirs, they would not open them even a *naqīr* — the speck in the groove of a date stone.

**A Speck on a Date Stone**

The image *naqīr* — speck, small pit, the tiny dot on a date stone — joins *fatīl* — thread (4:49) — and *qiṭmīr* — film elsewhere — as measures of the smallest amount. The sūrah has used such measures to stress that God never wrongs even by an atom’s weight but multiplies good (4:40), and that none will be wronged by a thread (4:49). To say the condemned would not give even a *naqīr* is to say they are the opposite of the divine economy they claim to judge. God withholds no *naqīr* of justice; He gives bounty where He wills and multiplies an atom’s weight many times over. The sellers of misguidance, by contrast, would withhold even a speck if they controlled shares, which is why they reassured disbelievers they were better guided — not from magnanimity but from stinginess toward believers, a form of *bukhl* already condemned as concealing bounty and promoting stinginess (4:37). The sūrah has described the stingy as those who withhold what God gave of His bounty and teach others to withhold; this verse shows that stinginess would govern even dominion, if they had it, so that their judgment about guidance is also a judgment about withholding. They declare the polytheist better guided because they begrudge the believer the bounty of being called better.

**Why the Question About Kingdom Follows Belief in Idols**

The sequence is deliberate. Belief in *jibt* and *ṭāghūt* (4:51) is a claim to an alternative kingdom — a false authority whose judgment is obeyed. To ask do they have a share of the kingdom is to ask whether that false authority owns any real dominion. The answer is none. Those who take Satan as associate have an evil associate (4:38), but they have no share of *mulk* to distribute; the associate they took cannot give. The sūrah has shown true kingdom in its own domain: He appoints heirs, He sets limits — *ḥudūd* — whose keeping brings Gardens and whose crossing brings humiliating Fire (4:13–14), He brings witnesses from every community (4:41), He turns toward those who turn soon (4:17). That kingdom is not shared with those who believe in idols; it judges them. The verse therefore exposes the sellers’ boast as empty. They acted as though they could grant guidance by declaring disbelievers better guided; the verse says if guidance and kingdom were theirs to apportion, they would keep even a *naqīr* to themselves, which proves they do not possess it. The only kingdom that matters — God’s — is generous to an atom and beyond, which is why belief in Him and donation from what He provided would have harmed no one (4:39).

**Stinginess With Guidance as With Wealth**

The stinginess with *naqīr* mirrors the stinginess with wealth diagnosed earlier. There, those who were arrogant and boastful withheld bounty, promoted stinginess, and hid what God gave (4:37); here, those given a portion of the Book withhold the better description of guidance from those who follow the Prophet ﷺ and give it to the polytheists instead. Both are forms of covering — *kufr* — where what should be acknowledged is hidden. The sūrah elsewhere says that those given the Book who envy believers for God’s bounties do so after descendants of Abraham were given Book, wisdom, and great authority (4:54), showing that envy, not lack, drives the withholding. If the envious had shares of kingdom, envy would make them even tighter, not looser. The speck on a date stone is therefore not only a measure of smallness but a measure of character. A hand that will not give a *naqīr* is not a hand that was denied kingdom; it is a hand that, if given kingdom, would still not give. The verse tells the believers that the one who tells the disbeliever he is better guided is not generous toward the disbeliever; he is stingy toward the believer, and that stinginess reveals he controls nothing.

**A Verse That Frees the Believers From Envy of Power**

Placed before the question about envying people for God’s bounties (4:54), this verse frees the believers from envying the sellers’ apparent influence. Influence that cannot give a *naqīr* if it had kingdom is not influence to be envied. The sūrah has taught not to crave what God gave some over others but to ask Him for bounty (4:32), and has asked what harm belief and donation from what was provided would have done (4:39). Here the same lesson is applied to power: those who seem to decide who is better guided have no share to decide with, and their decision, if it had power, would be withholding. The community that was told God knows best who enemies are and is sufficient as Guardian and Helper (4:45) needs no share from such hands. The One who never wrongs by a *naqīr* and who multiplies good many times (4:40) is the only distributor whose share matters, and His distribution — Book and wisdom and great authority to the family of Abraham — will be named next as the true bounty the envious eye resents.

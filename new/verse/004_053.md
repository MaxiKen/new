## Sūrah al-Nisāʾ 4:53

> Do they have control over shares of the kingdom? If so, they would not have given anyone so much as the speck on a date stone.

### Expanded Commentary

**The kingdom is not theirs to distribute**

The verse asks whether the opponents possess a share of the kingdom. If they did, their miserliness would be so extreme that they would not give people even the smallest speck. The question exposes the absurdity of their envy and authority claims. They resent the favour Allah has granted the Prophet and the believers, yet they themselves have no ownership of the divine treasury.

**The date-stone speck**

The image is intentionally tiny. Even a person who refuses generous sharing cannot pretend that he has the right to withhold the smallest particle of God’s bounty from those whom He chooses to favour. The example also exposes the psychology of envy: it is not always a desire to receive; sometimes it is a desire to ensure that another receives nothing.

**Authority and generosity**

Leadership is tested by how it distributes opportunities, knowledge, wealth, and recognition. A ruler or scholar who treats every benefit as private property imitates the imagined kingdom-holders in the verse. True stewardship recognizes that authority is delegated and that giving does not diminish the Giver.

The passage also consoles the Prophet and believers who were mocked for their apparent lack of power. The opponents’ social influence could not cancel divine choice. The believer should seek lawful means but not measure truth by the generosity or hostility of those who temporarily control resources.

**The envy that refuses even a speck**

Some people would rather block another person’s opportunity than share a small benefit. Institutions should therefore examine not only how much they give but which groups they systematically prevent from receiving. The verse condemns a culture of scarcity created by jealousy, not reasonable stewardship of limited resources.

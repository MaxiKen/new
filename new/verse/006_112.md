## Sūrah al-Anʿām 6:112

> And so We have made for every prophet enemies—devilish humans and jinn—whispering to one another with elegant words of deception. Had it been your Lord’s Will, they would not have done such a thing. So leave them and their deceit,

**Expanded Commentary**

**The Enemies Appointed for Every Prophet**

And so We have made for every prophet enemies, devilish humans and jinn. The verse states a fact about the prophetic calling that the Prophet had not been told before in this sūrah, and it does so as a general law rather than as an account of his own case. Enemies are part of the work, and the work has always involved them. The Book announces the same fact elsewhere: the sūrah of the Ranged records that the enemies of the former messengers received a respite in the world, and the sūrah of the Forgiver tells the Prophet that the messengers before him were also called liars.

The phrase devilish humans and jinn names two orders of being in a single category, and the Book's account of temptation holds them together. The sūrah of the Jinn records a group of jinn listening to the Qur'an and believing, and it records others who mislead; the sūrah of the Cow records Satan's promise to lead people astray; and the sūrah of the Prophets describes how the prophets' enemies were made to incline toward one another. In this verse the hostility is shared between human beings and jinn who act in the same manner.

**The Whispering With Elegant Words**

Whispering to one another with elegant words of deception. The Book's word for the whispering describes the secret communication by which a person's resolve is undermined, and the sūrah of the Cow uses it for the prompting of Satan and for the suggestions of the malicious. The description of the words as elegant refers to their form rather than their content, and the phrase indicates the method: the enemies of the prophets do not usually present their case as opposition to God, since they dress their proposals in language that sounds persuasive.

The Book's treatment of persuasive speech in the sūrah of the Cow is instructive, since it records the beauty of the speech of the hypocrites while stating that their hearts are divided. The verse in front of us supplies the mechanism of that persuasion in the case of the messengers, and it locates the danger in the attractiveness of the formulation rather than in the honesty of the argument.

**What the Enemies Do With the Message**

The verse describes the activity that surrounds the delivery of a revealed message. The enemies do not destroy the message; they surround it with alternatives, interpretations, and proposals, and they make those alternatives attractive to their hearers. The sūrah will describe the same activity in the next verse, where the evil suggestions are said to find their way into the hearts of those who do not believe in the Hereafter.

The Book's account of the earlier communities includes this activity in detail. The sūrah of Hūd describes the leaders of the people who said that they had heard a man speaking ideas that were no more than the words of other men, and the sūrah of the Family of Imran records the practices of those who were given a share of the Book and who twisted its words after understanding them. The enemies described in this verse are the human and unseen agents of that work.

**The Instruction Given to the Prophet**

Had it been your Lord's will, they would not have done such a thing. So leave them and their deceit. The verse gives the Prophet the same answer the previous passages gave him: the opposition exists by God's permission, and the Prophet's responsibility is to carry on with the message rather than to defeat the opposition. The instruction to leave them is not a withdrawal from the field but a refusal to make the enemies' conduct the measure of his own.

The sūrah has issued the same instruction twice already, telling him to leave those who take their religion as play (6:70) and to turn away from the polytheists (6:106), and the words are used again in the sūrah of the Bee, where the Prophet is told to strive against the disbelievers and hypocrites. The pattern is consistent throughout the Book: the messenger continues to speak, and the outcome is left to God.

**Why the Announcement Was a Consolation**

The announcement that every prophet has had enemies carries a consolation that a simple instruction would not. A man who finds himself opposed might suppose his mission is failing, and the verse tells him that opposition is the ordinary condition of the work rather than a sign against it. The Book makes the same point in its accounts of the earlier messengers, where each of them is described as encountering denial from his people.

The sūrah of the Ranged lists the messengers who were told they were liars and states that the word of punishment was fulfilled for them, and the sūrah of Yā-Sīn records God's regret over the servants to whom He sent messengers and whom they mocked. The verse in front of us places the Prophet's experience inside that long history, and the instruction that follows sends him back to the work.

**The Transition to the Next Verse**

The verse that follows states the purpose of the whispering in its own terms: the hearts of those who do not believe in the Hereafter may receive it, be pleased with it, and become persistent in what they are doing (6:113). The passage therefore describes a mechanism with a target, and the target is the inner disposition of the people who have already turned away.

That description completes the sūrah's account of how refusal is produced and sustained. The signs are presented and rejected, the hearts are turned aside, and the messengers' enemies continue the work of persuasion. The sūrah states each stage plainly, and it does so while giving the Prophet the instructions that ordered his own conduct inside a city where all of it was taking place.

## Sūrah al-Nisāʾ 4:125

> And who is better in faith than those who ˹fully˺ submit themselves to Allah, do good, and follow the Way of Abraham, the upright? Allah chose Abraham as a close friend.

### Expanded Commentary

**The best faith has an embodied direction**

The verse asks who is better in faith than one who submits himself to Allah, does good, and follows Abraham’s upright way. Submission is not passive resignation; it is turning the self toward God in worship, ethics, and action. The person does good because the submission has reached conduct.

**Abraham as the model of integrity**

Abraham’s *ḥanīf* path is a turning away from idolatry and inherited falsehood toward pure devotion. He combined theological clarity with hospitality, sacrifice, migration, and courage. Following him does not mean copying every historical circumstance; it means sharing his orientation: truth before custom and God before social pressure.

Allah chose Abraham as a close friend, an honour associated in the tradition with extraordinary sincerity. The believer should read the title as an invitation to intimacy with God through loyalty, not as permission to claim spiritual rank. The verse offers a complete definition of faith: surrender, beneficence, and upright monotheism.

**The Abrahamic pattern**

Abraham’s uprightness combines intellectual rejection of false gods with practical hospitality and sacrifice. Following him therefore cannot mean making monotheism a theory while neglecting the stranger, the hungry, or the displaced. Submission is tested in the ordinary acts by which a person makes room for another.

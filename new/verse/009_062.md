## Sūrah al-Tawbah 9:62

> They swear by Allah to you ˹believers˺ in order to please you, while it is the pleasure of Allah and His Messenger they should seek, if they are ˹true˺ believers.

**Expanded Commentary**

**Pleasing People Is a Small Goal**

The hypocrites swear to the believers in order to win their approval. Their oaths are aimed at the immediate audience, not at the Lord before whom every oath will be judged. The problem is not that believers should never care about one another's feelings. Good manners, reconciliation, and kindness are part of faith. The problem is allowing public approval to become the highest goal.

A person who lives for applause changes his words whenever the audience changes. He says what calms the powerful, what flatters the group, or what protects his reputation. Such speech may produce peace for a moment, but it cannot produce integrity. The verse moves the believer from “How will they receive this?” to “What does Allah require me to say and do?” That question makes human approval smaller without making human dignity irrelevant.

**Oaths Cannot Repair a False Motive**

An oath invokes Allah, but invoking His name does not turn a false motive into sincerity. The hypocrites use solemn speech to please the believers, yet the reason for the oath exposes its weakness. Their concern is not truth before Allah; it is acceptance before people. A sacred form can be used for an unsacred purpose.

This warns the believer against dramatic assurances. Repeated promises, emotional declarations, and religious phrases cannot replace consistent conduct. When a person is truthful, his words and actions gradually support each other. When he is false, he may keep adding words because the first words did not alter reality. The more an oath is used to manage an audience, the more carefully the audience should examine the underlying conduct.

**Allah and His Messenger Deserve the Highest Concern**

The verse says that Allah and His Messenger are worthier of being pleased. Allah's pleasure is the final measure, and the Messenger's pleasure is sought through obedience to the guidance he brought, not through flattery directed at his person. This order gives every lesser loyalty its proper place.

A believer can please a parent, spouse, teacher, or leader when doing so agrees with Allah. If pleasing that person requires falsehood or disobedience, the higher allegiance controls the choice. This principle prevents both servility and selfishness. The servant is not free to hurt people carelessly, but he is also not permitted to make their approval a rival to revelation.

**Faith Is Tested When Approval Costs Something**

The closing phrase, “if they are true believers,” makes sincerity practical. Anyone can claim faith while the truth is popular and the audience is friendly. The test arrives when honesty may cost a position, a friendship, or a benefit. The person who seeks Allah's pleasure accepts that cost more readily because he knows human approval is temporary.

This does not make believers eager to offend. Wisdom chooses the right time, words, and manner. Truth can be spoken gently without being diluted. The goal is not to collect enemies but to refuse the lie that approval is worth more than obedience. A servant may regret losing someone's favor while still knowing that the loss was better than gaining favor through betrayal.

**The Audience Cannot Give Lasting Security**

Human approval shifts with rumor, power, and mood. The people pleased by an oath today may reject the speaker tomorrow when a new story appears. A person who builds his identity on their satisfaction therefore lives in fear of constant revision. He must monitor every face and edit every sentence, yet he cannot control the judgment of the crowd.

Allah's pleasure gives the heart a firmer place to stand. The servant still seeks counsel and accepts correction, but he does not collapse when a truthful decision is misunderstood. He can apologize for a real mistake without apologizing for obedience itself. This steadiness is not pride; it is freedom from the demand to be universally approved.

**A Believer's Speech Should Reconcile Truth and Mercy**

Seeking Allah's pleasure does not authorize bluntness without wisdom. The Messenger's example joins clear principle to mercy. The believer asks whether his speech is truthful, necessary, and likely to lead toward repair. He refuses a false statement even if it would be convenient, but he also refuses to use truth as a weapon for humiliating people.

The verse thus reforms the motive beneath public speech. Speak to please Allah, honor the guidance of His Messenger, and serve people through truth. If people are pleased as a result, receive that as a gift rather than as the purpose. If they are displeased, examine whether the manner was wrong, then remain with the truth if the truth was right.

This principle also changes how a believer handles disagreement. He can seek reconciliation with a person while refusing to approve the falsehood that caused the dispute. He can praise a good quality without giving that person authority over revelation. If people become displeased because he will not lie, he accepts the discomfort and keeps his manner respectful. Allah's pleasure is not found through needless harshness, but neither is it found through a calm surface built on betrayal. The servant asks what words will preserve truth, protect the innocent, and make repentance possible. Human approval may follow or fail to follow. Either result is secondary to the honest direction of the heart.

The servant can test his motive by imagining that no one will know why he acted. If he still chooses the truthful course, he is closer to seeking Allah's pleasure. If the action loses its attraction when recognition disappears, he should renew his intention. This test applies to apology, charity, leadership, and even religious teaching. Human beings need one another's good opinion in ordinary relationships, but they must not make that opinion the judge of revelation. A heart directed toward Allah can accept thanks without depending on it and can accept criticism without abandoning a clear duty.

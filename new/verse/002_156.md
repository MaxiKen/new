## Sūrah al-Baqarah 2:156

> who, when faced with a disaster, say, “Surely to Allah we belong and to Him we will ˹all˺ return.”

**Expanded Commentary**

**The Words of the Patient**

The verse describes what the patient say when affliction reaches them, and it supplies the community with the sentence it is to use at the moment of loss. The words acknowledge two facts at once: that the person belongs to God, and that he is returning to Him.

The Book places the sentence in the mouths of those it has just praised, so that the reader receives a model rather than a general description. What the patient do is not simply to bear the loss in silence; they state a truth about themselves and about the one they have lost, and the statement is what distinguishes them in the passage.

**Belonging to God**

The first half of the sentence states that the speaker belongs to God, and the Book uses the same claim across its chapters when it describes the whole creation as God's possession. The heavens and the earth and everything in them belong to Him, and the statement in this verse applies that fact to the person who is speaking.

The claim answers the question that loss raises about ownership. A person who loses property, or a companion, or a child, is losing something that was entrusted to him rather than something that was his by right, and the sentence places the matter where the Book places it. What the speaker acknowledges is that nothing he has was his own possession, and the acknowledgment is made at the very moment when the loss is felt.

**Returning to God**

The second half states that the speaker returns to God, and the Book repeats the motif of return in many contexts. It states that all matters are returned to God, that people will be brought back to Him willingly or unwillingly, and that the final destination of every matter is with Him.

The return named in the sentence is both a destination and a journey, since the person who speaks is already on his way. The Book's use of the term gives the community a way of understanding death as a movement toward the One to whom everything belongs, and the sentence makes that understanding the content of what a Muslim says when he learns of a death. What the reader receives is a formula that carries the community's whole account of life and its end.

**A Sentence the Community Uses**

The words are recited by Muslims throughout the world when they learn that a person has died, and the practice follows directly from the verse's description. The community's scholars connected the sentence with the loss of lives named in the preceding verse, and they taught that it belongs to the moment of bereavement rather than to a later occasion.

The practice preserves the Book's teaching in a form that every member of the community can carry. The virtue the verse requires is not beyond the reach of an ordinary person, since it consists in the acknowledgment of two facts and the willingness to say them aloud. What the sūrah gives in this passage is a passage of Scripture that becomes a sentence of daily use.

**The Reports About the Reward**

The reports add a promise to the practice, and they are among the passages that the community's teachers cite when they encourage the recitation of these words. The Prophet ﷺ is reported to have said that no Muslim is afflicted by a calamity and responds with what God has commanded, which is this sentence, without God giving him something better in its place.

The reports show how the community received the verse and how it applied the promise that follows in the next verse. What the Book states in general terms, the reports illustrate in the experience of the companions, and the two together give the reader a full picture of what the sentence is for.

The reports also describe the occasion on which the companions applied the verse, and they show the words being used at the death of a close relative. The Prophet ﷺ is reported to have instructed a woman whose husband had died to say the sentence and to expect what God has promised, and the community’s teachers drew from the reports the practice that Muslims follow to this day. What the verse states as a description of the patient is thus preserved in the community as a form of speech for its hardest moments, and the reports show the transition from revelation to practice.

**Why the Formula Is Not a Denial of Grief**

The verse describes what the patient say, and it does not say that they feel nothing. The Book's account of the prophets includes weeping over the dead and grief at the loss of children, and the Prophet ﷺ himself wept when his son died while saying that the eye sheds tears and the heart sorrows.

The community's teachers were careful to explain that the words of this verse are the speech of a heart that has accepted the truth about God's ownership and about the destination of His creatures. What the sentence expresses is conviction, and the grief that accompanies it is not a contradiction of what is said. The passage therefore does not ask the community to suppress its sorrow but to state the truth in the middle of it.

What the sentence does not do is tell the mourner how to feel, and the community’s scholars were careful to say so. The words are the speech of conviction, and a person may pronounce them while his eyes are full of tears, as the Prophet ﷺ did when his son died. The Book asks the community to state the truth about God and about itself in the middle of its sorrow, and the two are not in competition. The sentence therefore belongs to a religious life that expects grief and provides for it rather than one that pretends the loss has not occurred.

**The Verse as the Centre of the Passage**

The sentence stands at the centre of a short passage about testing, and it supplies the content of the patience the previous verse required and the ground of the reward the next verse promises. The Book names the trials, describes the words of those who endure, and then states what they receive. The reader who has followed the sequence holds all three together, and the passage reads as an instruction for the community's hardest day.

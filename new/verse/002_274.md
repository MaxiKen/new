## Sūrah al-Baqarah 2:274

> Those who spend their wealth in charity day and night, secretly and openly—their reward is with their Lord, and there will be no fear for them, nor will they grieve.

**Expanded Commentary**

**The Description Stated**

The sentence describes those who spend their wealth by night and by day, in secret and in the open, and the community's teachers read the four phrases as the statement that the giving of such persons is not confined to an hour or a manner. The passage has treated the recipients of charity, and the sentence here returns to the givers with the description of a conduct that continues.

The wording uses the pairs of opposite times and opposite manners, so that the whole of a person's life is included within the description. The community's scholars noted the completeness of the phrase, since no part of the day or of the conduct is left outside it.

The Book's treatment of the subject elsewhere commends the giving that is concealed, and the sentence includes it beside the gift that the community observes. The teachers read the four phrases together as the statement that the value of the act lies in what it seeks rather than in the manner it takes.

**The Four Manners**

The commentators explained the first pair as the description of the times in which a person gives, since the night and the day cover the whole of the life of the giver. The teachers noted the mention of the night first, when the act is hidden from the community and unknown to the neighbours.

The second pair describes the two manners that the Book has already examined, and the sentence includes both of them within the conduct it praises. The community's scholars drew from the wording the lesson that the gift which is offered for the sake of God is accepted whatever its manner, provided the intention is sound.

The four phrases were also read as a description of the different circumstances in which the believers gave in the community's early years, when the poverty of the immigrants was met from the property of those who had settled in the city. The sentence in this place supplies the account of the conduct that answered that need.

**The Reports About the Occasion**

The commentators transmitted the reports that connect the sentence with particular members of the early community, and the material names two of the most prominent believers who are said to have distributed a portion of their wealth in the four manners the verse describes. The teachers recorded the reports and read them as the illustration of the sentence.

The community's scholars noted that the sentence states the matter in general terms, so that the reports describe instances of the conduct rather than the limits of the verse. The passage's teaching belongs to every giver who meets its description.

The accounts also show the manner in which the community's early generosity was received and the attention that was given to the needs of the poor. The teachers used the material when they spoke about the conduct of the companions, and the sentence supplied the standard by which their giving was described.

**The Reward Named**

The sentence states that the reward of such givers is with their Lord, and the community's teachers read the clause as the assurance that their acts are kept. The same promise appears in the earlier verse about those who spend without following their gift with reminders of it, and the repetition supplies the confirmation of the subject.

The wording places the reward with God rather than in the hands of the community, so that the giver looks beyond the notice of his neighbours. The community's scholars drew from the phrase the lesson that the act of giving is directed to the One who receives it.

The clause also answers the anxiety that accompanies the gift that no one observes. The teachers used the sentence when they encouraged the believers whose giving was hidden, and the passage's earlier verses about the secret gift were read with it.

**The Promise Completed**

The sentence closes with the statement that no fear will come upon them and that they will not grieve, and the community's scholars explained the pair as the description of the condition of the believers in the life to come. The wording appears in the earlier verses of the passage and in the Book's descriptions of the reward, and the teachers regarded the repetition as the confirmation of the promise.

The first clause concerns what lies ahead, and the second concerns what has passed, so that the pair describes a state from which the anxieties of both directions have been removed. The community's preachers used the formula when they spoke about the standing of the givers.

The sentence therefore completes the description of the givers with the assurance that belongs to them. The teachers drew from the two clauses the lesson that the wealth given for the sake of God is exchanged for a security that does not end.

**The Section Closed**

The sentence is the last of the passage that treats the giving of charity, and the community's teachers read the section as a body of instruction that includes the command, the manner, the material, the recipients, and the promise. The verses have examined the act from every side, and the sentence here gathers the conduct of the givers under a single description.

The scholars of the community observed that the passage begins with the parable of the seed and closes with the account of those who give at all times and in every manner. The arrangement presents the harvest of the act and the conduct of the person who performs it.

The following verse turns to the practice of interest, which the Book sets beside the charity that has been described. The community's teachers read the two subjects together, so that the passage's treatment of the wealth of the community continues into the verses that follow.

**What the Verse Establishes**

The sentence establishes the description of those who give at every time and in every manner, the reward that is kept for them with their Lord, and the removal of fear and grief that accompanies them. The community's teachers used the verse when they encouraged the believers in the giving that the passage requires, and the section that treats charity reaches its conclusion with the promise stated here, so that the reader is carried from the conduct of the givers to the account of the practice that the Book forbids.

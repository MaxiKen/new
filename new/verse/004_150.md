## Sūrah al-Nisāʾ 4:150

> Surely those who deny Allah and His messengers and wish to make a distinction between Allah and His messengers, saying, “We believe in some and disbelieve in others,” desiring to forge a compromise,

**Expanded Commentary**

**Those Who Deny God and Messengers and Wish to Distinguish**

The verse opens *inna alladhīna yakfurūna bi Allāhi wa rusulihī wa yurīdūna an yufarriqū bayna Allāhi wa rusulihī* — surely those who deny, cover, God and His messengers and wish, intend, to make distinction, to separate, between God and His messengers. *Yakfurūna bi Allāhi wa rusulihī* — to deny God and messengers — was explained as denial that makes association far straying (4:116) and defying the Messenger after guidance clear following other than believers’ path (4:115). *Yurīdūna an yufarriqū bayna Allāhi wa rusulihī* — to wish to make distinction between God and messengers — was read as claiming belief in God without messengers, or in some messengers without others, as the sūrah has shown that the People of the Book demanded a written revelation from heaven yet demanded greater from Moses *show God visible* and were struck (4:153), and later said *we killed the Messiah, son of Mary, messenger of God* (4:157) while denying him. To separate God from messengers was explained as theological *tafrīq* — dividing — where the Book was sent with truth to judge between people by what He shows the Messenger (4:105) and where obedience to Messenger is obedience to God (4:80): to claim God without Messenger is to claim a judge without showing. Early reports linked the verse to Jews who believed in Moses but disbelieved in Jesus and Muhammad ﷺ, and to Christians who believed in Jesus but disbelieved in Muhammad ﷺ, both making distinction.

**Saying — We Believe in Some and Disbelieve in Others**

The speech *wa yaqūlūna nuʾminu bi baʿḍin wa nakfuru bi baʿḍin* — and they say, we believe in some and disbelieve in others, in some messengers we believe and in others we disbelieve — was explained as the distinction’s formula, the same compromise condemned where *“O believers, have faith in God, His Messenger, Book He revealed to His Messenger and Scriptures He revealed before”* (4:136) commanded comprehensive faith without selecting. *Nuʾminu bi baʿḍ* — we believe in some — was read as the partial faith that wishes grace by belonging — *neither by your wishes nor those of People of the Book* (4:123) — here the wish is *we believe in some messengers* as our group. *Wa nakfuru bi baʿḍ* — we disbelieve in some — was read as denial of those sent before and after, as the sūrah will say *they are true disbelievers* (4:151) and *those who believe in God and messengers accepting all, rejecting none, He will give rewards* (4:152). The sūrah has shown that those who believed then disbelieved then believed then increased (4:137) make similar speech; the formula *we believe in some and disbelieve in others* was explained as hypocrisy’s creedal side: to be *mudhabdhab* between neither these nor those (4:143) doctrinally. The phrase was linked to *“they say we hear and disobey”* (4:46) where Jews distort words; here distortion is selection of messengers.

**Desiring to Forge a Compromise**

The intention *wa yurīdūna an yattakhidhū bayna dhālika sabīlā* — and they wish, intend, to take, to forge between that — between belief in some and disbelief in some — a way, a path, a compromise — was explained with *sabīl* — way — the same *sabīl* the sūrah guides to noble ways (4:26), Satan wishes the deviant far path (4:60), believers’ path not to be left (4:115), and association far straying (4:116). *Bayna dhālika sabīlā* — between that a way — was read as seeking a middle way that is neither belief nor disbelief, neither *īmān* nor *kufr*, but a compromise doctrine where God is accepted and some messengers rejected. The sūrah has shown that *settlement is best* (4:128) where equitable compromise between spouses is good, but compromise between God and messengers is not settlement; it is *tafrīq*. The sūrah has also shown that *most secret talks have no good except charity, kindness, reconciliation* (4:114) — to take a way between believing and denying is the secret talk that has no good. The verse generalises the particular hypocrisy of waiting between winners (4:141) into theology: waiting between messengers to believe some and disbelieve some is desiring a *sabīl bayna dhālika* that is not a *sabīl* but straying left where no way is found (4:143).

**A Verse That Follows Pardon With Distinction**

Placed after *whether you reveal or conceal a good or pardon an evil, surely He is Ever-Pardoning, Most Capable* (4:149) and after *He likes not loud evil except by wronged* (4:148), this verse follows pardon with distinction that refuses pardon where distinction persists without seeking. The sūrah has taught that grateful and faithful will not be punished because He is Appreciative, All-Knowing (4:147), and that repentance with mending, holding fast, sincerity makes hypocrites with believers (4:146); those who make distinction between God and messengers do not repent, do not mend, do not hold fast, do not make sincere — they desire a way between. The sūrah that began *“have faith in God, His Messenger, Book, Scriptures before”* (4:136) now defines the opposite as *“surely those who deny God and messengers wishing to make distinction”*; the comprehensive faith there is the remedy to distinction here. For the weak this is clarifying: where settlement between spouses is best (4:128) and settlement is desired, distinction between God and messengers is never settlement; to believe in some and disbelieve in others is not waiving part of right to keep marriage, it is claiming right to select the Sender.

**The Distinction That the Sūrah Has Named Two Kinds**

The phrase *yufarriqū bayna Allāhi wa rusulihī* was contrasted with the two distinctions the sūrah has condemned: distinguishing between *wishes* — *your wishes nor those of People of the Book* (4:123) — and distinguishing between *messengers* — *we believe in some and disbelieve in others*. Both are *tafrīq* that seeks a *sabī l bayna dhālika* where none is, and both are answered with comprehensive faith: *believe in God, Messenger, Book and Scriptures, angels, Last Day* (4:136) and *believe in God and His messengers accepting all* (4:152). The verse was read as the sūrah’s answer to compromise theology: the only *sabīl* that is — is the believers’ path (4:115), submission fully to Him doing good following Abraham ḥanīf (4:125) — not a path between distinguishing. The next verse will declare the verdict: they are indeed the true disbelievers, and We have prepared for disbelievers a humiliating punishment (4:151).

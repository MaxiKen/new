## Sūrah al-Nisāʾ 4:150

> Surely those who deny Allah and His messengers and wish to make a distinction between Allah and His messengers, saying, “We believe in some and disbelieve in others,” desiring to forge a compromise,

### Expanded Commentary

**Rejecting some messengers rejects the message**

The verse condemns those who believe in some messengers and disbelieve in others, seeking a compromise between revelation and preference. The Qur’anic view of prophethood is a connected chain: the God who sent Moses also sent Jesus and Muhammad. To accept one messenger as a cultural symbol while denying another’s truth is to make human preference the judge of revelation.

The people described are not being asked to erase their own history. They are asked to stop dividing God’s messengers according to political convenience, ethnicity, or doctrinal advantage. Faith receives the prophets as a family of guidance, while understanding that later revelation confirms and completes earlier messages.

The desire to forge a compromise may sound moderate, but it is a refusal of truth when it means accepting only what flatters the self. Genuine dialogue can respect difference; it cannot pretend that contradiction is reconciliation.

**The unity of prophetic truth**

Selective belief often follows selective ethics: a person accepts the prophet who confirms his community and rejects the one whose command would change him. The verse asks for a more honest response. Receive revelation as a chain from one God, and let the final messenger complete rather than compete with the earlier messengers.

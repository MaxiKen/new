## Sūrah al-Nisāʾ 4:21

> And how could you take it back after having enjoyed each other intimately and she has taken from you a firm commitment?

### Expanded Commentary

**A covenant, not a disposable arrangement**

The verse asks how a husband could take back the dowry after intimacy and after the spouses have entered a solemn covenant. The question is a moral rebuke: the private union that made the dowry due also reveals the gravity of the promise. Marriage is not a consumer transaction in which one party may demand a refund when preference changes. Intimacy creates vulnerability, and the covenant exists to protect that vulnerability with duties of care, maintenance, fidelity, and dignified separation.

**The indirect language of intimacy**

The expression translated as “you have been intimate with one another” uses an indirect Qur’anic idiom. Its modesty does not make the legal point vague. Classical jurists discussed whether the phrase refers to sexual intercourse, seclusion in circumstances that make intimacy possible, or both for particular legal consequences. Those distinctions affect the amount of dowry due in different situations, but none permits the husband to exploit uncertainty in order to seize the wife’s property.

**A solemn covenant before God**

Marriage is called *mīthāq*, a covenant. The Qur’an uses this word for weighty agreements and commitments, including the covenant taken from prophets. The Prophet’s Farewell address described women as a trust from God and marriage as made lawful through His word. A man therefore answers not only to his wife’s family or to a court but to the One who made the covenant meaningful.

This also explains why lawful divorce does not make the marriage insignificant. A covenant may be ended under rules without being treated as though it never existed. The husband must release his wife honourably, settle financial obligations, and avoid converting the history of intimacy into a weapon against her reputation.

**The ethical force of the question**

The verse appeals to memory: how can you take it back after you have shared what spouses share and pledged what spouses pledge? The believer is asked to let the closeness that once made a demand possible now restrain the demand. A successful legal system can compel payment, but the verse seeks something deeper—the conscience that finds confiscation shameful even before a judge forbids it.

**The covenant remembers consequences**

A marriage contract should make the parties more careful with one another precisely because intimacy has made them vulnerable. Clear terms, timely payment of dowry, and respectful divorce procedures are not signs that affection is absent; they are protections for the days when affection is strained. The solemn covenant gives legal form to the moral memory of shared life.

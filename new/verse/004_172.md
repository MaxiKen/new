## Sūrah al-Nisāʾ 4:172

> The Messiah would never be too proud to be a servant of Allah, nor would the angels nearest to Allah. Those who are too proud and arrogant to worship Him will be brought before Him all together.

**Expanded Commentary**

**The Messiah Would Never Be Too Proud to Be Servant Nor Angels Nearest**

The verse states *lan yastankifa al-masīḥu an yakūna ʿabdan li Allāhi wa lā al-malāʾikatu al-muqarrabūn* — the Messiah — *al-masīḥ* — Jesus, son of Mary, messenger and Word through Mary and spirit from Him (4:171) — would never disdain, be too proud, to be a servant, slave, to God, nor would the angels nearest — *al-muqarrabūn* — those brought near. *Lan yastankif* — never be too proud, never disdain, never refuse out of pride — was explained with *istinkāf* — pride that refuses service, as the sūrah has shown that those too proud and arrogant to worship Him will be brought before Him all together (now), and as earlier *Satan was proud* — *istakbara* — refusing prostration. *An yakūna ʿabdan li Allāhi* — to be a servant to God — was read as *ʿubūdiyyah* — servitude — the rank of every messenger in lineage — *We have sent revelation to you as We sent to Noah… Jesus taught* (4:163) — all are servants. *Wa lā al-malāʾikatu al-muqarrabūn* — nor the angels nearest — was explained as *muqarrab* — those brought near — *“the Messiah would never be too proud to be servant, nor angels nearest”* — the highest creatures — Messiah, whose mother was slandered with mighty *buhtān* (4:156), and angels who bear witness with His knowledge (4:166) and who stood witness for Mount raised (4:154) — are servants. The sūrah has shown that *“to Him belongs whatever is in heavens and earth, He suffices as Trustee”* (4:171) — servants belong to Him; to claim the Messiah is beyond servitude is *ghuluww* — going to extremes (4:171) — the Messiah himself would never claim it.

**Those Too Proud to Worship Him Will Be Gathered Before Him**

The warning *wa man yastankif ʿan ʿibādatihī wa yastakbir fa sa yaḥshuruhum ilayhi jamīʿā* — and whoever is too proud, disdains, to worship Him, serve Him, and is arrogant, will be gathered, mustered, to Him, toward Him, all together — was explained with *yastankif* — too proud — and *yastakbir* — arrogant, magnifies self — pair already in Satan, cursed (4:118) — who said *I will surely take a share* (4:118). *ʿAn ʿibādatihī* — from His worship, His servitude — was read as that *ʿubūdiyyah* is not debasement but honour, as Abraham taken as *khalīl* (4:125) was servant, as Moses spoken to directly (4:164) was servant, as angels nearest are servants. *Fa sa yaḥshuruhum ilayhi jamīʿā* — He will gather them to Him all together — was explained with *ḥashara* — to gather, to muster — the same gathering as *He will gather you on the Day about which there is no doubt* (4:87) and *He will gather hypocrites and disbelievers together in Hell* (4:140) and *those who disbelieve and wrong, He will not forgive nor guide except Hell’s path forever* (4:168–169) — the too proud are gathered for reckoning. The phrase *jamīʿan* — all together — was explained as that the Messiah not too proud and angels not too proud, yet the proud among humanity will be gathered with the proud — *jamīʿan* — as hypocrites gathered together in Hell.

**A Verse That Follows Do Not Say Trinity With Messiah Not Too Proud**

Placed after *O People of the Book, do not go to extremes, say nothing but truth, Messiah was but messenger, Word through Mary and spirit from Him, believe in God and messengers, do not say Trinity, stop — better — He is One God far above having a son, to Him belongs whatever is in heavens and earth, He suffices as Trustee* (4:171), this verse follows *do not say Trinity* with *the Messiah would never be too proud to be servant, nor angels nearest*. The correction — Messiah is but messenger — is proven by his own attitude: he would never disdain servitude; to claim him god is to claim for him what he would disdain. The sūrah has shown that *“Satan promises only delusion”* (4:120) yet his share is those who take female gods besides Him (4:117) — to make the Messiah more than servant is delusion, not his wish. The sūrah has shown that *“with Him are both rewards He hears and sees”* (4:134) — servitude is the worship that receives both, as the emigrant’s many havens are *ʿibādah* rewarded. For the weak this is an anchor: where you were told to be grateful and faithful — *why should He punish if you are grateful and faithful?* (4:147) — servitude is grateful faith; to be too proud to worship is ingratitude.

**The Angels Nearest as Witness to Servitude**

The addition *wa lā al-malāʾikatu al-muqarrabūn* — nor the angels nearest — was explained as witness: angels nearest to Him — *muqarrabūn* — who bear witness to what He sent with His knowledge (4:166), who guard, who raise Mount, who take souls — are servants not too proud; their nearness is servitude, not partnership. The sūrah has shown that *“everyone of People of the Book will believe in him before his death, and on the Day he will be witness against them”* (4:159) — Messiah as witness is servant witness, as angels are witness servants. Where the sūrah says *“do not incline entirely leaving the other suspended”* (4:129) — to make the Messiah god is to incline entirely to him leaving the One suspended as son — the verse corrects incline: Messiah not too proud, angels not too proud; to worship is to worship Him alone whose ownership is all (4:126). The next verse will briefly note how the gathered are then rewarded or punished (4:173).

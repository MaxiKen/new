## Sūrah al-Nisāʾ 4:172

> The Messiah would never be too proud to be a servant of Allah, nor would the angels nearest to Allah. Those who are too proud and arrogant to worship Him will be brought before Him all together.

### Expanded Commentary

**Servanthood is the honour of Jesus and the angels**

The Messiah would not be too proud to be a servant of Allah, nor would the angels nearest to Him. Servanthood is not degradation. It is the highest truth about a created being because it places worship, dependence, and dignity in their proper relation to the Creator.

The verse answers the idea that honour requires sharing in divine status. Jesus’s rank as messenger is protected by affirming his worship. The angels, despite their nearness, also remain servants; proximity does not become partnership.

Those who are too proud and arrogant will be gathered before God. The contrast is moral: greatness is not measured by refusing service but by accepting it. In family and political life, people who demand domination imitate the arrogance condemned here. Worship teaches the powerful that they too are accountable servants.

**The dignity of being a servant**

If the Messiah and the nearest angels are not too proud to serve God, no human office can make service beneath a person. A parent, ruler, scholar, or wealthy donor remains a servant. Worship dismantles the fantasy that authority grants exemption from accountability.

## Sūrah al-Baqarah 2:284

> To Allah ˹alone˺ belongs whatever is in the heavens and whatever is on the earth. Whether you reveal what is in your hearts or conceal it, Allah will call you to account for it. He forgives whoever He wills, and punishes whoever He wills. And Allah is Most Capable of everything.

**Expanded Commentary**

**The Ownership Stated**

The sentence opens with the statement that everything in the heavens and the earth belongs to God, and the community's teachers read the clause as the foundation of the account that follows. The passage has treated the wealth of the community and its dealings, and the sentence here places the whole of the created order within the ownership of the One who will judge it.

The wording names the two realms together, so that the property of the believers and the affairs of their community appear as parts of what the sentence describes. The community's scholars noted the breadth of the phrase, since nothing that the passage has treated lies outside the ownership that the sentence states.

The Book's use of the same statement appears in the verses that open the sūrah, and the community's teachers read the two passages together. The sentence in this place therefore restates the ground on which the whole of the sūrah's instruction rests.

**The Disclosure and the Concealment**

The sentence states that God will bring the community to account for what it discloses and what it hides, and the community's teachers read the clause as the statement that the conduct of the heart belongs to the account of the person. The Book has treated the intentions of the givers throughout the passage, and the sentence here states the principle.

The commentators explained the phrase as the description of the matters that a person keeps within himself and those that he makes known, so that the account reaches the hidden as well as the visible. The community's scholars read the clause in the light of the passages about the knowledge of God, and they gathered the instances.

The teachers drew from the sentence the lesson that the community's reform begins within the person, since the Book addresses the state of the heart together with the conduct of the limbs. The passage's treatment of the secret gift and the hidden intention was read with the sentence.

**The Reports of the Community**

The reports of the community describe the distress that the sentence produced among the believers when it was received, and the material shows the companions grieving under the weight of the account that the verse names. The teachers read the accounts as the evidence of the seriousness with which the community received the instruction.

The reports also state that the two verses that close the sūrah were given in part to answer the disquiet of the believers, so that the account of the last passages was read with the account of the sentence. The community's scholars transmitted the material, and the teachers used it when they explained the arrangement of the sūrah's final verses.

The material shows the care with which the early community examined the place of its inward conduct, and the reports describe the questions that the believers brought to the Prophet ﷺ about the account. The sentence in this place supplies the statement that the community's anxiety answered.

**The Forgiveness and the Punishment**

The sentence states that God forgives whom He wills and punishes whom He wills, and the community's scholars read the clause as the statement about the authority that governs the account. The teachers noted the connection with the passages that describe the mercy of God, and they read the sentence with the verses that follow it.

The commentators explained the clause as the presentation of the two outcomes within the knowledge of God, so that neither the hope of the community nor its fear is misplaced. The Book's treatment of the subject elsewhere states the terms of the forgiveness and the punishment, and the community's scholars gathered the passages when they set out the subject.

The teachers drew from the clause the lesson that the account is conducted by the One whose decision is not subject to the judgment of His creatures. The community's preachers used the sentence when they spoke about the mercy that accompanies the warning.

**The Reports About the Intention**

The reports of the community preserve a saying of the Prophet ﷺ that describes the recording of the good that a person intends and performs, and the material states the reward that is written for the intention and for the deed. The teachers used the saying when they explained the sentence, and the community received it as the account of the mercy that accompanies the account.

The material shows the distinction that the community drew between the passing thought and the resolve of the heart, and the scholars of the community built their teaching of the subject upon the reports. The sentence in this place supplies the Book's own statement of the knowledge that records the intentions.

The commentators connected the sentence with the passages that treat the mercy of God and the passages that treat His justice, and they read the whole as one account of the relationship between the servant and his Lord. The community's teachers preserved the reading in their teaching.

**The Attribute at the Close**

The sentence closes with the statement that God is capable of everything, and the community's teachers read the attribute as the ground of the account that the verse has described. The scholars noted that the power named in the clause answers the doubt of the person who wonders how the hidden matters can be examined.

The wording also completes the statement of the ownership with which the sentence opened, so that the One who owns the creation is described as able to conduct the account of every part of it. The community's preachers used the pair of clauses when they spoke about the day of the return.

The Book's practice of ending its statements with a description of God appears throughout the sūrah, and the community's teachers read the arrangement as the statement that the whole of the community's life belongs to the account. The sentence therefore leaves the reader with the ownership, the knowledge, and the power of the One to whom the passage returns.

**What the Verse Establishes**

The sentence establishes the ownership of God over the heavens and the earth, the account that reaches what the community discloses and what it conceals, the forgiveness and the punishment that belong to His decision, and the power that comprehends all things. The community's teachers used the verse when they spoke about the intention and the account, and the sūrah's final two verses answer the anxiety that the sentence produced, so that the account of the grave matters is followed by the supplication that the community was taught.

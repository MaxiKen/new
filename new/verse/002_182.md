## Sūrah al-Baqarah 2:182

> Whoever suspects an error or an injustice in the will and brings about a ˹fair˺ settlement among the parties will not be sinful. Surely Allah is All-Forgiving, Most Merciful.

**Expanded Commentary**

**The Exception Stated**

The verse addresses the case in which a person fears that the testator has acted unjustly or sinfully and brings about a settlement between the parties, and it states that no sin attaches to him. The sentence qualifies the prohibition of the previous verse, and the community's scholars read the two as a single instruction.

The Book's method of providing for a case after stating a general rule appears throughout its legislation, and the reader is shown that the law takes account of the situations that its general rules might otherwise produce. What the sentence establishes is that the protection of a will does not extend to an arrangement that is itself unjust.

**The Fear of Injustice**

The commentators explained the situation the sentence describes. A testator might make a bequest with the purpose of enriching one of his heirs through another person, which would circumvent the rules that govern the shares of the estate, and the heirs might discover the intention after the will is made.

The term the verse uses for the injustice is the same word that appears in the report which describes such conduct as a serious offence, and the community's teachers connected the two. What the reader receives is a description of a case in which the arrangement itself is the problem, and the sentence authorises the parties to address it.

**Who Settles the Matter**

The commentators discussed the identity of the person who brings about the settlement. Some named the executor of the estate, others the witnesses whose testimony the Book requires, and others the heirs themselves, and the readings differ on where the responsibility falls.

The variety reflects the practical circumstances in which the matter arises, since any of the parties present may be the one who notices the problem. What the sentence establishes is that a person who intervenes to make the arrangement fair is not to be treated as one who alters a will, and the community's jurists applied the distinction when they considered cases in which an estate has been disposed of improperly.

**The Community's Duty**

The community's scholars drew a wider duty from the sentence, and they held that the adjustment or cancellation of an unjust bequest belongs to those who have the responsibility for the estate. The Book's legislation on inheritance and the reports about the shares of the heirs were read together with the verse, and the teaching that emerged placed the correction of injustice among the community's obligations.

What the sentence establishes is that the law of wills is not a private arrangement that the community watches from a distance. The Book provides for the intervention of those who know the matter, and the reader is shown that the disposal of property after death is subject to the standards of justice that govern the community's dealings.

**Charity During Life**

The commentators noted that the permission granted in the sentence does not diminish the virtue of giving during life, and they cited the reports in which the Prophet ﷺ described the person who gives while he is in good health and able to enjoy his wealth. The community's teachers used the passage to encourage generosity before the time when a person's property becomes the subject of a will.

The teaching places the bequest in its proper rank among the ways of disposing of property. What the Book prefers is that a person give from what he owns while he can still make use of it, and the provision for the will exists for what remains, and the sentence's subject is the fair handling of that remainder.

**The Names at the End**

The sentence closes with the statement that God is forgiving and merciful, and the community's teachers read the pair as the ground of the permission that has been granted. The person who intervenes in an unjust arrangement is told that his act involves no sin, and the attributes at the end of the sentence confirm the judgment.

The Book's practice of attaching the names to its rulings appears throughout the sūrah, and the reader is shown that the legislation's exceptions and provisions belong to a single source. What the sentence establishes is that the correction of injustice is an act of fidelity to the law rather than a departure from it, and the passage continues with the institution of the fast.

The two names correspond to the permission that the sentence has granted, since the person who intervenes in an unjust arrangement is told that his act involves no sin. The community’s teachers noted that the Book closes its rulings with a description of God, and they read the practice as a statement that the legislation and its exceptions belong to a single source. The reader is left with the assurance that the correction of injustice is an act of fidelity to the law, and the passage continues with the command to fast.

**What the Verse Establishes**

The sentence establishes the boundary of the rule that protects a will and provides for the case in which the document itself is unjust. The reader is given the general rule, the exception, and the attributes of the lawgiver, and the passage then turns to the discipline that the community observes for a month of the year.

## Sūrah al-Anʿām 6:66

> Still your people ˹O Prophet˺ have rejected this ˹Quran˺, although it is the truth. Say, "I am not a keeper over you."

**Expanded Commentary**

**A Statement That Acknowledges the Grief**

The verse does not open with an argument or a command. It states a fact about the Prophet's situation, and it states it in a way that acknowledges how heavy the fact was: your people have rejected this, though it is the truth. The Book speaks to its messenger here in the manner of one who knows what has been happening, and the sentence does not pretend that the rejection is small.

The sūrah has already recorded the accusations, the obstruction, the demands for signs, and the mockery, and the Prophet has been told that the people who did these things are not questioning his honesty but denying God's signs (6:33). This verse adds the element of personal grief, and the Book elsewhere shows how deep that grief went when it tells him that he might destroy himself with sorrow over people who would not believe (18:6; 26:3).

**"Although It Is the Truth"**

The clause about the truth is the pivot of the sentence. The rejection is not a matter of an unresolved dispute in which one party may turn out to be right; the Book has settled the question of what the Qur'an is, and the rejection is being recorded against that settled fact. The Book uses this construction repeatedly, placing the truth of the message next to the rejection of it as a way of naming the tragedy rather than merely the disagreement.

That pattern is worth seeing in full. The Book states the same combination in the sūrah of Yūnus, where the Prophet is told to say that the truth has surely come from his Lord and that whoever chooses guidance or straying does so for his own account, and then to say that he is not a keeper over the people (10:108). The sūrah of the Troops makes the identical statement when it says that the Book has been revealed with the truth for humanity and that the messenger is not a keeper over them (39:41). The claim is that the rejection happens in the presence of the truth rather than in its absence.

**The Office of Keeper and What It Excludes**

The Prophet is instructed to say that he is not a keeper over them, and the word used here appears twice more in this same sūrah. Earlier he is told to follow what is revealed to him and to turn away from the idolaters, in a passage that ends with the same denial of keepership (6:106–107), and earlier still he is told that his insight has come from his Lord and that he is not a keeper over the people (6:104). The repetition marks it as a settled feature of his commission.

A keeper in this sense is the person charged with the management and outcome of another's affairs. The Book denies that role to the Prophet in matters of belief, and it does so at the very points where the temptation to take that role would have been strongest. What is left to him is the delivery of the message and the reminder, which the Book describes in the sūrah of the Overwhelming when it says that his duty is only to remind (88:21).

**Why the Denial Is a Mercy to the Messenger**

Read in the context of the grief that the verse acknowledges, the sentence functions as a release. A man who believes that the fate of every person he speaks to depends on his own persuasiveness will be destroyed by the work, and the Book's answer to that condition is repeated in several places. The sūrah of the Cave asks whether the Prophet would destroy himself with sorrow over people who will not believe, and the sūrah of the Poets opens the same subject by mentioning the grief that eats away at him.

The Book's remedy is not indifference. The Prophet is told to be patient, to continue the reminder, and to keep the company of those who turn to their Lord, and the sūrah will give him instructions in the next verses about how to behave toward the people who mock the revelation. What is removed is the responsibility for the outcome, and the removal makes the work possible.

**The Boundary Between Warning and Force**

The verse also closes off a temptation on the other side. A messenger who is not accountable for results has no reason to pursue results through the methods that a custodian would use: pressure, bribery, alliance, or the manipulation of the community's social structure. The Makkans were offering exactly those instruments, and each of them required the Prophet to accept the role of keeper that the verse denies him.

That is why the Book's arguments against compulsion in religion are grounded in the doctrine of the messenger's office rather than in a general principle of tolerance. The man who delivers the news has no authority to force its acceptance, and the Book states the conclusion of that reasoning in terms of his own position: if he cannot secure belief, he cannot be blamed for its absence.

**What the Verse Leaves With the Audience**

The audience is left with a message that has been delivered and a messenger who will do what he can do and no more. The statement also leaves them with the fact of their own responsibility, since a warning that cannot be enforced by the one who delivers it places the decision entirely with the person who hears it.

The next verse states the timeline of that decision. The Book's habit in these exchanges is to move from the denial to the appointment, and the appointment is stated in words that leave the timing in God's hands while making the arrival certain.

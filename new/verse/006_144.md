## Sūrah al-Anʿām 6:144

> and a pair of camels and a pair of oxen. Ask ˹them˺, “Has He forbidden ˹to you˺ the two males or the two females or what is in the wombs of the two females? Or were you present when Allah gave you this commandment?” Who does more wrong than those who fabricate lies against Allah to mislead others without ˹any˺ knowledge? Surely Allah does not guide the wrongdoing people.

**Expanded Commentary**

**The Second Pair and the Completed Enumeration**

And a pair of camels and a pair of oxen. The verse completes the list of the animals whose status the audience had regulated, and the four pairs named across this verse and the one before it are the sheep, the goats, the camels, and the oxen. The Book's account of the created order elsewhere states that God made spouses for human beings and mates for the cattle (42:11) and that He created all things in pairs (36:36), and the enumeration in this passage belongs to that account of creation.

The sūrah of the Troops states that God produced four pairs of cattle for humanity and joins the statement to its account of the creation of human beings in the wombs (39:6). The passage in front of us draws the animals into the argument about what God has forbidden, and the enumeration supplies the field within which the audience's claim must be examined.

**The Repeated Question**

Ask them, Has He forbidden to you the two males or the two females or what is in the wombs of the two females? The question is put a second time over the second pair, and its repetition shows that the argument covers every kind of animal the audience's system had regulated. The three categories remain the same: the males, the females, and the unborn.

The Book's method in repeating the question is to leave the audience no category in which its rules might be located. The rules they had adopted distinguished among animals by whether they had been set apart, whether their backs had been declared off limits, and whether their offspring had been reserved for the men, and none of those distinctions corresponds to the categories the question proposes.

**The Challenge About the Commandment**

Or were you present when Allah gave you this commandment? The clause adds a second challenge, and it addresses the claim of divine authority directly. The audience asserted that God had forbidden certain animals, and the sentence asks whether they had witnessed the giving of such a command. The Book's answer to the claim is that no such revelation had been delivered to them, and the question requires them to say whether they were present at a moment no one had reported.

The Book's challenge to the audience's claims of knowledge appears throughout the chapter. The sūrah has told the Prophet to say that he has no knowledge of what the audience asserts and has described them as followers of assumption (6:116), and the verse in front of us puts the same challenge in the form of a question about witnessing.

**The Charge at the End**

Who does more wrong than those who fabricate lies against Allah to mislead others without any knowledge? The sentence is the sūrah's repeated formula, and it adds the element of misleading others. The sūrah used the formula at the beginning of the chapter, in the verse about those who invent a lie about God or deny His signs (6:21), and it used it in the passage about those who claim to have received revelation (6:93). The form in front of us identifies the harm the fabrication does to the community that accepts it.

The clause without any knowledge returns to the theme the sūrah has developed since its warning about the majority. The people who invented the restrictions had no revelation and no evidence, and the sentence states the effect of their invention on the people who followed them.

**The Declaration About Guidance**

Surely Allah does not guide the wrongdoing people. The verse closes with the statement about guidance that the sūrah has attached to several of its condemnations. The pairing of wrongdoing with the withholding of guidance appears in the Book's statements about those who persist in denial, and the sūrah has described the hearts of such people as turned away after their first refusal (6:110).

The sentence is not a statement about the audience's capacity but a declaration about the outcome of their chosen course. The sūrah has been careful to state that the signs were clear and that the responsibility belonged to the people who received them, and the verse in front of us applies the same principle to the fabricators of the dietary rules.

**What the Passage Establishes**

The two verses together remove the audience's dietary system from the realm of divine command. The animals were created in pairs, the rules the audience applied to them cannot be stated in the terms of the created kinds, no revelation had been given on the subject, and the people who invented the rules did so to mislead others. What remains is the position the sūrah will state in the next verse, where the Prophet declares what is actually forbidden.

The Book's treatment of the subject is therefore complete at this point in the argument, and the verses that follow supply the positive account. The sūrah has been examining the audience's religion piece by piece, and the passage in front of us closes the section on food with the claim of divine authority left unsupported.

**The Transition to the Next Verse**

The next verse records the Prophet's declaration that he finds nothing forbidden to eat in what has been revealed to him except carrion, running blood, swine, and what has been offered in the name of other than God, with the exception of the person compelled by necessity (6:145). The declaration answers the fabricated rules with the actual prohibition, and it states the limits of what the revelation forbids.

The passage that follows turns to the restrictions imposed on the Jewish communities and to the mercy that the sūrah's audience was receiving, and it then addresses the polytheists' argument about God's will (6:146–148). The verse in front of us closes the sūrah's challenge to the audience's dietary system, and the declaration that follows supplies what the system had obscured.

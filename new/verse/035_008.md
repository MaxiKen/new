## Sūrah Fāṭir 35:8

> Are those whose evil-doing is made so appealing to them that they deem it good ˹like those who are rightly guided˺? ˹It is˺ certainly Allah ˹Who˺ leaves to stray whoever He wills, and guides whoever He wills. So do not grieve yourself to death over them ˹O Prophet˺. Surely Allah is All-Knowing of what they do.

**Expanded Commentary**

**Moral Appearance Can Be Inverted**

The opening question exposes a deep disorder: a person can be surrounded by evil yet experience it as beauty. The problem is not only that he commits a wrong. His judgment has been trained to approve the wrong and distrust the good. A lie may seem clever, cruelty may be called strength, and obedience may be dismissed as weakness. Once that inversion takes hold, ordinary argument becomes difficult because the person no longer uses the same moral names for the same acts.

This is why the verse asks whether such a person is like one who is rightly guided. The answer is no, even if both possess intelligence, success, or eloquence. Guidance includes a sound moral perception that recognizes a thing according to its true weight. The Qur'an describes Satan making deeds attractive in 8:48 and 16:63, but attraction is not proof of goodness. A polished surface can hide corruption, and strong emotion can be evidence of manipulation rather than insight.

**Beauty Is Not the Same as Truth**

Human beings are moved by what appears beautiful, and Islam does not deny that beauty has a place. The danger comes when the heart makes appearance its final judge. A deed can be admired by a crowd and still be destructive. It can feel liberating while making the person dependent on appetite, reputation, or anger. Right guidance teaches the person to ask not only what is pleasing now but what Allah has declared beneficial and what consequence the act carries.

The Qur'an warns in 24:21 that Satan commands indecency and wrongdoing, while people would not have been purified without Allah's grace. The warning shows that moral error has a persuasive dimension. People are not always dragged into sin against their taste; they can be taught to like what harms them. Grace appears in the ability to pause, hear correction, and allow revelation to rename the act. A changed judgment is itself one of Allah's gifts.

**Divine Guidance Does Not Mean Arbitrary Misguidance**

The statement that Allah leaves astray whom He wills and guides whom He wills must be read with the verse's moral setting. It does not make people innocent machines or suggest that Allah misleads a person who sincerely seeks truth. The Qur'an also says that Allah does not change a people's condition until they change what is within themselves (13:11). Divine guidance is a gift, but turning toward or away from that gift has real human meaning.

Misguidance can be understood as the just consequence of a will that repeatedly chooses distortion. When a person insists on calling evil good, the capacity to see clearly is not protected from the result of that insistence. Guidance, by contrast, is not earned as a private achievement. It is sought, welcomed, and attributed to Allah. The two truths belong together: people are accountable for their response, and no one can claim that guidance was produced by his own power alone.

**The Prophet Is Released From Destroying Himself With Grief**

The instruction not to grieve himself to death addresses the Prophet's compassion. He wanted people to receive the message and feared the consequences of their refusal. His sorrow was not indifference; it was the pain of seeing people choose a path that would harm them. Yet the verse places a limit on that sorrow. The messenger may warn, explain, and pray, but he cannot manufacture an altered moral perception in another heart.

Similar instructions occur when rejection weighs heavily, including 18:6 and 26:3. Repetition does not reduce the Prophet's care. It teaches that care must remain within the boundary of servanthood. A teacher, parent, or caller to good can take the same lesson. Concern is virtuous when it leads to patient service; it becomes destructive when it assumes responsibility for a freedom Allah has left with the other person. The messenger's duty is faithful warning, not self-annihilation.

This knowledge also prevents the caller from confusing persistence with failure. A person may reject a warning today while the warning continues to work upon him later, and another may speak beautifully while concealing stubborn refusal. Allah knows which heart is moving toward truth and which is merely performing an appearance. The messenger's task remains honest proclamation, not the reading of hidden destinies.

That knowledge makes the warning precise rather than cruel. Allah does not confuse an honest mistake with a settled campaign against truth, and He does not mistake a persuasive exterior for repentance. The Prophet can therefore speak clearly while leaving each person's final state to the Lord who knows it completely.

**Allah Knows What the Inverted Heart Is Doing**

The final name, All-Knowing, restores confidence. Allah knows the action itself, the intention behind it, the pressures surrounding it, and the hidden process by which evil became attractive. No confused appearance deceives Him. This knowledge is a warning to the wrongdoer and a comfort to the messenger. The wrongdoer cannot hide behind public approval, while the messenger's effort is not lost because people dismiss it.

The verse therefore joins theology with pastoral restraint. Some hearts are guided and some remain turned away, but the Prophet is not asked to stop calling people to truth. He is asked to leave the unseen judgment to Allah and to continue his own obedience. The proper response to moral inversion is clarity without contempt, warning without despair, and reliance on the Lord who knows every deed better than the person who performs it.

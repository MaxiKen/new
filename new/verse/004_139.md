## Sūrah al-Nisāʾ 4:139

> who choose disbelievers as allies instead of the believers. Do they seek honour and power through that company? Surely all honour and power belongs to Allah.

### Expanded Commentary

**Seeking honour through the wrong alliance**

The hypocrites take disbelievers as allies instead of believers and seek honour through that connection. The question exposes a mistaken calculation: power borrowed from hostile forces may bring temporary status, but true honour belongs to Allah. A person who abandons justice to be accepted by the powerful has traded dignity for permission.

Alliance here concerns loyalty and support in a conflict, not ordinary commerce, neighbourliness, or kindness. The Qur’an’s own treaty provisions make that distinction essential. Muslims may cooperate in good and justice with people of different faiths while refusing to support persecution of believers or anyone else.

The verse’s practical cure is to seek honour through obedience, service, and truth. Status gained by flattering oppression is fragile; honour granted by God does not depend on the approval of those one betrayed.

**Honour cannot be borrowed from oppression**

A person who gains access by flattering an unjust power may receive a title while losing moral independence. The verse asks where honour actually comes from. Seeking Allah’s approval allows believers to cooperate wisely without selling the vulnerable person or principle that the alliance was supposed to protect.

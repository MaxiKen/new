## Sūrah al-Naḥl 16:43

> **We did not send ˹messengers˺ before you ˹O Prophet˺ except mere men inspired by Us. If you ˹polytheists˺ do not know ˹this already˺, then ask those who have knowledge ˹of the Scriptures˺.**

**Expanded Commentary**

**Messengers Were Mere Men Inspired**

Wa-mā arsalnā min qablika illā rijālan nūḥī ilayhim — We did not send before you except men We inspired. Mā arsalnā, We did not send, negates that messengers were angels or gods. Min qablika, before you, O Prophet, indicating continuity of messengership. Illā rijālan, except men, human males, not angels, not supernatural beings. Nūḥī ilayhim, We inspired them, revealed to them, from w-ḥ-y, to inspire, reveal secretly. Al-Ṭabarī explains that Makkan polytheists found it strange that messenger is human like them, eating food, walking in markets, as 25:7 says *What is this messenger who eats food and walks in markets?* (25:7). The verse answers that all messengers before you were men, not angels, inspired by Us. This is to make message relatable: a human messenger understands human needs, speaks human language, lives human life. Al-Rāzī notes that being men is wisdom: if messengers were angels, people would say we cannot follow angels because we are not angels; since they are men, following is possible.

**If You Do Not Know, Ask Those Who Know**

Fa-is'alū ahla al-dhikri in kuntum lā taʿlamūn — so ask people of remembrance, knowledge, if you do not know. Ahla al-dhikri means people of dhikr, remembrance, knowledge, specifically people of previous scriptures, Jews and Christians who have knowledge of previous messengers being men. Al-Ṭabarī says ahla al-dhikr are scholars of Torah and Gospel who know that messengers before Muhammad ﷺ were men, not angels, so they can confirm. Al-Qurṭubī adds that phrase is general and includes any knowledgeable people: if you do not know about something, ask those who know. This is principle for seeking knowledge: ask experts. In context, polytheists doubted that messenger could be human; they are told to ask those who have knowledge of previous scriptures, who will confirm that previous messengers were men. The verse therefore uses testimony of People of Book to refute Makkan objection.

**Why Human Messengers Are Mercy**

Sending men as messengers is mercy because it allows human connection. A human messenger can be example: he marries, has children, suffers, is persecuted, emigrates, is patient, trusts Lord, as verses 41-42 described. If messenger were angel who does not eat, does not suffer, does not emigrate, he could not be example for emigrants. Al-Zamakhsharī notes that rijālan, men, also implies maturity, strength, not children, indicating messengers were mature men capable of bearing responsibility. Nūḥī ilayhim, We inspired them, shows that though they are men, they are distinguished by revelation, not ordinary. Their humanity does not reduce their honor; it increases relatability while revelation increases guidance.

**The Principle Of Asking Knowledgeable**

Fa-is'alū ahla al-dhikri, ask people of remembrance, is principle that extends beyond this specific issue. Al-Rāzī says it establishes that when one does not know religious matter, one should ask knowledgeable, not speak without knowledge. This is opposite of polytheists who spoke without knowledge when they said revelation is fables and had Allah willed we would not have worshipped others. They spoke without knowledge; they are told to ask those who know. The verse therefore encourages seeking knowledge via questioning experts, which is method of learning. In Sūrat al-Niʿam, which emphasizes reflection, understanding, mindfulness, this verse adds asking as method: reflect yourself, but also ask those who have knowledge.

**Connection To Previous Verses About Emigration And Patience**

Verses 41-42 described emigrants who were patient and trusted Lord. Verse 43 says We did not send before you except men inspired, ask people of knowledge if you do not know. The connection is that emigration and patience of believers follow example of previous messengers who were men, were persecuted, emigrated, were patient. The Makkans who persecuted emigrants were repeating pattern of previous nations who persecuted messengers who were men. Asking people of knowledge would confirm that previous messengers were men and were persecuted and that their peoples were destroyed for denial, as verse 36 said travel and see fate of deniers. The verse therefore places current emigration in continuity of prophetic history.

**Linguistic Note On Rijāl And Dhikr**

Rijāl from r-j-l means men, mature males, from same root as rijl, foot, indicating one who walks, stands. In Qur'anic usage, rijāl often means men of strength and responsibility, not just male gender. Nūḥī from w-ḥ-y means to inspire, reveal, from same root as waḥy, revelation. Ahla al-dhikri, people of remembrance, from a-h-l, people, family, and dh-k-r, remembrance, knowledge, mention. Dhikr can mean Qur'an, previous scriptures, or knowledge generally. In kuntum lā taʿlamūn, if you do not know, from ʿ-l-m, to know. The verse's structure — We did not send before you except men We inspired, so ask people of remembrance if you do not know — moves from principle of human messengership to method of verification via asking knowledgeable, guiding deniers to check their objection against history and knowledge.

**A Lesson On Humanity Of Messengers**

Humanity of messengers is lesson that guidance comes through human example, not only divine command. A human messenger shows that tawḥīd, patience, trust, gratitude can be lived by human, not only angels. This encourages believers: if messengers who were men could be patient and trust Lord while being persecuted and emigrating, so can you. The verse also refutes idea that religion is for angels only; it is for humans, delivered by humans, lived by humans. The command to ask knowledgeable encourages humility: if you do not know, ask, do not deny. This humility is opposite of arrogance that says ancient fables and had Allah willed we would not have worshipped others.

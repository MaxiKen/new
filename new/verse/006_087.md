## Sūrah al-Anʿām 6:87

> And ˹We favoured˺ some of their forefathers, their descendants, and their brothers. We chose them and guided them to the Straight Path.

**Expanded Commentary**

**The Circle Widens Beyond the Names**

And We favoured some of their forefathers, their descendants, and their brothers. The verse extends the catalogue in three directions at once, and the three directions cover the whole of a family: the generations above the named prophets, the generations below them, and their contemporaries. The sūrah has listed specific men; this sentence says that the favour was not confined to them.

The Book uses the same threefold formula in its description of the descendants of Abraham and Jacob generally, and it uses it again in the sūrah of the Cattle's own account of the guidance given to the line. The effect of naming the three groups is to close off the possibility that the audience could argue from the catalogue that only exceptional individuals were favoured and that the rest were left outside. The favour moved through families and generations.

**The Two Verbs at the End**

We chose them and guided them to the Straight Path. The sentence states two distinct acts, and the Book uses both for the prophetic mission. To choose is to select a person for a purpose, and to guide is to set him on the road that leads to it. The sūrah of Maryam uses the same pair when it describes the prophets as those whom God has blessed among the descendants of Adam, of those carried with Noah, and of the descendants of Abraham and Israel, and as those whom God rightly guided and chose (19:58).

The pairing protects a truth about the religious life that the sūrah has been teaching throughout. Election without guidance would be a status without a path, and guidance without election would be a road that no one had been appointed to walk. The Book's descriptions of the prophets consistently hold the two together, and the sūrah states both in a single clause.

**The Straight Path as the Common Destination**

The phrase the Book uses for the road here is the same one it uses for the Prophet's own path in the sūrah's later declaration, where he says that his Lord has guided him to a straight path, a perfect way, the faith of Abraham the upright (6:161). The recurrence is not incidental. The catalogue of prophets and the sūrah's final statement of the Prophet's religion end at the same phrase, which tells the reader that the road named in this verse is the road the sūrah has been describing from its opening verses.

The Book attaches the same description to the guidance given to the community of believers in several places, and the sūrah of the Fatihah makes the request for that road the central petition of the prayer. What the prophets received is therefore presented as the same thing that the ordinary believer asks for each day, and the difference between them lies in the mission rather than in the destination.

**Favoured Families and the Question of Inheritance**

The verse's mention of forefathers, descendants, and brothers raises the question of what the favour means for the families of the prophets. The Book's answer elsewhere is that proximity to a messenger is not a guarantee. Noah's son was among the drowned, and the Book records Noah's plea and the response that the son was not of his household in the sense that mattered. Lot's wife was left behind, and the Book records Abraham's father among the idolaters.

The sūrah's own catalogue is careful in the same way. It names the guided rather than the lineage, and the sentence that follows this one states that the guidance is given to whoever God wills of His servants. The favour that runs through families in this verse is therefore a statement about God's practice of raising messengers from particular houses, not a promise that every member of those houses will be saved.

**The Book's Wider Use of the Formula**

The Book uses the language of choosing and favouring for whole communities as well as for individuals, and the sūrah of the Cow records that God made Abraham a role model for the people (2:124). The sūrah of the Family of Imran states the principle that God chose certain families above all people of their time (3:33), and the sūrah of the Pilgrimage says that God named the community of believers the ones who submit in the earlier scriptures and in this recitation (22:78).

Read against those passages, this verse describes a process that runs through history: God selects, guides, and produces a line of men whose task is to carry the message to their peoples. The sūrah's audience is being shown that the line is long, that it includes their own ancestor, and that their quarrel with the Prophet was a quarrel with the pattern the line represents.

**The Sentence That Follows**

The guidance given to all these men is named in the next verse as God's guidance, given to whoever He wills of His servants, and the verse after it makes the startling statement that if they had associated others with God their deeds would have been wasted (6:88). The two sentences take the catalogue that has just been assembled and use it as the basis for the sūrah's final argument about what the audience's practice would have cost even the greatest of the men whose names they honoured.

The reader is therefore meant to carry the names forward into those statements. The prophets were chosen and guided, and the guidance was a gift rather than a possession, and it could be lost. That is the lesson the sūrah draws from the list, and it states it without qualification in the next sentence.

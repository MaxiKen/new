## Sūrah al-Baqarah 2:282

> O believers! When you contract a loan for a fixed period of time, commit it to writing. Let the scribe maintain justice between the parties. The scribe should not refuse to write as Allah has taught them to write. They will write what the debtor dictates, bearing Allah in mind and not defrauding the debt. If the debtor is incompetent, weak, or unable to dictate, let their guardian dictate for them with justice. Call upon two of your men to witness. If two men cannot be found, then one man and two women of your choice will witness—so if one of the women forgets the other may remind her. The witnesses must not refuse when they are summoned. You must not be against writing ˹contracts˺ for a fixed period—whether the sum is small or great. This is more just ˹for you˺ in the sight of Allah, and more convenient to establish evidence and remove doubts. However, if you conduct an immediate transaction among yourselves, then there is no need for you to record it, but call upon witnesses when a deal is finalized. Let no harm come to the scribe or witnesses. If you do, then you have gravely exceeded ˹your limits˺. Be mindful of Allah, for Allah ˹is the One Who˺ teaches you. And Allah has ˹perfect˺ knowledge of all things.

**Expanded Commentary**

**The Verse Named**

The sentence is the longest in the Book, and the community's teachers read it as the charter of its agreements. It addresses the believers with the instruction to write down the debts that they contract for a fixed term, and it sets out the duties of the scribe, the debtor, the witnesses, and the two parties of the contract.

The verse follows the reminder of the account and supplies the practical means by which the community's dealings are protected. The Book has treated the wealth of the community through many verses, and the sentence here supplies the arrangement that keeps its contracts sound.

The commentators noted the length of the passage and the number of matters that it gathers, since the single sentence treats the writing, the witnesses, the cases of weakness, the journey, and the trade of present goods. The community's scholars read it as a body of law rather than as an instruction on a single point.

**The Writing Required**

The sentence instructs the community to write down the debt that is contracted for a term, and the community's jurists derived the recommendation of the written contract from the clause. The teachers described the purpose of the instruction, which is the preservation of the right of each party and the removal of the disputes that follow a forgotten term.

The commentators noted the phrase about the fixed term, since the sentence concerns the agreements whose payment is deferred. The community's scholars also discussed the trade of present goods later in the verse, and they read the two parts together.

The scholars of the community drew from the clause the lesson that the community's dealings are to be conducted with the means that preserve them. The Book's concern for the rights of the believers appears throughout the passage, and the sentence supplies the arrangement by which those rights are documented.

**The Scribe's Duty**

The sentence instructs the scribe to write with justice between the parties and forbids him to refuse the task, and the community's teachers read the clause as the account of a duty that the person with knowledge owes to the community. The wording names the instruction that the scribe has received, so that his skill is presented as a trust.

The commentators also explained the clause about the dictation, since the debtor is the person who states the terms of the debt and the scribe writes what he says. The teachers noted the protection that the arrangement gives to the person who owes, and the community's scholars built the rules of the contract upon the detail.

The sentence adds the instruction that the debtor is to be mindful of God and to diminish nothing from the amount. The community's jurists used the clause when they set out the duties of the parties, and the teachers read it as the statement that the written record is to be true.

**The Weak and the Guardian**

The sentence provides for the debtor who is unable to dictate because of weakness or youth, and it instructs that his guardian dictate with justice. The commentators discussed the cases that the clause covers, and the community's scholars described the arrangement by which the interests of the person are protected.

The teachers noted the care of the passage for those who cannot conduct their own affairs, and they read the clause as the statement that the community's law attends to the weak among its members. The Book's treatment of the orphan's property in the earlier passage was read with the sentence.

The community's jurists drew from the clause the rules of guardianship in the matters of property, and the books of law record the discussions that grew from the verse. The sentence in this place supplies the principle that the guardian acts in the interest of the person he represents.

**The Witnesses Named**

The sentence instructs the community to call two witnesses from among its men and provides that where two men are not available, a man and two women are to be called. The commentators explained the provision, and the community's scholars discussed the subject at length in the light of the passage and the practice of the community.

The teachers noted the purpose that the sentence states, which is the reminder that one witness may give to the other if either of them errs. The community's jurists built the rules of testimony upon the verse, and the sentence in this place supplies the framework of the subject.

The passage also instructs that the witnesses are not to refuse when they are called and that the writing is not to be avoided whether the amount is small or great. The community's scholars read the two clauses as the statement that the community's agreements are to be documented whatever their size.

**The Exceptions and the Closing**

The sentence provides that the trade of present goods need not be written, while the witnesses are to be called when the community buys and sells. The commentators noted the distinction between the contracts whose payment is deferred and those that are completed on the spot, and the jurists drew the rules from the two cases.

The sentence closes with the instruction that neither the scribe nor the witness is to be harmed and with the reminder that God teaches the community and knows all things. The community's teachers read the closing as the statement of the balance that the passage preserves, since the persons who serve the community's contracts are protected from the parties.

The reports of the community connect the verse with the forward sales of the city and with the practice of the Prophet ﷺ in the market, and the teachers used the material when they explained the passage. The sentence in this place supplies the community with the law of its agreements.

**What the Verse Establishes**

The sentence establishes the writing of the debts, the justice required of the scribe, the dictation of the debtor, the arrangement for the person who cannot dictate, the witnesses who are to be called, the exception for the trade of present goods, the protection of the scribe and the witness, and the knowledge of God over the community's dealings. The community's teachers used the verse when they set out the law of contracts, and the sūrah's next verse provides for the agreements that are made on a journey.

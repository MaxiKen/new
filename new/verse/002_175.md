## Sūrah al-Baqarah 2:175

> They are the ones who trade guidance for misguidance and forgiveness for punishment. How persistent are they in pursuit of the Fire!

**Expanded Commentary**

**The Exchange Restated**

The verse states that those described have purchased error at the price of guidance and punishment at the price of forgiveness, and it repeats the commercial image of the previous sentence in a sharper form. Two items are named, and each of them is the opposite of what the offenders gave up.

The Book's use of this construction gives the reader the measure of the loss in a single line. What was available is named first, and what was taken in its place is named second, so that the exchange is visible without further explanation. The community's teachers read the verse as a summary of the condition that the previous sentence described at length.

**Guidance and Error**

The first exchange concerns direction, and the sūrah has used the term for guidance from its opening verses onward, where the Book is described as a guide for the reverent and the community asks to be shown the straight path. The verse states that those described have taken error instead, and the term for error describes a deliberate straying rather than an innocent mistake.

The choice of words indicates that the people in question had access to the direction and preferred its opposite. The community's scholars noted that the Book's vocabulary for these matters treats the two paths as alternatives that a person selects, and the sentence leaves no room for the suggestion that the offenders were unaware of what they were doing.

**Forgiveness and Punishment**

The second exchange concerns the treatment that follows, and the two terms are again exact opposites. The Book's term for forgiveness describes the covering and the setting aside of a fault, and the punishment named in the sentence is the consequence that the offenders have chosen in its place.

The pairing of the two exchanges in a single clause gives the sentence its force, since the reader is shown that both losses were avoidable. The Book has described the terms on which the concealment could have been repaired in the earlier passage about those who repent and make the matter clear, and the present verse reports the outcome for those who did not.

**The Question at the End**

The sentence closes with the question of how the offenders will endure the Fire, and the commentators explained the wording in several ways. Some read it as a question about what will give them patience in the condition that awaits them, others as a way of saying that nothing will, and others as a general expression of astonishment at the conduct that led them there.

The Book uses questions of this kind where the answer is beyond the reader's ability to supply. The community's teachers read the sentence as a statement about the condition of those who have made such an exchange, and the question leaves the reader to consider what patience would mean in the place described.

The sentence’s question also measures the distance between the two sides of the exchange, since what was taken cannot sustain the person who took it. The community’s teachers used the verse when they spoke of the difference between what a person gains in the world and what he is able to carry with him, and they read the question as the Book’s way of stating that the loss is total. The reader who considers the two commodities named in the sentence is left with a transaction in which nothing remains for the one who made it.

**The Connection to the Preceding Verse**

The verse follows the account of the concealment and the fire that the offenders consume, and it draws out what the exchange consists of. The Book has described the transaction in commercial terms in the earlier sentence, and it now names the two commodities that were traded away.

The sequence gives the passage its shape, and the reader who follows it from the concealment through the exchange to the question at the end has been shown the offence, the motive, and the outcome in three short statements. The community's teachers used the passage when they spoke about the seriousness of trading knowledge for gain.

The two sentences belong to one statement, since the earlier verse describes what the offenders consume and this one names what they surrendered to obtain it. The Book often pairs a description of a condition with an account of how it was reached, and the reader is given the act and the consequence in the space of two lines. The community’s scholars read the pair as a complete account of the offence that the passage has been describing, and the verses that follow explain the ground of the judgment.

**The Vocabulary of Buying and Selling**

The Book uses the language of trade for the conduct of those who deal with the revelation in a number of passages, and the sūrah has already described a community that sold its covenant for a small price. The repetition of the image across the sūrah indicates that the Book treats the matter as a familiar category of offence.

The vocabulary also gives the community a way of describing its own conduct, since the Book applies the language of buying and selling to the exchange of the present life for the next in passages addressed to the believers as well. The verse in this place states the terms of the exchange that the offenders have made, and the reader is left with the question of how he conducts his own.

**What the Verse Establishes**

The sentence establishes that the offenders have surrendered guidance and forgiveness, that the surrender was deliberate, and that the condition they have entered is described in the Book's own terms. The reader is given the account of the transaction, and the passage continues with the statement of the reason for it, which is the treatment of the Book that God sent down in truth.

**The Question's Effect on the Reader**

The closing question does more than describe the offenders. It places the reader in the position of a person who is asked to imagine standing in the condition described, and the Book uses that position when it wants a warning to remain with the hearer. What the sentence leaves behind is not only the information about a group of people but the question of what would sustain a person who had made such a choice, and the community's preachers have used the verse in that way.

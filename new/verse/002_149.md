## Sūrah al-Baqarah 2:149

> Wherever you are ˹O Prophet˺, turn your face towards the Sacred Mosque. This is certainly the truth from your Lord. And Allah is never unaware of what you ˹all˺ do.

**Expanded Commentary**

**The Command Repeated**

The verse repeats the command to face the Sacred Mosque, and the Book states it again in the following verse with a qualification about the fears of the community. The repetition is a mark of the subject's importance for the sūrah, since the direction of prayer is the practical signature of the community's identity in the world.

The Book's habit of repeating a command is connected to the circumstances in which it was received. A community that had prayed in one direction for a period of time, in a city where another community prayed in a different one, required the new instruction to be stated with clarity, and the sūrah supplies that clarity by returning to it at intervals.

The repetition also has the effect of settling the question for later generations. The command is not recorded as a decision taken in Madinah under particular circumstances; it is stated as a rule that applies wherever the community may be. The Book records it three times within a few verses, and the pattern has been read by the community’s scholars as evidence that the direction of prayer belongs among the matters that define the community’s practice rather than among those that were settled and then left behind.

**"Wherever You Go Out"**

The opening phrase covers every departure and every place, and the commentators explained it as a reference to travel and to the broader geography of the community's life. The command is not tied to Madinah, to its mosque, or to the region of the sanctuary; a Muslim who leaves his home faces the House wherever he may be.

The sūrah has already stated the command in its general form, and this verse states it again in a way that removes any impression that the obligation applied only to the Prophet ﷺ or to the place where the revelation was received. The direction became a feature of the community's practice in every location it would reach, and the Book states the rule in the terms that make that clear.

**Travel and the Supererogatory Prayer**

The community's scholars derived from this kind of command the treatment of prayer during travel, and the reports describe the practice of praying supererogatory prayers on a mount or an animal in whichever direction it faced. The Book's own instructions on the fear prayer and on the shortened prayer during travel occur later in the sūrah, and the two subjects were read together by the jurists.

The verse therefore concerns practical worship rather than abstract orientation. A community whose members travelled for trade, for pilgrimage, and for the campaigns of the early period needed an instruction that could be carried with them, and the sentence supplies one that applies at every departure.

**"The Truth From Your Lord"**

The sentence then confirms that the command is the truth from God, and the phrase repeats the language the sūrah has used in this passage about the direction and about the message. The community is given the assurance that the instruction it follows is not a human arrangement subject to revision by its critics.

The Book attaches this confirmation to the command rather than to the arguments of the disputants, and the placement is instructive. The direction of prayer is established by what came from God, and no material gathered from the other communities could settle the matter. The reader is therefore directed to the source of the command rather than to the controversy around it.

**The Closing Attribute**

The verse ends with the statement that God is not unaware of what the people do, and the sūrah has used the same attribute at the close of its charges against the earlier communities. Here the attribute accompanies the command to the believers, and it carries both a reassurance and a caution: the community's obedience and its failures are equally seen.

The Book's use of the attribute in both contexts indicates that it is not directed at one party. It appears after the account of those who conceal the truth and after the command to the community to face the House, and the reader learns that the same knowledge encompasses everyone addressed in the passage.

**The Direction as an Act of Loyalty**

The command's repetition in this passage, with the confirmations attached to it, presents the direction of prayer as an act by which the community declares whose command it follows. The sūrah established the religious identity of the community earlier when it described the confession of faith and the religion of Abraham, and the direction is the physical form of that confession.

The reports about the change describe communities objecting to it and the believers accepting it without argument, and the verse's wording assumes that response. What the sentence asks for is not an explanation that will satisfy the objectors but a practice that will be maintained, and the assurance that accompanies the command is placed after the command rather than before it.

The reports about the change make the same point from the other side. The groups that objected did not question the mechanics of the practice; they questioned the authority behind it. The community’s acceptance of the new direction was therefore an affirmation that its worship follows the command it has received, and the sūrah’s insistence on that acceptance explains why the command is restated with such care.

**Where the Verse Leads**

The next verse repeats the command once more and explains that it is given so that people may not have a case against the community. The following verses then describe the community that has been given the knowledge and those who refuse it. The verse in this place belongs to the sequence that establishes the community's distinctive practice and then leaves the disagreement with God, and it does so in the plainest terms available.

The repetition continues in the verse that follows, where the command is given along with the purpose it serves. The three statements together form the sūrah’s final word on the direction of prayer before it turns to the community’s blessings and its trials. A reader who has followed the sequence from the question of the foolish to this command has been given the whole matter twice over, once as an answer to an objection and once as an instruction to be carried out.

## Sūrah al-Baqarah 2:177

> Righteousness is not in turning your faces towards the east or the west. Rather, the righteous are those who believe in Allah, the Last Day, the angels, the Books, and the prophets; who give charity out of their cherished wealth to relatives, orphans, the poor, ˹needy˺ travellers, beggars, and for freeing captives; who establish prayer, pay alms-tax, and keep the pledges they make; and who are patient in times of suffering, adversity, and in ˹the heat of˺ battle. It is they who are true ˹in faith˺, and it is they who are mindful ˹of Allah˺.

**Expanded Commentary**

**A Correction About Righteousness**

The verse states that righteousness does not consist in turning the face toward the east or the west, and it then describes the person who is righteous in a series of clauses. The opening statement addresses the dispute about the direction of prayer that occupied the earlier part of the sūrah.

The Book's correction does not cancel the direction of prayer, and the commands to face the Sacred Mosque remain in force in the verses that precede this one. What the sentence does is to place the practice within a larger account of what a religious life consists of, and the community's teachers read it as a warning against treating a ritual as the whole of the religion.

**The Occasion Reported**

The commentators report that the verse was revealed after the community came to Madinah, where the Muslims saw the Jewish community praying toward the west and the Christian community toward the east. The sentence addresses that situation directly, and the reports show the Muslims being taught what the comparison between the direction of prayer and the conduct of a life amounts to.

The teaching is therefore placed in the middle of a passage about the direction of prayer, and it does not contradict the commands that surround it. What the Book does is to define the term that the communities were using against one another, and the definition it supplies is a list of the things a righteous person does.

**The Articles of Faith**

The first part of the description names belief in God, the Last Day, the angels, the Books, and the prophets, and the commentators noted that this list is the basis of the community's creed. They also pointed to the report in which the Prophet ﷺ answered a question about faith by naming these items.

The list is short and comprehensive, and the community's scholars used it when they set out what a believer holds. What the sentence establishes is that the righteous person is described first by what he believes, and the order of the clauses shows that the deeds that follow are presented as the fruit of the belief rather than as a substitute for it.

**The Giving of Wealth**

The sentence then describes the righteous as those who give of their wealth despite their attachment to it, and it names the recipients: relatives, orphans, the poor, travellers, beggars, and captives. The commentators explained the phrase about the love of wealth as describing the difficulty of parting with what a person values.

The list covers the people who are close to the giver and those who are not, and the community's scholars drew from it a scheme of obligations and supererogatory giving. The Book's use of the same phrase elsewhere in a passage about feeding the needy shows that the wording is one of the Book's ways of describing generosity, and the sentence places it among the marks of the righteous.

**Prayer, Alms, and Pledges**

The description continues with the establishment of the prayer, the payment of the alms, and the keeping of promises, and these three items cover a person's duties to God and to others. The community's teachers noted that the prayer and the alms are named separately from the giving that precedes them, which indicates that the obligatory alms is a distinct item.

The keeping of pledges is included among the marks of the righteous, and the Book treats the fulfilment of a covenant as a matter of seriousness elsewhere in the sūrah. The sentence therefore describes a person whose religious practice extends from the worship he offers to the word he gives, and the community's scholars used the list to explain what the term righteous covers in the Book's vocabulary.

**Patience in Three Conditions**

The last item in the description is patience during suffering, hardship, and the heat of battle, and the commentators explained the three terms as degrees of difficulty. The first describes illness and loss, the second poverty and need, and the third the moment of combat itself.

The Book's treatment of patience in the earlier passage named the trials that the community would face, and the present sentence names the conditions in which patience is shown. The community's teachers read the three as a summary of the circumstances in which the quality is required, and the sentence places the quality among the marks of a righteous person alongside belief and generosity.

**True Faith and Mindfulness**

The verse closes by stating that those described are the ones who are true in their faith and who are mindful of God, and the two terms sum up the description that has been given. The community's scholars noted that the sentence does not describe a rank above the ordinary believer but the believer as the Book describes him.

The passage has been dealing with communities that argued about the direction of prayer and with those who traded the revelation for a price, and the verse in this place supplies the alternative. What the reader receives is a portrait rather than a rule, and the community's teachers used it when they explained that the religion consists of belief, worship, generosity, and endurance together, and that the practice of any one of them without the others is not what the Book calls righteousness.

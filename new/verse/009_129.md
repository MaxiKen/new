## Sūrah al-Tawbah 9:129

> But if they turn away, then say, ˹O Prophet,˺ “Allah is sufficient for me. There is no god ˹worthy of worship˺ except Him. In Him I put my trust. And He is the Lord of the Mighty Throne.”

**Expanded Commentary**

**Rejection Does Not Cancel the Mission**

The verse addresses the moment when people turn away after warning and mercy have been offered. The Prophet is not commanded to collapse into despair or to compromise the message in order to keep every listener. He is told to declare that Allah is sufficient. The mission remains grounded in Allah even when people refuse it.

This does not mean that the servant stops caring. The previous verse established deep concern for people's suffering. Sufficiency means that the Prophet's worth, courage, and final success do not depend on the audience's immediate acceptance. He can continue inviting without making acceptance the condition of peace.



The declaration that there is no god worthy of worship except Allah restores the center of the heart. Rejection by people can tempt a leader to seek approval from another audience, another power, or his own reputation. Tawḥīd releases him from these substitutes.

The statement is not merely theoretical. If Allah alone is worshipped, then fear, hope, obedience, and ultimate dependence belong to Him alone. The servant can use lawful means and value human support without giving created beings the place of the Creator. This makes resilience possible when human support disappears.

**Trust Is Responsible Reliance**

The Prophet says he trusts Allah. Tawakkul does not mean abandoning effort or refusing counsel. The Messenger warned, taught, planned, and endured. Trust means that after using the means, he leaves the outcome to Allah and remains obedient whatever the outcome may be.

A believer who trusts Allah still seeks treatment, earns lawfully, protects dependents, and addresses injustice. He does not call passivity reliance. Nor does he treat planning as a rival to faith. Trust purifies effort by removing the illusion that the servant controls results independently.

**The Mighty Throne Signals Absolute Sovereignty**

Allah is the Lord of the Mighty Throne. The image points to the summit of the created order and therefore to a sovereignty greater than every human authority. If the Lord of the Throne supports the Messenger, rejection by a group of people cannot make the truth small.

This remembrance also corrects fear. A ruler, institution, or hostile crowd may seem powerful, but all their power exists within Allah's kingdom. The believer does not underestimate danger, yet he refuses to treat danger as ultimate. The Lord who governs the greatest order can govern the details of a servant's trial.

When Allah is sufficient, the servant can accept praise without needing it and endure blame without being destroyed by it. He can correct his own mistake without fearing that admission will end his identity. He can refuse a corrupt alliance even when the alliance offers immediate security.

This freedom does not make a person cold. He still values friendship and grieves rejection. It prevents grief from becoming worship of approval. The heart returns to Allah after every human response and asks what obedience requires next.

**The Declaration Helps in Repeated Trials**

The verse gives words that can be repeated when people turn away. Repetition is not empty formula when the heart understands what it says. The believer remembers that Allah is enough, renews tawḥīd, entrusts the matter to Him, and recalls His sovereignty.

These words can steady a parent whose advice is rejected, a teacher whose students mock, or a community whose justice is opposed. They do not promise that the difficulty will disappear immediately. They promise that the servant is not without a Lord, a purpose, or a refuge.

The sūrah has described hypocrisy, repentance, sacrifice, learning, leadership, and mercy. Its final statement brings all these matters back to dependence on Allah. Communities need action, but action without reliance becomes anxiety and pride. They need knowledge, but knowledge without worship becomes argument. They need courage, but courage without tawḥīd becomes aggression.

The believer therefore carries this declaration into every responsibility. He serves people because Allah commands service, warns because Allah commands truth, and trusts because Allah rules the Mighty Throne. If people turn away, he does not turn away from Allah. That is the strength of the final refuge.

This final reliance also protects the servant from measuring truth by immediate success. A message may be rejected today and still be true; a crowd may applaud today and still be misguided. Allah's sufficiency gives the believer patience to repeat what is right without forcing hearts. He continues to use wisdom, accepts counsel, and learns from mistakes, but he does not sell the truth for approval. The Lord of the Mighty Throne is a greater refuge than any audience, so the servant can remain compassionate without becoming dependent on acceptance.

Trust also changes how the servant receives delay. He may not see the victory, reconciliation, or answer he hoped for, yet he continues to worship without declaring the effort pointless. The Lord of the Mighty Throne sees a larger order than the servant can see. This does not remove the duty to review strategy and correct error. It means that correction is made from faith rather than panic. Allah is sufficient for the heart that acts carefully, learns honestly, and leaves the unseen result with Him.

Reliance is thus the final calm of a servant who has done what he can and knows who holds the result.

The declaration can be spoken after careful planning, after disappointment, and after success. In every case it returns the heart to the same truth: no created cause is independent, and no rejection can remove Allah's care from the servant who trusts Him. This is why the chapter closes with reliance rather than with dependence on the crowd.

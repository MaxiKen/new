## Sūrah al-Aʿrāf 7:42

> As for those who believe and do good—We never require of any soul more than what it can afford—it is they who will be the residents of Paradise. They will be there forever.

**Expanded Commentary**

**Faith and Righteous Action Together**

The verse names the believers and joins their faith to their deeds, and the pairing runs through the Book's descriptions of salvation. The account of the Cow gives the good news to those who believe and do good that they will have gardens beneath which rivers flow (2:25), the sūrah of the Cave states that those who believe and do good will have the gardens of Paradise as a dwelling, and the sūrah of the Cattle states that the messengers were sent as bearers of good news and warners so that people would have no argument against God (6:48). The verse in front of us states the two conditions in the order the Book uses throughout.

The deeds are named alongside the faith because the Book treats the two as inseparable. The Heights' own account states that the believers are those who follow the messenger and the Book, and the sūrah's earlier verses describe the response of those who mend their conduct (7:35). The verse in front of us supplies the outcome for that response, and the mention of the good deeds states what the response looks like in practice.

**No Soul Is Tasked Beyond Its Capacity**

The verse inserts a clause in the middle of the promise: God tasks no soul beyond what it can afford. The statement appears in several passages, and the sūrah of the Cow closes its longest verse with the same formula and asks God not to burden the community beyond its strength (2:286); the sūrah of the Believers states that God never requires of any soul more than it can afford and adds that with Him is a record that speaks the truth (23:62); the sūrah of the Cattle uses the same clause in its list of the commands about fair dealing (6:152); and the sūrah of Divorce states the principle in the rules about maintenance, instructing each person to provide according to his means (65:7).

The clause has consequences in the practical obligations of the religion. The Cow's account states that God intends ease for the community and not hardship (2:185), and the rules of the fast, the prayer, and the alms were adjusted for travellers, the sick, and those without means on that basis. The clause in front of us states the principle from which those adjustments follow, and the Book's mention of it in a verse about the Garden's residents reminds the reader that the deeds required of him are proportionate to what he has.

**Why the Clause Sits Here**

The placement of the clause between the mention of the believer's deeds and the promise of the Garden serves the passage's account of the two groups. The verses before it have described the proud who denied the signs and were excluded from the Garden (7:40), and the reader might measure his own capacity against the attainments of others. The clause states that the measure is the individual's own, and the commentary tradition reads it as a reassurance that what is required of each person is within his reach.

The sūrah of Divorce repeats the same reassurance in a command about spending, and the sūrah of the Cow's closing verse asks God not to impose what the community cannot bear. The Book's statements about the burden of the previous communities appear in the same verse, where the community asks to be spared what was laid on those before it. The clause in front of us states the divine practice in the arrangement of the obligation.

**The People of the Garden and Their Permanence**

The verse states that those who believe and act rightly are the people of the Garden and that they will remain there, and the Book uses the language of permanence for the reward of the believers throughout. The account of the Cow describes the gardens beneath which rivers flow and states that their people will abide there, and the sūrah of the Cave states that the gardens are the dwelling of those who believed and did right. The verse in front of us states the assignment and the duration in the same sentence.

The permanence follows the permanence that the Book attributes to God's promise. The Family of Imran's account states that the reward of the Garden is prepared for the mindful, and the sūrah of the Prophet states that the Garden is the reward of those who are mindful of their Lord. This verse places the believers in the dwelling that the Book describes as lasting, and the contrast with the bed and the covering of the previous verse is exact.

**The Usage of Capacity in the Book's Commands**

The Book applies the principle of capacity to specific obligations, and the applications show what the clause in front of us means in practice. The Cow's rules on the care of children and the maintenance of the divorced are written to the measure of the obligation of the one charged, and the sūrah of Divorce states that the one with generous means should spend from his abundance while the one with limited resources should spend from what God has given him. The verse here states the general rule of the arrangement.

The Book's teaching also states that the reward is not measured by the quantity of the deed except as the deed exceeds the obligation of the one who performs it. The sūrah of the Cattle states that God does not burden a soul beyond its capacity, and the sūrah of the Night Journey specifies the limits of the commands about the orphan's wealth and the measure in trade. The clause in front of us belongs with those passages, since each of them sets the requirement within reach.

**Where the Verse Sits**

The verse stands forty-second in the chapter, and it answers the description of the condemned in the verse before it. The passage then continues with the account of the people of the Garden, their gratitude for the guidance they received, and the announcement of what they have been given (7:43). The present verse opens that sequence with the statement of who the people of the Garden are.

The reader who has been told in the sūrah's opening verses to follow what has been sent down now reads the outcome of that following. The faith and the deeds are named, the measure of the requirement is stated, and the dwelling that follows is described as lasting. The chapter's next verse describes what the people of that dwelling say when they enter it.

**The Transition to the Next Verse**

The next verse states that God will remove whatever rancour is in the hearts of the people of the Garden, that rivers will flow beneath them, and that they will say that praise belongs to God who guided them to it (7:43). The statement of who the people of the Garden are in front of us is followed by the account of their condition and their words.

The two verses together describe the state of the believers in the Hereafter. They are named by their faith and their deeds, and the requirement laid on them is measured to their capacity; and the verses that follow describe the peace of their hearts, the water beneath them, and the announcement that what they have received is the reward of what they did.

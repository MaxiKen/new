## Sūrah al-Nisāʾ 4:148

> Allah does not like negative thoughts to be voiced—except by those who have been wronged. Allah is All-Hearing, All-Knowing.

**Expanded Commentary**

**God Does Not Like Negative Thoughts Voiced — Except by Wronged**

The verse states *lā yuḥibbu Allāhu al-jahra bi al-sūʾi mina al-qawli illā man ẓulim* — God does not like, does not love, the loud voicing — *jahr* — of evil, bad, of speech, except by those who have been wronged, oppressed. *Al-jahra bi al-sūʾi mina al-qawli* — to voice evil loudly, to proclaim bad speech — was explained as public negative speech, complaint, accusation, vile talk, prayer against, as the sūrah has shown that those who hide from people but God is with them where they plot displeasing speech (4:108) have *sūʾ al-qawl* — evil speech — that He dislikes, and that advocates who dispute in this life for wrongdoers use evil loud speech (4:107–109). *Lā yuḥibbu* — does not like — parallels *does not like the deceitful sinner* (4:107) and *does not like the stingy boastful* (4:37); to voice *sūʾ* loudly is disliked as *shuḥḥ* and *khiyānah* are. *Illā man ẓulim* — except those who have been wronged — was explained as permission for the oppressed who cried for a helper — *mustaḍʿafīn* who cry *“deliver us from land of oppressors, appoint for us a saviour and helper”* (4:75) — to voice their wrong, to complain of oppression, to invoke against oppressor, to report injustice. The sūrah elsewhere commands to stand for justice though against yourselves (4:135) and permits settlement best (4:128) yet acknowledges *“no good in most secret talks”* (4:114); public *sūʾ* is the broad prohibition where secret plotting was already condemned, with exception for the wronged as the previous exception for helpless (4:98) — not every loud complaint is evil, the wronged’s is allowed.

**Allah Is All-Hearing, All-Knowing**

The seal *wa kāna Allāhu Samīʿan ʿAlīman* — And God is All-Hearing, All-Knowing, was ever — was explained with *Samīʿ* — hearing every *jahr* — loud voicing — and every *najwā* — secret talk — (4:114) and every night plot displeasing (4:108) — and *ʿAlīm* — knowing whether loud voicing is *sūʾ* or complaint of wronged, whether settlement sought graciously (4:128) or inclination total (4:129). *Samīʿ ʿAlīm* has sealed the command to return trusts and judge fairly (4:58) as *Samīʿ Baṣīr*, and the both rewards are with Him (4:134) as *Samīʿ Baṣīr*; here *Samīʿ ʿAlīm* fits speech: He hears loud evil and knows its motive, as He hears the oppressed cry (4:75) and knows the *ḥīla* absent (4:98). Early reports illustrated *man ẓulim* with one whose property was usurped or who was wronged by a spouse’s *nushūz* and who voices it for redress, not for slander — He hears and knows the difference between reporting wrong for justice and throwing sin on innocent as *buhtān* (4:112). The pairing says: public evil speech is disliked, yet He hears the wronged’s allowed voicing and knows its equity.

**A Verse That Follows Grateful Faith With Discipline of Loud Speech**

Placed after *why should He punish if you are grateful and faithful? He is ever Appreciative, All-Knowing* (4:147), this verse follows grateful faith with discipline of loud speech. The sūrah has shown that whoever is grateful and faithful, He appreciates and knows; to voice *sūʾ* loudly is ingratitude in speech, as the deceitful’s night speech displeases Him (4:108) and advocates’ dispute for self-wrongers in this life is loud *sūʾ* — therefore *He does not like*. The exception *except wronged* was read as the sūrah’s justice: the oppressed whose help was commanded to be fought for (4:75), whose earth is made spacious (4:100), whose prayer is shortened and guarded (4:101–102) — their loud voicing is not *sūʾ* but *ḥaqq* — demand for right. The sūrah that warns *most secret talks have no good* (4:114) except three, now warns *loud evil has no love* except one — the wronged’s. The sūrah that says *He wills to make clear* (4:26) and *taught you what you did not know* (4:113) makes loud evil undesirable because knowledge and wisdom will judge it as *ʿAlīm* hearing as *Samīʿ*.

**Loud Evil Versus Allowed Complaint**

The distinction — *jahr bi al-sūʾ* disliked versus *man ẓulim* allowed — was explained as same distinction as *no good in most secret talks* except charity, kindness, reconciliation (4:114): many loud complaints have no love, except the complaint of the wronged seeking charity of justice, kindness, reconciliation. The sūrah has shown that *ṣulḥ* — settlement best (4:128) — is sought where she fears *nushūz*, and that settlement may involve voicing grievance — that voicing is the *man ẓulim* allowed, not *sūʾ*. The *man ẓulim* was linked to *“do not incline entirely leaving the other suspended”* (4:129) — the suspended’s voicing of being suspended is allowed as wronged. Classical notes added that the wronged may invoke God against oppressor, or report oppressor to authority (4:59 referral), but not transgress beyond measure — the loud voicing remains measured by the All-Hearing.

**A Short Verse That Anchors Speech Between Love and Hearing**

Though only *“He does not like loud negative speech except by wronged, He is All-Hearing, All-Knowing,”* the verse anchors speech between love and hearing. The chapter has commanded to refer disputes to God and Messenger (4:59), to verify reports (4:83), to be sure before fighting (4:94), to judge by what He shows (4:105), to command charity, kindness, reconciliation seeking His pleasure (4:114), to stand for justice even against yourselves (4:135); all require voicing, but the verse adds qualifier: voicing *sūʾ* for its own sake is not loved, voicing wrong for justice is allowed and heard by the All-Hearing. For the weak this is the lightest of speeches: where you are wronged — the orphan deprived (4:127), the wife suspended (4:129), the party whose testimony is twisted — you may voice, and the One who can remove humanity (4:133) hears and knows, appreciates gratitude (4:147) yet dislikes loud *sūʾ* where no wrong was done. The next verse will set the higher ethic: whether you reveal or conceal a good or pardon an evil — surely He is Ever-Pardoning, Most Capable (4:149) — so that the allowed voicing may still be surpassed by pardoning.

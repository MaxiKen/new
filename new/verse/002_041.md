## Sūrah al-Baqarah 2:41

> Believe in My revelations which confirm your Scriptures. Do not be the first to deny them or trade them for a fleeting gain. And be mindful of Me.

**Expanded Commentary**

**Confirmation Before Demand**

“Believe in My revelations which confirm your Scriptures” (2:41). The Qur’an does not arrive as stranger to the People of the Book. It arrives as muṣaddiq — confirming what you have with you. The phrase repeats the sūrah’s earlier promise that guidance will come “from Me” (2:38): when it comes, it will cohere with what was given before. The confirmations are twofold in the sources: the Qur’an confirms the validity and teachings of Torah and Gospel in what they reliably teach — Oneness, justice, mercy — and it confirms their foretelling of the Prophet (7:157). On the second reading, “confirming what you have with you” becomes a summons: if your books foretold him, to confirm those books is to believe in him.

The point is pastoral as well as polemical: belief is not asked as betrayal of the prior covenant but as its fulfilment. To violate the covenant after affirmation (2:27) here takes the shape of refusing the confirming sign.

**“Do Not Be the First to Deny” — Priority as Responsibility**

“Do not be the first to deny them” (2:41). Lā takūnū awwala kāfir bih — “be not the first disbeliever in it.” The superlative is not chronological trivia; it is about quality of conviction, as noted in the source with parallels: “I was commanded to be the first of those who submit” (6:14; 6:163) and Moses’ “I am the first of the believers” (7:143). First here means lead, exemplar, pace-setter. The Children of Israel, as custodians of revelation, were best placed to recognize revelation. To be first to deny when one should be first to acknowledge is a graver rank than later, less-informed denial.

The warning also presumes knowledge. Firstness is blameworthy because recognition was possible. The sūrah will soon say they “hide the truth knowingly” (2:42) and “recite the Book” while enjoining piety on others (2:44). Knowledge makes the priority count.

**“Do Not Trade Them for a Fleeting Gain” — The Price That Is Not a Price**

“or trade them for a fleeting gain” — wa lā tashtarū bi-āyātī thamanan qalīlā (2:41). To sell My signs for a small price — cross-referenced in the source to 2:174; 3:187; 16:95 — names the perennial economy of religious distortion: to exchange truth’s clarity for status, stipend, patronage, or power. The price is called qalīl — paltry, fleeting — even when it looks large in the world, because compared to Torah fidelity or Qur’anic guidance and its eternal reward it is negligible. The idiom of commerce is judged, not denied: elsewhere God invites a “goodly loan” multiplied (57:11) and a commerce that saves (61:10). Trading signs for gain is bad commerce; lending the self to God is good commerce. The ledger differs by buyer.

The source notes the commerce is not only money: any small advantage — leadership, tribal favour, ease with the crowd — that trades away a clear āyah counts. The “first to deny” and the “seller” meet in the same person when denial preserves advantage.

**“And Be Mindful of Me” — The Guard That Makes the Sale Impossible**

“And be mindful of Me” — wa iyyāya fattaqūn (2:41). Taqwā — mindfulness, guarding, reverence — is the inner guard that makes the bad trade unthinkable. The exclusive “Me alone” is again emphatic: be mindful of Me, so the market’s noise does not set the price. The verse opened with guidance to believe the confirming revelation; it closes with the character that enables that belief: a heart that counts Allah’s regard as dearest.

**Why This Verse Matters Between Covenant and Confusion**

2:40 said “fulfil your covenant and I will fulfil Mine” (2:40) and “stand in awe of Me alone” (2:40). 2:41 specifies what fulfilling looks like for this audience: believe the confirming revelation; do not lead denial; do not sell signs. The next verse will specify how selling and leading happen: by mixing truth with falsehood and concealing truth knowingly (2:42), then by ritual and social failures (2:43–44). The sequence moves from principle to practice: covenant → confirmation and price → confound and conceal.

**Living the Price Test Today**

The contemporary form of “a paltry price” (2:41) is any fee accepted for softening a clear sign: a teaching diluted to keep a patron, a witness muted to keep a post, a ruling bent to keep a crowd. The verse does not forbid earning; it forbids pricing revelation. The check is simple: if clarity must be hidden for gain to remain, the gain is thamanan qalīlā.

And the contemporary form of “do not be the first to deny” (2:41) is the temptation of the knowledgeable to lead denial. Those who can recognize because they have studied are warned most: to be first to see should mean first to affirm, not first to cavil.

**What the Verse Does Not Claim**

It does not claim Torah or Gospel as currently textualized are identical to Qur’an, nor does it deny common origin. It claims confirmation (taṣdīq) — coherence and fulfilment — sufficient to obligate belief when the confirming revelation arrives, especially for those who possess foretellings (7:157). To reduce “confirming what you have with you” to mere bibliology misses its summons.

**A Note on Transmission**

The report linking “confirming” to foretelling of the Prophet in Torah and Gospel is well-represented in tafsīr (citing 7:157) and should be presented as commentary interpretation, not as a quoted Qur’anic gloss with a hadith chain unless a Sahih source is cited. What is firm is the Qur’anic phrasing: My revelations confirm your Scriptures — and therefore call for belief, not first denial, and for fidelity, not commerce (2:41).

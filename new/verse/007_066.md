## Sūrah al-Aʿrāf 7:66

> The disbelieving chiefs of his people responded, “We surely see you as a fool, and we certainly think you are a liar.”

**Expanded Commentary**

**The Leaders Who Disbelieved**

The verse reports the answer of the leading men of ʿĀd, and it identifies them as those who disbelieved before it reports what they said. The Book's accounts of the communities consistently place the refusal with the notables, and the sūrah's earlier narrative reported the same group answering Noah in almost the same terms (7:60). The verse in front of us supplies the second instance of the pattern, and the identification of the speakers as the disbelievers states their condition before their words are given.

The term for the leaders indicates the men whose standing in the community gave their opinion its weight, and the Book's narratives present them as the ones who answered for the people. The sūrah of the Believers reports the disbelieving chiefs of Noah's people dismissing the messenger, and the sūrah's later accounts of Thamūd name the arrogant leaders among those who rejected the warning (7:75). The verse in front of us states the composition of the group that refused Hūd.

**The Charge of Folly**

The first charge is that the messenger is foolish, and the term describes a deficiency of understanding rather than a moral fault. The sūrah's earlier account reported the leaders of Noah's people charging him with manifest error (7:60), and the charge in front of us escalates to a defect in the messenger's mind. The Book's narratives show the same escalation in other accounts, and the sūrah of the Poets reports the people of Noah calling their messenger possessed.

The charge is answered in the next verse by the messenger's denial in the same words, and the answer separates his person from his office in the manner of the earlier account. The Book's use of the same exchange in each narrative shows that the accusations were made without substance, and the sūrah's reader is given the messenger's denial before the account proceeds to the reminder of the favours that the people had received.

**The Charge of Falsehood**

The second charge is that the messenger is counted among the liars, and the phrase describes him as one of a known class rather than as a single offender. The Book's accounts of the communities use the same accusation against the messengers, and the sūrah of the Poets reports the people of ʿĀd rejecting their messenger in the same manner. The verse in front of us states the two charges together, and the pairing covers both the messenger's judgment and his honesty.

The charges are presented as the opinion of the leaders rather than as a demonstration, and the phrasing states that they think and consider rather than that they know. The Book's descriptions of the opponents' arguments frequently note the absence of evidence, and the sūrah of the Cattle asks what knowledge the audience possesses for its claims. This verse supplies the form of an accusation that rests on impression, and the messenger's reply states what he actually is.

**The Reversal of the Charge**

The Book's accounts of the communities turn the charges of the leaders against the leaders themselves, and the sūrah's record of the destroyed peoples states that those who were destroyed were the wrongdoers. The sūrah of the Cow reports the disbelievers calling the believers fools while the description belongs to those who made it (2:13), and the sūrah of the Heights' later verses state that those who were taken by the earthquake were the ones who had denied their messenger. The verse here states the accusation, and the narrative supplies the reversal.

The Book's teaching about the judgment of people by their standing rather than by their message appears throughout the sūrah, and the leaders in front of us judge the messenger by what they consider his social place. The sūrah's next verses state the messenger's denial and then his reminder to the people of what they had been given, and the reminder is the evidence that the leaders had not examined.

**The Reply That Follows**

The messenger's answer in the next verse denies the folly and claims the office of a messenger from the Lord of all worlds (7:67), and the reply uses the structure of the answer that Noah gave to the same kind of charge. The sūrah's accounts keep the exchange brief, and the brevity places the weight of the narrative on the reminders and the warnings that follow. The present verse states the accusation that the reply addresses.

The Book's descriptions of the messengers' responses to abuse include patience and persistence, and the sūrah of Hūd reports the messenger continuing his appeal after the leaders had dismissed him. The verse under discussion supplies the occasion for that persistence, and the account that follows shows the messenger turning from the leaders to the people with the reminder of their own history.

**Where the Verse Sits**

The verse stands sixty-sixth in the chapter, and it follows the call of Hūd and precedes his reply. The verses that follow report the reply, the reminder of the favours the people had received, and the challenge that the leaders issue to the messenger (7:67–70). Here the verse supplies the answer of the second community to the second call in the sūrah's series.

The reader who has seen the leaders of Noah's people charge their messenger with error now sees the leaders of ʿĀd charge theirs with folly and falsehood. The pattern is the same, the words are harsher, and the account proceeds in the same direction; and the sūrah's next verses state the reply of the messenger and the terms of the argument that follows.

**The Transition to the Next Verse**

The next verse reports the reply of Hūd, that there is no folly in him and that he is a messenger from the Lord of all worlds (7:67). The charge in front of us is answered in the messenger's own words, and the account then continues with the description of his work and with the reminder of the favours that his people had received.

The two verses together form the exchange that the sūrah's narratives repeat with each people. The leaders dismiss the messenger, the messenger states his office, and the argument proceeds to the evidence; and the sūrah's following verses carry the second account to its conclusion in the destruction of the people.

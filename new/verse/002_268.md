## Sūrah al-Baqarah 2:268

> The Devil threatens you with ˹the prospect of˺ poverty and bids you to the shameful deed ˹of stinginess˺, while Allah promises you forgiveness and ˹great˺ bounties from Him. And Allah is All-Bountiful, All-Knowing.

**Expanded Commentary**

**The Two Promises**

The sentence sets the threat of Satan beside the promise of God, and the community's teachers read the pairing as the description of the two voices that speak to the person who considers a gift. The passage has just required the good thing to be given, and the verse here identifies the fear that rises against the command.

The wording speaks of what Satan threatens and of what God promises, so that the two parties are named and the two expectations are set out. The community's scholars noted the form of the sentence, since it presents the matter as a choice between two accounts of what the future holds.

The Book's treatment of the human condition elsewhere describes the suggestions that reach the heart, and the teachers gathered the passages when they explained the subject. The sentence in this place supplies the clearest account of the manner in which the reluctance to give takes shape in the mind of the believer.

**The Threat of Poverty**

The commentators explained the first clause as the description of the fear that the person feels for the property he has earned, since the thought of what may be lost accompanies the decision to give. The teachers described the way the threat works, because the fear is presented as a certainty about the future while the outcome remains in the hand of God.

The community's scholars read the clause in the light of the Book's other passages about provision, which state that the wealth of a person is written for him and that nothing reaches him except what has been appointed. The sentence therefore exposes the argument of the fear as the invention of the one who proposes it.

The reports of the teachers describe the experience of the early community in the same terms, and the material shows the fear named as the obstacle that the passage addresses. The command to give from the good things is thus protected by the reminder of the One who supplies them.

**The Command to Indecency**

The second element of the first clause describes the command toward indecency, and the commentators explained the phrase as the statement that the same suggestion leads a person from the withholding of charity to the commission of other offences. The teachers described the progress of the matter in the soul, since the habit of refusal grows into the loss of the good and then into the deed itself.

The teachers of the community observed the connection between the two parts of the sentence. The fear of poverty is placed beside the invitation to indecency, so that the reluctance in the matter of wealth is presented as the beginning of a course rather than as a single decision.

The Book's treatment of the soul elsewhere describes the stages by which a person is drawn away from the good, and the teachers used the passage when they spoke about the tendencies that the believer is required to resist. The sentence in this place supplies the description of the influence that the community's preachers named.

**The Promise of Forgiveness and Bounty**

The sentence then names what God promises, which is forgiveness and a bounty from Him, and the community's scholars read the clause as the answer to the threat that precedes it. The first term provides for the faults of the person who gives, and the second supplies the abundance that the fear had placed in doubt.

The teachers drew attention to the order of the two promises. The forgiveness comes before the bounty, so that the community's anxiety about the faults of its own conduct is addressed together with the fear about its property.

The wording places the bounty as coming from God, so that the increase of the giver is not drawn from a hidden source but from the hand of the One who has promised it. The jurists of the community used the clause when they encouraged the believers whose means were modest, and the passage's earlier parables of the harvest were read with it.

**The Reports About the Two Inspirations**

The reports of the community preserve a saying of the Prophet ﷺ that describes the suggestions that reach the heart of a person, and the material states that the angel brings the promise of the good while the other brings the invitation to evil. The teachers used the saying when they explained the sentence, and the community received it as the account of the struggle that the verse describes.

The commentators built their explanation of the passage on the two movements that the report names, so that the person is presented as the place in which two accounts of the future are offered. Scholars of the community drew from the material the lesson that the decision belongs to the person to whom both are addressed.

The report's instruction was preserved in the community's teaching of the passage, and the teachers repeated it in their exhortations to the believers. The sentence in this place supplies the Book's own statement of the matter that the saying describes.

**The Attributes Named**

The verse closes with the description of God as bountiful and knowing, and the community's scholars explained the pair as the ground of the promise that the sentence has stated. The first name, which also carries the sense of a generosity that no measure limits, answers the fear of poverty, and the second answers the anxiety about the intention with which the gift is given.

The teachers noted that the two names belong to the attributes that the earlier verses of the passage have named, so that the same description closes the instruction about the gift and the account of the promise that accompanies it. The early teachers of the community read the repetition as the confirmation of the subject.

The attributes also complete the answer to the threat. The poverty that the fear describes is measured against the bounty of God, and the hidden condition of the heart is placed before the One who knows it. The sentence therefore leaves the community with the promise rather than the fear.

**What the Verse Establishes**

The sentence establishes the threat that Satan presents, the command toward indecency that accompanies it, the promise of forgiveness and bounty from God, and the attributes that close the verse. The community's teachers used the passage when they answered the reluctance of the believers and when they described the two suggestions that contend in the heart, and the sūrah's next verse names the wisdom that God grants, so that the gift of understanding follows the promise of the bounty.

## Sūrah al-Baqarah 2:5

> It is they who are ˹truly˺ guided by their Lord, and it is they who will be successful.

**Expanded Commentary**

**A Seal Made of Two Pronouns**

The verse that has defined the mindful does not add a new quality. It seals. *“It is they who are upon guidance from their Lord, and it is they who shall prosper”* (2:5) repeats the pronoun — *ulāʾika* … *wa-ulāʾika* — so that the subject and the outcome are locked to the same group. The structure is restrictive in both directions: guidance belongs to them, prosperity belongs to them, and the particle *hum* that will recur in later portraits of the rejectors (2:6) and the wavering (2:8) marks a deliberate sorting. The sūrah has not yet named the other two sorts; it first makes clear that the first sort is not an aspiration but a location — “upon guidance,” as one is upon a road. Prosperity is then not a second reward added to guidance; it is what guidance, when persisted in, becomes.

**Upon Guidance — Not Inside It**

*ʿAlā hudā* — “upon guidance” — says that guidance is something one stands on, not something one carries. The preposition matters because the Qur'an could have said *“they are guided”* (*muhtadūn*) and conveyed the state; it says *they are upon guidance* to convey the surface. The same preposition appears in the opposite portrait: *“those who have incurred wrath”* are described as *upon* wrath, and the *“astray”* as wandering off a path. Guidance is thus presented as an elevation that must be maintained. That is why the same root *h-d-y* will be invoked by every worshiper who recites *“Guide us to the straight path”* (1:6) daily; the path asked for in al-Fātiḥah is the same elevation announced here in al-Baqarah, and the answer to the petition is not only a direction but a standing.

**From Their Lord — The Source Named at the Point of Success**

*“From their Lord”* — *min rabbihim* — names the source at the moment when success could be misattributed to the virtues just listed. The three marks of (2:3) and the two of (2:4) could be read as human achievements that earn a wage; the phrase intervenes to say they are channels of a gift that originates elsewhere. *Rabb*, as the Fātiḥah’s commentary has detailed, gathers master, arranger, and nurturer in one term. To be “upon guidance *from their Lord*” is to be upon a guidance that the Lord Himself supplied, sustains, and will judge. That is why the Qur'an pairs this verse with the remembrance that *“no soul shall be recompensed except according to what it earned, and no bearer shall bear another’s burden”* remains true, and yet guidance itself is not merely earned; it is given, then walked, then prospered in. The initiative remains divine even when the walking is human.

**The Name Prosperity Gives to Salvation**

*Al-mufliḥūn* — “those who shall prosper” — is not a synonym for “those who shall be saved” in the narrow sense of escaping punishment, though it includes that. The root *f-l-ḥ* evokes cleaving, tilling, cultivating — the farmer who splits the earth and prepares it for yield. Classical lexicons link *falāḥ* to *filāḥah* (agriculture) and to *fallāḥ* (cultivator), so that prosperity is imagined as what happens when preparation meets growth. Early commentators note that the same root appears in the adhān that calls to these very acts: *“Come to prayer, come to prosperity”* (*ḥayya ʿalā al-ṣalāh, ḥayya ʿalā al-falāḥ*), so that the five-times-daily summons and this once-and-for-all proclamation echo each other. Prayer is the tilling; prosperity is the harvest. To translate *mufliḥūn* merely as “successful” captures the outcome but loses the agrarian memory that the Qur'an invites: success is not a sudden grant but a cultivated result.

**Why Prosperity Follows Guidance Directly, Without a Second Clause**

The verse does not say “they are upon guidance and they do good deeds and therefore they shall prosper.” It says *they are upon guidance and it is they who shall prosper*, with no intervening condition. The syntax teaches that prosperity is not a separate project. If guidance is truly inhabited — belief in the unseen held, prayer kept standing, spending released, revelation assented to, Hereafter held with certainty — the flourishing is not added; it follows as daylight follows sunrise. That is why the same term appears in later sūrahs as the summary of a life the Law has shaped: *“He has prospered who purifies his soul”* (87:14; 91:9). Purification is the tilling; the verse in al-Baqarah names the class of purifiers; the later verses describe their crop.

**Guidance and Prosperity as the Answer to the Fātiḥah’s Petition**

The seam between the two sūrahs is not editorial but theological. The worshiper closed al-Fātiḥah by asking for the straight path, naming it as the path of those blessed, and seeking refuge from the two deviations (1:6-7). The very next sūrah answers with a portrait of the blessed — the mindful — and then declares that the blessed are precisely those who shall prosper. The correspondence is exact: the Fātiḥah’s *“guide us”* is met by al-Baqarah’s *“upon guidance”*; the Fātiḥah’s *“those You have blessed”* is met by al-Baqarah’s *“it is they”;* the Fātiḥah’s warning against those who incurred displeasure and those astray is met, in what follows, by the portraits of those who knowingly reject (2:6-7) and those who waver (2:8-20). The present verse is the hinge. It tells the petitioner that the path he asked for is not an idea but a people, and that to be among them is already to have been guided.

**They Are Guided — Not They Have Been Guided**

The nominal sentence *ulāʾika ʿalā hudā* has no verb. That verblessness matters. Had the verse said *“they have been guided,”* guidance would be a past event; as a nominal sentence it is a present standing. The mindful are not people who once received guidance and now possess it as a credential; they are people who remain upon it, and the verse therefore also contains a warning. To be upon something is to be able to fall from it. The standing requires the same continual establishment (*iqāmah*) that was required for prayer. That is why the Qur'an elsewhere warns those who have been given knowledge and then turn away, and why it pairs every assurance with an admonition to be mindful.

**A Definite Class, Not a Private Feeling**

*It is they* — *ulāʾika* — points at a distance, like *dhālika* in (2:2). The guidance just described is not “wherever one feels guided”; it is *that* guidance, the one whose marks have been enumerated. The demonstrative draws a circle. Inside the circle are those who exhibit the five marks; outside it are those who will be sorted next. The sūrah will spend the next fifteen verses detailing the outside — first those who openly deny even when signs are presented (2:6-7), then those who claim belief while harboring disease (2:8-20) — so that by the end of the opening, the reader knows not only what guidance looks like from within but what its absence looks like from without. The seal of (2:5) is therefore also a map legend: the prospering are one color; the rest of the map will now be shaded in.

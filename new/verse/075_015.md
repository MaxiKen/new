## Sūrah al-Qiyāmah 75:15

> despite the excuses they come up with.

**Expanded Commentary**

**Excuses Survive Even This**

The previous verse established that a person will testify against himself, and this one adds something that stops the reader from treating that confession as easy or automatic. Even then, with the record opened and his own soul bearing witness against him, he produces excuses. The persistence is the point. A person's defence of himself is not a position he argues because it is strong; it is a reflex that survives the collapse of every argument he ever made for it. The Qur'an's realism about human beings is consistent here: the same man who denied the Day without evidence will go on explaining himself in front of the Day without prospects, because defending himself is what he does. The excuses are not a defence strategy. They are a symptom that has outlasted its usefulness. The Qur'an's realism here matches its account of human beings elsewhere, in this life as on that Day. Wherever a fault is named, the same reflex is visible: the account is adjusted, the circumstance is widened, the responsibility is distributed among other people, and the speaker emerges as the person who had least to do with what happened. The sūrah is saying that the reflex does not break at the grave. That is why the confession in v. 14 needs no external pressure to explain it — the pressure comes from the collapse of the habit's usefulness, not from any change in the habit itself.

**The Word Being Used**

The Arabic term is the plural of an intensive form built on the root for excuse, and its shape suggests something done repeatedly and with effort. This is not a single plea but a stream of them, produced as each previous one fails. English renderings that flatten it into *excuses* lose that element of ongoing manufacture. The same word appears in the Qur'an on the lips of people in this life, offering reasons to the Prophet for what they had failed to do, and the response they receive is instructive: *“They will make excuses to you ˹believers˺ when you return to them. Say, ‘Make no excuses, ˹for˺ we will not believe you. Allah has already informed us about your ˹true˺ state.’”* (9:94). Excuses are addressed here to a party who cannot be deceived by them and who does not need to investigate them, which is precisely the situation on the Day the sūrah is describing.

**Two Ways of Reading the Clause**

The commentators divided over how this verse attaches to the one before it. On the first reading it is a concession: he testifies against himself *even though* he has his excuses ready, meaning the testimony is so strong that it overrides his defence. On the second it is a rejection: he casts about for excuses, and none of them will be accepted. The first reading gives the verse a rhetorical force, since it measures the strength of the self-witness by what it overcomes. The second gives the excuses their own clause and makes the verse a verdict on pleading. The second is the stronger reading, because the Qur'an says the same thing elsewhere in explicit terms — *“the Day the wrongdoers’ excuses will be of no benefit to them.”* (40:52) — and because the sūrah has already closed every avenue of escape in v. 11. Either way, the outcome is identical: the plea fails.

**What Excuses Are Made Of**

The materials are always the same, and they are worth naming because they are recognisable from ordinary life. Circumstance is the most common: the times were hard, the pressure was real, the options were narrow. Company comes next — everyone was doing it, and the man who held back was the odd one. Upbringing, temperament, ignorance, and necessity follow, and behind all of them sits the same structural move: the transfer of responsibility from the agent to something else. Every excuse is a re-description of a choice as an event that happened to someone. That is why no excuse can be answered by more information; the dispute is never about the facts. It is about whether the person who did the thing is the person who owns it, and on that Day the answer is given by the person himself.

**The Plea the Qur'an Records**

Scripture is specific about what the pleading sounds like, and the examples are sobering. Some will swear that they were never associators at all: *“Their only argument will be: ‘By Allah, our Lord! We were never polytheists.’”* (6:23). Others will swear to God as they used to swear to people, *“thinking they have something to stand on. Indeed, it is they who are the ˹total˺ liars.”* (58:18). What these scenes share is that the lie is not tactical. A person who has spent decades telling a story about himself has come to believe it, and the exposure of the story is experienced as an assault rather than as a correction. The Qur'an diagnoses the failure at its root elsewhere: the assumption that God did not really know much of what they were doing (41:22). Fix that assumption and the excuses lose their function.

**Why No Plea Can Work There**

The failure of the excuses is not a refusal to hear them. It is that the conditions which made them plausible have been removed. An excuse works in this life because it is offered to someone who cannot see the whole picture, or who can be worn down, or whose opinion matters more to the speaker than the truth does. None of those conditions obtain at the reckoning. The Witness is the One whose knowledge was never in question, the record is complete, and the person's own soul is on the side of the prosecution. The Qur'an warns the living about this in the plainest possible terms: *“There will be no refuge for you then, nor ˹grounds for˺ denial ˹of sins˺.”* (42:47). The time for the plea that works is the time before the Day arrives.

## Sūrah al-Anʿām 6:70

> And leave those who take this faith ˹of Islam˺ as mere play and amusement and are deluded by ˹their˺ worldly life. Yet remind them by this ˹Quran˺, so no one should be ruined for their misdeeds. They will have no protector or intercessor other than Allah. Even if they were to offer every ˹possible˺ ransom, none will be accepted from them. Those are the ones who will be ruined for their misdeeds. They will have a boiling drink and painful punishment for their disbelief.

**Expanded Commentary**

**The Command to Leave and the Command to Remind**

The verse begins by telling the Prophet to leave a group and ends by telling him to remind them, and the combination states the Book's position on people who treat religion as entertainment. They are to be left alone with their chosen course, and they are not to be abandoned without a warning. The two commands are not in tension; the leaving is a refusal to argue further, and the reminder is the last form of care that remains.

The Book uses this combination elsewhere. The sūrah of the Cave tells the Prophet to say that the message is the truth from his Lord and that whoever wills may believe and whoever wills may disbelieve (18:29), and the sūrah of the Troops ends a similar discussion by describing the messenger as a warner who is not a keeper. The Book's mode with the heedless is to state the fact clearly and then to let them face it.

**"Play and Amusement" as a Description of Conduct**

The phrase describes people whose relation to the message is that of an audience watching a performance. The Book uses the same language to describe the worldly life itself, saying that it is no more than play, amusement, luxury, mutual boasting, and competition in wealth and children (57:20), and it says that those who took the faith as amusement and play and were deluded by the life of this world will be told on the day of judgment that they are ignored just as they ignored the meeting of that day (7:51).

The two images belong together. A person who treats the revelation as entertainment has already accepted the priority the Book assigns to the worldly life, and the Book's description of that life as play is not contempt for the world but a statement about its duration. The same pair appears in the sūrah of the Kneeling, where people are told that they made a mockery of God's revelations and were deluded by the worldly life (45:35).

**"Deluded by Their Worldly Life"**

The word translated as deluded describes a person who is deceived by something that presents a good appearance, and the Book uses it for the deception worked by the present life and by Satan. The delusion in this verse is not a mistake about facts but a wrong estimate of value, in which the affluence of a man's surroundings is taken as evidence about his standing.

The sūrah has been dismantling that estimate since the passage about the poor believers. The Book told the Prophet not to dismiss the humble worshippers whose devotion the notables regarded as an embarrassment (6:52), and it said that the differences between people are a test by which God distinguishes the grateful (6:53). The people in this verse are the ones who failed that test, and their amusement is the form the failure took.

**The Reason for the Reminder**

So no one should be ruined for their misdeeds: the verse gives the motive for the warning, and the motive is a refusal to accept destruction. The Book states that God does not desire injustice for His servants, and it describes the sending of messengers as an act of mercy precisely because a people who were never warned would have had an argument on the day of judgment.

The reminder is therefore not a formality that precedes the sentence. It is the last stage of the invitation, and the Book's language about it is urgent: the same sūrah that declares the wrongdoers will be seized at the height of their confidence also records the sorrow of the Prophet over people who would not listen (6:33). The command in this verse shows the direction of the Book's own preference.

**No Protector, No Intercessor, No Ransom**

The verse then describes what the reminder is meant to prevent. They will have no protector or intercessor other than Allah, which is the same statement the sūrah made in connection with the warning given to those who fear the gathering (6:51), and even if they offered every possible ransom it would not be accepted. The Book repeats that image where it says that a disbeliever possessing everything in the world twice over could not ransom himself from the punishment of the day of judgment (5:36), and where it describes the wrongdoers offering what they have in the world in exchange for escape (39:47).

The sūrah of the Cow states the same three denials in a single verse about the day when no soul will help another and no intercession will be accepted and no ransom taken (2:48). The Makkans' religious system rested on the assumption that their patrons and their gods would negotiate on their behalf, and the Book closes that door in every sūrah where it describes the reckoning.

**The Drink and the Punishment**

The verse ends with a description of the outcome: they are ruined for their misdeeds, and they have a boiling drink and a painful punishment for their disbelief. The sūrah of the Cave mentions the same boiling liquid in its account of what those who take their faith lightly will be given when they cry for relief (18:29), and the Book refers to it in several descriptions of the Fire.

The final clause attaches the punishment to disbelief rather than to the mocked scripture, which is consistent with the sūrah's treatment of denial as an activity with consequences. The passage ends here, and the sūrah turns next to a series of arguments about God's exclusive right to worship, moving from the description of the heedless to the case against the gods they invoked.

## Sūrah al-Baqarah 2:50

> And ˹remember˺ when We parted the sea, rescued you, and drowned Pharaoh’s people before your very eyes.

**Expanded Commentary**

**The Parting Remembered as Rescue Watched**

“And remember when We parted the sea, rescued you, and drowned Pharaoh’s people before your very eyes” (2:50). Wa idh faraqnā bikumu al-baḥra — idiomatically “We parted the sea for you,” literally “by means of you,” as the source notes. The preposition hints that the people themselves are the occasion for which the sea is split: the waters part because a people is to pass. The act is then distributed: rescue for you, drowning for Pharaoh’s house, and witnessing for you — wa antum tanẓurūn — while you were looking on.

The same sea, the same hour, the same decree: water as path for one and as grave for the other. The Qur’an elsewhere recalls both deliverance and drowning together — see 7:136, 10:90 and 20:77ff among others, with Exodus 14–15 as scriptural parallel — and treats drowning elsewhere as recurring judgment, as with Noah’s people (11:36–48) and warnings that sinking remains possible (17:69; 36:43). The sūrah here keeps the account tight: parted, rescued, drowned, watched.

**“The Sea” — Unnamed, Yet Identified by Memory**

Al-baḥr is unnamed here — the sea — understood as saltwater (Q) and taken by many commentators to be the Red Sea. The anonymity is Qur’anic register: the sea need not be named because the generations that heard this knew which sea, and the later reader knows it is the sea that stood between bondage and promise. The definite article suffices; geography is secondary to deliverance.

**“Rescued You, and Drowned Pharaoh’s People” — One Act, Two Outcomes**

Fa-anjaynākum wa aghraqnā āla Firʿawn. Rescue precedes drowning in wording as in mercy: the passage is first made, then the pursuer is taken. The house (āl) that once slaughtered sons (2:49) is now made to taste water. The reversal is not arbitrary vengeance but the house tasting its own policy: those who made the world unpassable are stopped at a passage.

The pairing also answers the opening’s promise that guidance removes fear and grief (2:38) and its warning that denial ends in Fire (2:39): water here is both mercy for followers and judgment for deniers, at once.

**“Before Your Very Eyes” — Witness as Responsibility**

“while you were looking on” — wa antum tanẓurūn — is not decorative. To watch is to be made witness. A people that watched water stand as walls and then fall is left without excuse for soon saying “we will not believe until we see Allah openly” (2:55) or for taking up a calf when Moses was away (2:51). Sight given becomes accountability incurred. The source’s note about tanẓurūn as literal looking underlines that this was not report but lived seeing; memory is therefore commanded as ethics.

**Literally “By Means of You” — The People as Occasion**

The source’s literalism — “We parted the sea by means of you” — has a homiletic point: the sea is parted on account of the people for whom it parts. Creation serves khilāfah (2:30) and is prepared “for you” (2:29). Here that service takes the most dramatic form: the elements make way. The same earth whose residence was given “for a term” (2:36) now yields a corridor. Stewardship and rescue cohere: the world that is made for humans is also moved for them, when the mover wills.

**From Trial (2:49) to Sight (2:50)**

The sūrah’s balāʾ sequence tightens: 2:49 named affliction and test; 2:50 names opening and sight. The test does not end with relief; relief itself becomes test — will the delivered be grateful (2:52) and guided (2:53)? The sūrah answers with failures retold (calf 2:51, demand to see God 2:55), yet failures retold after witness make the later forgiveness more striking: “then We pardoned you after that, that you may give thanks” (2:52).

**Living the Parting**

- Remember particular waters. The verse general to all who are delivered, but the help is first remembered as dated and eyed: a sea at a time, a people watching. To generalize too quickly is to thin gratitude. Name your sea.
- Keep rescue and drowning together. To forget that the same water that bore one drowned another is to privatize salvation and miss its warning. Deliverance is not indulgence; it is vocation.
- Let witness become practice. If you watched God make a way where there was no way, the calf (2:51) should become unthinkable and the demand for more sight (2:55) unnecessary. To forget sight is to repeat the sin the sūrah will recount next.

**What Not to Claim**

This verse does not detail the mechanics of parting, nor name the hour’s wind or rod beyond what other passages supply, nor fix a cartographic line on a modern map as doctrine. It states what was done, for whom, to whom, and before whose eyes (2:50). Speculations beyond that should be marked UNVERIFIED when lacking prophetic wording. What is firm is parting, rescue, drowning of Pharaoh’s house, and witnessing as covenantal memory.

## Sūrah al-Baqarah 2:234

> As for those of you who die and leave widows behind, let them observe a waiting period of four months and ten days. When they have reached the end of this period, then you are not accountable for what they decide for themselves in a reasonable manner. And Allah is All-Aware of what you do.

**Expanded Commentary**

**The Waiting Period for Widows**

The verse instructs those who are left behind by a deceased husband to wait four months and ten days before they may marry again, and it then permits them to dispose of themselves honourably once the term has ended. The sentence follows the rules about the care of children and treats the position of the widow.

The Book's concern is the conclusion of the marriage in an orderly manner and the protection of the woman's position, and the community's teachers read the sentence as the instruction that governs the period after a husband's death. Readers are shown that the legislation provides for the household in every circumstance the community meets.

**The Term and Its Purpose**

The sentence names four months and ten days as the period, and the community's scholars explained the term as a provision by which the parentage of a child is established. The length allows the condition of the womb to become clear in the ordinary case, and the community's jurists built the law on that purpose.

The commentators noted the care with which the term is measured, and they described the practice of the community that the ruling replaced, in which a widow could be bound to a much longer period of seclusion. Readers are given the account of a ruling whose length is determined by the purpose it serves, and the sentence supplies the term that the community observed.

**The Practice Before the Ruling**

The reports describe the customs of the time, in which a widow remained in a state of withdrawal for a year or more, dressed in her worst clothes and confined to a small room. The sentence shortened the period and removed the conditions that had no bearing on the purpose it serves.

The community's teachers read the change as an instance of the legislation's attention to the position of women, and the reports show what the sentence addressed. The passage shows that the Book's treatment of the subject distinguishes between the period required by its purpose and the customs that surrounded it, and the sentence supplies the rule that settled the matter.

**The Reports About the Term**

The commentators discussed the relation between the period named in the sentence and the case of a widow who is found to be pregnant, and the community's jurists concluded that her term lasts until she delivers. The reports also discuss the widow who learns of her husband's death after some time has passed, and the teachers set out the applications.

The material shows the community working through the questions that the sentence leaves open, and the reader is given the account of a ruling that the community applied in its households. The community's scholars noted the concern for the child's parentage that runs through the rules of the waiting period, and the sentence in this place supplies the term from which the applications are drawn.

**The Permission After the Term**

The sentence states that the community commits no wrong in allowing the women to dispose of themselves honourably once the term has ended, and the community's teachers read the clause as the restoration of the woman's freedom to marry. The Book's practice of closing its rules with a permission appears throughout its legislation.

The phrase places the responsibility for the conduct with the woman herself, and the community's scholars noted that the permission is bounded by the standard of honour that the passage names. The passage shows that the Book's legislation on the household leaves the parties with the freedom to arrange their own affairs within the limits it has set, and the sentence supplies the rule.

**The Closing Names**

The sentence closes with the description of God as aware of what the community does, and the community's scholars read the pair as the ground of the instruction the sentence has given. The attributes accompany the rules about the household as they accompany the sūrah's other legislation.

The Book's practice of ending a passage with a description of God gives the reader the disposition in which the rules are received, and the community's teachers used the clause when they spoke about the conduct of the family after a death. The text supplies the assurance that the fulfilment of the instructions is observed by the One who knows.

**The Widow's Term and the Community**

The customs that the sentence replaced were severe, and the reports describe the widow who withdrew from the world for a year, dressed in her oldest clothes, and remained in a small room until the term was complete. The revelation shortened the period and fixed it at four months and ten days, so that her mourning for her husband became a defined observance rather than a condition from which she could not escape.

The jurists derived the rules of the widow's waiting period from the sentence together with the reports that describe the practice of the Prophet ﷺ and his companions. The woman who is pregnant waits until she delivers, and the term runs from the death rather than from the moment she learns of it in the ordinary case.

The purpose the teachers named for the term is the establishment of the child's parentage and the protection of the woman's position. The verse then permits the widow to dispose of herself in an honourable manner once the months have passed, and the community's practice followed the sentence in the marriages that were contracted after its term.

**What the Verse Establishes**

The sentence establishes the waiting period of four months and ten days, the purpose that the term serves, the permission to marry once the term ends, and the knowledge of God that accompanies the arrangements. The community's teachers used the verse when they set out the law on the subject, and the reader is shown that the Book's legislation provides for the widow's position with the same care it gives to the rules of divorce.

The teachers read the change as evidence of the attention the legislation gives to the widow, and the community's law of mourning was built upon the terms the sentence supplies. The verse also states the permission that follows the period, so that the woman's freedom is restored by the same passage that fixes her observance.

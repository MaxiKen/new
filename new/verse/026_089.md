## Sūrah al-Shuʿarāʾ 26:89

> Only those who come before Allah with a pure heart ˹will be saved˺.”

**Expanded Commentary**

**The Sound Heart Is the True Provision**

After removing wealth and children as final supports, Abraham identifies what saves: coming before Allah with a pure heart. The heart is not a passing mood or a private feeling separated from conduct. It is the center of intention, trust, worship, and moral direction. A sound heart arrives before God free from the loyalties that make created things rivals to Him.

The verse's condition is exact. External possessions cannot save, but neither can a religious appearance that leaves the heart divided. A person may have little wealth and no influential family while still carrying pride, envy, hypocrisy, or attachment to false powers. Salvation is tied to the inner state that directs the life, not to a social profile.

**Purity Means Freedom From Idolatry**

The immediate setting makes the first meaning clear. Abraham has spent the passage exposing idols and turning worship to the Lord of all worlds. A pure heart is therefore one that does not assign God's rights to created objects. It is free from the worship of images, ancestors, status, desire, or any other rival raised to ultimate authority.

This purity is not achieved by refusing every created means. A person can work, love family, seek treatment, and accept help while knowing that none of these is a lord. The heart is sound when it uses means without worshipping them. It receives blessings without making blessings the center of hope and fear.

**A Sound Heart Knows What It Will Meet**

The source tradition connects the sound heart with recognition that God is real, that the Hereafter will come, and that God will resurrect what is in the graves. This knowledge gives the heart a stable direction. It does not merely accept a doctrine; it lives as though resurrection and accountability are true.

Such a heart cannot make the present world its final home. It may enjoy lawful life, but it measures decisions by the coming meeting with God. Wealth and children lose their power to deceive because the heart knows they cannot cross the boundary into final judgment. Awareness of resurrection makes sincerity practical rather than decorative.

**Purity Is Sincerity Without Hidden Rivals**

A heart can perform good actions while seeking admiration, influence, or control. The act may look correct, but its inner direction remains divided. A pure heart seeks God without making the approval of people its real reward. Sincerity does not mean that a person never notices praise or feels pain from criticism. It means those reactions do not govern worship.

The demand is high, but it is not an invitation to obsessive self-inspection. The servant asks God to cleanse the heart and then works against the attachments he recognizes. Purity grows through repentance, truthful intention, and repeated return to God. It is a living condition, not a label a person awards himself.

**The Heart Can Be Spiritually Healthy or Sick**

The Qur'anic image of a sound heart allows a comparison with illness. A sick heart may still recognize truth in some measure but resist it through doubt, envy, pride, or desire for false authority. Spiritual illness affects how a person interprets signs and chooses actions. It is not identical to every emotional struggle or intellectual question.

A healthy heart can face fear and uncertainty without surrendering its central trust. It can be corrected because it values truth more than self-defense. The heart Abraham describes is not a heart that never feels pain. It is a heart whose pain does not turn into rebellion against God and whose desires do not become competing gods.

**Coming Before Allah Requires Movement**

The verse speaks of those who come before Allah with a sound heart. The image is active. The heart is brought to the meeting through a life of choices, not discovered suddenly as a hidden possession. Worship, repentance, justice, and patience are the ways in which the inner state becomes visible. A person cannot claim purity while deliberately feeding the attachments that corrupt it.

At the same time, the servant comes rather than presenting himself as self-made. The heart's soundness is a gift sustained by God. Human effort matters, but effort is a response to guidance. Abraham's prayer for forgiveness and his request for wisdom show the posture that protects purity: dependence, hope, and honest awareness of need.

**A Verified Parallel Places Abraham Beside the Sound Heart**

The parallel account in 37:84 describes Abraham as coming to his Lord with a pure heart. That wording confirms that the quality in this verse is not an abstract virtue detached from Abraham's life. It describes the inner foundation of his rejection of idols and his single-minded worship.

The parallel also guards against reducing purity to outward calm. Abraham's heart was sound while he confronted his people and endured their opposition. A pure heart can be courageous and forceful against falsehood. Its gentleness is not surrender; its firmness is not hatred. Both are governed by devotion to God.

**The Criterion Is Available to Every Servant**

The verse does not say that only the famous, wealthy, or socially protected can come before God with a sound heart. Wealth and children have just been stripped of final power, leaving a criterion that is inward but not exclusive. Every servant can seek sincerity, abandon false worship, repent from corruption, and ask God for a heart that is sound.

This gives the Day of resurrection both seriousness and hope. No external advantage can save a divided heart, but no person is barred from seeking purification while life remains. Abraham's prayer leads the reader away from what cannot help and toward the one condition that matters: arriving before Allah with worship, trust, and allegiance undivided.

## Sūrah al-Baqarah 2:267

> O believers! Donate from the best of what you have earned and of what We have produced for you from the earth. Do not pick out worthless things for donation, which you yourselves would only accept with closed eyes. And know that Allah is Self-Sufficient, Praiseworthy.

**Expanded Commentary**

**The Command to Spend Well**

The sentence addresses the believers and instructs them to give from the good things that they have earned and from what God has brought forth from the earth, and the community's teachers read the command as the statement about the quality of what is offered. The passage has treated the manner of the giving, and the sentence here adds the standard that applies to the gift itself.

The wording joins two sources of wealth, the trade of the community and the produce of the land, so that the instruction covers the whole of what its members owned. The teachers noted the breadth of the phrase, since the command applies to the merchant and the farmer alike.

The Book's treatment of generosity elsewhere states that the believers will not attain the piety the passage describes until they spend from what they love, and the community's scholars read the two verses together when they taught the subject. The sentence in this place supplies the standard by which the gift is measured.

**The Good Things Named**

The commentators explained the phrase as the wealth that is wholesome and lawfully acquired, and they discussed whether the wording concerns the value of the property or its standing in the law. The two readings were set out and combined, since the community's teachers held that the Book requires the gift to be both lawful and good.

The teachers drew attention to the person who gives what he would not accept himself, since the sentence names the practice and answers it with the reminder that the giver is a person like the one who receives. The Book's teaching about the community's shared life runs through the passage, and the sentence supplies one of its clearest statements.

The phrase also implies the obligation that accompanies the acquisition of property, since the wealth that is earned is described as the material from which the community's service is drawn. The scholars of the community used the sentence when they set out the duties that attach to possession.

**The Bad Excluded**

The sentence forbids the choice of the worthless things for donation and describes the giver who would accept such a thing only with his eyes closed, and the community's teachers read the phrase as the statement about the knowledge that the giver himself possesses. The person who offers what he would not take has testified against his own gift.

The commentators explained the image of the closed eyes as the description of the reluctance with which a person receives a thing of no value, so that the sentence measures the gift by the standard of the giver's own judgment. The Book's argument in the passage rests on the plain sense of fairness among its members.

The teachers also noted that the prohibition concerns the intention with which the worthless thing is chosen. The community's scholars explained that the command does not forbid the gift of a small amount, and that what it forbids is the search for the dregs of a person's property in order to preserve the rest.

**The Reports About the Practice**

The commentators connected the sentence with the practice of those who hung the bunches of dates in the mosque for the poor of the community, and they transmitted the report that a group brought inferior dates after the good ones had been given. The teachers read the report as the occasion for the instruction, and the sentence answered the practice it described.

The material shows the manner in which the community's charity was conducted in its first years, since the gifts were placed where those in need could take from them. The reports describe the care of the Prophet ﷺ in the conduct of the community's giving, and the teachers used them when they explained the passage.

The community's scholars drew from the reports the lesson that the Book's requirement concerns the gift and the intention together. The sentence does not forbid the small gift; it forbids the gift that the giver himself would not wish to receive.

**The Attributes Named**

The verse closes with the description of God as self-sufficient and praiseworthy, and the community's scholars explained the pair as the ground of the command. The first name states that God has no need of the gift, and the second places the praise with the One from whom the wealth and the ability to give it have come.

The teachers drew from the attributes the lesson that the instruction of the sentence is given for the benefit of the giver rather than for the need of the receiver. The community's preachers used the clause when they answered those who imagined that their gifts conferred an obligation on God.

The Book's treatment of the subject elsewhere describes the wealth of the community as a trust that was lent to it, and the community's scholars gathered the passages when they spoke about property. The two names at the close of the sentence supply the disposition in which the instruction is received.

**The Place in the Passage**

The sentence stands within the long treatment of charity that occupies the middle of the sūrah, and the community's teachers read it as the instruction about the material of the gift after the verses that treated its manner. The passage has dealt with the reminder of the gift and the injury that follows it, and the sentence here turns to the thing that is offered.

The teachers also noted the connection with the command that follows, where the community is warned against the threat of poverty that discourages the gift. The passage's arrangement presents the standard of the gift, the obstacle that stands before it, and the promise that answers the obstacle.

The community's scholars read the whole section as a body of instruction about the conduct of the wealthy, and the sentence in this place supplies the rule by which the community's judges assessed the gifts that were brought before them.

**What the Verse Establishes**

The sentence establishes the command to give from the good things that the community has earned and from the produce of the earth, the prohibition of the worthless gift, the standard that the giver's own judgment supplies, and the attributes of God that close the instruction. The teachers used the verse when they taught the conduct of charity, and the sūrah's following verses treat the fear of poverty that discourages the act, so that the standard and its obstacle are presented in order.

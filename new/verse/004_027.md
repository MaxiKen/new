## Sūrah al-Nisāʾ 4:27

> And it is Allah’s Will to turn to you in grace, but those who follow their desires wish to see you deviate entirely ˹from Allah’s Way˺.

### Expanded Commentary

**The difference between guidance and appetite**

Allah wishes to turn toward believers in mercy, while those who follow desires want them to deviate greatly. The contrast is not between people who have no desires and people who have them. Desire is part of human nature. The question is whether desire is disciplined by revealed guidance or whether it recruits interpretation, custom, and power to make its own path appear lawful.

In the context of marriage, the warning includes those who want vulnerable women, orphans, and dependants to remain available to their appetites. A guardian may call exploitation protection; a husband may call confiscation divorce; a family may call coercion tradition. The verse exposes the underlying movement: desire seeks not merely a concession but a major deviation from the boundary.

**Why the deviation is “great”**

Small compromises can accumulate into an entire social order. One family hides a dowry, another excludes a daughter, another treats a widow as property, and soon the exception becomes the custom. The Qur’an describes the desired deviation as great because it would move the community away from the central direction of mercy and justice, not merely because one technical rule has been broken.

**Mercy as a social force**

Allah’s turning toward believers is not sentimental softness. It appears as clarification, protection, and a route back from inherited wrongdoing. Believers imitate that mercy by making rights intelligible, helping people leave harmful arrangements, and refusing to use law as camouflage for domination. A community should be especially alert when those with power insist that their desires define what is reasonable.

**Desire can recruit interpretation**

The verse is a warning for readers of every age: a person may search for a legal opinion only after deciding what he wants, then call the preferred answer guidance. The cure is to disclose the interest at stake, consult someone independent, and accept that revelation may correct the desire rather than bless it.

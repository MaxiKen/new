## Sūrah al-Baqarah 2:159

> Those who hide the clear proofs and guidance that We have revealed— after We made it clear for humanity in the Book—will be condemned by Allah and ˹all˺ those who condemn.

**Expanded Commentary**

**The Crime the Passage Returns To**

The sūrah has named this crime before — hiding the truth knowingly (2:42), hiding the testimony received from Allah (2:140), the group that recognizes the Prophet as a father recognizes his child and conceals anyway (2:146) — and now it returns to it with the full weight of a formal sentence. The elements are stated in order: the thing hidden (*clear proofs and guidance*), its origin (*that We have revealed*), the aggravating circumstance (*after We made it clear for humanity in the Book*), and the penalty (*condemned by Allah and all those who condemn*). The aggravating circumstance is what makes the crime what it is. Concealing something obscure is a failing; concealing something God Himself clarified — published, made plain, written down for humanity in the Book — is an act against the clarity itself. The concealers are not withholding a secret they possess; they are fighting a disclosure God has already made, and the verse treats the fight as the essence of the offense.

**General Crime or Historical Case**

Al-Rāzī weighed two readings of the verse's scope and the tradition preserves both. The general reading: it is sinful for any person to obscure a truth of religion — the scholar who hides a ruling, the teacher who softens a doctrine for comfort, the witness who stays silent when his silence misleads. The historical reading: the verse addresses the People of the Book around the Prophet, and specifically the occasion when Muslims asked a group among them to point out the description of the coming prophet written in their own Book, and they refused. Al-Ṭabarī adds a third shade: the concealment of the truth of Islam itself — undercutting it, calling it false, failing to make known the foretelling of the Prophet that their scriptures contain. The readings are not rivals; the historical case is the general crime's first and fullest instance. The verse is revealed about a specific refusal and legislates about a category, which is how the Qur'an's occasions of revelation typically work: the event is the case law, the wording is the statute.

**Condemned by Allah**

The penalty begins with the divine condemnation — *yalʿanuhumu Allāh* — and the curse in Quranic usage has a fixed meaning the exegetes state plainly: being distanced from God and driven away from His mercy. The curse is not a wish of harm but an exclusion of closeness; the one cursed is placed outside the radius in which mercy operates. That the sentence opens with God's own curse sets its scale — no human condemnation can add to it and no human acquittal can subtract from it. The concealment was an act against guidance, which is mercy's road; the penalty is therefore the withdrawal of exactly what was fought. The symmetry is exact in a way the verse leaves unargued: the hider of mercy's directions is hidden from mercy. What makes the sentence dreadful is not pain but distance — the curse of this verse is the same state the Qur'an elsewhere describes as God not speaking to them on the Day nor purifying them (2:174), the administrative silence of heaven toward those who administered silence on earth.

**And by All Who Condemn**

The second half of the penalty widens the condemning court to everything able to curse. The exegetes identified the *cursers* in three candidate bodies. Some said the beasts of the earth — the animals who are deprived of rain and provision because of the sins of men, and who feel the weight of the concealers' crime in their own skins. Others said the angels and the believers, whose curse is their invocation of God's curse upon those He has cursed — the reading that 2:161 makes explicit, where the curse of God, the angels, and mankind all together falls on those who die disbelieving. Al-Rāzī added the denizens of Hell, who will curse the concealers for misleading them into the same fire. The three readings compose into a single picture: a verdict ratified across creation — by the creatures who suffer its consequences, by the beings who know its falsity, and by the damned who followed it. No concealment is private; every hidden truth has downstream victims, and the verse gives them all a voice in the sentence.

**The Fellowship of the Concealers**

The verse belongs to a family the Qur'an returns to whenever religious knowledge is traded or buried. Those who hide what Allah revealed, trading it for a fleeting gain, *"consume nothing but fire into their bellies"* (2:174) — the concealed truth becomes the fuel of the concealer. The covenant of the Scripture-people to make the Book known and not hide it was answered by casting it behind their backs and trading it for a miserable profit (3:187) — concealment as covenant-breaking, with an ancient signature on the promise. And the Messenger's arrival is described as *"revealing much of what you have hidden of the Scriptures"* (5:15) — the prophetic mission itself framed partly as an un-concealment, a forced disclosure of what was buried. Read with these, 2:159 is not an isolated threat but one station in a sustained campaign: revelation discloses, some hide what was disclosed, the hiding is cursed from every side, and a later prophet uncovers it again. The clarity God made in the Book is fought, but it is never defeated.

**Why Concealment Draws the Widest Curse**

Among the sins the sūrah names, few draw a curse of this breadth, and the reason is structural: other sins damage their doer and their immediate victims, but the concealment of guidance attacks the means by which any victim could be saved. The murderer kills one body; the concealer of the proofs endangers every soul that needed the hidden truth to find its way. Knowledge in the Qur'an's economy is a trust held for others — the scholar holds what the seeker cannot reach alone, and the holding is a stewardship, not a possession. This is why the penalty is communal in its prosecution: creation, angels, believers, and the damned all have standing in the case, because all were downstream of the concealed stream. And it is why the very next verse opens a door — the exception of repentance — whose conditions will match the crime: the public harm must be publicly undone.

## Sūrah al-Baqarah 2:159

> Those who hide the clear proofs and guidance that We have revealed— after We made it clear for humanity in the Book—will be condemned by Allah and ˹all˺ those who condemn.

**Expanded Commentary**

**The Charge of Concealment**

The verse states that those who hide the clear proofs and the guidance that God has sent down will be cursed, and the wording identifies the material being concealed before it identifies the people who conceal it. What is hidden is the evidence that makes the truth plain and the direction that shows how to live by it.

The Book has made this charge before in the sūrah, when it described the group that recognised the Prophet and withheld what they knew. The verse here states the consequence, and it does so in a form that applies to anyone who hides what God has made clear. The sentence therefore functions as a rule as well as a verdict on a particular community.

**The Clear Proofs and the Guidance**

The pair of terms covers two aspects of revelation, and the commentators explained the first as the evidence that establishes the truth and the second as the teaching that directs conduct. The Book uses the two together when it describes what the messengers brought, and the sūrah's opening verses used the same vocabulary for the Book itself.

The pairing shows what is at stake when such material is concealed. A person who hides an argument removes the means by which a claim can be examined, and a person who hides a guidance removes the direction that a life requires. The verse treats both as one offence, and the reader is shown that the concealment of religious knowledge deprives people of the means of knowing and of acting.

**The Public Character of the Offence**

The sentence states that God made the matter clear for humanity in the Book, and the phrase identifies the revelation as common property rather than as the possession of a group. What God has published for all people cannot be treated as a private asset by those who happen to hold it.

The community's scholars drew the lesson that religious knowledge is a trust, and they applied the verse to anyone who withholds what he knows from someone who needs it. The Book's wording places the revelation in the public domain, and the offence described in the sentence is the attempt to return it to private ownership. The reader is told that the truth has been made clear, and that hiding it after that clarity is a deliberate act.

**Who the Verse Addresses**

Commentators read the sentence either as a general statement about anyone who obscures a religious truth or as a reference to the People of the Book who were asked about the description of the Prophet ﷺ and refused to disclose it. Some reports describe believers asking a group among them to point out in their scriptures the messenger foretold there, and receiving no answer.

The other reading connects the verse to the concealment of the religion itself, by denying it and by failing to make known what the earlier books had announced. Under both readings the offence belongs to people who possessed the answer to a question that was put to them, and the sentence states what becomes of those who withhold it.

**The Cursing of God and of the Cursers**

The end of the sentence states that those who conceal are cursed by God and by those who curse, and the commentators explained the second phrase in several ways. Some identified the cursers as the angels and the believers, others as the creatures of the earth, and others as the people of the Fire, each of whom curses those who misled them.

The Book's term for cursing describes being driven away from mercy, and the sentence states that the exclusion is announced by every party that has reason to announce it. What the reader receives is a picture of a general condemnation, and the verses that follow describe the one condition under which it is lifted.

**Concealment in the Sūrah's Argument**

The charge belongs to the series of accusations the sūrah has levelled against the communities that received earlier scriptures, and each of them concerned the handling of knowledge. The Book described those who wrote the scripture with their own hands, those who altered the words they had heard, and those who cast the Book behind their backs.

The sentence in this verse names the simplest form of the offence, which is the refusal to speak. What the sūrah establishes through the series is that the communities were not accused of ignorance; they were accused of dealing with what they knew in ways that served their position. The reader is shown a catalogue of the ways in which knowledge can be mishandled, and the present verse supplies the most direct of them.

**The Way Out Named in the Next Verse**

The sentence is followed by a verse that names those who repent, correct what they have done, and make the matter clear, and the two belong to one statement. The Book states the offence and immediate consequence, and then it states the terms on which the offender is restored.

The structure gives the community both the gravity of the offence and the openness of the remedy, and the community's scholars used the pairing when they treated the withholding of knowledge as a matter that can be repaired. What the passage asks of the reader is that he speak, and the next verse tells him what is granted to the one who does.

**What the Verse Establishes**

The sentence establishes that the revelation is not a private possession, that concealing it is a serious offence, and that the offence is answered by a general condemnation. The reader is left with a duty that concerns what he says about what he knows, and the passage continues with the description of those who make amends. The two verses together form an instruction about knowledge that the community has carried with it in its teaching and in its dealings.

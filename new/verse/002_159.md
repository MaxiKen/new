## Sūrah al-Baqarah 2:159

> Those who hide the clear proofs and guidance that We have revealed— after We made it clear for humanity in the Book—will be condemned by Allah and ˹all˺ those who condemn.

**Clear Proofs and Guidance**

Two things are named as the object of the concealment, and al-Ṭabarī distinguishes them before he joins them. The *bayyināt* are the evidences that make a matter manifest, the verses and signs that leave no honest reader in doubt; the *hudā* is the direction that follows once the matter is manifest, the path the evidence points to. The two are set together because a proof without direction leaves a person informed and unguided, and a claim of guidance without proof leaves him following an assertion. Al-Rāzī adds a third element supplied by the middle clause: the disclosure had already taken place in the Book, so the concealment does not suppress an obscure teaching but buries a public one.

**The People Who Hid What They Knew**

Abū al-ʿĀliya states that the sentence came down about the People of the Scripture who hid the description of Muḥammad ﷺ after they had found it in their own books, and al-Ṭabarī records the same reading from several of the early teachers. The Book had placed the obligation on them in plain terms: God took a covenant from those given the Scripture that they would make it clear to people and not conceal it (3:187). Al-Ḥasan al-Baṣrī and Qatāda widened the address, and the community's exegetes followed them, holding that the wording is general enough to fall on anyone entrusted with a truth who withholds it when it is needed — the verse names a class of act, not only a single episode in the history of one nation.

**The Curse, and Those Who Curse**

*Laʿnah* is expulsion from mercy, as Ibn Kathīr defines it, a verdict that sets a person outside the good God sends rather than a mere expression of displeasure. The sentence then divides the cursers into two: God curses them, and those who curse curse them. Al-Ṭabarī takes the second group to be the angels and the believers, a reading al-Rabīʿ ibn Anas and Qatāda share, so that the unseen world and the faithful join the verdict of their Lord. Mujāhid gives the image a wider frame: when the earth suffers drought, he reports, the animals say that this is because of the sinners among the children of Adam, and they call down God's curse on them. Al-Qushayrī, working in the same register, writes that the gnats in the air and the stillness of the water pronounce against them. The concealer acts alone in the quiet of his study, and the whole creation is described as ranged against him.

**The Verse Abū Hurayra Would Not Forget**

Al-Bukhārī records that Abū Hurayra, the Companion who transmitted more report than any other, said that were it not for a verse in the Book of God he would have related nothing to anyone — and then he recited this sentence. The comment has been read as the mark of a teacher who understood the weight of withholding, and al-Nawawī, commenting on the reports of transmission, treats it as evidence that the obligation to speak falls on the one who knows when the questioner stands in need. The same tradition gives the limit of the warning: caution in a hostile setting, or waiting for the right audience, is not the concealment the verse condemns, because the act it names is the suppression of a truth that the questioner requires.

**The Bridle of Fire**

The Prophet ﷺ stated that whoever is asked about knowledge he possesses and then conceals it will be bridled with a bridle of fire on the Day of Resurrection. The report reaches the community through several chains in the Musnad of Aḥmad, and the Sunan collections carry it, Abū Dāwūd among them at 3658 and al-Tirmidhī at 2649; Ibn Kathīr cites it in his treatment of this verse. The image is exact: the organ that kept silent is the organ that is bridled, and the punishment fits the act as a rider's rein fits the animal it governs. The verse's curse and the ḥadīth's bridle describe one sentence delivered twice, once in the language of the Hereafter and once in the language of an arrest.

**The Door Left Open**

The warning closes without a full stop in the argument. The next sentence names those who repent, put their conduct right, and make the truth known, and promises them the turning of God toward them. Al-Ṭabarī reads the two verses as a single unit, so that the severity of the first is bounded on both sides — by the disclosure already made in the Book, and by the remedy already announced before the reader can begin to despair. The community's teachers drew the pastoral rule from this arrangement: the concealer is not told that his case is closed but that his concealment is undone by a public correction.

**The Scholar and the Concealer**

The two sides of this verse are set against each other by a report the community's teachers placed beside it. The Prophet ﷺ stated that whoever travels a road in search of knowledge is set by God on a road to the Garden, and that the inhabitants of the heavens and the earth ask forgiveness for the scholar until the fish in the water do so (Abū Dāwūd, al-Tirmidhī). Ibn Kathīr brings the report forward at this verse to show that the same created order which seeks pardon for the teacher is the order that curses the one who keeps his knowledge back. Mujāhid's account of the animals calling down God's curse in a year of drought belongs to the same understanding: the creation is depicted as knowing who served it and who withheld, and as answering each in kind.

**What the Verse Establishes**

Knowledge of the signs of God is a trust held for the people who need it, not private property held for the one who received it. The disclosure was made in the Book, so ignorance is not a defence and silence is not neutrality. The curse pronounced here is not the heat of a human temper but the verdict of the created order, borne by a man who acts in secret and answered by angels, believers, and animals. And the sentence stops short of finality, leaving a remedy that the following verse names in full.

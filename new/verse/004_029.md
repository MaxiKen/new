## Sūrah al-Nisāʾ 4:29

> O believers! Do not devour one another’s wealth illegally, but rather trade by mutual consent. And do not kill ˹each other or˺ yourselves. Surely Allah is ever Merciful to you.

### Expanded Commentary

**Property requires mutual consent**

The verse prohibits consuming one another’s wealth through false means, while permitting trade conducted by mutual consent. The prohibition encompasses theft, fraud, bribery, deception in measurement, gambling-like transactions, usurpation, and any arrangement in which apparent agreement is produced by coercion or concealed information. Wealth is not purified merely because money changes hands; the manner of transfer matters.

The permission for trade is qualified by mutual consent. A contract may be signed and still be morally defective if a party was misled, threatened, or unable to understand the material terms. In contemporary markets, this principle reaches predatory lending, hidden fees, exploitative employment, manipulation of vulnerable consumers, and digital designs that trick a person into payment.

**Do not destroy yourselves**

The prohibition on killing yourselves has both a direct and a wider reading. It forbids suicide and self-destructive violence, and it also warns against a social order in which people consume one another’s wealth until life becomes unbearable. A person who bankrupts himself through reckless gambling or ruins another through fraud has participated in the destruction the verse condemns.

The command must be taught with compassion to those facing suicidal thoughts. It is not a weapon for shaming the suffering; it is a protection of life and a call to immediate support, safety, and mercy. Human judgment about the final state of someone who dies by suicide belongs to Allah, who knows distress and capacity better than people do.

**Mercy and the limits of ownership**

The verse ends by invoking Allah’s mercy. Property rights are real, but they do not make the owner morally independent. Trade serves life when it is honest; it becomes a form of mutual injury when one person’s gain depends on another’s ignorance or desperation. The believer asks not only “Can I obtain this?” but “Was it transferred justly, and will it preserve rather than destroy the people involved?”

**Consent needs truthful information**

A buyer cannot consent to what the seller deliberately conceals, and an employee cannot freely accept terms when the employer has made refusal impossible through deception. The verse’s economic ethic therefore demands transparency, fair bargaining power, and remedies for fraud. Profit is wholesome when the other party’s informed choice remains real.

## Sūrah al-Baqarah 2:227

> But if they settle on divorce, then Allah is indeed All-Hearing, All-Knowing.

**Expanded Commentary**

**The Alternative Stated**

The verse states that if the parties resolve on divorce then God is hearing and knowing, and the sentence completes the ruling of the previous verse by naming the alternative to the husband's return. The Book has described the limit on the oath of abstention and now states what follows a settled decision to separate.

The sentence's brevity matches the matter it addresses, and the community's teachers read the clause as the permission that follows the failure of the reconciliation. Readers are shown that the Book provides for both outcomes and that the choice belongs to the parties, with the outcome placed before the One who hears and knows.

**The Resolution to Divorce**

The commentators explained the phrase as describing a decision that has been settled rather than a passing intention, and they connected it with the procedure by which a husband declares divorce. The sentence's wording indicates that the matter has moved beyond the stage at which a return is contemplated.

The Book's treatment of the subject elsewhere supplies the steps that follow the declaration, and the community's jurists built the law on the combination of the passages. Readers are given the account of a decision whose consequences the following verses set out, and the sentence in this place marks the point at which the marriage is brought to its end.

**The Names and Their Force**

The sentence closes with the description of God as hearing and knowing, and the community's scholars read the pair as the ground of the ruling. The first name concerns the words by which the decision is expressed, and the second concerns the intention behind it.

The community's teachers noted that the same pair of names appears at the close of the passages about oaths, and they read the repetition as a statement that the whole matter of the community's commitments is conducted before the One who perceives both the speech and the purpose. The passage shows that the Book's legislation on the household rests on that knowledge, and the sentence supplies the reminder that accompanies the ruling.

**The Reports About the Occasion**

The commentaries connect the passage with the practices of the society in which the community was formed, and they describe the arrangements by which a man could keep his wife in a condition of uncertainty. The sentence and the verses around it address those arrangements, and the community's scholars read the passage as a whole.

The reports show how the early community handled the cases the verses describe, and they record the consultations that the companions brought to the Prophet ﷺ. The account describes a body of law that was applied in the community's own households, and the sentence in this place supplies one of its provisions.

**The Procedure and Its Limits**

The following verses set out the waiting period, the right of the husband to take back his wife within it, and the rule about the number of times the declaration may be made. The community's jurists derived the whole procedure from those verses, and the sentence in this place supplies the decision that sets the process in motion.

The Book's method of stating a general provision and then supplying its details appears throughout its legislation, and the reader is shown the arrangement in the order in which the community applied it. The community's teachers used the verses as a single unit in their teaching, and the sentence's brevity gives the process its point of departure.

**The Verse in the Passage**

The sentence stands between the description of the oath of abstention and the rules about the waiting period, and it completes the treatment of the husband's alternatives. The Book has described the return and now names the divorce, so that the reader is given both courses and the passage then turns to the consequences that follow the second.

The section's concern is the orderly conclusion of a marriage that cannot continue, and the community's teachers read the verses as a body of instruction that protects both parties. The passage closes with the decision and with the statement that it is made before God, and the verses that follow describe the waiting period that the wife observes.

**The Path Out of the Marriage**

The sentence names the second course that remains when the reconciliation fails, and the verses that follow set out the procedure by which it is carried out. The jurists derived from them the waiting period, the right of return within it, and the limit of the declarations, so that the divorce became a process with stages rather than a single act.

The community's practice followed the same arrangement. The reports describe the companions who separated from their wives and the witnesses they called, and the teachers noted the care with which the early judges required the terms of the law to be observed.

The Book places the announcement of the decision before the One who hears and knows, and the sentence's attributes give the matter its gravity. The passage does not present the separation as a good, and it does not deny the difficulty of the household that reaches it; it supplies instead an orderly way in which the parties may part without wrong.

**What the Verse Establishes**

The sentence establishes the option of divorce, the settled character of the decision, and the knowledge of God that accompanies it. The community's teachers used the verse when they set out the law of divorce, and the reader is shown that the Book's legislation provides for the ending of a marriage as well as for its continuation.

The sentence gave the community the beginning of a procedure that its jurists developed in detail, and the teachers used the verse when they explained that the Book neither traps the spouses together nor leaves the separation to the will of one of them. The attributes at the close accompany the whole section, so that the conduct of the parties is understood to rest under the knowledge of God.

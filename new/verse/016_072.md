## Sūrah al-Naḥl 16:72

> **And Allah has made for you spouses of your own kind, and given you through your spouses children and grandchildren. And He has granted you good, lawful provisions. Are they then faithful to falsehood and ungrateful for Allah’s favours?**

**Expanded Commentary**

**A Verse That Continues The Argument Against Ingratitude**

This verse continues the sūrah's argument that began with blessings and moved to human response. The translation says: And Allah has made for you spouses of your own kind, and given you through your spouses children and grandchildren. And ... The Arabic uses terms that tie back to previous verses about creation, provision, and accountability. Al-Ṭabarī explains that this verse addresses those who attribute to Allah what they hate for themselves, or who deny His favours, or who are reminded of His power to punish or forgive. The verse is placed after discussion of daughters and evil judgment to show that disbelief in Hereafter leads to evil qualities and misattribution. The sūrah's method is to alternate blessings with warnings, and this verse is part of warning that follows blessing, reminding that blessings are from Allah and misuse has consequence. The reader is invited to see connection between this verse and earlier catalogue: all blessings from Allah, yet some assign share to idols, attribute daughters to Him, and deny favours, showing ingratitude that will be questioned.

**Key Words And Their Roots**

The verse uses Arabic words that carry rich meaning. For example, the verb for punish, attribute, or send down comes from roots that indicate action with purpose. Classical lexicons like Lisān al-ʿArab define these roots with examples from pre-Islamic poetry and Qur'anic usage. Al-Qurṭubī notes that understanding root helps see why this wording chosen: it indicates not only action but attitude behind it, whether gratitude or ingratitude, justice or injustice. The translators add brackets like ˹immediately˺ or ˹idols˺ to clarify meaning that is implied in Arabic but needs explicit in English. These brackets are not in Arabic but reflect interpretive effort to make sense clear. The verse's Arabic rhythm, with its balance of clauses, makes it memorable and easy to recite, which is part of Qur'an's inimitability. The choice of words also links to other verses in sūrah that use same root, creating internal echo that reinforces theme.

**The Theological Point About Divine Power And Mercy**

One central theological point in this verse is that Allah's power is absolute and His mercy includes delay. If Allah punished immediately for every wrongdoing, no living being would remain, as verse 61 says. But He delays for appointed term, giving time to repent, reflect, and be grateful. This delay is due to graciousness and mercy, as verse 47 said Lord is Ever Gracious, Most Merciful. Al-Rāzī explains that delay does not mean approval; it means forbearance that allows return. The verse also shows that attributing to Allah what you hate for yourself, or claiming finest reward while doing evil, is lie that will lead to Fire, as verse 62 says. The promise of Fire for those who fabricate lies is just, because they fabricated against Allah who gave them provision. The verse therefore balances power and mercy: power to punish immediately, mercy to delay, justice to punish eventually if ingratitude persists.

**Practical Lessons For Daily Life**

In daily life, this verse teaches several practical lessons. First, recognize that whatever blessings you have are from Allah, as verse 53 said, and do not assign share to idols or attribute to self alone. Second, when hardship touches you, cry to Him alone, as verse 53 said, and remain grateful after hardship removed, not associating others as verse 54 warned. Third, avoid attributing to Allah what you hate for yourself, whether in theology or in social matters like devaluing daughters, as verses 57-59 condemned. Fourth, understand that delay in punishment is mercy that gives time to change, not permission to continue. Al-Qurṭubī says believer should use delay to increase gratitude, patience, trust, and asking knowledgeable, as verses 41-44 described. The verse also encourages reflection on fate of deniers, as verse 36 said travel and see, to learn from history. Practically, this means visiting places, reading stories, and seeing that denial leads to bad end, while gratitude leads to good home in world and greater in Hereafter.

**How This Verse Connects To The Whole Sūrah**

Sūrat al-Naḥl is called Sūrat al-Niʿam, chapter of blessings, because it enumerates blessings systematically, then shows human responses: gratitude vs ingratitude, reflection vs heedlessness, humility vs arrogance. This verse fits in that flow by showing either warning against immediate punishment, or example of misattribution, or clarification purpose of Book, or sign in rain, cattle, fruits, bees. Each verse adds layer to argument that Allah alone creates, sustains, knows, and deserves worship. The verse's placement after previous warnings and before next commands is deliberate: it shows consequence of previous attitude and prepares for next. For example, after discussing daughters and evil judgment, verse 60 summarizes evil qualities belong to disbelievers in Hereafter and finest belong to Allah. After discussing delay for appointed term, verse 62 shows they attribute to Allah what they hate and claim finest reward, yet Fire awaits. The reader is invited to see connection and respond with gratitude, not ingratitude.

**Reflection That Leads To Action**

Reflection, tafakkur, is purpose of many verses in this sūrah, as seals say for those who reflect, understand, are mindful, listen. This verse invites reflection on its specific content: if Allah delayed punishment for appointed term, reflect on what you would do with remaining time; if they attribute to Allah what they hate, reflect on whether you attribute to Allah what you would hate for yourself; if Book was revealed to clarify differences, reflect on differences you have and seek clarification via Qur'an and Sunnah. Al-Zamakhsharī notes that reflection in Qur'an is not idle thinking but thinking that leads to action: gratitude, patience, trust, asking knowledgeable, explaining to people. The verse therefore ends with implicit call: reflect and act, before appointed term arrives that cannot be delayed or advanced, as verse 61 says. The wise person uses delay to prepare, uses blessings to be grateful, uses hardship to supplicate, and remains with Allah alone in all states, so that at death angels greet with peace and say enter Paradise for what you used to do, as verse 32 described, rather than being among those who wrong themselves and are told enter gates of Hell as evil home for arrogant.

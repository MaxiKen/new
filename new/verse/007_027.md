## Sūrah al-Aʿrāf 7:27

> O children of Adam! Do not let Satan deceive you as he tempted your parents out of Paradise and caused their cover to be removed in order to expose their nakedness. Surely he and his soldiers watch you from where you cannot see them. We have made the devils allies of those who disbelieve.

**Expanded Commentary**

**The Warning to the Children of Adam**

The verse addresses the children of Adam with the warning not to let the adversary deceive them as he caused their parents to leave the Garden. The address connects the instruction about clothing with the account of the fall, and it treats the experience of the first pair as a warning that their descendants are expected to use. The Book's other addresses to the children of Adam in this sūrah follow the same method, and the warning is repeated in the verse that follows the passage on conduct.

The verse states that the adversary stripped the parents of their covering in order to expose their nakedness to them, and the description matches the narrative of the previous verses. The warning therefore rests on a case that the audience has just heard in full, and the instruction not to repeat the experience is given to people who know its steps.

**The Enemy Sees and Is Not Seen**

The verse states that the adversary and his tribe see the children of Adam from where they cannot see him, and the commentary tradition explains the difference in terms of the created natures that the sūrah has already named. The adversary was created from fire and the human being from clay, and the subtle nature of the former allows him to observe without being observed. The sūrah's account of the temptation of the first pair illustrates the point, since the whisper reached them without any visible approach.

The Book's treatment of the unseen world includes the warning that its inhabitants are real and active, and the sūrah of the Jinn describes a company of the jinn listening to the recitation and carrying the news to their people. The verse in front of us states the practical consequence of the difference: the human being cannot watch the enemy, and he must therefore rely on the guidance that names the enemy's methods.

**The Allies of the Deniers**

The verse closes with the statement that the satans have been made the allies of those who disbelieve, and the Book uses the language of alliance for the relation between the enemy and those who follow him. The sūrah of the Cow describes the hypocrites going out to the satans and asserting that they are with them, and the sūrah of the Women names the party of the adversary among those who take him as a patron (4:76). The sūrah of the Cattle states that enemies have been made for every prophet from among the satans of humanity and the jinn, who inspire one another with flowery speech in order to deceive (6:112).

The statement in front of us describes the arrangement rather than the inclination of the deniers alone. The Book presents the alliance as something that has been established, and the sūrah of the Cattle states that hostile companions have been assigned to those who turn away from the revelation. The verse places the whole matter within the divine ordering of the world that the sūrah describes.

**Allies and the Sūrah's Warning About Protectors**

The mention of allies follows the command in the third verse of the sūrah not to take protectors besides God, and the two passages together define the choice that the chapter presents. The audience is told that it may not take patrons in place of God, and it is told that the relationship it forms by following the adversary is one of alliance with him. The Book's contrast between the allies of God and the allies of the enemy appears in the sūrah of Yūnus, which states that the friends of God have no fear nor do they grieve (10:62).

The sūrah's later passages describe the leaders of misguidance and their followers in the Fire, and the dialogue between them turns on the alliance that the followers had formed (7:38–39). The verse in front of us states the beginning of that relationship in the world, and the chapter's warnings about following the practices of the ancestors and the protectors are all instances of it.

**The Sin That the Verse Names**

The verse speaks of those who disbelieve rather than of those who commit particular acts, and the Book's use of the term covers the settled rejection of the message. The sūrah's audience is addressed as people who have not yet been classified by the community, and the verse states the condition under which the alliance is formed. The Revelation's call to the children of Adam is therefore presented as an invitation to leave the alliance that the enemy has been given with those who reject the message.

The Book's treatment of disbelief in this sūrah is developed in its narratives of the peoples who refused the prophets, and each of those accounts presents a community that chose the alliance and paid for it. The verse in front of us states the principle, and the narratives supply the evidence.

**Where the Verse Sits**

The verse stands twenty-seventh in the chapter, and it follows the instruction about clothing and precedes the passage on the shameful deeds that the audience justified by the practice of their ancestors. The address to the children of Adam continues through several verses, and the warning in front of us supplies the reason for the instructions that follow.

The reader who has been told what God has provided is now told what he is protected against. The enemy's sight and the human being's blindness are stated in a single clause, and the revelation with its descriptions of the enemy's methods is presented as the means by which the blindness is compensated.

**The Transition to the Next Verse**

The next verse reports the excuse of the disbelievers, that they found their forefathers following the shameful deeds they commit and that God commanded them, and it gives the answer that God never commands what is shameful and asks how they attribute to God what they do not know (7:28). The warning about the enemy in front of us is followed by the warning about the reasoning that the enemy supplies.

The address to the children of Adam is thus continued with the exposure of the arguments that keep a community in its practices. The verse in front of us names the deceiver, and the verse that follows names the deception.

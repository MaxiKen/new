## Sūrah al-Nisāʾ 4:161

> taking interest despite its prohibition, and consuming people’s wealth unjustly. We have prepared for the disbelievers among them a painful punishment.

**Expanded Commentary**

**Taking Interest Despite Prohibition and Consuming Wealth Unjustly**

The verse continues *wa akhdhihimu al-ribā wa qad nuhū ʿanhu wa aklihim amwāla al-nāsi bi al-bāṭil* — and their taking interest — *ribā* — though they were forbidden it, though prohibition had come, and their consuming people’s wealth wrongly, unjustly, by false means — *bi al-bāṭil*. *Akhdhihimu al-ribā* — their taking ribā — was explained as Jewry taking interest despite Torah forbidding — *“you shall not lend at interest to your brother”* — as earlier warnings against devouring wealth by false means (2:188), against consuming orphans’ wealth as fire (4:10), and against devouring wealth unjustly and killing yourselves though He is Merciful (4:29) make clear. *Wa qad nuhū ʿanhu* — though they were forbidden — was read as that *ribā* is not ignorance but disobedience after *sulṭān mubīn* — compelling proof — Moses was given (4:153) and Mount raised for firm covenant (4:154): where interest is forbidden yet taken, the Mount’s covenant is broken again. *Wa aklihim amwāla al-nāsi bi al-bāṭil* — and eating people’s wealth unjustly — was explained as consuming wealth by false claim, bribery, selling religion, concealing description of Prophet ﷺ for fee — a trade where those given a portion trade it for misguidance wishing you deviate (4:44) and hindering many from God’s way (4:160) includes economic harm. The two — *ribā* and *akl bi al-bāṭil* — were read as the sūrah’s pairing after forbidding *ṭayyibāt* for wrongdoing and hindering many (4:160): as those given a portion trade guidance, here they trade wealth by *ribā* and false consumption.

**We Have Prepared for Disbelievers Among Them a Painful Punishment**

The seal *wa aʿtadnā li al-kāfirīna minhum ʿadhāban alīmā* — and We have prepared for the disbelievers among them a painful, aching punishment — was explained with *aʿtadnā* — prepared, made ready — as the same preparation for true disbelievers who distinguish between God and messengers — humiliating punishment prepared (4:151) — and for those who consume orphans’ wealth — Fire, and for intentional killer — Hell, anger, curse (4:93). *Li al-kāfirīna minhum* — for the disbelievers among them, from among them — was explained as not all among them: as the sūrah will say *but those well-grounded in knowledge among them who believe in what was revealed to you and before* — to them great reward (4:162) — punishment is for *kāfirīn minhum* — disbelievers among them — not for every Jew, as *we forgave them after calf* (4:153) showed some forgiven. *ʿAdhāban alīmā* — painful punishment — was read as pain matching the pain of consuming fire (4:10), of humiliating punishment for ally seekers (4:102), of lowest depth without helper (4:145). Scripture has shown that *“whoever commits evil will be rewarded accordingly and find no protector besides Him”* (4:123) — *ribā* and *akl bi al-bāṭil* are evil rewarded accordingly — painful. Where the Book says *“God does not wrong even an atom”* (4:40) — interest even at atom of increase is measured and not wronging to punish, because it wrongs the single soul’s trust.

**A Verse That Follows Forbidding *Ṭayyibāt* With Forbidding Wealth**

Placed after *We forbade Jews certain good foods that had been lawful for wrongdoing and hindering many from God’s way* (4:160), this verse follows forbidding *ṭayyibāt* — pure foods — with forbidding wealth — *ribā* and *akl bi al-bāṭil* — for which painful punishment is prepared. That to God belongs whatever is in heavens and earth (4:126) shows that wealth taken by *ribā* is taken from His ownership unjustly, as female gods invoked besides Him (4:117) are taken from His worship unjustly. The law lightens because humanity is weak (4:28) yet does not license devouring wealth unjustly (4:29) — *ribā* is the lightening inverted: to make wealth increase by hardship on debtor. Earlier the Book praised charity, kindness, reconciliation seeking His pleasure (4:114) as the good secret talk; interest is the evil secret talk that devours. For the weak this is protective: where interest is taken from the debtor and wealth consumed unjustly from people, the sūrah that promised many havens to the emigrant (4:100) and enrichment to both after separation (4:130) declares that to take interest though forbidden will be painful punishment for disbelievers among them, while help is with the well-grounded who give alms (4:162).

**Taking Despite Prohibition as Breaking After Knowledge**

The phrase *wa qad nuhū ʿanhu* — though they were forbidden — was explained as breaking after knowledge, after *sulṭān mubīn* Moses was given (4:153), after Mount raised (4:154), after firm covenant taken (4:154) where Sabbath not to transgress was commanded — prohibition of *ribā* is of that rank. To take despite prohibition was read as the litany’s continuation: demand greater than a book, thunderbolt, calf after clear signs yet forgiven, Mount raised, firm covenant, then breaking, rejecting signs, killing prophets, claiming *ghulf* sealed, denying, slandering Mary with *buhtān ʿaẓīm*, boasting killing Messiah not killed but made to appear — now forbidding *ṭayyibāt* for *ẓulm* and *ṣadd* — now *ribā* despite prohibition and *akl bi al-bāṭil*. The pattern is breaking after knowledge, as *those who believed then disbelieved then increased* (4:137) — increase after being shown. The sūrah that warns *most secret talks have no good except charity, kindness, reconciliation* (4:114) here unmasks *ribā* as the secret talk’s economy.

**A Short Verse That Anchors Wealth in Disbelief Among Them**

Though only *“taking interest despite prohibition and consuming wealth unjustly, We have prepared for disbelievers among them painful punishment,”* the verse anchors wealth in disbelief among them, not in all. The exception — *illā* — appears next: *but those well-grounded in knowledge, the faithful who believe in what was revealed to you and what was revealed before — especially those who establish prayer — and those who pay alms and believe in God and Last Day, to them We will grant great reward* (4:162). Elsewhere the promise says *“whoever does good male or female with faith will enter Paradise not wronged a speck”* (4:124) and *with Him are both rewards* (4:134) He hears and sees; to give alms — *zakat* — is the opposite of taking *ribā* and consuming unjustly. The next verse will name those exceptions as proof that the *ḥarramnā ṭayyibāt* — forbidding good foods — is not permanence: to them great reward includes *ṭayyibāt* restored.

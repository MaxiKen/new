## Sūrah al-Nisāʾ 4:57

> As for those who believe and do good, We will admit them into Gardens under which rivers flow, to stay there for ever and ever. There they will have pure spouses, and We will place them under a vast shade.

**Expanded Commentary**

**Those Who Believe and Do Good**

The verse opens with *wa alladhīna āmanū wa ʿamilū al-ṣāliḥāt* — as for those who believe and do good — the counterpart to those who rejected signs and will be cast into Fire with skin renewed for tasting (4:56). The sūrah has defined belief in this context as trust in God and the Last Day with donation from what was provided (4:39), as belief in what was revealed confirming what was with them (4:47), and as not elevating themselves but letting God elevate whomever He wills without wrong even by a thread (4:49). Doing good — *ʿamilū al-ṣāliḥāt* — was read as acting in the ways the sūrah has detailed: returning trusts, giving dowries graciously, honouring obligatory shares, provision to bystanders with kind speech, trade by mutual consent, and seeking reconciliation with two mediators. The pairing belief and doing good gathers the two parts of the sūrah’s ethic: the inner turn toward God who wills to make clear and turn toward you (4:26–27) and the outer deed that keeps limits inside weakness (4:28, 4:31). The verse therefore inherits all those prior acts as its content. To believe and do good here is to have lived the sūrah’s first fifty verses as worship.

**Gardens Under Which Rivers Flow, Forever and Ever**

The reward *sa nudkhiluhum jannātin tajrī min taḥtihā al-anhār khālidīna fīhā abadā* — We will admit them into Gardens under which rivers flow, to stay there for ever and ever — repeats the sūrah’s earlier promise for keeping limits (4:13) with the intensified *abadā* — for ever and ever. The repetition *khālidīna fīhā abadā* was stressed as permanence without end, answering the impermanence of estates divided — whether little or much (4:7) — and of dowries given, and of trade that passes. The sūrah has already said that whoever keeps God’s limits is admitted to Gardens with ultimate triumph (4:13) and that whoever avoids major sins is admitted to a place of honour (4:31); this verse gathers those promises into the final form where the honour is named as everlasting. The image of rivers flowing under Gardens was read as abundance without labouring for allotment, the opposite of the anxious division of shares — half, quarter, eighth, sixth, third — that required witnesses and calculation (4:11–12). There, water must be measured; here, rivers flow without measuring. The permanence answers the Fire’s renewal of skin for tasting (4:56); where one side renews for pain, the other abides for delight without renewal needed, because the abode itself is vast shade.

**Pure Spouses and Vast Shade**

The two gifts *lahum fīhā azwājun muṭahharatun wa nudkhiluhum ẓillan ẓalīlā* — there they will have pure spouses, and We will place them under a vast shade — were read as the personal and the environmental. *Azwāj muṭahharah* — pure spouses — were explained as spouses purified of physical and moral impurity, and in the sūrah’s context they complete the marriage ethic that began with limits on marrying former wives of fathers (4:22), the list of prohibited degrees (4:23), the lawful field sought with wealth in chastity (4:24), and the concession for believing bondwomen with chastity and permission (4:25). The pure spouse is the counterpart to the shelter — *muḥṣin* — that lawful marriage was meant to be. Where chastity was kept and dowries given graciously (4:4), the recompense is companionship that is pure without effort. *Ẓill ẓalīl* — vast, deep, dense shade — was read as shade that is both cool and lasting, the opposite of the blazing Hell — *saʿīr* — that sufficed as torment (4:55) and the Fire that burns skin anew (4:56). The Qur’an elsewhere describes shade as a Garden’s comfort where the sun does not blaze; here the shade is called vast, suggesting a canopy that does not thin. Together the pure spouse and the vast shade gather intimacy and rest, the two things marriage and household were meant to provide on earth, now given without flaw.

**A Promise That Balances the Preceding Threat**

The verse follows the severest description of Fire with a parallel description of Gardens, so that the two renewals stand side by side. Rejected signs lead to skin renewed for tasting (4:56); belief and good deeds lead to Gardens renewed without need for replacement, with *khālidīna abadā* stressing that the abiding itself is the renewal. The sūrah has balanced promise and threat since it said *“these are the limits; whoever obeys... Gardens, whoever disobeys and exceeds... Fire, humiliating, abiding”* (4:13–14), and it keeps that balance here at the wider scale. The pairing also mirrors the sūrah’s ethic of *iḥsān* (4:36) and its warning that God does not like the arrogant boaster who is stingy and promotes stinginess (4:37). To believe and do good is to have spent for God, not for show (4:38), from what He provided, believing in Him and the Last Day (4:39); the recompense for that spending is Gardens and shade and pure companionship, where spending is no longer needed. The symmetry teaches that the choice between Fire and Garden was never only about after; it was about how one handled trusts, limits, and words while *“Allah is ever Watchful”* (4:1) and *Witness over all* (4:33).

**The Human Weakness That Can Still Enter Gardens**

The promise is placed after the sūrah’s lightening — *humankind was created weak* (4:28) — and its covering of lesser faults when major sins are avoided (4:31), so that the Gardens do not seem unreachable for the weak. To believe and do good does not require the self-purification that fabricates (4:49–50) but the purification God gives — *Allah elevates whomever He wills* (4:49) — without wrong even by a thread. The small good that is never wronged but multiplied (4:40) is enough to set the multiplication and the great reward from grace in motion, and that great reward is named here as vast shade. The sūrah therefore invites the weak to see themselves in this verse: the one who returned orphan wealth with witnesses, who gave a small provision with kind speech (4:8), who traded by mutual consent (4:29), who asked God for bounty rather than craved another’s (4:32), is among those who believe and do good. For them, the rivers that flow without allotment and the shade that never thins are the counterpart to the meadow they kept shaded for others on earth, and the pure spouses are the counterpart to the chastity they guarded when they could have sought *“those beyond, in fornication”* but chose the shelter instead.

## Sūrah al-Baqarah 2:27

> those who violate Allah’s covenant after it has been affirmed, break whatever ˹ties˺ Allah has ordered to be maintained, and spread corruption in the land. It is they who are the ˹true˺ losers.

**Expanded Commentary**

**Three Traits That Make the Rebellious Recognizable**

The previous verse ended with “the rebellious” (2:26), and this verse gives them a portrait in three strokes: they violate Allah’s covenant after it has been affirmed, they sever what Allah has ordered to be maintained, and they spread corruption in the land (2:27). The portrait is not private feeling but public conduct: a broken pact, a cut tie, a corrupted earth. The sentence ends with the verdict — “It is they who are the losers” (2:27).

**“Allah’s Covenant” — What Pact Is Meant**

The source notes three historically cited meanings that cohere rather than compete. One is the primordial covenant of acknowledgment — that human beings would recognize Allah’s Oneness — echoed in “Am I not your Lord?” (7:172). Another is the oath of the Quraysh that if a warner came they would be most guided (35:42), fulfilled and then betrayed. A third is the particular covenant taken from the People of the Book to affirm truth when it arrived. All three share one core: to know and then to deny after knowing. The modifier “after it has been affirmed” (2:27) heightens blame: this is not error from ignorance but violation after confirmation.

**“Whatever Ties Allah Has Ordered to Be Maintained” — More Than Kinship**

The phrase covers ties that Allah has commanded to be joined. The sources stress kinship — arḥām — citing “Reverence… family relations” (4:1), “family relations have the strongest claim” (8:75; 33:6), and “would you perchance… break your family relations?” (47:22). Yet the sense is wider than blood: any bond Allah has commanded to be kept — between servant and Lord, prophet and community, word and practice, people and trust, kin and kin — falls under the command to maintain. The verb yaqṭaʿūn (they cut) suggests deliberate severance, not mere neglect.

This breadth matters because the fāsiq’s habit is to separate what God has joined and to join what God has separated: to cut mercy from justice, knowledge from action, private faith from public fidelity, kin from kin, people from land.

**Corruption That Shows**

“Spread corruption in the land” (2:27) is the outward fruit of inner severance. Fasād is not only sin in the heart but spoil on the earth — injustice in measure, betrayal in contract, violence that ruins settlement, degradation of the soil and water and commons under greed. The sūrah earlier linked this same phrase to the claim of the corrupt who said “We only seek to put things right” (2:11) while ruining them. Here it is shown as the natural outcome of pact-breaking and tie-cutting: when bonds are cut, the land suffers.

**Why “Losers” and Not Only Sinners**

“It is they who are the losers” (2:27) — al-khāsirūn — bears more than moral censure; it names a loss. Khusar is to have capital and return with nothing. The rebellious lose selves before they lose arguments; they lose coherence before they lose status. The Qur’an elsewhere says the losers are those who lose themselves and their families on the Day of Resurrection (39:15). The present verse keeps the loss grounded: by violating covenant, severing ties, and corrupting provision, they forfeit the very conditions that would have sustained them.

**Reading 2:27 Without Narrowing It**

If the verse is restricted to “others” — only hypocrites, only one community — its force is blunted. The source cites hypocrites as typical referents without making them exclusive. The marks travel: any people who affirm a trust and then betray it, who cut what should be joined, and who ruin what should be tended, enter the description. Any people who keep covenant, maintain ties, and repair the earth exit it. The description is diagnostic, not tribal.

The neighboring cross-reference to brotherhood between Isaac and Ishmael — noted in early commentary to remind that ties crossed even the line between Israel and the Arabs — reinforces that the command to maintain ties is not parochial; it traverses lineage rather than being limited by it.

**From Parable to Practice**

2:26 said a parable can guide or let stray. 2:27 shows which life turns that test into straying: the life that trades covenant for convenience. Hearing without keeping, learning without joining, living without mending — this sequence turns even clear parables into stumbling stones. Conversely, fidelity to covenant, care for ties, and repair of the land turn the same parables into lamps.

**A Practical Measure Today**

Three simple audits make this verse readable upon ourselves:

- Covenant: where have we affirmed — by testimony, oath, contract, or baptism of intention — and then violated after affirmation? The remedy is tawbah that re-affirms, not merely regrets.
- Ties: what bond has Allah ordered to be maintained that we have cut — kinship, promise to a teacher, trust to a spouse or partner, word to a community, or the tie between knowledge and action? The remedy is reconnection with whatever costs that reconnection requires.
- Land: where does our livelihood spread corruption — in waste, deceit, exploitation, or neglect of what feeds us? The remedy is islāḥ that mends more than it takes.

These three do not exhaust the verse, but they prevent it from staying abstract.

**Connection Inside the Sūrah**

2:27 gathers the threads of the opening taxonomy: the mindful who “establish prayer…and spend” (2:3) and “have certainty” (2:4) are covenant-keepers who join what God joins; the disbelievers who are warned and unheeding (2:6) and the diseased hearts who “spread corruption” (2:11) are covenant-breakers who sever and corrupt. What 2:26 framed as a test of receptivity to parables, 2:27 frames as a test of fidelity in bonds.

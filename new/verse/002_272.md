## Sūrah al-Baqarah 2:272

> You are not responsible for people’s guidance ˹O Prophet˺—it is Allah Who guides whoever He wills. Whatever you ˹believers˺ spend in charity, it is for your own good—as long as you do so seeking the pleasure of Allah. Whatever you donate will be paid back to you in full, and you will not be wronged.

**Expanded Commentary**

**The Address to the Messenger**

The sentence addresses the Prophet ﷺ and states that he is not charged with the guidance of the people he calls, and the community's teachers read the clause as the comfort given to a messenger whose care for his people was known. The Book has described the disputes and the refusals of the earlier verses, and the sentence here relieves the messenger of a burden that belongs to God.

The wording places the guidance with God, so that the task of the messenger is described as the conveyance of the message. The community's scholars noted the point, since the passage's concern is the manner of the call rather than the response to it.

The sentence also belongs to the passage that treats the community's charity, and the teachers read the two subjects together. The messenger is released from the responsibility for the faith of those who receive, and the community is instructed about the gift that it gives and the account that belongs to it.

**The Guidance Stated**

The commentators explained the clause as the statement that the acceptance of the faith rests with the decision of God, and they connected it with the passages in which the Book declares that the messenger cannot guide the person he loves. The community's scholars gathered the verses on the subject when they set out the limits of the prophetic office.

The teachers drew from the sentence the lesson that the community's duty is the invitation, while the outcome belongs to the One who knows the hearts. The passage's treatment of the disputes with the people of the Book was read in that light, so that the messenger's part in the matter was understood as the clear delivery of what he received.

The wording also protects the messenger from the reproach of those who refused, since the Book states that the responsibility for the refusal rests with the person who made the choice. The community's preachers used the clause when they answered those who measured the message by its reception.

**The Occasions Reported**

The commentators connected the sentence with several occasions in the community's early life, and the reports describe believers who withheld their charity from relatives who had not accepted the faith. One account names a woman who asked about her grandparents among those who associated partners with God, and others describe the families whose connections reached into the tribes that had not entered the community.

The material also records the practice of the Prophet ﷺ, who gave to those who asked of him without regard to their religion. The teachers read the sentence and the reports together, since the instruction answered the scruples of the believers and confirmed the practice of their messenger.

The teachers of the community discussed the occasions at length, and the positions they transmitted show the care with which the passage was applied to the circumstances of the city. The sentence in this place supplies the rule that settled the matter.

**The Charity for All**

The sentence states that whatever good the community spends is for its own benefit, and the community's teachers read the clause as the statement that the gift serves the giver whatever the faith of the person who receives it. The Book's treatment of the subject elsewhere describes the act of the righteous as a benefit for the soul that performs it, and the passages were gathered by the commentators.

The wording also removes the expectation of the return from the person who is given to, so that the community's gift is placed in the hand of God. The teachers drew from the clause the lesson that the charity which is offered for the sake of the Lord does not depend on the character or the response of the recipient.

The jurists of the community used the sentence when they set out the scope of the charity that the believers may offer, so that the rule reached the households of the city whatever their religion. The passage therefore presents the gift as a service that the community owes to the need it meets.

**The Face of God**

The sentence adds the condition that the community seeks the Face of God in what it gives, and the commentators explained the phrase as the statement about the purpose of the act. The Book uses the figure of the Face for the pleasure of God, and the passages that speak of those who give for it were gathered by the teachers.

The wording also completes the clause about the benefit, since the act that is performed for the sake of the Lord is the one that returns to the giver. Scholars of the community read the two together, so that the gift becomes an investment in the account of the person who offers it.

The teachers drew attention to the phrase when they taught the intention of the giver, and the reports of the community show the formula used in its exhortations. The sentence in this place supplies the Book's own statement of the purpose that the passage requires.

**The Repayment**

The sentence promises that what the community spends will be paid back in full and that the community will not be wronged, and the community's teachers read the clause as the assurance that accompanies the instruction. The Book's earlier verses have compared the gift to a seed and to a garden, and the sentence here states the account in the language of a debt that is settled.

The reports of the community include the account of a man whose charity was given in the night to persons he did not know, and the teachers used the material to illustrate the manner in which the account is kept. The community's preachers repeated the clause when they encouraged the believers whose giving had brought them no acknowledgement from the people.

The promise of the full repayment also places the reward beyond the measure of the gift. The early teachers of the community read the sentence with the verses that describe the multiplication of the reward, so that the three statements were understood as one assurance given in different figures.

**What the Verse Establishes**

The sentence establishes the release of the messenger from the responsibility for the guidance of his people, the benefit that the giver draws from his own gift, the purpose of the act in the pleasure of God, and the promise of the full repayment that will not be withheld. The community's teachers used the verse when they set out the scope of charity and the duty of the call, and the sūrah's next verses describe the poor who are the proper recipients of the gift, so that the instruction continues with the account of those for whom it is intended.

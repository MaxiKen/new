## Sūrah al-Tawbah 9:18

> The mosques of Allah should only be maintained by those who believe in Allah and the Last Day, establish prayer, pay alms-tax, and fear none but Allah. It is right to hope that they will be among the ˹truly˺ guided.

**Expanded Commentary**

**The Positive Standard for Custody**

Verse 18 answers the boundary established in verse 17 by describing who should maintain the mosques of Allah. Four qualities are named. The custodian believes in Allah and the Last Day, establishes prayer, pays alms-tax, and fears none but Allah. The list moves from conviction to practice, from worship to social responsibility, and from both to moral courage. A mosque's true maintenance requires all four dimensions.

Belief in Allah gives the institution its foundation. Belief in the Last Day prevents the custodian from treating short-term popularity as the final measure. Prayer keeps the building centered on worship rather than on administration. Alms-tax tests whether religious leadership recognizes the needs of people. Fear of Allah protects the institution when donors, officials, or crowds demand a compromise. The verse describes a whole character, not a single qualification.

**Faith and the Last Day Set the Direction**

The first two qualities name the horizon of the custodian's work. Belief in Allah means that the mosque belongs to Him and that its teaching cannot be remade according to private preference. Belief in the Last Day means that every decision will be judged beyond the institution's public reputation. Together they protect against two forms of corruption: turning religion into personal property and treating worldly success as proof of divine approval.

A mosque may be wealthy, crowded, and admired while its decisions are driven by fear of losing status. Verse 18 asks a deeper question. Are those responsible for the house acting before Allah's judgment? The Last Day makes hidden motives relevant. A small act of honest maintenance may be more valuable than a grand project designed to attract praise. The custodian serves under an account that cannot be managed by public relations.

**Prayer and Alms Keep Worship Whole**

Prayer and alms-tax repeat the pair from verse 11. Prayer establishes regular orientation toward Allah; alms-tax establishes a regular duty toward people. The mosque is not maintained by prayer if it becomes indifferent to poverty, and it is not maintained by charity if prayer is displaced by social prestige. The two practices prevent the institution from becoming either a private spiritual club or a secular charity wearing religious language.

The word establish suggests more than occasional performance. Prayer is given a stable place in the rhythm of the community. Likewise, alms-tax is a structured obligation, not only a spontaneous act of generosity when the donor feels inclined. A mosque that helps its people pray and fulfills its material responsibilities is alive in the sense intended by the verse. Its walls are maintained because its purpose is maintained.

**Fear None but Allah Protects the Message**

The final qualification is fear of none but Allah. It does not demand that a person feel no natural concern about danger or loss. It means that no human threat is allowed to overrule a known duty. Mosque leaders often depend on donors, congregants, institutions, or officials. Those relationships can be managed wisely without making their approval the source of truth.

This condition links the verse with 9:13, where the believers are told that Allah is more deserving of fear than the hostile army. The same fear-audit applies inside a mosque. A custodian who speaks firmly to enemies but changes the religion to keep a wealthy supporter has not met the verse's standard. Courage is not loudness. It is the freedom to do what Allah requires when another person can punish or reward you.

**“It Is Right to Hope” Is Humility**

The verse ends by saying that such people may be among the guided. The expression of hope is striking. Those who meet the listed qualities are not told to congratulate themselves as though guidance were a possession they had earned beyond question. The language keeps humility alive. They have taken the means of guidance, but they remain dependent on Allah to preserve and complete it.

Classical exegetes discussed the force of this expression, including the principle that Allah's promise is certain even when it is phrased as hope. Whether read as promise or gracious encouragement, the effect is the same: service in a mosque should produce gratitude rather than entitlement. The custodian does not say, I deserve to lead because I have built. He asks Allah to make the work a means of guidance.

**The Institution Must Reflect the People Named**

A community can test its institutions by the four qualities of this verse. Does its leadership confess Allah and answer to the Last Day? Are prayer and alms central rather than decorative? Can its teachers correct wealthy or powerful people when necessary? Do its budgets, hiring decisions, and public statements show fear of Allah more than fear of losing applause?

The questions apply to large mosques and small prayer rooms, schools, charities, and households. Maintenance includes cleaning, but it also includes protecting the moral purpose of the place. A mosque may need modern management, sound finances, and professional administration. None of those replaces the character named here. They become useful when they serve it.

**A Calling for Ordinary Servants**

Verse 18 does not limit the maintenance of Allah's mosques to famous scholars. The person who opens the doors, teaches a child, pays the light bill, feeds a traveler, or quietly gives to the poor can participate in this work. The standard is not celebrity. It is a combination of faith, worship, service, and courage.

For believers, that makes the verse both demanding and hopeful. The mosque is not maintained by a building committee alone. Every worshipper contributes to its atmosphere. The person who prays, gives, refuses to compromise truth, and remembers the Last Day is helping the house become what its name claims. Guidance is hoped for by such people because they are not merely preserving a structure. They are preserving a place where Allah is known.

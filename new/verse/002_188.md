## Sūrah al-Baqarah 2:188

> Do not consume one another’s wealth unjustly, nor deliberately bribe authorities in order to devour a portion of others’ property, knowing that it is a sin.

**Expanded Commentary**

**The Two Prohibitions**

The verse forbids the consumption of one another's property unjustly and the offering of property to judges in order to take a portion of what belongs to others. The sentence names a private act and a public one, and the community's scholars read the two as a pair.

The Book has been treating the arrangements that govern a community's life, and the sentence addresses the means by which property passes from one person to another. What the reader receives is a rule about the acquisition of wealth and a rule about the institutions that settle disputes over it.

**The Unjust Consumption of Property**

The first prohibition concerns dealings between people, and the commentators explained the phrase as covering theft, fraud, and every transaction that transfers property without a right. The Book's treatment of the subject elsewhere includes the prohibition of interest and the protection of the property of orphans.

The wording describes the property as belonging to one another, which places the community's members in a relation of mutual obligation. What the sentence establishes is that the goods in a person's possession are not available for the taking, and the community's teachers read the verse as one of the Book's foundations for the security of property.

**Bringing Disputes Before Judges**

The second prohibition concerns the use of the courts, and the commentators explained it as describing a person who brings a claim before a judge in order to obtain what is not his. The sentence names the knowledge of the wrongfulness as part of the offense, so that the act is deliberate.

The Book's teaching about adjudication elsewhere requires the judge to decide according to what he finds, and the sentence in this place addresses the party who presents a false case. What the reader receives is a statement about the limits of a legal system, and the community's scholars noted that a judgment in a person's favour does not make the property his.

**The Report About Judgments**

The commentators preserve the Prophet's statement that he was a human being, that one of two disputants might present his case more skilfully, and that the one in whose favour he ruled against the right of another Muslim would receive only a portion of the Fire. The report was read by the community's teachers as the decisive statement on the subject.

The statement shows that the authority of a judgment extends to the settlement of the dispute but not to the moral condition of the property. What the sentence in this verse establishes is that the parties remain accountable for what they know, and the reader is shown that the Book addresses the conscience of the person who profits from a false claim.

**The Knowledge That Makes It Wrong**

The sentence includes the phrase that the offenders know they are committing a sin, and the community's scholars read the qualification as a description of the act's character rather than as a condition for its prohibition. What makes the conduct an offense is the intention of the person who brings the claim.

The Book's treatment of responsibility elsewhere attaches the judgment to the knowledge of the one who acts, and the sentence here states the same principle in a matter of property. What the reader receives is the assurance that the courts cannot settle the question of what a person truly owns, and the passage places the final accounting with God.

**The Passage's Treatment of Wealth**

The sentence follows the rules of the fast and precedes the question about the new moons, and it stands among the instructions that govern the community's dealings. The Book returns to the subject of property in later passages, where it legislates about debt and about the recording of transactions.

The community's teachers read the verse with those passages and with the prohibitions of interest and of the property of orphans, and they described the Book's treatment of wealth as a coherent body of teaching. What the sentence establishes is that the community's economy is governed by the same standards that govern its worship, and the reader is left with a rule that shapes the ordinary conduct of buying and selling.

**What the Verse Establishes**

The sentence establishes the protection of property, the limits of adjudication, and the responsibility of the party who profits from a false claim.

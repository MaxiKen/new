## Sūrah al-Tawbah 9:44

> Those who believe in Allah and the Last Day do not ask for exemption from striving with their wealth and their lives. And Allah has perfect knowledge of those who are mindful ˹of Him˺.

**Expanded Commentary**

**Faith Makes Duty More Valuable Than Comfort**

The verse describes a settled response from believers: they do not seek exemption from striving with wealth and life. This is not a claim that every believer feels no fear or has unlimited strength. It identifies the direction of the heart. Faith in Allah gives the command its authority, and faith in the Last Day gives sacrifice its horizon. A cost that appears final in this world is not final in the account of the Hereafter.

The contrast is therefore between seeking a lawful accommodation and seeking an excuse that removes responsibility. The ill, dependent, or genuinely unable person is not condemned for accepting what Allah has permitted. The condemned posture is the desire to escape a duty simply because it is difficult. Sincere faith makes the servant ask how he can contribute, not how he can preserve every comfort.

**Wealth and Life Are Named Together**

Striving with wealth and striving with life require different forms of surrender. Wealth can be given while a person remains physically safe. Personal effort can demand time, reputation, travel, skill, or danger. By mentioning both, the verse prevents a narrow idea of sacrifice. Someone who gives only what does not inconvenience him may still be withholding the very thing the moment requires.

The order also speaks to different capacities. One person may have money but little physical strength. Another may have energy but limited resources. A third may offer organization, care, counsel, or protection. The verse does not erase these differences. It makes them avenues through which faith becomes visible. What matters is not that all give the same amount, but that none treats his capacity as private property beyond Allah's claim.

**The Last Day Corrects the Short View**

Without the Last Day, sacrifice can seem like a bad bargain. The believer may lose money, safety, or status and receive no immediate worldly return. Faith changes the calculation without turning obedience into a commercial transaction. Allah is not indebted to the servant. The servant is certain that Allah does not lose the good done for His sake.

This certainty also keeps the believer from measuring success only by visible victory. A sincere effort may fail in its immediate aim and still be accepted. The Last Day reveals intention, endurance, and hidden service that worldly records cannot measure. It also reveals the cost of cowardice that seemed profitable at the time. Accountability gives present action its true weight.

**Mindfulness Is Known to Allah Before It Is Praised by People**

The verse ends with Allah's perfect knowledge of those who are mindful of Him. Taqwā is not a costume of eagerness. A person may volunteer loudly to gain honor, while another serves quietly with a valid limitation and a truthful heart. Allah knows both the ability and the intention. His knowledge protects the weak from false accusation and exposes the strong when their display is hollow.

That ending should make the believer humble. He should not claim that his sacrifice proves his superiority. Nor should he resent another person's different role when Allah knows the reason for it. Mindfulness includes fear of pretending to be brave and fear of using weakness as a shield. The servant offers himself without demanding that people certify his sincerity.

**No Exemption Is Sought From the Work of the Heart**

Even when a person cannot join a physical effort, he is not released from truthfulness, prayer, support, and concern. The principle reaches beyond the immediate expedition. A believer who cannot give wealth may give service. One who cannot travel may prepare others, care for dependents, or defend the truth with sound speech. The absence of one form of striving does not authorize indifference to every form.

Conversely, visible activity cannot excuse neglect of the heart. Wealth given for praise and labor offered for reputation are not the ideal named here. Faith joins the outer action to inner accountability. The most demanding struggle may be to surrender the desire to be admired for what one has done.

**A Community Built on Voluntary Readiness**

A healthy community cannot be maintained by forcing everyone into one role. It needs people who are ready before the emergency, who give without waiting to be begged, and who accept that service may be costly. This readiness makes collective action possible and reduces the burden on the few who always step forward.

The verse therefore praises a quality, not a performance for spectators. Believers do not ask first whether the road will reward them. They recognize Allah's claim, examine their ability, and offer what is available. Their trust is not blind enthusiasm. It is the calm conviction that a life spent for Allah is not wasted, even when its best work is known only to Him. This conviction also prevents competition over visible sacrifice. One servant may carry supplies, another may protect a family, and another may teach the next generation. Allah can join these hidden acts into a result no individual could produce. The believer offers his share without claiming that another's share is small. Readiness is therefore communal: each person brings what he has, and Allah knows how the different gifts fit together.

Voluntary readiness does not abolish consultation. A believer may ask whether a task is truly required, whether another person is better suited, and whether dependents have been protected. These questions serve obedience when they seek the right action. They become evasions when the answer is already known and the questions are only delaying tactics. Allah knows the difference between a servant who wants to serve wisely and one who wants permission to remain untouched. The verse makes that inward difference more important than the public appearance of courage. A quiet, prepared contribution can be more sincere than a loud promise that never becomes action.

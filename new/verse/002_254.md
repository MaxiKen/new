## Sūrah al-Baqarah 2:254

> O believers! Donate from what We have provided for you before the arrival of a Day when there will be no bargaining, friendship, or intercession. Those who disbelieve are ˹truly˺ the wrongdoers.

**Expanded Commentary**

**The Command to Spend**

The verse addresses the believers and instructs them to spend from what they have been provided before a day arrives in which there is no bargaining, no friendship, and no intercession, and it closes with the statement that the disbelievers are the wrongdoers. The sentence follows the account of the differences among the peoples and prepares the reader for the verse that follows.

The Book's concern is the use that the community makes of what it has been given, and the community's teachers read the sentence as the command that sums up the sūrah's earlier instructions about property. Readers are shown that the passage addresses the community directly, and the sentence supplies the obligation in the form of an appeal.

**The Address Used**

The sentence opens with the address to those who have believed, and the community's scholars noted that the same address introduces several of the sūrah's most important commands. The wording gathers the community and places the instruction before it as a body.

The Book's treatment of the subject elsewhere describes the property that the community holds as a trust, and the community's teachers gathered the passages when they spoke about the duty of spending. Readers are given the account of an address that has been studied for the obligations it imposes, and the sentence supplies the words in which the community is called.

**The Day Described**

The sentence names the three things that will not be available on the day it describes, and the commentators explained them as the commerce by which a person might settle his account, the friendship that might be called upon, and the intercession that might be offered on his behalf. The community's teachers read the clause as the statement about what the day will not admit.

The sentence places the spending before the arrival of the day, and the community's teachers read the clause as the statement that the act must be performed in the present life. The wording gives the instruction its point, since the opportunity belongs to the time before the account.

**The Closing Statement**

The sentence closes with the description of the disbelievers as the wrongdoers, and the community's scholars read the clause as the statement about the condition of those who refuse the command. The commentators noted the gravity of the description, since the term applies to those who withhold what is due.

**The Day Without Bargaining**

The day the sentence describes has been named in the community's teaching by what it lacks, and the three absences remove every expectation of help. The bargain that a person might offer is not accepted, the friendship of the world does not carry a claim, and no intercessor stands except with the permission that the Book names elsewhere.

The teachers drew from the description the urgency of the command, since the wealth that is stored is of no use once the account begins.

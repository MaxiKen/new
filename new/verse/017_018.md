## Sūrah al-Isrāʾ 17:18

> **Whoever desires this fleeting world ˹alone˺, We hasten in it whatever We please to whoever We will; then We destine them for Hell, where they will burn, condemned and rejected.**

**Expanded Commentary**

**Whoever Desires Fleeting World**

Man kāna yurīdu al-ʿājilata — whoever is desires the hasty, fleeting. Man, whoever, conditional. Kāna, is, was, indicates state. Yurīdu from r-w-d, to desire, want, intend, same root as aradnā, We intend, in verse 16, and arāda, desires, in verse 19. Al-ʿājilata from ʿ-j-l, hasty, fleeting, immediate, from same root as ʿajūlan, hasty, ever hasty, in verse 11, and ʿajila, to hasten, and taʿjīl, hastening. Al-ʿājilah, the hasty one, meaning worldly life, immediate life, fleeting world, as opposed to al-ākhirah, Hereafter, lasting, latter. Al-Ṭabarī explains that whoever desires fleeting world alone, immediate world, not Hereafter, making world sole goal, We hasten for him in it what We will, etc. Al-Rāzī says al-ʿājilah is world because it is hasty, immediate, fleeting, passes quickly, as opposed to Hereafter which is delayed but lasting, remaining. The person who desires ʿājilah alone is hasty, as human is ever hasty.

**We Hasten Whatever We Please To Whoever We Will**

ʿAjjalnā lahu fīhā mā nashā'u li-man nurīdu — We hasten for him in it what We will to whom We will. ʿAjjalnā from ʿ-j-l, to hasten, expedite, give quickly, same root as al-ʿājilah and ʿajūlan and ʿājilah. Lahu, for him. Fīhā, in it, in fleeting world. Mā nashā'u, what We will, from sh-y-ʾ, to will, wish. Li-man nurīdu, to whom We will, for whom We intend, from r-w-d. Al-Ṭabarī says We hasten for him in fleeting world whatever We please to whoever We will, meaning not all who desire world get all they desire, but We give hastily in world what We will to whom We will, according to wisdom, not necessarily what they desire fully, limited by will. Al-Qurṭubī notes that ʿajjalnā indicates hastening, giving quickly in world, but limited to what We will and to whom We will, not all desires fulfilled, showing that worldly gain is controlled, not guaranteed.

**Then Destined For Hell Condemned Rejected**

Thumma jaʿalnā lahu jahannama yaṣlāhā madhmūman madḥūran — then We made for him Hell he burns condemned rejected, then We destine them for Hell where they will burn condemned and rejected. Thumma, then, after worldly hastening, indicates sequence. Jaʿalnā from j-ʿ-l, to make, as verse 12 jaʿalnā night and day signs. Lahu, for him. Jahannama, Hell, Gehenna, from Hebrew Gehinnom, valley. Yaṣlāhā from ṣ-l-y, to burn, roast, enter fire, endure fire, from same root as ṣalā, to burn, and iṣṭilā', burning. Madhmūman from dh-m-m, to blame, condemn, from same root as dhamm, blame, and madhmūm, blamed, condemned. Madḥūran from d-ḥ-r, to drive away, reject, banish, from same root as daḥr, rejection, and madḥūr, driven away, rejected, banished from mercy. Al-Ṭabarī says then We make for him Hell he will burn, condemned, blamed, and rejected, driven away from mercy, banished. Al-Rāzī says madhmūman madḥūran indicates two punishments in Hell: blame and rejection, condemned by Allah and angels and rejected from mercy, unlike appreciated striving for Hereafter.

**Why Fleeting World Called ʿĀjilah**

Why al-ʿājilah? Because world is hasty, immediate, fleeting, passes quickly, and human is ever hasty, ʿajūlan, desires hasty, prays for evil as for good out of haste. The person who desires ʿājilah is hasty, wants immediate, not patient for Hereafter, which is lasting. Previous verse said human ever hasty, and here says whoever desires hasty world, We hasten for him what We will. The connection is that haste leads to desiring fleeting world alone. The sūrah's earlier warning do not hasten command at hand and human is ever hasty frames this: desiring ʿājilah is manifestation of haste, wanting immediate reward without patience for Hereafter.

**Connection To Seeking Bounty And Provision**

Verse 12 said made day bright so you may seek bounty of Lord and know number years and calculation. Verse 20 says each We extend these and those from gift of Lord, bounty not withheld. Verse 18 says whoever desires fleeting world alone, We hasten for him what We will then destine them for Hell condemned rejected. The connection is that seeking bounty is allowed, even commanded, but desiring fleeting world alone, making it sole goal, without seeking Hereafter, leads to Hell. Seeking bounty from Lord with gratitude as means to strive for Hereafter is good, but desiring ʿājilah alone as ultimate goal, without faith and striving for Hereafter, is blameworthy. The distinction is intention and goal: seeking bounty as part of seeking Lord's bounty with gratitude vs desiring fleeting alone as ultimate.

**Link To Next Verse About Hereafter**

Next verse says whoever desires Hereafter and strives for it accordingly and is true believer, it is they whose striving will be appreciated. The pair shows two paths, two desires, two ends: desiring ʿājilah vs desiring ākhirah, hastening worldly vs striving for Hereafter, condemned rejected vs appreciated. The sūrah's theme of guidance to most upright includes choosing Hereafter while seeking bounty in world lawfully, not making fleeting sole goal. The moral code that follows details striving for Hereafter: honoring parents, giving relatives due, etc.

**Linguistic Note On ʿAjila And Madhmum Madhur**

Yurīdu from r-w-d, to desire. ʿĀjilah from ʿ-j-l, hasty, immediate, fleeting, from ʿajila, to hasten. ʿAjjalnā from ʿ-j-l, to hasten, from ʿajjala, to hasten. Mā nashā'u from sh-y-ʾ, what We will. Nurīdu from r-w-d, We will, intend. Jaʿalnā from j-ʿ-l, to make. Jahannam, Hell, from Gehenna. Yaṣlā from ṣ-l-y, to burn. Madhmūm from dh-m-m, condemned, blamed, from dhamma, to blame. Madḥūr from d-ḥ-r, driven away, rejected, from daḥara, to drive away, reject. The verse's structure — whoever desires fleeting, We hasten for him in it what We will to whom We will, then We make for him Hell he burns condemned rejected — with desire, hastening limited by will, then Hell with double description, shows that fleeting desire leads to limited hastened worldly gain then severe punishment with condemnation and rejection.

**How This Verse Warns Against Worldliness**

This verse warns against making fleeting world sole desire: you may get hastened portion what Allah wills, to whom He wills, but then Hell condemned rejected. Al-Qurṭubī says believer should desire Hereafter, not make world sole goal, should seek bounty in world but with Hereafter in mind, as Qur'an guides to most upright. The sūrah's moral code includes not killing children for fear of poverty, not approaching adultery, etc., which are often motivated by worldly desire and haste. The verse teaches that desiring ʿājilah alone leads to Hell, while desiring Hereafter and striving for it as believer leads to appreciated striving, mighty reward. The practical lesson is to check intention: are you seeking fleeting alone or seeking bounty as part of seeking Lord's pleasure and Hereafter? If fleeting alone, change.

**A Reflection On Condemned Rejected**

Madhmūman madḥūran — condemned, rejected — two words describing state in Hell: blamed, condemned, and driven away, rejected from mercy, banished. This contrasts with appreciated striving for Hereafter in next verse, mashkūran, appreciated, thanked. The person who desires fleeting alone is condemned and rejected, while who desires Hereafter and strives has striving appreciated. The choice is clear: fleeting hastened limited gain then condemned rejection, vs Hereafter lasting reward with appreciation. The believer who reflects on this will not pray for evil out of haste, will not desire fleeting alone, but will desire Hereafter and strive with faith, following most upright guidance, so that striving appreciated not condemned.

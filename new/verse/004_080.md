## Sūrah al-Nisāʾ 4:80

> Whoever obeys the Messenger has truly obeyed Allah. But whoever turns away, then ˹know that˺ We have not sent you ˹O Prophet˺ as a keeper over them.

**Expanded Commentary**

**Obedience to the Messenger Is Obedience to God**

The statement *man yuṭiʿi al-rasūla fa qad aṭāʿa Allāha* — whoever obeys the Messenger has truly obeyed Allah — gathers the sūrah’s long argument about referral. The sūrah commanded *obey God and obey the Messenger and those in authority among you, and refer disputes to them* as best outcome (4:59), said messengers were sent to be obeyed by God’s leave (4:64), and made acceptance of the Prophet ﷺ as judge without resistance the condition of true belief (4:65). Between those, it exposed those who claim belief yet seek false judges (4:60) and who turn away stubbornly when called to the revelations and the Messenger (4:61). This verse’s *man yuṭiʿi* — whoever obeys — was read as obedience in the matters the sūrah has detailed: returning trusts to rightful owners and judging fairly (4:58), not devouring by falsehood but trading by mutual consent (4:29), guardianship with witnesses (4:6), and fighting for the oppressed where commanded (4:75). To obey the Messenger in those is not to obey a second source beside God; the sūrah has said that the Messenger is sent to make clear (4:26) what God wills to make clear, and that good that befalls is from God and evil from the self (4:79), so obeying the clarifier is obeying the One who gave the limit. The *fa qad* — has truly — was stressed as already accomplished: the act of obeying the Messenger is counted as obedience to God without remainder.

**But Whoever Turns Away — You Are Not a Keeper Over Them**

The counterpart *wa man tawallā fa mā arsalnāka ʿalayhim ḥafīẓā* — but whoever turns away, We have not sent you as a keeper over them — was read as release of the Prophet ﷺ from compulsion. *Tawallā* — to turn away, to turn the back — matches the hypocrites’ *yaṣuddūna ʿanka ṣudūdā* — turning away stubbornly, barring (4:61) — and the lagger’s lagging (4:72). *Ḥafīẓ* — keeper, guardian over — was explained as one who keeps count and compels, as God is ever Watchful (4:1) and sufficient as Reckoner (4:6). The Prophet ﷺ is not that keeper; his mission is conveyance and clarification, not coercion of hearts that God knows (4:63). The sūrah elsewhere says *“Allah knows what is in their hearts, so turn away from them, caution them, and give penetrating advice”* (4:63), and *“put your trust in God, He is sufficient as Trustee”* (4:81); the same trust appears here. The community that was told *“had they come after wronging themselves seeking forgiveness and the Messenger prayed for them they would have found God ever Accepting”* (4:64) is told that coming remains their choice. To obey the Messenger is to have obeyed God; to turn away is to bear the turning, because the sender has not made the Messenger a keeper who forces, but a witness who will be brought as witness over his community (4:41) where no statement can be hidden (4:42).

**Why the Identity Holds**

The identity — obeying him is obeying Him — was explained as following from *bi idhni Allāh* — by God’s leave — that messengers are sent to be obeyed (4:64). The leave means the limits the Messenger judges by — half, quarter, eighth, sixth — after debts and bequests (4:11–12), the four witnesses required before sanction (4:15), the earth that purifies where water fails (4:43) — are not his invention but the One who sent him. The sūrah has shown that God is sufficient as Witness (4:79) that he is sent to all people, and sufficient as Trustee (4:81) for those who put trust in Him when others spend the night contradicting what was said. To obey the Messenger in those limits is therefore not to follow a man for the man’s sake but to follow the One whose command is always executed (4:47) through the man He chose to make the command audible. The Qur’an elsewhere says *“say, if you love God, follow me, God will love you,”* and this verse is the sūrah’s legal form of that love: follow the one sent to be obeyed, and you have followed the One who sent.

**A Line Between Claim and Obedience**

Placed after the correction that good is from God and evil from self (4:79), this verse draws the line between claim and deed. Those who say *“we obey”* but spend the night contradicting what was said (4:81) claim obedience with the tongue while turning with the heart; the verse says true obedience is to the Messenger, and it is counted as obedience to God. Those who claim *“we believe in what was revealed to you and before you”* yet seek *ṭāghūt* (4:60) claim belief as *yazʿumūna* but do not obey; the verse excludes them. The line matters because the sūrah’s public ethic — return trusts, judge fairly, take precautions and go forth (4:58, 4:71) — depends on a single authority to be obeyed. Without that, each dispute would be referred to the judge who favours him, the *ṭāghūt* whose schemes are weak (4:76) but whose flattery is strong. With it, the community has a *ra’s* — head — whose judgment, when accepted without *ḥaraj* and with whole submission (4:65), keeps the path straight that would have been granted had they done what was advised (4:68). The verse therefore does not add a new law; it names the hinge on which the sūrah’s laws turn.

**Not a Keeper As Constraint and Mercy**

The final *We have not sent you as a keeper over them* was read as both constraint on the Prophet ﷺ and mercy for all. Constraint, because he is not to coerce the turning; the sūrah’s method for hypocrites is turn away, caution, and penetrating advice (4:63), not forced compliance. Mercy, because the same God who is sufficient as Witness that the Messenger is sent (4:79) is also *tawwāb* — ever Accepting — for those who come after wronging themselves (4:64). To turn away remains possible, but the door to return remains open until death is present (4:18). The verse therefore steadies the believers who might despair at the hypocrites’ lagging and at the fearful who ask *why have You ordered us to fight* (4:77): the Prophet ﷺ is not a keeper who must bring them by force; his sufficient task is conveyance, and the believers’ task is obedience and referral where they can, with trust that God suffices as Trustee. The one who obeys him has obeyed God; the one who turns bears the turning where witnesses will be brought and no statement hidden. Between those two, the sūrah leaves the path clear and the keeper’s role in the right hands.

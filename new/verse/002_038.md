## Sūrah al-Baqarah 2:38

> We said, “Descend all of you! Then when guidance comes to you from Me, whoever follows it, there will be no fear for them, nor will they grieve.

**Expanded Commentary**

**A Second Descent That Carries a Promise**

“We said, ‘Descend all of you! Then when guidance comes to you from Me, whoever follows it, there will be no fear for them, nor will they grieve’” (2:38). The command to descend recurs — earlier “Descend as enemies to each other, with residence and provision for a term” (2:36), now “Descend all of you” with an added horizon: guidance will come, and following it lifts fear and grief. The same earth that was named as trial terrain becomes pilgrim road.

The plural “all of you” (jamīʿan) has been read as Adam, his wife, and Iblīs, or more broadly Adam and Eve as representatives of their progeny including the adversary’s progeny. The moral effect is the same: the descent is not private exile but entry into collective history, and the promise is not private comfort but a way for all who choose it.

**“When Guidance Comes to You from Me” — The First Prophecy of Prophecy**

“Then when guidance comes to you from Me” (2:38) — fa-immā yaʾtiyannakum minnī hudan — is often taken as the earliest Qur’anic notice of prophecy and revelation as a standing divine practice. God is ever-guiding. The guidance (hudan) is glossed in the source as prophets and clarifications (bayān). The verse does not yet name a book or a messenger; it names the source — “from Me” — and the condition — “whoever follows it.” That breadth makes the promise portable across generations: wherever guidance from Me arrives, this outcome follows.

The early scholar Ḥasan al-Baṣrī’s report — “O Adam, there are four matters containing all things… one for Me, one for you, one between you and Me, one between you and mankind” — is cited in the source as an expansion of this promise: worship without association, reward for deeds, calling and answering between servant and Lord, and love between people as one loves for oneself. The chain should be treated as UNVERIFIED as a prophetic hadith; its sense tracks Qur’anic themes and illustrates how later teachers paraphrased the “guidance” as lived practice.

**“No Fear for Them, Nor Will They Grieve” — Two Anxieties Lifted**

“There will be no fear for them, nor will they grieve” (2:38) — lā khawfun ʿalayhim wa lā hum yaḥzanūn. The pair names the two temporal fears that press earthly residence: fear of what is ahead (khawf) and grief over what is behind or lost (ḥuzn). Guidance followed does not promise earth without loss; it promises hearts without crushing. Fear looks forward to judgment, to provision, to enmity; grief looks back to the garden left, the time wasted, the wrong done. Both are answered: no fear about what is to come, no grief that corrodes what has been, when the follower keeps to hudan from Me.

The same assurance will become a refrain for the friends of God: “no fear shall be upon them, nor shall they grieve” (2:112; 10:62). Its first placement here, immediately after descent and immediately after forgiveness (2:37), shows that security is not won by pretending descent did not happen, but by following after it.

**Following, Not Merely Hearing**

Fa-man tabiʿa hudāya — “whoever follows My guidance” (2:38). Ittibāʿ is more than assent; it is walking behind, conforming step to sign, letting guidance order residence and provision. The earth given as “residence and provision for a term” (2:36) now receives a way to inhabit it without being consumed by enmity. The guidance addresses precisely the enmity announced at descent: to follow is to live among enemies without becoming an enemy to mercy.

**Why This Verse Repeats After Repentance**

The sequence slip (2:36) → words and relenting (2:37) → descent with promise (2:38) shapes earthly hope: forgiveness is not followed by abandonment, but by instruction. Adam is forgiven above, then instructed for below. The pattern establishes prophethood as mercy, not as afterthought: humanity will not be left to infer alone how to turn mustaqarr into mended dwelling.

**Living the Promise**

- Expect guidance, and expect to need it. The verse normalizes that life after descent requires hudan from Me, not merely prudence.
- Measure guidance by fear and grief. Where following grows, forward dread and backward sorrow ease — not by denial, but by proportion.
- Practice the “four matters” gloss as personal audit even if its chain is UNVERIFIED: worship without association, awareness that deeds return, calling and calling back between you and Him, and dealing with people by the love you desire for yourself. Each is a form of following.

**Boundary of What to Claim**

The “all of you” should not be inflated into a claim about who exactly is physically present at the command beyond what the text says. Whether Iblīs and his progeny are included or whether Adam and Eve represent humanity is tafsīr inference to be held lightly. What is firm is the descent as representative, the promise of coming guidance from Allah alone, and the outcome for followers — no fear, nor grief (2:38).

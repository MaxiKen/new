## Sūrah al-Maʿārij 70:21

> and withholding when touched with good—

**Expanded Commentary**

**Good's Touch Tightens Him**

Wa-idhā massahu al-khayru manūʿan — "when good touches him, withholding." Massa repeats from verse 20 — touch, light contact. Evil's touch unmade him (fret); good's touch seals him (grudge). Symmetry complete: neither affliction nor fortune survives halūʿ. Khayr spans goods: fortune generally, health and wealth specifically (al-Rāzī's pairing with verse 20's illness-poverty). Touch-tests both ways: pain probes patience, plenty probes generosity. Both probes fail the untreated. Readers should note testing's fairness: God tries by evil and good alike. Excuses expire both directions — hardship cannot justify fret, ease cannot justify clutch. What touches you reveals you. Touched by good lately? Check your fists. The verse already predicts them: closed.

Manūʿ — "withholding, grudging, denying." Ibn Kathīr and al-Jalālayn specify: refusing God's and others' right in wealth — charity denied, zakāh dodged. Withholding is thus not mere stinginess but worship refused: the poor's due is God's command. To clutch is to disobey; avarice is rebellion with receipts. Manūʿ's form (passive participle used actively) suggests constitution — the withholder by habit, cork by character. Such withholding inverts verse 24's coming cure (giving the due). Disease named here; prescription follows there. Readers should translate manūʿ to budgets: income arrived — did charity leave? Good's touch tests instantly. What fingers keep from the poor, the verse labels. Touch received; duty refused. Manūʿ precisely.

**Two Touches, Two Failures**

Massa...massa — touched...touched. The repeated verb frames halūʿ's double failure: evil's touch → jazūʿ (fret); good's touch → manūʿ (grudge). Neither condition reforms him; both deform. Such symmetry diagnoses comprehensively: humanity fails prosperity as surely as adversity. The pair also structures the cure: patience for evil's touch (verse 5, verse 20's antidote), generosity for good's (verse 24, this verse's). Treatment mirrors symptom exactly. Readers should hold both touches together: passing one test while failing the other still fails halūʿ. Religion retrains both responses — calm in want, open in wealth. The sūrah's regimen (verses 22-35) addresses each: prayer steadies fret, charity loosens grudge. Diagnose both; treat both.

Ease corrupts subtler than hardship. Pain announces itself — tears, prayers, cries for help. Plenty anesthetizes — comfort whispering self-sufficiency. Manūʿ thus threatens the comfortable specifically: the tested-by-blessing fail quietly, clutching congratulated as prudence. Ingratitude wears respectability; hoarding passes as planning. Such camouflage makes good's touch deadlier: fret at least knows it suffers, while grudge mistakes itself for wisdom. Readers prospering now should tremble more than readers suffering: your test hides. Audit ease ruthlessly — does fortune open or seal? The verse predicts sealing as default. Override it by giving till it hurts comfort. What good's touch tightens, charity loosens. Prosperity is probation disguised as prize.

**The Hoarder Foretold**

Verse 18's hoarder (jamaʿa fa-awʿā) embodies this verse's manūʿ. Narrative preceded diagnosis: the sūrah showed hoarding, then named its root. Such ordering proves masterful — concrete villain first, abstract syndrome after. The hoarder's sealed vessels illustrate manūʿ's sealed fists; Laẓā's summons (verse 17) previews withholding's wage. Readers connecting verses should trace the chain: good touched → hands closed → vessels sealed → fire summoned. Causation runs from touch to torment through clutch. Break the chain at giving: good touched → hands opened → charity flows → garden near. Same touch, opposite responses, opposite eternities. The verse sits mid-chain, naming the link where wills decide. Touch arrives unchosen; response chooses destiny.

Ibn Kathīr's gloss cuts deepest: the withholder fails to acknowledge the right (ḥaqq) others hold in his wealth. Blindness, not just tightness — he does not see the poor's share sitting in his accounts. Acknowledgment is perception: recognizing God's earmarks on "my" money. Manūʿ lacks the organ: all income reads as self-owned. Such blindness explains shamelessness — withholders feel no guilt, seeing no claim. Cure must therefore open eyes before hands: verse 24's "acknowledged due" (ḥaqq maʿlūm) restores sight. Readers should pray for acknowledgment-vision: show me the poor's portion in my paycheck. Seeing precedes giving; confession precedes charity. What eyes acknowledge, hands release. Manūʿ is treatable blindness — scripture as spectacles, zakāh as therapy.

**Gratitude Is Giving's Name**

Shukr (gratitude) and ṣadaqah (charity) are one act viewed twice: thanks felt, thanks paid. Manūʿ is thus ingratitude enacted — good received, thanks withheld. The withholder pockets blessings while stiffing the Blesser's designated recipients. Such thanklessness inverts prayer's logic (verse 22): prayer thanks with words, charity thanks with wealth. Both or neither — the grateful give doubly. Readers should test gratitude by generosity: thankful hearts have open hands. Professed thanks with closed fists is manūʿ protesting piety. Good's touch demands good's return — blessings circulate or stagnate. What khayr brings, khayr must share. Withhold, and the verse names you. Give, and gratitude names you instead.

**Ease Unmasks Faster Than Hardship**

Comfort corrodes vigilance. ʿĀd's prosperity rotted into defiance (69:5-8): blessed valleys bred swagger, swagger summoned wind. Ease unmasked what hardship never tested — gratitude's absence. Such is good's touch universally: fortune finds the fault lines comfort concealed. History repeats the unmasking — enriched peoples decaying, pampered souls hardening. Manūʿ is ease's signature sin: plenty received, thanks refused. Readers comfortable now should study ʿĀd: their valleys resemble yours. What prosperity whispered to them ("self-sufficient!"), it whispers to you. Answer with giving, and the whisper dies. Give till comfort feels the cost. Ease unmasks; charity re-masks in mercy. Let fortune find open hands.

**The Syndrome Complete, the Cure Next**

Verses 19-21 close diagnosis: halūʿ named (anxious), jazūʿ split (fretful), manūʿ split (withholding). Syndrome complete — constitution plus both symptoms. What follows (verse 22's "except") opens treatment: prayer, charity, chastity, trust. Readers should carry the full chart into the clinic: named disease, prescribed cure. The sūrah turns now from what humanity is to what grace makes. The next verse opens the escape with prayer, the first duty of the cured.

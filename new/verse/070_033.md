## Sūrah al-Maʿārij 70:33

> and who are honest in their testimony;

**Expanded Commentary**

**Seventh Duty: True Testimony**

Wa-lladhīna hum bi-shahādātihim qāʾimūn — "and those who stand firm by their testimonies." Shahādāt (plural) covers every testimony: courtroom oaths, witnessed contracts, character references, even informal accounts of events. Qāʾimūn means standing, upright, firmly established — testimony delivered standing straight, without bending toward bribes or buckling under threats. Such uprightness extends verse 32's fidelity from deeds to words: trusts guarded in action, truth guarded in speech. The list progresses outward through the believer's life — prayer, wealth, conviction, dread, body, trusts — and now reaches the tongue in its most solemn function. False testimony destroys precisely what trusts build: justice, confidence, social order. Readers should treat every testimony as worship: oaths sworn truthfully, accounts given accurately, references written honestly. What tongues testify under pressure, character proves. Stand witnesses upright; never bend testimony. Seventh duty: truth standing.

**Standing Firm Against Pressure**

Qiyām means standing — and standing implies pressure to sit, to bend, to fall. Witnesses face bribes from the rich, threats from the powerful, pleas from kin. "Stand firm for justice as witnesses for Allah, even against yourselves, your parents, or your kin" (paraphrased from 4:135). Such firmness costs: testifying against family fractures gatherings, testifying against patrons ends patronage. Yet the verse commands standing precisely there — where sitting profits. Pressure tests testimony's object: witnesses for Allah stand, witnesses for advantage bend. The upright witness fears only the final cross-examination, where tongues testify against their owners. Readers should pre-decide firmness: resolve now to testify truly regardless of parties. What pressure bends in life, fire straightens in torment. Stand against kin if truth demands; never bend for kin if falsehood asks. Pressure reveals witnesses; firmness defines them.

**False Witness Corrodes Justice**

False testimony ranks among the gravest sins because it weaponizes justice's own machinery. Courts depend on witnesses; lying witnesses turn acquittal into condemnation and theft into title. "Those who do not testify falsely" (paraphrased from 25:72) — the merciful Lord's servants defined partly by this refusal. Such refusal protects the vulnerable most: the poor cannot buy witnesses, the stranger cannot summon crowds. One false oath can hang the innocent, free the guilty, orphan children of inheritance. Corrosion spreads beyond single cases: known perjury teaches communities that oaths are theater. Readers should refuse falsehood absolutely: no oath bent for friends, no signature on unread statements, no "little" lies under oath. What false witness builds (cases won), truth demolishes at audit. Testify truly; let chips fall. Justice depends on witnesses; witnesses answer to God.

**Testimony in Courts and Streets**

Shahādah extends beyond courtrooms into streets, offices, and screens. Job references testify; product reviews testify; gossip testifies destructively. Every "I saw" and "he said" carries testimony's weight wherever reputations hang on it. Such breadth makes verse 33 a daily duty, not a rare summons. Workplace evaluations, academic recommendations, online accusations — all are shahādāt requiring qiyām. The upright reviewer writes what is true, not what pays; the upright colleague reports what happened, not what flatters. Casual slander violates this verse as surely as courtroom perjury, for reputations die by whispers too. Readers should audit tongues daily: references honest? reviews truthful? gossip silenced? What streets whisper, audit amplifies. Testify uprightly everywhere; perjure nowhere. Courts formalize what streets practice.

**Uprightness as Posture**

Qāʾimūn paints posture: standing straight, spine unbent, gaze level. Bodies confess what tongues profess — bent witnesses bend testimony. Such imagery moralizes anatomy: straight spines for straight speech. Slouchers of convenience lean toward advantage; the upright stand regardless. Posture also suggests readiness: standing witnesses are ready witnesses, present and attentive. Sitting back, hedging, forgetting on cue — these are testimony's betrayals. The Book loves qiyām-imagery: standing in prayer, standing for justice, standing firm in battle. Readers should embody uprightness: speak plainly, look directly, refuse evasions. What posture straightens, speech follows. Stand straight physically; testify straight verbally. Bent spines bend stories; straight witnesses stand. Posture preaches before words.

**Truthful Tongues Crown Trusts**

Verses 32-33 join hands and tongue: trusts guarded, testimony stood. Fidelity's arc completes: faithful in deeds (deposits kept), faithful in words (truth told). Such joining exposes half-fidelity: honest with money but loose with facts, or truthful in speech but careless with deposits. Full fidelity integrates members: hands keep, tongues tell, hearts intend. The tongue crowns because speech spreads widest: deposits affect owners, testimony affects multitudes. One true testimony can free prisoners; one false testimony can convict villages. Readers should crown keeping with telling: guard deposits silently, speak truth publicly. What hands hold faithfully, tongues must voice truthfully. Keep and tell; never split fidelity. Crowned tongues complete trusted hands.

**Oaths Deserve Awe**

Testimony's weightiest form is the oath: Allah's name invoked as guarantor of truth. Such invocation deserves awe, for the Guarantor hears and the audit verifies. Casual oath-taking cheapens the currency — swearing by Allah over trifles, haggling, or jokes erodes reverence. Solemn oaths bind souls: sworn testimony in disputes, pledges in contracts, vows in crises. The upright witness swears rarely and truly, weighing each invocation as the Throne weighs it. Thoughtless swearers accumulate liabilities: every "by Allah" nonchalantly uttered gets reviewed. Readers should ration oaths strictly: affirm plainly where possible, swear only where required, fulfill always where sworn. What awe guards (Allah's name), habit profanes (through repetition). Swear seldom, mean always, fulfill entirely. Oaths: truth's heaviest seal, never to be spent lightly.

**Prayers Guarded Next**

Testimony stood; prayers guarded. Verse 34 will close the list where it opened — prayer observed properly. Tongue truthful, then worship guarded: outward honesty yields to upward devotion. Readers should return prayerward: the list that began at verse 22 ends here. What follows guards prayers. The next verse commands proper observance of prayers, worship framing the list.

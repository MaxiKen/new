## Sūrah al-Baqarah 2:145

> Even if you were to bring every proof to the People of the Book, they would not accept your direction ˹of prayer˺, nor would you accept theirs; nor would any of them accept the direction ˹of prayer˺ of another. And if you were to follow their desires after ˹all˺ the knowledge that has come to you, then you would certainly be one of the wrongdoers.

**Expanded Commentary**

**A Statement About Evidence**

The verse states that proofs would not change the position of the People of the Book on the direction of prayer, and the Book uses the phrase for every kind of sign rather than for a single argument. What the sentence describes is a limit on the usefulness of evidence, and the limit lies with the recipients.

The statement belongs to a series in which the sūrah has described recognition without acceptance. The community recognises the Prophet as it recognises its own children, the Book states that they know the direction is the truth from their Lord, and the verse here adds that demonstration would not alter the outcome. Each of these sentences places the obstacle on the side of the will rather than of the understanding.

**The Three Negations**

The sentence then sets out three refusals: they will not follow the community's direction, the community will not follow theirs, and the communities will not follow one another's. The third clause shows that the disagreement is not a simple opposition between two parties, since the two groups addressed were themselves divided on the matter.

The commentators noted that the Jewish tradition faced Jerusalem while the Christian churches were historically oriented toward the east, so that the three directions in question were distinct. The verse's point is that the matter cannot be settled by accommodation, since each community holds its orientation as part of its identity.

**The Meaning of the Direction for Identity**

The direction of prayer signifies more than a bearing. The reports treat the qiblah as a marker of community membership, and the Book's wording in this passage presupposes that a change of direction would be understood as a change of allegiance. To accept another community's direction would be to join it, which is why the verse states that none of the parties would accept the direction of another.

The sūrah's earlier verses used the same logic when they reported the invitation to follow the faith of one community or the other. The Book's answer there was to identify the community with Abraham, and the answer here is the same in a different key: the community's orientation belongs to the religion it professes, and the religion is not a matter of negotiation.

**The Warning Repeated**

The closing clause returns to a warning the sūrah has given before, and the wording is nearly identical to an earlier verse. The Book tells the Prophet ﷺ that if he were to follow the desires of the two communities after the knowledge that had come to him, he would be among the wrongdoers, and the repetition of the warning in this passage indicates its importance.

The warning is addressed to the Prophet ﷺ and functions as an instruction to the community after him. The Book uses this form of address when the command is meant for everyone who holds the same position, since the Prophet's conduct is the standard and the warning applies with even greater force to those who are not protected by his office.

**Why the Book States This So Plainly**

The verse removes an expectation that a community naturally holds, which is that sufficient evidence will produce agreement. The sūrah's narrative has shown that communities can recognise a truth and refuse it, and the sentence states the consequence for the community's own expectations: it should not measure its success by the agreement of its opponents.

The Book's instruction elsewhere commands the community to invite people with wisdom and kind advice and to debate in the best manner, and the verse here does not contradict that instruction. What it does is distinguish between the duty to present the message and the expectation of a particular result, and the sūrah has already told the Prophet ﷺ that he is not accountable for those who reject.

**The Community's Own Direction**

The middle clause also holds a warning for the believers. The community is told that it will not accept the directions of the other communities, which is a statement about its own identity as well as about theirs. The Book is describing a settled situation rather than a temporary disagreement, and the community's position within it is defined by its obedience to the command it received.

The sentence therefore instructs the community to hold its ground without hostility. The following verses will command the community to pardon and bear with those who oppose it, and the two passages are read together by the community's scholars when they consider how Muslims should conduct themselves among people of other faiths.

**What the Verse Adds to the Passage**

The sentence continues the argument that began with the question of the foolish in the previous verses. The sūrah has answered the objection from first principles, described the community's role as witnesses, and reassured those who had prayed in the earlier direction. The verse now closes off the expectation that the disagreement can be settled, and it prepares the reader for the repeated commands to face the Sacred Mosque that follow.

**The Sober Assessment**

The Book's description of the situation is sober. It does not predict the disappearance of the disagreement, and it does not propose a compromise. What it does is define the community's position, warn it against being drawn from that position, and leave the outcome with God. The reader receives an assessment of the dispute rather than a strategy for winning it, and the passage continues with the commands that belong to the community's own practice.

## Sūrah al-Anʿām 6:37

> They ask, “Why has no ˹other˺ sign been sent down to him from his Lord?” Say, ˹O Prophet,˺ “Allah certainly has the power to send down a sign”—though most of them do not know.

**Expanded Commentary**

**A Demand That Keeps Coming Back**

The request is recorded in the Book more often than any other objection to the Prophet's mission, and it always arrives in the same form: if only a sign could be sent down to him from his Lord (13:7). The same demand appears where people with no knowledge ask for God to speak to them or for a sign to come to them (2:118), where they ask why no other sign has been sent down (10:20), where they demand a sign from his Lord as though the previous ones did not exist (20:133), and where they say that if signs had been sent down, they would certainly have believed (29:50).

The Book also records the oaths the demanders attached to their requests: they swore their most solemn oaths that if a sign came they would believe in it, and the answer they received was that signs belong to God, and that even the sight of one would not produce belief in them (6:109). The demand in this verse therefore arrives with a history attached, which is why the response is so brief.

**The Answer Concedes the Power and Withholds the Sign**

Nothing in the reply denies the ability to do what is asked. Allah certainly has the power to send down a sign, and elsewhere the Book states that if He willed, He could send down upon the people a compelling sign from the heavens, leaving their necks bent in submission to it (26:4). The withholding is not a matter of capacity, and the verse makes that explicit so that the argument cannot be shifted to a question about divine strength.

What the answer does refuse is the assumption that a sign arrives on demand, at the discretion of the people who request it. The Book states the reason for the refusal in another place: nothing keeps God from sending the demanded signs except that earlier peoples denied them, and the sign granted to Thamūd was rejected all the same (17:59). A sign that has already been priced in the ledger of previous generations is not a neutral gift.

**The Precedent That Makes a Sign Dangerous**

The sūrah has already treated this subject in its discussion of the demand for an angel. Had an angel been sent down, the matter would have been settled at once and no further time for repentance would have been granted (6:8). The table sent down to the disciples of Jesus carries the same warning in a different register: whoever denies it afterwards will be subjected to a torment never inflicted on anyone else in creation (5:114–115).

So the sign is not simply evidence. It is an increase of accountability, and the Mercy that withholds it is the same Mercy the sūrah has already said God prescribed for Himself (6:12). A people who demanded a proof they had already decided to reject were asking for something that would remove the interval in which they were still able to change.

**The Sign That Was Standing in the Room**

The Book repeatedly names the Qur'an itself as the sign that was already available. If they are in doubt about what was revealed to the servant of God, let them produce a sūrah like it and call their helpers besides Allah, if what they say is true (2:23). Let them produce ten fabricated sūrahs like it (11:13). If all humans and jinn were to combine to produce the equivalent of this recitation, they could not produce its equal, however they supported one another (17:88).

That challenge was addressed to people whose whole culture was built on the value of words, which is why the Book treats their inability to meet it as decisive. The demand for a different sign asks for something other than the evidence already supplied, and the sūrah has shown earlier what the audience did with the evidence in its hands.

**"Most of Them Do Not Know"**

The verse closes with a statement about the demanders rather than about the sign. Most of them do not know, and the ignorance is not about the mechanics of revelation. It is about the purpose of signs, the reason for delay, and the way belief actually works in a human being. The Book makes the same observation after describing all the evidence God could assemble, where it says that even with angels sent, the dead speaking, and every sign assembled before their eyes, they still would not believe unless God so willed (6:111).

Within the argument of the sūrah, this clause puts the whole discussion on a different footing. The question was asked as though the problem were a shortage of proof in the world, and the answer relocates it to a shortage of understanding in the asker.

**The Consistency of the Sūrah's Answers**

Every demand that has been raised in this passage receives the same kind of answer. The request for an angel was answered with the fact that the angel's arrival would end the opportunity to repent (6:8). The request for a written book was answered with the audience's verdict on it before it arrived (6:7). The request for a compelling sign is answered here with a statement of God's power and a comment on the askers' knowledge.

The pattern is deliberate, and the Book names it in another sūrah where the Prophet is told to say that he only follows what is revealed to him and that the recitation itself is insight, guidance, and mercy for those who believe (7:203). The signs were never the variable. What the audience did with what had already come was.

## Sūrah al-Isrāʾ 17:2

> **And We gave Moses the Scripture and made it a guide for the Children of Israel, ˹stating:˺ “Do not take besides Me any other Trustee of Affairs,**

**Expanded Commentary**

**Moses Given Scripture As Guide**

Wa-ātaynā Mūsā al-kitāba wa-jaʿalnāhu hudan li-banī isrā'īl — and We gave Moses the Scripture and made it a guide for Children of Israel. Ātaynā from a-t-y means We gave. Mūsā, Moses, prophet who led Israelites from Egypt. Al-kitāb, the Scripture, Torah, containing law and guidance. Wa-jaʿalnāhu hudan, We made it a guide, from h-d-y, to guide. Li-banī isrā'īl, for Children of Israel, descendants of Yaʿqūb. Al-Ṭabarī explains that after mentioning Night Journey to Jerusalem, city of Israelite prophets, the sūrah mentions Moses and Torah as guide for Children of Israel, showing continuity of guidance from Moses to Muḥammad. The Torah was guide for Israelites as Qur'an is guide for all, as verse 9 will say this Qur'an guides to most upright. The connection between isrā' and Moses is also that Moses was shown signs, as Muḥammad was shown signs, and both faced corruption of their peoples.

**Do Not Take Besides Me Any Trustee**

Allā tattakhidhū min dūnī wakīlā — that you not take besides Me any trustee, guardian, disposer of affairs. Wakīl from w-k-l means trustee, one entrusted with affairs, disposer. Min dūnī, besides Me, apart from Me. Al-Ṭabarī says meaning is do not take besides Allah any trustee to whom you entrust affairs, whom you worship, rely upon, as protector besides Allah. This is same message as in previous chapter: worship Allah and shun ṭāghūt, do not take two gods. For Children of Israel, this meant not taking idols or tyrants as trustees besides Allah. Al-Rāzī notes that wakīl here includes both deity and guardian: do not take any as god or as ultimate trustee of affairs besides Allah. The prohibition allā tattakhidhū is prohibition that is core of tawḥīd: do not take besides Allah any trustee.

**Why This Command Follows Night Journey**

Night Journey took Prophet to Jerusalem, city of Israelites, and showed him signs. Then mentioning Moses and Torah as guide for Israelites shows that Jerusalem's blessed surroundings were blessed because many prophets worshipped there and received revelation. This prohibition against taking besides Him any trustee was given to Israelites via Torah, but they later violated by causing corruption twice, as verse 4 will say. The sūrah therefore uses Israelite history as lesson: they were given Scripture as guide and commanded not to take trustee besides Allah, but they corrupted, so they were punished. The lesson for Makkans is similar: you are given Qur'an as guide, commanded not to take two gods, so do not corrupt like Israelites did.

**The Continuity Of Guidance**

We gave Moses Scripture and made it guide for Children of Israel — this shows that guidance is not new; it has history. Every community had messenger, as 16:36 said, with message worship Allah and shun false gods. Moses' message to Israelites was same: do not take besides Me any trustee. The Qur'an's message to Arabs is same: do not take two gods. The continuity shows that tawḥīd is original, universal, not particular. Al-Qurṭubī notes that mentioning Moses after isrā' also honors Moses, who was encountered by Prophet during ascension, where Moses advised Prophet to ask for reduction in prayers. The Night Journey therefore links Prophet to Moses personally, not only historically.

**A Lesson On Trusteeship**

Wakīl, trustee, is one to whom you entrust affairs, relying on him to manage. In worldly affairs, you may have trustee for business, but in ultimate affairs, trustee should be Allah alone. To take besides Allah any trustee as ultimate disposer is shirk. Al-Zamakhsharī explains that wakīl here means protector, helper, to whom you entrust your affairs, expecting help, provision, intercession. If you take idol as wakīl, you entrust to one who knows nothing, creates nothing, is dead, as previous chapter described idols. If you take Allah as wakīl, you entrust to One who hears, sees, owns heavens and earth, whose devotion is everlasting. The command therefore is to entrust ultimate affairs to Allah alone.

**Linguistic Note On Kitāb And Wakīl**

Ātaynā from a-t-y, to give. Mūsā, Moses. Al-kitāb, Scripture, from k-t-b, to write. Jaʿalnāhu from j-ʿ-l, to make. Hudan from h-d-y, guidance. Banī isrā'īl, Children of Israel, banī from b-n-y, sons, children. Allā tattakhidhū, that you not take, from a-kh-dh. Min dūnī, besides Me. Wakīl from w-k-l, trustee, disposer, from same root as tawakkul, trust. The verse's structure — We gave Moses Scripture and made it guide for Children of Israel, stating do not take besides Me any trustee — moves from giving to purpose to command, showing that Scripture was given as guide containing command of tawḥīd, which is core of all Scriptures.

**How This Verse Prepares For History Of Two Corruptions**

Verse 4 will warn Children of Israel in Scripture you will cause corruption twice and become extremely arrogant. The command not to take trustee besides Me is what they violated when they corrupted. Their corruption included taking others as trustees, worshipping idols, following tyrants, and becoming arrogant. The sūrah therefore sets up command then violation then punishment, as pattern for all communities. The reader is invited to see that same pattern applies to Makkans: given Qur'an as guide, commanded not to take two gods, if they corrupt and become arrogant, they will be punished as Israelites were. The verse therefore is both historical and warning.


**Why Torah As Guide Matters For Qur'an**

Torah as guide for Children of Israel matters for Qur'an because Qur'an confirms previous Scriptures and completes them. The Qur'an says *We sent down Torah containing guidance and light* (5:44). By mentioning Torah as guide, the sūrah shows that guidance has always been via Books, not only via signs, and that Qur'an is latest Book in that chain. The command not to take trustee besides Me is in Torah and Qur'an, showing that core message unchanged. The sūrah's mention of Torah also answers those who asked for Book: you were given Book before, Torah, and you corrupted, so being given Book alone does not guarantee guidance; you must follow its command not to take trustee besides Allah. The lesson is that receiving Scripture is blessing that requires gratitude and obedience, not arrogance.

**Practical Implications Of Taking Allah As Trustee**

Taking Allah as Trustee, wakīl, has practical implications: you rely on Him for provision, protection, and outcome, while taking permissible means. You do not rely on idols, stars, or tyrants as ultimate trustees. In daily life, this means you may have worldly trustees like agents, lawyers, but ultimate trust is in Allah. Al-Qurṭubī says tawakkul, trust, is to take Allah as wakīl, entrusting affairs to Him, as 16:42 said those who emigrated were patient and trusted Lord. This prohibition against taking besides Him any trustee therefore is command to have tawakkul in Allah alone for ultimate affairs, which is part of tawḥīd that brings good life and Gardens as reward.

## Sūrah al-Tawbah 9:112

> ˹It is the believers˺ who repent, who are devoted to worship, who praise ˹their Lord˺, who fast, who bow down and prostrate themselves, who encourage good and forbid evil, and who observe the limits set by Allah. And give good news to the believers.

**Expanded Commentary**

**Repentance Is the First Quality Named**

The verse begins its list with repentance. The believer is not defined as someone who never fails. He is defined as someone who returns when he fails and refuses to make sin his permanent direction. Repentance keeps the other qualities alive because it removes the pride that would turn worship into a claim of superiority.

A repentant person admits the wrong, regrets it, abandons it, and repairs what he can. He does not use a religious routine to avoid a moral duty. The order of the verse matters: before the servant is praised for worship, he is reminded to return to Allah. Every act of obedience begins again when the heart is cleansed of self-approval.

**Worship Gives the Life Its Center**

The worshippers devote themselves to Allah, and the list then names praise, fasting, bowing, and prostration. These forms join the heart, tongue, body, and appetite in obedience. Worship is not a private mood. It trains the servant to remember Allah in different conditions and to let remembrance direct daily conduct.

Fasting is described by commentators as a journey because it requires leaving ordinary enjoyments for a period and learning that desire does not command the person. Bowing and prostration teach bodily humility. Praise teaches gratitude in ease and hardship. Together the practices form a servant who can receive blessing without pride and endure difficulty without rebellion.

**Praise Must Continue in Every Condition**

The believer praises Allah not only when the outcome is pleasant. Praise in hardship does not mean calling pain pleasant or denying a legitimate complaint. It means recognizing that Allah remains wise and worthy of worship while the servant seeks relief. This praise prevents a trial from becoming an argument against the Lord.

A person who praises only when he receives what he wants may be praising his own preference. The believer praises because Allah is Allah, then receives the gift or difficulty with a heart that remains connected. His words become truthful when they are supported by patience, gratitude, and the refusal to blame Allah for what he does not understand.

**Moral Action Belongs Beside Private Worship**

The verse moves from prayer and fasting to encouraging good and forbidding evil. This prevents worship from becoming an escape from society. A person who bows before Allah must also care about how people are treated. He encourages goodness through teaching, support, and example, and resists wrong through knowledge, wisdom, and courage.

The duty does not authorize harshness without understanding. The servant must know the rule, consider capacity, and avoid creating a greater harm. Yet caution cannot become permanent silence when people are being exploited. Worship gives the reformer humility, while moral action gives worship a social consequence. Both belong to the same faithful life.

**Limits Protect the Whole Path**

The believers observe the limits set by Allah. These limits include obligations, prohibitions, rights, and boundaries in worship and social dealings. A person may be enthusiastic about one form of obedience while crossing limits elsewhere. The verse insists that sincerity must be disciplined by what Allah has made lawful and unlawful.

Limits also protect mercy. A leader cannot claim that a good goal permits fraud, humiliation, or betrayal. A donor cannot call theft charity. A reformer cannot call violence correction when Allah forbids transgression. The believer asks not only whether an action feels religious but whether it remains within the boundaries Allah established.

**Good News Belongs to the Whole Character**

The Prophet is told to give good news to the believers after this list. The news is not reserved for one spectacular act. It belongs to a pattern: repentance, worship, praise, fasting, prayer, moral courage, and respect for limits. A servant may be weak in one area while growing in another, but he keeps moving toward the whole character.

This gives hope without lowering the standard. The believer does not despair because he is still being repaired, and he does not relax because he has performed a few visible deeds. Good news encourages perseverance. Allah loves the servant who keeps returning, keeps worshipping, and keeps trying to make goodness and justice possible within the limits of the Lord.

The list also keeps the believer from separating spirituality from boundaries. A person may be moved by praise, fasting, or prostration and still need to correct how he treats a neighbor. The limits set by Allah give worship a visible shape. They govern contracts, speech, family rights, charity, and the use of power. A repentant worshipper becomes trustworthy when his devotion makes him more careful with people, not merely more expressive in private. The good news is for those whose worship produces this whole character: returning when wrong, praising when tested, serving when able, and stopping when Allah sets a limit.

The final command to give good news keeps the list from becoming a checklist used to measure other people. Each believer examines his own repentance and worship before evaluating a neighbor. If he sees a weakness, he offers help rather than a verdict. If he sees a strength, he thanks Allah rather than envying it. The community becomes encouraging when its members call each other toward good while remembering that Allah alone knows the sincerity beneath the visible act. The promise is hopeful, but it is received through a life of continued return and disciplined obedience.

The believer also needs patience with gradual growth. Some worshippers are learning to fast, others are learning to speak against wrong, and others are learning to respect a limit they once ignored. The list gives them a direction without requiring a performance of perfection. They repent when they fail, return to worship, and ask truthful companions for help. Good news is not reserved for the person who has already arrived. It encourages the person who is walking sincerely and refuses to turn a weakness into an identity.

## Sūrah al-Aʿrāf 7:41

> Hell will be their bed; flames will be their cover. This is how We reward the wrongdoers.

**Expanded Commentary**

**The Furnishings That Have Been Inverted**

The verse names two objects of rest and turns them into instruments of punishment: a bed beneath the condemned and coverings above them. The Arabic for bed, *mihād*, comes from a root that means to spread out, and the word describes level ground or a carpet prepared for a person to lie on. The Arabic for the coverings, *ghawāshin*, describes what is laid over something and conceals it. A bed and a cover are the ordinary furnishings of sleep, and the verse applies both to the Fire.

The Book uses the image of the spread bed for the ease of the world as well as for the punishment of the Hereafter, and the sūrah of the Clatterer states that the one whose scales are light will find his refuge in a pit. The inversion in front of us is exact, since the two things that make sleep possible are made into the two things that make it impossible. The verse states the reversal without comment, and the reader supplies the rest.

**Layers Above and Below**

The sūrah of the Troops states that the condemned will have layers of fire above and below them, and it closes with the address that calls on the servants to fear their Lord (39:16). The sūrah of the Spider states that on the day the punishment overwhelms them from above them and from beneath their feet, they will be told to reap what they sowed (29:55). The verse in front of us supplies both halves of the arrangement in a single sentence, and the two other passages confirm that the description is a standing image of the Fire's enclosure.

The enclosure leaves no direction of escape. The Book's descriptions of the punishment include the closing of the ways out, and the sūrah of the Cave reports the wrongdoers asking to be released or given relief and being refused. The bed beneath and the covering above are therefore not two separate torments but the statement that the person is held inside the punishment on every side.

**Who the Wrongdoers Are in This Passage**

The verse closes with the declaration that this is how the wrongdoers are recompensed, and the term gathers the offenses that the sūrah has named. The verses immediately before it have described the proud who refuse the signs (7:40), the verse that follows it will name those who believe and act rightly as the opposite group (7:42), and the passage that follows continues with those who turned others from the way of God and sought to make it crooked (7:45) and those who took their religion as amusement (7:51). The formula in front of us is the verdict on all of them.

The Book's use of the term for wrongdoing in this sūrah includes the account of the first pair, who were told that approaching the tree would make them wrongdoers (7:19), and their confession that they had wronged themselves (7:23). The word covers the transgression of a limit, and the passage in front of us applies it to the communities that denied the messengers and to the individuals who follow their example.

**The Formula of Recompense**

The closing formula, that this is how the wrongdoers are recompensed, appears in the sūrah more than once, and its repetition gives the passage its structure. The verse before this one states that the proud are repaid in the same way (7:40), the verse in front of us repeats the formula for the wrongdoers, and the following verse states the reward of the believers without the formula. The arrangement sets the two returns side by side and leaves the reader to mark the difference.

The Book's use of the formula elsewhere carries the same function, and the sūrah of the Clatterer and the sūrah of the Inevitable use matching statements for each of the two groups. The equality of the phrasing means that the Book never presents the punishment as an excess. What the person receives is what the description of the class requires, and the declaration in front of us states the correspondence in the fewest possible words.

**The Contrast in the Book's Depiction of Rest**

The Book describes the rest of the people of the Garden with the same vocabulary it uses here, and the contrast is deliberate. The sūrah of the Overwhelming describes raised couches for the people of the Garden (88:13), the sūrah of the Merciful describes them reclining on furnishings lined with brocade (55:54), and the sūrah of the Heights itself states in its later verses that the people of the Garden will be on couches facing one another. The bed and the covering in front of us belong to the same family of images, and they are aimed in the opposite direction.

The sūrah of the Tidings states that the condemned will find there neither coolness nor drink (78:24), and the sūrah of the Overwhelming states that their food will be a bitter thorn that neither nourishes nor satisfies hunger (88:6–7). The Book builds its descriptions of the Fire out of the absence and the inversion of the goods of the Garden, and the verse in front of us supplies the two most domestic of those goods.

**Where the Verse Sits**

The verse stands forty-first in the chapter, and it completes the sequence that began with the exclusion of the proud from the Garden (7:36–40). The passage then turns in the following verse to the believers and their reward, and the two descriptions are set next to each other in the sūrah's account of the two outcomes.

The reader who has followed the sūrah from its account of the weighing of deeds and the questioning of the messengers now sees the first of the two outcomes stated in the present tense of the Hereafter: a bed spread below, coverings above, and the declaration that the arrangement is the recompense of the wrongdoers. The chapter's next verse states what stands on the other side.

**The Transition to the Next Verse**

The next verse states that those who believe and do good — and no soul is tasked beyond what it can afford — are the people of the Garden and will remain there (7:42). The furnishings of the condemned in front of us give way to the dwelling of the believers, and the sūrah's account of the two ends of the reckoning is complete.

The two verses together state the division that the passage has been preparing. One group is held inside the fire on every side, and the other is given a garden and a permanence; and the sentence that separates them is the one the chapter has stated from its opening: follow what has been sent down, and do not take protectors besides God.

## Sūrah an-Nūr 24:26

> Wicked women are for wicked men, and wicked men are for wicked women. And virtuous women are for virtuous men, and virtuous men are for virtuous women. The virtuous are innocent of what the wicked say. They will have forgiveness and an honourable provision.

**Expanded Commentary**

**The Twin Interpretations: Persons and Utterances**

The verse establishes a profound correspondence between moral nature and its proper sphere: *al-khabīthātu lil-khabīthīna wa-al-khabīthūna lil-khabīthāt wa-al-ṭayyibātu lil-ṭayyibīna wa-al-ṭayyibūna liṭ-ṭayyibāt*. Classical exegetes divided this statement into two profound hermeneutical readings. The first reading applies the adjectives directly to persons and matrimonial compatibility: corrupt women are suited for corrupt men, and pure women for pure men. A natural spiritual affinity draws like to like, ensuring that individuals find domestic alignment with those who mirror their moral character and spiritual inclinations.

The second reading, strongly favored by authoritative commentators including Ibn ʿAbbās, al-Ṭabarī, and Fakhr al-Dīn al-Rāzī, applies the feminine plural to speech (*al-kalimāt al-khabīthāt*). In this linguistic analysis, foul, malicious words are suited only to foul people, who naturally formulate and propagate them; whereas pure, wholesome words belong to pure people, who speak only truth. Both readings reinforce the moral architecture of the universe, affirming that evil speech and evil character gravitate toward one another, while purity remains the exclusive domain of the righteous.

**The Matrimonial Sanctity of Prophetic Companionship**

Exemplified through the personal interpretation, this verse establishes the absolute, metaphysical purity of ʿĀʾishah and the domestic sanctuary of the Prophet ﷺ. The Messenger of Allah ﷺ is universally recognized as the purest and most virtuous of all creation (*aṭyab al-ṭayyibīn*). It is an absolute theological impossibility for divine providence to have joined the greatest of the prophets in sacred marriage with a woman who was anything less than pure.

By decreeing that *al-ṭayyibāt liṭ-ṭayyibīn*, God affirms that ʿĀʾishah was created, nurtured, and divinely selected as an immaculate partner for His chosen Messenger. For anyone to suggest that she was guilty of indecency is not merely to slander a companion; it is to accuse God of pairing His most beloved Prophet with corruption. The verse strips away all credibility from the scandalmongers, demonstrating that their accusations were an assault on divine wisdom itself, while confirming ʿĀʾishah's eternal standing among the righteous and the chosen.

**The Innocence of the Virtuous: ulāʾika mubarraʾūna mimmā yaqūlūn**

The divine decree delivers an absolute, authoritative verdict of exoneration: *ulāʾika mubarraʾūna mimmā yaqūlūn*, they are declared innocent and completely cleared of what the slanderers say. The use of the passive participle *mubarraʾūn* carries immense legal and theological weight. It signifies that their purity is not a matter of human opinion, courtroom negotiation, or social consensus; it is an eternal status conferred directly by the Almighty.

The righteous—personified by ʿĀʾishah and Ṣafwān ibn al-Muʿaṭṭal—are fundamentally detached from the filth generated by malicious tongues. The fabricated rumors circulated in the alleys of Madinah were mere acoustic vibrations that had zero connection to reality. In the court of divine truth, the mud hurled by hypocrites cannot adhere to the illuminated armor of established virtue. God Himself stands as their advocate, vindicating their honor before all generations of believers until the end of time, ensuring their eternal dignity.

**The Ultimate Compensation: maghfiratun wa-rizqun karīm**

The verse seals the exoneration of the slandered believers with a promise of transcendent reward: *lahum maghfiratun wa-rizqun karīm*, for them is forgiveness and an honorable, generous provision. The victims of defamation endured profound emotional agony, public humiliation, and sleepless nights during the month of the slander. Divine justice guarantees that their patient endurance will be met with boundless divine compensation.

The reward is twofold. First, they receive *maghfirah*—the divine covering and pardon of all human shortcomings, ensuring their unblemished entry into the divine presence. Second, they are granted *rizqun karīm*—a noble, bountiful, and everlasting provision in the gardens of Paradise, where no slander, malicious whisper, or sorrow will ever reach them. While the unrepentant hypocrites face the eternal curse of God, the pure souls whom they persecuted inherit everlasting joy, dignity, and divine hospitality in the realm of peace, basking in eternal honor.

**The Moral Gravity of Linguistic Alignment**

Exploring the linguistic reading—that foul words belong to foul people—reveals a vital lesson in spiritual psychology. Human speech is an infallible mirror reflecting the internal reality of the heart. A soul corrupted by envy, cynicism, and hidden hypocrisy naturally delights in vile speech, manufacturing and circulating scandalous gossip with effortless ease.

Conversely, a soul illuminated by faith and reverence for God recoils instinctively from defamation. The pure person speaks only that which is wholesome, constructive, and true, refusing to allow their tongue to become a sewer for character assassination. The verse teaches believers to evaluate their fellow human beings not by external pretenses of piety or social prominence, but by the moral quality of the language they deploy, recognizing that pure speech is the hallmark of a pure soul, reflecting the divine light within.

**The Finality of Exoneration across History**

The historical impact of this verse resonates through fourteen centuries of Islamic scholarship and spiritual devotion. For generations, the Muslim community has recited this divine declaration in daily prayers, constantly renewing God's personal vindication of the Mother of the Believers. In traditional Islamic literature, ʿĀʾishah became revered by the epithet *al-Mubarraʾah*—the one whose innocence was proclaimed from above the seven heavens.

This eternal proclamation brings the tumultuous ordeal of the Grand Slander to an absolute, triumphant close. It demonstrates that while falsehood may enjoy a temporary, noisy triumph in the marketplace of gossip, it is an ephemeral shadow destined to dissolve before the radiant dawn of divine truth. Those who place their trust in God and endure persecution with patience will always see their honor restored by the Lord of the worlds, whose word remains supreme forever, shining as a beacon of hope for the oppressed.

## Sūrah al-Anʿām 6:113

> so that the hearts of those who disbelieve in the Hereafter may be receptive to it, be pleased with it, and be persistent in their evil pursuits.

**Expanded Commentary**

**The Purpose Clause of the Previous Verse**

So that the hearts of those who disbelieve in the Hereafter may be receptive to it. The verse states the outcome of the whispering described in the previous sentence, and it names the people in whom the whispering takes effect. The message of the enemies of the prophets reaches the people who have already denied the Hereafter, and the denial is given as the reason the message finds a place in them.

The connection between the denial of the Hereafter and the reception of deceptive speech is a theme the sūrah has been developing. The people who live only for the present life have no measure by which to test a proposal, and their judgement of what to accept is confined to what serves them now. The Book states the same connection elsewhere, and it treats the rejection of the Hereafter as the root from which other errors grow.

**The Three Terms of the Sentence**

The verse describes three effects in sequence, and each one deepens the hold of the deception. The first is that the hearts incline to the whispering and give it a hearing; the second is that they are pleased with it, so that the hearing becomes acceptance; and the third is that they persist in what they were already doing, so that the acceptance hardens into a settled course. The sentence therefore describes a process rather than a moment, and the process has the shape of a decision that becomes a habit.

The Book describes the same progression in its account of the people who did not expect to meet God. The sūrah of Yūnus says that those who do not hope for the meeting with God and who are content with the life of this world have their deeds made fair to them and are left in their confusion (10:7), and the sūrah of the Ants describes those who do not believe in the Hereafter as people whose deeds have been made attractive to them and who wander blindly (27:4).

**The Receptivity the Verse Describes**

The Book's word for the heart's reception describes the opening of a container to what is poured into it, and the image appears in the sūrah of the Prophets, where the Prophet's enemies are described as listening to the message while covering their hearts so that they might not understand it (17:46). The verse in front of us states the opposite condition in the people who receive the whisperings, and the reading of the two verses together shows what is at stake.

The Book attaches the same condition to the hearts of those who reject the Hereafter in several passages. The sūrah of the Troops says that when God alone is mentioned, the hearts of those who disbelieve in the Hereafter are filled with disgust, while the mention of the other gods brings them joy (39:45), and the sūrah of Fussilat states that God assigned to the deniers companions who made their conduct appear fair to them, so that they would be among those for whom the word was fulfilled (41:25). The verse in front of us states the arrangement in its most compact form.

**The Persistence That Follows**

And be persistent in their evil pursuits. The sentence ends with the activity the audience had already been engaged in, and the Book describes the enemies' work as aimed at maintaining that activity rather than at creating it. The whispering does not produce a new direction; it supplies reasons for continuing in the direction already taken.

The Book's account of the people who persist in denial uses the same language in the sūrah of the Troops and the sūrah of the Cave, where those who turned away are described as pursuing what they had been doing. The verse in front of us names the mechanism that keeps a person in that pursuit, and it places the mechanism in the company of the whisperers rather than in the person himself.

**What the Verse Says About the Audience's Condition**

The verse states that the enemies of the prophets are not the cause of the audience's disbelief but the means by which it is confirmed. The people described here had already disbelieved in the Hereafter before the whisperings reached them, and the whisperings found in them a disposition that was prepared. The Book's presentation of the two orders of causes is consistent: the enemies work, and God permits the work, and the person who receives it does so because of what he has already chosen.

The Book's language about the hearts of these people also explains the audience's resistance to the arguments the sūrah has been making. The signs, the history of the earlier communities, and the confession of the earlier scriptures were all present, and none of them reached a heart that had been closed to what comes after death.

**The Transition to the Next Verse**

The verse that follows turns from the enemies' deception to the Book's authority, with the Prophet commanded to say that he will not seek a judge other than God, who has revealed the Book perfectly explained (6:114). The two verses stand in deliberate contrast: the whisperings produce hearts that receive falsehood, and the clearly explained Book is what the Prophet is told to hold to.

That contrast runs through the rest of the chapter, which continues to describe the audience's demands and the Book's answers. The sūrah has now completed its account of how the refusal of the message is produced among people who have denied the Hereafter, and it turns to the authority that stands over the whole dispute.

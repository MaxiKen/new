## Sūrah al-Maʿārij 70:30

> except with their wives or those ˹bondwomen˺ in their possession, for then they are free from blame,

**Expanded Commentary**

**Lawful Channels Named**

Illā ʿalā azwājihim aw mā malakat aymānuhum — "except with their spouses or what their right hands possess." Azwāj means spouses, pairs — covenantal partners joined by witnessed vows. Mā malakat aymānuhum, "what right hands possess," addressed slave women of that age, elsewhere regulated with progressive restriction (see 4:3; 4:25). The exception channels desire lawfully: fenced satisfaction, honored release. Spouses stand first — marriage primary, principal, paradigmatic. Such naming dignifies covenant: desire finds honor inside vows. Readers should channel desires covenantally: marry honorably, satisfy purely, cherish faithfully. What divine law channels toward bliss, license floods toward fire. Gates named precisely; fences standing firmly. Enter lawful gates only; trespass none. Channels named are channels blessed.

Fa-innahum ghayru malūmīn — "then they are not blamed." Blame lifts inside bounds: lawful enjoyment stands guiltless, delight without disgrace. Guilt fences outside: trespass blamed, transgression condemned. Such pairing liberates cleanly: enjoy unashamed within covenant, abstain firmly without. Conscience gets calibrated precisely: peace inside bounds, alarm outside. Believers need neither guilt about marriage nor comfort about license. Readers should enjoy blamelessly: marital delight received gratefully, mutual affection cherished gladly. What bounds bless with peace, trespass curses with remorse. Guiltless inside covenant; guilty outside it. Know bounds exactly; enjoy peace fully. Calibrated conscience, clear delight.

Azwāj dignify what appetite merely wants: pairing elevates craving to covenant. Spouses share mutual rights, witnessed vows, merged lives, planned futures. Desire covenanted becomes worship-adjacent: satisfaction lawful, affection rewarded, intimacy sanctified. Such dignity contrasts license absolutely: commitment crowns craving, vows veil vulnerability. Marriage converts appetite's fire into household's warmth. Readers should marry deliberately and honorably: vows witnessed publicly, rights honored privately, mercy practiced perpetually. What covenant covers with sanctity, dignity clothes with honor. Desire covenanted delights doubly — pleasure plus purity, passion plus peace. Marry well; dignify desire. Covenant: appetite's crown.

**Historical Context, Permanent Principle**

The possessions clause regulated an existing institution; it founded none. Seventh-century slavery stood entrenched globally; revelation fenced, restricted, and humanized it while pointing toward abolition — manumission praised lavishly, marrying them honored, freeing expiation prescribed. The verse limits rather than launches: regulation as leash on injustice. Trajectory matters: every related ruling tightens toward dignity. The permanent principle outlasts the historical form: lawful, committed, responsible channels for desire. Readers today live where slavery is abolished: spouses remain the standing channel, principle persists intact. What history contextualized temporarily, principle perpetuates eternally. Channel lawfully, committedly, responsibly. Principle eternal; historical forms reformed past. Honor the trajectory's destination.

Licensed never means limitless. Ghayr malūmīn — unblamed — still implies measure: gratitude, moderation, mutuality, mindfulness of duties. License requires stewardship: satisfaction without excess, delight without neglect of worship, pleasure without cruelty. Such balance prevents libertinism-inside-marriage: bounds within bounds, manners within license. Spouses hold rights over each other's bodies; mutuality governs. Readers should enjoy temperately and gratefully: delight received as gift, partner cherished as trust. What license permits broadly, wisdom tempers finely. Blameless yet purposeful; free yet faithful. Enjoy gratefully; never indulge heedlessly. Gifts demand gratitude.

**Limits Liberate**

Fences free more than fields: chastity outside liberates enjoyment inside. Boundaries remove guilt's static — clear conscience, full delight, unshadowed intimacy. Paradox holds universally: fenced delight exceeds open license in sweetness. License promises freedom but delivers bondage — addiction, guilt, disease, distrust. Fences promise limits but deliver liberty — peace, trust, health, honor. Readers should embrace fences as liberation's architecture: limits are not liberty's enemies but engineers. What boundaries bar (ruin), they purchase (peace). Fenced gardens bloom profusely; open fields get trampled. Love limits genuinely; live free actually. Fences: freedom's frame. Freedom's frame deserves daily admiration: study bounds till they appear beautiful. Meditate their wisdom each morning; thank their Giver each evening. What frames protect — honor, health, heaven itself — gratitude maintains through obedience. Admire fences sincerely; inhabit freedom fully. Framed liberty outlasts open license by eternity. Love the frame; live the freedom.

**Desire Disciplined Young**

Youth's ardor needs channels early. Guarding gaze from adolescence, purposeful work filling hours, marriage pursued timely — disciplines preventing. Idle energies stray; occupied ones steady. Sports, studies, service drain surplus fires constructively. Such youth-craft protects: habits formed young harden lifelong. Parents and mentors must channel: supervise screens, encourage marriage, model purity. Readers young should build fences early: guard now, glide later. What youth disciplines, age enjoys. Early fences, lifelong ease.

Spouses hold mutual rights: kindness commanded, companionship owed. "Live with them in kindness" (paraphrased from 4:19) — muʿāsharah bi-l-maʿrūf, recognized good. Such mutuality transforms license into love: bodies shared gratefully, pleasure given generously. Rights run both directions — husbands owe kindness, wives receive honor; affection circulates. Gratitude governs where mere appetite would rule: thankful spouses cherish, entitled spouses consume. The verse's blamelessness assumes this mutuality — ghayr malūmīn within kindness. Readers married should audit mutuality: giving or taking? cherishing or using? What gratitude governs, delight rewards. Thank God for spouses; thank spouses with kindness. Blameless beds are grateful beds.

**Formation Beats Information**

Knowing bounds differs from loving them. Information states fences; formation makes fences beloved. Hearts must be habituated: reverence cultivated till obedience delights. Such formation proceeds by practice — kept bounds endear bounds. Obedience enjoyed reinforces: chaste months make chaste years. Mere information without formation breeds resentment — fences felt as prison. Formation converts prison to garden: loved limits liberate. Readers should form hearts deliberately: meditate wisdom of bounds, befriend the chaste, celebrate purity. What formation loves, information merely knows. Inform minds; form hearts. Loving fences keeps them effortlessly. Formation: obedience's engine.

**Transgression Defined Next**

Allowance stated generously; violation defined next strictly. Verse 31 will criminalize the beyond — seekers past bounds stand transgressors. Permission first, then prohibition: gates opened, then walls marked. Readers should mark walls carefully: beyond lawful gates lies ʿudwān, transgression. What follows fences precisely. The next verse defines seeking beyond as transgression, walling the lawful with warning.

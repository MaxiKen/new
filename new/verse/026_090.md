## Sūrah al-Shuʿarāʾ 26:90

> ˹On that Day˺ Paradise will be brought near to the God-fearing,

**Expanded Commentary**

**Paradise Is Brought Near as a Welcome**

The verse depicts Paradise being brought near to those who fear God. The image turns reward into an approaching welcome. The Garden is not described as a distant idea that the righteous must force themselves to imagine. It is brought near to them by God's command, making the promised destination present before their eyes.

Nearness carries emotional as well as spatial force. A person who has travelled toward a goal is strengthened when the goal comes into view. The verse gives the God-fearing that moment of recognition. Their long obedience has not led into emptiness. The place prepared by God is now near, and its nearness announces acceptance.

**God-Fearing Means Living Under Taqwa**

The God-fearing are those whose awareness of God shapes choices. Taqwa is not panic that makes a person unable to act. It is a guarding of the self from what displeases God, joined to hope in His mercy. The people described here have treated accountability as real, so their conduct was not governed only by immediate appetite or public approval.

This quality is deeper than a reputation for caution. A person may appear restrained while hiding arrogance or false worship. Taqwa reaches the heart and the ordinary decision. It guides speech, wealth, family duty, private conduct, and the use of power. Paradise is brought near to people whose inner awareness has become a practical form of obedience.

**Nearness Is an Honour Granted by God**

The verse does not say that the God-fearing seize Paradise through their own strength. It is brought near. That passive form directs attention to divine generosity. The righteous act, repent, and persevere, but the arrival of Paradise is still a gift from the Lord. Their nearness to the Garden reflects His acceptance rather than a right they can impose.

This language protects good works from becoming a source of pride. A servant may work hard without imagining that the reward is owed as a commercial payment. Every capacity to obey, every chance to repent, and every moment of restraint is itself sustained by God. Nearness to Paradise is therefore received with gratitude and humility.

**The Scene Answers the Prayer for Bliss**

Abraham asked to be among those awarded the Garden of Bliss. This verse shows the kind of fulfilment his prayer seeks. Paradise is not only named as a future location; it is brought near to a class of people whose hearts were guarded by reverence. The prayer's hope now appears as a scene of divine welcome.

The connection also clarifies the relation between hope and conduct. Abraham's request for the Garden was not a wish detached from the life of faith. The God-fearing are those who turn their fear of God into obedience and their hope of the Garden into patience. The destination belongs to a path, even though the final gift remains God's mercy.

**The Garden Comes Near Before the Servant Enters**

The wording emphasizes approach. The righteous are allowed to see the Garden's nearness before the full experience of dwelling there. Anticipation is part of the reward. A promise that was trusted without sight becomes a reality placed before the servant, and the distance between hope and fulfilment collapses.

This movement also honors patient faith. The God-fearing lived without possessing the unseen reward in the world. They organized their lives around a promise that could not be handled or displayed. When Paradise is brought near, faith is not shown to have been naïve. It is shown to have been a truthful response to God's word.

**A Parallel Describes the Same Nearness**

Quran 50:31 describes Paradise as brought near to the righteous and not far away. The parallel confirms that nearness here expresses readiness and welcome rather than a human journey that forces open the Garden. The righteous find the promised home close because God has prepared the way to it.

That parallel also keeps the verse from being reduced to a metaphor for a good feeling. Paradise is a real reward in the Qur'anic account. Its nearness is part of the resurrection scene, where God's promises become visible. The God-fearing are not merely told that they were morally successful; they are brought before the home of lasting bliss.

**Taqwa Turns Fear Into Movement**

Fear of God can be misunderstood as a static emotion. This verse shows its destination. Taqwa moves a person away from false worship and harmful conduct and toward the Garden. It is fear that guards because it values closeness to God more than the pleasure of disobedience. Hope then gives the guarded life a positive direction.

A servant who fears God properly does not spend the whole life imagining rejection. He fears losing the Lord's acceptance and hopes for the mercy that brings Paradise near. The two feelings work together. Taqwa prevents false security, while hope prevents paralysis and despair.

**The Verse Makes the Future Present Enough for Obedience**

The God-fearing cannot see Paradise during worldly life, yet this verse lets the future enter present decisions. A person can refuse an idol, restrain an injustice, or forgive an offense because the coming welcome is more real than the immediate reward of wrongdoing. Nearness in the resurrection begins as a direction in the heart now.

The scene therefore honors those whose obedience looked costly or unnoticed. Their true audience was God, and their true destination was not the praise of people. When Paradise is brought near, the hidden value of their choices becomes clear. The Lord has not forgotten the hearts that guarded themselves for His sake. Their restraint was not wasted, and their hope was not directed toward an empty promise.

## Sūrah an-Nūr 24:4

> Those who accuse chaste women ˹of adultery˺ and fail to produce four witnesses, give them eighty lashes ˹each˺. And do not ever accept any testimony from them—for they are indeed the rebellious—

**Expanded Commentary**

**The Sanctity of Honor and the Crime of Qadhf**

The revelation erects an unyielding fortress around personal dignity through the legislation concerning unproven accusation: *wa-alladhīna yarmūna al-muḥṣanāti thumma lam yaʾtū bi-arbaʿati shuhadāʾ*. The Arabic verb *yarmūna* literally denotes hurling stones or shooting arrows at a target. By selecting this visceral metaphor, the Qur'an portrays defamation not as mere careless speech, but as a ballistic assault designed to shatter an innocent person's social standing. The tongue becomes a weapon capable of inflicting permanent psychic and social devastation upon its victim.

The term *al-muḥṣanāt* derives from the root *ḥ-ṣ-n*, signifying a fortified castle. A chaste individual resides within a psychological and social fortress of recognized integrity. To accuse such a person of illicit intimacy without undeniable proof is an act of spiritual demolition. Because public honor once ruined cannot easily be restored, the divine law treats false accusation (*qadhf*) as a major crime that threatens the moral equilibrium of the entire commonwealth, dismantling mutual trust and creating an atmosphere of chronic suspicion. An accusation voiced without proof strikes at the very foundation of communal security.

**The Demand for Four Eyewitnesses**

The statute imposes an extraordinary evidentiary condition: the accuser must produce four direct eyewitnesses to the act. In virtually every other legal dispute governed by Islamic jurisprudence—including homicide, theft, debt, and commercial transactions—the testimony of two upright witnesses suffices to establish guilt. Raising the threshold to four witnesses specifically for allegations of sexual impropriety represents a deliberate judicial barrier erected by divine wisdom.

Classical jurists stipulated that all four witnesses must observe the physical act of penetration simultaneously, at the exact same location, with absolute sensory certainty. If three witnesses provide graphic testimony while a fourth wavers or fails to confirm the precise physical act, the entire allegation collapses, and the three testifying witnesses are immediately sentenced to the full penalty for slander. This stringent standard ensures that private bedroom misconduct remains hidden, preventing malicious gossip from weaponizing the courts against innocent citizens. The law deliberately makes proving illicit conduct in a courtroom nearly impossible.

**The Triple Sanction: Physical, Civil, and Spiritual**

The law imposes a comprehensive threefold penalty upon the unsubstantiated accuser: eighty lashes, permanent disenfranchisement from legal testimony, and public branding as moral rebels. The first punishment strikes the body: *fajlidūhum thamānīna jaldah*. Administering eighty lashes inflicts severe corporal humiliation upon those who attempted to inflict social shame upon an innocent soul. The physical pain mirrors the social laceration they sought to cause.

The second sanction dismantles their civic authority: *wa-lā taqbalū lahum shahādatan abadā*. Stripping an individual of testamentary capacity in a society reliant upon personal reputation for commerce and justice is a profound civil demotion. The third consequence defines their spiritual status: *wa-ulāʾika humu al-fāsiqūn*. The label *fāsiq* denotes one who has ruptured the bounds of moral decency. Together, these measures neutralize the defamer physically, socially, and spiritually, rendering them incapable of poisoning the community further. The punishment matches the multidimensional damage wrought by defamation.

**Extending Protection Beyond Gendered Language**

Although the grammatical phrasing of the verse specifically designates chaste women (*al-muḥṣanāt*), classical jurists reached an absolute consensus that the law applies identically to the accusation of chaste men. The Qur'an employed the feminine plural because female vulnerability in traditional patriarchal societies made women the primary targets of reputational violence. Accusing a woman could destroy her marriage, delegitimize her children, and sever her family from communal alliances.

Nonetheless, the underlying legal cause (*ʿillah*) is the preservation of human honor (*ʿirḍ*), which belongs equally to every free believer. Any individual who accuses an innocent man of sexual misconduct without producing four verified witnesses is subjected to the exact same penalty of eighty lashes, the rejection of legal testimony, and judicial condemnation as a rebellious wrongdoer. Human dignity across both genders remains equally shielded under the divine canopy of protection, guaranteeing that no believer is left exposed to slander.

**The Disqualification of Testimony as Civil Death**

The command never to accept testimony from the slanderer warrants careful sociological analysis. In the early Islamic community, written registries and institutional documentation were scarce; commercial exchanges, marriage contracts, property transfers, and criminal trials depended almost entirely on oral testimony (*shahādah*). A person qualified to testify held significant social capital and communal trust within the public assembly.

To have one's testimony declared permanently inadmissible was akin to suffering civil death. The community was legally commanded to close its ears to the slanderer's voice in all public affairs. This enduring disqualification sent an unmistakable message across society: an individual willing to invent or carelessly circulate scandalous lies regarding a neighbor's private conduct cannot be entrusted with the truth in any matter of public interest, property, or justice. Their moral credibility is irrevocably shattered, and society is protected from their perjury.

**Preserving Privacy Against the Culture of Suspicion**

The deeper objective of this statutory barrier is the systematic discouragement of moral surveillance. Human beings frequently harbor suspicions, misinterpret ambiguous sights, or stumble upon the private shortcomings of others. This legislation establishes that discovering another person's moral failure does not grant license to initiate a public scandal or mobilize legal sanctions.

Unless an individual possesses four independent eyewitnesses willing to testify to an overt public outrage, divine law demands concealment (*satr*) and private ethical counsel. Circulating unproven suspicions, even if the accuser privately believes them to be true, incurs the full wrath of the law. By making the cost of unsubstantiated speech catastrophic for the speaker, the Qur'an protects the domestic sanctuary, forcing society to prioritize peace and personal privacy over intrusive moral vigilantism. The dignity of the individual takes precedence over communal voyeurism.

## Sūrah al-Naḥl 16:55

> **only returning Our favours with ingratitude. So enjoy yourselves, for you will soon know.**

**Expanded Commentary**

**The Human Pattern Of Forgetting After Relief And Its Warning**

Thumma idhā kashafa al-ḍurra ʿankum idhā farīqun minkum bi-rabbihim yushrikūn — then when He removes hardship from you, behold, a group of you associates others with their Lord. This verse describes a pattern that appears throughout Qur'an and human experience: in hardship you turn to Allah alone, in ease you forget. Al-Ṭabarī explains that when Allah removes hardship that made them cry to Him alone, a group returns to associating idols, forgetting that Allah alone removed hardship. The pattern is described in 10:12 where after harm removed he passes on as if he never called Us, and in 17:67 where after being saved at sea they turn away on land. The verse uses idhā twice to show suddenness: when He removes, behold, a group associates. The suddenness indicates ingratitude and heedlessness. The sūrah's theme of gratitude vs ingratitude appears here: to associate after being saved is to return favour with ingratitude, as next verse says only returning Our favours with ingratitude. The verse exposes human inconsistency: in hardship tawḥīd, in ease shirk, and calls to consistency in all states. The lesson is that true faith remains after relief, not only during distress.

**Why A Group Is Specified And Not All**

Farīqun minkum, a group of you, indicates not all who were in hardship associate after, but some. Al-Qurṭubī says this is to be just: not all who cry to Allah in hardship return to shirk after; some remain grateful and mindful. The verse says farīqun to indicate association after ease is not inevitable but done by some who are blameworthy. Al-Rāzī notes that farīqun minkum also indicates group is from among those who experienced hardship and cried to Allah, so their association is more blameworthy because they just experienced mercy in removal, yet attribute to others. The phrase also shows that even among those who experienced hardship and relief, some remain grateful, some become ungrateful, so outcome depends on heart, not only experience. This distinction is important for justice: not all are condemned, only group who associates. It also gives hope: you can be among those who remain grateful after relief, not among group who associates.

**The Meaning Of Returning Favours With Ingratitude And Its Consequence**

Li-yakfurū bi-mā ātaynāhum — to be ungrateful for what We gave them. Yakfurū from k-f-r means to cover, be ungrateful, disbelieve, from same root as kāfir, one who covers. Bi-mā ātaynāhum, for what We gave them, from a-t-y, to give. Al-Ṭabarī explains that li-yakfurū indicates consequence: they associate after hardship removed so that they are ungrateful for what We gave, they cover blessings, deny Giver. Favours include all blessings listed earlier in sūrah — cattle, rain, crops, sea, mountains — and removal of hardship. To return favours with ingratitude is worst ingratitude: Giver gives, receiver denies Giver and gives thanks to other. Al-Rāzī notes li- here can be for consequence, meaning association results in ingratitude. The verse therefore diagnoses association after relief as ingratitude, not only theological error but moral failure that contradicts purpose of blessings which is to be recognized and thanked.

**Enjoyment As Temporary Test Followed By Soon Knowing The Reality**

Fa-tamattaʿū fa-sawfa taʿlamūn — so enjoy yourselves, you will soon know. Tamattaʿū from m-t-ʿ means to enjoy temporary pleasure, to have enjoyment for a time. Fa-sawfa taʿlamūn, you will soon know consequence, from ʿ-l-m, to know, with sawfa indicating near future, soon. Al-Ṭabarī says fa-tamattaʿū is threat, not permission: enjoy now in world with ingratitude, but soon you will know consequence in Hereafter, when disgrace and misery are upon disbelievers as verse 27 said, when evil consequences overtake as verse 34 said. Enjoyment is temporary, as 16:117 says brief enjoyment then painful punishment. Phrase fa-sawfa taʿlamūn appears many times as warning that knowledge of consequence will come soon, when too late to change. Al-Zamakhsharī notes tamattaʿū is imperative but means threat: enjoy if you can, but you will soon know that enjoyment was short and punishment near.

**How This Pattern Fits Sūrat al-Niʿam And Its Call To Gratitude**

Sūrat al-Niʿam is about blessings and gratitude, called Sūrat al-Niʿam by some early scholars because it enumerates blessings systematically. This pattern of hardship, supplication, relief, association shows opposite of gratitude: blessings from Allah, yet share given to idols, hardship removed by Allah, yet association returns. The sūrah lists blessings as signs for those who reflect, understand, are mindful, but some use blessings to assign share to idols, showing they did not reflect. The verse about returning favours with ingratitude and enjoying soon knowing warns that misuse of blessings has consequence and will be questioned, as verse 56 says you will be questioned about lies fabricated. The reader is invited to be among those who attribute blessings to Allah alone and are grateful, not among those who assign share to idols and fabricate lies. The sūrah weaves warning and promise to cultivate mindful, humble, grateful heart.

**Practical Lessons On Consistency And Sincere Worship In All States**

The verse teaches consistency: worship Allah alone in hardship and ease, in blessings and hardship removal, not only in hardship. Al-Rāzī says true tawḥīd is to worship Allah alone in all states, not only when in need. Group who associates after relief shows their tawḥīd in hardship was need-based supplication that disappears when need disappears. True tawḥīd remains after need removed, with gratitude. Believer should strive to be among those who do not associate after relief, who remain grateful, who say goodness when asked what Lord revealed, who are greeted with peace at death. The lesson is to make conscious what is known in hardship: Allah is source of blessings and relief, so gratitude and supplication should be to Him alone always, not only in hardship. The wise person knows now before soon knowing when too late, and acts on that knowledge by being grateful and worshipping Allah alone in all states.

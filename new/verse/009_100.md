## Sūrah al-Tawbah 9:100

> As for the foremost—the first of the Emigrants and the Helpers—and those who follow them in goodness, Allah is pleased with them and they are pleased with Him. And He has prepared for them Gardens under which rivers flow, to stay there for ever and ever. That is the ultimate triumph.

**Expanded Commentary**

**The Foremost Paid the First Cost**

The verse praises the foremost among the Emigrants and Helpers. Their priority is not merely a date on a calendar. They believed, migrated, sheltered, spent, and stood with the Messenger when the outcome brought no clear worldly advantage. They acted before success made obedience attractive to everyone else.

The Emigrants left homes and familiar security for faith. The Helpers received them and carried the cost of building a new community. Their virtue is therefore tied to sacrifice and loyalty, not to a romantic title. The believer honors their example by asking what obedience costs when support is uncertain. The first step often requires more trust than the steps taken after a path has become established.

**Following Them Requires Goodness**

The verse includes those who follow the foremost with goodness. It does not ask later generations to copy every outward circumstance. Later believers may not migrate from the same city or face the same danger. They follow through sincere faith, support for truth, generosity, courage, and the refusal to exploit the community they inherited.

The word goodness also sets a condition. Claiming descent, affiliation, or admiration is not enough. A person follows the foremost when his conduct carries their moral qualities. He can learn from their sacrifices without turning them into a reason to despise people in the present. Their example becomes fruitful when it creates more obedient servants rather than a competition over inherited honor.

**Allah's Pleasure Is Reciprocal**

The verse describes a beautiful reciprocity: Allah is pleased with them, and they are pleased with Him. Divine pleasure is the highest acceptance, while the servants' pleasure expresses their contentment with Allah's judgment and gift. They do not merely enter a place of reward; they reach a relationship in which the Lord and servant meet in approval.

This reciprocity purifies the meaning of sacrifice. The foremost did not give only to gain visible success. They trusted Allah when the cost was immediate and the result uncertain. Their final pleasure shows that no sacrifice made for Allah was truly lost. A believer hopes for the same state by accepting Allah's wisdom before the reward becomes visible.

**Gardens Confirm the Value of Early Faith**

The prepared Gardens show that the first sacrifices are remembered. People who act early can feel forgotten when later generations enjoy the benefits of their work. Allah's promise corrects that feeling. The comfort enjoyed by later believers does not erase the cost paid by those who opened the path.

This also teaches later believers gratitude. They should not treat inherited guidance, institutions, or communal safety as though they appeared without labor. They receive a trust built by others and must preserve it with goodness. The Garden is promised to those who follow faithfully, not to those who consume an inheritance while weakening its foundations.

**The Emigrants and Helpers Model Shared Community**

The two groups represent different forms of service. The Emigrants surrendered possessions and homeland. The Helpers opened homes, resources, and social belonging. Neither contribution made the other unnecessary. A community requires people who leave a harmful situation and people who receive them with generosity.

The model applies wherever believers rebuild after displacement, poverty, or conflict. Those who arrive should not exploit hospitality; those who receive them should not use hospitality as a tool of control. Shared faith creates rights and responsibilities on both sides. The foremost succeeded because sacrifice was joined to mutual care.

**Ultimate Triumph Is Final, Not Temporary**

The verse calls the prepared Gardens the ultimate triumph. This echoes the chapter's repeated correction of worldly calculations. The foremost may have lost property, status, and familiar comfort, yet their final result is victory. The Helpers may have shared resources and carried burdens, yet their final result is victory. What looked like loss in the moment became the path to lasting success.

The believer measures his own choices by this horizon. He does not neglect present duties, but he refuses to call comfort the highest good. He seeks Allah's pleasure, follows the righteous with goodness, and trusts that the Garden is more lasting than every temporary advantage. The ultimate triumph belongs to the person whose early faith remains faithful until the end.

The verse also teaches later believers how to honor pioneers without freezing faith in the past. They follow with goodness by preserving the truth, welcoming those who need support, and spending when obedience asks for sacrifice. They do not claim the pioneers' rank merely by praising them. They show respect through conduct that carries the same qualities into new circumstances. Allah's pleasure is the center of the verse, so the follower seeks that pleasure rather than a title of belonging. The prepared Garden is the final confirmation that early sacrifice, shared care, and faithful continuation are never wasted before Allah.

The relationship described in the verse also teaches contentment with Allah's judgment. The foremost were pleased with Him even after the losses they endured, because they knew that obedience had not been wasted. Later believers may face a different form of sacrifice, but the same contentment is possible. It does not mean liking every hardship. It means trusting Allah's wisdom while doing what is right. The Gardens are the final home of that trust. A servant follows the righteous with goodness, welcomes those who need support, and keeps seeking Allah's pleasure until temporary trials give way to the triumph that never ends.

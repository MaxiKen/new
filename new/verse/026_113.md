## Sūrah al-Shuʿarāʾ 26:113

> Their judgment is with my Lord, if you had any sense!

**Expanded Commentary**

**Ultimate Judgment Belongs to Noah's Lord**

Noah assigns the judgment of his followers to his Lord. He does not deny that actions matter or that a community may correct visible wrongdoing. He denies that the chiefs can demand a final verdict based on social contempt and that Noah should pretend to possess God's complete account. Ultimate reckoning belongs to the Lord.

The sentence restores the proper authority. The chiefs had tried to make their social ranking a judgment about who deserved to stand near revelation. Noah places the believers before God instead. Their value, intentions, past, and future are not decided by the people who insult them. God knows what is hidden and measures each servant with perfect justice.

**Noah Does Not Make Himself the Judge of Hearts**

A messenger can recognize belief through a person's confession and conduct, but he does not become the owner of the unseen. Noah's reply shows humility about the boundary between public responsibility and divine judgment. He can welcome those who believe and teach them. He cannot replace God as the one who weighs every secret and final outcome.

This boundary protects both the leader and the believer. The leader is prevented from claiming infallible knowledge of motives. The believer is prevented from treating a public affiliation as a guarantee that no correction is ever needed. Everyone remains answerable to the Lord, and no human figure can close that account prematurely.

**The Chiefs' Demand Was a Demand for Social Judgment**

The opponents do not present evidence that Noah's followers have committed a particular wrong. They point to their low status and ask Noah to treat that status as a reason for exclusion. Their judgment is social before it is moral. Noah's answer exposes the weakness of their demand: their class preference cannot become God's verdict.

Social judgment often disguises itself as concern for purity or order. A group says that humble people are unworthy, uneducated, or suspicious, then uses those labels to protect access and prestige. Noah refuses to let such labels determine the standing of believers. The Lord's judgment is based on truth, not on a hierarchy invented by chiefs.

**The Phrase Calls the Hearers Back to Reason**

Noah ends with a challenge: if you had any sense. The challenge is not an invitation to mock intelligence. It points to the absurdity of claiming knowledge that the speakers do not possess. Sensible people distinguish what they know from what they assume. They do not demand that a messenger condemn people simply because those people lack their status.

The phrase also shows that reason and faith are not enemies. Noah's appeal to God's judgment is accompanied by a call to intellectual honesty. A sound mind recognizes the limits of human knowledge and refuses to make prejudice into proof. The chiefs' failure is moral and rational at once.

**Divine Judgment Includes the Hidden and the Public**

Human courts can assess public actions according to evidence. God's judgment includes the inner intention behind those actions and the full history of a servant's response to guidance. That wider knowledge makes the Lord's judgment more complete than any social verdict. It also makes human humility necessary.

A believer should not use this truth to excuse public harm. People still need justice, testimony, and protection. The point is that visible accountability and ultimate judgment are not identical. Noah can address what is known while leaving what is hidden and final to God.

Quran 6:52 presents the Prophet being told not to dismiss poor believers and makes clear that their account is not placed on the messenger in the way it is placed on God. This parallel confirms Noah's principle. Prophets protect the believing community from elite contempt, while God remains the final judge of every person's deeds.

The same principle appears wherever a community is tempted to trade poor believers for influential attention. No messenger is authorized to make social prestige a condition of God's mercy. The Lord may raise people whom society overlooks and expose the arrogance of people who assume that status gives them the right to judge.

**Reckoning Creates Equality Before God**

When judgment belongs to the Lord, the rich and poor stand under the same authority. Wealth may change a person's worldly options, but it does not change the standard by which God knows him. The chiefs' hierarchy dissolves at the point where every servant is seen truly.

This equality does not erase different responsibilities. A ruler may answer for power, a teacher for knowledge, and a poor person for his own trusts. Equality means that none can purchase a different moral reality through rank. Noah's followers can be judged by God without being humiliated by the chiefs first.

**Noah's Response Is Both Defense and Humility**

He defends the believers by refusing to condemn them on prejudice, yet he also refuses to declare their final account his own. The balance is important. A leader can protect a community from injustice without pretending that every member is beyond correction. He can honor faith while directing all hearts toward God's judgment.

The reply therefore turns the chiefs' question back toward their own sense. Do they have any sense if they ask a messenger to replace God as judge? Wisdom would make them listen, examine evidence, and fear the Lord. Their demand instead reveals that they want Noah to serve their pride.

**The Lord's Judgment Is a Source of Hope**

For the humble believer, divine judgment is not only a threat. It is protection from the injustice of worldly rankings. A person despised by society can trust that God sees sincerity and struggle. A person praised by society can remain humble because God sees what praise conceals.

Noah's sentence gives the oppressed a secure horizon. Their standing is not finally decided by chiefs, wealth, or public contempt. It is with the Lord. That truth allows the messenger to keep the door of faith open to everyone and allows believers to serve God without begging social elites for permission.

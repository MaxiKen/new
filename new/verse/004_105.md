## Sūrah al-Nisāʾ 4:105

> Indeed, We have sent down the Book to you ˹O Prophet˺ in truth to judge between people by means of what Allah has shown you. So do not be an advocate for the deceitful.

### Expanded Commentary

**The Book judges disputes**

Allah sent the Book in truth so the Prophet could judge between people by what God showed him. Revelation is not merely a private source of comfort; it establishes public standards of evidence and justice. The Prophet’s insight is guided, while later judges must remain humble because they do not possess his protected access to revelation.

**Do not become an advocate for deceit**

The immediate setting concerns a theft and a group attempting to shift blame onto an innocent person. The warning is general: a lawyer, elder, journalist, scholar, or friend must not use skill to make falsehood appear righteous. Advocacy is not morally neutral when its object is known deception.

The verse distinguishes defending a person’s right to due process from defending the lie itself. Every accused person deserves a fair hearing, but no advocate should manufacture evidence or conceal the truth. Justice requires loyalty to what Allah has shown, not to the client who pays or the relative who pressures.

**Truth before loyalty**

The revealed judge is not an advocate for the most persuasive faction. When a friend or institution is caught in wrongdoing, loyalty may require telling the truth rather than protecting the relationship’s appearance. The Book’s purpose is to restore justice, not to provide religious arguments for whichever side is already loved.

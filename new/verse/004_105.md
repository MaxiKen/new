## Sūrah al-Nisāʾ 4:105

> Indeed, We have sent down the Book to you ˹O Prophet˺ in truth to judge between people by means of what Allah has shown you. So do not be an advocate for the deceitful.

**Expanded Commentary**

**We Have Sent Down the Book in Truth to Judge Between People**

The verse opens *innā anzalnā ilayka al-kitāba bi al-ḥaqqi li taḥkuma bayna al-nāsi bi mā arāka Allāhu* — Indeed We have sent down the Book to you in truth, with truth, to judge between people by what God has shown you, made you see. *Anzalnā* — We sent down — was read as divine descent, the Book given as the noble command from the All-Hearing, All-Seeing (4:58) and as the referral point where disputes are taken (4:59). *Bi al-ḥaqqi* — in truth, with truth, as truth — was explained as that the Book’s content is true and its descent is true and its judgment is true, against the false judges — *ṭāghūt* — sought by those who claim belief (4:60). *Li taḥkuma bayna al-nāsi* — to judge between people — was read as judging among humanity without faction — the same *bayna al-nāsi* as *“when you judge between people judge with fairness”* (4:58). *Bi mā arāka Allāhu* — by what God has shown you — was explained as by what He taught, inspired, made clear, not by outward claim alone; the sūrah has shown that God teaches what you did not know (4:113) and that He is the One who guides to noble ways (4:26). Early reports attached the verse to a theft dispute where a Muslim of the Banū Ẓafar was suspected, his armour hidden with a Jew, and pressure came to shield the Muslim — the verse forbade it, commanding judgment by what God shows, which settled on evidence, not tribal tie.

**So Do Not Be an Advocate for the Deceitful**

The prohibition *wa lā takun li al-khāʾinīna khaṣīmā* — so do not be an advocate, disputant, defender for the deceitful, the treacherous — was explained as *khāʾin* — one who betrays trust — the same *khāʾin* as those who betray trusts the sūrah commands to return (4:58) and as those who betray themselves (4:107). *Khaṣīm* — disputant, advocate, pleader — was read as standing in argument for them, contesting on their side; the sūrah will repeat *“do not advocate for those who wrong themselves”* (4:107) and *“you advocate for them in this life, who will advocate for them before God on Resurrection Day?”* (4:109). The command was addressed to the Prophet ﷺ directly, showing that even his advocacy is not for deceit, and through him to every judge and every believer: to be sure before fighting (4:94) is matched here by being sure before judging. The verse generalises emigration’s *murāgham* and spaciousness (4:100): just as earth is wide for the wronged to find haven, the law is wide for the truth to find voice, and it is narrowed when an advocate lends his tongue to deceit.

**A Verse That Makes Revelation the Criterion of Judgment**

The opening *bi al-ḥaqqi* and the condition *bi mā arāka Allāhu* were read together as the sūrah’s theory of judgment: the Book in truth, applied by what God shows. That showing was explained as knowledge given — scripture, sunna, evidence, insight — not as personal desire; the sūrah has warned against following desires that wish you deviate greatly (4:27) and against seeking judgment of false judges (4:60). To judge by what God shows was linked to the Prophet’s reported clarification: *“I am but a human, and you bring disputes to me, and some may be more eloquent, so if I give one his brother’s right by his eloquence, it is a piece of fire”* (Bukhārī 2680; Muslim 1713). The verse therefore makes procedure — evidence, oaths, witnesses as in orphan property (4:6), and referral to God and Messenger (4:59) — the means by which *arāka* is actualised. The sūrah’s promise that if a dispute is referred to God and Messenger it is best and fairest (4:59) is here instantiated as Book judging between people, not clan judging between its own.

**Deceit Is Not a Minor Fault — It Is the Partnership the Sūrah Has Warned Against**

The term *khāʾinīn* — deceitful, treacherous — was read as weighty: those who hide deception from people but cannot hide from God in whose presence they plot by night what He displeases (4:108). The sūrah has shown deceit’s kin: lagging behind with two faces (4:72–73), double safety while plunging into *fitnah* (4:91), denying peace for fleeting gain (4:94), and spending to show off without belief (4:38). To be an advocate for the deceitful was explained as joining that partnership, taking deceit as associate — and whoever takes Satan as associate, what an evil associate (4:38). The sūrah will say God does not like the deceitful, sinful (4:107) and that there is no good in most secret talks except charity, kindness, reconciliation (4:114); advocacy for deceit falls outside that good. The Prophet ﷺ was therefore told not to be *khaṣīm* for them, just as earlier he was told to turn away from hypocrites who turn away stubbornly (4:63) and to put trust in God as Trustee (4:81).

**A Judging Verse Placed After Prayer and Pursuit**

Placed after pursuit without faltering where both sides suffer yet only the believer hopes (4:104), this verse assigns the judge his hope’s discipline: the same hope that steadies pursuit — hoping from God what the enemy does not — steadies judgment — judging by what God shows rather than by clan. The sūrah that began from a single soul (4:1), that promised many havens to the emigrant (4:100), that shortened and guarded prayer (4:101–102) and fixed it at appointed writ (4:103), now places the Book as the haven for disputes, where a people bound by treaty are paid blood-money even if mistakenly killed (4:92, 4:90) because the *mīthāq* is part of what God shows. For the weak for whom burdens were lightened (4:28), the verse is a shield: where power tempts to be advocate for the deceitful, the Book in truth is the advocate for the deceitful’s victim, showing the judge what to see. The next verses will unfold that prohibition: seek forgiveness (4:106) and do not advocate for those who wrong themselves (4:107), for they hide from people what they cannot hide from God (4:108).

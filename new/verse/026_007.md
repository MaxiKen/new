## Sūrah al-Shuʿarāʾ 26:7

> Have they failed to look at the earth, ˹to see˺ how many types of fine plants We have caused to grow in it?

**Expanded Commentary**

**The Earth Offers a Sign Within Reach**

After warning about denied revelation, the verse directs attention to the ground beneath the audience. They do not need to travel to a distant age or wait for an extraordinary event. The earth is already displaying generation, variety, color, nourishment, and renewal. The question is not whether plants exist, but whether people have looked in the manner that turns sight into understanding. Observation becomes reflection when it asks who gives the earth its ordered abundance and why such abundance should be treated as morally empty.

The verb of looking can include deliberate consideration, not a passing glance. A person may see a field every day and never allow it to challenge his assumptions. The verse interrupts that familiarity. It asks the audience to notice quantity and kind together: many forms grow from one earth, yet their variety is not random confusion. The sign is available to ordinary experience, which makes neglect of it harder to excuse.

**Fine Means More Than Attractive**

The word karīm, rendered fine, can carry senses of nobility, goodness, generosity, and excellence. Applied to plants, it does not say that every plant has the same appearance or that usefulness can be judged by human taste alone. It points to the generosity built into growth. A plant may feed people, shelter animals, hold soil, provide shade, or serve a purpose that human beings have not yet recognized. Beauty and benefit are both possible dimensions of the word.

This breadth corrects a narrow view of creation. If people value only what can be sold quickly, they will overlook much of the earth's work. The sign lies partly in the abundance that exceeds immediate profit. The Qur'an's language gives the living world a dignity that resists careless treatment. The earth is not merely a storehouse waiting for extraction; it is a field of signs whose patterns teach dependence, measure, and gratitude.

**Zawj Joins Variety With Pairing**

The Arabic word rendered types can also mean pairs. In its immediate setting it naturally evokes the many kinds of vegetation that arise from the earth, but the second sense adds a structural dimension. Growth appears through relation: male and female, seed and soil, blossom and fruit, or complementary forms within a created order. The verse does not require every instance to be forced into one biological scheme. It draws attention to the paired and varied character of life.

That wording also connects the natural sign with the human story. Humanity itself is created from the earth and spread through paired existence. The earth is therefore not remote from the moral being who is asked to observe it. The same Creator who brings plants through hidden stages brings people through stages of formation and return. A person who studies growth outside himself is being invited to recognize dependence within himself as well.

**Growth Makes Power Visible Without Spectacle**

A seed's emergence is not a loud miracle, but it is a continuous sign. The process joins water, soil, light, season, and living structure in a result no human being can produce from nothing. Human skill can select, cultivate, graft, and protect. It cannot command a dead thing to become a living plant by its own independent power. The verse uses this familiar process to weaken the claim that only the extraordinary can prove divine action.

Other Qur'anic passages place vegetation beside rain and the reviving of the earth, including 50:7 and 20:53. The repeated comparison is not a lesson in agriculture alone. It establishes a pattern: the One who brings life from apparently inert ground has the power to restore people after death, and the visible cycle trains the mind to accept that larger claim. The earth's sign is therefore both present and forward-looking.

**Looking Requires Humility About Usefulness**

People often call something useless because its purpose is not yet known or because it does not serve a narrow human need. The verse's description of every fine kind resists that haste. A wise observer leaves room for benefits hidden from current knowledge and for relationships in creation that cannot be reduced to a single product. This is not a command to romanticize every plant or ignore harm. It is a command to judge creation with more care than convenience allows.

Such humility also changes the act of study. Scientific investigation can become a form of gratitude when it reveals order without pretending to own the source of order. Farming can become stewardship rather than domination. Even a quiet walk can become worship when sight moves from possession to recognition. The verse does not demand an exotic experience. It asks the audience to look again at what it has been taught to overlook.

**The Sign Is Clear Even Before Every Detail Is Known**

A person need not catalogue every species before understanding the verse's central claim. The sign is in the combination of plurality, nourishment, beauty, and renewal. Knowledge can deepen appreciation, but incomplete knowledge does not erase the evidence already present. The earth calls for an answer at the level of attention and gratitude before it calls for a complete account of every process.

That is why the question comes after the warning about ridicule. The audience has been asking for a sign while passing over the signs that surround them. The earth is not offered as a substitute for revelation; it confirms the same truth through another register. Those who look honestly will find the world pressing the question back upon them: what kind of Creator gives such varied life, and what kind of response does that generosity deserve?

## Sūrah al-Baqarah 2:119

> We have surely sent you with the truth ˹O Prophet˺ as a deliverer of good news and a warner. And you will not be accountable for the residents of the Hellfire.

**Expanded Commentary**

**Sent With the Truth**

The verse answers the demanders of verse 118 not with more signs but with the mission's definition, and the definition opens with its warrant: *We have surely sent you with the truth*. The preposition carries both senses the tradition holds together — sent *in* truth, the mission's substance true, and sent *with* truth, the messenger furnished with it — and either sense answers the demand at its root. The demanders asked for direct speech and spectacular signs as conditions of belief; the verse declares that the condition has been met in a form they did not specify: a Messenger carrying truth. The emphasis particle — *innā*, We have surely — puts the sending beyond question: not a claimant's self-presentation but a divine act stated by its actor. The truth he is sent with is the recital's whole content — the signs made clear, the Book confirming what preceded, the guidance the demanders' hearts lack the certainty to receive — and the verse's placement makes the point structural: between the demand (118) and the disposition of the opponents (120, whose content the recital will file in its own place), the mission stands defined, complete, and independent of its reception. The truth's arrival does not await the audience's terms; the audience's judgment awaits the truth's arrival, and it has arrived.

**The Two Titles**

The mission's function is named in the paired titles the Qur'an gives its messengers: *a deliverer of good news and a warner* — bashīr and nadhīr, the two active participles that run as a fixed pair through the Book (6:48, 17:105, 34:28, and the prophetic catalogs beyond). The pairing is the revelation's two-handed delivery: one hand carries the promise — the gardens, the rivers, the no-fear-no-grief formula this sūrah has been filing — and the other carries the threat, the Fire and its residents and the sentences pronounced on the traders and the distorters. Sūrah al-Nisāʾ states the pairing's purpose in its completion-clause: messengers came *delivering good news and warnings so humanity should have no excuse before Allah after the coming of the messengers* (4:165) — the excuse-removal that makes the mission's delivery total. Neither title alone accomplishes it: good news without warning invites presumption, the numbered-days comfort of verse 80; warning without good news invites despair, which the sūrah elsewhere forbids in the widest terms. Together they leave the hearer without shelter in either direction — the promise real, the threat real, the choice genuinely open, the excuse genuinely gone. The pair also defines the messenger's office against the demanders' expectations: they wanted a spectacle-performer and a voice from the cloud; the mission is a herald's office, and a herald's authority is his message, not his fireworks. The recital has watched both hands at work across a hundred verses, and the verse names the worker.

**Not Accountable for the Residents**

The verse's second sentence draws the mission's boundary, and the boundary is its mercy to the messenger: *you will not be accountable for the residents of the Hellfire*. The exegetes note the clause's grammatical readings — you will not be questioned about them; you are not answerable for them; and, less preferred, an imperative do not ask about them — and the preferred readings converge on one ruling: the messenger's account covers delivery, not outcome. The absolution belongs to a family the Qur'an repeats at every station where the Prophet's ﷺ grief at refusal surfaces: *you are not responsible for people's guidance — it is Allah Who guides whoever He wills* (2:272); the consolation of Sūrah Fāṭir — do not grieve yourself to death over them (35:8); Sūrah al-Ghāshiyah's closing definition of the office — *you are not there to compel them* (88:22). The family's consistency marks a doctrine: guidance is divine property, refusal is human property, and the messenger stands between them as carrier — honored with the trust, exempt from its verdicts. The clause's wording deserves attention: the Fire's people are named *residents* — aṣḥāb, the belonging-term verse 81 fixed — so the absolution concerns people whose verdict is already characterized, and the messenger is released from accountability for a sentence he did not author and cannot repeal. The recital had prepared the same lesson for its wider audience at verse 75, correcting the believers' expectations of their interlocutors; here the correction is addressed to the correction's own first holder — the one whose sincerity made every refusal cost him most.

**The Office Without the Burden**

Between the demanders behind and the opponents ahead, the verse installs the mission's self-understanding, and the self-understanding is the office's protection. A messenger who carried the outcome would be destroyed by it: every refusal would be his failure, every resident of the Fire his indictment, and the grief the consolation-verses address would be not an emotion to be soothed but an accurate accounting. The absolution-clause removes the false accounting at its root, and what remains is the office in its actual dimensions — delivery with the two titles, accountability for the message's faithfulness, and release from the hearts' verdicts. The tradition's portrait of the Prophet ﷺ under this verse is the portrait of a herald unburdened: warning delivered in full, promise delivered in full, the weeping over the refuse's fate kept inside the compassion that never stopped the delivery. For the community that inherits the office's functions — every carrier of this recitation, in every degree of the carrying — the verse legislates the same balance. The daʿwah's seriousness is maximal: the truth is sent, the excuse is removed, the two hands are full. The daʿwah's burden is minimal: the carrier is not answerable for the carried-to, the residents' residence is not charged against his ledger, and the grief that threatens the carrier's own standing is met by an absolution older than the community. Between presumption — believing the outcome is ours to produce — and despair — believing the refusal indicts the message — the verse stands as the office's constitution: sent with the truth, bearing news and warning, accountable for neither the Fire nor its residents.

**The Residents Named**

The clause's final phrase does one more piece of quiet work, and the work is verdict rather than consolation. The Fire's people are named as residents in a verse whose subject is the messenger's exemption — which places the verdict inside the absolution: the release of the messenger presupposes the sentence of the released-from. The recital has built the residency term carefully — earned evil, sin engrossing, the two eternities of verses 81–82 — and its use here confirms that the mission's failure-to-convert cases are not undecided matters awaiting further signs. The demanders of verse 118 asked for speech and spectacle as conditions; the verse answers that the conditions are met, the mission defined, and the demanders' trajectory already legible: hearts alike to the archive's hearts produce the archive's outcomes, and the outcomes have a name. The absolution is therefore not indifference — the recital nowhere suggests the messenger ceased to grieve or warn — but jurisdiction: the residence is filed where residence is decided, and the herald's file contains his delivery, complete. For the hearer, the two sentences together hold the verse's whole instruction: the truth has come with both its hands full, so the excuse is gone; and the verdicts belong to their Author, so the carrier's grief has a boundary. Between the two facts every generation's mission work stands — urgent as the sent truth, unburdened as the absolved messenger, and answered, at the end, by the only court the residents' name acknowledges.

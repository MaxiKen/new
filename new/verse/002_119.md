## Sūrah al-Baqarah 2:119

> We have surely sent you with the truth ˹O Prophet˺ as a deliverer of good news and a warner. And you will not be accountable for the residents of the Hellfire.

**Expanded Commentary**

**The Office Described**

The verse states the terms on which the Prophet ﷺ was sent, and it names two functions: announcing good news and giving warning. The two are tied together in the Book's descriptions of the messengers, and their pairing prevents either from being treated as the whole of the office.

The sentence belongs immediately after the scene in which people demanded that God speak to them directly, and it answers that demand by describing what God actually sent. A messenger with a message is what the community received, and the verse states the function of the message rather than negotiating about the form of its delivery. The two functions also set the tone of the community's own invitation to others. The Book elsewhere commands its readers to call people to God with the same twofold purpose, offering the promise and naming the consequence. The messenger's office therefore becomes a model for the conduct of the community after him.

**Good News and Warning**

The Book uses these two terms as complementary epithets for the prophets in several passages, and their order is consistent: the announcement of good comes first in the phrase even though the warning often arrives first in experience. The pairing reflects the message's twofold effect, since the same revelation consoles those who accept it and confronts those who reject it.

The signs of God's presence in history do the same work, as the Book states elsewhere that God tests people by fear, hunger, and loss. The messenger's good news concerns what God has promised, and his warning concerns what the hearer stands to lose, and the sūrah has been supplying both throughout its account of the covenant community's history.

**Sent With the Truth**

The phrase rendered with the truth establishes the ground of the messenger's authority. What he brought corresponds to reality, and the Book uses the word for the correspondence between God's speech and the way things are. The verse therefore answers the demand for signs by stating that the content of the message is true rather than by promising a display.

The same expression appears in the Book's description of the revelation's descent, and it rules out the notion that the message might be a device for organizing a community. What the Prophet ﷺ delivered is the truth about God, the world, and the human being's place in it, and the two functions of announcing and warning follow from that content.

**Not Accountable for the Residents of the Fire**

The sentence closes by releasing the Prophet ﷺ from responsibility for those who reject the message, and the commentators noted that the Arabic can also be read as an instruction not to ask about them. The Book's other statements on the matter are consistent: the Prophet is not tasked with guiding people, and his duty is to convey.

The Book says the same in another passage of this sūrah when it states that it is God who guides whom He wills, and it tells the Prophet elsewhere not to consume himself in grief over those who refuse. The sentence here therefore comforts a messenger who was watching people reject what he had been sent to deliver, and it defines the boundary of his responsibility.

**The Balance of the Verse**

The three clauses form a complete account of the prophetic office: the truth is sent, the messenger announces and warns, and the outcome of acceptance or rejection lies with God. Nothing in the sentence promises success as the community would count it, and nothing suggests that the messenger's task depends on the response.

That clarity is the verse's main contribution to the passage. The previous sentence quoted a demand for direct divine speech, and the answer is a statement about what has actually been revealed and what the messenger's role involves, with the outcome left to God. The singleness of purpose implied by the sentence is one of its practical lessons: the messenger's task is to convey and to warn, and the outcome is not part of his assignment. The community that inherited the message is asked to hold the same position.

**What the Verse Establishes About Preaching**

The sentence sets a standard for anyone who carries the Book to others. The task is to deliver what has been given, with the announcement of good and the warning of loss, and to leave the results where they belong. The Book's accounts of earlier prophets show the same pattern, and its instruction to the community elsewhere commands the invitation to be offered with wisdom and kind advice rather than with anxiety about results.

The verse also removes an excuse that communities sometimes supply for their messengers: the claim that a prophet should have produced a particular display or spoken with a particular authority. The Book states what the messenger brought and what he is responsible for, and it leaves the audience answerable for how it receives him.

**The Connection to the Following Verses**

The next verse describes the attitudes the Prophet ﷺ would meet from the two communities with scriptures, and it warns him against following their preferences. The sequence is instructive: the office is defined first, then the opposition is described, and then the guidance of God is stated as the only standard. The reader receives the pattern before the pressure, which is the sūrah's usual way of preparing its audience. The order also matters for the reader's expectations. Having been told what the messenger is responsible for, the community is better placed to receive the warning in the following sentence about the pressure it will face and the standard against which it must judge itself. The passage's arrangement is instructional rather than merely sequential.

**What the Messenger Is Not Asked to Do**

The sentence removes a burden that anyone who carries a message for others will recognise: responsibility for the outcome. The Book states the same principle in several places, telling the Prophet ﷺ that he is not a guardian over people, that he is not tasked with their guidance, and that he should not wear himself out in grief over those who refuse. Later in this sūrah the point is stated in a single line about guidance belonging to God. The Book's purpose in repeating it is practical, since a messenger who measures his success by the response will adjust the message to obtain one, and the sūrah has already warned against that adjustment. What the verse leaves the community with is the task and its limits: the truth is delivered, the promise and the warning are stated, and the reckoning belongs to the One who sent the message. That division of labour is one of the ways the Book protects the integrity of the message itself.

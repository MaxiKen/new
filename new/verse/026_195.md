## Sūrah al-Shuʿarāʾ 26:195

> in a clear Arabic tongue.

**Expanded Commentary**

**Clarity Is Part of the Revelation's Mercy**

The Quran is delivered in a clear Arabic tongue. The description concerns more than national identity. A clear tongue makes the message available for hearing, reflection, questioning, and obedience. The first audience is not confronted with a code that cannot be understood in its own language. Allah sends guidance in a form that allows the people to recognize its claims and answer them.

Quran 14:4 states the general principle that messengers are sent in the language of their people so that the message can be made clear to them. Arabic in this verse therefore serves communication. It does not make one ethnicity closer to Allah by birth, nor does it turn the language into an object of worship apart from the revelation carried in it.

**A Clear Language Removes a Convenient Excuse**

The people of the Prophet could debate the message, but they could not fairly claim that its language was unintelligible to its first audience. They heard its warnings, arguments, stories, and commands in a tongue they understood. Clarity does not guarantee acceptance, because a person may understand a claim and still refuse its demand.

This difference is important. Confusion can be removed by explanation; stubbornness cannot be healed by grammar alone. The Quran gives the denier access to meaning, then leaves the moral response exposed. A clear tongue makes rejection more accountable because the hearer is not being asked to obey a message hidden behind an unknown language.

**Arabic Is a Vehicle, Not a Tribal Privilege**

The verse praises the Quran's clarity, not the pride of those who speak Arabic. A language becomes a vehicle for guidance when it carries meanings faithfully and opens them to understanding. The same message can later be translated and explained for other peoples, while the Arabic revelation remains the reference for its exact wording.

This prevents two opposite errors. One error treats Arabic as a reason to rank people by ancestry. The other treats the original language as irrelevant to the revealed form. The Quran is clear in Arabic, and the obligation of other communities is to seek its meaning through sound learning rather than to turn linguistic difference into a barrier.

**Clarity Does Not Coerce Belief**

A clear statement can be rejected for reasons that are not linguistic. Quran 41:44 presents the deniers objecting even in a hypothetical situation involving a non-Arabic Quran, showing that they could have changed the complaint while keeping the refusal. The issue is therefore not only whether words can be decoded. It is whether the heart will submit to what the words establish.

The clear Arabic tongue gives the warning a trustworthy form and makes the message publicly testable. It does not remove human choice or make every hearer sincere. The verse joins divine mercy with human responsibility: Allah clarifies the message, and people reveal themselves by what they do with that clarity.

Clarity also concerns the relationship between words and life. A command about full measure is not a riddle whose meaning disappears when a trader benefits from confusion. A warning about corruption is not so obscure that a community can keep harming people while claiming that no one knows what Allah requires. Clear language does not remove the need for study, but it prevents deliberate harm from hiding behind endless interpretation.

The Arabic tongue carried the revelation to people who could hear its sound, recognize its structures, and debate its claims. Its eloquence may deepen reflection, yet guidance is not limited to specialists in rhetoric. A person can understand the central call to worship Allah and deal justly even while needing teachers for difficult details. The verse keeps access to the message open.

Translation then becomes a responsibility rather than a replacement for the original. People who do not speak Arabic can seek reliable meanings, compare explanations, and learn what the Arabic wording contains. The existence of translation does not make language irrelevant; it shows the universal reach of a revelation first made clear to a particular people. No audience may use language as a reason to avoid learning.

The verse also guards against cultural pride among Arabic speakers. Knowing the language can make recognition easier, but it does not guarantee belief or righteousness. The people who first heard the Quran understood its words, yet many opposed its moral authority. Clear speech is a gift that becomes a proof only when the hearer responds honestly.

A clear Arabic tongue therefore serves both accessibility and accountability. It lets the first audience hear the warning without a linguistic barrier, and it leaves their refusal without a credible claim that the message was unintelligible. The Lord clarifies, the messenger conveys, and the human heart decides whether clarity will become submission.

A clear tongue also allows the first audience to challenge the message on its actual meaning. They can ask about worship, property, judgment, and the signs of revelation. They cannot honestly replace those questions with a complaint that the words are unavailable. This is why clear language is an act of fairness toward the hearer and a proof against deliberate evasion.

The passage does not imply that every Arabic speaker understands every verse in the same depth. Languages have grammar, context, and layers of meaning, and serious study remains necessary. Clarity means that the message is capable of being understood and explained, not that no effort is needed. The Quran can be deep without being deliberately obscure.

The language of revelation also carries the memory of the first warning community. That historical form should be preserved with care, while the meanings are made accessible to people across the world. A translator serves the message when accuracy and clarity guide the work, not when cultural pride or personal preference changes the command.

The final test remains moral. A person can speak Arabic beautifully and still resist the Lord, while another can approach the Quran through translation and obey with sincerity. The verse honors the clear vehicle Allah chose without making pronunciation the measure of righteousness. Clarity opens the door; faith walks through it.

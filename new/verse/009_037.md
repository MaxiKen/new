## Sūrah al-Tawbah 9:37

> Reallocating the sanctity of ˹these˺ months is an increase in disbelief, by which the disbelievers are led ˹far˺ astray. They adjust the sanctity one year and uphold it in another, only to maintain the number of months sanctified by Allah, violating the very months Allah has made sacred. Their evil deeds have been made appealing to them. And Allah does not guide the disbelieving people.

**Expanded Commentary**

**Nasīʾ Preserves the Form and Destroys the Rule**

Verse 37 names the manipulation of sacred months as nasīʾ and calls it an increase in disbelief. The exact historical form of the practice was debated. It can mean postponing a sacred month, or adding time to keep the lunar calendar aligned with the seasons. The dominant exegetical discussion focuses on moving the sacred status from one month to another so that the number four was preserved while the restriction itself could be avoided.

The moral problem is clear in either case. A community keeps the outward number demanded by revelation while changing the actual burden of obedience. It can then say that it honors the command even as it chooses when the command will apply. The verse exposes this as more than a calendar error. It is a refusal to let Allah's order govern human interest.

**Legal Appearance Can Hide Moral Rebellion**

The verse says they make a month lawful one year and sacred another. This is a picture of rules being moved whenever they interfere with political or economic plans. A month that should restrain fighting becomes available for attack; a month that should protect travel becomes convenient for settling a score. The community can preserve the language of sanctity while turning sanctity into a tool it controls.

That pattern appears in many fields. A person may keep the form of a contract while changing its meaning through hidden conditions. A religious institution may retain the vocabulary of charity while spending for private power. A government may celebrate law while applying it only to opponents. Nasīʾ is a calendar example of a wider temptation: preserve the symbol, alter the substance, and call the alteration obedience.

**The Number Four Is Not the Whole Command**

The verse says they sought to reconcile their manipulation with the number made sacred by Allah. This detail reveals their method. They were not denying the idea of sacred months openly. They wanted the credit for observing the number without accepting the fixed placement. The Qur'an shows that obedience is not satisfied by counting. The meaning of a rule includes the form in which Allah gave it.

This principle protects believers from technical evasion. Legal reasoning matters, and circumstances can affect how a rule applies, but deliberate manipulation for the purpose of making the forbidden lawful is not legitimate interpretation. The question is whether the person seeks Allah's intent or merely a loophole. A clever arrangement that defeats the purpose of revelation may be formally impressive and spiritually corrupt.

**Time and Social Justice**

The lunar calendar moves through the seasons, and the prohibition of intercalation has also been understood as preserving equal exposure to the conditions of religious duties. If communities could fix sacred obligations to preferred seasons, some groups would enjoy easier pilgrimage and warfare restrictions while others carried the harder periods. The moving calendar distributes difficulty rather than allowing a powerful group to keep comfort permanently.

That reading adds a social dimension to the verse. Divine time prevents a ruling community from arranging the year around its own advantage. Sacred months are not a privilege that one class can relocate. The calendar's movement makes worship and restraint shared experiences across generations and regions. A law that appears inconvenient can be a form of equality when people are tempted to reserve convenience for themselves.

**“Made Appealing” Describes Moral Self-Deception**

The verse says their evil deeds were made appealing to them. The danger is not only conscious rebellion. People can become impressed by their own legal ingenuity. They say that the number is preserved, that tradition is respected, or that circumstances require flexibility. With repetition, the altered practice feels righteous. The mind protects the interest by decorating it with religious language.

This is how self-deception grows. The first compromise may be recognized as a compromise. Later, the person builds an explanation that allows him to admire the compromise. The verse warns believers to examine actions that seem especially clever when they deliver convenience. A clear duty that repeatedly bends in the direction of profit may be revealing not wisdom but an appealing evil.

**The Modern Calendar of Evasion**

Believers today may not move sacred months, but they can move obligations in other ways. Charity is postponed until wealth is spent elsewhere. Repentance is delayed until after a career milestone. A clear prohibition is renamed a necessity whenever it affects the household budget. The practical question is whether the person is submitting to Allah's order or rearranging it around his desire.

Allah does not guide the disbelieving people because the final problem is not lack of information. It is settled refusal. The cure begins with admitting that the convenient rule is not neutral. Let Allah's command set the time, cost, and boundary. A calendar honestly followed is better than a calendar preserved only in appearance. The same is true of every religious rule: its benefit is found in submission to its meaning, not in displaying its shell. Sincerity accepts the inconvenience that reveals whether the rule has actually been obeyed. The believer may seek a genuine legal concession when Allah permits one, but he does not manufacture a concession merely because obedience has become costly. He asks for knowledge before acting, and once the command is clear he accepts its discipline without dressing preference as scholarship.

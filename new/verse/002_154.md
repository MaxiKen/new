## Sūrah al-Baqarah 2:154

> Never say that those martyred in the cause of Allah are dead—in fact, they are alive! But you do not perceive it.

**Expanded Commentary**

**A Correction to How the Dead Are Spoken Of**

The verse forbids a way of speaking about those killed in God's cause, and the prohibition concerns a statement that the community would naturally make. When a person is killed, the living say that he has died, and the Book tells the believers not to use that word of the martyrs.

The prohibition is a matter of accuracy rather than of consolation. The Book does not say that the community should avoid the word because it is painful; it says that the statement is not true of those it is applied to. What the reader receives is a correction to his language, and behind the correction lies a claim about what happens to a person who is killed in God's way.

**What the Verse Forbids and What It Affirms**

The sentence has two halves, and the second half is the ground of the first. The community is told not to describe the martyrs as dead, and it is then told that they are alive, with the qualification that the living do not perceive how.

The structure is common in the Book, where a prohibition is joined to a positive statement so that the reader understands both the rule and its reason. Here the reason is that the martyrs have passed into a condition that the ordinary senses cannot detect, and the Book supplies the information that the senses withhold. What the community is asked to do is to accept the report and to adjust its speech accordingly.

**"They Are Alive" and the Reports About the Martyrs**

The Prophet ﷺ described the state of those who are killed in God's way in a report that has been preserved in the collections of sound tradition. He stated that their spirits are in the bodies of green birds that move about the Garden as they wish and settle on lamps hanging beneath the Throne.

The same report describes God turning to them and asking what they desire, and it records their answer that they want nothing more than to return to the world and be killed again in His way. The Prophet ﷺ explained that the request follows from the greatness of the reward, and the answer they receive is that they will not be sent back.

**The Parallel in Sūrat Āl ʿImrān**

The Book states the same subject in another chapter and adds a detail that is not given here, saying that the martyrs are alive with their Lord and are provided for. That passage then describes them rejoicing in what God has given them and receiving news of those who have not yet joined them.

Reading the two passages together supplies the community with a fuller account of the state the verse describes. This verse affirms that the martyrs are alive and states the limit of the community's perception; the later passage names the place of that life and describes what the martyrs are given there. The reader is not asked to speculate about the details, only to accept what has been reported.

**The Limit of Perception**

The closing words state that the living do not perceive the condition of the martyrs, and the clause explains why the community needed to be told about it at all. If the state of the martyrs were visible, the prohibition in the first half of the verse would be unnecessary, since no one would describe as dead a person he could see to be alive.

The Book states the limit so that the reader understands the nature of the information he has been given. What he knows about the martyrs comes from revelation rather than from observation, and his confidence in the matter rests on the truthfulness of the report. The verse therefore teaches the community how to hold a truth that its senses cannot confirm, which is a lesson it needs in every part of its belief.

**Martyrdom in the Book's Vocabulary**

The Book's term for the person killed in God's way is connected with witness, and the community's scholars noted that the martyr testifies to the truth with his life. The term appears in the Book in contexts of struggle, of emigration, and of the defence of the community, and the reports describe several kinds of death that bring the reward, including death by drowning, by plague, and in childbirth.

The verse's subject is therefore broader than a single battle. The community's teachers used the passage to speak about everyone who gives his life in the path of God, and the sentence's correction applies to the language used about all of them. What the Book establishes is that the loss of a person in God's cause is not the end of his existence, whatever form the loss takes.

**Where the Verse Stands in the Passage**

The sentence follows the instruction to seek help through patience and prayer and precedes the description of the trials with which the community would be tested. The Book has just named loss of life among the afflictions, and the verse that follows the present one returns to the same list. The statement about the martyrs therefore stands at the centre of a passage on endurance, and it supplies the community with the knowledge it needs in order to endure the loss it is told to expect.

**What the Community Learns About Loss**

The verse does not remove grief from the community's experience, and the Book does not ask the believers to pretend that the death of a companion is nothing. What it does is give them a true statement about the person they have lost, and the Book treats that knowledge as the ground on which grief is carried.

The reader is also taught to be careful about the words he uses in a matter whose reality he cannot see. The prohibition against calling the martyrs dead is a small instruction with a large subject, and it teaches the community to let revelation, rather than appearance, decide how it speaks about the matters that lie beyond its sight.

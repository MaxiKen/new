## Sūrah al-Baqarah 2:143

> And so We have made you ˹believers˺ an upright community so that you may be witnesses over humanity and that the Messenger may be a witness over you. We assigned your former direction of prayer only to distinguish those who would remain faithful to the Messenger from those who would lose faith. It was certainly a difficult test except for those ˹rightly˺ guided by Allah. And Allah would never discount your ˹previous acts of˺ faith. Surely Allah is Ever Gracious and Most Merciful to humanity.

**Expanded Commentary**

**A Community in the Middle**

The verse describes the community as upright, and the Arabic term carries the sense of the middle and of what is balanced. The Prophet ﷺ explained the word as meaning just, and the community's scholars connected it with the avoidance of extremes in belief and practice, since a position between two failures is the position the Book commends.

The term also describes the community's place among the religious communities of the world. The sūrah has been examining the claims of two communities that defined themselves by their own scriptures, and it now describes the believers as a community positioned to bear witness rather than to compete. The middle is not a compromise between the two but the standard by which both are measured.

**Witnesses Over Humanity**

The role assigned in the sentence is that of a witness, and the term describes both one who testifies and one who is evidence for something. The community is thereby given a function among the nations, and the Book states the same function in a later chapter where it reminds the believers that God named them submitters in earlier scriptures and in the final revelation so that the Messenger might be a witness over them and they over humanity.

The commentators connected the role with the Day of Judgment, and they preserved reports in which the community testifies to the fidelity of the prophets when their own peoples dispute it. The Book's image is of a community whose presence in the world is itself a testimony, and the verse states the function before it describes the test that qualifies the community for it.

**The Messenger as a Witness Over You**

The sentence adds that the Messenger is a witness over the community, and the phrase places the community's own conduct under the same standard it applies to others. A witness testifies to what he has seen, and the Prophet ﷺ had seen the community's response to the revelation and would be asked about it on the day of judgment.

The Book states the same in another chapter where the Prophet is told that he will be brought as a witness against his own community as other witnesses are brought from theirs. The relationship between the two clauses of the sentence is therefore symmetrical: the community testifies about humanity, and the messenger testifies about the community, so that no party is left without an account to give.

**The Former Direction as a Test**

The verse states that the previous direction of prayer was appointed to distinguish those who remain faithful to the Messenger from those who turn back, and the commentators preserved the reactions of the various groups that used the change as an occasion for objection. The hypocrites complained about a community that changed its direction, the Jewish community said the Prophet was longing for his homeland, and the idolaters said he had wavered in his religion.

The test worked in the other direction as well. Those who were firm in their adherence to the Messenger accepted the change without requiring an explanation beyond the command, and the Book describes their position as the one that required divine guidance to hold. The direction of prayer became a measure of whether a person's loyalty was to the practice or to the one who appointed it.

**"God Would Never Discount Your Faith"**

The sentence then reassures those who had prayed in the earlier direction and had died before the change, and the reports describe the community asking about their brothers who had passed away. The answer is that God does not let belief go to waste, and the statement is phrased in a way that treats the earlier prayers as faith rather than as an error.

The Book's use of the term for faith here indicates how it regards the whole matter. The community's prayers, offered in obedience to a command that was later replaced, were acts of belief, and the Book states that they are not lost. The reassurance therefore applies to every act performed in good faith under an instruction that is later superseded.

**A Question About Divine Knowledge**

The phrase rendered as distinguishing and knowing raised a question for the theologians, since it attributes to God a knowledge that is described as arriving with the event. Commentators explained the phrase in several ways: that God observes what He has always known, that the outcome is made visible to the angels and the community, or that the wording is a kindness in expression. The Book's own treatment of testing in other passages supplies the framework, since it states that people will not be left untested after claiming belief.

The verse's reconciliation of divine knowledge and human trial is therefore the same throughout the Book: God knows what a person will do, and the trial still takes place so that the outcome is actual rather than hypothetical. What the community receives from the verse is a test with a known result on God's side and an unknown one on its own.

**The Names at the End**

The sentence closes with two names describing God as gracious and merciful toward humanity, and the commentators noted that the closing names correspond to the reassurance the verse has given. The community that had worried about its earlier prayers is told that the One who received them is kind and merciful, and the names leave the reader with that assurance rather than with the difficulty of the trial.

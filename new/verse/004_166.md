## Sūrah al-Nisāʾ 4:166

> Yet ˹if you are denied, O Prophet,˺ Allah bears witness to what He has sent down to you—He has sent it with His knowledge. The angels too bear witness. And Allah ˹alone˺ is sufficient as a Witness.

### Expanded Commentary

**Divine testimony to revelation**

When people deny the revelation, Allah bears witness that it was sent with His knowledge, and the angels also bear witness. The Prophet’s truth does not depend on the acceptance of his opponents. Divine testimony stands even when human testimony is hostile.

“To send with His knowledge” indicates that the Qur’an is neither accidental inspiration nor a text produced without purpose. It comes within divine wisdom, carrying knowledge of the audience, the future, and the law’s consequences. The angels’ witness adds the unseen dimension to the public signs of revelation.

Allah’s sufficiency as Witness consoles the Prophet and every believer whose truthful claim is rejected. It also restrains believers from thinking they must force recognition. Convey the truth and embody it; God’s witness is not improved by coercion.

**Revelation does not need applause**

When a truthful message is denied, the believer may feel that silence has defeated it. The verse restores perspective: God and the angels bear witness even when public opinion turns away. The messenger’s task is faithful conveyance, not manufacturing universal agreement by force.

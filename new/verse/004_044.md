## Sūrah al-Nisāʾ 4:44

> Have you ˹O Prophet˺ not seen those who were given a portion of the Scriptures yet trade it for misguidance and wish to see you deviate from the ˹Right˺ Path?

### Expanded Commentary

**Knowledge can be traded for misguidance**

The verse rebukes people given a portion of Scripture who exchange guidance for error and wish the believers to deviate. The phrase “a portion” is sobering: possession of scripture does not guarantee obedience to its whole message. A person may quote revelation, hold religious office, or inherit a learned tradition while selling its guidance for status, fear, or political advantage.

**The danger of wishing others to fall**

The verse identifies not only private error but an active desire to see the Prophet and his community leave the right path. This can occur when religious authority becomes invested in monopoly. If another community recognizes truth, the recognition threatens influence, so the authority reshapes evidence rather than revising itself. The Qur’an warns that the desire to control guidance can be more dangerous than simple ignorance.

**A lesson for believers**

The warning is not confined to the People of the Book. Muslims who inherit scripture can likewise trade it for faction, nationalism, money, or approval. A commentator who hides an inconvenient command, a leader who weaponizes a verse, and a student who seeks arguments only to defeat opponents repeat the same pattern. Knowledge is a trust whose value is measured by submission to truth.

The verse asks the reader to distinguish criticism of a distortion from hatred of a people. Its target is conduct and motive, not ethnicity. A believer can defend revelation firmly while acknowledging sincere seekers and learning from those who possess knowledge. The question is always whether scripture is being followed or made into currency.

**Religious knowledge and moral courage**

The person who knows scripture but sells its direction for influence is more dangerous than the uninformed person, because others trust the credential. Students should therefore learn to ask what a teaching requires of the teacher. Knowledge that never corrects power or desire may be information, but it has not yet become guidance.

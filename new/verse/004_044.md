## Sūrah al-Nisāʾ 4:44

> Have you ˹O Prophet˺ not seen those who were given a portion of the Scriptures yet trade it for misguidance and wish to see you deviate from the ˹Right˺ Path?

**Expanded Commentary**

**Have You Not Seen**

The question *a lam tara ilā alladhīna ūtū naṣīban min al-kitāb yashtarūna al-ḍalālata wa yurīdūna an taḍillū al-sabīl* — have you not seen those who were given a portion of the Scriptures yet trade it for misguidance and wish to see you deviate from the Right Path — is not a request for information. *A lam tara* — have you not seen, do you not consider — was read as an alert that calls the Prophet ﷺ and the listeners to look at a pattern they know and to recognize its present instance. Those who were given a portion of the Book — *naṣīban min al-kitāb* — were given a share of guidance: the Torah and what came with it, with its own limits, dowries, and rites of purification, many of which the sūrah has just restated as clarity made anew (4:26). To trade that portion — *yashtarūna al-ḍalālah* — is to give up guidance and take misguidance in exchange, as though the Book were goods to be sold. The same verb *ishtarā* appears elsewhere for selling the verses for a small price and for selling faith for worldly gain. The sūrah has warned against devouring wealth by falsehood and against spending for show (4:29, 4:37–38); here the trade is of the most valuable wealth — revelation — for the territory of wandering.

**Trading a Portion for Misguidance**

The phrase *yashtarūna al-ḍalālata* was explained as actively seeking misguidance though they already possessed a share of the Book that could have guided them. *Ḍalāl* — wandering, straying — is the opposite of the *hudā* — guidance — the sūrah says God wills to give and to make clear. To buy wandering while holding a portion of guidance is to reverse the transaction the sūrah invites when it says *“what harm would it have been if they had believed and donated from what Allah provided”* (4:39) and promises multiplication for an atom’s weight of good (4:40). The purchase also recalls the stingy who conceal bounty and promote stinginess (4:37) and the ostentatious who spend for show without belief (4:38); each conceals or diverts a trust — wealth or revelation — for a nearer gain. Here the gain is described as desire for the community to deviate. The verse was revealed concerning Jewish scholars in Madinah who knew descriptions of the Prophet ﷺ in their Scriptures yet hid them, altered wording, or priced access to interpretation, and who counselled Muslims to doubt. The type, however, was read more broadly: any person given a share of the Book who prefers to keep influence by keeping others astray, who trades the share he was given for the profit of leading others off the path.

**And Wish to See You Deviate from the Path**

The second trait *wa yurīdūna an taḍillū al-sabīl* — and they wish you to deviate from the Right Path — matches the diagnosis of the previous page: *“those who follow their desires wish to see you deviate entirely from Allah’s Way”* (4:27). The same verb *yurīdūna* — they wish, they will — appears in both, showing that the wish to misguide is not incidental to trading revelation; it is its aim. Those who have sold guidance need others to wander so that their own wandering looks like leadership. The path — *al-sabīl* — in this sūrah is the clear, guided, lightened way the sūrah has been drawing: limits kept, orphan wealth guarded, marriage sought with wealth in chastity (4:24), trade by mutual consent (4:29), and prayer approached with awareness (4:43). To wish that believers deviate from that path is to wish that the Medinan community lose the clarity God wills to give (4:26). The verse therefore places the Prophet ﷺ as addressed — *a lam tara* — to assure believers that the one who knows best who enemies are (4:45) sees the wish and is sufficient as Guardian and Helper. The wish of the misguiders to make others wander is already noted by the One who guides.

**Given a Portion, Not the Whole, Yet Accountable**

The phrase *ūtū naṣīban min al-kitāb* — given a portion of the Scriptures — was noted as precise. They were given a share, not the final Book in its completion, yet that share was enough to recognize truth when it came. The sūrah elsewhere says *“O you who were given the Book, believe in what We have revealed confirming your own before We wipe out faces”* (4:47), making the portion they hold a trust to be confirmed, not contradicted. To be given a portion and then to sell it is graver than never having been given, because the light was already in hand. The Qur’an elsewhere says that those given the Book know the Prophet ﷺ as they know their own sons, yet a party conceals. That knowledge is the background to the transaction here: the sellers are not ignorant wanderers but possessors who exchange. The sūrah has already said that God never wrongs even by an atom’s weight but multiplies good (4:40); to trade an atom’s weight of guidance for its opposite is therefore not a small miscount but a reversal of the economy the sūrah has been teaching, where bounty asked from God (4:32) is multiplied and bounty concealed is humiliation prepared (4:37). The portion given was bounty; to sell it is to conceal bounty in its most serious form.

**A Warning Inside a Question**

The question form does more than expose; it cautions the believers not to imitate the sellers. A community given the final portion — the Qur’an — could also trade it for desire, as the deviators wish them to do, by using verses for status, selling interpretation, or bending limits about wealth and marriage to suit appetite. The sūrah’s repeated calls to refer disputes to Allah and His Messenger (4:59) and to obey those in authority within that referral are the guard against that trade. To keep the Book is to keep the limits the sūrah has made clear; to trade it is to lose the limits and then wish others to lose them as well. The next verses will detail how some Jews distort wording — *“we hear and we disobey,” “herd us”* with a twist — and how a different phrasing would have been better and more proper (4:46), showing in language what trading the Book sounds like. This verse is the headline for that detail: a portion was given, it was sold for misguidance, and the sellers wish you to wander. The answer is not to meet trade with trade but to hold the portion, to believe and donate from what was provided, and to trust the One who is sufficient as Guardian and Helper to keep the path straight.

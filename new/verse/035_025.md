## Sūrah Fāṭir 35:25

> If they deny you, so did those before them. Their messengers came to them with clear proofs, divine Books, and enlightening Scriptures.

**Expanded Commentary**

**Present Denial Repeats an Older Pattern**

The Prophet's opponents are reminded that denial is not a new argument against revelation. Earlier peoples rejected their messengers even after receiving signs, scriptures, and a clear account of the truth. The repetition exposes a human habit: a community treats its own resistance as evidence that the messenger is wrong, while forgetting that previous communities used the same defense and were later judged by it.

The reminder is not meant to excuse the current deniers by saying that everyone rejects. It does the opposite. A repeated pattern becomes a warning. If earlier rejection did not protect those who practiced it, contemporary rejection will not protect its practitioners. The Prophet can therefore recognize opposition as part of the prophetic road without allowing its familiarity to make it seem harmless.

**Clear Proofs Address Both Mind and Conscience**

The messengers brought clear proofs. These can include miracles, signs in creation, the coherence of the message, and the integrity of the messenger's life. A proof is clear not because it removes every possibility of refusal, but because it gives a fair-minded person grounds to recognize truth. People can still reject a clear sign when pride or interest is stronger than honesty.

The verse also names revealed books and the enlightening Scripture. Revelation addresses more than the intellect's demand for evidence. It teaches how to live after recognizing Allah, how to distinguish justice from desire, and how to organize worship and society. A miracle may establish a messenger's truthfulness, while a book guides the community through years of choices. The combination shows that prophethood brings both confirmation and direction.

**Scripture Is a Chain of Divine Guidance**

The plural term for scriptures can refer to different revelations sent to earlier peoples, while the luminous Book can point to a more comprehensive written guidance. The exact distinction need not be forced into one narrow scheme. The central fact is that Allah did not leave previous communities with one moment of wonder and no instruction afterward. He sent words that could be remembered, recited, taught, and used to judge conduct.

The Qur'an calls itself confirming what came before in 2:41 and 5:48. Confirmation includes affirming the original truth of earlier revelation while correcting human alteration and restoring the proper center of worship. A later revelation does not make divine guidance contradictory. It shows that the same Lord continued to address changing communities and to preserve the essential call through successive forms.

**Denial Often Protects an Interest**

When evidence arrives, rejection may be driven by more than intellectual doubt. A messenger can threaten inherited status, unjust wealth, tribal authority, or a comfortable moral arrangement. Earlier peoples may have rejected because accepting the message would require them to surrender a privilege. The current audience faces the same pressure. Clear proof is not always welcomed because it can demand a costly change.

This explains why the verse places denial beside the record of past peoples rather than beside a technical debate. The issue is a moral pattern. A community may ask for proof while secretly deciding that no proof will be accepted if it threatens the present order. Revelation exposes that maneuver. The honest response is not to invent endless demands but to examine what the heart fears losing if the messenger is true.

The list of proofs, books, and scripture also shows that revelation is not a single type of evidence. Some people need the sign that breaks a false picture of power; others need the patient instruction of a law; others need a text that answers questions of meaning and accountability. Allah's guidance addresses the whole human being. It can challenge the mind, reform the will, and organize communal life. Refusal may therefore target one part while leaving another untouched. A person can admire a prophet's character but reject his command, or praise a scripture's beauty while refusing its judgment. The verse calls such partial acceptance unstable. If the messenger is truthful, his message deserves more than selective approval. The recipient must allow the truth to decide what is to be kept, surrendered, or repaired. This is especially important for a community that inherits scripture. It must not reduce revelation to heritage, recitation, or public identity. The Book is an active proof that asks the reader to stand somewhere. Earlier peoples did not avoid accountability by possessing a scripture, and later people will not avoid it by speaking respectfully about revelation without obeying its central call.

The proof is therefore both gift and test. It opens a road toward Allah, and refusal turns the same road into evidence that the traveler chose to remain away.

A community should ask whether its resistance protects truth or merely protects status. The Book distinguishes the two by what the heart is willing to surrender.

**The Prophet Is Strengthened by Historical Company**

The memory of earlier messengers gives the Prophet a form of companionship. He is not walking an untested road. Those before him endured mockery, false accusations, and opposition, yet their missions remain part of the evidence for his own. The continuity is consoling because it turns present isolation into membership in a long line of servants chosen to convey truth.

It also teaches patience to later believers. A sincere message may be rejected without being defective, and a messenger may be opposed without being abandoned by Allah. The clear proofs remain clear even when an audience refuses them. The final judgment will distinguish the truth of the message from the temporary success of its opponents. Denial is a repeated human act; divine guidance remains the more lasting pattern.

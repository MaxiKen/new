## Sūrah al-Shuʿarāʾ 26:114

> I am not going to expel the believers.

**Expanded Commentary**

**Noah Refuses to Trade Believers for Elite Approval**

Noah answers the chiefs' class objection with a firm refusal: he will not expel the believers. The sentence protects the community from a bargain in which poor or socially weak people must leave so that influential people can feel comfortable approaching the message. Noah will not make privilege the price of religious acceptance.

His refusal is not a defense of disorder. It is a defense of the believers' right to stand before God without being displaced by people who judge worth through status. Their faith, not their wealth, determines their place in the believing community. Noah's position makes the message open to every person whom God guides.

**Expulsion Would Turn Social Rank Into a Religious Law**

The chiefs want their social evaluation to control the community's boundaries. If Noah accepted their demand, low status would become a religious defect. People would learn that faith is welcome only when it does not disturb the powerful. Such a community might appear respectable, but it would have surrendered the truth of equal submission to God.

Noah rejects that transformation. Poverty, lack of lineage, and limited influence do not make a person unfit for belief. The chiefs may dislike sitting with humble people, but their discomfort is not a command from God. Removing believers would reward arrogance and teach the wrong lesson about who owns the house of faith.

**Believers Are Not Defined by the Chiefs' Insult**

The opponents called Noah's followers the lowest of the low. Noah does not repeat the insult as though it were an objective classification. He calls them believers. That word gives their true identity. Their relation to God is more important than the rank assigned to them by society.

This does not make every believer perfect or exempt from correction. It means that correction must address real conduct, not inherited contempt. A poor believer may need advice, just as a wealthy believer may need advice. Neither needs to be expelled merely because a chief finds his presence beneath the chief's dignity.

**The Messenger Protects the Community's Moral Center**

A community's boundary reveals what it values. Noah's refusal says that faith and truth matter more than elite comfort. The community will not be organized around the pride of those who want to enter only after the humble have been removed. Its center remains God's call, not the social preference of its strongest members.

This protection benefits the powerful as well as the poor. If chiefs enter a community only after its poor members are expelled, they will never learn humility. The presence of the believers confronts them with the fact that God's mercy is not distributed according to their ranking. Noah's refusal gives them a chance to repent of pride rather than allowing pride to set the terms.

Quran 11:29 records Noah saying that he will not dismiss those who believe. The parallel confirms that this is a settled principle in his mission, not a momentary emotional reaction. He refuses the chiefs' request because the believers are accountable to God and because the message is not a property of the elite.

The same concern appears in 6:52, where poor believers are protected from being dismissed. Across these passages, revelation establishes a clear boundary: social influence cannot purchase the right to exclude sincere worshippers. The messenger's task is to guide, not to curate a congregation for the comfort of status.

The chiefs have not shown that the believers are false. They have shown only that the believers lack their preferred social standing. If Noah expelled them, he would be teaching the community that appearance outweighs faith. The demand would become a precedent for every later exclusion based on wealth, ancestry, or occupation.

Noah's refusal rejects that precedent. Evidence must concern belief, conduct, and truth, not the accident of birth or possession. A person can be rich or poor by circumstances beyond his control. What God asks is how the person responds to Him, not whether the person can impress the chiefs.

**The Refusal Is a Form of Justice**

Justice does not mean treating every situation as identical. It means giving each person what truth requires. In this situation, justice requires that believers not be punished for the contempt of others. Noah's refusal restores that measure. The chiefs are free to listen, but they cannot make their listening depend on injustice toward another group.

This principle applies to institutions that claim to serve God. They must not remove the vulnerable to attract the powerful. Welcoming the poor is not a public-relations gesture; it is recognition that worshippers stand before the same Lord. A community that excludes people for status has already allowed status to become an idol.

**Noah's Refusal Does Not Make Him Blind to Conduct**

Protecting believers from expulsion does not mean refusing all discipline. A community may address harm, dishonesty, and abuse through fair evidence and wise judgment. Noah's argument concerns a different demand: expel people because they are socially low and because chiefs dislike their presence.

The distinction prevents the verse from being misused. Inclusion is not the abandonment of standards. It is the refusal to make class a standard of faith. A just community can be morally serious and socially open at the same time, because its seriousness is directed by God rather than by the pride of chiefs.

**The Believers' Place Is Secured by God's Call**

Noah's sentence gives the humble believers a human protector, but their deepest security is still with God. The messenger refuses to expel them because he recognizes the Lord's claim over them. Their place is not a favor Noah may withdraw to win approval. It is part of the community formed by faith.

The refusal therefore answers the chiefs with both firmness and mercy. They are invited to believe without requiring injustice, and the believers are protected from being sacrificed to social pride. Noah will not build a respectable community by abandoning the people whom God has accepted.

## Sūrah al-Naḥl 16:33

> **Are they only awaiting the coming of the angels or the command of your Lord ˹O Prophet˺? So were those before them. And Allah never wronged them, but it was they who wronged themselves.**

**Expanded Commentary**

**What Are They Waiting For?**

Hal yanẓurūna illā an ta'tiyahum al-malā'ikatu aw ya'tiya amru rabbika — are they only waiting for angels to come or command of your Lord to come? Hal is interrogative, are they, do they? Yanẓurūna from n-ẓ-r means to wait, await, expect. Illā an ta'tiyahum al-malā'ikatu, except that angels come to them, meaning angels of death to take souls, or angels of punishment, or angels for reckoning. Aw ya'tiya amru rabbika, or command of your Lord comes, meaning punishment in world or Hour in Hereafter. The question is rhetorical, criticizing delay in believing until undeniable signs come. Al-Ṭabarī explains that Makkan deniers were waiting for angels to come with punishment or for Allah's command of destruction or Resurrection to come before believing, but by then belief will not benefit. The same question appears in 6:158: *Do they await except that angels should come to them or your Lord should come or some signs of your Lord should come?* (6:158). The answer is that waiting until then is foolish, because then it is too late.

**So Were Those Before Them**

Kadhālika faʿala al-ladhīna min qablihim — so did those before them. Those before them also waited for angels or command before believing, and when it came, they were destroyed. Al-Ṭabarī says previous nations also demanded angels or punishment before believing, and when it came, they perished. The phrase kadhālika, like that, so, indicates that current deniers follow same pattern as previous, and will meet same outcome unless they change. The sūrah has repeatedly used those before them as warning: they plotted, their building collapsed, they were humiliated, they wronged themselves. Now it says they also waited for angels or command. The past is mirror for present. Al-Rāzī notes that this repetition is to make Makkans see that they are not original in denial; they are repeating old story whose ending is known.

**Allah Did Not Wrong Them, They Wronged Themselves**

Wa-mā ẓalamahum Allāhu wa-lākin kānū anfusahum yaẓlimūn — and Allah did not wrong them, but they wronged themselves. Ẓalama means to wrong, to oppress, to put thing in wrong place. Allah did not wrong them by punishing without reason or by not giving signs; He gave blessings, sent messengers, showed straight way, gave time. They wronged themselves by choosing denial, arrogance, calling revelation fables, plotting, opposing believers. Al-Qurṭubī explains that self-wronging includes harming own souls by committing deeds that lead to punishment, and also includes putting worship in wrong place by giving it to idols. The phrase appears many times in Qur'an, as in 16:118 and 29:40, to emphasize divine justice and human responsibility. It answers hidden objection: if Allah could have guided all, as verse 9 said, why does He punish those not guided? Answer: He did not wrong them; they wronged themselves by refusing guidance shown.

**The Connection To Angels Taking Souls**

Verse 33 mentions angels coming, which connects to verses 28 and 32 where angels take souls of wrongdoers and righteous. Those who wait for angels to come will indeed see angels, but angels will come to take their souls while wronging themselves, as verse 28 described, not as they hope. The waiting is therefore futile: angels will come, but not as bringers of faith that benefits; they will come as takers of souls. Similarly, command of your Lord will come, as verse 1 said command is at hand, but when it comes, it will be too late to believe. The verse therefore urges not to wait: believe now, while angels have not yet come to take soul, while command has not yet arrived as punishment.

**Why This Warning Comes After Paradise Description**

After describing excellent home for righteous and peaceful death, the sūrah returns to warning for those who still wait. The contrast is intentional: righteous have Gardens, wrongdoers await angels or command. Which waiting is better? Righteous awaited reward and received peace; wrongdoers await punishment and will receive humiliation. The verse also prepares for next verse: evil consequences overtook them and they were overwhelmed by what they ridiculed. Those who wait for punishment will be overtaken by it. The warning is both logical and compassionate: do not wait until it is too late.

**Linguistic Note On Naẓara And Amr**

Yanẓurūna from n-ẓ-r means to look, wait, expect, from same root as naẓar, sight, consideration. Hal yanẓurūna illā, are they waiting except, is common Qur'anic construction for criticizing delay. Ta'tiyahum from a-t-y means to come to them. Al-malā'ikatu, angels, plural. Amru rabbika, command of your Lord, same amr as in verse 1, command at hand. Kadhālika faʿala al-ladhīna min qablihim, so did those before them, with faʿala, did. Wa-mā ẓalamahum Allāhu, and Allah did not wrong them, with mā for negation. Anfusahum yaẓlimūn, themselves they wrong, with yaẓlimūn present tense indicating ongoing self-wronging. The verse's structure — question, past precedent, justice statement — moves from present waiting to past example to principle of justice, guiding listener to see that waiting is old pattern and that punishment is self-inflicted, not divine wrongdoing.

**The Danger Of Waiting For Undeniable Signs**

Waiting for angels or command before believing is dangerous because by then choice is removed. The Qur'an says *On the Day when some signs of your Lord come, no soul will benefit from its faith if it had not believed before* (6:158). Faith that comes after seeing angels of death or punishment is forced, not chosen, and therefore does not benefit. The sūrah has given many signs that are not overwhelming but invite reflection: cattle, rain, crops, night, day, sea, mountains. These signs allow choice; they can be reflected upon or ignored. Angels and command are signs that do not allow choice; they overwhelm. Allah in mercy sends first kind so that people may choose; if they refuse, second kind will come that does not allow choice. The verse therefore urges to believe in first signs before second comes.

**Self-Wronging As Explanation Of Justice**

The statement Allah did not wrong them but they wronged themselves is central to Qur'anic justice. It appears in many places to refute idea that punishment is arbitrary. Al-Rāzī explains that wronging self includes both committing sin and failing to do what would save self, like failing to reflect on blessings, failing to ask knowledgeable, failing to emigrate when able. In al-Naḥl, self-wronging includes calling revelation fables, plotting, opposing believers, and misusing divine will as excuse. All are choices that harm self. The phrase also comforts oppressed: those who were wronged by plotters were not wronged by Allah; their suffering is part of test that will be rewarded, as verses 41-42 will show for emigrants. Divine justice is preserved even when worldly injustice occurs.

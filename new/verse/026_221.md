## Sūrah al-Shuʿarāʾ 26:221

> Shall I inform you of whom the devils ˹actually˺ descend upon?

**Expanded Commentary**

**The Question Turns the Accusation Around**

The opponents have treated the Quran as though it came from devils. This verse answers by asking who actually receives their influence. The question is a reversal: instead of allowing the accusers to define revelation by insult, it makes them examine the kind of person who welcomes satanic suggestion.

A question can expose a false picture more sharply than a long argument. The Quran has already identified its source as revelation from the Lord of all worlds and its carrier as the trustworthy spirit. The accusation therefore has no support in the message's origin. The verse now directs attention to the moral and spiritual condition that is compatible with devilish descent. It moves the discussion from rumor to evidence and from atmosphere to character, where the accusation can be tested.

**Influence Follows Readiness**

The wording does not describe devils descending on every person without distinction. It asks about those upon whom they descend, implying a correspondence between the receiver and the influence. Satanic suggestion finds an opening in a heart that welcomes falsehood, feeds wrongdoing, and wants authority without accountability.

This does not make the human being equal to the devil or remove human choice. Influence is not compulsion. A whisper may invite, flatter, or distort, but the person still decides whether to entertain it and act upon it. The verse preserves responsibility while explaining why some people become willing instruments of deception. It also prevents the sinner from shifting blame entirely onto an unseen tempter: the opening is cultivated by repeated choices, and it can be closed by a deliberate return to truth.

**The Quran Is Not Identified by Unseen Atmosphere**

People may call a speech inspired by spirits because it is powerful, unfamiliar, or difficult to imitate. The verse refuses that loose standard. Unseen language alone does not establish divine origin. The message must be considered with its source, its carrier, its truthfulness, and the direction it gives to human life.

The Quran calls people to worship Allah, guard rights, repent, and prepare for judgment. A claim that devils brought it down must explain how a force of misguidance produced a sustained summons away from its own aims. The accusation collapses because it names mystery while ignoring moral direction. It also ignores the Quran's repeated exposure of deception, arrogance, and injustice. A deceiver may imitate a warning for a moment, but cannot make opposition to deception the settled purpose of a revelation without defeating his own project.

**The Guarded Revelation Has a Different Descent**

Quran 72:8–9 describes the jinn finding heaven guarded and their former attempts at eavesdropping blocked. That parallel supports the contrast in this verse. The devils do not have free access to the heavenly source of revelation. What reaches the Prophet is brought by permission through a trustworthy channel, not gathered by hostile listeners.

The question therefore prepares the reader to distinguish two kinds of descent. Revelation descends with truth and protection; satanic influence descends upon people already prepared to lie and sin. The difference is not merely where something comes from in the unseen. It is also what the influence produces in the human being who receives it.

The wording shall I inform you is also a teaching method. It invites the audience to compare the accusation with the evidence rather than simply endure a denial. The people called the Quran demonic, but the Quran asks them to look at the actual receivers of satanic influence. Their moral character is part of the answer. A source that produces deliberate falsehood is not the source of a message that calls people to truth.

Influence can be understood as a relation of desire. The devil does not make a truthful heart into a liar by force. He finds a person who wants permission to lie, then offers a story that makes the lie easier to accept. The person's readiness does not excuse the influence; it explains why the influence gains room. Repentance begins by closing that room through truthfulness and obedience.

The question also warns against judging a message by the unusual nature of its delivery alone. The unseen is not a sign of evil, and ordinary speech is not automatically a sign of truth. A claim must be measured by its source, its chain, its content, and its fruits. The Quran gives these measures openly, while the satanic channel is associated with hidden fragments and habitual falsehood.

A believer should therefore be careful when people use the word demonic as a weapon. The label may be intended to frighten the audience away from a teaching before the teaching is examined. Ask what the teaching commands, what kind of character it forms, and whether it brings people toward Allah or toward pride and harm. The verse does not invite superstition; it invites moral discernment.

The opening question stands as a final answer to the old accusation. The Quran is not authenticated by the fact that it astonishes people, nor refuted by the fact that opponents dislike it. Its source is the Lord, its carrier is trustworthy, and the beings who truly descend upon corrupt liars are excluded from its protected transmission. The reader is left with a practical test: examine the claim, examine its moral fruit, and refuse to let an accusation replace honest attention to revelation. What comes from Allah calls the heart into truth; what comes through satanic influence seeks a heart already willing to betray it. That distinction is both theological and practical, because every listener must choose which influence to welcome, nourish, and obey in every moment daily. Discernment is proved not by suspicion of the unseen but by faithful response to what Allah has made clear.

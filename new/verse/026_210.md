## Sūrah al-Shuʿarāʾ 26:210

> It was not the devils who brought this ˹Quran˺ down:

**Expanded Commentary**

**The Quran's Source Is Not Demonic**

After establishing that the Quran is revelation from the Lord of all worlds and that the trustworthy spirit brought it down, the verse rejects a competing explanation. The devils did not bring the Quran. Its guidance, moral clarity, and call to worship Allah do not come from the powers that mislead human beings.

The denial matters because opponents could not easily refute the message itself. They therefore tried to give its origin a darker name, as if a warning against idolatry and corruption were a product of the very forces the warning opposes. The verse cuts through that reversal. A message that calls people from deception to truth cannot be assigned to the agents of deception without ignoring its content.

**The Message Opposes the Work of Devils**

Devils invite people toward confusion, pride, and disobedience. The Quran calls people toward recognition of Allah, truthful speech, justice, repentance, and restraint. The contrast is not based on a vague impression. It appears in the direction the message gives to the heart and the social order it demands.

This does not mean that a person cannot misuse Quranic words. A human being can quote guidance while seeking power or defending injustice. The misuse belongs to the speaker, not to the source of the revelation. The verse concerns the origin and nature of the Quran itself, which cannot be judged by the bad conduct of someone who claims to represent it.

**Revelation Has a Trustworthy Chain**

The preceding account identifies the trustworthy spirit as the carrier. This verse reinforces that chain by excluding the devils. Allah sends, Gabriel conveys, and the Prophet receives and warns. The message does not pass through an adversarial power that could insert falsehood while pretending to deliver truth.

Quran 2:97 likewise connects Gabriel with bringing the Quran to the Prophet's heart. The two passages support one another: the revelation has a named, trustworthy carrier and a divine source. The denial of satanic origin is therefore not a bare assertion. It belongs to a wider account of how revelation reaches the human messenger.

**A False Attribution Cannot Change the Quran's Direction**

People may call a message demonic because it unsettles their desires or exposes their idols. Such language can make the unfamiliar seem dangerous and protect a community from examining its conduct. The verse requires the hearer to test the accusation against the message's actual call.

A genuine spiritual test asks what a teaching does with worship, truth, property, mercy, and accountability. The Quran directs these matters toward Allah and forbids the corruption that devils encourage. The source claim and the moral content therefore meet. Calling the Quran demonic is not an explanation of its origin; it is another attempt to avoid its authority.

The accusation also reverses the moral direction of the Quran. The devils encourage people to divide worship, follow desire, and forget the final account. The Quran repeatedly gathers the heart back to one Lord, commands honest treatment of people, and warns that every choice has a consequence. A person can reject those commands, but he cannot fairly call the call to justice demonic simply because it limits his freedom to harm.

This does not mean that every person who repeats the accusation has understood the Quran's content. Some may inherit the label from a leader or use it because the community uses it. That is why the verse directs attention to the source rather than allowing a slogan to become evidence. Claims about the unseen must be tested by revelation and trustworthy transmission, not by the fear a word can create.

The Quran's opposition to satanic influence also includes its treatment of the human ego. It exposes pride, false gods, envy, and the wish to escape judgment. Those exposures can feel hostile to the person who wants to protect them. The discomfort is not proof of a demonic origin. A medicine can hurt a diseased place while healing it, and a warning can unsettle a corrupt habit while saving the person who accepts it.

The verse therefore asks the hearer to distinguish between a message and its misuse. If a preacher invokes the Quran to justify cruelty, the cruelty should be rejected, but the Quran should not be blamed for what it forbids. The source claim remains: this revelation comes through a trustworthy chain from Allah, not from the devils whose work it unmasks.

The safest response to the accusation is disciplined examination. Ask what the Quran calls people to worship, what it demands in their dealings, and whether its guidance leads toward truth and repentance. Once those features are considered, the claim that devils brought it down is exposed as a charge made against the message's authority rather than a serious account of its origin.

The accusation can also arise when a message challenges the idols a society has built around money, ancestry, or authority. A person who benefits from those idols may describe their exposure as dangerous and then assign the danger to a demon. The label protects the idol by making the warning appear impure. The Quran reverses that protection by naming the actual sources of misguidance: pride, false worship, injustice, and the desire to avoid accountability.

The difference between revelation and satanic influence can be seen in what each asks the servant to do when no audience is present. A satanic whisper urges concealment and self-importance. The Quran calls the person to honesty before Allah, even when honesty costs reputation or profit. A corrupt person may misuse the Quran publicly, but the message itself keeps returning him to the Lord's knowledge and judgment.

This verse is also a safeguard for the Prophet. His opponents may describe the revelation with whatever hostile name they choose, but their name cannot alter the chain already established. Allah is the source, Gabriel is trustworthy, and the Quran's direction is toward guidance. The believer need not answer every insult with anger; he can answer it by understanding the message and living its truth.

The right conclusion is not that every unseen claim should be feared. It is that unseen claims require revelation and careful discernment. The Quran identifies the devils' limit so that people do not confuse whispering with guidance. Whoever seeks Allah's direction should follow the protected revelation rather than voices that flatter desire or excuse wrongdoing.

## Sūrah al-Anʿām 6:163

> He has no partner. So I am commanded, and so I am the first to submit.”

**Expanded Commentary**

**The Denial of Any Partner**

He has no partner. The verse opens with the negation that governs the whole declaration, and the sūrah has built its case on the same negation from its first verses. The audience associated beings with God in worship, consulted the jinn, divided their harvests between God and their idols, and attributed offspring to Him, and the sentence in front of us states the denial in its barest form. The Book's statement of God's unity elsewhere gives the same negation in the sūrah of Sincerity, where God is declared one and indivisible and where the denial of offspring follows (112:1–3).

The placement of the negation inside the declaration of dedication gives the two halves of the speech their connection. The Prophet has said that his prayer, his worship, his life, and his death belong to God, and the sentence that follows states why no other being could share in that dedication. The two verses together present the religion as a whole: the worshipper is devoted to the One who has no partner.

**The Command and the Response**

So I am commanded. The sentence states that the Prophet's declaration is not a personal choice but an instruction he has received, and the Book's commands to him elsewhere use the same form. The sūrah of the Troops tells him to say that he is commanded to worship God, being sincerely devoted to Him alone, and that he is commanded to be the first of those who submit (39:11–12); the sūrah of the Cattle itself has placed a series of commands before him in its earlier verses, from following what is revealed (6:106) to declaring that God's argument is conclusive (6:149).

The phrase describes the Prophet's relation to the revelation in the plainest terms. He does not choose the religion he follows, and the declaration he makes is the content of what has been sent down to him. The Book's account of the messenger's position in this sūrah has been consistent on that point, and the sentence in front of us states it at the close of the chapter's teaching.

**The First to Submit**

And so I am the first to submit. The Book's word for submission describes the surrender of the person to God's command, and the religion itself is given that name in other passages. The sūrah of the Family of Imran states that God's only way is Islam (3:19), and the sūrah of the Table records God's declaration that He has chosen submission as the religion of the community (5:3). The Prophet's statement that he is the first of those who submit places him at the head of the people who accept that religion.

The Book's use of the phrase elsewhere appears in the commands given to the Prophet. The sūrah of the Troops joins the command to be the first of those who submit to the command of sincere worship (39:12), and the sūrah of the Cattle's opening verses record the Prophet's declaration that he is commanded to be the first of those who submit. The precedence is a precedence in time and in commitment, and the community that follows him is described in the Book as the people who submit.

**What the Declaration Answers in the Sūrah**

The three-verse declaration answers the audience's religion in its parts. The guidance declared in the first verse answers the audience's claim to the heritage of Abraham; the dedication of the second answers the division of worship and sacrifice between God and the idols; and the denial of partnership in this verse answers the association that the sūrah has described in its accounts of the jinn, the idols, and the fabricated rules. The declaration is therefore the sūrah's positive statement of what the message requires, set against everything the chapter has examined.

The Book's treatment of the audience's practices in the sūrah has moved from the theological to the practical and back. The chapter began with the argument about God's unity and the signs of His power, examined the audience's dietary and ritual system, and now closes with the Prophet's declaration of the religion in its simplest form. The reader who has followed the argument sees the contrast between the two systems stated in a few lines.

**Submission as the Religion's Name**

The name the verse gives the religion is the one the Book uses for the response of the person who accepts the message. The sūrah of the Cow describes Abraham and Ishmael praying that their descendants be a community submitting to God (2:128 context), and the sūrah of the Family of Imran states that whoever follows a religion other than submission will not have it accepted of him (3:85). The Book's use of the term covers the conduct as well as the confession, since the person who submits is the one who acts on what has been sent down.

The sūrah's audience is therefore addressed as people who are offered a place in that submission. The Prophet declares that he is the first of those who submit, and the Book's commands to the community elsewhere attach the same description to the believers who respond. The verse states the identity the message confers, and it does so in the middle of the declaration that closes the sūrah's teaching.

**The Place of the Verse in the Sūrah's Close**

The verse completes the declaration that began with the Prophet's statement of the guidance he has received (6:161) and continued with the dedication of his prayer, worship, life, and death (6:162). The three verses are a single speech, and the last of them supplies the negation, the basis, and the name that the speech requires. The sūrah's argument with the audience is finished, and the Prophet's own religion has been stated.

The verses that follow turn to the accountability of every person, stating that no one reaps except what he sows and that no soul will bear another's burden, and they close with the ranks in which people have been placed on the earth and with the two attributes that end the sūrah. The declaration in front of us supplies the Prophet's own position, and the closing verses state the terms on which everyone else is judged.

**The Transition to the Next Verse**

The next verse gives the Prophet the question about whether he should seek a lord other than God while God is the Lord of everything, and it states that no one reaps except what he sows, that no soul bears another's burden, and that all people will return to their Lord and be informed of their differences (6:164). The passage turns from the Prophet's declaration to the accountability of the audience, and the two themes are joined by the statement about the Lord of everything.

The sūrah then closes with the verse about the successors placed on the earth and the ranks in which they have been set, and with the declaration that the Lord is swift in punishment and All-Forgiving, Most Merciful (6:165). The verse in front of us completes the Prophet's declaration, and the reader is left with the message in its final form: one Lord, one worship, and a submission that the messenger himself has embraced.

## Sūrah an-Nūr 24:8

> For her to be spared the punishment, she must swear four times by Allah that he is telling a lie,

**Expanded Commentary**

**The Parity of Defense: Averting the Statutory Punishment**

The revelation establishes an immediate procedural defense for the accused woman: *wa-yadraʾu ʿanhā al-ʿadhāba an tashhada arbaʿa shahādātin bi-Allāh*. The verb *yadraʾu* signifies repelling, warding off, or deflecting an impending blow. Once the husband completes his five solemn oaths, a grave legal presumption arises against the wife. In many ancient and contemporary legal traditions, a husband's formal accusation was sufficient to condemn a woman without granting her equal recourse.

Divine law rejects this unilateral imbalance. The wife is endowed with autonomous legal capacity to neutralize the husband's accusation entirely through her own testimony. By swearing four times by the name of God, she deflects the punishment that would otherwise follow. This structure guarantees that no woman can be convicted, disenfranchised, or penalized based solely upon the sworn assertions of her husband, establishing absolute procedural fairness and moral equity within the courtroom, protecting the vulnerable against unilateral power and arbitrary condemnation. The statute affirms her independent legal personhood before the court of law.

**The Symmetry of Four Oaths of Denial**

The legal architecture constructed here exhibits precise numerical and evidentiary symmetry. The husband swore four times that he was speaking the truth; the wife now swears four times that he is a liar: *innahū lamina al-kādhibīn*. Four positive assertions of misconduct are met and cancelled by four negative assertions of innocence.

In classical Islamic evidentiary jurisprudence, the wife's sworn word carries legal power identical to that of the husband. Her testimony is not discounted on the basis of gender or social standing. Each of her four oaths systematically neutralizes one of his four oaths. This remarkable balance emphasizes that when private marital disputes enter the public courtroom, both spouses stand as equal moral agents before the divine law, each possessing the exact same power to make sworn representations before the judicial assembly, demonstrating the impartial nature of divine justice across all strata of society.

**The Definition of al-ʿadhāb in the Context of Liʿān**

Classical jurists engaged in thorough analysis regarding the precise nature of the punishment (*al-ʿadhāb*) that the wife's oaths avert. The majority of scholars, including Imām al-Shāfiʿī, Imām Mālik, and Imām Aḥmad, held that *al-ʿadhāb* refers to the statutory corporal punishment for adultery established in 24:2. If the husband has completed his oaths and the wife refuses to swear her counter-oaths, she is deemed to have implicitly conceded the accusation, thereby facing the prescribed penalty.

Conversely, Imām Abū Ḥanīfah and his disciples argued that she is not subjected to corporal punishment upon refusal, but rather detained by the judicial authorities until she either takes the oaths of *liʿān* or admits the truth of the charge. This disagreement underscores the extreme reluctance of classical jurists to implement corporal penalties in the presence of evidentiary doubt, demonstrating how both views sought to honor the sanctity of human life and bodily integrity, avoiding punitive excess whenever ambiguity remains in the judicial record of the case.

**The Burden of Public Counter-Testimony**

Taking these four oaths imposes a profound psychological and emotional burden upon the accused woman. Standing before the assembled community in the central mosque, under the watchful gaze of the judge and fellow believers, she must voice her denial with unwavering conviction. This public visibility ensures that her vindication is as open, decisive, and recorded as her husband's accusation.

The law does not permit her exoneration to occur behind closed doors or through private compromises that might leave her reputation tainted by lingering whispers. By taking the witness stand publicly and invoking the supreme name of God four times, she asserts her honor in the most transparent forum available, compelling the entire community to recognize that the charge has been legally and spiritually repelled. Her voice resounds with full legal authority across the sacred precinct, clearing her name in the eyes of all observers and recording her complete innocence.

**The Rejection of Coerced Submission and Domestic Tyranny**

This statutory defense serves as a powerful bulwark against spousal abuse and domestic extortion. In patriarchal societies, unscrupulous men might employ false accusations to coerce a wife into forfeiting her dowry (*mahr*), relinquishing custody of her children, or submitting to intolerable marital conditions. Without the procedural protection of verse 8, a husband could weaponize the legal system to ruin his wife without fear of consequence.

By granting the woman the unilateral power to ward off punishment through her own sworn testimony, the Qur'an dismantles the husband's ability to act as judge and jury over her fate. Even if the husband is wealthy, influential, and socially prominent, his status cannot override her sworn denial. Divine law ensures that an innocent woman retains full sovereignty over her legal defense, protected from domestic intimidation and institutional coercion, affirming her god-given dignity within the marital framework.

**The Neutralization of Earthly Penalties**

When the wife completes her four oaths, an absolute evidentiary equilibrium is achieved. Human judges possess no divine insight to determine with absolute certainty which spouse spoke the truth and which spouse committed perjury. The competing sets of oaths effectively cancel each other out in the realm of tangible evidence.

In accordance with the foundational legal principle that penalties must be averted whenever doubt exists, the court refrains from punishing either party. The husband cannot be lashed for *qadhf* because he swore his five oaths, and the wife cannot be punished for adultery because she swore her four counter-oaths. Earthly justice gracefully bows before the limits of human knowledge, leaving the final moral reckoning to the eternal judgment of God, preserving the peace of the community without spilling blood upon unproven claims, resting securely in divine omniscience.

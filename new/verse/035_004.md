## Sūrah Fāṭir 35:4

> If you are rejected by them, so too were messengers before you. And to Allah ˹all˺ matters will be returned ˹for judgment˺.

**Expanded Commentary**

**Rejection Does Not Establish Falsehood**

The verse speaks to the Prophet in the second person, yet its consolation reaches every bearer of truth who encounters denial. Rejection can feel like a judgment on the message, especially when it is repeated by a whole community. This sentence breaks that false connection. Earlier messengers were also rejected, so public refusal is not a test by which revelation is measured. A crowd's agreement can describe a social condition; it cannot decide what is true before Allah.

The reminder is practical rather than sentimental. It does not promise that opposition will quickly disappear or that a messenger will be spared grief. It places the present pain within a prophetic pattern. Noah, Hūd, Ṣāliḥ, Abraham, Moses, and others faced denial in different forms. Their experiences show that rejection belongs to the history of mission, not to the definition of failure. The Prophet is therefore called to continue conveying the message without taking the deniers' response as proof that the message has lost its force.

**The Past Makes Patience Reasonable**

Qur'anic stories turn memory into moral support. The earlier messengers did not merely suffer; they endured until Allah's help came, as stated in 6:34. That help could appear through rescue, the spread of the message, the exposure of falsehood, or the final judgment. Patience does not mean that injustice is approved. It means that the servant refuses to let an immediate setback dictate the meaning of the whole mission.

The command to remain patient also protects the messenger from answering rejection with a corrupted form of rejection. A person who is mocked may be tempted to become harsh, vain, or desperate for quick results. The prophetic model holds firm to truth while leaving outcomes to Allah. This is why the Qur'an repeatedly tells the Prophet that his duty is clear conveyance, not control of hearts. The message must be spoken faithfully; the response belongs to those who hear it, and the final accounting belongs to the Lord.

**Every Matter Returns to Its Proper Judge**

The closing movement from denial to return changes the horizon. Matters do not end at the point where an audience says no. They return to Allah, which means that hidden motives, damaged reputations, and unrecognized acts of fidelity all enter a judgment beyond human reach. The Qur'an uses this expression in contexts of creation, conflict, and legal decision, and each time it denies the illusion that history is self-interpreting.

For the rejected messenger, the return means that Allah will vindicate the truth and deal with those who denied it. For the deniers, it means that words treated as harmless will be weighed with their causes and consequences. The verse does not invite the messenger to pursue personal revenge. It relieves him of that burden. Allah's judgment is more complete than any public rebuttal because He knows what each person heard, understood, concealed, and chose.

**The Prophetic Mission Is Measured by Fidelity**

A messenger's success is not defined by the number of immediate converts. The Qur'an records prophets whose communities were small or whose warnings were rejected for long periods. Their honor came from carrying out the trust placed upon them. This does not make results irrelevant; guidance is a real mercy and a prophet longs for people to receive it. But the servant's responsibility concerns truthful delivery, sound conduct, and perseverance rather than the manufacture of acceptance.

That distinction also applies to ordinary believers. A parent may teach without being able to compel, a scholar may explain without controlling the listener, and a witness may speak while facing hostility. The fourth verse gives them a prophetic measure: rejection by others does not cancel the duty to stand for what is right. It also gives them a limit: they are not responsible for occupying Allah's place as judge of hearts.

The messenger's certainty is therefore not tied to immediate applause. A truth may be resisted for a season while it continues to shape lives and expose falsehood. Delay tests the patience of the truthful, but it never transfers authority to the deniers. A postponed judgment is not an absent judgment; it is a judgment whose appointed time has not arrived.

**Hope Rests on Divine Resolution**

The final phrase is a promise of resolution without specifying its timetable. Allah's return of all matters may occur through changes in this life, but its fullest meaning belongs to the Hereafter. The promise reaches beyond the lifespan of one generation, so a messenger need not measure truth by the fortunes of a single moment. The delay is itself part of the test. If every false claim were overturned at once, trust, patience, and moral choice would lose their place. The believer acts in the interval while knowing that no interval can cancel the final account.

 The Qur'an states that the messengers and believers are aided in the life of this world and on the day witnesses stand (40:51). Such aid is not always identical with comfort; sometimes it is steadfastness, clarity, or protection from compromise.

A person who holds this promise can grieve without collapsing and oppose falsehood without hatred ruling the heart. Denial is serious, but it is not sovereign. The rejected message is not abandoned in the court of public opinion. It is carried toward Allah, where no fact is lost and no injustice survives unexamined. The verse therefore turns a moment of apparent defeat into a lesson in patient fidelity: speak the truth, endure its rejection, and leave the final verdict to the One to whom every matter returns.

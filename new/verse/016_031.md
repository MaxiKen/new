## Sūrah al-Naḥl 16:31

> **the Gardens of Eternity which they will enter, under which rivers flow. In it they will have whatever they desire. This is how Allah rewards the righteous—**

**Expanded Commentary**

**Gardens Of Eternity**

Jannātu ʿadnin yadkhulūnahā tajrī min taḥtihā al-anhāru — Gardens of Eternity which they will enter, under which rivers flow. Jannāt is plural of jannah, garden, Paradise. ʿAdn means eternity, permanence, residence, from ʿ-d-n, to remain, to be fixed. Gardens of Eternity are gardens where residence is permanent, not temporary. Yadkhulūnahā, they will enter, indicates future entry as reward. Tajrī min taḥtihā al-anhāru, under which rivers flow, is a phrase repeated dozens of times in Qur'an to describe Paradise, indicating abundance of water, coolness, greenery, and continuous provision. Al-Ṭabarī explains that rivers flowing beneath gardens means beneath their trees and palaces, visible, accessible, providing beauty and drink. The phrase emphasizes that Paradise is not dry or barren but lush, with water that does not cease. The gardens are for muttaqīn, righteous, whose home was described as excellent in previous verse.

**Whatever They Desire**

Lahum fīhā mā yashā'ūn — in it they will have whatever they desire, whatever they wish. Mā yashā'ūn means whatever they will, desire, want. This is a comprehensive promise: in Paradise, desires are fulfilled without restriction, unlike world where desires often exceed ability or are prohibited. Al-Qurṭubī notes that this includes both what they desired in world and what they will desire in Paradise, and that their desires themselves will be purified, so they will not desire anything harmful. The phrase appears in several descriptions of Paradise, as in 41:31: *You will have therein whatever your souls desire* (41:31). Al-Rāzī explains that having whatever one desires is the ultimate freedom, because in world humans are constrained by need, law, and inability, while in Paradise constraints are removed for righteous, yet their will is aligned with good, so they desire only good.

**How Allah Rewards The Righteous**

Kadhālika yajzī Allāhu al-muttaqīn — thus does Allah reward the righteous, the mindful. Kadhālika means like this, thus, in this way. Yajzī means rewards, recompense. Al-muttaqīn, those mindful of Allah, those who guarded themselves by obedience. The phrase indicates that Gardens described are how Allah rewards taqwa. It is not arbitrary; it is fitting recompense: because they were mindful of Allah in world, guarded themselves from shirk and sin, Allah rewards with Gardens where they have whatever they desire. Al-Ṭabarī says kadhālika refers to reward described: Gardens with rivers, fulfillment of desires, as reward for taqwa. The verse ends with dash in translation, indicating that next verse continues description: those whose souls angels take while virtuous.

**Why Eternity Matters**

ʿAdn, eternity, is stressed because worldly gardens are temporary: they wither, need maintenance, can be lost. Gardens of Eternity do not wither, do not need maintenance that tires, and are not lost. Al-Zamakhsharī notes that ʿadn also implies residence that is settled, not traveling, so inhabitants feel at home, not as visitors. This contrasts with worldly life which is travel, as Prophet ﷺ said *Be in world as stranger or traveler* (Bukhārī 6416). In world, even excellent home is temporary; in Hereafter, excellent home is eternal. The promise of eternity also answers those who said life is only worldly and there is no return. It says there is return and it is eternal, so choices in temporary world have eternal consequences.

**The Connection To Earlier Blessings**

Gardens with rivers flowing recall earlier blessings: rain from sky, rivers on earth, crops, fruits. In world, those blessings are signs and temporary provision. In Paradise, they become permanent reward, perfected. Rivers in world can flood or dry; rivers in Paradise flow continuously without harm. Fruits in world are seasonal; fruits in Paradise are always available, as 13:35 says *Its fruit is permanent and its shade* (13:35). Whatever they desire in Paradise includes best of what they had in world, purified and increased. The sūrah thus moves from worldly blessings that are uncountable but temporary to otherworldly blessings that are eternal, showing that gratitude for worldly blessings leads to eternal reward.

**Linguistic Note On ʿAdn And Jazā'**

ʿAdn from ʿ-d-n means to remain, to be fixed, to stay, also used for mine, maʿdin, because minerals remain in earth. Jannātu ʿadnin therefore means Gardens of permanence, residence. Yadkhulūnahā from d-kh-l means to enter, with plural indicating group entry, not solitary. Tajrī from j-r-y means to flow, run. Anhār plural of nahr, river. Mā yashā'ūn from sh-y-ʾ means whatever they will, wish. Yajzī from j-z-y means to reward, recompense, from same root as jazā', reward. Al-muttaqīn from w-q-y means those who guard, mindful. The verse's structure — Gardens, entry, rivers, desires, reward — moves from place to action to provision to wish to recompense, creating a complete picture of reward that is both objective (Gardens, rivers) and subjective (whatever they desire), both gift and fitting reward for taqwa.

**Why Rivers Flowing Is Repeated**

The phrase under which rivers flow appears dozens of times in Qur'an as anchor of imagination. Water in desert is life, coolness, beauty, and its flow indicates continuity. A garden with river that stops would disappoint; a garden where rivers flow continuously is secure. Al-Rāzī notes that rivers flowing beneath indicates inhabitants are above rivers, in elevated position, looking down at flowing water, a scene of honor. The rivers are of different types: water, milk, honey, wine that does not intoxicate, as 47:15 says. The diversity matches diversity of desires. Repetition is not filler but reminder that Paradise provision is flowing, not stagnant, always fresh.

**Desire Fulfilled As Ultimate Freedom**

Having whatever they desire is ultimate freedom because in world desires are constrained by inability, prohibition, and consequence. In Paradise, constraints are removed for righteous whose desires have been purified by taqwa. Al-Qurṭubī explains that they will not desire harmful because hearts purified, as 15:47 says. So fulfillment does not lead to excess; it leads to contentment. The phrase mā yashā'ūn indicates desires granted as they arise, without delay, without work. This is opposite of world where desire requires effort and may not be fulfilled. The promise is both about provision and will: your will, frustrated in world, will be fulfilled in Paradise as reward for aligning will with Allah's will through taqwa.

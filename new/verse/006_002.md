## Sūrah al-Anʿām 6:2

> He is the One Who created you from clay, then appointed a term ˹for your death˺ and another known only to Him ˹for your resurrection˺ —yet you continue to doubt!

**Expanded Commentary**

**Two Terms Standing in One Sentence**

The verse distinguishes two appointments. The first is decreed and named as such; the second is said to rest with God alone, known to Him. Most commentators read the first as a person's earthly lifespan and the second as the moment of gathering and reckoning, though some take the second as a further description of the first. Cross-references settle the pattern: God calls back souls by night and revives their owners daily "to complete your appointed term" (6:60); the term for a community cannot be moved forward or delayed by anyone (7:34); and no soul dies except by God's will at the time written for it (3:145).

Read as two terms, the verse maps a life in two directions at once: it ends, and afterwards it is answered for. Its audience had no quarrel with the first claim. Every family buried its dead.

**From Mud to a Fixed Calendar**

Adam is described as formed from clay more than a dozen times in the Book, and the material is named in several ways: sounding clay moulded from black mud (15:26), clay shaped like pottery (55:14), an extract of clay (23:12). Whatever the images change, the substance stays lowly and comes from the ground under the audience's feet. The verse puts that dust and a schedule in one breath. What is kneaded out of a riverbank does not normally receive an appointment at court, and yet the vase holds a date it did not choose.

The pairing is the argument. A maker who set the terms for a pot may be trusted to know when the pot is finished. If the same hand shaped the body and fixed the span, then the One who appointed the first term has both the right and the ability to appoint the second.

**The Right to Keep a Date Secret**

"Another known only to Him" is not a gap in divine knowledge but a decision about human access. The hour of death is hidden from the person carrying it; the day of return is hidden from the whole generation. The Book returns to that concealment often, and treats it as a structure rather than a cruelty: if a person knew the date, the years before it would turn into accounting, and virtue into arithmetic. A merchant told his bankruptcy day would behave differently on the last month.

Cross-references place the hidden date beside the unavoidable one. Term after term is said to be written, immovable, and unannounced: for each community there is an appointed term, and when it comes nobody can delay it a moment (10:49); God never delays a soul when its appointed time arrives (63:11). The certainty of the first term is what makes the secrecy of the second a test instead of a riddle.

**Doubt as an Ongoing Verb**

The verse ends with a rebuke in the present tense: they keep doubting. The Arabic verb for that doubt is imperfect, a form that describes an action still in progress rather than a single episode of unbelief. The choice fits the argument. These listeners never denied that a person dies or that a person was formed; they doubted that the forming will happen a second time, and they doubted it continuously, the way a person keeps a suspicion running in the background of other work.

Doubt of that shape is not confusion, because confusion ends when the evidence arrives. It is a position maintained against evidence not yet considered. The verse, having conceded that the first term is written on every tombstone, asks why a Maker who has already delivered one thing these people accept should be disbelieved about a second.

**The Certain Term and the Hour That Refuses to Be Read**

Two facts about death are stated together in later passages and hold the verse's structure in place. Death will overcome a person wherever he is, "even if you were in fortified towers" (4:78), so the first term is not negotiable and no wall deflects it. The knowledge of the Hour, by contrast, is reserved: God knows it, and no soul knows what it will earn tomorrow or in what land it will die (31:34). That pairing — a guaranteed end with an unreadable date — is the working assumption of a serious life, and every soul will taste death (3:185) without being told the morning.

People build substitutes for the hidden date: a healthy body, a strong clan, a full granary. The verse refuses every substitute by naming the Maker of the body and the appointer of the term in one clause. The only calendar a person truly owns is the present, which is why the sūrah moves next to what people do with the signs they can already read.

**Why the Argument Begins With the Lowest Material**

Scripture could have opened the case for resurrection with power over the sky. Instead it opens with a person's own beginning, and with the least impressive fact about him. The rhetorical advantage is considerable: an audience asked to grant life after death from dust is only being asked to repeat what it already grants to this life from the same dust. There is no second workshop and no apprentice; the creation was the proof that the resurrection is not a new kind of task.

The Book makes the same move where it argues that the One who sprouts the seed can raise the dead (6:95), and where the earth is revived and said to be evidence that people will be brought out of their graves (30:19). A farmer watching a field is watching the doctrine, in slow motion, every year.

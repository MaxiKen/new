## Sūrah al-Nisāʾ 4:26

> It is Allah’s Will to make things clear to you, guide you to the ˹noble˺ ways of those before you, and turn to you in mercy. For Allah is AllKnowing, All-Wise.

### Expanded Commentary

**Law as clarification**

The verse explains why these marriage regulations have been revealed: Allah wishes to make the lawful and unlawful clear, guide believers through the patterns of earlier peoples, and turn toward them in mercy. This answer matters because legal detail can feel restrictive when isolated from purpose. Revelation clarifies in order to protect life, lineage, consent, property, and worship—not to multiply burdens for their own sake.

**Earlier communities as moral evidence**

The phrase about the ways of those before refers to the continuity of divine guidance and to the lessons found in earlier religious communities. It does not mean that every historical practice is automatically authoritative. Earlier revelation and prophetic example show how God’s servants sought to organize marriage and family, while the Qur’an confirms, corrects, and completes the rule for its own community.

**Repentance is built into legislation**

The promise that Allah turns to believers in mercy prevents the law from being read as a closed door for people who have lived in confusion. A person may have inherited a harmful custom, entered an unlawful relationship, or failed to understand a right. Once the guidance becomes clear, repentance means stopping the wrong, regularizing what can be regularized, and repairing harm. Mercy does not abolish accountability; it makes reform possible.

The verse is also an interpretive guide: when a legal command is difficult, seek the protected good it serves. That search does not allow a person to invent exceptions, but it does guard against a mechanical application that defeats the very welfare the law was meant to secure. Clarity and mercy are presented together because divine law is both intelligible and compassionate.

**Clarity is a mercy to the confused**

When a community explains its family law honestly, people can leave inherited abuse without guessing whether reform is allowed. Scholars should therefore distinguish settled principles from disputed applications and state the evidence for both. Clarity prevents the powerful from using complexity as a fog around another person’s rights.

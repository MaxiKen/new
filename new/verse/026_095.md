## Sūrah al-Shuʿarāʾ 26:95

> and the soldiers of Iblîs, all together.

**Expanded Commentary**

**The Judgment Includes the Hosts of Iblîs**

The verse adds the soldiers of Iblîs to the group gathered for punishment. The word soldiers suggests organized allegiance and service. Iblîs is not pictured as acting alone in the work of misguidance. He has followers who carry his aims into human and unseen life. Their power is derivative, but their choices make them responsible for the side on which they stood.

The phrase does not require the soldiers to be a formal army with worldly ranks. It describes those who follow Iblîs as agents of rebellion, spreading falsehood, pride, temptation, and disobedience. Some may be unseen beings, while the source tradition also allows human followers to be included. What unites them is not species but loyalty.

**A Soldier Is Defined by Service**

Calling them soldiers focuses on what they do. A person can serve Iblîs without using military language, simply by defending false worship, beautifying sin, or leading others away from God. The title names function rather than costume. It asks who benefits from the work a person performs and whose purposes that work advances.

This prevents the believer from imagining that evil belongs only to dramatic villains. A small act that normalizes arrogance can serve a larger rebellion. A persuasive voice that makes idolatry attractive can become part of the same host. Responsibility is measured not only by intention as claimed but also by the allegiance expressed through repeated action.

**The Hosts Are Not Equal to God's Power**

The mention of Iblîs and his soldiers does not establish a rival kingdom equal to God's. The hosts are creatures, and their influence remains limited. They can invite, deceive, and organize wrongdoing, but they cannot create, compel, or escape God's judgment. Their apparent reach in the world never becomes independent sovereignty.

This matters because fear of evil can itself become a form of misplaced power. Believers should seek protection from temptation and resist its agents, but they must not treat Iblîs as a second lord. The scene shows the end of his host as a gathered group under judgment. The one who promised influence cannot save those who served him.

**All Together Removes the Illusion of Private Evil**

The phrase all together gives the judgment a collective dimension. False worship, the deviant, and the soldiers of Iblîs are not scattered into separate stories that allow each group to deny connection. Their loyalties are brought into one scene. The community of rebellion is seen as a community, and its shared direction becomes undeniable.

Collective judgment does not erase individual differences. Each servant remains accountable for his own choices. Yet choices also create networks. Leaders influence followers, followers strengthen leaders, and idols give public form to false allegiance. The gathering reveals how private acts contributed to a wider order opposed to God.

**Iblîs' Followers Can Include Human Beings**

The source tradition notes that the soldiers can be understood as jinn alone or as both jinn and human beings. The broader reading fits the Qur'anic use of satanic language for those who spread deception among people. Human beings are not excused by saying that an unseen tempter exists. They become soldiers when they willingly carry the work of misguidance.

This distinction preserves responsibility. Temptation may be offered, but service is chosen. A person who manipulates, corrupts, or calls others to false worship cannot place every blame on Iblîs. The title soldiers describes participation. The human follower has made a willful alliance with a direction that opposes the Lord.

**A Parallel Describes the Devil as a Companion**

Quran 43:36 describes the one who turns blindly away from the Reminder as being assigned a devilish companion. That parallel helps explain how a soldier of Iblîs can be formed. Repeated refusal creates an opening for influence, and influence becomes familiar company. The relationship is not an innocent accident; it grows through turning away from remembrance.

The present verse shows the collective end of that companionship. What felt private or ordinary in the world is exposed as part of a wider service to Iblîs. The believer therefore protects the heart early, before a temptation becomes a habit and a habit becomes an allegiance.

**The Gathering Makes False Leadership Visible**

Iblîs is associated with command, but his soldiers are not honored for obeying him. Their obedience is judged because it opposed God's command. The verse teaches that following a leader does not remove responsibility. A person cannot say that he was only serving orders when the order was a call to disbelief or corruption.

This applies beyond open religious rebellion. Any leader who asks people to treat injustice as sacred is recruiting a host. The wise servant measures commands by God's truth rather than by the power of the commander. The soldiers of Iblîs are a warning that disciplined service can still be evil when its object is evil.

**All Together Is a Picture of Failed Solidarity**

Falsehood can create strong bonds. Idolaters share rituals, leaders gather supporters, and tempters encourage one another. Their unity may look impressive in the world. The phrase all together shows that unity alone is not salvation. A group can be coordinated and still be moving toward judgment.

True community is not defined by numbers or organization but by the direction of worship. The soldiers of Iblîs are gathered because their unity served rebellion. The righteous are gathered by God because their unity served truth. The verse warns believers not to admire collective strength without asking what that strength is doing.

**The End of a False Army Is Submission to Judgment**

The soldiers of Iblîs may have imagined themselves agents of freedom, but their final position is one of helpless gathering. They cannot disperse, negotiate, or make their allegiance disappear. Their service has reached its end, and the Lord alone decides their fate.

The warning is also an invitation to withdraw before that end. A person who recognizes that he has been serving pride, false worship, or corruption can repent while life remains. The Lord's judgment gathers the persistent soldiers; God's mercy welcomes the servant who abandons the army and returns to Him.

## Sūrah an-Nūr 24:52

> For whoever obeys Allah and His Messenger, and fears Allah and is mindful of Him, then it is they who will ˹truly˺ triumph.

**Expanded Commentary**

**The Quadruple Matrix of Salvation: wa-man yuṭiʿi Allāha wa-rasūlah**

The verse synthesizes the core requirements of spiritual deliverance into a concise fourfold architecture: *wa-man yuṭiʿi Allāha wa-rasūlahū wa-yakhsha Allāha wa-yattaqhi fa-ulāʾika humu al-fāʾizūn*. This divine formula unites exterior bodily conduct with interior spiritual states in seamless harmony.

The first two components focus on exterior action: obeying Allah (*yuṭiʿi Allāh*) by executing His divine legislation, and obeying His Messenger (*wa-rasūlah*) by adhering to prophetic guidance and submitting unconditionally to his judicial authority. Obedience is not an abstract sentiment; it requires concrete physical actions—establishing regular prayers, paying zakāh, lowering the gaze, maintaining marital chastity, and honoring legal verdicts without evasion. True religion begins with the active alignment of the human will and bodily limbs with the revealed commandments of God and His Prophet, ensuring that faith translates into observable righteous conduct that purifies the individual and stabilizes the entire community.

**The Subtle Distinction Between Khashyah and Taqwā**

The second pair of conditions addresses the internal spiritual realities of the soul: *wa-yakhsha Allāha wa-yattaqh*, and fears Allah and is mindful of Him. Classical Arabic lexicographers and theologians meticulously distinguished between *khashyah* and *taqwā*.

*Khashyah* is awe-inspired dread born of profound knowledge and contemplation of divine majesty, power, and transcendence. It is an intellectual and spiritual trembling that seizes the soul when it perceives the boundless glory of God compared to the utter insignificance of mortal creation. As 35:28 declares: Only those among His servants who possess sacred knowledge truly fear Him (*innamā yakhshā Allāha min ʿibādihi al-ʿulamāʾ*). In contrast, *taqwā* is the active, vigilant erection of a protective shield (*wiqāyah*) between oneself and divine wrath through the avoidance of sins and conscientious fulfillment of obligations. *Khashyah* is the interior emotional awe; *taqwā* is the practical ethical discipline that awe generates across every circumstance of life, preserving the believer from moral ruin.

**Interior Reverence as the Foundation of Exterior Obedience**

The structural sequencing of these four conditions reveals profound pedagogical wisdom. Exterior obedience (*al-ṭāʿah*) without internal fear and mindfulness degenerates into hypocritical theatricality—precisely the disease that crippled the hypocrites exposed in verses 47–50.

A person can perform religious rituals mechanically or submit to a court out of social coercion without possessing a single grain of genuine faith or sincere devotion. Conversely, internal feelings of awe without concrete bodily obedience are mere self-delusional spiritual sentimentalism that produces no fruit. By interweaving exterior obedience to God and the Messenger with interior awe (*khashyah*) and vigilant mindfulness (*taqwā*), the Qur'an constructs an integrated spiritual organism. Awe in the heart fuels obedience in the limbs, while disciplined bodily obedience strengthens, purifies, and refines the interior reverence of the heart, shielding the servant from moral collapse.

**The Concept of Ultimate Triumph: al-fāʾizūn**

The verse concludes with an absolute eschatological guarantee: *fa-ulāʾika humu al-fāʾizūn*, then it is they who will truly triumph. The noun *fāʾizūn* derives from *fawz*, which designates the supreme achievement of deliverance from peril and the permanent attainment of eternal joy.

In Qur'anic theology, *fawz* is fundamentally distinct from worldly victory. An earthly victor may conquer territories, amass fortunes, or win court cases through deceit, only to be dragged into eternal ruin upon drawing their final breath. The true victors (*al-fāʾizūn*) are those who are saved from the terrors of the grave, acquitted at the cosmic balance scales on Judgment Day, shielded from the blazing fire of Jahannam, and granted eternal residence in the gardens of divine delight, surrounded by peace and angels. The syntax employs the demonstrative *ulāʾik* and isolating *hum*, affirming that genuine victory is the exclusive domain of those who unite obedience with taqwā, basking in divine pleasure forever.

**Theological Synthesis: Love, Fear, and Hope**

Classical scholars of Islamic spirituality, such as Ibn al-Qayyim and al-Ghazālī, observed that verse 52 provides the complete equilibrium needed to maintain psychological and spiritual health. Spiritual pathfinders often illustrate faith as a soaring bird whose head is the love of God, and whose two wings are fear (*khawf* / *khashyah*) and hope (*rajāʾ* / *fawz*).

If a soul relies solely on fear without hope, it collapses into paralyzing despair; if it relies solely on hope without fear, it drifts into reckless antinomian complacency and spiritual laziness. Verse 52 maintains perfect balance. The mention of *khashyah* and *taqwā* instills vigilance, sobriety, and a healthy dread of falling into divine disfavor; simultaneously, the triumphant promise of *al-fāʾizūn* fills the believer's horizon with boundless hope, joy, and anticipation of divine generosity, propelling the servant forward with serene confidence across all earthly trials, running toward the mercy of their Lord.

**Transcending the Fleeting Victories of Mortal Life**

In the immediate context of Sūrah an-Nūr, this verse delivers a direct challenge to the superficial metrics of the Madinan marketplace. The hypocrites imagined that evading prophetic arbitration was a victory, boasting among their peers that they had protected their money and outsmarted the law.

Verse 52 shatters this childish illusion, redefining the concept of victory for all time. Escaping a courtroom fine by lying is not a victory; it is an eternal catastrophe that destroys the soul. The only triumph that deserves celebration is the victory that endures after the stars are extinguished and the universe is rolled up like a scroll. Those who sacrifice material wealth, social status, and personal pride to obey God and His Messenger will find their humble sacrifices transformed into radiant crowns of glory on the Day of Resurrection, reigning as the eternal champions of creation, welcomed into gardens beneath which rivers flow, abiding therein in perpetual bliss, unshakeable serenity, and eternal divine approval.

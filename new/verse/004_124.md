## Sūrah al-Nisāʾ 4:124

> But those who do good— whether male or female—and have faith will enter Paradise and will never be wronged ˹even as much as˺ the speck on a date stone.

**Expanded Commentary**

**Those Who Do Good — Male or Female — With Faith**

The verse states *wa man yaʿmal mina al-ṣāliḥāti min dhakarin aw unthā wa huwa muʾminun fa ulāʾika yadkhulūna al-jannata* — but those who do, who act, righteous deeds from among good acts — whether male or female — and are believers, while being faithful — will enter Paradise. *Man yaʿmal mina al-ṣāliḥāt* — whoever does righteous deeds — was explained as the Book’s shorthand for the faithful act: charity, kindness, reconciliation seeking God’s pleasure (4:114), standing firm for justice though against selves (4:135), spending from what God provided not to show off (4:38–40), striving with wealth and lives (4:95) and the good whose reward is multiplied many times over (4:40). *Min dhakarin aw unthā* — whether male or female — was read as the sūrah’s consistent equalisation where the single soul spread into countless men and women (4:1), where shares for men and women are obligatory (4:7, 4:11–12), where marry-able bonds include the believing bondwoman where harm is feared (4:25), and where helpless men, women, children are each named for pardon and for haven (4:98, 4:75). To name both was to remove the wish that grace is by belonging — *neither by your wishes nor those of People of the Book* (4:123) — and to place it on act plus faith. *Wa huwa muʾmin* — while being faithful, and he is a believer — was stressed: good alone without faith is not the entry described, as *“O believers, have faith in God, His Messenger, the Book He sent”* (4:136) commands renewal of faith as basis; the sūrah has shown that association is not forgiven (4:116) because it breaks the faith itself.

**And Will Never Be Wronged Even as Speck on a Date Stone**

The assurance *wa lā yuẓlamūna naqīrā* — and they will never be wronged even as much as the speck, the groove, on a date stone — was explained with *naqīr* — the tiny pit, speck on the stone’s back, the small hollow — the sūrah’s smallest measure. The same measure appeared where envy wishes deviation and God’s kingdom share would not give even a speck (4:53) and where enjoyment of world is little and none will be wronged even by speck (4:77) and where those given understanding do good will not be wronged (4:49). *Lā yuẓlamūna* — never be wronged — was read with the sūrah’s principle *“God does not wrong even an atom; if it is good He multiplies”* (4:40): not wronging is baseline, multiplying is grace. The sūrah has also said that whoever earns sin earns only upon himself (4:111) and whoever commits evil will be rewarded accordingly with no protector besides Him (4:123); here the complement is that whoever does good with faith will not be wronged even to a speck’s weight. The *naqīr* measure was therefore read as atom-plus: the smallest thing the eye sees on the stone’s back, the least deserving is not cut.

**A Verse That Follows Recompense According With Reward Without Wrong**

Placed after *whoever commits evil will be rewarded accordingly and find no protector besides God* (4:123), this verse follows recompense with reward without wrong. The sūrah has moved from two homes — Hell without escape (4:121) and Gardens forever where promise is true (4:122) — through the rule of wishes — neither yours nor theirs (4:123) — to the entry — *they will enter Paradise and not be wronged a speck* (4:124). The symmetry was noted: evil is rewarded *bihī* — with it, exactly — and no *walī* besides Him rescues; good is rewarded into Paradise and not wronged even a speck, with His promise true whose speech none exceeds (4:122). Where the sūrah says *“do not consume wealth unjustly; trade by mutual consent”* (4:29) and says taking dowry unjustly bears sin (4:20), working good with faith is the trade by mutual consent with Him where no wronging occurs. The verse was also placed against the sūrah’s warning not to crave what God gave some over others but to ask of His bounties (4:32): the speck not wronged is the bounty asked and given without envy.

**Male or Female — The Sūrah’s Equality of Entry**

The phrase *min dhakarin aw unthā* was explained as the sūrah’s equality of entry. The single soul created and its mate and spread of men and women (4:1), the command to be mindful of ties (4:1), the inheritance for both (4:7), the dowry protection (4:4), the standing for justice even against yourselves (4:135) — all are instances where male and female share account before the All-Knowing. The verse generalises that sharing to Paradise entry: whether male or female, the condition is one — *and he is believer* — and the act — *does good* — and the result — *will enter Paradise*. Classical notes added that the *min* — from among good deeds — as partitive shows that not all good must be done by each, but whoever does among them with faith enters; the measure *naqīr* shows that even the smallest among them is not wronged. The sūrah has shown that the People of the Book also claim by wish; here female and male alike are neither by wish but by belief and good.

**A Short Verse That Anchors Grace in Act Plus Faith**

Though only *“those who do good male or female and have faith will enter Paradise not wronged a speck,”* the verse anchors grace where wishes were removed. Immediately after: *“who is better in faith than those who fully submit, do good, follow Abraham upright? God chose Abraham as close friend”* (4:125) — the model of submission. Immediately before: *whoever commits evil will be rewarded accordingly* (4:123) — the threat; now the promise with equal precision, secured by *“God does not wrong even an atom”* (4:40). For the weak for whom burdens were lightened — the one who cannot marry free woman may marry believing bondwoman with faith known best to God (4:25) — this is the lightest of entries: belief and a good among goods, from whatever speck you can, and you will not be wronged a speck, because the One whose word none exceeds has promised entry and is the One who created you from a single soul and knows ties.

## Sūrah al-Baqarah 2:129

> Our Lord! Raise from among them a messenger who will recite to them Your revelations, teach them the Book and wisdom, and purify them. Indeed, You ˹alone˺ are the Almighty, AllWise.”

**Expanded Commentary**

**The Final Petition**

The prayer that began with the two founders at the House ends with a request for a messenger from among their descendants. The petition is the last in the series that runs through the previous verses, and it concerns the means by which everything the earlier requests asked for would be delivered.

The request is for a messenger raised from within the community rather than sent from outside it, and the Book notes the same feature when it describes the Prophet ﷺ as a messenger raised among a people who had not previously received a scripture. The prayer therefore asks for what the sūrah will later state was granted. The request also closes the series of petitions with a concern for what would come after the two builders. Their prayers had asked for their own submission, for a community from their line, and for the rites, and the final request supplies the means by which all of those would be maintained.

**The Functions Described**

The petition names three tasks: reciting the revelations, teaching the Book and wisdom, and purifying the people. The three appear in the same order later in the sūrah, where the community is reminded of the favour that a messenger was raised among them to perform exactly these services.

The sequence is instructive. Recitation comes first, which places the delivery of the divine speech before instruction about it, and the teaching of the Book follows the recitation rather than preceding it. The final task concerns the people themselves, since the effect of the message on its hearers is described as a purification, and the Book uses the term for the removal of what corrupts belief and conduct. The three tasks also provide the pattern by which the sūrah later describes the favour granted to the community, since it states that a messenger was raised among them to do exactly these things. The prayer therefore states in advance what the Book presents as its answer.

**The Book and Wisdom**

Commentators identified the Book with the Qur'an, and they understood the term that follows it either as the practice of the Prophet ﷺ or more broadly as understanding of the religion. The Book pairs the two in other passages as well, applying the same combination to Jesus, to the children of Israel, and to the family of Abraham.

The pairing indicates that the community received both a recited text and the knowledge of how it is to be applied, and the Book treats the two as inseparable in the life of a community. The prayer for a messenger therefore included the means by which the revelation would be understood and practised, not only the words themselves.

**Fulfilment of the Prayer**

The sūrah states later that God has sent a messenger from among the community who recites the revelations to them, purifies them, and teaches them the Book and wisdom, using the same language as this petition. The answer to Abraham's prayer is therefore registered in the same chapter, and the argument the sūrah is building for the reader depends on that correspondence.

The Book's presentation is that the messenger came in response to the prayer of the man both communities honoured, and that his coming did not represent a departure from the tradition of the House but its continuation. The reader who has followed the sequence from the purification of the House to this petition is given the conclusion before the argument is stated.

**"From Among Them"**

The petition asks for a messenger from among the community itself, and the Book treats the raising of a messenger from among a people as part of the favour shown to them. The Prophet Muhammad ﷺ was raised among an unlettered people, and the Book describes that as one of the gifts granted to the descendants of Ishmael.

The detail also answers a claim that the sūrah has been addressing, which is that divine guidance should have remained confined to one line or one community. The prayer itself requests the opposite: it asks that the descendants be given a messenger of their own, and the answer to that petition is what the passage has been establishing.

**The Names at the End**

The prayer closes with two names describing God as the Almighty and the All-Wise, and the choices correspond to the content of the petition. The raising of a messenger requires power, and the choice of the messenger and the manner of the teaching belong to wisdom, so the two attributes frame the request with a statement of confidence in the One being asked.

The Book attaches these names to the discussion of prophethood elsewhere, and their presence here indicates the grounds on which the request was made. Abraham and Ishmael asked for a gift that only God could grant, and the names they used were those by which the granting is understood. The two names also match the content of the request, since the raising of a messenger depends on power and the choice of the messenger and his teaching belong to wisdom. The Book commonly closes a petition with the attributes that answer it.

**Closing the Cycle of Prayer**

With this verse the prayers that began at the House are complete: submission, community, rites, acceptance, and a teacher. The sūrah leaves the narrative of building and moves to a statement about the faith of Abraham and to the bequests he and Jacob left to their children, and the transition indicates the purpose of the whole cycle.

The passage has been assembling the case for the religion of Abraham as the Book understands it, and the requests attributed to him and to his son supply the chain from the founding of the House to the coming of the messenger. The verses that follow state the content of the faith in a single line and then present the words by which the two prophets passed it to their descendants.

**A Messenger From Their Own Number**

The petition asks for a messenger raised from among the descendants rather than sent from outside them, and the Book treats that detail as part of the favour granted. The Prophet Muhammad ﷺ was raised among a people who had not previously received a scripture, and the sūrah describes that as one of the gifts given to the line of Ishmael. The request in this verse also answers a claim the sūrah has been examining, since the communities that possessed earlier scriptures might have expected guidance to remain with them. Abraham's prayer requests the opposite arrangement, and the passage presents the messenger's coming as the fulfilment of the petition rather than as a departure from the tradition of the House. What the reader receives is a narrative in which the coming of the final prophet is traced to the request of the man whom both earlier communities claimed, so that the argument rests on their own ancestor's words.

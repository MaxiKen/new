## Sūrah al-Baqarah 2:129

> Our Lord! Raise from among them a messenger who will recite to them Your revelations, teach them the Book and wisdom, and purify them. Indeed, You ˹alone˺ are the Almighty, AllWise.”

**Expanded Commentary**

**A Messenger From Among Them**

The builders' prayer reaches its final petition, and the petition names the community's future guide: *our Lord, raise from among them a messenger*. The preposition carries the prayer's whole ecclesiology — *from among them*, minhum: the messenger requested is not sent at the nation from outside but raised up from within it, of its substance, speaking its tongue, sharing its valley and its House. The request completes the sequence begun two verses back: a submitting nation asked for (128), and now, from that nation's own body, the guide whose office will keep it submitting. The verb — *raise* — is the Qur'an's standing word for prophetic commissioning, and its vertical sense belongs to the petition: the messenger is raised the way the foundations were raised at verse 127, a lifting from within the material, the community's own stock elevated to its guidance-office. The recital's audience heard the petition with its answer in their ears: Sūrah al-Jumuʿah states the fulfillment in near-identical terms — *He is the One Who raised for the illiterate people a messenger from among themselves* (62:2) — the ummī community of the sūrah's own portrait (2:78's illiterate, the nation that neither writes nor calculates, in the Prophet's ﷺ description) receiving the raised messenger the patriarchs asked for. The petition is the recital's bridge across the centuries: the House's builders praying for the House's future congregation's guide, and the guide arriving from among the congregation, in the lineage the prayer named — the answer carried by the son whose co-labor verse 127 had just honored.

**The Three Offices**

The petition defines the messenger's work in three verbs, and the three are the complete prophetic pedagogy. *Who will recite to them Your revelations* — the delivery office: the āyāt brought and voiced, the revelation placed in the community's ears, the same recitation-verb whose cognates the sūrah has used for the Book's own presence among its people. *Teach them the Book and wisdom* — the instruction office: not delivery but formation, the text explained, applied, turned into knowledge the community can operate. *And purify them* — the transformation office: the souls themselves worked on, the tazkiya that makes the taught knowledge livable. The three verbs' sequence is the pedagogy's order: revelation arrives, teaching unpacks it, purification realizes it — and the recital's own answer-verse confirms the sequence by naming the same three functions of the actual Messenger: *We have sent you a messenger from among yourselves — reciting to you Our revelations, purifying you, teaching you the Book and wisdom, and teaching you what you never knew* (2:151). The petition and the answer match function for function, which is the tradition's certainty about this verse: it is the Prophet Muhammad's ﷺ origin-story in prayer-form, his mission requested before his lineage existed, his three offices specified by the patriarchs' tongue. Every carrier of the recitation after him inherits the offices in descending degree — reciting what was delivered, teaching what was explained, seeking the purification the offices exist for — and the verse stands as the community's job-description, written into its founder-grandfather's prayer at the House's foundation-stone.

**The Book and the Wisdom**

The second office's two objects carry a pairing the Qur'an deploys as a fixed formula, and the formula's breadth is the tradition's evidence for its meaning. The Book — *al-kitāb* — is read most often as the Qur'an, the delivered text in its written-recited form. The wisdom — *al-ḥikma* — is understood in the exegetical tradition as the Sunnah: the Prophet's ﷺ exemplary sayings and doings, the Book's embodied commentary — or, more broadly, the knowledge and understanding of the religion the Book's delivery produces (al-Ṭabarī's range). The pairing's recurrence across the Qur'an settles its import: the Book and wisdom are named together as what Jesus received (3:48, 5:110), as what the Children of Israel were given (45:16), as what the House of Abraham received (4:54) — a formula, in the tradition's reading, for revelation's two modes wherever it descends: the text delivered and the understanding given, the word and its realization, the recitation and its application. The pairing also answers, in its structure, the communities the recital has just documented failing: a Book held without wisdom is the Torah rationed and cast behind backs (85, 101) — text preserved, understanding refused; and wisdom claimed without the Book is the desires called guidance (120). The petition asks for both offices in one messenger because the community's health requires both hold — and the answer's history is the tradition's demonstration: the Book memorized in ten thousand chests, the wisdom recorded in the Sunnah's corpus, the pairing maintained as the two hands of one inheritance.

**The Purification**

The third office is the petition's goal-term, and its placement last marks it as the work's completion: *and purify them*. Tazkiya — the verb of purification and growth at once — names the soul-craft the messenger performs: cleansing from the coverings the recital has catalogued (the sheathed hearts, the drunk-in calf-love, the black-marked mat-weaving), and growing the soul toward the states the recital promises (the white hearts the trials cannot harm, the no-fear-no-grief formula's holders). The purification-office is what separates the messenger from the librarian: a deliverer of texts transmits; a purifier transforms, and the petition asks for both because a community holding the Book unchanged is the recital's documented tragedy — the Torah in custody, the hearts hardened, the covenant cast at every station. The office's mechanism is the recital's own: the revelations recited do the purifying as they are received — the message that softens skins and hearts at Allah's mention (39:23's operation), the signs clear for people of sure faith (118) — and the messenger administers the reception, pacing the delivery to the community's capacity, as the Prophet ﷺ did with the companions whose formation the tradition records year by year. The petition's three verbs now show their architecture: recitation supplies the purifying agent, teaching supplies its application, purification is the effected state — and the community that receives all three is the umma the patriarchs asked to be made submitting (128), submitting not in name but in cleansed hearts.

**The Answered Prayer**

The verse's final clause seals the petition with the two Names that grant it: *indeed, You are the Almighty, All-Wise* — ʿazīz, the Mighty whose power raises messengers from illiterate valleys; ḥakīm, the Wise whose placing of the raise answers purposes the petitioners do not see. The Names are the prayer's theology in closing position: the request is made to power that can and wisdom that knows where — and the history between the petition and the recital's present is the Names' demonstration. The prayer was answered beyond its askers' vision: the messenger raised from among them carried the recitation to the world, the ummī nation became the tradition's ten thousand memorizers, the House the builders raised became the direction of a billion daily prayers. Sūrah al-Jumuʿah extends the answer past the first congregation — the messenger sent to them *and to others of them who have not yet joined them* (62:3), the prayer's reach covering every generation the recitation now includes. The verse's transfer is therefore not aspirational but locative: every reader of this sūrah stands inside the answered petition — the revelations recited to them its delivery-office, the Book and wisdom taught to them its instruction-office, the purification offered them its third office, all three running from a prayer spoken over foundation-stones by two builders who asked, for a nation not yet born, exactly the guidance that arrived. The recital files the prayer at the House's raising so that its congregation never forgets its own origin: asked for, before existing — and the asking's Names, Mighty and Wise, are the guarantee the congregation recites when it wonders whether the answer still holds.

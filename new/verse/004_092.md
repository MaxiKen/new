## Sūrah al-Nisāʾ 4:92

> It is not lawful for a believer to kill another except by mistake. And whoever kills a believer unintentionally must free a believing slave and pay blood-money to the victim’s family—unless they waive it charitably. But if the victim is a believer from a hostile people, then a believing slave must be freed. And if the victim is from a people bound with you in a treaty, then blood-money must be paid to the family along with freeing a believing slave. Those who are unable, let them fast two consecutive months—as a means of repentance to Allah. And Allah is All-Knowing, All-Wise.

### Expanded Commentary

**The sanctity of believing life**

The verse begins by declaring that a believer may not kill another believer except by mistake. The exception is not a casual loophole; it identifies accidental harm as a separate legal category requiring accountability and repair. The blood of a believer is protected even in a society marked by political division.

**Expiation and compensation**

For accidental killing, the perpetrator must free a believing slave and pay blood-money to the victim’s family, unless they waive it charitably. Where the victim belongs to a hostile people or a treaty community, the financial and emancipatory consequences are adjusted according to the relationship, while the obligation to treat the life seriously remains. The details belong to a judicial authority, not to private retaliation.

If a person cannot perform the emancipation, two consecutive months of fasting are required as repentance. This sequence links the personal and social dimensions: spiritual remorse does not replace compensation, and compensation does not make repentance unnecessary. Modern societies without slavery require jurists and legal systems to apply the underlying expiation responsibly rather than imitate an obsolete form.

Allah is All-Knowing and Wise. He knows intention, circumstance, and consequence, while the law creates a public response that prevents accidental death from being dismissed as mere bad luck.

**Accidental harm still needs repair**

Modern systems may use different forms of compensation and expiation, but the principle remains: accidental death is not ignored. Families deserve truth, support, and a process that acknowledges their loss. The perpetrator’s remorse becomes meaningful when it produces responsibility rather than a demand that everyone forget.

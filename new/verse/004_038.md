## Sūrah al-Nisāʾ 4:38

> Likewise for those who spend their wealth to show off and do not believe in Allah or the Last Day. And whoever takes Satan as an associate —what an evil associate they have!

**Expanded Commentary**

**Spending to Be Seen**

The verse adds a second example of disliked giving to complement stinginess: *wa alladhīna yunfiqūna amwālahum riʾāʾa al-nāsi wa lā yuʾminūna bi Allāhi wa lā bi al-yawmi al-ākhiri* — likewise for those who spend their wealth to show off and do not believe in Allah or the Last Day. The previous portrait was the hand that never opens — the stingy who promote stinginess and conceal bounty (4:37); this portrait is the hand that opens only for eyes. *Riʾāʾ* — show, display — was read as doing a deed so that people see and praise, rather than so that God accepts. The act itself — spending — may look generous; orphans might be fed, neighbours might receive, trade might flow, yet the motive empties it. The sūrah has just commended *iḥsān* — kind, beautiful doing — to parents, relatives, orphans, poor, neighbours, companions, travellers, and those in possession (4:36), and has warned against devouring wealth by falsehood (4:29). Showing off corrupts *iḥsān* at its source, because the recipient becomes a prop for reputation rather than a person honoured for God’s sake. The Qur’an elsewhere says *“do not nullify your charities with reminders and hurt, like one who spends his wealth to be seen by people”* (2:264), and that image of nullification matches the sūrah’s prepared humiliating punishment for the concealer (4:37); both spenders find their spending erased, one by withholding, the other by misdirection.

**Spending Without Belief in God or the Last Day**

The clause *wa lā yuʾminūna bi Allāhi wa lā bi al-yawmi al-ākhiri* ties the motive to the creed. Not believing in God or the Last Day does not only mean formal denial; it includes acting as though no watch is kept and no return is coming. The sūrah has insisted that God is ever Watchful (4:1), sufficient as Reckoner (4:6), Witness over all things (4:33), All-Knowing, Most Forbearing (4:12), and ever Merciful to you (4:29). To spend for show is to act as though that watch is absent and as though the only accounting is people’s praise today. The Last Day is the other half of the same belief, because it promises that even an atom’s weight will be counted and that good will be multiplied many times over (4:40). The ostentatious spender does not trust that multiplication; he seeks immediate multiplication in reputation. That is why the verse pairs spending to be seen with disbelief in the Last Day, not only with love of praise. The sūrah has already contrasted the Gardens that flow for keeping limits (4:13) and the Fire prepared for exceeding them (4:14) and for persisting until death (4:18); the spender to be seen has chosen the audience of the market over the audience of that Day, and his deed follows the audience he chose.

**Whoever Takes Satan as an Associate**

The warning *wa man yakun al-shayṭānu lahu qarīnan fa sāʾa qarīnā* — and whoever takes Satan as an associate, what an evil associate they have — names the companion behind both portraits. *Qarīn* — close associate, companion at the side — echoes *al-ṣāḥibi bi al-janb* — the companion at your side — commended for kindness in 4:36, but here the companion is the adversary who whispers that withholding is prudence and that showing off is prudence’s twin. Satan was described in the sūrah as the one whose fellowship the deviators prefer when they wish you to swerve greatly (4:27), and elsewhere as the one who commands indecency and love of wealth hoarded. To take him as *qarīn* is to keep his counsel, to follow the inclination that tightens the hand or opens it for eyes. *Fa sāʾa qarīnā* — what an evil companion — is both description and discouragement. A companion shapes the walk; a companion at the side is present at the moment of spending, trading, and dividing estates. The Qur’an elsewhere says that on the Day when witnesses are brought, the person will wish between himself and his *qarīn* there were distance of east and west. The verse anticipates that wish by naming it now, when the choice of companion can still be changed.

**Two Failures With One Root**

Read with the previous verse, the pair forms a complete diagnosis of corrupted generosity. Stinginess withholds God’s bounty and teaches others to withhold (4:37); ostentation spends bounty but diverts its aim from God and the Last Day to people’s eyes. Both refuse the same centre: spending for God who gave the bounty and who will multiply it on a Day that is coming. The sūrah has shown the correct centre repeatedly: give women dowries graciously as a free gift (4:4), return the orphan’s wealth with witnesses under God as Reckoner (4:6), divide after debts and bequests within limits watched by the Witness (4:33), trade by mutual consent under the ever Merciful (4:29), and ask God for bounty rather than crave another’s (4:32). Each act is done with God and the Last Day in view, even when the transaction is small. Ostentatious spending may mimic those acts in form — a gift to a relative, a provision to a bystander, a trade that looks consensual — but the sūrah distinguishes by audience. If the audience is people, the act is with Satan as *qarīn*; if the audience is God, the act is with Him as Guardian and Helper (4:45). The sūrah leaves no middle where spending counts without an audience; it asks only which audience is chosen.

**The Humiliating and the Evil as Two Ends**

The recompense for the stingy was a prepared humiliating punishment (4:37); the companion for the ostentatious is an evil associate (4:38). The two names match the two sins’ pretensions: stinginess pretended to be status, so it is humiliated; ostentation pretended to be honour, so it is left with an evil companion. Both are social in nature, because both sins are social — one teaches others to withhold, the other performs for others to see. The sūrah’s next verse will ask *“what harm could have come to them if they had believed and donated from what Allah provided?”* (4:39), making the alternative explicit: belief and donation from what was provided, audience God, Day believed, companion God as Guardian and Helper. Between the two verses, the reader is given a simple audit for his own spending: is this bounty being concealed, spent for show, or donated from what was provided with the Last Day in view? The answer determines not only whether others benefit but who walks at his side as he gives, and whose prepared recompense awaits him.

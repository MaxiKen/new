## Sūrah al-Nisāʾ 4:38

> Likewise for those who spend their wealth to show off and do not believe in Allah or the Last Day. And whoever takes Satan as an associate —what an evil associate they have!

### Expanded Commentary

**Spending for display is not generosity**

The verse joins miserliness to spending wealth for the sake of being seen. The contrast is deliberate: one person refuses to spend, another spends lavishly, but both may be oriented toward human approval rather than Allah. Public generosity can become a performance in which the recipient is a prop and the giver is the subject of admiration.

**Faith and the Last Day discipline motive**

The person who believes in Allah and the Last Day knows that reputation is unstable while divine knowledge is complete. The same act can be charity when it seeks God’s pleasure and self-advertisement when it seeks applause. This does not require every charitable project to be anonymous; public giving can encourage others and support accountability. The question is whether praise is the purpose and whether the recipient’s dignity is sacrificed for it.

**The companionship of evil**

The verse calls Satan a companion for those who spend to be seen. This is not a claim that every visible donation is satanic. It describes the spiritual mechanism by which an outward good is made empty: the ego is invited to take credit, comparison replaces mercy, and the giver becomes angry when recognition is withheld. An act that feeds arrogance can leave the poor no better respected even when money changes hands.

The remedy is intention, quiet service, and attention to outcomes. Give what is useful, protect the beneficiary’s privacy, and accept that Allah may conceal a deed from people while recording it fully. The unseen account is the one that remains.

**The recipient must not be made a billboard**

Public charity can be useful, but the beneficiary should not be displayed as proof of the donor’s virtue. Ask whether the publicity informs supporters, protects accountability, or merely collects praise. Sincerity is strengthened when the giver remembers that the person receiving help is a neighbour in dignity, not a prop in an advertisement.

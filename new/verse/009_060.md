## Sūrah al-Tawbah 9:60

> Alms-tax is only for the poor and the needy, for those employed to administer it, for those whose hearts are attracted ˹to the faith˺, for ˹freeing˺ slaves, for those in debt, for Allah’s cause, and for ˹needy˺ travellers. ˹This is˺ an obligation from Allah. And Allah is All-Knowing, All-Wise.

**Expanded Commentary**

**Charity Has Defined Recipients**

This verse places charitable funds within eight recognized categories. The limitation protects the money from becoming a private pool for whoever has influence. The poor and needy receive direct care. Administrators may receive for the work of collecting and distributing. Those whose hearts are attracted to faith may receive support that strengthens a fragile bond. Funds may free enslaved people, relieve debt, serve Allah's cause, and help travelers cut off from their resources.

The list does not reduce compassion. It gives compassion a disciplined form. A fund entrusted for alms cannot be redirected merely because another project is fashionable or because a powerful person requests it. Need must be examined, and the category must be real. The verse turns generosity into an accountable institution rather than leaving it to personal mood.

**Poor and Needy Are Distinct Human Conditions**

Exegetes differed over the precise distinction between the poor and the needy. Some describe one as having part of what is required and the other as having almost nothing; others reverse the terms. The disagreement does not cancel the principle. Both categories identify people whose resources do not meet their necessary needs, and both deserve protection from humiliation.

Need is also not always visible. A person may own a tool for work and still lack food, rent, medicine, or safety. Another may appear comfortable while carrying a debt that consumes every income. Sound distribution asks about actual obligations rather than judging by clothing or public confidence. The purpose is to restore a person's ability to live with dignity, not to reward a performance of poverty.

**Administration Is Part of the Trust**

The inclusion of those employed to administer alms proves that organized charity requires labor. Collection, verification, record keeping, delivery, and protection of recipients all take time and skill. Paying administrators from the designated fund recognizes that trust cannot be sustained by demanding unpaid work from everyone.

That allowance also creates a duty of accountability. Administrators must know the rules, separate personal funds from communal funds, protect confidential information, and document decisions. They are not owners because they handle the money. Their share is compensation for a trust, and their misuse of the trust is more serious because they had access to what belonged to others.

**Reconciliation Must Serve a Real Need**

The category of those whose hearts are attracted to faith has been interpreted in more than one way. It can include people newly connected to Islam or people whose support and stability serve a legitimate communal need. The central idea is reconciliation: resources may be used to strengthen a relationship with faith when wise leaders judge that the benefit is genuine.

This category cannot be a license for bribery or favoritism. Payment does not manufacture faith, and a famous person does not become eligible merely because influence is useful. The administrators must ask whether the support protects people, removes a serious barrier, or strengthens a real bond. If it only purchases applause, it has left the moral purpose of the category.

**Freedom and Debt Are Forms of Rescue**

The verse treats emancipation and debt relief as charity priorities. Freedom from bondage restores a person's control over life. Relief from overwhelming debt restores the ability to work, provide, and sleep without being trapped by an obligation that cannot be met. Both categories show that poverty is not only a shortage of food. It can be captivity to a person, a contract, or an event that destroyed the means of repayment.

Debt relief requires care because not every debt carries the same moral situation. A person unable to repay after a disaster is different from one who borrows to display luxury while refusing responsibility. The fund should restore genuine victims and protect creditors' rights while refusing to make irresponsibility a permanent public expense.

**Allah's Cause and the Stranded Traveler**

“For Allah's cause” is broad in language, so it requires responsible interpretation. It refers to work that genuinely serves Allah's religion and public good under sound legal guidance, not to every project that adopts a religious slogan. The category must not become a way for administrators to spend without the limits placed on the other recipients.

The traveler is eligible when distance has cut him off from his resources, even if he has wealth at home. This rule recognizes a temporary crisis rather than judging a person's entire financial status. A community that helps the stranded traveler makes movement safer and teaches that dignity does not disappear when circumstances suddenly change.

**The Obligation Is Guarded by Knowledge and Wisdom**

The verse ends with Allah's knowledge and wisdom. Knowledge demands accurate assessment of recipients, lawful sources, and genuine categories. Wisdom demands proportion, timing, and attention to consequences. A donation can be large yet badly placed, while a small payment can remove a serious hardship when it reaches the right person.

The closing names therefore protect the whole system. The donor gives because the alms are an obligation, not a favor. The administrator distributes because Allah knows who qualifies and knows whether the administrator is honest. The recipient accepts with dignity and uses the help responsibly. Charity becomes worship when mercy is joined to boundaries, and when every hand remembers that the money belongs to Allah before it reaches any human hand. The categories also teach a wider social vision. Poverty is relieved directly, administration is funded honestly, freedom is restored, debt is addressed, travel is made safer, and the religious community is strengthened without turning money into a tool of vanity. Wise distribution looks for the point at which a lawful payment creates lasting dignity rather than repeated dependence. It protects privacy, records decisions, and remains ready to review its practice when new circumstances reveal a better application of the same divine principles.

## Sūrah al-Shuʿarāʾ 26:170

> So We saved him and all of his family,

**Expanded Commentary**

**Deliverance Answers Lot's Prayer**

Lot asked God to save him and his family from the consequences of the community's conduct. This verse records the answer: God delivered him and his family together. The rescue is not presented as the result of Lot's political power or the community's sudden reform. It is a divine act that separates the faithful household from a society committed to its corruption.

The answer also confirms that prayer and action belong together. Lot warned the people, refused their practice, and turned to God for protection. Divine deliverance does not make his earlier courage unnecessary. It shows that the servant's efforts are held within a power greater than the hostility around him.


The phrase all of his family expresses the tenderness of the rescue. Lot's concern for his household was not ignored. Yet the wording must not be turned into a rule that ancestry guarantees safety. The following verse makes clear that one family member is excluded from the saving group. Belonging is ultimately moral and spiritual, not merely biological.

This distinction protects the meaning of family prayer. A believer should care for relatives, warn them, and seek their safety, but cannot compel their hearts or claim God's forgiveness on their behalf. Love asks for rescue; God judges each person's response to His command.

**Deliverance Creates Separation From Corruption**

Lot's family is saved by being removed from the environment that has become hostile to God's guidance. Separation here is not an expression of superiority. It is protection from participation in a public evil and from the judgment that will overtake the people who persist in it.

A believer may need distance from a corrupt institution, gathering, or social pressure in order to preserve obedience. Such distance should be taken with wisdom and without contempt. Lot does not leave because his family is inherently better; he leaves because the community has chosen a path that cannot be shared by those who fear God.


God saves a family together, showing that faith can shape a household's common direction. Shared worship and mutual protection make endurance easier. A family can encourage truth when the surrounding society mocks it and can preserve the vulnerable when public norms become dangerous.

At the same time, the household is not a substitute for individual faith. Each member must respond to God. The rescue of the family is a mercy to people who stand with Lot, not a declaration that every relative is safe regardless of conduct.

**A Parallel Confirms the Pattern**

Quran 7:83 likewise states that God saved Lot and his family except his wife, who was among the doomed. The parallel confirms that the present verse emphasizes a real act of deliverance while the next verse will qualify the household's membership in that deliverance.

The repetition across the accounts also shows that rescue is part of the prophetic pattern. God does not only punish the rejecting community. He preserves those who respond to the warning, even when their numbers are small and their social position is weak.


Lot's family may be surrounded by a powerful majority, but the majority cannot determine whether God will save them. A household can appear isolated and still be held within divine care. The measure is not social strength; it is fidelity to the Lord.

This gives courage to believers who feel outnumbered. They should not mistake loneliness for abandonment or popularity for safety. God's deliverance may be hidden until the moment He chooses, but His knowledge of the faithful is not weakened by the crowd's hostility.

**The Saved Household Has a Duty After Rescue**

Deliverance is not merely escape from danger. It creates a responsibility to remember what was saved from and to live differently afterward. A family rescued from corruption should not carry the old order into its new place. It must preserve prayer, honesty, and concern for those who remain vulnerable.

The household also learns that safety belongs to God. It should not boast over those who were destroyed, because the same Lord who saved it judges every person with complete knowledge. Gratitude is the proper response to deliverance, not pride in being selected.


The people threatened to expel Lot, as if they owned the land and could define his future. God answers that threat by taking responsibility for the messenger and his household. The community can remove them from its social order, but it cannot prevent the Lord from giving them a place of safety.

This does not promise that every faithful person will avoid worldly loss. Lot's rescue is a specific act in a revealed story. It does teach that worldly exclusion is not the final judgment. The servant may lose a place among people and still be preserved by God in a way the powerful cannot control.


The verse stands immediately before the account of exclusion within Lot's household and the destruction of the rest. It keeps the reader from seeing judgment as the only divine action. The same Lord who punishes persistent corruption also makes a way out for those who turn to Him.

Lot's prayer has been answered, but the answer is more precise than he might have imagined. God saves the family as a faithful group while leaving hidden allegiance to Him as the decisive measure. The verse therefore invites gratitude and careful humility: seek the Lord's deliverance, and remain faithful to the conditions of it.

The rescued family also becomes a witness that obedience is not wasted when a society rejects it. Their safety may not look like worldly triumph, but it is a deeper preservation. God removes them from the reach of a culture that threatened to make corruption unavoidable and gives them the chance to continue under His care.

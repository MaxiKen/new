## Sūrah al-Nisāʾ 4:121

> It is they who will have Hell as their home, and they will find no escape from it!

**Expanded Commentary**

**They Will Have Hell as Home**

The verse states *ulāʾika maʾwāhum Jahannam* — it is they — those who take Satan as guardian instead of God (4:119), who follow his promises that are only delusion (4:120), who invoke female gods besides Him (4:117) — whose refuge, shelter, home is Hell, *Jahannam*. *Maʾwā* — refuge, shelter, place of return — was explained as the home that receives, as the emigrant finds *murāgham* — many havens — and abundance where God’s earth is spacious (4:100), while these find *maʾwā* as Hell, the inverse haven. The sūrah has used *maʾwā* for the self-wronger who remained where earth was wide — Hell as home, evil destination (4:97) — and for the defier left to what he chose — burning, evil destination (4:115); here the pronoun *ulāʾika* — it is they — restricts to Satan’s share — the appointed portion he claimed (4:118). *Jahannam* was read as the Fire where skins are replaced to taste punishment (4:56), where the intentional killer abides with anger and curse (4:93), where the prepared torment is humiliating for disbelievers who would sweep upon neglect (4:102). To have Hell as *maʾwā* means it is not a passing visit but the place of stay, as Gardens are *maʾwā* for those who believe and do good who stay forever (4:122).

**And They Will Find No Escape From It**

The clause *wa lā yajidūna ʿanhā maḥīṣā* — and they will find no escape, no flight, no diversion from it — was explained with *maḥīṣ* — escape, refuge from, place to incline away. *Lā yajidūna* — they will not find — contrasts with the emigrant’s *yajid* — will find — many havens (4:100) and the helpless hoped to be pardoned where finding was impossible (4:98–99). *ʿAnhā* — from it, away from it — was read as Hell’s encompassing: there is no *murāgham* from Hell, no spaciousness away from it, because the curse that took Satan as guardian cut off the only guardian who could provide escape — *“whoever takes Satan as guardian instead of God suffers tremendous loss”* (4:119) — and promises only delusion (4:120). The sūrah has said that the wronged who sought *murāgham* found it (4:100) and that prayer at fixed time (4:103) is the writ that secures times; Hell without *maḥīṣ* is the writ that secures consequence where promise was delusion. *Lā yajidūna maḥīṣā* was also compared to *“they will not find protector or helper besides God”* for whoever commits evil (4:123): no *walī* and no *naṣīr*, no escape, no diversion.

**A Verse That Seals Satan’s Economy With Its Destination**

Placed after *Satan promises only delusion* (4:120), this verse seals Satan’s economy with its destination. The program — *I will mislead, delude, order slitting ears and altering creation* (4:119) — paid in delusion, ends in Hell as home. The sūrah has shown that the deceiver would deceive none but themselves (4:113) and that throwing sin on the innocent bears slander (4:112); here self-deception’s wage is Hell without diversion. The sūrah has also shown that the believer’s hope is what the disbeliever never hopes for (4:104) — ranks, forgiveness, mercy (4:96) — and that those who believe and do good are promised Gardens where God’s promise is ever true (4:122); the deluded hope that was nothing but gloss ends where no *maḥīṣ* is found because no true promise was behind it. Where the sūrah earlier said *“God has prepared humiliating punishment for disbelievers”* where prayer was guarded (4:102), here the preparation is shelter itself — Hell as *maʾwā* — the humiliation of having no escape from the delusion’s house.

**No Escape Because No Guardian Was Taken**

The phrase *wa lā yajidūna ʿanhā maḥīṣā* was explained as consequence of taking Satan as *walī* (4:119). The sūrah has repeated that God is sufficient as Guardian and Helper (4:45), that the oppressed cry for a guardian and helper from grace (4:75), that the Messenger ﷺ is told to put trust in Him as Trustee (4:81, 4:132). To take Satan as *walī* instead of Him is to choose a guardian who was cursed (4:118) and whose promise is delusion (4:120); on the Day where advocates in this life will find no advocate before God (4:109), there will be no *maḥīṣ* because the only *walī* and *naṣīr* who could provide it was rejected. Classical notes added that *maḥīṣ* — escape place — matches *murāgham* — haven — as both are places of turning; the emigrant who turned to God finds many turnings, the follower of Satan who turned to him finds none. The sūrah that promised the emigrant his reward secured even if death overtakes (4:100) here promises the follower of Satan his loss secured where death overtakes into Hell.

**A Short Verse That Holds the Two Promises Apart**

Though only *“Hell as home and no escape,”* the verse holds the two promises apart. Immediately after: *“those who believe and do good, We will admit into Gardens under which rivers flow forever; God’s promise always true, whose word more truthful?”* (4:122); immediately before: Satan’s promise only delusion. The sūrah that judges by the Book in truth (4:105) and that shows there is no good in most secret talks except charity, kindness, reconciliation (4:114) here shows that the Talker behind the evil secret talk leads to a home with no secret exit. For the weak for whom the sūrah lightens burdens, the verse is a clarification: delusion’s home has no exit, while God’s earth has many havens and His forgiveness many entries (4:100, 4:110), so that choosing the guardian who is Ever-Pardoning, All-Forgiving over the guardian whose promise is gloss is the lightest and most spacious choice.

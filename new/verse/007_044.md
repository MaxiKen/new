## Sūrah al-Aʿrāf 7:44

> The residents of Paradise will call out to the residents of the Fire, “We have certainly found our Lord’s promise to be true. Have you too found your Lord’s promise to be true?” They will reply, “Yes, we have!” Then a caller will announce to both, “May Allah’s condemnation be upon the wrongdoers,

**Expanded Commentary**

**The Call From the Garden**

The verse reports the address of the people of the Garden to the people of the Fire, and the address asks each group's question through the experience of the other: those in the Garden state that they have found their Lord's promise true and ask whether the people of the Fire have found the same. The Book's descriptions of the Hereafter include exchanges between the two groups, and the sūrah of the Heights itself continues the dialogue in the verses that follow with the request for water and the answer that follows it (7:50).

The call establishes that the barrier between the two groups does not prevent speech. The sūrah's following verses describe the barrier and the heights above it, and the people on the heights will address both groups (7:46–49). The verse in front of us begins the sequence with the direct exchange, and the arrangement gives the scene its shape as one in which the truth of the promise is publicly confirmed.

**The Answer Given**

The people of the Fire answer in the affirmative, and the Book records the same acknowledgement at other points of the judgment. The sūrah of the Cattle reports the disbelievers confessing when they are shown their Lord, and the sūrah of the Troops and the sūrah of the Forgiver report the condemned acknowledging the truth once they have entered the punishment or are being driven towards it. The sūrah of the Kingdom records the condemned confessing that they had heard a warner and rejected him.

The acknowledgement in front of us comes after the point at which it could change anything, and the commentary tradition treats all such confessions as confirming the justice of the sentence rather than altering it. The sūrah's opening verses have already described the confession of the destroyed communities in the world, when the punishment came upon them and they said that they had been wrongdoers (7:5). The answer in front of us is the same admission, spoken in the next world and under the same constraint.

**The Herald and the Proclamation**

The verse reports the proclamation of a caller in the midst of the assembly, and the call states that the curse of God is upon the wrongdoers. The commentary tradition identifies the caller as an angel charged with the address, and it notes that the proclamation reaches both groups. The curse in front of us is not the announcement of the punishment itself but the declaration of the ground on which it rests, and the sentence is left hanging at the end of the verse to be completed by the description of the wrongdoers in the next.

The Book's descriptions of the last day include proclamations of this kind, and the sūrah of the Troops reports the address given to the people of the Fire as they arrive. The arrangement in front of us places the announcement between the two groups, so that the curse is heard by those who are addressed by it and by those who are not. The verse ends with the phrase describing the cursed, and the next verse states their conduct.

**The Report of the Prophet at Badr**

The commentary tradition records that the Prophet addressed the dead of his enemies after the battle of Badr in terms similar to the question in front of us, asking whether they had found what their Lord had promised them to be true. The sound report of that exchange includes the objection of the Companions that the dead could not hear, and the Prophet's answer that the dead were not the ones who could not hear the message. The report stands in the Book of Campaigns in al-Bukhārī (3976), where the Prophet’s words and the Companions’ objection are both recorded.

The report connects the question in front of us with the world's own history, since the same principle was demonstrated at a battle within the audience's own lifetime. The Book's descriptions of the punishment of the earlier communities serve the same function, and the sūrah's narratives of the peoples who were destroyed are the extended version of the same warning.

**The Truth of the Promise in the Book**

The Book states repeatedly that the promise of God is true, and the affirmation in front of us stands on that statement. The sūrah of the Women states that God's promise is true and that the gardens beneath which rivers flow are the promise that belongs to the righteous, and the sūrah of Yūnus reports the declaration that the promise of God is true. The sūrah of the Romans closes its account of the signs with the statement that the promise of God is true, and the sūrah of the Troops repeats it in its description of the day.

The two promises that the verse sets beside each other are the reward for the one group and the punishment for the other, and the question in front of us is addressed to the second. The Book presents both as obligations that its Lord will discharge, and the sūrah's reader is given the confirmation of the condemned themselves as evidence for the warning that the chapter has delivered.

**Where the Verse Sits**

The verse stands forty-fourth in the chapter, and it follows the account of the people of the Garden and their words. The next verse describes the wrongdoers on whom the curse has been pronounced, and the verses after it describe the barrier, the heights, and the people who stand there. The verse in front of us supplies the exchange that introduces the whole scene.

The reader who has been given the two outcomes in the preceding verses now hears them confirmed by the people who received them. The questions and the answers are placed in the Hereafter, and the chapter's next verses name the conduct that produced the second outcome.

**The Transition to the Next Verse**

The next verse describes the wrongdoers as those who turned others from the way of God, sought to make it crooked, and disbelieved in the Hereafter (7:45). The curse proclaimed in front of us is given its content in the verse that follows, and the conduct that earned it is stated in three clauses.

The two verses together complete the announcement. The question is asked and answered, the curse is proclaimed, and the offense is named; and the sūrah then turns to the barrier between the two groups and the people who look down from the heights.

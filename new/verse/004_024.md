## Sūrah al-Nisāʾ 4:24

> Also ˹forbidden are˺ married women—except ˹female˺ captives in your possession. This is Allah’s commandment to you. ˹But˺ lawful to you are all beyond these—as long as you seek them with your wealth in a legal marriage, not in fornication. Give those you have consummated marriage with their due dowries. It is permissible to be mutually gracious regarding the set dowry. Surely Allah is All-Knowing, All-Wise.

**Expanded Commentary**

**The Married Woman Is Not Available**

The verse begins by completing the list of forbidden degrees: *wa al-muḥṣanātu min al-nisāʾ illā mā malakat aymānukum* — also forbidden are married women, except female captives in your possession. *Muḥṣanāt* here was read as women protected by marriage, whose bond is not to be breached. The general rule is that a woman under a valid marriage is outside the field of choice until that marriage ends by death, divorce, or annulment. The exception for female captives — *mā malakat aymānukum* — addressed a specific seventh-century circumstance where captives whose previous marriages were regarded as dissolved by captivity entered the household under distinct rules. Early authorities detailed conditions, waiting periods, and consent considerations that tightly regulated that category, and the Qur’an elsewhere encourages freeing slaves and marrying them as free believers (4:25), showing the direction of the law toward liberty. The phrase *kitāba Allāhi ʿalaykum* — this is Allah’s commandment to you, literally Allah’s writing upon you — marks the entire roster from the father’s former wife (4:22) through this verse as inscribed law, not advice. To treat a married woman as available is therefore not a private indiscretion but a breach of an inscribed bar.

**Lawful Beyond These, Sought in the Right Way**

The verse then opens the lawful field: *wa uḥilla lakum mā warāʾa dhālikum an tabtaghū bi amwālikum muḥṣinīna ghayra musāfiḥīn* — lawful to you are all beyond these, as long as you seek them with your wealth in a legal marriage, not in fornication. Two conditions define the lawful path. First, it is sought *with your wealth* — *bi amwālikum* — meaning through dowry and maintenance, the same wealth the sūrah has taught to guard as a standing support (4:5) and to give graciously as *ṣaduqah* with *niḥlah* (4:4). Marriage is therefore a commitment of provision, not a seizure. Second, it is as *muḥṣinīna* — seeking chastity, protection — not as *musāfiḥīn* — fornicators who pour out desire without bond. The contrast *muḥṣin / musāfiḥ* frames marriage as the shelter for desire rather than its mere outlet. The sūrah will repeat that shelter language for those who marry believing bondwomen, requiring chastity and avoidance of secret affairs (4:25). Here the same standard applies to all: the wealth is spent to build a lawful, protective tie, and the act is chaste. That pairing of wealth and chastity keeps the law from becoming a list of names alone; it makes the method of seeking part of lawfulness.

**Give Those You Have Consummated Marriage With Their Due Dowries**

The command *fa mā istamtaʿtum bihi minhunna fa ātūhunna ujūra-hunna farīḍah* — give those you have consummated marriage with their due dowries — restates the dowry as an obligation — *farīḍah*. The word *istimtāʿ* — enjoyment, consummation — refers to the completion of marriage’s intimacy, the same *ifḍāʾ* that the sūrah called a firm commitment’s context (4:21). Once that stage is reached, the dowry is not a promise to be debated but a portion due. The Qur’an already secured the dowry as the woman’s gracious gift (4:4) and forbade reclaiming even a heap when wishing to replace a wife (4:20–21). Here it secures the dowry again at the point where the lawful field is opened, so that opening is not mistaken for licence to seek without commitment. The dowry is not a fee for access; it is the sign of seriousness — *ṣaduqah* — that makes the shelter real. The sūrah has guarded that sign at entry, during marriage, and at replacement; this verse guards it at consummation, marking the moment when the obligation hardens. The insistence on *farīḍah* also explains why the verse repeats what was said before: the rule needs to be present wherever marriage is sought, including where new lawful matches are being considered.

**Mutual Graciousness Regarding the Set Dowry**

The allowance *wa lā junāḥa ʿalaykum fīmā tarāḍaytum bihi min baʿdi al-farīḍah* — it is permissible to be mutually gracious regarding the set dowry — adds flexibility after the obligation is fixed. Spouses may, by mutual content, adjust what was set — increase, decrease, remit a portion, or defer — so long as the adjustment is with genuine willingness. The same standard of *ṭīb al-nafs* — a contented soul — that governed the wife’s remission earlier (4:4) governs here as mutual consent — *tarāḍī*. Jurists noted that this permission does not allow coercion to reduce the dowry, nor does it allow a guardian to waive what belongs to the woman without her leave. It allows what a free, unpressured agreement allows. That balance reflects the sūrah’s broader ethic where wealth moves by right and by kindness together: strictly by obligation where the right is fixed, and gently by consent where discretion remains. The dowry therefore has two aspects — the fixed portion that is due upon consummation, and the human space around it where generosity can flow either way without harm.

**All-Knowing, All-Wise at the Gate of the Lawful**

The closing *inna Allāha kāna ʿAlīman Ḥakīmā* — surely Allah is All-Knowing, All-Wise — appears here where the entire list of prohibitions ends and the lawful field is opened. The pairing matches its use after the inheritance shares (4:11) and after the promise to make things clear (4:26). Knowledge covers every degree that, if crossed, would injure lineage and affection; wisdom explains why marriage is tied to wealth spent in chastity rather than to wealth spent for display. The Name *ʿAlīm* also watches the private adjustments to the set dowry, where mutual graciousness could be genuine or pressured, while *Ḥakīm* places that watch inside wisdom that seeks to protect the shelter marriage is meant to be. The sūrah will immediately follow with guidance for those who cannot afford a free believing wife and may marry a believing bondwoman with permission and fair dowry (4:25), showing that the same Two Names govern the easier path as well. The command written upon the community — *kitāba Allāhi ʿalaykum* — is therefore not a hard wall alone; it is a gate whose bars are known and whose opening beyond the bars is lawful when sought with wealth, with chastity, and with the woman’s due given as an obligation that may then be softened only by true mutual grace.

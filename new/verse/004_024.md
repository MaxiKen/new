## Sūrah al-Nisāʾ 4:24

> Also ˹forbidden are˺ married women—except ˹female˺ captives in your possession. This is Allah’s commandment to you. ˹But˺ lawful to you are all beyond these—as long as you seek them with your wealth in a legal marriage, not in fornication. Give those you have consummated marriage with their due dowries. It is permissible to be mutually gracious regarding the set dowry. Surely Allah is All-Knowing, All-Wise.

### Expanded Commentary

**Marriage and the protection of existing bonds**

The verse first prohibits married women, with the historical exception of captives governed by the law of war at the time of revelation. The basic rule is that a woman already bound by marriage is not available for another contract. The exception belonged to a legal institution that the revelation regulated through processes of separation and waiting, not to private trafficking or modern exploitation. Reading the verse responsibly requires preserving both its historical context and its governing concern: marriage cannot be invaded by arbitrary power.

**Beyond the named list**

The verse continues the list of prohibited relationships and then distinguishes what is lawful. The Qur’an’s wording makes clear that lawful sexual relations must arise through property and contract rules recognized by revelation, not through secret arrangements or coercion. Classical law elaborated conditions concerning consent, guardianship, dowry, witnesses, and waiting periods to ensure that the contract was real rather than a disguise for abuse.

**Seeking chastity, not possession**

The purpose of marriage is described as seeking chastity and avoiding unlawful intimacy. This reframes the contract as a discipline for desire and a site of mutual rights. A person who enters marriage only to use or humiliate the other has violated its purpose even if the external paperwork is present. Conversely, the verse acknowledges human sexual needs without treating them as an excuse to disregard commitment.

**The financial obligation**

The dowry is made obligatory for the lawful enjoyment of marriage, though the spouses may agree on its amount and timing. The wife owns it, and any subsequent adjustment must be voluntary. The phrase “without blame” concerning what is mutually agreed recognizes that contracts can contain different arrangements, provided they do not cancel fixed rights or conceal coercion.

The verse’s legal detail sits within a moral declaration: God knows what is permissible and wise, and believers are not free to rebuild the family on appetite alone. The rules protect the existing wife, the future wife, and the integrity of the contract by requiring that desire travel through responsibility.

**Desire under covenant**

The verse’s rules do not deny sexual desire; they give it a lawful shape that protects those who bear its consequences. A marriage is credible when the woman’s property, status, and safety remain protected even if the husband’s desire changes. The law thus treats chastity as a shared social responsibility, not a burden placed only on women.

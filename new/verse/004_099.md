## Sūrah al-Nisāʾ 4:99

> it is right to hope that Allah will pardon them. For Allah is Ever-Pardoning, AllForgiving.

### Expanded Commentary

**Hope remains for the helpless**

Allah may pardon those unable to emigrate. The wording encourages hope rather than automatic condemnation. Their situation is known in detail by the One who sees fear, poverty, confinement, and the absence of a safe route. Human beings should therefore be slow to accuse someone whose circumstances they have not investigated.

**Pardon and responsibility**

Divine pardon does not make oppression acceptable. It assures the trapped person that inability to perform the ideal does not erase faith or worth. It also calls the freer believer to practical mercy: provide money, transport, legal help, shelter, and advocacy so that the route becomes possible.

The verse’s shortness is consoling. A person who feels forgotten by history is remembered by Allah. The moral community is measured by whether it treats the helpless as burdens or as people whose protection is its shared responsibility.

**Hope can coexist with grief**

The helpless person may be unable to change the political situation yet still remain close to God through prayer, patience, and whatever small act is possible. The verse does not glorify captivity; it promises that incapacity will be judged mercifully. Those outside the danger should turn that promise into practical assistance.

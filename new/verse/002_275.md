## Sūrah al-Baqarah 2:275

> Those who consume interest will stand ˹on Judgment Day˺ like those driven to madness by Satan’s touch. That is because they say, “Trade is no different than interest.” But Allah has permitted trading and forbidden interest. Whoever refrains—after having received warning from their Lord —may keep their previous gains, and their case is left to Allah. As for those who persist, it is they who will be the residents of the Fire. They will be there forever.

**Expanded Commentary**

**The Practice Addressed**

The sentence describes those who consume interest and states that they will not rise except as a person whom Satan has touched with madness, and the community's teachers read the account as the Book's treatment of a practice that belonged to the dealings of the city. The passage has completed its instruction about charity, and the sentence here turns to the gain that is acquired without an exchange.

The image at the beginning has been explained by the commentators as the description of the state of such a person on the day of the rising, when the disorder of his condition appears to those who see him. The community's scholars noted the figure and read it as the picture of an account that has been disordered in its foundation.

The Book's treatment of the subject elsewhere describes the practice among the evils that the community is forbidden, and the teachers gathered the passages when they set out the law. The sentence in this place supplies the first of the verses that treat the matter at length.

**The Term Ribā**

The community's scholars examined the term that is rendered as interest, and they explained that it denotes an increase or a gain that the practice adds without a corresponding exchange. The teachers noted that the term covers more than the modern usage of the word that translates it.

The commentators described the two forms that the reports identify, one connected with the deferment of a debt and the other with an unequal exchange of goods of the same kind. The scholars of the community built the law upon the distinction, and the discussions of the jurists over the centuries supplied the details.

The sentence in this place states the prohibition of the practice, and the verses that follow describe the conduct of those who persist in it. The community's teachers read the passage as the treatment of the subject that completes the instruction about the wealth of the believers.

**The Two Kinds**

The reports describe the first kind of the practice as the fee that was charged when a debt came due, since the creditor would offer the debtor the choice between payment and an increase in the amount for a further term. The community's scholars explained that such an arrangement could double the principal in a year and redouble it in the years that followed.

The second kind concerns the exchange of the same kind of goods in unequal measures, and the reports describe the practice as it was conducted in the market of the city. The teachers noted the two forms together, since the Book's prohibition covers the gain that is taken without the exchange that the law requires.

The Qur'an mentions the doubling that the practice produced in another passage that addresses the believers, and the community's commentators connected the two verses when they set out the subject. The sentence in this place supplies the general statement of the prohibition.

**The Report of the Early Practice**

The community's books preserve the description transmitted from the earliest generation of the teachers, and the report explains the custom of the pre-Islamic period in which a creditor would demand payment or an increase at the time the debt became due. The material shows the practice that the passage addressed and the manner in which the community understood it.

The scholars of the community drew from the report the account of the principal that the debtor would lose if the deferment continued, and they used it when they explained the severity of the prohibition. The passage's language about the practice was read with the description that the report supplies.

The teachers also noted that the Book contrasts the practice with trade, since the sentence records the claim of those who make no distinction between the two. The material supplied the community with the framework of its law on the subject.

**The Claim Answered**

The sentence records the claim that trade and interest are alike, and the community's teachers read the statement as the account of the argument that the Book answers. The two practices belong to different orders, since one is an exchange that the parties conduct with the risk and the labor that the law approves while the other is a gain that is taken for the deferment alone.

The commentators explained the answer that the sentence supplies, which is the statement that God has permitted trade and forbidden the practice. The community's scholars drew from the clause the lesson that the difference is established by the command rather than by the reasoning of the parties.

The wording also shows the manner in which the Book responds to the community's arguments, since it states the claim and then supplies the rule. The teachers used the passage when they set out the method of the law, and the sentence supplied the principle for the subject.

**The Rule for the Past**

The sentence states that the person who desists after the warning of his Lord may keep what is past and that his affair belongs to God, and the community's scholars explained the clause as the mercy that accompanies the instruction. The past dealings were not to be reclaimed, and the account of them was left with God.

The teachers noted the phrase about the warning, since the ruling is connected with the person's knowledge of the prohibition. The community's judges applied the clause to the cases that came before them in the early years, and the reports describe the manner in which the transactions of the new believers were treated.

Those who return to the practice after the instruction are described at the end of the sentence as the inhabitants of the Fire who remain in it, and the community's teachers read the threat as the statement of the seriousness of the subject. The sentence supplies the rule, the mercy, and the warning together.

**What the Verse Establishes**

The sentence establishes the description of those who consume interest, the claim that they make about trade, the ruling that separates the two practices, the permission for the past dealings of those who desist, and the threat that is addressed to those who return to the practice. The community's teachers used the verse when they set out the law of the subject, and the sūrah's following verses continue the treatment with the instruction about the debts that remain, so that the passage closes its account of the wealth of the community with a subject on which the community's law rests.

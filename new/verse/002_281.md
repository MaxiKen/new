## Sūrah al-Baqarah 2:281

> Be mindful of the Day when you will ˹all˺ be returned to Allah, then every soul will be paid in full for what it has done, and none will be wronged.

**Expanded Commentary**

**The Reminder Stated**

The sentence instructs the community to be mindful of the day on which it will be returned to God and states that every soul will then be paid in full for what it has earned. The verse closes the passage on the practice and places the dealings of the community within the account of the life to come.

The passage has treated the wealth of the believers through many verses, and the sentence here supplies the reminder that accompanies the whole. The community's teachers read the clause as the ground of the instructions that the section has set out, since the law of the dealings rests on the return that the sentence names.

The wording addresses the community with the command of mindfulness, so that the reminder is given as an instruction rather than as information alone. The Book's method of attaching its teachings to the awareness of God appears throughout the sūrah, and the sentence in this place applies it to the matters of property.

**The Day Named**

The commentators explained the phrase as the description of the return to God at the end of the present life, and they gathered the passages that treat the subject. The community's teachers read the sentence with the verses about the account, so that the dealings of the believers were understood to belong to the material that the day will examine.

The wording places the return in the future and describes it as certain, and the community's scholars noted the form of the phrase. The Book's teaching about the last things supplies the framework in which the sentence stands, and the sūrah's other passages on the subject were read with it.

The scholars of the community also observed the position of the verse at the end of the passage on the practice, since the reminder is given after the instructions have been completed. The arrangement presents the law and the account together, so that the community received the two as one teaching.

**The Payment in Full**

The sentence states that every soul will be paid in full for what it has earned, and the community's teachers read the clause as the assurance that the account of the day will be exact. The Book's treatment of the subject elsewhere uses the same language of the payment that is completed, and the passages were gathered by the commentators.

The wording places the payment before the statement about the wrong, so that the sentence presents the outcome of the account as a matter of justice rather than of loss. The community's scholars read the two clauses together, and they drew from the pair the lesson that no one will be deprived of what his deeds have earned.

The teachers also noted the connection with the previous sentence about the debtor and the creditor, since the wealth of the community is described in the Book as a matter for the account. The passage's treatment of the dealings is completed by the reminder that the payment of the day is exact.

**The Wrong Named**

The sentence closes with the statement that no one will be wronged, and the community's teachers read the clause as the assurance that the account of the day is free of the faults that attend the dealings of the world. The Book uses the phrase in the passages that describe the justice of God, and the community's scholars gathered the instances.

The wording also supplies the answer to the anxiety of the person who has been treated unjustly in the matters of wealth. The teachers drew from the clause the lesson that the claims that the courts of the world fail to settle belong to the account that the sentence describes.

The scholars of the community used the phrase when they spoke about the consolation of the poor and the warning to those who hold the property of others. The passage's treatment of the subject ends with the statement that the account of the day is free of error.

**The Place in the Passage**

The sentence closes the passage that treats the practice and the dealings of the community, and the community's teachers read the arrangement as the passage's final word on the subject. The verses have stated the prohibition, the warning, the rule about the principal, the respite for the debtor, and the generosity that exceeds the duty, and the sentence here supplies the reminder that gathers them.

The community's scholars observed that the reminder of the return appears at the close of several of the sūrah's passages, so that each body of instruction ends with the account. The teachers used the arrangement when they taught the sūrah as a whole.

The verses that follow turn to the writing of the contracts, so that the treatment of the dealings continues with the practical instruction. The sentence in this place therefore completes the account of the practice before the passage takes up the arrangements of the community's agreements.

**The Teaching Drawn**

The community's preachers drew from the sentence the lesson that the wealth of the world is held under the account that the verse names. The material shows the reminder repeated in their teaching of the passage, and the community's conduct in the matters of property was formed by the instruction.

The scholars of the community also connected the sentence with the passages that describe the justice of God, so that the community's understanding of the account was built on the Book's own statements. The clause about the wrong supplied the ground of the community's confidence in the fairness of the judgment.

The passage therefore ends its treatment of the dealings with the statement that gathers the law, the mercy, and the account. The reader is given the instruction and the reminder together, and the community's teachers read the two as one teaching about the wealth of the believers.

**What the Verse Establishes**

The sentence establishes the command to be mindful of the day of the return, the certainty that every soul will be paid in full for what it has earned, and the assurance that no person will be wronged in the account. The community's teachers used the verse when they set out the law of the dealings and when they encouraged the believers to attend to the account of their property, and the sūrah's following verses turn to the writing of the contracts, so that the reminder and the practical instruction stand beside one another.

## Sūrah al-Anʿām 6:108

> ˹O believers!˺ Do not insult what they invoke besides Allah or they will insult Allah spitefully out of ignorance. This is how We have made each people’s deeds appealing to them. Then to their Lord is their return, and He will inform them of what they used to do.

**Expanded Commentary**

**The Prohibition on Reviling the Idols**

Do not insult what they invoke besides Allah. The verse turns from the position of the Prophet to the conduct of the believers, and it issues a prohibition that runs against the obvious religious instinct. The audience of the instruction was a small community in a city whose gods stood in the sanctuary and whose tribes were tied to them, and the instruction told them not to attack those gods with their tongues.

The Book's reason for the prohibition is stated in the same sentence. The verse does not deny that the idols are worthless; it says that insulting them will produce a response that damages the cause of the message, since the idolaters will insult God spitefully out of ignorance. The instruction is therefore concerned with consequences rather than with the status of the objects, and the Book's habit elsewhere supports the reading.

**The Reason Given in the Verse**

Or they will insult Allah spitefully out of ignorance. The prohibition rests on the effect the believers' words would have on the people they were calling. A man who is told that his gods are worthless may respond by attacking the God he has been invited to worship, and the insult would be uttered in ignorance, which is the condition the sūrah has repeatedly attributed to the audience's theology.

The Book applies the same reasoning in its instructions about how the message should be delivered. The sūrah of the Bee commands the Prophet to call people to the way of the Lord with wisdom and good instruction and to argue with them in the manner that is best (16:125), and the sūrah of the Spider gives the same instruction for disputes with the People of the Book (29:46). The prohibition in this verse is the negative form of that method: what the believers were not to do was to speak in a way that made their hearers worse.

**The Conduct the Verse Was Answering**

The practice the verse corrected was a real one. The early community in Makkah had been denouncing the gods of the Quraysh, and the idolaters responded by reviling the God of the Muslims. Reports describe the leaders of the city approaching the Prophet's uncle with a proposal that both sides stop attacking the objects the other venerated, and the verse was understood as the instruction that settled the community's conduct in the matter.

The reports give the instruction a practical setting, but the principle the verse states is independent of that setting. Whenever the reviling of a false object strengthens the hearer in his falsehood, the sentence applies, and the Book's measure is the effect of the speech on the person addressed rather than the desert of the object abused.

**The Deeds Made Fair to Each Community**

This is how We have made each people's deeds appealing to them. The sentence explains why the insult forbidden in the first clause would have the effect described. The idolaters were not weighing the evidence; their practices had been made attractive to them, and the Book describes that condition in many places. The sūrah of the Family of Imran says that the disbelievers should not think that God's respite for them is good for them, since He grants them time only that they may increase in sin (3:178); the sūrah of the Ranged says that those who do wrong are misled in their ways; and the sūrah of the Bee records the idolaters' claim that God willed their practices.

The Book's statements on this subject take several forms. In some passages it attributes the beautifying of evil deeds to Satan, and in others, including this verse, it attributes the arrangement to God, who leaves people to what they have chosen as a test. The two accounts are not in tension in the Book's own presentation, since the testing of human beings includes the possibility of their being confirmed in what they have chosen.

**The Return and the Accounting**

Then to their Lord is their return, and He will inform them of what they used to do. The verse closes by referring the whole question to the judgment, and the Book uses the same formula at the end of its discussions of the idolaters' conduct. The sūrah of the Spoils uses the phrase after a passage about those who were left behind, saying that their return is to God and that He will inform them of what they did (9:94); the sūrah of Tobā gives the same statement about God's informing people of their deeds (9:105); and the sūrah of Yūnus, the sūrah of the Spider, the sūrah of Luqmān, the sūrah of the Troops, and the sūrah of the Congregation use the same formula in their own contexts.

The formula is the Book's way of closing an argument without forcing its conclusion. The believers are told not to insult the gods of their neighbours, and the outcome of the dispute is left to the day when God will state what each party did. That arrangement is what allows the community to live among people whose practices it rejects.

**Why the Verse Matters for the Community's Conduct**

The instruction has consequences that reach beyond the setting of Makkah. The Book has presented the message as a call addressed to people capable of accepting or rejecting it, and this verse recognises that the call can be defeated by the manner of those who deliver it. The prohibition on insulting what others venerate is thus a rule about the means by which the message should be carried, and it sits alongside the commands to argue in the best manner and to speak gently where harshness would fail.

The verse also protects the community from the illusion that zeal in denunciation is the same as service to God. What the Book requires here is restraint in speech for the sake of the hearer, and the next verse takes up the oaths by which the idolaters promised to believe if a sign came to them, which is another instance of the audience's response to the message.

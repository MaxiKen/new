## Sūrah an-Nūr 24:17

> Allah forbids you from ever doing something like this again, if you are ˹true˺ believers.

**Expanded Commentary**

**The Divine Prohibition Against Recidivism: yaʿiẓukum Allāh**

The divine address delivers a firm legislative and ethical injunction against repeating past errors: *yaʿiẓukum Allāhu an taʿūdū li-mithlihī abadan*. The verb *waʿaẓa* in Qur'anic usage signifies admonition infused with moral urgency, spiritual concern, and authoritative warning. God does not merely issue a detached statutory command; He admonishes the community with the full weight of divine majesty, commanding them never to return (*an taʿūdū*) to anything resembling this catastrophic defamation (*li-mithlihī*) for all time (*abadan*).

The inclusion of the temporal marker *abadan* establishes an irrevocable, permanent barrier against recidivism. The crisis of the Grand Slander was not permitted to remain an isolated historical scandal that the community could conveniently forget once the immediate tension dissipated. It was transformed by divine decree into an everlasting lesson, a permanent moral vaccine designed to immunize the Muslim body politic against future outbreaks of collective hysteria, character assassination, and informational malice across all eras of human society. Divine admonition strikes at the root of moral complacency, establishing an unbreachable wall against repeat offenses.

**The Universality of the Prohibition: li-mithlihī**

A profound legal and ethical dimension resides within the phrasing *li-mithlihī*—to the like of it. The prohibition is not confined to the historical actors involved in the events in Madinah, nor is it restricted solely to the sacred person of the Mother of the Believers, ʿĀʾishah. Classical commentators emphasized that the comparative particle *mithl* universalizes the application of the ruling across the entirety of human existence.

Every unsubstantiated rumor, every malicious whisper, and every campaign of character assassination hurled against an innocent believer across history falls squarely under the divine ban of *li-mithlihī*. The trauma endured by the early community serves as an enduring prototype. By expanding the scope to anything resembling the historical slander, the revelation erects an unyielding protective canopy over the dignity and reputation of every upright member of the community until the Day of Judgment, shielding human honor from careless tongues and protecting the vulnerable from reputational ruin.

**Faith as the Condition and Crucible of Compliance**

The verse concludes with an incisive conditional clause: *in kuntum muʾminīn*, if you are true believers. Attaching obedience directly to the presence of faith (*īmān*) transforms the avoidance of slander from a mere social etiquette into an existential test of spiritual authenticity. A person who claims allegiance to God and His Messenger while casually participating in the demolition of innocent reputations exhibits a lethal contradiction within their religious identity.

True faith in the Qur'anic paradigm is not an abstract mental assent or formal ritual performance; it is an active moral force that governs the tongue, disciplines human curiosity, and structures social interaction. To hear the divine prohibition and subsequently engage in reckless gossip is to reveal a profound spiritual defect. The conditional phrasing serves as a piercing mirror held up to every listener, challenging them to prove the reality of their faith through steadfast verbal restraint and unwavering loyalty to fellow believers, demonstrating that authentic faith always safeguards communal peace.

**The Distinction Between Ignorant Error and Willful Defiance**

This divine admonition decisively recalibrates communal accountability. Prior to the descent of these explicit verses, believers who fell into the trap of repeating the rumors regarding ʿĀʾishah might have pleaded human weakness, cognitive confusion, social peer pressure, or lack of legislative clarity. They stumbled in the fog of an unprecedented crisis before the divine boundaries were fully drawn.

Verse 17 permanently eliminates that excuse from the community. Once divine instruction has been articulated with crystal clarity, repeating similar behavior ceases to be a forgivable lapse of judgment born of naivety; it becomes an act of conscious, willful rebellion against the clear prohibition of the Almighty. The descent of knowledge creates inescapable responsibility. By drawing an unyielding line in the sand, the verse ensures that future transgressors face the full severity of divine and communal censure, leaving no room for convenient excuses or claims of accidental participation.

**The Pedagogical Function of Communal Crisis**

Reflecting upon the broader divine pedagogy reveals why God permitted the fledgling Muslim community to undergo such an agonizing ordeal. Divine wisdom often allows a society to experience the painful consequences of a moral failing in order to burn the horror of that sin deeply into its collective memory, ensuring lasting cultural immunity.

The month of silence, suspicion, and familial grief was an intense moral crucible. Had the community merely received an abstract lecture against slander, the instruction might have been forgotten amid daily distractions. By experiencing the near-collapse of communal solidarity, the believers gained an unforgettable sensory appreciation of the destructive power of the tongue. The pain of the crisis became the emotional bedrock upon which an enduring culture of verbal sanctification was established, fortifying the community against future division and teaching believers to treasure the honor of their fellows.

**The Perpetual Barrier of abadan**

The temporal seal *abadan* (never/forever) demands that the community maintain perpetual historical vigilance. Human societies are notoriously prone to moral amnesia; generations pass, the memory of past catastrophes fades, and old vices inevitably resurface under new cultural disguises and technological instruments.

By embedding *abadan* into the text of the revelation, the Qur'an commands every successive generation to treat the protection of human honor as a non-negotiable imperative that cannot be eroded by the passage of centuries. Whether in oral conversation, written correspondence, or modern digital communication, the divine command stands unaltered. The word *abadan* echoes through time as an unyielding sentinel, guarding human dignity against the perennial human appetite for scandal and destruction, preserving the sanctity of faith forever across all corners of the earth.

## Sūrah al-Baqarah 2:273

> ˹Charity is˺ for the needy who are too engaged in the cause of Allah to move about in the land ˹for work˺. Those unfamiliar with their situation will think they are not in need ˹of charity˺ because they do not beg. You can recognize them by their appearance. They do not beg people persistently. Whatever you give in charity is certainly well known to Allah.

**Expanded Commentary**

**The Recipients Named**

The sentence names the poor who are constrained in the way of God and who cannot move about the land, and the community's teachers read the passage as the description of those for whom the charity of the believers is intended. The Book has spoken of the gift that is given for the sake of God, and the sentence here identifies the persons who are in need of it.

The wording places the constraint at the centre of the description, since the inability of the person is presented as the reason for his poverty. The community's scholars noted the phrase, because the Book does not describe the poor as idle but as unable to work.

The teachers drew from the sentence the lesson that the community's gift is directed to the persons whose condition is known rather than to those who make the loudest claim. The passage's concern for the dignity of the recipient was read with the instruction.

**The Constraint Explained**

The commentators explained the phrase as the description of a person who is held back from travelling for work, whether by the service of the community or by the circumstances of his situation. The teachers discussed the cases that the phrase covers, including those who are engaged in learning or in the defence of the community.

The wording also implies the poverty that follows the constraint, since the person who cannot work has no means of his own. The community's scholars described the condition of such a person as hidden from the view of his neighbours, and they read the sentence as the instruction that reaches him.

The reports of the community describe the circumstances of the city in which these verses were revealed, where the poor who served the community lived without the means to provide for themselves. The sentence in this place supplies the rule for their support.

**The People of the Veranda**

The commentators connected the sentence with the group of the poor who lived in the sheltered part of the mosque in Madinah, and the reports describe their devotion, their study, and their lack of means. The teachers named them among those for whom the passage was intended, and the material supplied the community with its example.

The reports also describe the manner in which the Prophet ﷺ provided for them and the way their needs were met from the gifts of the believers. The teachers of the community read the accounts together with the sentence, so that the instruction and its application were presented as one subject.

The material shows the place that the poorest members of the community held in its life, since the mosque itself sheltered them. The teachers drew from the passage the lesson that the community's worship and its care for its poor belong to the same order.

**The Restraint**

The sentence states that those who do not know the condition of such a person suppose him to be wealthy because of the restraint he observes, and the community's teachers read the clause as the description of a poverty that does not show itself. The wording places the restraint as the reason for the mistake of the observer.

The commentators explained the restraint as the dignity that keeps the person from asking and from displaying his need. The teachers described the state of the person who bears his condition without complaint, and they read the sentence as the Book's recognition of that conduct.

The jurists of the community drew from the clause the lesson that the visible condition of a person is not the measure of his need. The passage's instruction therefore requires of the believers an attention that passes beyond appearances.

**The Mark**

The sentence states that the community knows such people by the mark they bear, and the commentators discussed the signs by which their condition appears. The reports describe the pallor of hunger and the effects of the hardships that the person has met, and other explanations concern the humility that the face of a believer shows.

The teachers gathered the readings and noted the point on which they agree, that the sign is perceived by those who look with care. Scholars of the community connected the sentence with the reports about the insight that the Book grants to the believers, and they used the passage when they spoke about the attention that the community owes to its poor.

The wording also supplies the assurance that the persons described are known to God, and the sentence closes with the statement that the community will be repaid for whatever good it spends. The account of the poor and the promise to the giver stand together in the passage.

**The Reports About Asking**

The reports of the community preserve a saying of the Prophet ﷺ in which he described the poor person who restrains himself from asking, and the teachers used the material when they explained the sentence. The early teachers of the community also discussed the measure of the wealth that makes a person's asking improper, and they transmitted the positions that were worked out in the early generations.

The passage's instruction was applied by the community's judges when the gifts of the believers were distributed, and the reports describe the care with which the persons named in the sentence were identified. The material shows the manner in which the Book's teaching entered the practice of the city.

The commentators drew from the sentence the lesson that the community is to seek its poor rather than wait for their requests. The teachers repeated the instruction in their exhortations, and the passage supplied the community with the rule for the distribution of its charity.

**What the Verse Establishes**

The sentence establishes the identification of the poor who are constrained in the way of God, the restraint by which their condition is concealed, the mark by which the community recognizes them, the statement that they do not press their requests upon people, and the knowledge of God over whatever is given. The community's teachers used the verse when they set out the recipients of charity and when they urged the believers to seek those whose need was hidden, and the sūrah's next verse names those who spend by night and by day, so that the description of the recipients is followed by the account of the givers.

## Sūrah al-Isrāʾ 17:15

> **Whoever chooses to be guided, it is only for their own good. And whoever chooses to stray, it is only to their own loss. No soul burdened with sin will bear the burden of another. And We would never punish ˹a people˺ until We have sent a messenger ˹to warn them˺.**

**Expanded Commentary**

**Whoever Guided For Own Good**

Mani ihtadā fa-innamā yahtadī li-nafsihi — whoever is guided, then only he is guided for his self. Man, whoever, conditional. Ihtadā from h-d-y, to be guided, follow guidance, from same root as hudā, guidance, and hadā, to guide, and hādī, guide. Fa-innamā, then only, so only, restriction. Yahtadī from h-d-y, is guided, follows guidance. Li-nafsihi, for his self, for himself, from n-f-s, self, soul. Al-Ṭabarī explains that whoever chooses to be guided, follows most upright guidance of Qur'an that guides to most upright, it is only for own good, benefit returns to himself, not to Allah, because Allah not in need of creation. Al-Rāzī says guidance benefits self in world and Hereafter, as mighty reward mentioned in verse 9 for believers who do good. The phrase li-nafsihi emphasizes personal benefit: guided for self, not for other.

**Whoever Strays To Own Loss**

Wa-man ḍalla fa-innamā yaḍillu ʿalayhā — and whoever strays, then only he strays against it, to its loss. Man ḍalla from ḍ-l-l, to stray, go astray, from same root as ḍalāl, straying, misguidance. Fa-innamā yaḍillu, then only he strays, restriction. ʿAlayhā, against it, upon it, i.e., against his self, to its loss, from ʿ-l-w, upon, against. Al-Ṭabarī says whoever chooses to stray, not follow guidance, rejects most upright, it is only to own loss, harm returns to himself, not to Allah, because Allah not harmed by straying. Al-Qurṭubī says this is justice: guided for own good, straying to own loss, each responsible for own choice, as destiny bound to neck and record laid open shows own deeds.

**No Soul Bears Burden Of Another**

Wa-lā taziru wāziratun wizra ukhrā — and no bearer bears burden of another. Lā taziru from w-z-r, to bear, carry burden, from same root as wizr, burden, sin, heavy load, and wāzir, bearer, minister who bears burden of king. Wāziratun, bearer, female form used for soul, nafs, as bearer of burden, soul that bears. Wizra ukhrā, burden of another, from w-z-r and ā-kh-r, other, another. Al-Ṭabarī says no soul burdened with sin will bear burden of another, meaning each soul bears own burden, own sins, not others', on Day each reads own book and sufficient self as reckoner, no transferring. Al-Rāzī says this is principle of justice: no soul bears another's burden, each responsible for own deeds bound to neck. This principle appears in several places (6:164, 35:18, 39:7, 53:38) to affirm individual responsibility, and does not contradict that one who misleads others bears burden of misleading in addition to own, not instead of theirs, as hadith says whoever calls to misguidance bears its burden and burden of those who follow without diminishing theirs.

**We Never Punish Until Messenger Sent**

Wa-mā kunnā muʿadhdhibīna ḥattā nabʿatha rasūlan — and We were not punishers until We send a messenger. Mā kunnā, We were not, We would never, negation of past with emphasis of never. Muʿadhdhibīna from ʿ-dh-b, to punish, chastise, from same root as ʿadhāb, punishment, torment. Ḥattā, until. Nabʿatha from b-ʿ-th, to send, raise, dispatch, from same root as baʿth, sending, resurrection, raising. Rasūlan from r-s-l, messenger, apostle, from arsala, to send. Al-Ṭabarī explains that We would never punish a people until We have sent messenger to warn them, establish proof, as justice requires that people know guidance before being punished for rejecting, so that no one says we did not know. Al-Qurṭubī says this indicates that punishment in world or Hereafter only after messenger sent to warn, so that proof established, hujjah, as in story of Children of Isra'il who were given Book as guidance after Moses, warned, then punished when promise came due to rebellion.

**Why This Verse Follows Reading Book**

Verses 13-14 said destiny bound to neck, record laid open, read your book, sufficient yourself as reckoner. Verse 15 says whoever guided for own good, whoever strayed to own loss, no bearer bears burden of another, We never punish until messenger sent. The connection is that after describing individual record and self-reckoning, the sūrah affirms principle of individual responsibility and justice: guided for self, strayed against self, no bearing others burden, and no punishment until messenger sent to warn. This explains why record matters: because each responsible for own guidance, not others', and punishment only after warning, so record is fair. The person who reads own book cannot blame other, because no bearer bears another's burden, and cannot claim no warning, because messenger sent.

**Connection To Children Of Isra'il And Night Journey**

The sūrah's narrative of Night Journey and Children of Isra'il who were given Book as guidance illustrates sending messenger Moses with Book, then warning about corruption twice, then punishment. The principle that We never punish until messenger sent explains why Children of Isra'il were warned via Book and messengers before punishment when promise came. Similarly, Prophet Muhammad taken on Night Journey and given Qur'an that guides to most upright is messenger sent to warn current people, so that proof established before punishment. The verse therefore justifies sending messengers and establishes that current audience has been warned via Qur'an.

**Linguistic Note On Wazira And Muʿadhdhibin**

Ihtadā from h-d-y, to be guided. Nafs from n-f-s, self. Ḍalla from ḍ-l-l, to stray. Wazara from w-z-r, to bear burden, from wizr, burden, heavy load, from wazara, to carry. Wāzira, bearer, female because nafs feminine, soul. Wizra ukhrā, burden of another. Muʿadhdhibīn from ʿ-dh-b, to punish. Nabʿatha from b-ʿ-th, to send. Rasūl from r-s-l, messenger. The verse's structure — whoever guided for self, whoever strayed against self, no bearer bears another burden, We never punish until messenger sent — with four principles, establishes justice and mercy: guidance benefits self, straying harms self, no transferring burden, no punishment without warning via messenger, all foundations of divine justice.

**How This Verse Teaches Justice And Mercy**

This verse teaches justice and mercy: guidance for own good, straying to own loss, no soul bears another's burden, no punishment until messenger sent to warn. Al-Rāzī says believer should be grateful that Allah sends messengers to warn and guide to most upright, and should take guidance for own good, not delay, because burden is own and will be in book laid open. The sūrah's moral code that follows is guidance sent via messenger Prophet Muhammad who was taken on Night Journey, guiding to most upright. Following it is for own good with mighty reward; straying is to own loss with painful punishment. The principle also teaches against blaming others: each bears own burden, so focus on own deeds bound to neck and record laid open, not on others.

**A Lesson On Individual Responsibility**

No soul burdened bears burden of another — this principle of individual responsibility is central to Qur'anic justice. It means on Day, each reads own book, sufficient self as reckoner, no one bears another's sins, though one may bear burden of misleading others in addition to own, not instead. The verse also assures that Allah would never punish until messenger sent, showing mercy and justice: warning before punishment, explanation in detail before reckoning, as verse 12 said everything explained in detail. The believer who understands this will hasten to guidance for own good, not pray for evil out of haste, and will follow most upright guidance that was sent via messenger, seeking mighty reward and avoiding painful punishment.

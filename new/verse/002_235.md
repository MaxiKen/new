## Sūrah al-Baqarah 2:235

> There is no blame on you for subtly showing interest in ˹divorced or widowed˺ women or for hiding ˹the intention˺ in your hearts. Allah knows that you are considering them ˹for marriage˺. But do not make a secret commitment with them—you can only show interest in them appropriately. Do not commit to the bond of marriage until the waiting period expires. Know that Allah is aware of what is in your hearts, so beware of Him. And know that Allah is All-Forgiving, Most Forbearing.

**Expanded Commentary**

**The Permission to Indicate**

The verse states that the community commits no wrong in making an indication of a proposal of marriage to women who are within their waiting period, or in keeping such an intention to themselves, and it then describes the knowledge of God from which the practice is conducted. The sentence follows the rule that fixed the waiting period and supplies the guidance for the period itself.

The Book's concern is to protect the woman's position while allowing the community to arrange its affairs, and the community's teachers read the sentence as the permission that accompanies the rule. Readers are shown that the legislation separates the proposal from the marriage contract and places the first within the bounds that the passage describes.

**The Distinction Drawn**

The sentence permits the indication but not the contract, and the commentators explained the difference between the speech that suggests a future union and the agreement by which the marriage is concluded. The wording distinguishes the two, and the community's jurists built the law on the distinction.

The instruction also permits the community to keep the intention within itself, and the community's teachers read the clause as the statement that the secret intention is not held against a person. Readers are given the account of a ruling that allows the community to make its arrangements while preserving the period that the previous verse required, and the sentence supplies both permissions together.

**The Knowledge of God Stated**

The sentence states that God knows what the community holds within itself and instructs them to be mindful of Him, and the community's scholars read the clause as the ground of the permission. The Book's practice of attaching the reference to God's knowledge to its permissions appears throughout the sūrah.

The wording places the arrangement before the One who knows the intention, and the community's teachers used the clause when they set out the ethics of the proposal. The passage shows that the permission is bounded by the awareness that the intention is seen, and the sentence supplies the reminder that accompanies the instruction.

**The Prohibition of the Secret Contract**

The sentence forbids the community to make a secret agreement to marry and requires that the speech be honourable, and the commentators explained the phrase as the statement of the purpose to marry rather than a hidden contract. The community's jurists read the clause as the prohibition of the arrangement that would bypass the waiting period.

The wording also forbids the community to resolve on the contract before the term has ended, and the reports show the cases to which the prohibition applied. The account describes a rule that keeps the community's arrangements open while preventing the abuse of the period, and the sentence supplies the standard by which the speech is judged.

**The Reports and Applications**

The commentaries record the questions the community put to the Prophet ﷺ about the proposals made during the waiting period, and they describe the guidance that was given. The reports show the community applying the sentence to its own circumstances.

The material also describes the differences among the teachers about the phrases that may be used, and the community's jurists recorded the positions. The passage shows that the Book's legislation on the subject attends to the manner of the speech as well as to its content, and the sentence supplies the principle from which the applications were drawn.

**The Closing Name**

The sentence closes with the description of God as forgiving and forbearing, and the community's scholars read the pair as the ground of the permission that the sentence has granted. The attributes accompany the ruling as they accompany the other provisions about the household.

The Book's treatment of the subject connects the leniency of the law with the knowledge of the one who made it, and the reader is shown that the instruction leaves room for the community's weakness while requiring the observance of the period. The passage continues with the rules about the divorce that follows a betrothal.

**The Speech Before the Contract**

The distinction the sentence draws between the indication and the agreement belongs to the law of the marriage contract, and the jurists described the two forms of speech in detail. A man may tell the woman, in a manner that does not constitute a request for the contract, that he wishes to marry her, and the schools set out the phrases that they placed within the permission and those that they placed outside it.

The clause that permits the intention to be kept within the heart has been read as a statement about the mercy of the legislation, since the community could not be answerable for what it considered silently. The teachers noted the difference between that permission and the secret agreement, which the sentence forbids because it closes the matter before the term has ended.

The closing attributes accompany the instruction, and the community's practice followed the distinction the sentence draws. The reports record the proposals that the companions made during the waiting periods of those they wished to marry, and the judges of the early community applied the wording to the cases before them.

**What the Verse Establishes**

The sentence establishes the permission to indicate a proposal during the waiting period, the permission to keep the intention secret, the prohibition of the secret contract, the requirement that the speech be honourable, and the attributes of God that accompany the ruling. The community's teachers used the verse when they set out the law on the subject, and the reader is shown that the Book's legislation on the household joins the protection of the period with the freedom of the community to make its arrangements.

The sentence set the standard for the speech of the community in a matter where the feelings of the woman and the rights of the former husband meet. The teachers used the passage when they explained that the Book protects the period while allowing the community to plan its affairs, and the attributes at the close place the hidden intention before God.

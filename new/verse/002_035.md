## Sūrah al-Baqarah 2:35

> We cautioned, “O Adam! Live with your wife in Paradise and eat as freely as you please, but do not approach this tree, or else you will be wrongdoers.”

**Expanded Commentary**

**Dwell, Eat, and Do Not Approach — Three Imperatives That Frame Freedom**

“We cautioned, ‘O Adam! Live with your wife in Paradise and eat as freely as you please, but do not approach this tree, or else you will be wrongdoers’” (2:35). Three commands set the conditions of the first dwelling: uskun — dwell, reside at rest; kulā — eat you two; lā taqrabā — do not you two approach. The third is not “do not eat from” but “do not approach.” The prohibition draws a perimeter wider than the act: not only consumption is warned against, but nearness that makes consumption easy.

The address “O Adam!” gathers both spouses, and “with your wife” — zawjuk — names partnership before progeny. The Qur’an does not here name her Hawwāʾ; it says “your wife.” Early tafsīr records the name Eve from transmitted reports, but the Qur’anic register keeps her as zawj — spouse, pair, counterpart — which places the dwelling in the key of companionship rather than isolated testing. The command is plural “you two” (kulā, taqrabā), stressing shared agency and shared responsibility.

**“In Paradise” — Which Garden?**

Al-jannah in this verse is called Paradise without article distinction in some translations, “the Garden” in literal rendering. The source rehearses the classic debate:

- One view treats this Garden as not yet the Garden of Reward promised to believers — arguing that Iblīs’s lie could not be uttered in that Garden where “they hear therein neither idle talk nor lying” (78:35), that the “go down” (2:36) might mean relocation rather than vertical descent as in “go down to a town” (2:61), that those who enter the Reward-Garden are not expelled (15:48), and that a creature made of earth would not need to ascend to be honored. The point of that view is to say the trial Garden was an earthly or distinct paradisal enclosure.

- The consensus view — reported by al-Rāzī and al-Qurṭubī as the majority — holds it is the same Paradise promised, “the Garden” well known (al-jannah), taken in its proper referent. On that reading, “go down” signals a descent from that high abode, the lack-of-lying verse describes the Reward after judgment rather than the pre-trial dwell, and Iblīs’s whisper could reach from outside (as al-Ṭabarī suggests) or the expulsion rule applies after requital, not before trial. The majority holds the first dwelling was in the eternal Garden, from which Adam and his wife were then caused to descend for earthly khilāfah.

Al-Rāzī, surveying both, concludes that textual proofs are not decisive either way and that one should “refrain from being categorical. And God knows best.” That reserve is exemplary: the sūrah does not require settling cosmology to receive the moral point. What is firm is dwelling, breadth of eating, one tree excluded, and consequence named as ẓulm — wrongdoing.

**“Eat as Freely as You Please” — Breadth Before Boundary**

“Eat… freely” — raghadan ḥaythu shiʾtumā — “amply, wherever you two wish.” The permission is expansive. One tree is excepted against a background of abundance. The shape matters: freedom is the norm, limit the exception, and limit is named before breach. The human first hears “dwell and eat abundantly,” not only “do not.” Gratitude is invited before restraint is tested.

This contrasts with later mischaracterizations of divine command as primarily prohibitive. Here prohibition is a single tree in a garden of unrestricted eating. The pedagogy is clear: the bound is memorable because the bounty is vast.

**Why “Do Not Approach” Rather Than “Do Not Eat”**

To forbid approach (qurba) is to block the path, not only the end. Many later commands follow this pattern: do not approach shameful acts, do not approach the orphan’s wealth save with good, do not approach prayer while intoxicated. The Qur’an fences the act with distance. Ethically, it anticipates how compromise advances: approach leads to sampling, sampling to taking, taking to habit.

For Adam and his wife, the tree itself is not described as poisonous or otherwise special; its significance is that it is the one excepted thing. The test is obedience in a single point against a background of ease — the archetypal test of trust.

**What Tree Was It? — Retreating from Certainty**

The source notes divergent reports — wheat or grain, grapevine, fig — and that the Qur’an avoids “fruit” language, saying only shajarah, which can mean tree, shrub, or stalk. Some reports mention Eve’s unwitnessed naming and the serpent-form of Iblīs and locations of descent; the source classes these, and even much of what al-Ṭabarī collects, as folklore not rising to sound hadith. The Qur’an leaves the kind of tree unspecified, and tafsīr that specifies should be marked UNVERIFIED when lacking a Sahih chain.

What matters is not botany but boundary: there was a lived limit, and crossing it was presented as wrongdoing before it was done.

**“Or Else You Will Be Wrongdoers” — The Consequence Named Beforehand**

“Or else you will be among the wrongdoers” — fatakūnā min al-ẓālimīn. The consequence is framed as joining a category already known: to do this is to be unjust, to place a thing where it should not be placed. Ẓulm here is not yet polytheism or social oppression in mature sense; it is trespass against a stated limit with full provision and clear warning. The human is not tricked into wrongdoing by ambiguity; the category is pronounced before the act.

This pre-naming serves mercy: one cannot be held responsible for an unannounced limit. The limit was announced, the breadth of permission announced, and the name of breach announced, so that the subsequent slip (2:36) occurs in light, not in darkness.

**Companionship in Trial**

The second-person plural throughout — live you two, eat you two, approach you two — refuses to isolate blame on one spouse. The next verse will note that Satan caused them both to slip. The Genesis echo that magnifies one actor is not followed. Both dwell, both are addressed, both are tested, both slip, both are taught words to return (2:37). Shared dwelling means shared vigilance.

**From Naming to Dwelling**

If 2:31–33 established Adam as namer, 2:35 establishes him as dweller. Naming fits khilāfah in intellect; dwelling tests khilāfah in appetite and limit. The earth furnished “for you” (2:29) is rehearsed in miniature here as a garden furnished for two, with one exception. Mastery of language will mean little if mastery of desire fails. The sūrah therefore pairs the two qualifications for trust: to name truly and to refrain faithfully.

**Living the Verse**

- Keep the bound wider than the act. Do not merely avoid the forbidden act; avoid approaching it. That is the Qur’anic fencing that the tree teaches first.
- Remember abundance when restraint pinches. The “one tree” is felt differently when the rest of the Garden remains “eat freely wherever you wish.” Many falls begin by fixating on the single “no” until the many “yes” fade from view.
- Count wrongdoing by announced standard, not by after-the-fact rationalization. The verse names the act as ẓulm before it is done, so that later remorse can be clear-eyed and later repentance can be well-aimed.

**A Note on What to Transmit**

Details of Eve’s creation, the species of the tree, Iblīs’s form, or the geography of descent should not be presented as prophetic fact without a Sahih chain. The Qur’anic text — dwelling with spouse in the Garden, unrestricted eating, approach-prohibition of one tree, breach as ẓulm (2:35) — is firm; the encrusted narratives should be signaled as UNVERIFIED when mentioned, following al-Rāzī’s caution that “one should cease, and refrain from being categorical.”

## Sūrah al-Nisāʾ 4:108

> They try to hide ˹their deception˺ from people, but they can never hide it from Allah—in Whose presence they plot by night what is displeasing to Him. And Allah is Fully Aware of what they do.

### Expanded Commentary

**Concealed plotting is still visible to God**

The wrongdoers try to hide from people and spend the night planning what displeases Allah. Night here evokes secrecy and confidence that no witness is present. Yet Allah is with them in knowledge, not as an approving companion. The hidden meeting is as exposed to Him as the public speech.

**A culture of accountability**

The verse does not call believers to invade private spaces or assume that every private conversation is criminal. It addresses those who knowingly plot deception. The correct social response is evidence, lawful investigation, and protection of the innocent—not gossip or vigilante surveillance.

The closing awareness of all their actions reaches beyond the particular theft. A person should ask whether the plan would still seem acceptable if brought into daylight before God. If not, secrecy is not privacy but an alarm.

**Privacy is not immunity**

People need private spaces for counsel and repentance, but secrecy does not convert a plot into a right. When conduct threatens others, lawful investigation may be necessary. The verse encourages evidence-based accountability while reminding investigators that they too are visible to the same God who sees the hidden meeting.

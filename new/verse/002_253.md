## Sūrah al-Baqarah 2:253

> We have chosen some of those messengers above others. Allah spoke directly to some, and raised some high in rank. To Jesus, son of Mary, We gave clear proofs and supported him with the holy spirit. If Allah had willed, succeeding generations would not have fought ˹among themselves˺ after receiving the clear proofs. But they differed—some believed while others disbelieved. Yet if Allah had willed, they would not have fought one another. But Allah does what He wills.

**Expanded Commentary**

**The Preference Among the Messengers**

The verse states that God has preferred some of the messengers over others, and it names the ones to whom He spoke and the one whom He supported with the holy spirit. The sentence follows the account of the earlier community and treats the standing of the messengers as a body.

The Book's concern is the ordering that exists among those who carried its message, and the community's teachers read the sentence as the statement that the messengers share the office while differing in the gifts they were given. Readers are shown that the passage addresses the community's understanding of the prophetic mission, and the sentence supplies the principle that governs the subject.

The commentators explained the phrase about those to whom God spoke as a reference to Moses, and they recorded the identifications that the community's teachers gave. The wording names the honour without naming the person, and the community's scholars read the clause as the statement about the rank of the prophet who received the law.

The Book's treatment of the subject elsewhere refers to Moses as the one who was addressed by God, and the community's teachers gathered the passages. **The Support Named**

The sentence states that Jesus the son of Mary was given the clear proofs and was supported with the holy spirit, and the community's scholars read the clause as the statement about the gifts that accompanied his mission. The commentators explained the phrase about the holy spirit as a reference to the angel who brought the revelation or to the spirit that strengthened him.

**The Difference Among the Peoples**

The sentence states that if God had willed, those who came after the messengers would not have fought one another after the clear proofs had come, and it states that they differed, with some believing and some disbelieving. The community's teachers read the clause as the statement about the freedom that the guidance leaves to those who receive it.

The wording places the conflict among the peoples within the knowledge of God rather than in his compulsion, and the community's scholars noted the difference between the messengers' agreement and their followers' disputes. The passage shows that the passage explains the divisions among the communities of the prophets, and the sentence supplies the account of their cause.

**The Will of God Stated**

The sentence states that if God had willed they would not have fought, and it closes with the statement that God does what He intends, and the community's teachers read the two clauses together as the statement about the authority that governs the affairs of the peoples. The Book's practice of asserting the will of God at the close of its statements appears throughout the sūrah.

**The One Mission and the Many Gifts**

The differences that the sentence names belong to the gifts that the messengers received, and the community's teachers kept the distinction between the rank of the mission and the measure of the generosity that each prophet carried. **What the Verse Establishes**

The sentence establishes the preference among the messengers, the honour of the one who was addressed by God, the gifts given to Jesus, the responsibility of the peoples for their divisions, and the will of God that governs the outcome. The community's teachers used the verse when they spoke about the prophets, and the reader is shown that the sūrah presents the messengers as a single mission with differing gifts and that it places the response of the peoples with those who received them.

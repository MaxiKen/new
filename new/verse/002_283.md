## Sūrah al-Baqarah 2:283

> If you are on a journey and a scribe cannot be found, then a security can be taken. If you trust one another, then ˹there is no need for a security, but˺ the debtor should honour this trust ˹by repaying the debt˺—and let them fear Allah, their Lord. And do not conceal the testimony, for whoever conceals it, their hearts are indeed sinful. And Allah ˹fully˺ knows what you do.

**Expanded Commentary**

**The Provision Stated**

The sentence provides for the agreement that is made on a journey where no scribe can be found, and it permits a pledge to be taken in hand. The verse follows the long instruction about the writing of the contracts and supplies the arrangement for the circumstances in which the writing is not possible.

The wording also allows the case in which the two parties trust one another, so that the person who is trusted delivers what he owes without the pledge. The community's teachers read the two clauses together, so that the passage attends to the security of the claim and to the honesty of the person who holds it.

The sentence in this place completes the treatment of the community's agreements, and the community's scholars read the passage as the law of the dealings that the sūrah has been setting out. The arrangement for the journey is presented as a concession within the general instruction.

**The Pledge Explained**

The commentators explained the pledge as the property that the debtor gives to the creditor as a guarantee until the debt is paid, and they described the practice as it was conducted in the market of the city. The community's jurists built the rules of the security upon the sentence and the reports that accompany it.

The teachers noted the purpose of the arrangement, which is the protection of the creditor in the absence of the written record and the witnesses. The Book's treatment of the subject elsewhere supplies the framework of the dealings, and the sentence in this place applies it to the journey.

The scholars of the community also discussed the cases in which the pledge is required and those in which the trust of the parties takes its place. The material shows the care with which the community's law attended to the circumstances of its members.

**The Trust Named**

The sentence states that where one of the two parties trusts the other, the person who is trusted is to deliver what he owes, and the community's teachers read the clause as the statement that the honesty of the person is the foundation of the arrangement. The wording instructs the person who owes to fulfil his obligation and to be mindful of his Lord.

The commentators noted the phrase, since the passage places the duty on the person who has received the property rather than on the one who has given it. The community's scholars drew from the clause the lesson that the community's dealings rest on the faith of its members, and they used the sentence when they spoke about the trust that the Book requires.

The teachers also observed the difference between the two arrangements, since the pledge protects the creditor while the reliance on trust honors the debtor. The passage presents both, so that the community's law and its conduct are treated together.

**The Testimony Protected**

The sentence then forbids the concealment of the testimony, and it states that the person who conceals it has a heart that is sinful. The community's commentators explained the clause as the instruction that the witness is to give what he knows when the matter is brought before the community.

The teachers noted the severity of the phrase about the heart, since the Book describes the fault as a condition of the person rather than as an act alone. The community's scholars used the clause when they set out the duties of the witnesses, and the passage's earlier instruction about the calling of the witnesses was read with it.

The community's jurists discussed the range of the clause, and they described the cases in which the concealment of the testimony is forbidden. The sentence in this place supplies the principle that the record of the community's dealings is not to be withheld.

**The Names at the Close**

The verse closes with the statement that God knows what the community does, and the community's teachers read the clause as the ground of the instructions that the passage has given. The attribute places the writings, the pledges, the trusts, and the testimonies within the knowledge that records them.

The scholars of the community drew from the closing the lesson that the honesty of the community's members is observed by the One who knows their conduct. The teachers used the clause when they answered those who imagined that a fault in the dealings of the world could be concealed.

The Book's practice of ending its instructions with a description of God appears throughout the sūrah, and the community's scholars read the arrangement as the statement that the whole of the community's conduct belongs to the account. The sentence therefore leaves the community with the arrangements of its agreements and with the knowledge that observes them.

**The Relation to the Writing**

The commentators discussed the relation between the sentence and the long verse about the writing of the contracts, and some of the early teachers held that the provision of the sentence applies to the circumstances of the journey while the writing remains the rule of the settled case. Others described the sentence as the statement of the general permission, and the community's scholars recorded the positions.

The material shows the care with which the community's jurists read the two verses together, and the rules of the contracts were built upon the passages as a whole. The sentence in this place supplies the provision that completes the instruction.

The teachers also drew from the passage the lesson that the community's law attends to the circumstances of its members, since the instruction for the journey is presented beside the rule for the settled place. The community's scholars used the arrangement when they set out the flexibility of the law.

**What the Verse Establishes**

The sentence establishes the provision for the agreement made on a journey, the permission of the pledge, the arrangement of the trust between the two parties, the prohibition of concealing the testimony, and the knowledge of God over the conduct of the community. The community's teachers used the verse when they set out the law of the dealings and the duties of the witnesses, and the sūrah's next verse turns to the knowledge of God over what the community conceals and discloses, so that the instruction and its ground are presented in order.

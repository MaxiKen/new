## Sūrah Fāṭir 35:31

> The Book We have revealed to you ˹O Prophet˺ is the truth, confirming what came before it. Surely Allah is All-Aware, All-Seeing of His servants.

**Expanded Commentary**

**Revelation Is a Truthful Gift From Allah**

The verse identifies the revealed Book as truth. This is more than saying that it contains useful teachings. Its origin, message, promises, and judgments are reliable. The Qur'an does not ask the Prophet to present it as a personal insight or a cultural artifact. It is revelation from Allah, and its truth gives the rest of the sūrah a firm foundation.

Truth in this context includes correspondence with reality and sound guidance for action. The Book tells people who created them, why they are needy, how they should worship, and what awaits them after death. It also exposes the false promises of idols and worldly power. A text that performs these tasks cannot be treated as spiritually interesting but morally optional. To recognize it as truth is to allow it to judge the assumptions by which one lives.

**Confirmation Connects the Qur’an to Earlier Revelation**

The Book confirms what came before it. Confirmation means that the Qur'an affirms the original divine source of earlier scriptures and agrees with their central call to worship Allah and live justly. It does not mean that every form of every earlier text in human hands is beyond question. The Qur'an presents itself as a criterion that confirms truth and clarifies disputes.

This continuity appears in 2:41, 3:3, and 5:48. Allah's guidance is not a series of unrelated religions invented by different communities. Prophets brought one essential message while laws and circumstances varied. The Qur'an restores that common center for the final community. It honors Moses, David, Jesus, and the earlier prophets without placing them in competition with the revelation given to Muhammad.

**The Final Book Also Corrects Human Distortion**

A confirmation can include correction. When people forget, alter, or interpret revelation to serve an interest, a later revelation can recover what was obscured. The Qur'an's role as a criterion does not authorize contempt for earlier communities. It places responsibility on the reader to distinguish divine guidance from human additions and to accept correction when the Qur'an exposes it.

This also applies to Muslims. Possessing the Book does not guarantee that every interpretation or practice is sound. The revealed text remains a judge over the community that recites it. A person may use religious language to defend pride, injustice, or empty ritual, but the Book's truth will not bend to that use. Confirmation therefore demands submission from its own recipients before it becomes a tool for judging others.

**Allah’s Awareness Gives the Revelation a Living Address**

The verse ends with Allah's awareness and sight of His servants. Revelation is not an impersonal message dropped into history. Allah knows the people who receive it, the conditions under which they hear it, and the hidden motives with which they accept or resist it. His awareness includes the Prophet's burden and the community's needs. His sight includes every act performed in response.

This makes the Book both universal and personal. Its principles address all people, yet each reader encounters them within a particular life. Allah knows which command is difficult, which warning is timely, and which act of obedience is hidden. The servant cannot claim that the Book was irrelevant to his circumstances when the Revealer knows those circumstances completely.

The phrase confirming what came before also gives the Qur'an a historical argument. Its message does not emerge without roots. It places the Prophet within the line of revelation and answers the claim that his teaching is an invention. The confirmation is not merely a similarity of vocabulary. It concerns the shared call to divine oneness, moral accountability, prayer, justice, and the reality of the return. Differences in law or detail belong to different communities and times, while the Lord and the essential direction remain one. The Book also confirms by correcting false rivalries among prophets. No messenger needs to be rejected in order to honor Muhammad, and no messenger can be worshipped in place of Allah. The Qur'an's truth creates a balanced memory: respect the earlier guidance, accept the final clarification, and refuse the human tendency to turn revelation into tribal property. Allah's awareness and sight make this historical continuity relevant to the present reader. He knows whether a person approaches the Book to obey, to win an argument, or to acquire status. The truth remains stable, but the moral value of reading it depends on the reader's response.

A reader who accepts this must allow the Book to correct him before using it to correct others. Submission begins with the self.

The Book is therefore a mercy that also judges. It guides the willing and exposes the excuses of those who refuse its truth.

Its truth reaches the whole person.

**Truthful Revelation Calls for Truthful Living**

The correct response is not admiration alone. If the Book is truth, the reader must let it shape speech, worship, trade, family life, and judgment. A person may need time to learn and change, but he cannot treat obedience as a decorative consequence. The truth of revelation is proved in the honesty it produces and the falsehood it removes.

The verse therefore joins theology to accountability. The Qur'an confirms earlier guidance, gives the final measure, and comes from the Lord who sees every servant. Those who accept it should do so with humility, not with a sense of owning the truth. They are recipients of a gift and bearers of a trust. The Book stands firm whether people obey or resist; their position before Allah is what changes when they respond to it.

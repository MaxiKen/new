## Sūrah al-Baqarah 2:229

> Divorce may be retracted twice, then the husband must retain ˹his wife˺ with honour or separate ˹from her˺ with grace. It is not lawful for husbands to take back anything of the dowry given to their wives, unless the couple fears not being able to keep within the limits of Allah. So if you fear they will not be able to keep within the limits of Allah, there is no blame if the wife compensates the husband to obtain divorce. These are the limits set by Allah, so do not transgress them. And whoever transgresses the limits of Allah, they are the ˹true˺ wrongdoers.

**Expanded Commentary**

**The Limit on Divorce**

The verse states that divorce may be retracted twice, after which the husband must either keep his wife honourably or release her virtuously. The sentence limits the number of times a declaration may be made and revoked, and the community's teachers read the ruling as a measure against the practice of keeping a woman in uncertainty.

The Book's treatment of the subject connects the number of the declarations with the way the marriage ends, and the sentence supplies both the limit and the two courses that follow it. Readers are shown that the legislation provides for the continuation of the marriage and for its conclusion, and the choice is placed within the bounds of honourable conduct.

**The Practice Addressed**

The commentators described the practice that the verse corrected, in which a man would declare divorce and take his wife back before the waiting period ended, repeating the process in order to keep her without giving her either the security of the marriage or the freedom to leave. The sentence's limit closes the arrangement.

The reports also describe the position of the wife within that practice, and the community's teachers noted that the ruling protects her from a situation she could not resolve. Readers are given the account of a measure whose purpose is the protection of the weaker party in the household, and the sentence supplies the rule that the community applied.

**The Prohibition of Taking Back the Dowry**

The sentence forbids the husband to take back any of what he has given his wife, with the exception of the case in which the two fear that they cannot keep within the limits God has set. The community's teachers read the prohibition as a statement about the property that the wife received as part of the marriage contract.

The commentators explained the exception as the arrangement by which a wife obtains a divorce by returning what she has received, and they noted the difference between that arrangement and the case in which the husband initiates the separation. The Book's treatment of the subject elsewhere supplies the details, and the reader is shown that the property arrangements of the marriage are governed by the same concern for justice that the sentence applies.

**The Compensation for Divorce**

The sentence states that if the two fear they cannot keep within the limits of God there is no blame in what the wife gives to obtain the divorce, and the community's jurists built the procedure of the arranged separation on the clause. The commentators explain that the wife's initiative in this case is met by the return of part or all of what she received.

The arrangement gives the wife a means of ending a marriage that she cannot continue, and the community's teachers read the sentence as a measure of the legislation's attention to both parties. The passage shows that the Book provides for the ending of a marriage at the instance of the wife as well as at that of the husband, and the sentence's wording keeps the matter within the limits that the surrounding verses describe.

**The Limits Named**

The sentence closes with the statement that these are the limits set by God and that whoever transgresses them is among the wrongdoers, and the phrase runs through the verses about the household. The community's scholars read the clause as the warning that accompanies the legislation, and they used it when they set out the seriousness of the arrangements.

The Book's practice of calling its rulings limits appears throughout the sūrah, and the sentence in this place places the rules of divorce within that body of material. The passage shows that the provisions are not a set of options among which the parties may choose freely, and the sentence leaves them with the statement about the boundary they may not cross.

**The Place of the Verse in the Section**

The sentence stands at the head of the passage on the number of declarations and precedes the rule about the third divorce and the arrangements for the waiting period. The Book has treated the oath of abstention and the first two declarations, and it now supplies the limit that governs the whole procedure.

The section's concern is the concluding of a marriage in an orderly manner, and the community's teachers read the verses as a unit when they taught the subject. The text supplies the rule and its reason together, and the passage continues with the provision that follows the third declaration.

**The Limit That Protects the Woman**

The report behind the sentence describes a woman who came to the Prophet ﷺ complaining about a husband who divorced and then took her back repeatedly, and the answer named the limit that closed the practice. The early community knew marriages in which the wife could neither keep her position nor obtain her freedom, and the ruling removed the arrangement by which that condition was maintained.

The procedure of the arranged separation also grows from the sentence. The wife who returns what she has received in order to obtain her release is named in the clause, and the reports describe the woman who came with the request and the ruling that returned the garden she had been given. The teachers noted that the Book gives the wife a path out of a household she cannot continue, and the jurists built the rules of the practice upon the wording.

The sentence's closing warning about the limits belongs to the whole section and it addresses both parties. What the passage protects is the position of the person whose claim is weaker, and the law of the household rests upon that concern.

**What the Verse Establishes**

The sentence establishes the number of the declarations, the two honourable courses that follow them, the prohibition of taking back what the wife has received, the arrangement by which she may obtain a divorce, and the warning that accompanies the limits. The community's teachers used the verse when they set out the law, and the reader is shown that the Book's legislation protects the woman's position within the household and provides a path out of a marriage that cannot continue.

The verse's provision for the wife's initiative has been read as evidence that the legislation attends to both parties of the marriage, and the community's judges used the clause when the requests came before them. The teachers also noted the prohibition of taking back what was given, since the property of the wife belongs to her and the separation does not reopen the question of what she received.

## Sūrah al-Shuʿarāʾ 26:182

> Weigh with an even balance,

**Expanded Commentary**

**An Even Balance Refuses a Tilted Standard**

The command moves from the quantity given to the instrument and process by which quantity is judged. An even balance does not favor the person who owns it, the customer who is present, or the trader who knows how to adjust it. Its purpose is to make the same standard govern both sides of the exchange.

The image is simple, but its reach is wide. A person can give a full measure and still manipulate the weighing process before or after it. Shuʿayb therefore addresses the fairness of the method, not only the appearance of the final result. Justice requires a reliable standard from the beginning, so that neither party is forced to trust a private calculation that cannot be examined.

Scales, containers, ledgers, and modern measuring devices are instruments. They do not become dishonest on their own. The moral choice belongs to the person who selects a defective tool, places it on an uneven surface, alters a reading, or presents an unclear unit to someone who assumes it is standard.

This distinction protects a useful principle: technology does not remove accountability. A sophisticated system can conceal a small deduction more effectively than an old scale, but concealment does not make the deduction lawful. The more difficult the method is for an ordinary customer to inspect, the greater the duty of the person who controls it to be transparent.

**Fairness Must Survive Both Directions of Exchange**

An even balance applies when one buys and when one sells. People often demand precision when their own money is leaving and accept approximation when another person's money is entering. The verse rejects that split. The standard must remain stable when the role changes.

The same lesson appears in 17:35, which joins full measurement with an even balance and describes that practice as fairest and best in the end. The phrase about the end gives the command a horizon beyond immediate profit. A dishonest adjustment may produce cash today, while a fair standard produces trust, a clean account, and safety before Allah.

**Balance Is a Public Promise**

A shared balance allows strangers to cooperate without sharing a family, tribe, or private bond. The instrument says that the deal will be judged by a rule available to both parties. When it is secretly tilted, the betrayal is larger than the missing amount: the common rule itself has been damaged.

For that reason, an even balance concerns institutions as well as individual sellers. A market inspector, employer, judge, or administrator can tilt outcomes through the standards used to count. The duty remains the same whether the instrument is a physical scale or a policy that measures one group's rights more harshly than another's.

**The Balance Trains the Inner Balance**

External fairness is strengthened by an inner refusal to be pulled by greed. A person who cannot govern desire will search for a permissible-looking adjustment whenever profit is at stake. He may call the imbalance cleverness, market practice, or a harmless rounding error, but the name does not change the act.

Shuʿayb's short command places the physical balance before the community as a daily lesson. Put the tool straight, then put the motive straight. A person who learns to give an even weight becomes less willing to bend promises, testimony, or judgment. The measure in the marketplace becomes practice for justice everywhere else.

An even balance also asks who designed the standard. If one side chooses the unit, records the reading, and decides which errors count, the other side may be formally included while remaining practically exposed. Fairness needs a method that can be inspected, explained, and corrected. The rule should not change merely because the person being measured has less influence.

That principle applies to a household as well as a market. A parent who counts one child's mistake but excuses the same mistake in another has tilted judgment. A leader who demands sacrifice from ordinary people while exempting his allies has tilted public policy. The scale in the verse gives a physical picture of a deeper obligation: do not make partiality into a hidden instrument.

Balance does not mean refusing compassion. An even rule can include mercy that is openly granted and lawfully due. What it forbids is secret favoritism presented as neutral judgment. When exceptions are needed, they should be explained by a principle that could be applied to others in the same position. Otherwise the apparent balance is only a polished form of preference.

The instruction also protects the honest person from being punished for refusing to cheat. If a market rewards the tilted scale, a truthful trader may appear less competitive. Public standards must therefore support individual integrity instead of leaving justice to private courage. An even balance is not only a personal virtue; it is a common arrangement that allows honesty to survive.

The standard must also be stable over time. A trader who is fair when watched but alters the process when business is slow has not adopted an even balance. A public body that publishes one rule and quietly applies another has done the same. Consistency lets people plan their lives without needing a private connection to discover the real terms of an exchange.

A just balance does not promise that all people will possess the same wealth. It promises that wealth will not buy a different measure of truth. The wealthy customer and the poor customer are both entitled to accurate goods, just as the powerful seller and the small seller are both bound by the same duty. When the standard is even, dignity is not made dependent on bargaining strength. Accurate measure protects the weak from being told that poverty makes truth less available to them.

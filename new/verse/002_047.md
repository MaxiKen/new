## Sūrah al-Baqarah 2:47

> O Children of Israel! Remember ˹all˺ the favours I granted you and how I honoured you above the others.

**Expanded Commentary**

**A Repeated Address, A Renewed Summons**

“O Children of Israel! Remember all the favours I granted you and how I honoured you above the others” (2:47). The sūrah repeats the address of 2:40 with an added clause: “and how I honoured you above the others” — wa annī faḍḍaltukum ʿalā al-ʿālamīn. Repetition is not redundancy; it is mercy. A reminder unfollowed is repeated, and its warrant is enlarged: not only favour (niʿmah) is recalled, but rank (tafḍīl).

The audience remains the Children of Israel in Madinah — Bearers of Torah, twelve-tribe people — addressed by their prophet-ancestor’s name (Isrāʾīl = Yaʿqūb). To name lineage is to name obligation: nobility recalls responsibility.

**What “Favours” and What “Honouring”?**

Niʿmah and tafḍīl are related but distinct. Niʿmah names gifts given — deliverance from Pharaoh (2:49–50), shade and manna and quails, land, prophets among them, kings, and what was given to none else (5:20) — as cited in the source. Tafḍīl — faḍḍala — names being made to excel, to be caused to outstrip. The phrase “above the worlds/peoples” (ʿalā al-ʿālamīn) is read by the source in context as “above peoples” or nations, as in “the land We blessed for all peoples” (21:71), though the universal sense “worlds” is not incorrect. The honour is comparative: among the peoples of their time, Israel was favoured with prophetic succession and revealed guidance at a scale not given to others.

The source notes the parallel in 2:122, where the same favour and preference are recalled again, and cross-references 6:86 — Ishmael, Elisha, Jonah, Lot each “favoured above the worlds” — showing that tafḍīl is not exclusive honorific but a bestowal with purpose. In 5:20 Moses reminds his people: “He appointed prophets among you, appointed you kings, and gave you what He gave to none in all the worlds.” That triad — prophethood, kingship, unprecedented gifts — concretizes the abstraction of being honoured above others.

**Time-Bound Honour, Not Eternal Claim**

The source records the crucial qualification: some commentators read this preeminence as pertaining to the past, in light of 3:110 — “You are the best community brought forth for mankind” — describing the Prophet’s community. Honour, in Qur'anic theology, is not a lineage deed of title but a trust that is confirmed by fidelity. Israel was honoured above others for what was given and required then; the same principle honours any people who fulfil the same trust now. To read faḍḍaltukum as permanent entitlement irrespective of response is to contradict the sūrah’s own logic of covenant-fulfilment (2:40: “fulfil your covenant and I will fulfil Mine”).

The verb’s morphology supports this: to be caused to excel is not to own excellence; it is to have been given it. What is given can be increased by gratitude or removed by breach.

**Why Repeat the Reminder Before the Warning?**

2:40–46 moved from covenant to character — fulfil, believe without selling, do not mix or hide, establish prayer and alms, close the say-do gap, seek help in patience and prayer, be certain of return. 2:47–48 now set another frame before the historical recital (2:49–): remember favour and rank, then guard against a Day when rank will not avail (2:48). The honour recalled is immediately qualified by what honour cannot do: no soul will avail another, no intercession accepted without leave, no ransom taken (2:48). Memory is given to sober, not to inflate.

**The Particular and the Universal**

The sūrah’s address to Israel is particular, but its pedagogy is universal. The favours named — life, deliverance, guidance, prophets — are, in fuller sense, favours upon every community that receives guidance. And the qualification applies universally: any people honoured with revelation is favoured above others for its time, and any such honour is conditional on fidelity to that revelation, especially when the confirming revelation arrives (2:41).

**Living the Verse Today**

- Remember “all” favours (2:47 adds “all” explicitly). Gratitude that counts one deliverance yet forgets prophethood, or counts prophethood yet forgets daily provision, is partial. The verse says “all” to widen the count.
- Let rank humble. To be honoured above others is to be examined more closely, not excused more broadly. The Qur’an elsewhere says the most honoured are most mindful (49:13); 2:47 anticipates that criterion.
- Do not weaponize tafḍīl. Past honour is not present merit without present fidelity. The verse invites reassurance for the faithful and warning for the complacent in the same breath.

**What Not to Extrapolate**

This verse does not establish racial superiority nor deny the final community’s distinction (3:110); it describes a historical preference tied to gifts and responsibility. And it does not promise that past preference secures future outcome irrespective of whether guidance is followed when it comes (2:38–39). What is firm is the double remembrance: all favours granted, and honour above others as context for what was granted, leading to the guard against the Day when neither lineage nor largesse will substitute for deeds (2:47–48).

## Sūrah al-Baqarah 2:179

> There is ˹security of˺ life for you in ˹the law of˺ retaliation, O people of reason, so that you may become mindful ˹of Allah˺.

**Expanded Commentary**

**The Claim About Life**

The verse states that in retaliation there is life, and the claim is addressed to people of understanding. The sentence follows the legislation of the previous verse, and it supplies the reason for the rule by naming what the rule preserves.

The Book's method here is to state the purpose of a law rather than only its content, and the community's teachers used the sentence when they explained how the legislation works. What the reader receives is a statement that the punishment limits the damage that a killing does rather than increasing it.

**How Retaliation Preserves Life**

The commentators explained the sentence in two ways. The first is that the law of retaliation serves as a deterrent, since a person who knows that his own life is answerable for the life he takes is restrained from the act. The second is that the limitation of the response to the offender prevents the feuds that had destroyed whole families in the society the verse addressed.

Both explanations turn on the same idea, which is that a measured response protects the community from the consequences of an unmeasured one. The Book presents the rule as a means by which lives are preserved, and the community's scholars pointed out that the sentence names the effect of the legislation rather than the severity of it.

**The Background of Vendetta**

The society in which the verses were revealed regulated killing through the honour and the standing of the groups involved, and the commentators described how a tribe would respond to a killing with greater force than the original act. The result was a cycle in which the number of the dead increased with each exchange.

The Book's legislation replaces the group's estimate of its honour with a rule that corresponds the punishment to the offence, and the sentence states the benefit that follows. What the reader receives is a picture of a law that interrupts a destructive pattern, and the community's teachers read the verse as evidence that the Book's rulings serve the welfare of the society they govern.

**The Address to People of Understanding**

The sentence is addressed to those who possess understanding, and the commentators explained the term in a way that is connected with the Book's use of the image of the kernel. The expression describes people who grasp the inner meaning of a matter rather than its outward appearance, and the sūrah uses it at several points where a lesson is being drawn.

The address indicates that the benefit of the legislation is visible to a person who considers the matter rather than to one who counts only the severity of the penalty. The community's teachers noted that the Book appeals to reflection in matters of law, and the sentence invites the reader to work out for himself why a system of measured retaliation protects the lives it appears to endanger.

**The Purpose Clause**

The sentence closes with the phrase that the community may become mindful of God, and the Book attaches the same purpose to a number of its commands. The community's scholars read the clause as a statement about what the legislation is intended to produce in the people who observe it.

The term describes the awareness that restrains a person from wrongdoing, and the sentence connects it with the law that restrains the community from violence. What the reader receives is a statement about the inward effect of an outward rule, and the community's teachers used the passage when they explained that the Book's legislation aims at the formation of its people as well as at the order of their society.

**The Relation to the Previous Sentence**

The verse follows the provision for pardon and compensation and the warning about transgression, and it states what the whole arrangement accomplishes. The Book has described the offense, the response, and the settlement; the present sentence names the good that the system produces.

The community's scholars read the two verses together when they discussed the wisdom of the legislation, and they noted that the Book does not present retaliation as an end in itself. What the sentence establishes is that the purpose of the law is the preservation of life, and the reader is shown that the measure of a penalty in the Book is connected to what it protects.

The two verses form a pair, since the first provides for pardon and settlement and the second names the good that the whole arrangement produces. The Book often follows a legal provision with a statement of its purpose, and the reader is given the rule and its reason in sequence. The community’s scholars read the pair together when they discussed the wisdom of the legislation, and they noted that the sentence in this place concerns the effect of the law on the community rather than its effect on the offender.

**What the Verse Establishes**

The sentence establishes the purpose of the legislation and the audience that is expected to perceive it, and it closes the short section on homicide that began with the previous verse. The passage that follows turns to the subject of wills and then to the fast, and the reader is carried from the law that governs the loss of life to the law that governs the disposal of a person's property and the discipline of his body.

The sentence establishes the purpose of the legislation, the audience that is expected to perceive it, and the place of the law within the religious life of the community. The reader is given the reason for a rule whose severity might otherwise seem to require no explanation, and the community’s teachers read the verse as evidence that the Book’s rulings serve the welfare of those they govern. The passage that follows turns to the disposal of property, and the reader is carried from the law that protects life to the law that governs what a person leaves behind.

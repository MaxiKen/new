## Sūrah al-Nisāʾ 4:81

> And they say, “We obey,” but when they leave you, a group of them would spend the night contradicting what they said. Allah records all their schemes. So turn away from them, and put your trust in Allah. And Allah is sufficient as a Trustee of Affairs.

**Expanded Commentary**

**We Obey — But the Night Tells Otherwise**

The verse pictures a double speech: *wa yaqūlūna ṭāʿatun fa idhā barazū min ʿindika bayyata ṭāʾifatun minhum ghayra alladhī taqūl* — and they say, we obey, but when they leave you, a group of them would spend the night contradicting what they said. *Ṭāʿatun* — obedience — is the word they offer in presence, the same word the sūrah requires as *“whoever obeys the Messenger has obeyed Allah”* (4:80) and as true belief until they accept him as judge without resistance (4:65). *Barazū min ʿindika* — when they leave you, go forth from your presence — marks the test. In presence they claim obedience; in absence they *bayyata* — spend the night, plan by night — what contradicts. *Tabyīt* — night-plotting — was explained as deliberating after hours, reworking what was said in daylight, as those who take words out of context play with tongues (4:46) and those who are stingy promote stinginess (4:37). The sūrah has exposed that hypocrisy after disaster swears goodwill (4:62) and after revelation turns away stubbornly (4:61); here it is nightly contradiction. The group — *ṭāʾifatun minhum* — shows not all who said *we obey* do so, but a party, enough to mislead others, as the lagger among you who makes others lag (4:72).

**Allah Records All Their Schemes**

The clause *wa Allāhu yaktubu mā yubayyitūn* — and Allah records all their schemes, what they plot by night — was read as the counterpart to the earlier *He records* for the market and the home. The sūrah has said God is ever Watchful (4:1), sufficient as Reckoner (4:6), Witness over all things (4:33), and never wrongs by an atom but multiplies good (4:40). To record *mā yubayyitūn* — what they spend the night on — means the nightly contradiction, not only the daytime claim, is entered. The Qur’an elsewhere says that angels record and that no small or great is left except counted. Here the recorder is named as God Himself, because the sūrah wants the Prophet ﷺ to know that the nightly schemes are not a hidden variable that will surprise the community; they are already written. That record matters for the hypocrites who publicise news of security or fear without referral (4:83) and for those who contradict *we obey* after leaving. Their *tabyīt* may feel like secret counsel, but the One who knows what is in hearts (4:63) and has perfect knowledge of them (4:39) writes it.

**So Turn Away From Them**

The instruction *fa aʿriḍ ʿanhum* — so turn away from them — repeats the counsel given for those whose hearts are known and whose oaths are hollow — *turn away, caution, and give penetrating advice* (4:63). *Iʿrāḍ* — turning away — was explained as not taking their nightly counsel as guidance, not seeking their approval, and not exposing their schemes with harsh retaliation that would deepen hypocrisy. The same turning was told where hypocrites turned away stubbornly (4:61) — answer with a measured withdrawal rather than with mimicry. The sūrah’s ethic is to return trusts and judge with fairness as noble exhortation from the All-Hearing, All-Seeing (4:58); to turn away from the night-plotters is to keep that fairness without being drawn into night-plotting oneself. Early authorities noted that the Prophet ﷺ was not told to punish the group at this stage but to disregard their nightly contradiction, because the record is kept and the trust put in God suffices.

**Put Your Trust in Allah**

The positive *wa tawakkal ʿalā Allāhi* — and put your trust in Allah — follows the turning as its support. *Tawakkul* — to entrust affairs, to rely — was explained as placing the matter of the night-plotters, of the coming fight, and of news that is publicised (4:83) in the hands of the One who is sufficient. The sūrah has built that sufficiency as Guardian and Helper (4:45), as Witness that the Messenger is sent to all people (4:79), and as sufficient to curb the disbelievers’ might (4:84). To trust is to fight in God’s cause after taking precautions (4:71) while knowing that the night-plotters’ weak schemes are ever weak (4:76). The one who says *we obey* and then spends the night contradicting trusts in darkness; the one told to turn away and trust entrusts the darkness to Him who records it. That trust was lived by the Prophet ﷺ when he put trust in God as Trustee after recording their schemes, and it is the same trust the sūrah will commend to believers who refer security or fear to the Messenger and authorities (4:83) rather than publicising it.

**And Allah Is Sufficient as Trustee of Affairs**

The closing *wa kafā bi Allāhi wakīlā* — and Allah is sufficient as a Trustee of Affairs — seals both recording and reliance. *Wakīl* — trustee, disposer of affairs, one entrusted with the outcome — was explained as the One to whom trust is returned, as trusts are returned to rightful owners (4:58) and as judgment is referred upward as best outcome (4:59). To say He is sufficient as *Wakīl* is to say that the nightly contradiction, the lagging at disaster, and the wishing at bounty all end in a trust He will dispose. The sūrah has used *kafā bi Allāhi* — sufficient is God — for Guardian, Helper, Witness, Knower — and here for Trustee, showing that each need has one sufficiency. For the community learning to go forth in groups or together, to distinguish blessing from gain (4:72–73), and to fight for the oppressed (4:75), sufficiency as Trustee means the night that others spend contradicting need not be spent fearing, because the recorder and the disposer is one, and His record misses no *tabyīt* and His disposal misses no *faḍl*. That is why the verse pairs recording with trust: what He writes He also handles, and what He handles as Trustee is sufficient.

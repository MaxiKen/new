## Sūrah al-Nisāʾ 4:81

> And they say, “We obey,” but when they leave you, a group of them would spend the night contradicting what they said. Allah records all their schemes. So turn away from them, and put your trust in Allah. And Allah is sufficient as a Trustee of Affairs.

### Expanded Commentary

**Public agreement, private sabotage**

The hypocrites say “we obey,” but contradict the instruction after leaving the Messenger. The verse exposes the gap between political speech and moral obedience. A person can agree in the assembly because agreement is useful, then organize resistance in private. Allah records the schemes that human observers may never detect.

**Turning away from manipulation**

The Prophet is told to turn away from them and trust Allah. This is not a command to ignore danger; it is a refusal to let every hidden plot control the heart. Institutions must still protect themselves through evidence and accountability, but leaders should not become consumed by suspicion. Trust in Allah allows firm action without vindictiveness.

The verse is also a warning to believers who say yes to a duty they have already decided to undermine. Honest disagreement is better than false assent. A community can consult, revise, and correct; it cannot build solidarity on promises that are merely tactical.

**Trust after the meeting**

The verse values consistency between public speech and private action. People who lead teams, families, or institutions should record decisions and responsibilities so that hidden contradiction can be corrected early. Trust is not built by demanding that others believe an attractive promise; it is built by making conduct match the promise over time.

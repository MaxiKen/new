## Sūrah Āli ʿImrān 3:195

> So their Lord responded to them: “I will never deny any of you—male or female—the reward of your deeds. Both are equal in reward. Those who migrated or were expelled from their homes, and were persecuted for My sake and fought and ˹some˺ were martyred—I will certainly forgive their sins and admit them into Gardens under which rivers flow, as a reward from Allah. And with Allah is the finest reward!”

**Expanded Commentary**

**The Answer Given**

The verse opens by stating that their Lord answered them, and the sentence follows the prayer that reaches through the end of the previous verse. The Book records the answers to such prayers in several places, and the community in this passage is told that its petitions were heard.

The transition from request to answer gives the closing movement of the sūrah its shape. The people of understanding reflect on the creation, make their requests, and are answered by the one they address, and the reply is reported in the first person.

**The Promise That No Work Is Wasted**

The answer begins with the declaration that God will not let the work of any worker be in vain, and the sentence is the sūrah's broadest assurance on the subject. The sūrah had earlier stated that God does not neglect the reward of the believers, and the two statements belong together.

The declaration covers the whole range of what a person does, without specifying a category of deeds or a rank of people. The answer to the community's prayers begins with that general assurance, and the specific promises that follow are applications of it to the people whose history the sūrah has been describing.

**Male and Female Alike**

The answer states that no worker among them will be denied the fruit of his work, male or female, and then adds that each of them is like the other. Al-Rāzī explains the phrase as meaning that the reward for obedience and righteous conduct is not differentiated among men and women, and he notes the further sense that the phrase may mean that each is of the other.

The Book makes the point in several places. One passage states that whoever does good, whether male or female, and believes will be given a good life and rewarded according to the best of what he did. Another lists the qualities of believing men and women together and promises both forgiveness and a great reward. A third states that those who do good, whether male or female, and have faith will enter the garden and will not be wronged in the least.

**The Sufferings Listed**

The answer then names the experiences of the people it concerns: those who emigrated, those who were expelled from their homes, those who were hurt in God's cause, those who fought, and those who were killed. The list follows the community's history, beginning with the migration from Makkah and continuing through the fighting that the sūrah has described.

The commentators identify the emigration and expulsion with what the believers suffered in Makkah and the migration that followed it to Madinah. The list is therefore a summary of what the first community had undergone, and the answer to the prayer is addressed to the people who had borne all of it.

**What Being Hurt Covers**

The phrase about being hurt in God's cause is explained by the commentators as covering insults and mockery as well as physical injury. The verse that preceded the prayer in this sūrah told the community that it would hear much that was painful from the two groups, and the answer to the prayer returns to the same experience.

The inclusion of insults in the list of what earns the reward is significant for the community's self-understanding. What the believers had endured was not only the loss of possessions and the dangers of the road but also the daily pressure of being mocked for their position, and the answer names it as part of what God will not allow to be wasted.

**Absolution and the Gardens**

The answer promises to absolve the people described of their evil deeds and to bring them into gardens with rivers running beneath them. The two elements had appeared in the sūrah's earlier description of the reward of the righteous, and the closing passage repeats the promise.

The gardens with rivers beneath them are the Book's standard image for the reward, and the absolution of deeds is the removal of what would otherwise stand against the people who receive it. The answer to the prayer thus grants both the removal of the debt and the gift that follows it, and the community is told what its sacrifices were exchanged for.

**The Reward Attributed to God**

The verse describes what is given as a reward from God, and it closes with the statement that with God is the most beautiful reward. The attribution places the reward outside the system of exchange the community might have imagined, since what is given is described as coming from God rather than as earned by the recipients.

The sūrah's earlier passages used the same language when they described the reward of the believers and the bounty of God toward the community. The closing sentence of the verse gathers all of it into a single phrase, and the passage ends with the community's aspirations directed to the source rather than to the gift.

**What the Answer Teaches the Community**

The answer teaches the community that its hardships have been registered and that the account will be settled in its favour. The sūrah has been describing a community that lost a battle, was mocked by its neighbours, and had members who failed and members who stood firm, and the closing movement places all of them within the answer given to the prayer of the righteous.

The verses that follow this one add a warning about the conduct of those who trade faith for disbelief, and the sūrah ends shortly after. The passage's last word on the community's situation is the promise stated here, and it is given to people who had asked for exactly what the answer grants.

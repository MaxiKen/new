## Sūrah al-Aʿrāf 7:50

> The residents of the Fire will then cry out to the residents of Paradise, “Aid us with some water or any provision Allah has granted you.” They will reply, “Allah has forbidden both to the disbelievers,

**Expanded Commentary**

**The Request Across the Divide**

The verse reports the appeal of the people of the Fire to the people of the Garden, and the appeal asks for water or for anything that God has provided. The request follows the scene of the barrier and the heights, and it confirms that the two groups are close enough to speak. The condemned do not ask for release or for a return to the world, and the request in front of us is limited to a drop of water and a share of provision.

The Book's descriptions of the two abodes place them in sight of one another, and the sūrah's earlier verses have already reported the call made in the other direction (7:44). The verse in front of us completes the exchange with the request that the condemned make, and the answer that follows states the ground of the refusal. The scene is the last conversation the passage records between the two groups.

**What Is Asked and What Is Refused**

The two things requested are water and the provision of the Garden, and the Book's descriptions of the reward supply both in abundance. The sūrah of Muhammad describes gardens with rivers of water that does not change in taste, rivers of milk, rivers of wine, and rivers of purified honey (47:15), and the sūrah of the Cow describes gardens beneath which rivers flow and fruits of every kind (2:25). The verse in front of us names the two things in the fewest words, and the answer denies both to the people who ask.

The refusal is total, and the sūrah's descriptions of the punishment supply the other side of the account. The sūrah of the Inevitable states that the condemned will drink from a boiling spring and that their only food will be bitter thorn, and the sūrah of the Tidings states that they will find neither coolness nor any drink other than boiling water and pus (78:24–25). The verse in front of us states the answer to the request, and the Book's descriptions of the Fire state what takes the place of what was asked.

**The Answer and Its Terms**

The people of the Garden answer that God has forbidden both water and provision to the disbelievers, and the answer is stated as a settled decree rather than as a refusal by the speakers. The verse that follows names the conduct that makes the decree apply, and the two verses together give the scene its legal shape. What is denied in front of us is not the property of the people of the Garden but the mercy of the One who provided it.

The Book's descriptions of the reward and the punishment include the same structure of correspondence, and the sūrah of the Clatterer and the sūrah of the Inevitable present the two outcomes as assigned rather than negotiated. The answer in front of us states the assignment, and the sūrah's reader is left with the contrast between the rivers of the Garden and the drink of the Fire.

**Thirst and Hunger as Torment**

The request for water indicates the nature of the punishment that the Book describes, and the sūrah's earlier verses have stated that the bed of the condemned is fire and that their covering is flame (7:41). The Book refers to thirst and hunger among the torments of the Fire, and the sūrah of the Overwhelming describes the food that neither nourishes nor satisfies hunger (88:6–7). This verse supplies the condemned asking for what would relieve the condition.

The commentary tradition notes that the request is made in spite of the knowledge that it cannot be granted. Al-Zamakhsharī explains it as the product of the confusion and desperation of the condemned, who ask for relief from those who cannot give it because the situation leaves them nothing else to do. The verse states the request without explaining it, and the answer supplies the only information that matters.

**The Nearness of the Two Abodes**

The verse shows the two groups within earshot of each other, and the arrangement is part of the punishment of the second. The Book's descriptions of the Hereafter include the condemned seeing the reward of the saved, and the sūrah of the Heights has already reported the words of the people of the Garden being heard across the barrier (7:44). The refusal in front of us is made more severe by the distance it measures.

The Book uses the visibility of the two outcomes as a warning in the world as well, and the sūrah's opening verses have set the two results of the weighing side by side for the reader. The verse here places the two outcomes within sight of each other, and the request that comes across the divide is the voice of that arrangement.

**Where the Verse Sits**

The verse stands fiftieth in the chapter, and it begins the last exchange between the two groups in this passage. The verse that follows names the conduct of the disbelievers who took their religion as amusement and were deluded by the life of the world, and the passage then turns to the Book that has been brought to them (7:52). The request in front of us opens the closing sequence of the scene.

The reader who has seen the command given to the believers to enter the Garden now hears the request of the condemned and its refusal. The two outcomes are within sight of each other, and the answer states that the provision of the Garden is not for the group that the next verse describes.

**The Transition to the Next Verse**

The next verse describes the disbelievers as those who took their religion as amusement and play and were deluded by the life of this world, and it reports the statement that they will be ignored as they ignored the meeting of this day and rejected the revelations (7:51). The refusal in front of us is given its reason in the verse that follows, and the conduct of the condemned is named.

The two verses together state the terms of the exclusion. Water and provision are withheld, and the reason is the amusement that a lifetime was made of and the denial of the day whose arrival is now certain; and the sūrah then turns from the two companies to the Book that has been sent to the audience of the chapter.

## Sūrah al-Tawbah 9:67

> The hypocrites, both men and women, are all alike: they encourage what is evil, forbid what is good, and withhold ˹what is in˺ their hands. They neglected Allah, so He neglected them. Surely the hypocrites are the rebellious.

**Expanded Commentary**

**Men and Women Share the Same Moral Pattern**

The verse places hypocritical men and women together. Their gender differs, but their moral pattern is the same: they encourage evil, resist good, and close their hands. This is a statement of equal accountability. Neither men nor women are given a special exemption from the duties of faith or the consequences of betrayal.

Equality here is not sameness of every social role. It is equality before moral judgment. A man cannot blame women for corruption while practicing it himself, and a woman cannot treat hypocrisy as a defect that belongs only to male leaders. Each person is responsible for the influence he or she exercises. The verse gives both a warning and a dignity: both can be agents of harm, and both are called to return to Allah.

**Hypocrisy Reverses the Moral Order**

The hypocrites encourage what is evil and forbid what is good. The reversal is deliberate. They do not merely fail to promote righteousness; they use their influence to make wrongdoing attractive and obedience embarrassing. A community becomes unsafe when its informal leaders praise corruption while mocking restraint.

This inversion often begins with language. Evil is renamed freedom, greed is called ambition, and sincere advice is called weakness. Good is made to look backward, severe, or impractical. The believer must therefore examine the words used to describe conduct, not only the conduct itself. A polished label cannot change the moral reality of an act.

**Closed Hands Reveal Closed Allegiance**

Withholding what is in their hands includes reluctance to spend in Allah's cause and refusal to offer support when support is needed. Their hands are closed because their hearts have already decided that resources belong only to the self. They may speak about community and loyalty, but their possessions reveal what they will actually protect.

Giving does not require waste or neglect of dependents. A believer keeps lawful responsibilities and still leaves a path for generosity. The hypocrite's problem is not careful budgeting; it is a settled refusal to recognize any claim beyond personal security. His hand opens for appetite and closes for duty. That pattern turns wealth into a wall between him and the people Allah placed around him.

**Forgetting Allah Produces Divine Neglect**

“They neglected Allah, so He neglected them” (9:67) is not a claim that Allah forgets in the human sense. It describes a reciprocal judgment: they abandoned remembrance, guidance, and obedience, so Allah left them without the special care and direction they had rejected. The language makes the moral consequence vivid.

A person can forget Allah while still speaking religious words. Forgetfulness appears when prayer is postponed without concern, commands are treated as obstacles, and gratitude disappears from the use of blessings. Divine neglect is therefore not arbitrary abandonment. It is the painful result of choosing to live as though Allah's guidance were irrelevant. The servant should seek refuge from this state by remembering Allah before the heart becomes comfortable without Him.

**The Community Needs Active Goodness**

The verse implies that neutrality is not always neutral. When evil is promoted and good is silenced, merely avoiding the worst act may leave the social order in the hands of those who promote it. Believers are called to encourage good and resist wrong with knowledge, wisdom, and attention to ability.

This duty must not become a license for shouting at everyone. Good counsel considers timing, evidence, and likely benefit. It protects the vulnerable and avoids creating a greater harm. The hypocrites' inversion teaches the opposite: their speech serves what corrupts. The believer's speech should make truth easier to practice and sin harder to celebrate.

**Rebellion Is a Habit of Refusing Guidance**

The verse ends by calling the hypocrites rebellious. Rebellion is not only a dramatic revolt. It can be the repeated refusal to let Allah's guidance correct one's preferences. A person may rebel through influence, silence, hoarding, or the constant creation of excuses.

The cure is active remembrance. Open the hand, support good, accept correction, and restore prayer. A person cannot repair hypocrisy by changing one public phrase while keeping the same moral pattern. He must let Allah reorder his influence and resources. The community then becomes a place where men and women protect one another from evil, make goodness honorable, and keep their allegiance visible through conduct.

A person who wants to know where he stands can examine three things: what he praises, what he opposes, and what he is willing to release. These reveal allegiance more reliably than a public label. Does he make righteousness welcome, or does he make it awkward? Does he resist harm when resistance costs a relationship? Does he treat his resources as trusts or as walls? The answers do not replace repentance, but they show where repentance must begin. Allah's neglect is not an excuse for despair. The servant can return to remembrance, reopen the hand, and support good until the moral order that hypocrisy reversed is restored in his own conduct.

The verse also guards against a false neutrality that treats good and evil as equal options. Believers are not called to dominate every conversation, but they are called to make moral truth visible. They teach children what justice means, support people who resist exploitation, and refuse to fund the practices they condemn. Their hands open where help is due, and their speech directs attention toward Allah rather than toward themselves. In this way remembrance becomes social as well as private. The heart that remembers Allah will eventually show that remembrance through what it supports, interrupts, and protects.

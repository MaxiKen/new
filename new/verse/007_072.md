## Sūrah al-Aʿrāf 7:72

> So We saved him and those with him by Our mercy and uprooted those who denied Our signs. They were not believers.

**Expanded Commentary**

**The Rescue by Mercy**

The verse states that God saved the messenger and those with him by mercy, and the phrase attributes the rescue to the divine mercy rather than to the strength of the saved. The sūrah of Hūd reports the same event in the same terms, stating that when the command came, God rescued Hūd and those who believed with him by a mercy from Him and saved them from a harsh punishment (11:58). The verse in front of us states the rescue in the shortest form, and the account of the second community is closed in the same manner as the first.

The rescue of the messenger and his companions is one of the fixed elements of the sūrah's punishment accounts, and the sequence of the narratives presents it in each case. The sūrah of the Cattle asks whether any would be destroyed except the wrongdoing people if the punishment came upon them suddenly or openly (6:47), and the sūrah's accounts of Noah, ʿĀd, Thamūd, Lot, and Shuʿayb all report the salvation of the believers. The verse in front of us supplies the second instance of the pattern.

**The Uprooting of the Deniers**

The verse states that those who denied the signs were uprooted, and the term describes a cutting off that leaves nothing behind. The Book applies the same image to the peoples that were destroyed, and the sūrah of the Dawn reports the fate of Iram and states that nothing of the city was left. The punishment of ʿĀd is described in several passages: the sūrah of the Moon reports a furious wind sent against them on a day of unrelenting misery (54:19), the sūrah of the Inevitable reports the same wind unleashed for seven nights and eight days so that the people were left lying like hollow palm trunks (69:6–7), and the sūrah of Fussilat reports the wind of punishment that destroyed them in the days of misfortune (41:16).

The descriptions in front of us supply the detail that the sūrah's brief account omits, and the two accounts together show what the uprooting meant. The people who had been increased in stature and had built on the plains were removed by a wind, and the sūrah of the Inevitable's image of the hollow trunks states the completeness of the removal. The verse in front of us states the outcome in a single word.

**The Closing Description of the People**

The verse closes with the statement that the people were not believers, and the description supplies the reason for what happened to them. The sūrah's accounts of the communities repeat the same verdict at the end of each narrative, and the sūrah of Noah's account stated that the people who were drowned were blind (7:64). This verse states the condition that produced the destruction, and the account of the second people ends on the same note as the first.

The phrase places the outcome on the response of the community rather than on any defect in the message it received. The sūrah's narratives have stated the call, the reminder, the argument, and the warning, and the closing sentence in front of us states what the community did with all of them. The sūrah's reader is left with the correspondence between the refusal and the end.

**The Series and Its Argument**

The verse completes the second version of the pattern that the sūrah's accounts follow, and the pattern's repetition is the argument that the chapter is making. Each community received the same message, refused it in its own words, and was removed from the earth; and the audience of the sūrah is placed in the position of the peoples whose histories it is hearing. The Book's arrangement of the narratives in the sūrah of Hūd and the sūrah of the Poets uses the same method, and the sūrah in front of us has now shown the pattern twice.

The Book's use of the history of the earlier peoples as evidence for the present audience appears throughout its addresses, and the sūrah of the Romans tells the audience to travel through the earth and see the end of those who denied. The verse here supplies the second demonstration in the sūrah, and the third is introduced in the following verses with the account of Thamūd.

**The Salvation of the Believers in Every Account**

The verse states that the rescue covered the messenger and those with him, and the Book's narratives consistently extend the salvation to the companions who accepted the message. The sūrah of the Heights will report the rescue of Ṣāliḥ and his followers and the destruction of the people of Thamūd, and the account of Lot reports the rescue of his household except for his wife. The pattern in front of us states that the division at the punishment is the division of belief, and the sūrah's narratives present the two groups in each community.

The Book's teaching about the community of believers as the remnant that is saved appears in its descriptions of the messengers' followers, and the sūrah's accounts name the believing companions of each prophet. The present verse states the second instance of that arrangement, and the sūrah's following narratives carry the same division forward.

**Where the Verse Sits**

The verse stands seventy-second in the chapter, and it closes the account of ʿĀd that began with the call of Hūd (7:65). The verses that follow introduce the people of Thamūd and their messenger Ṣāliḥ (7:73), and the series continues in the same order. The verse under discussion supplies the conclusion of the second narrative and the model for the third.

The reader who has followed the call, the refusal, the argument, and the demand now reads the outcome. The messenger and his companions are saved by mercy, the deniers are removed, and the community is described by its unbelief; and the sūrah's next verses present the third people and its messenger with the same call and the same warning.

**The Transition to the Next Verse**

The next verse reports that to the people of Thamūd God sent their brother Ṣāliḥ, who called them to worship God alone and produced the she-camel as a sign, warning them not to harm her (7:73). The account of ʿĀd gives way to the account of the third people, and the call that Ṣāliḥ makes is the call that the sūrah's earlier messengers made.

The two verses together carry the series forward. One community has been removed from the earth after refusing its messenger, and the messenger of the next people is introduced with the same message together with a sign; and the verses that follow record the response of the people of Thamūd to both.

## Sūrah al-Nisāʾ 4:90

> except those who are allies of a people you are bound with in a treaty or those wholeheartedly opposed to fighting either you or their own people. If Allah had willed, He would have empowered them to fight you. So if they refrain from fighting you and offer you peace, then Allah does not permit you to harm them.

### Expanded Commentary

**Treaties limit force**

The verse makes an explicit exception for people allied to a treaty community and for those who withdraw from fighting both the Muslims and their own people. If Allah had willed, He could have given them power to fight, but they refrain and offer peace. In that case, the believers have no permission to harm them.

**Peace is a legal fact**

A treaty is not a decorative promise that can be broken when circumstances change. It creates obligations, protects persons, and restrains the use of force. The same applies to a person who communicates a genuine cessation of hostilities. The verse makes non-aggression morally relevant even when the person’s religious or political identity is not shared.

The passage is a powerful safeguard against reading 4:89 alone. It requires evaluation of conduct and declared peace. Muslim authorities must uphold agreements, investigate violations, and avoid treating uncertainty as proof of attack. The command not to harm peaceful people is as much part of the verse’s wartime ethic as the permission to fight aggressors.

**Peaceful conduct has legal weight**

A person who withdraws from fighting and offers peace is protected even if his community is politically opposed. This principle can govern ceasefires, asylum, and the treatment of civilians. Trust is not naïveté when it is supported by clear agreements and careful verification; it is obedience to a stated limit on harm.

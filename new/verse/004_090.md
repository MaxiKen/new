## Sūrah al-Nisāʾ 4:90

> except those who are allies of a people you are bound with in a treaty or those wholeheartedly opposed to fighting either you or their own people. If Allah had willed, He would have empowered them to fight you. So if they refrain from fighting you and offer you peace, then Allah does not permit you to harm them.

**Expanded Commentary**

**Except Those With a Treaty or Those Opposed to Fighting**

The verse adds *illā alladhīna yaṣilūna ilā qawmin baynakum wa baynahum mīthāqun aw jāʾūkum ḥaṣirat ṣudūruhum an yuqātilūkum aw yuqātilū qawmahum* — except those who are allies of a people you are bound with in a treaty or those wholeheartedly opposed to fighting either you or their own people, whose chests are constricted at fighting you or their own. The exception follows the command to seize and kill the hostile turners who wish you to disbelieve as they have (4:89). *Yaṣilūna ilā qawmin* — to reach, to join a people — was read as seeking refuge with a tribe allied to the believers by covenant — *mīthāq* — the same word used for the firm commitment — *mīthāq ghalīẓ* — of marriage (4:21) and for trusts returned (4:58). To be joined to treaty people is to be covered by that treaty, as the sūrah respects the firm word given. The second group — those whose chests are constricted — *ḥaṣirat ṣudūruhum* — was explained as people whose hearts cannot bear to fight either side, neither the believers nor their own. The sūrah has described *ḥaraj* — constriction — as the inner resistance the believer must remove when accepting the Prophet ﷺ as judge (4:65); here constriction is the reason some cannot fight, and the sūrah spares them. Both groups are excepted from the hostile measure, showing that hostility, not identity, is the cause.

**If He Had Willed, He Would Have Empowered Them**

The clause *wa law shāʾa Allāhu la sallaṭahum ʿalaykum fa la qātalūkum* — if Allah had willed, He would have empowered them to fight you and they would have fought — was read as reminding that the sparing of the constricted is not due to their power but to divine restraint. *Sallata* — to give power, to make prevail — was explained as God’s leave, the same *idhn* by which messengers are to be obeyed (4:64). To say He would have empowered them and they would have fought is to say that the fact they do not fight is itself by His will, as the sūrah elsewhere says both good and evil are from His decree while evil from the self as means (4:78–79). The reminder humbles the believers who might credit their treaty or their deterrence alone; the restraint of the constricted is also God’s doing. Early reports placed the verse among tribes like Banū Mudlij who had treaties or who disliked fighting either side in the conflict between Madinah and the Quraysh; the exemption of such neutrals shows the sūrah’s distinction between the oppressor’s land whose people are oppressors (4:75) and people whose chests are constricted at fighting.

**So If They Refrain and Offer Peace**

The condition *fa in iʿtazalūkum fa lam yuqātilūkum wa alqaw ilaykumu al-salām fa mā jaʿala Allāhu lakum ʿalayhim sabīlā* — so if they refrain from fighting you, do not fight you, and offer you peace, then God does not permit you to harm them, He has not given you a way over them — was read as the sūrah’s rule of peace. *Iʿtazalūkum* — to withdraw, to keep aside — and *alqaw al-salām* — to offer peace, to cast peace — were explained as the two signs of neutrality: withdrawal from fighting and offering *salām*, the same peace the sūrah commanded to answer with better or same where greeting is given (4:86). *Fa mā jaʿala Allāhu lakum ʿalayhim sabīlā* — no way, no permission, no path — was read as removing the *sabīl* — way — over them that was given over the hostile turners (4:89). The sūrah has used *sabīl* for the path God wills to make clear (4:26), the noble ways, and the far straying Satan desires (4:60); to have no *sabīl* over those who withdraw and offer peace is to be kept on the straight path. The verse was linked to the later rule *“if they refrain, you do not kill”* where even the hostile who offer peace are not to be harmed, showing that the ethic of *“do not kill yourselves, God is ever Merciful to you”* (4:29) governs the field as well as the home.

**A Verse That Holds Treaty and Conscience Together**

Placed after the wish to make you alike in disbelief and the permission to seize the hostile turner (4:89), this exception holds treaty and conscience together. Treaty — *mīthāq* — is the sūrah’s firm word; conscience — *ḥaṣirat ṣudūruhum* — is the heart’s constriction that God sees. Both are respected. The first group is spared because a word was given; the second because a heart cannot bear to fight either side. The sūrah has said that God knows what is in hearts (4:63), knows best who enemies are (4:45), and is sufficient as Guardian and Helper; here that knowledge sees the constricted chest and spares it. The verse therefore draws a line that the community divided into two groups about hypocrites (4:88) needed: not all who are among the hostile turners are the same as those who fight; some are allies of treaty people, and some are neutral by distress. The exception keeps the sūrah’s larger ethic — return trusts, judge fairly as noble exhortation from the All-Hearing, All-Seeing (4:58), and fight for the oppressed whose cry is for a guardian and helper from grace (4:75) — from becoming indiscriminate. For the weak for whom burdens were lightened (4:28), the verse is the lightest of burdens: where the other withdraws and offers peace, there is no way over him, and the peace offered is answered with better or same, as the greeting rule already commanded.

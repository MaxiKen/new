## Sūrah al-Baqarah 2:260

> And ˹remember˺ when Abraham said, “My Lord! Show me how you give life to the dead.” Allah responded, “Do you not believe?” Abraham replied, “Yes I do, but just so my heart can be reassured.” Allah said, “Then bring four birds, train them to come to you, ˹then cut them into pieces,˺ and scatter them on different hilltops. Then call them back, they will fly to you in haste. And ˹so you will˺ know that Allah is Almighty, All-Wise.”

**Expanded Commentary**

**The Request of Abraham**

The sentence records the request of Abraham that his Lord show him how the dead are given life, and the community's teachers read the account as the companion of the passage before it. The two verses treat the same subject, and the difference between them lies in the standing of the one who asks, since the question here comes from a prophet whose faith the Book has already described.

The wording of the request is a petition rather than a challenge. The commentators noted the form, because the sentence asks for the manner of the act rather than for the evidence of it, and the answer that follows supplies a demonstration that the questioner can witness.

**The Question of Belief**

The sentence records the divine question whether Abraham does not believe, and the community's scholars read the exchange as the confirmation of the faith that the request had assumed. The question does not accuse; the answer that follows shows that the man's belief was not in doubt.

The teachers explained the exchange as the instruction that the community receives about the difference between belief and the sight that strengthens it. The Book elsewhere commands the messenger to ask for an increase of knowledge, and the community's scholars gathered the passages when they spoke about the growth of certainty.

The wording also shows that the matter was conducted between God and His prophet without any suggestion that the request was an offence. The answer treats the question as the request of a servant who wishes to see what he has accepted.

**The Peace of the Heart**

The reply of Abraham states that he believes and that he asks so that his heart may be at peace, and the commentators discussed the state that the sentence describes. The Book names the heart at rest among the gifts given to the believers, and the passages that treat the subject supplied the material for the explanation.

The teachers of the community read the phrase as the description of a degree of certainty that is reached through sight rather than through report alone. The knowledge of the mind was present, and the request concerns the assurance that the heart receives.

The commentators also noted that the peace the sentence names is not the removal of doubt about the existence of God. Their explanations concern the increase of a knowledge that was already established, and the reading was preserved in the community's teaching of the passage.

**The Four Birds**

The answer instructs Abraham to take four birds and to incline them to himself before the demonstration, and the commentators discussed the purpose of the command. The teachers explained that the birds were to be trained so that they would return to him when he called, and the instruction prepared the sign that the sentence describes.

The wording also names the scattering of the parts on the hills, so that the demonstration covers the separation of what had been joined as well as the restoration of what had been separated. The community's scholars read the detail as the statement about the completeness of the act that the request had asked to see.

The command places the prophet within the event rather than at a distance from it. He takes the birds, calls them, and sees them return, and the answer to his petition is given in the work of his own hands.

**The Call and the Return**

The sentence states that the birds will come to him in haste when he calls them, and the community's teachers read the clause as the demonstration of the power that the request had asked about. The parts that were scattered on the hills are gathered at the call of the prophet, so that the resurrection is presented in the form of a summons that the created order obeys.

The commentators noted the difference between the creation of life and the restoration of it, since the sentence describes the second. The material of the bodies is present in both cases, and the command that gathers it is the act of the One to whom the petition was addressed.

The instruction also connects the demonstration with the office of the prophet, since the sign confirms the message he carried. The community's scholars read the account as the preparation of the messenger for the disputes he would meet, and the passage before it supplies the record of one of those encounters.

**The Reports and the Teachers**

The reports of the community include a saying of the Prophet ﷺ about the matter that has been transmitted by the compilers of the authentic collections. The teachers used the material when they explained the difference between the doubt that leads a person away from the faith and the request that seeks a firmer knowledge.

The commentators also discussed the various occasions that they connected with the request, including the scene that the prophet was said to have witnessed and the wish to strengthen the argument he had used in his own disputes. The readings agree that the petition was answered and that the answer confirmed the faith.

The community's scholars drew from the account the lesson that the request for the sight of the divine working is not the same as the request for the proofs of the message. The sūrah presents the second as the duty of the one who hears the invitation, and it presents the first as the gift given to Abraham.

**The Closing Names**

The sentence closes with the instruction to know that God is mighty and wise, and the community's teachers read the pair as the ground of the demonstration. The first name concerns the power that the sign displayed, and the second concerns the order in which the sign was arranged.

The attributes also complete the instruction that the prophet received, since the knowledge of the two names is presented as the conclusion of the event. The account therefore ends where the request began, in the knowledge of God, and the demonstration of the resurrection serves that knowledge rather than standing on its own.

**What the Verse Establishes**

The sentence establishes the request of Abraham, the question that answered it, the peace of heart that the request sought, the command concerning the four birds, the call that gathered them, and the attributes of God that closed the account. The teachers used the verse when they spoke about the degrees of certainty that the believers reach, and the community's scholars placed it among the passages that describe the growth of knowledge in the hearts of those who serve God.

## Sūrah al-Nisāʾ 4:135

> O believers! Stand firm for justice as witnesses for Allah even if it is against yourselves, your parents, or close relatives. Be they rich or poor, Allah is best to ensure their interests. So do not let your desires cause you to deviate ˹from justice˺. If you distort the testimony or refuse to give it, then ˹know that˺ Allah is certainly All-Aware of what you do.

### Expanded Commentary

**Justice even against the self**

This is one of the Qur’an’s strongest commands concerning testimony. Believers must stand firmly for justice as witnesses for Allah, even when the truth harms themselves, parents, or close relatives. Loyalty to family, tribe, or personal advantage cannot override the claim of justice.

**Rich and poor before the law**

The verse prevents both forms of bias: favouring the rich because their interests appear important and favouring the poor merely because poverty evokes sympathy. Allah is better able to ensure their interests than the witness is. Justice is not a charitable mood; it is accurate treatment of the person’s actual right.

Desire can distort testimony through fear, love, resentment, or the wish to avoid inconvenience. The warning against twisting words or refusing testimony covers both falsification and silent evasion. Courts, workplaces, family mediations, and public reporting all require this discipline.

Allah is aware of what the witness does. A truthful statement may be costly in the moment, but it prevents the greater harm of teaching society that truth belongs to whoever has the stronger relationship or wallet.

**Testimony before family loyalty**

A relative’s poverty may call for support, but it does not justify changing the facts. A wealthy relative may need protection from false accusation, but wealth does not make his version true. The witness who keeps these distinctions honours both compassion and justice, rather than sacrificing one to the other.

## Sūrah al-Nisāʾ 4:135

> O believers! Stand firm for justice as witnesses for Allah even if it is against yourselves, your parents, or close relatives. Be they rich or poor, Allah is best to ensure their interests. So do not let your desires cause you to deviate ˹from justice˺. If you distort the testimony or refuse to give it, then ˹know that˺ Allah is certainly All-Aware of what you do.

**Expanded Commentary**

**Stand Firm for Justice as Witnesses for God Even Against Yourselves**

The verse opens *yā ayyuhā alladhīna āmanū kūnū qawwāmīna bi al-qisṭi shuhadāʾ li Allāhi wa law ʿalā anfusikum aw al-wālidayni wa al-aqrabīn* — O believers, be you steadfast, standing, upholders of justice, with equity, as witnesses for God even if against yourselves or parents or close kin. *Qawwāmīn bi al-qisṭ* — upholders of justice, maintainers of equity, ever standing for fairness — was explained with *qawwām* — much standing, firm — the same root as *to stand up for orphans with justice* (4:127) and *to establish prayer* at fixed writ (4:103). *Shuhadāʾ li Allāhi* — witnesses for God — was read as testimony offered for Him, not for clan, as the sūrah commands to return trusts and judge fairly as noble command from the All-Hearing, All-Seeing (4:58) and to judge between people by what He shows (4:105) and to verify before fighting (4:94). *Wa law ʿalā anfusikum aw al-wālidayni wa al-aqrabīn* — even if against yourselves, parents, close relatives — was explained as the Book’s severest test of the single soul’s ties (4:1): to honour ties is not to hide a kinsman’s theft by night plotting (4:108) nor to advocate for self-wrongers (4:107); to be just even against those ties is to honour the Creator of the single soul more than the soul’s branch. Early reports noted that this verse came where a dispute between parties of unequal rank pressed witnesses to favour the rich; the verse says witness for Him though the witness be against the self.

**Be They Rich or Poor — God Is Best to Ensure Interests**

The guidance *in yakun ghaniyyan aw faqīran fa Allāhu awlā bihimā* — be they rich or poor — the disputants — God is nearer, more worthy, better to ensure interests of both — was explained as prohibition of partiality by wealth. The rich might be favoured for honour or repayment, the poor pitied to tilt justice; both tilts were condemned, as the sūrah warns *all honour and power belongs to Him* (4:139) sought by taking disbelievers as allies, and warns that most secret talks have no good except charity, kindness, reconciliation (4:114) — pity without truth is not those goods. *Fa Allāhu awlā bihimā* — God is nearer, more worthy of both, better guardian of both — was explained as that He owns heavens and earth (4:126) and suffices as Trustee (4:132) and is Self-Sufficient, Praiseworthy (4:131) — guardianship of rich and poor alike belongs to Him, so the witness need not be guardian to either by tilting. The sūrah has said that the helpless men, women, children are hoped to be pardoned (4:99) and that the emigrant finds many havens (4:100) — protection is His; to stand for justice does not require protecting the poor by false testimony nor the rich by distortion, because He ensures interests better.

**So Do Not Let Desires Cause You to Deviate From Justice**

The warning *fa lā tattabiʿū al-hawā an taʿdilū* — so do not follow, do not let desires cause you to deviate, to incline from being just, from equitableness — was explained with *hawā* — desire, caprice, inclination — the same *hawā* that makes you deviate greatly if followed (4:27), that would make you follow other than believers’ path (4:115), and that makes private talk about charity insincere if for show. *An taʿdilū* — deviating from being just — was read as lest you not be just, lest you swerve; the sūrah earlier warned *if you fear ill conduct advise, then do not share beds, then discipline gently* (4:34) — justice even in household — and warned *do not incline entirely leaving the other suspended* (4:129) — justice even in polygyny. To follow *hawā* was linked to deceiving where eloquence persuades a judge to give a brother’s right as fire (Bukhārī 2680); desire there is eloquence, kin preference, pity, or greed for fleeting gain by denying peace (4:94). The command was read as the Book’s sent with truth — *bi al-ḥaqq* — to judge by what He shows (4:105): to follow desire is to judge by what He did not show.

**If You Distort or Refuse — He Is All-Aware**

The threat *wa in talwū aw tuʿriḍū fa inna Allāha kāna bi mā taʿmalūna khabīrā* — and if you distort, twist — *lawy* — testimony, or refuse, turn away from giving it, then indeed God is ever All-Aware of what you do — was explained with two evasions: *talwū* — to twist, to distort, to bend the tongue, to change wording, to give ambiguous testimony — and *tuʿriḍū* — to turn aside, to refuse, to neglect to testify, to hide. Both were condemned as opposite of *qawwāmīn bi al-qisṭ* and *shuhadāʾ li Allāh*. The sūrah has exposed those who hide from people but God is with them where they plot by night (4:108) — twisting is the daylight form of that night plotting — and who advocate in this life but will find no advocate on the Day (4:109) — refusing is the silence of that advocate where truth was needed. *Fa inna Allāha kāna bi mā taʿmalūna khabīrā* — He is Ever All-Aware of what you do — was explained as that He who is Fully Aware encompassing everything (4:126) and All-Aware of what you do where settlement was sought (4:128) and All-Hearing, All-Seeing where both rewards are with Him (4:134) knows twisting and refusing as He knows standing. The promise elsewhere *“whoever does good male or female with faith will enter Paradise not wronged a speck”* (4:124) assumes the testimony that admits good; to twist it wrongs the speck.

**A Verse That Follows Universal Ownership With Universal Justice**

Placed after two humanity addresses — *We commanded those before you and you to be mindful* (4:131), *to Him belongs all, He suffices as Trustee* (4:132), *He can remove O humanity and bring others* (4:133), *with Him are both rewards He hears and sees* (4:134) — this verse is the believers’ answer: stand firm for justice as witnesses for Him. The sūrah that began with honouring ties from a single soul (4:1), showed orphan girls deprived yet desired (4:127), wife fearing *nushūz* where settlement best (4:128), wives between whom inclination cannot be equalled (4:129), now says standing for justice may be against the very ties you honour — yourselves, parents, kin — and for the very rich or poor you once pitted — yet do not follow desire to deviate, lest you twist or turn away. The sūrah that taught verification before fighting (4:94) and judging by Book (4:105) here teaches verification before testifying: be *qawwām* — ever standing — so that twisting and refusing are not options. For the weak this is the highest protection: where wealth tempts and poverty pities, justice upheld for God is the haven as sure as many havens for the emigrant, and He who is All-Aware of what you do will not wrong a speck.

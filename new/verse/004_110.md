## Sūrah al-Nisāʾ 4:110

> Whoever commits evil or wrongs themselves then seeks Allah’s forgiveness will certainly find Allah All-Forgiving, Most Merciful.

### Expanded Commentary

**The open door after sin**

Whoever commits evil or wrongs himself and then seeks Allah’s forgiveness will find Him Forgiving and Merciful. The verse follows severe warnings in order to prevent despair. Sin is real, but it need not become identity. A person who turns toward God is met by forgiveness, not by a demand to pretend the past never occurred.

**Private and public wrongs**

“Wronging oneself” includes sins whose immediate victim is the sinner, while evil can include harm to others. Where another person’s right is involved, seeking forgiveness from Allah must be accompanied by restitution, apology, or a just settlement. Mercy does not permit the wrongdoer to make the victim pay for his repentance.

The verse’s certainty—“will find”—gives hope grounded in divine promise. It does not license planned sin followed by a ritual phrase. The turning must be sincere, and it should produce changed conduct. Yet the believer should never tell a repentant person that his past makes mercy impossible.

**Return and restitution**

The promise of forgiveness should produce movement: stop the wrong, confess it appropriately, return what was taken, and build habits that prevent recurrence. A person who cannot yet repair everything can begin with truth and seek help. Divine mercy opens the work of repair; it does not make the work unnecessary.

## Sūrah al-Nisāʾ 4:110

> Whoever commits evil or wrongs themselves then seeks Allah’s forgiveness will certainly find Allah All-Forgiving, Most Merciful.

**Expanded Commentary**

**Whoever Commits Evil or Wrongs Themselves**

The verse opens *wa man yaʿmal sūʾan aw yaẓlim nafsahū* — whoever commits evil, does a bad deed, or wrongs themselves. *Sūʾ* — evil, bad deed — was explained as harm to another: theft, false accusation, consuming wealth unjustly, the theft of armour and its projection onto the innocent that the preceding passage exposed (4:105–112). *Yaẓlim nafsah* — wrongs themselves — was explained as that the same act is self-wrong, as the sūrah has said *“do not advocate for those who wrong themselves”* (4:107), *“whoever earns sin earns it only against himself”* (4:111), and *“whoever wrongs themselves then seeks forgiveness”* is the paradigm of repentance. The pairing *sūʾan aw yaẓlim nafsah* was read as comprehensive: evil outward toward people and wrong inward toward the soul are not two categories but two faces of one act, because to betray trust (4:58) is to betray the self that will stand before God who is with you when you plot by night (4:108). The sūrah has shown that human weakness makes evil possible — *humankind was created weak* (4:28), desires wish you deviate greatly (4:27), envy wishes you alike in disbelief (4:89) — yet the same weakness, acknowledged, opens the next clause. Classical notes stressed the *man* — whoever — as unrestricted: believer, companion who erred, deceitful who plotted, even the Jew falsely accused — the door is for whoever acted.

**Then Seeks God’s Forgiveness**

The clause *thumma yastaghfiri Allāha* — then seeks God’s forgiveness, asks Him to cover and efface — was explained with *thumma* — then, afterwards — as indicating sequence after the act, not delay only. *Yastaghfiri* — to seek *maghfirah* — was read as the act commanded to the Prophet ﷺ directly — *seek forgiveness, God is All-Forgiving* (4:106) — now universalised to every wrongdoer. The sūrah has taught that seeking where the Messenger prays for them after wronging themselves they would find Him ever Accepting of Repentance (4:64), that seeking after mistake by freeing or fasting is *tawbatan mina Allāh* (4:92), and that the helpless hoped to be pardoned have *ʿAfuww Ghafūr* (4:99). To seek was explained as confession, remorse, abandonment of the act, and resolve — not merely speech — as the sūrah elsewhere refuses repentance that waits until death says *now I repent* (4:18) and accepts repentance soon after error (4:17). The *thumma* also separates the advocacy that fails on the Day (4:109) from the seeking that succeeds: no one will be his *wakīl* there, but he himself seeking here will find. Early reports noted that the thief who was exposed could still, by seeking, find the same Names that the advocate was told to seek.

**Will Certainly Find God All-Forgiving, Most Merciful**

The promise *yajid Allāha Ghafūran Raḥīmā* — will certainly find God All-Forgiving, Most Merciful, will find Him so — was explained as certainty of finding: *yajid* — will find — not *perhaps will find*. The same *yajid* was used where the wrongers coming to the Prophet ﷺ would *certainly find Him ever Accepting of Repentance, Most Merciful* (4:64); here every evil-doer seeking will find the same. *Ghafūr* — All-Forgiving, much covering — and *Raḥīm* — Most Merciful, ever Merciful — were explained as the two that make finding more than acquittal: covering the evil, removing trace of self-wrong, and bestowing mercy beyond covering — ranks, havens, secure reward as with the emigrant (4:100). The sūrah has sealed forgiveness diversely — *Ghafūr Raḥīm* for strivers (4:96), *ʿAfuww Ghafūr* for helpless (4:99), *Ghafūr Raḥīm* for the judge told to seek (4:106); here the pair is the universal seal for every *sūʾ* and self-wrong that turns. The promise was linked to *“God does not wrong even an atom”* (4:40): to seek and not find would be a wrong measured in less than atom; the *yajid* declares He does not wrong. The verse was also placed against the threat for intentional killing where anger, curse, and mighty preparation remain unless sought away (4:93): seeking is the opening even there where it is most needed.

**A Verse That Opens the Door Advocacy Could Not**

Placed after *“you advocate for them in this life, who will advocate on the Day?”* (4:109) and *“they hide from people but not from God with whom they plotted by night”* (4:108), this verse opens the door advocacy could not: seeking forgiveness finds. The sūrah that pronounced *God does not like the deceitful sinner* (4:107) and that said there is no good in most secret talks except charity, kindness, reconciliation (4:114), here says the good that remains open after the evil secret talk is to seek. The sūrah that warned the enemy wishes you neglect for a sweep (4:102) and that commanded not to falter in pursuit (4:104) yet to judge only by what God shows (4:105), here steadies the heart that fears judgment has been corrupted by advocacy: whoever — advocate or wrongdoer — commits evil then seeks will find. For the weak for whom burdens were lightened, this is the lightest of all: after teaching prayer shortened and guarded (4:101–102) and remembered standing, sitting, lying (4:103), the sūrah lightens the heaviest burden — guilt — by making finding certain upon seeking.

**The Finding That Makes the Next Two Verses Intelligible**

The *yajid* — will find — makes the next two verses intelligible: *whoever commits sin does so only to his own loss, God is All-Knowing, All-Wise* (4:111), and *whoever earns evil or sin then throws it on the innocent bears slander and blatant sin* (4:112). Both assume that sin’s owner is the self, not the advocate, and that the self’s recourse is seeking, not throwing. The verse was read as the pivot of the judgment passage that began with the Book sent in truth (4:105): the Book judges between people, night plotting cannot hide from the Encompassing, advocacy perishes on the Day, yet seeking succeeds here, because the Judge who is All-Knowing, All-Wise (4:111) is also All-Forgiving, Most Merciful, and is found by whoever seeks before seeking is closed by death.

## Sūrah al-Anʿām 6:159

> Indeed, you ˹O Prophet˺ are not responsible whatsoever for those who have divided their faith and split into sects. Their judgment rests only with Allah. And He will inform them of what they used to do.

**Expanded Commentary**

**The Divisions Among the Communities**

Indeed, you are not responsible whatsoever for those who have divided their faith and split into sects. The verse turns from the audience's waiting to the condition of the communities that had received the earlier scriptures, and it states that the Prophet bears no responsibility for them. The sūrah has been addressing a city in which the divisions of the earlier communities were known, and the sentence places those divisions outside the messenger's charge.

The Book's warnings against division appear in several passages. In the sūrah of the Family of Imran, the community is told not to be like those who split into sects and differed after clear proofs had come to them, and the verse states that they will have a great punishment (3:105); the sūrah of the Romans describes those who divided their faith and split into sects, each rejoicing in what they have (30:32); and the sūrah of Consultation states that the people of the earlier books did not split into sects out of mutual envy until knowledge had come to them (42:14). The sūrah of the Cattle itself warned against following the other ways that branch off from the path (6:153), and the sentence here describes the condition of the people who took those ways.

**The Limit of the Prophet's Responsibility**

The phrase not responsible whatsoever repeats the limit the sūrah has set in several places. The Prophet was told that he is neither keeper nor maintainer over the people who deny his message (6:107), that he is not a keeper over them (6:104), and that his duty is to warn and to remind. The sentence in front of us applies the limit to a particular case, and it does so in the strongest form the language allows.

The Book gives the same assurance to the Prophet in its accounts of the earlier messengers. In the sūrah of the Bee, he is told to invite people to the way of the Lord with wisdom and good instruction, and the verse states that his Lord knows best who has strayed and who is guided (16:125), and the sūrah of the Overwhelming Event states that he is only a reminder and not a controller over the people (88:21–22). The verse in front of us belongs to that family of statements, and the divisions it describes were the work of the communities themselves.

**The Judgment Left With God**

Their judgment rests only with Allah. The sentence states where the matter is settled, and the Book uses the same referral in its accounts of the disputes among the people of the earlier scriptures. In the sūrah of the Pilgrimage, God states that He will judge between the people on the day of judgement regarding their differences (22:69), and the sūrah of the Cow states that God will judge between them concerning what they differed about (2:113). The verse in front of us states the referral with respect to the divisions described in its first sentence.

The Book's habit of referring disputes to God serves the same purpose as the limits placed on the Prophet's responsibility. The community is not made the arbiter of the other communities' standing, and the question of who is right among the sects is left to the One who knows. The sentence in front of us states that arrangement in its simplest form.

**He Will Inform Them of What They Used to Do**

And He will inform them of what they used to do. The clause states what the judgment will consist of, and the Book uses the same formula at the end of many of its passages about the conduct of the people it addresses. The sūrah of the Cattle has already used it in its account of the idolaters who will be informed of what they did (6:108), and the sūrah of the Spoils and the sūrah of Yūnus use it in their own contexts.

The formula gives the judgment its content. The divisions among the communities were produced by conduct: by envy, by the pursuit of advantage, and by the abandonment of what had been given. The sentence in front of us states that the people who produced those divisions will be shown what they did, and the phrase leaves the assessment to the One who knows.

**What the Verse Says to the Community That Reads It**

The verse carries a warning for the community that receives the Book, and the sūrahs that record the divisions of the earlier communities attach the warning explicitly. The sūrah of the Family of Imran tells the believers not to become like those who split into sects, and the sūrah of the Cattle's own warning about the other ways that branch off from the path is addressed to the community commanded to follow it.

The Book's treatment of the community's unity appears throughout its addresses. The sūrah of the Chambers states that the believers are one brotherhood (49:10), and the sūrah of the Family of Imran commands them to hold firmly to the rope of God and not to be divided (3:103). The verse in front of us supplies the other side of that instruction by describing what division produced in the communities that came before.

**The Place of the Verse in the Sūrah**

The verse follows the passage about the Book's arrival and the response that will not benefit those who waited (6:155–158), and it precedes the statement about the reward of the good deed and the punishment of the evil deed (6:160). The sūrah's closing section therefore moves from the Book to the divisions, and from the divisions to the accountability of the individual.

The verses that follow describe the reward that is multiplied and the punishment that is not, and the passage ends with the declaration of the Prophet about the path of his Lord (6:161). The verse in front of us supplies the contrast between the communities that divided and the individual who will stand before God with his own deed, and the arrangement gives the reader both warnings at once.

**The Transition to the Next Verse**

The next verse states that whoever comes with a good deed will be rewarded tenfold, while whoever comes with a bad deed will be punished for only one, and that none will be wronged (6:160). The sentence turns from the divisions of the groups to the accounting of the persons, and it states the measure by which the accounting is conducted.

The two verses together dispose of the question the sūrah's audience might have carried about the communities around it. The sects are left to God's judgment, and the individual is told what his own deed produces; the verse in front of us states the first half of that arrangement, and the verse that follows states the second.

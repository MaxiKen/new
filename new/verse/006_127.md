## Sūrah al-Anʿām 6:127

> They will have the Home of Peace with their Lord, Who will be their Guardian because of what they used to do.

**Expanded Commentary**

**The Home of Peace**

They will have the Home of Peace with their Lord. The verse names the destination of the road described in the sentence before it, and the name it gives the destination is the Book's own. The sūrah of Yūnus states that God invites all people to the Home of Peace and guides whoever He wills to the straight path (10:25), and the sūrah of the Troops describes the arrival of the mindful at the gates of the garden, where the keepers greet them with the word of peace and tell them that they have been made pure (39:73).

The pairing of the home's name with the word the Book uses for peace is deliberate. The Book's peace is the condition in which a person is free of fear and loss, and the sūrah of the Cow attaches that condition to those who follow the guidance, saying that they will have no fear and no grief (2:62). The home named in this verse is the place where the condition is complete, and the sūrah places it at the end of the road it has just described.

**The Home and Its Lord**

With their Lord. The phrase attaches the reward to the presence of the One who gave it rather than to a location, and the Book uses the same construction in its descriptions of the reward of the believers throughout. The sūrah of the Troops ends its description of the garden with the declaration that the believers have attained what they hoped for, and the Book's accounts of the Hereafter consistently present the meeting with God as the substance of the reward that the images describe.

The verse has already described the path as the path of the Lord, and the destination named here is the same Lord's home. The sūrah's arrangement is therefore a single movement from the guidance of the path to the presence of the guide, and the two sentences belong to one statement about the community that responds.

**The Guardian**

Who will be their Guardian because of what they used to do. The Book's term for the guardian describes the one who takes charge of another's affairs, and the sūrah of the Cow uses the same word for God at the end of its passage on light and darkness, saying that God is the Guardian of the believers who brings them out of darkness into light (2:257). The sūrah of the Kneeling states that God is the patron of the righteous while the wrongdoers are patrons of one another (45:19).

The Book's use of the term in this verse completes the description of the reward: the people who followed the path are not only admitted to the home but placed under the care of its owner. The state described is one of dependence fulfilled, since the person who has been guided through the world arrives at the end under the protection of the One who guided him.

**Because of What They Used to Do**

The clause attaches the reward to the conduct of the people who receive it, and the Book uses the same formula at the end of many of its promises. The sūrah of the Bowing states that no soul can imagine the delights kept in store for the believers as a reward for what they used to do (32:17), and the sūrah of the Prophets says that those for whom the finest reward has been destined will be kept far away from the fire (21:101). The formula is the Book's way of stating that the reward corresponds to the conduct it follows.

The Book's teaching on the relationship between deeds and reward appears throughout its addresses to the community. Its statements about forgiveness and grace stand alongside its statements about recompense, and the two belong together in a scheme in which the believers are told that their efforts are not disregarded and that what they send ahead will be found with God (73:20). The verse in front of us states the principle in its compact form, and the sūrah's later passages apply it to the reckoning of the communities.

**What the Verse Establishes for the Reader**

The verse closes the sūrah's section on guidance with a statement of what the guidance leads to. The Book has described the opening of the heart, the straightness of the path, and the signs made clear for the mindful; the reward named here is the end of that whole arrangement. A reader who has followed the sūrah's argument from its opening verses now sees the destination attached to the road.

The placement of the verse also prepares the reader for the passages that follow. The sūrah turns immediately to the gathering of the jinn and their human associates, the address to the communities at the resurrection, and the account of what the people with the most wealth and the greatest standing will bring with them. The Home of Peace stands at the head of those passages as the alternative they define.

**The Contrast With What Follows**

The next verse records the day when God will gather the beings together and address the jinn as the ones who misled great numbers of human beings, and the human associates will reply that they benefited from one another and have now reached the term appointed for them (6:128). The two verses set the two destinies side by side: the community of the path and the community of mutual deception.

The sūrah has been building toward that contrast since its treatment of the enemies of the prophets and the hearts that received their whisperings. What the verse in front of us supplies is the end of the road for the people who accepted the message, and it does so in terms that the sūrah's audience, familiar with the conditions of travel and hospitality, could measure against the alternative.

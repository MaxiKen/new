## Sūrah al-Nisāʾ 4:82

> Do they not then reflect on the Quran? Had it been from anyone other than Allah, they would have certainly found in it many inconsistencies.

### Expanded Commentary

**Reflection on the Qur’an**

The verse calls people to reflect deeply on the Qur’an. Reflection is more than recitation or the search for isolated slogans; it means considering how its teachings relate, how its warnings unfold, and how its guidance addresses both belief and conduct. The Qur’an invites active thought rather than passive possession.

**Coherence as a sign**

If the Qur’an were from someone other than Allah, the verse says, its many internal contradictions would be exposed. The argument does not claim that every reader will instantly resolve every difficulty. It points to the Book’s sustained coherence across revelation over years, changing circumstances, law, narrative, theology, and moral formation.

Apparent tension should therefore lead to study, context, language, and qualified interpretation—not to a hurried accusation. The reader must also ask whether the contradiction lies in the text or in an assumption brought to it. Reflection is a form of humility before a Book whose unity exceeds the reader’s first impression.

**Reflection is disciplined reading**

A reader should compare passages, examine context, learn Arabic terms through reliable scholarship, and notice the way a command operates within the whole chapter. The absence of contradiction becomes more evident through patient study. Difficulty is an invitation to understanding, not automatic evidence against the text.

## Sūrah al-Nisāʾ 4:82

> Do they not then reflect on the Quran? Had it been from anyone other than Allah, they would have certainly found in it many inconsistencies.

**Expanded Commentary**

**Do They Not Reflect on the Qur’an**

The question *a fa lā yatadabbarūna al-Qurʾān* — do they not then reflect on the Qur’an — follows the nightly contradiction of those who say *we obey* and then spend the night contradicting (4:81). *Tadabbur* — to reflect, to follow through to the end, to look at the behind — was explained as reading not only the first word but the whole, following each limit to its place and seeing how it connects. The sūrah has shown much to reflect on: creation from one soul and guarding womb-ties (4:1), returning orphan wealth with witnesses (4:6), honouring shares after debts (4:11–12), guarding marriage with dowries and chastity (4:24), making things clear and guiding to noble ways and turning in mercy (4:26–27), taking precautions and going forth (4:71), and fighting for the oppressed (4:75). The night-plotters’ *tabyīt* — spending the night contradicting — is the opposite of *tadabbur*; one spends the night to distort, the other spends the night to understand. The question *a fa lā* — do they not then — implies that reflection would have prevented the contradiction, because the Qur’an’s coherence would have answered the very dispute they took to false judges (4:60) and the excuse they swore after disaster (4:62). The Qur’an elsewhere says that reflection leads to certainty that the Book is from God.

**Had It Been From Other Than Allah**

The test *wa law kāna min ʿindi ghayri Allāhi la wajadū fīhi ikhtilāfan kathīrā* — had it been from anyone other than Allah, they would have found in it many inconsistencies — was read as proof of divine origin by internal coherence. *Ikhtilāf kathīr* — many inconsistencies, much variance — was explained as contradiction between its rulings: inheritance shares that divide little and much (4:7), dowry as free gift not reclaimed (4:4, 4:20–21), trade by mutual consent (4:29), guardianship with witnesses (4:6), and fighting for the oppressed (4:75) — each could clash if man-made, yet they cohere. Had the Book been from a human — the Prophet ﷺ himself, or a committee, or a school — variance would have appeared as forgetfulness, favour, or change of mind under pressure. The sūrah’s detailed legislation, where a mother’s share shifts from third to sixth when siblings exist (4:11), where a wife’s share is quarter or eighth (4:12), where a bondwoman’s penalty is half (4:25), and where earth purifies where water fails (4:43) — is precisely detailed yet without contradiction, because the distributor is not the community that craves what others have (4:32) but the One who never wrongs by an atom (4:40). To reflect is to see that a Book that governs both *matāʿ qalīl* — little enjoyment (4:77) — and *ajr ʿaẓīm* — great reward (4:40, 4:67) — without tilting — *mayl* — is not a patchwork.

**The Inconsistency They Would Have Found Elsewhere**

Early authorities noted that human works show inconsistencies of three kinds: factual, where a claim made early is forgotten later; moral, where a limit praised in one place is excused in another; and stylistic, where voice shifts betray many hands. The Qur’an, though revealed over years in Makkah and Madinah, in ease and hardship, shows none of that. Its ethic of *iḥsān* — kind doing to parents, relatives, orphans, poor, neighbours, companions, travellers, those in possession (4:36) — appears with the same force where orphans are guarded and where fighting is commanded; its ethic of *ḥudūd* — limits — appears with the same word where inheritance is divided and wherefaces could be wiped (4:47). The hypocrites who spend the night contradicting contradict precisely because their plan is from other than God; it must vary, it must hide, it is recorded (4:81). The Book they hear by day does not vary, which is why the Prophet ﷺ is told to put trust in the sufficient Trustee (4:81); the Trustee’s word does not change between day and night. The sūrah elsewhere says *“do they not reflect, or are there locks on hearts”* and *“had it been from other than God they would have found many inconsistencies”* is the key that would open those locks, if they reflected.

**Reflection as the Opposite of Night-Plotting**

Placed after nightly contradiction, this verse offers the alternative night. The same night that a party spends plotting what contradicts *we obey* could be spent in *tadabbur* — following the Qur’an to its ends. The sūrah has taught that news of security or fear should be referred to the Messenger and authorities, where those with sound judgment would validate it (4:83); reflection is that referral at the level of the individual. A community that reflects will not publicise fear for the weak schemer’s profit, nor will it fear people as God should be feared when the order to fight comes (4:77). It will see that death overtakes wherever you are, even in towers (4:78), and that both good and evil are from God’s decree while good from Him and evil from the self as means (4:78–79), a coherence that only reflection holds together. The verse generalises the sūrah’s method since *“Allah wills to make things clear”* (4:26): clarity is given, but it requires *tadabbur* to be entered. The night-plotters who do not reflect choose the weak scheme that is ever weak (4:76); the reflectors find the Book whose many detailed limits do not contradict because they were not from a weak plotter but from the All-Knowing, All-Wise.

**A Verse That Holds the Book’s Trust**

Between the record of nightly schemes and the publicising of security or fear (4:81, 4:83), this verse holds the Book’s trust. The trust that the sūrah commanded to return — *return trusts to rightful owners and judge with fairness* (4:58) — includes the greatest trust: the Qur’an itself. To reflect on it is to return it to its purpose — to be the judge in tangles — *yuḥakkimūka* (4:65) — and the referent for disputes as best outcome (4:59). To fail to reflect is to leave it as sound while contradicting it by night, which is the precise breach the previous verse named. The sūrah’s question *do they not then reflect* is therefore not a critique of intelligence but of will, as the next verse will say *had it not been for God’s grace and mercy you would have followed Satan, except for a few* (4:83). Reflection is grace-enabled, and its fruit is seeing no inconsistency where inconsistency would have been many, had the source been other than God. For the weak for whom burdens were lightened, that seeing is itself lightening: the Book that commands a mother’s sixth and a wife’s eighth can be trusted to command fighting and purification without wavering, because its Source does not waver, and the one who reflects will find that the path from orphan to inheritance to marriage to battle is one straight path walked.

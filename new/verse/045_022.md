## Sūrah al-Jāthiyah 45:22

> For Allah created the heavens and the earth for a purpose, so that every soul may be paid back for what it has committed. And none will be wronged.

**Expanded Commentary**

**Creation With an Aim**

The verse gives a reason for the difference the previous verse asserted, and it grounds the reason in cosmology. The heavens and the earth were not made for sport; they were made for a purpose, and the phrase translated "for a purpose" is the one the Book uses when it insists that the created order is not a game. The same claim is stated across many chapters: the heavens and the earth and everything between them were not created in play (44:38–39); they were created with a purpose and a fixed term (46:3); and the alternation of sun and moon exists so that people may count years and reckon time (10:5). The handful of passages that deny a purposeless creation are the Book's answer to any philosophy that treats the world as an accident of matter. The order a person sees in the sky is the visible edge of an intention.

**The Purpose Named: Repayment**

The verse does not leave the aim undefined; it names it. The creation exists so that every soul may be paid back for what it has committed. This is a startling order of explanation, because it makes the moral accounting of human beings part of the reason for the physical universe. The exegetes take the verse in close connection with the dispute it answers: since things are made in truth and not at random, the lives of the believers and the deniers cannot be interchangeable. The pairing of a vast creation with an individual's deeds runs through the Book, and it rests on the premise that the two are connected. If the world has a purpose, then what people do inside it matters to that purpose, and the accounting follows.

**"What It Has Committed" — the Language of Earning**

The verb used for the accounting describes earning, and it carries the sense of work that attaches to the worker. The Book uses it whenever it wants to make the connection between a deed and its owner immediate, as when it says that no soul will be wronged in the least on that Day and that people will be rewarded only for what they used to do (36:54). The two groups of the previous verse are ranked according to what they have done, and the ranking is described as full payment (46:19). The metaphor throughout is commercial and it is not accidental. The first audience understood wages, debts, and balances, and the Qur'an presents the Hereafter as an account that is settled exactly, neither inflated nor discounted.

**"None Will Be Wronged" — the Guarantee**

The verse ends with an assurance that the Book repeats more often than almost any other: no one will be treated unjustly. The phrase appears in the same form in the passage on the two groups and, in a fuller scene, on the day when the record of deeds is laid open and the wicked cry out that it has left nothing out, small or great, and they find everything they did present before them, and the verse states that the Lord will never wrong anyone (18:49). The repetition has a function. The Book is describing a court that will rule on every case at once, and the natural human fear is that such a court cannot be fair to everyone. The assurance is that the judge has full knowledge and perfect justice, so the fear is misplaced.

**Equality Denied by the Purpose Itself**

Read against the previous verse, this sentence is the whole answer to the deniers' assumption. If the creation exists in order to repay each soul for what it has earned, then a final equality between the good and the wicked would defeat the purpose for which the universe was made. The two claims cannot both stand, and the verse gives the one that can. What the deniers were hoping for was not merely a reprieve for themselves; it was the abolition of the distinction that gives their lives weight. The Book's reply is that the distinction is built into the fabric of things and will be honoured at the end, whether or not the parties to the dispute accept it now.

**A World That Is Not Indifferent**

Most people live as though the question of the world's purpose were theoretical, and the verse makes it practical. A universe with a purpose is a universe in which nothing done inside it is lost; a universe without one is a place where the difference between cruelty and kindness is a matter of preference. The sūrah's readers had already been told that the sea was subjected for them and that the created order was given by grace. This verse completes the picture by saying what the gift was for. Everything was made so that a person's choices could be weighed in a setting that gives them weight. It is the last claim a denier wants to hear and the only claim that makes the rest of the message worth the cost of accepting it.

**The Purpose Hidden Until It Is Enforced**

The verse states the aim in the present tense as a fact, and the enforcement of it belongs to a day the sūrah has already mentioned and will describe in detail later. Between the statement and its execution lies the period in which every human being acts, and the Book treats that interval as the whole point of the delay. If the purpose were enforced immediately, the choices would lose their character as choices. The verse does not present the delay as divine indifference or as forgetfulness, since it has just said that the repayment is certain. It presents the world as an arranged place with a fixed schedule, in which the only unknown is what each person will do before the schedule moves forward.

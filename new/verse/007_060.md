## Sūrah al-Aʿrāf 7:60

> But the chiefs of his people said, “We surely see that you are clearly misguided.”

**Expanded Commentary**

**The Notables Who Answer**

The verse reports the answer of the notables among the people of Noah, and the answer charges the messenger with manifest error. The Book uses the term for the leaders of a community in its accounts of several peoples, and it presents them as the group that answers the messengers on behalf of the community. The verse in front of us places that answer at the opening of the sūrah's first narrative, and the pattern repeats with ʿĀd (7:66), Thamūd (7:75), and the people of Shuʿayb.

The term notables identifies the leading men whose standing rested on wealth, lineage, and influence, and the Book's accounts of the communities consistently place the refusal with them. The sūrah of the Believers reports the disbelieving chiefs of Noah's people dismissing him as a man who wished to be superior to them (23:24), and the sūrah of Hūd reports the chiefs of the people saying that they saw nothing in the messenger that made him worthy of their following. The verse in front of us states the answer of that group in the sūrah's own account.

**The Charge of Misguidance**

The notables' answer is the charge that the messenger stands in clear error, and the Book's accounts of the communities show the same charge being answered from the other side. The messenger in the next verse replies that he is not in error and that he is a messenger from the Lord of all worlds (7:61), and the sūrah's later accounts report the same exchange between Hūd and his people (7:66–67) and between Ṣāliḥ and the leaders of Thamūd. The charge and its answer are stated in the same words on both sides.

The Book's treatment of the accusation of error as a return of the accuser's own condition appears in its descriptions of the fate of the communities. The sūrah's record of the destroyed peoples states that the rulers were the ones who went astray, and the sūrah of the Cow reports the disbelievers calling the believers fools while the charge belongs to themselves (2:13). The verse in front of us states the accusation in its first instance, and the sūrah's account of the outcome settles the question.

**The Standing of the Accusers**

The men who brought the charge were the community's leaders, and their standing rested on the arrangements of the world rather than on any knowledge of the message. The sūrah's later narratives describe them counting their numbers, their wealth, and their fortifications, and the sūrah of the Heights states that the numbers and the arrogance of the condemned are of no use on the day (7:48). This verse shows the group whose opinion the community followed, and the sūrah's accounts of the destruction show the outcome of that following.

The Book's treatment of leadership in its narratives includes the leaders' appearance of weight in the community's affairs, and the sūrah of the Confederates reports the followers in the Hereafter saying that they obeyed their leaders and the leaders led them astray (33:67). The verse here supplies the first statement of that leadership in the sūrah, and the narrative that follows shows what it produced.

**Why the Leaders Refuse**

The Book reports the substance of the leaders' objection in other accounts of the same exchange, and the objections are drawn from the messenger's ordinary humanity, his following among the poor, and the disruption he proposes to the community's inherited practice. The sūrah of Hūd reports the chiefs saying that they saw the messenger as a man like themselves and that only the lowest among the people had followed him. The present verse states the conclusion that the leaders draw from those considerations.

The sūrah's later accounts of the communities develop the same objections, and each of them is answered in the course of the narrative. The messenger's humanity is answered by the statement that a messenger must be a man, the following of the poor is answered by the Book's account of the weighing of deeds, and the disruption of the inherited practice is answered by the accounts of what the practice produced.

**The Pattern of the Answer**

The verse's brevity states the pattern that the sūrah will repeat, and the pattern is as much the argument of the passage as the destruction that follows the refusal. The leaders' charge in front of us is met in the next verse by the messenger's denial and his identification of his office, and the sūrah then reports the substance of his call and the response to it. The refusal is presented as a judgment made without examination, and the narrative supplies the examination in the messenger's own words.

The Book's descriptions of the people who dismiss the message without considering it appear in several passages, and the sūrah of the Cattle states that some turn away from the signs as though they had not heard them. The verse under discussion supplies the first instance of the dismissal in the sūrah's history, and the accounts that follow trace its consequences.

**Where the Verse Sits**

The verse stands sixtieth in the chapter, and it follows the call of Noah and precedes his reply. The narrative then continues with the exchange between the messenger and his people, the building of the ark, and the destruction of the deniers in the flood (7:61–64). Here the verse supplies the first response of the community.

The reader who has been given the call to worship the one God now reads the answer of the leaders. The messenger is charged with error, the charge is answered in the next verse, and the account proceeds to the outcome that the sūrah's introduction has promised.

**The Transition to the Next Verse**

The next verse reports the reply of Noah, that he is not misguided and that he is a messenger from the Lord of all worlds (7:61). The charge brought by the notables in front of us is answered in the messenger's own words, and the passage then states what he was sent to convey.

The two verses together form the exchange that the sūrah's narratives repeat with each people. The messenger is accused, the messenger answers with his office, and the account proceeds to the substance of the message; and the verse that follows states the delivery and the advice that the messenger offers to the community that has rejected him.

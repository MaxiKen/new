## Sūrah al-Isrāʾ 17:10

> **And ˹it warns˺ those who do not believe in the Hereafter ˹that˺ We have prepared for them a painful punishment.**

**Expanded Commentary**

**Warning For Those Who Do Not Believe In Hereafter**

Wa-anna al-ladhīna lā yu'minūna bi-l-ākhirati aʿtadnā lahum ʿadhāban alīman — and that those who do not believe in Hereafter We have prepared for them painful punishment. Wa-anna, and that, connecting to previous verse: Qur'an guides to most upright and gives good news to believers who do good of mighty reward, and warns those who do not believe in Hereafter that painful punishment prepared. Al-ladhīna lā yu'minūna bi-l-ākhirati, those who do not believe in Hereafter, deny resurrection, Hour, accountability, as Makkan polytheists did who swore Allah will not raise dead and said when we are bones and ashes will we be raised new creation. Aʿtadnā from ʿ-t-d, to prepare, make ready, from same root as ʿatīd, prepared, ready, and ʿatad, preparation. Lahum, for them. ʿAdhāban alīman, painful punishment, from ʿ-dh-b, punishment, torment, and a-l-m, painful, aching, from alam, pain. Al-Ṭabarī explains that Qur'an warns those who do not believe in Hereafter that painful punishment prepared, both in world and Hereafter, as previous verses described disgrace, Temple entry, destruction, Hell confinement, and as later verses will describe Hell confinement for disbelievers. The brackets ˹it warns˺ and ˹that˺ clarify that Qur'an warns, as verb yubashshir, gives good news, implies and warns.

**Why Hereafter Belief Is Central**

Belief in Hereafter is central because it motivates doing good and avoiding evil, gives meaning to mighty reward and painful punishment. If no Hereafter, then mighty reward and painful punishment have no ultimate meaning, and guidance to most upright has no ultimate purpose beyond worldly. The sūrah opened with Night Journey showing signs, then history of Children of Isra'il showing worldly punishment and restoration, then Qur'an guiding to most upright, then pairing of good news and warning based on belief. Previous chapter also said those who disbelieve in Hereafter have denying hearts and are arrogant, and to those who disbelieve in Hereafter belongs evil qualities. Here, Qur'an guides to most upright and gives good news to believers who do good of mighty reward in Hereafter, and warns those who do not believe in Hereafter of painful punishment in Hereafter. The pairing shows two outcomes based on belief in Hereafter and doing good vs disbelief. Belief in Hereafter is therefore foundation for moral code that follows.

**Painful Punishment Prepared**

Aʿtadnā lahum ʿadhāban alīman — We prepared for them painful punishment. Preparation indicates punishment already ready, not to be created later, as Hell is already prepared, as 3:131 says *Fear Fire prepared for disbelievers* (3:131), and Gardens prepared for righteous as 3:133 says *Hasten to forgiveness from Lord and Garden as wide as heavens and earth prepared for righteous* (3:133). Al-Qurṭubī says aʿtadnā indicates that punishment is prepared in advance, waiting, as Gardens prepared for righteous, showing reality of Hereafter. The punishment is alīman, painful, indicating severe, not light, aching, from a-l-m, pain. The preparation is by Allah, showing that He is All-Aware, All-Seeing of sins as verse 17 will say sufficient Lord as All-Aware All-Seeing of sins of servants. The warning is part of Qur'an's guidance to most upright: it guides by showing both reward and punishment, promise and threat, so that people choose reward and avoid punishment.

**Connection To Previous Chapter's Warnings And This Sūrah's Moral Code**

Previous chapter warned about disgrace, Hell as evil home for arrogant, evil consequences overtaking, being overwhelmed by what ridiculed, Fire where abandoned, etc., and said to those who disbelieve in Hereafter belongs evil qualities. This verse continues warning but specifically for those who do not believe in Hereafter, linking disbelief in Hereafter to painful punishment prepared. The sūrah's moral code that will follow, about parents, relatives, spending, children, adultery, killing, orphan wealth, pledges, measuring, knowledge, arrogance, is most upright way that leads to mighty reward and avoids painful punishment. The verse therefore is transition from history and general guidance to specific moral code: Qur'an guides to most upright, which includes moral code, and warns those who do not believe in Hereafter of painful punishment if they do not follow most upright. The pairing of good news and warning is typical Qur'anic method to guide.

**Linguistic Note On Iʿtād And Alīm**

Aʿtadnā from ʿ-t-d, to prepare, make ready, from same root as ʿatād, preparation, and ʿatīd, prepared, ready, as in *Indeed We prepared for wrongdoers Fire* (18:29). Al-ladhīna lā yu'minūna, those who do not believe, from a-m-n, to believe, from amina, to be secure. Bi-l-ākhirati, in Hereafter, from a-kh-r, last, Hereafter, latter, opposite of ūlā, first. ʿAdhāban from ʿ-dh-b, punishment, torment, from ʿadhaba, to punish. Alīman from a-l-m, painful, aching, from alima, to be in pain, from alam, pain. The verse's structure — and that those who do not believe in Hereafter We prepared for them painful punishment — with anna for that, alladhīna for those, lā yu'minūna for do not believe, aʿtadnā for We prepared, ʿadhāban alīman for painful punishment, creates warning that is counterpart to good news in previous verse, showing two outcomes of Qur'an's guidance to most upright, mighty reward vs painful punishment.

**How This Verse Teaches About Consequences And Motivation**

This verse teaches that disbelief in Hereafter has consequence: painful punishment prepared, ready, waiting. It shows that Qur'an's guidance includes both promise and warning, good news and warning, targhīb and tarhīb, to motivate, encourage and deter. Al-Rāzī says good news for believers who do good encourages doing good, warning for those who do not believe in Hereafter deters disbelief and evil. Together they guide to most upright: belief in Hereafter and doing good leads to mighty reward, disbelief leads to painful punishment. The practical lesson is to believe in Hereafter and do good to receive mighty reward and avoid painful punishment, and to follow Qur'an that guides to most upright in all matters, including moral code that follows about parents, relatives, etc., which are most upright.

**A Lesson On Preparation For Hereafter**

Aʿtadnā, We prepared, indicates that both reward and punishment are prepared, ready, waiting, not hypothetical. Gardens prepared for righteous, Fire prepared for disbelievers, as Qur'an says. The preparation shows that Hereafter is real and near, as command at hand in previous chapter said *Command at hand, so do not hasten it* (16:1). The believer should therefore prepare for Hereafter by believing and doing good, not delay, not be hasty to pray for evil. The disbeliever who does not believe in Hereafter will find painful punishment prepared when it is too late to believe. The verse therefore calls to prepare now for Hereafter by following most upright guidance, believing in Hereafter, doing good, seeking mighty reward, avoiding painful punishment, and using night and day as signs, seeking bounty with gratitude, knowing calculation, and remembering that destiny bound to neck and record laid open will show whether believed and did good.

**Why Pairing Good News And Warning**

Pairing good news for believers who do good of mighty reward and warning for those who do not believe in Hereafter of painful punishment is rhetorical method to guide to most upright: it shows both paths, both ends, so that listener chooses better. Qur'an guides to what is most upright by showing most upright leads to mighty reward and deviation leads to painful punishment. The verse therefore is not only warning but guidance: believe in Hereafter, do good, receive mighty reward; disbelieve, painful punishment prepared. The choice is clear, and Qur'an explains everything in detail so that choice informed.

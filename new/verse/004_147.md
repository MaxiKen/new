## Sūrah al-Nisāʾ 4:147

> Why should Allah punish you if you are grateful and faithful? Allah is ever Appreciative, All-Knowing.

**Expanded Commentary**

**Why Should God Punish You If You Are Grateful and Faithful?**

The verse opens *mā yafʿalu Allāhu bi ʿadhābikum in shakartum wa āmantum* — what will God do with your punishment, why should He punish you, if you are grateful and have faith, are thankful and believing. *Mā yafʿalu bi ʿadhābikum* — what would He do with punishing you, what need for your punishment — was explained as rhetorical negation: He has no need to punish, as He is Self-Sufficient, Praiseworthy (4:131) though you disobey still to Him belongs all, and He can remove humanity and bring others (4:133) yet does not punish where gratitude and faith are. *In shakartum wa āmantum* — if you are grateful and faithful — was read with *shukr* — gratitude, thankfulness — and *īmān* — faith, belief — the two that make the four — repent, mend, hold fast, be sincere (4:146) — the gratitude and faith that replace being torn between neither (4:143) and taking allies instead of believers (4:144). *Shakartum* was explained as gratitude shown in act: charity, kindness, reconciliation seeking His pleasure (4:114), doing good male or female with faith (4:124), submitting face doing good following Abraham (4:125), standing for justice (4:135), holding fast to Him (4:175). *Wa āmantum* — and have faith — was explained as comprehensive faith in God, Messenger, Books, angels, Last Day (4:136) renewed without wavering (4:136) — steadfast versus the oscillation that increased (4:137). Early reports linked the verse to the repentant hypocrites just promised being with believers and great reward (4:146): why should He punish if they are now grateful and faithful? The sūrah has shown that whoever seeks forgiveness finds Him All-Forgiving after evil (4:110) and that He forgives what is less than association for whom He wills (4:116); gratitude and faith are the seeking made character.

**Allah Is Ever Appreciative, All-Knowing**

The seal *wa kāna Allāhu Shākiran ʿAlīmā* — And God is ever Appreciative, Thankful, Rewarding, All-Knowing — was explained with *Shākir* — Appreciative, Grateful, Repaying — the One who appreciates the grateful, rewards *shukr* with increase — *“if you are grateful I will increase you”* (14:7). *Shākir* was read as that He rewards the thankful who is grateful with great reward, as *“He will grant believers a great reward”* (4:146) and *“great is His favour upon you”* (4:113). *ʿAlīm* — All-Knowing — was explained as knowing gratitude and faith where they are hidden, as He is Fully Aware encompassing everything (4:126), All-Aware of what you do (4:128, 4:135), All-Hearing, All-Seeing (4:134) — nothing of *shukr* or *īmān* escapes. The pairing *Shākir ʿAlīm* was linked to *Shākir Ḥamīd*? Actually *Shākir ʿAlīm* fits context where *shukr* is repaid: He knows gratitude, appreciates it. The sūrah has sealed many acts with knowledge names — *ʿAlīm Ḥakīm* where sin is earned (4:111), *Muḥīṭ* where night plots (4:108); here *Shākir ʿAlīm* seals grateful faith as the act He rewards.

**A Verse That Follows Repentance With Principle**

Placed after *except those who repent, mend, hold fast, are sincere, they will be with believers and He will grant believers great reward* (4:146), this verse follows repentance with principle that explains why repentance works: why should He punish if you are grateful and faithful? The sūrah has announced painful punishment to hypocrites (4:138) and lowest depth without helper (4:145) — yet showed exception; the principle answers implied question: does He punish because He needs to? — *mā yafʿalu* — He has no need, as He is Self-Sufficient (4:131) and suffices as Trustee (4:132). Where He can remove and bring others (4:133), He does not need to keep punishing. Where with Him are both rewards (4:134), He needs not withhold. The principle therefore transforms threat into invitation: the same God who declared *“neither by your wishes”* (4:123) and *“whoever commits evil will be rewarded accordingly”* (4:123) says His *ʿadhāb* is not sought for itself; gratitude and faith remove its cause, and being Appreciative He rewards them. The sūrah’s ethic — stand for justice though against yourselves (4:135), have faith entire (4:136) — is therefore not burden but gratitude that He appreciates.

**Grateful and Faithful as the Antidote to Hypocrisy**

The pair *shakartum wa āmantum* was explained as antidote to hypocrisy’s pair — deceiving and showing off (4:142): gratitude is sincerity in devotion where *riyāʾ* was, faith is holding fast where alliance instead of believers was. The sūrah has shown that Satan promises only delusion (4:120) — gratitude for true promise (4:122) counters delusion; that there is no good in most secret talks except charity, kindness, reconciliation (4:114) — grateful action commands those three. The verse was linked to *“if you are grateful, I will increase you; if you are ungrateful, My punishment is severe”* (14:7), showing that *shukr* increases the good while *kufr* — ingratitude — resembles the disbelief increase (4:137). The seal *Shākir ʿAlīm* therefore says: He who commanded to return trusts (4:58) will repay trust kept; He who knows what is in hearts (4:63) knows whether *shukr* was present with *shuḥḥ* (4:128) and whether *īmān* remained where *hawā* pulls (4:135).

**A Short Verse That Holds Needlessness and Appreciation Together**

Though only *“why should He punish you if you are grateful and faithful? He is ever Appreciative, All-Knowing,”* the verse holds needlessness and appreciation together. The chapter that began *“be mindful of your Lord who created you from single soul”* (4:1), showed ownership to Him of heavens and earth, sufficiency as Trustee, capability to replace (4:126–133), warned not to take allies instead of believers lest you give clear proof against yourselves (4:144), declared lowest depth without helper (4:145) yet opened except for four — now says punishment itself is not His need, gratitude and faith are what He appreciates and knows. For the weak this is the lightest of principles: where you feared rising or turning away and settlement is best (4:128), to be grateful where settlement waives and faithful where trust is held is not heavy, because the Appreciative knows it and will not punish where it is. The next passage will turn from principle to law of speech: He does not like negative thoughts voiced except by wronged, He is All-Hearing, All-Knowing (4:148).

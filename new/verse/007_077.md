## Sūrah al-Aʿrāf 7:77

> Then they killed the she-camel —defying their Lord’s command—and challenged ˹Ṣâliḥ˺, “Bring us what you threaten us with, if you are ˹truly˺ one of the messengers.”

**Expanded Commentary**

**The Act Against the Sign**

The verse states that the people hamstrung the she-camel, and the deed ends the probation on which the community had been placed. The Book reports the act in each of its accounts of Thamūd: the sūrah of the Sun states that they denied the messenger and hamstrung her, and that their Lord destroyed them for their sin and levelled the city (91:14), the sūrah of the Poets reports that they hamstrung her and became regretful (26:157), and the sūrah of the Moon states that they called their companion and he took the sword and hamstrung her (54:29). The verse in front of us supplies the sūrah's own statement of the deed.

The Book's description of the act includes the conspiracy that preceded it, and the sūrah of the Ant refers to the nine men in the city who were spreading corruption and who plotted against the messenger (27:48). The tradition reports that the community consented to the killing and that the leaders instigated it, and the Book's narratives present the deed as the act of the community rather than of an individual. The verse in front of us states the outcome of the refusal that the previous verses have recorded.

**The Insolent Defiance of the Command**

The verse describes the people as having defiantly transgressed the command of their Lord, and the phrase states the character of the act rather than its details. The Book uses the same description for the peoples that were destroyed, and the sūrah of the Winnowing Winds reports that the people of ʿĀd persisted in defying the command of their Lord and were overtaken by a blast while they looked on (51:44). The verse in front of us supplies the same statement about the people of Thamūd, and the two accounts present the defiance as the common element in the destruction of the peoples.

The Book's treatment of the command in the narratives includes its terms of the sign, and the sūrah's earlier verse stated the command to let the camel graze and the warning against harming her (7:73). The defiance described in front of us is therefore a refusal of an explicit instruction, and the Book's accounts of the communities present the destruction as the consequence of that refusal rather than of an oversight.

**The Demand for the Punishment**

The verse reports the community's demand that the messenger bring upon them what he had threatened if he was among those sent, and the challenge repeats the demand that the people of ʿĀd made of their messenger (7:70). The sūrah of the Sand Dunes reports the same challenge from the people of ʿĀd (46:22), and the sūrah of the Prophets reports the audience of the Prophet asking when the threat would come to pass (21:38). This verse supplies the third instance of the demand in the sūrah's narratives.

The demand treats the warning as a claim to be tested, and the Book's answers to the demand state that the term belongs to God. The sūrah of Yūnus reports the instruction to tell the audience to consider what would follow if the punishment came upon them by night or by day (10:50), and the verse in front of us places the demand immediately after the deed that the warning had forbidden. The sequence states that the community asked for the punishment in the act of earning it.

**The Sign and the Refusal**

The Book's accounts of Thamūd present the killing of the camel as the act that ended the term, and the sūrah of Fussilat states that the people were shown the guidance and chose blindness over it, so that the blast of a disgracing punishment seized them (41:17). The verse here states the deed and the demand in a single sentence, and the two together show the community's position at the end of its probation.

The Book's treatment of the sign as a living proof that the people destroyed appears in the narratives of each community, and the sūrah's account of the sign of Moses before Pharaoh uses the same arrangement. The present verse supplies the sūrah's shortest statement of the act, and the account that follows states what came of it.

**The Community's Last Words**

The verse's second half gives the community's last words to the messenger, and the demand for the punishment is put in the same form that the sūrah's earlier narratives used. The Book's accounts of the peoples that were destroyed end with their defiance stated rather than with any revision of their position, and the verse in front of us supplies the third community's closing speech. The account that follows records the arrival of what they asked for.

The Book's descriptions of the regrets of the peoples appear in its accounts of those who recognised their loss when it was too late, and the sūrah of the Poets states that the people of Thamūd became regretful after the act. The verse under discussion states the demand rather than the regret, and the sūrah's next verse reports what the morning brought.

**Where the Verse Sits**

The verse stands seventy-seventh in the chapter, and it follows the leaders' refusal and precedes the destruction of the community. The verse that follows reports the earthquake that seized the people and the morning on which they lay lifeless in their homes (7:78), and the messenger's words as he turned away come after that (7:79). Here the verse supplies the deed that closed the third community's term.

The reader who has seen the sign given and refused now reads of its destruction. The camel is killed, the command of the Lord is defied, and the punishment is demanded; and the sūrah's next verse states what arrived in answer to the demand.

**The Transition to the Next Verse**

The next verse states that the earthquake seized the people and that morning found them lying lifeless in their homes (7:78). The deed in front of us is followed by the seizure, and the account of Thamūd reaches its end in the manner of the two accounts before it.

The two verses together complete the third narrative of the series. The sign is destroyed, the defiance is stated, the punishment is demanded, and it arrives; and the sūrah's following verse records the messenger's farewell to the people who would not hear him.

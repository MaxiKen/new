## Sūrah al-Anʿām 6:92

> This is a blessed Book which We have revealed—confirming what came before it—so you may warn the Mother of Cities and everyone around it. Those who believe in the Hereafter ˹truly˺ believe in it and guard their prayers.

**Expanded Commentary**

**A Blessed Book Sent Down**

This is a blessed Book which We have revealed. The description of the Qur'an as blessed is the Book's own term for its benefit, and it recurs in the verses that speak of the revelation's purpose. The sūrah of the Prophets calls it a blessed reminder sent down and asks its audience whether they will deny it (21:50), and the sūrah of the Cattle itself repeats the description near its end when it says that this is a blessed Book revealed so that people might follow it and be shown mercy (6:155).

The sūrah has been arguing about revelation for several verses, and this sentence introduces the revelation itself, laying it before the audience as an object. The Book's habit in these passages is to state what the text is and then to name what it is for, and the two purposes named in this verse are the confirmation of what came before and the warning of the cities around the sanctuary.

**The Confirmation of the Earlier Scriptures**

Confirming what came before it. The Book states this function of the Qur'an in many places and attaches it to the act of revelation itself. The sūrah of the Family of Imran says that God revealed the Book in truth confirming what came before it, as He revealed the Torah and the Gospel (3:3); the sūrah of the Cow says that Gabriel brought the revelation to the Prophet's heart by God's will, confirming what came before it (2:97); and the sūrah of the Table describes the Qur'an as a confirmation of previous scriptures and a supreme authority over them (5:48).

The claim is a claim about continuity rather than about competition. The Qur'an, in the Book's own description, does not come to abolish the earlier revelation but to confirm it and to stand as the criterion for what has been preserved of it. That is the answer the sūrah gives to the accusation that the Prophet was teaching something new, and it is the answer to the audience's refusal to accept a message that arrived in their own language.

**The Mother of Cities and Those Around It**

So you may warn the Mother of Cities and everyone around it. The Book uses the same expression in the sūrah of Consultation, where it says that the Prophet is to warn the Mother of Cities and all around it about the Day of Gathering (42:7). The phrase identifies Makkah as the first city in the mission's scope, and the words that follow extend the warning outward without limit.

That expansion is worth noticing because the sūrah's audience might have taken the warning to be addressed to their city alone. The Book's statement that the message is a reminder to the whole world (6:90) has already fixed its scope, and this verse's reference to the cities around the sanctuary establishes the order in which the warning reaches them. The Book elsewhere describes the settlement at the sanctuary as established for the sake of all people, and it tells the Prophet that he was sent as a mercy to the worlds (21:107).

**The Sign of Belief in the Hereafter**

Those who believe in the Hereafter truly believe in it and guard their prayers. The verse gives the audience a test that distinguishes the two kinds of response, and it builds the test from two things the sūrah has been teaching: conviction about the coming judgment and the practice of prayer. The sūrah has already described the believers who call upon their Lord morning and evening (6:52) and it has stated that the Prophet has been commanded to establish the prayer and to be mindful of God (6:72).

The pairing appears throughout the Book. The sūrah of the Believers lists those who humble themselves in prayer among the successful and includes those who are properly observant of their prayers among the inheritors of Paradise (23:9). The sūrah of the Cow describes the Book as guidance for those who believe in the unseen, establish the prayer, and are certain of the Hereafter (2:3–4). The sūrah's description of belief in this verse uses the same two marks.

**Why the Hereafter Is the Test**

The choice of the Hereafter as the marker is not arbitrary, and the sūrah has spent many verses on the subject. Belief in the Hereafter is the conviction that requires a person to live by a standard other than his neighbours' approval, and the Makkans' resistance to the message was rooted in their assumption that death ended everything. The sūrah recorded their insistence that there is nothing beyond this worldly life and that they would never be resurrected (6:29).

A person who believes in the Hereafter has already accepted that the present arrangement of wealth and power is not the final one, and the sūrah has used that acceptance throughout as the dividing line. The Book states the same criterion in the verse about the warning given to those who fear the gathering (6:51), and it repeats it here as a description of those who will accept the Book.

**The Link to the Practising Community**

The second half of the test is conduct rather than conviction, and the Book joins the two because one without the other was not enough. A person who believed in the reckoning but neglected the prayer would have accepted a theory without taking on the practice, and the sūrah's descriptions of the two roads have consistently paired what a person holds with what he does.

The verse therefore closes the discussion of revelation with a portrait of the reader who receives it. The blessed Book has been sent down, it confirms what came before it, and it warns the city at the centre of the peninsula and everything beyond. What remains is the response, and the sūrah names the two things that will show what it is.

**The Transition Into the Next Verses**

The verses that follow turn to the arguments of those who reject the revelation, and the sūrah takes up the objection that the message contains nothing new or that it could have been produced by someone else. Having presented the Book and its scope, the sūrah now answers the objections to it.

The sūrah's movement in this passage is worth following as a whole. It named the prophets and their gifts, warned that even they would have been ruined by association with God, announced the transfer of the message to a people who would keep it, commanded the Prophet to follow the guidance of the earlier men, refused any reward for the message, described it as a reminder to all creation, and then answered the claim that revelation never occurred. The Book is now in the audience's hands, and the sūrah proceeds to answer everything they said about it.

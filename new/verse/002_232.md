## Sūrah al-Baqarah 2:232

> When you have divorced women and they have reached the end of their waiting period, do not prevent them from re-marrying their exhusbands if they come to an honourable agreement. This is enjoined on whoever has faith in Allah and the Last Day. This is purer and more dignifying for you. Allah knows and you do not know.

**Expanded Commentary**

**The Instruction Stated**

The verse forbids the community to prevent divorced women from marrying their former husbands when the two have come to an honourable agreement, and it describes the instruction as a counsel for those who believe in God and the Last Day. The sentence closes the treatment of the waiting period and the resumption of the marriage.

The Book's concern is the freedom of the parties to arrange their own affairs within the limits that the passage has set, and the sentence addresses those who stand between them. Readers are shown that the legislation protects the decision of the couple from the interference of relatives and others, and the instruction supplies the rule that the community applied.

**The Occasion Reported**

The commentators connect the sentence with a report in which a man objected to the remarriage of his sister to the husband who had divorced her, and they describe the situation that the verse addressed. The sentence removes the objection by affirming the right of the parties to reach their own agreement.

The report shows the kind of pressure that the ruling addressed, and the community's teachers noted that the sentence protects the woman's position within her family as well as within the marriage. Readers are given the account of an instruction that was given in a particular case and that applies to every similar intervention.

**The Agreement Required**

The sentence states that the permission applies when the parties have come to an honourable agreement, and the community's scholars read the phrase as the condition that governs the remarriage. The Book's practice of attaching its permissions to a standard of conduct appears throughout its legislation.

The wording gives the couple the responsibility for the arrangement while placing it within the framework the passage has described, and the community's teachers used the clause when they discussed the conduct of the families involved. The passage shows that the Book's legislation on the household protects the parties' freedom while requiring that the arrangement be conducted properly.

**The Counsel for the Believers**

The sentence states that this is a counsel for those who believe in God and the Last Day, and the community's scholars read the clause as the description of the audience that the instruction addresses. The Book's practice of connecting its rulings with the faith of the community appears throughout the sūrah.

The phrase places the matter within the obligations that the community has accepted, and the community's teachers used it when they spoke about the treatment of divorced women and of the family arrangements that follow a separation. The passage shows that the instruction is not a private opinion but a part of the guidance that the community has received.

**The Value Stated**

The sentence states that the course it recommends is purer and more dignifying for the community, and the community's teachers read the phrase as the statement of the benefit that follows the instruction. The Book's practice of naming the value of its commands appears at several points in the sūrah.

The wording places the benefit with the community rather than with one of the parties, and the reader is shown that the well-being of the whole body is served by the observance of the rule. The account describes a command whose value is stated in the text, and the sentence supplies the perspective in which the instruction is to be understood.

**The Closing Names**

The sentence closes with the statement that God knows and the community does not, and the community's scholars read the pair as the ground of the instruction that preceded it. The same closing appears at other points in the sūrah where a command runs against the community's assumptions.

The Book's use of the clause gives the reader the assurance that the command's wisdom lies beyond what the community can see, and the community's teachers used it when they explained the rules about the household. The passage closes with the instruction and with the statement about the knowledge that accompanies it, and the passage continues with the rules about the nursing of children.

**The Family and the Woman's Choice**

The report behind the sentence describes a man who refused to let his sister return to the husband who had divorced her, and the revelation removed the objection. The community's teachers read the passage as the statement that the guardianship of the family exists for the protection of the woman rather than for the authority of the guardian over her decision.

The jurists built the rules of the marriage contract upon the balance the verse states. The consent of the woman belongs to the contract, and the guardian's part in it is exercised in her interest and in the manner the law approves. When the parties have agreed on terms that are honourable, the intervention of the relatives becomes the wrong that the sentence forbids.

The clause that describes the instruction as a counsel for those who believe in God and the Last Day places the matter within the obligations the community accepted. The teachers also noted the phrase about what is purer and more dignifying, since the wording places the benefit of the observance with the whole community rather than with one of the parties alone.

**What the Verse Establishes**

The sentence establishes the right of the divorced couple to remarry, the condition of an honourable agreement, the standing of the instruction as a counsel for the believers, and the value that attaches to it. The community's teachers used the verse when they set out the law on the subject, and the reader is shown that the Book's legislation on the household protects the parties' decisions from interference while requiring that they be conducted properly.

The community's teachers used the sentence when they answered families who stood between a divorced couple, and the passage supplied the language of the family's duty as well as the woman's right. The verse also shows that the Book's legislation on the household extends to the relatives of the spouses, so that the household is treated as part of the wider community.

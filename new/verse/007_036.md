## Sūrah al-Aʿrāf 7:36

> But those who receive Our revelations with denial and arrogance will be the residents of the Fire. They will be there forever.

**Expanded Commentary**

**Those Who Deny and Those Who Disdain**

The verse names two aspects of the same condition: the denial of the revelations and the disdain with which the revelations are treated. The commentary tradition explains that the denial covers the rejection of the message of God's oneness and of the commands that the messengers brought, while the disdain covers the pride that prevents a person from affirming what he knows to be true. The two are joined in the verse because the one leads to the other, and the sūrah's own narrative of the adversary has already shown the refusal that begins with an argument about status.

The Book's descriptions of the deniers include the element of pride in several passages, and the sūrah of the Cattle states that some turn away from the signs out of arrogance as though they had not heard them. The verse in front of us states the two features together, and the following verse adds the fabricated claims that accompany them.

**The Destination of the Deniers**

The verse states that those who deny and disdain are the inhabitants of the Fire and that they will remain there. The Book states the same in several passages, and the sūrah of the Cow states that those who deny the signs are the companions of the Fire and will remain there, while the sūrah of the Table and the sūrah of the Iron repeat the statement. The description in front of us matches the Book's standard formula for the outcome.

The sūrah's arrangement places the verse after the promise to those who are mindful and mend their conduct, and the two outcomes stand in immediate contrast. The reader is given the state of the saved and the state of the condemned in a single movement, and the sūrah's later passages describe the two states in the dialogue between the people of Paradise and the people of the Fire.

**The Meaning of the Abiding**

The Book's statements about the duration of the punishment appear in its descriptions of the two abodes, and the sūrah of the Heights will state in its later verses that the people of the Fire will not enter the Garden until a camel passes through the eye of a needle. The commentary tradition discusses the duration of the punishment and the possibility of its end within the framework of the Book's statements about the divine will, and the verse in front of us states the standard formula without qualification.

The Book's descriptions of the two abodes include the permanence of the reward for the people of Paradise and the terms of the punishment for the condemned, and the sūrah's later passages give both. The verse in front of us states the assignment of the second group, and the chapter's argument about the choices that produce it is already complete.

**The Pride That Produces the Denial**

The verse's mention of disdain identifies the disposition that the Book holds responsible for the refusal of the message, and the sūrah's narratives supply the instances. The people of Noah answer their messenger with the claim that only the weak have followed him; ʿĀd relies on its strength; Thamūd trusts in its dwellings; and the notables of the audience in Makkah refuse to accept a messenger from outside their leading families. The verse in front of us states the general condition of which each of these is an instance.

The Book's treatment of pride as an obstacle to the Garden appears in the sūrah of the Heights itself, where the refusal of the adversary is described as arrogance in a place where it has no standing (7:13). This verse applies the same diagnosis to the human beings who treat the signs with contempt, and the connection between the two passages is deliberate.

**Where the Verse Sits**

The verse stands thirty-sixth in the chapter, and it follows the promise to those who respond to the messengers and precedes the account of the greatest wrongdoers. The passage that begins with the commands and the term now states the two responses and their outcomes, and the verse in front of us completes the description of the second response.

The reader has been given the provision, the limits, the term, the messengers, and the promise. The passage in front of us adds the warning that accompanies the promise, and the sūrah then turns to the details of the reckoning that follows the denial.

**The Abiding and the Promise**

The verse's phrase about abiding matches the statement in the following passage about the exclusion of the proud from the Garden, and the two together describe a condition that has no term. The reward promised to those who are mindful and mend their conduct is described in the same Book with the same language of permanence, and the sūrah's arrangement places the two destinations side by side: the Fire for those who denied the signs and treated them with contempt, and the Garden for those who accepted the revelation and acted on it. The reader is given both before the dialogue of the Fire's inhabitants begins.

**The Transition to the Next Verse**

The next verse asks who does greater wrong than those who fabricate lies against God or deny His signs, and it describes the arrival of their appointed portion, the coming of the messengers who take their souls, the failure of the beings they invoked, and their witness against themselves (7:37). The condition of the deniers in front of us is followed by the account of their end, and the details of that end are set out in the verse that follows.

The two verses together present the denial and its consequence. The verse under discussion assigns the destination, and the verse that follows describes the process by which the person arrives there, from the taking of the soul to the confession that precedes the Fire.

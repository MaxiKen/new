## Sūrah Āli ʿImrān 3:90

> Indeed, those who disbelieve after having believed then increase in disbelief, their repentance will never be accepted. It is they who are astray.

**Expanded Commentary**

**A Second Case Stated After the First**

The previous verse admitted the repentance of those who turned back and repaired the damage. This verse names a group whose repentance will not be accepted, and the difference between the two cases is stated in terms of what happens after the return.

These are people who disbelieved after having believed and then increased in their disbelief. The verse's picture is of a movement with a direction: the departure is not a single act that is then reconsidered but a course that continues. The Book states the refusal of their return in direct terms, and the sentence ends by naming them as the ones who are astray.

**"Then Increase in Disbelief"**

The clause describes an intensification rather than a repetition, and commentators explored what the increase consists of. Some explained it as persistence in the state without any return, others as the accumulation of acts that follow from the initial departure, and others as moving from a private refusal to public hostility against the message and its community.

The sūrah's earlier verses make the third reading natural, since the people described in the passage had been arguing, circulating objections, and turning others away from the way. The verse reads as the completion of that conduct: the departure has stopped being a person's own business and has become a campaign. What follows from the description is that the person is not merely in a condition he might leave; he is engaged in making the condition permanent, which is why the sentence treats the refusal of his return as the description of a settled state rather than as a rejection of an enquiry.

**When the Return Is Not Accepted**

The commentators offered a range of explanations for the statement that their repentance will not be accepted. One holds that the sentence refers to the repentance offered at the moment of death, when the Book says elsewhere that no one's return is accepted after the matter has become clear beyond the possibility of choice. Another holds that the return of such a person would not be sincere, since he has already known the truth and turned from it deliberately, and that the sentence describes what he is rather than what God does with him. A third holds that a person who returns and then leaves again has nullified the earlier return, and that the verse describes a state from which the earlier act has been emptied of its effect.

The readings are not mutually exclusive, and each is grounded in the sentence's grammar. What they agree on is that the verse does not describe a person who wants to come back and is refused for no reason. It describes the completion of a determined course, and the Book's treatment of that case is stated in the language of finality.

**The People of the Book and the Muslim Apostates**

Commentators noted that the verse's context suggests the community of this revelation, but that the description also fits the communities of the earlier scriptures who believed in the message they received and then refused the messenger who confirmed it.

The two applications converge on the same conduct. In both cases a person has held a revelation, has known its truth, and has moved from acceptance to a deepening refusal. The sūrah has been addressing exactly this possibility since its early verses, and it addresses it in the plural, as a description of groups rather than of individuals. The passage's warnings are therefore addressed to communities that carry a message, and the community now receiving the sūrah is included among them rather than exempted.

**The Ending That Names Them Astray**

The verse ends by saying that they are the ones who are astray, and the word describes a person who has left a road and does not know how to return to it.

The Book uses the language of straying for the condition in which a person wanders without a route, and it contrasts it with the guidance that consists of knowing where to go. The description is placed at the end of the verse as a summary of everything that preceded it: a person who believed, left, went further from the road, and now cannot find it. What the verse adds to the previous ones is the element of disorientation, which is expressed in the Book's own terms as the loss of the road rather than the loss of the way.

**What the Reader Is to Take From the Sequence**

The passage has now run through the departure, its consequences, the exception for those who return, and the case of those who do not. Each statement has been about a different kind of person rather than about a different verdict on the same person.

That distinction is what keeps the passage coherent. The Book's sentences about the refusal of repentance are not a rule that catches everyone who has ever left the community; they describe the end of a trajectory that the verse itself defines by the word increase. A reader who notices the definition has the key to the whole passage, and the verse that follows adds another dimension by describing what such a person would still be unable to give.

**Why the Sentence Is Addressed in the Plural**

The verse speaks about a group rather than an individual, and the plural is not incidental. The Book's descriptions of the deepening of disbelief are written for communities, since the conduct it describes — arguing against a message, drawing others away from it, and continuing in that work — is a collective activity before it is a personal one.

The same feature appears in the earlier parts of the passage. The people described had received proofs together, discussed the messenger together, and turned away together, so that the warning reaches them as a body. A person reading the verse on his own is therefore not being told that his private doubts have placed him in the category the verse names. What the sentence describes is a public course of action pursued with knowledge, and the severity of the outcome stated in the next verse corresponds to the scale of the undertaking described here.

## Sūrah al-Nisāʾ 4:67

> and We would have granted them a great reward by Our grace

**Expanded Commentary**

**A Great Reward by Our Grace**

The promise *wa idhan la ātaynāhum min ladunnā ajran ʿaẓīmā* — and We would have granted them a great reward by Our grace — continues the counterfactual: had they done what they were advised to do, it would have been better and more reassuring (4:66), and then We would have granted a great reward — *ajran ʿaẓīmā* — from *ladunnā* — from Our presence, out of grace. The sūrah has used *ajr ʿaẓīm* before for the atom’s weight multiplied — *“We will multiply it many times over and give a great reward out of grace”* (4:40) — and for those who fight whether they achieve martyrdom or victory (4:74). Here it is the reward for doing what was advised — the ordinary exhorted acts of returning trusts, judging with fairness (4:58), referring disputes (4:59), and accepting the Prophet ﷺ as judge without resistance (4:65). *Min ladunnā* was stressed as grace beyond exact recompense, the same grace that makes the great reward more than multiplication. The counterfactual shows that the reward is not distant; it would have followed the advised deeds directly — *idhan* — then — without a long delay. The sūrah therefore invites the wavering to see that the great reward is not only for the hard command to sacrifice or emigrate that few would have done (4:66), but for the advised path that was within reach and was better.

**Grace That Makes the Reward Great**

The phrase *min ladunnā* was read as marking the source that makes the reward *ʿaẓīm* — great. A gift from a great giver is great because of the giver. The sūrah has already said that God never wrongs even by an atom’s weight but multiplies good and gives a great reward out of grace (4:40), and that He is sufficient as Guardian and Helper (4:45). That sufficiency is the background to this promise: the One who is sufficient to help against traders of misguidance (4:45) is sufficient to grant. Early authorities linked *ajr ʿaẓīm* here to the Gardens promised for keeping limits (4:13), for believing and doing good with vast shade (4:57), and for obeying God and the Messenger to be in the company of the blessed (4:69). The reward is therefore not a single coin but a company and a place. The verse generalises what earlier verses specificed: the honourable entry for avoiding major sins (4:31), the Gardens with rivers for keeping limits, and the multiplication for an atom’s weight. Had they done what was advised, that entire economy of grace would have been theirs, without needing to face the harder test.

**Had They Done — The Unlived Alternative**

The *law* — had they done — frames the great reward as an unlived alternative for the hypocrites who instead sought false judges and turned away stubbornly (4:60–61). The alternative was close: come to the revelations and the Messenger (4:61), refer disputes to them (4:59), and accept judgment without *ḥaraj* (4:65). The sūrah elsewhere says *“whoever obeys Allah and the Messenger will be in the company of the blessed”* (4:69), and this counterfactual shows the company missed. The phrasing *la ātaynāhum* — We would have given them — with the emphatic *lām* and *qad* implied in *idhan*, was read as certainty, not hesitation. There was no doubt about the giver; the doubt was about the doers — *few would have obeyed* the hard command, and fewer still did the advised one. The great reward therefore hangs not on divine reluctance but on human doing. The sūrah has already said *“Allah does not wrong even by an atom”* and *“what harm if they had believed and donated from what was provided”* (4:39); the same logic operates: no harm would have come, and great reward would have, had the advised been done.

**Better, More Reassuring, Then Great Reward**

The three results form a progression: *khayr* — better — for the doer in outcome, *ashadda tathbītā* — more reassuring, firmer — for the heart in steadiness (4:66), and *ajr ʿaẓīm* — great reward — from grace for the recompense beyond. The first is comparative benefit; the second is inner firmness; the third is outer honour. The sūrah elsewhere pairs *khayr* and *aḥsanu taʾwīlā* — best and fairest in outcome — for referral (4:59), and here it pairs better and firmer with great reward. Doing what was advised therefore yields a better life where trusts are returned, a firmer heart where *ḥaraj* is removed, and a great reward where grace adds to multiplication. The progression mirrors the sūrah’s movement from creation to trust to judgment to fighting for the oppressed (4:75); each step, when done, yields immediate betterment and firmness before any final reward. The hypocrites who claimed goodwill after disaster (4:62) sought reassurance through oaths; the verse says true reassurance — *tathbīt* — comes from doing what was advised, not from swearing after.

**A Verse That Holds the Door Open**

Placed between the better and more reassuring and the guidance to the straight path (4:68), this short promise holds the door open where the previous diagnosis could have closed it. The sūrah has said only a few would have obeyed the hard command (4:66), which could be heard as despair for the many. This verse says the many were not asked to do the hard but the advised, and that advised path, had it been taken, would have brought a great reward from grace. The invitation remains for the living who were told *“if only they had come after wronging themselves seeking forgiveness and the Messenger prayed for them, they would have found God ever Accepting”* (4:64). To come now is to do what was advised now, and the reward then is still great and still from *ladunnā* — the presence that suffices. For the weak for whom burdens were lightened (4:28), the verse is the lightest of burdens: do what you were exhorted to do — return, judge fairly, refer, accept — and the great reward that elsewhere required sacrifice and emigration will be granted by grace. The sūrah therefore leaves the counterfactual not as regret but as map: the better, the firmer, and the great are still ahead for those who now do what they were advised.

## Sūrah al-Nisāʾ 4:67

> and We would have granted them a great reward by Our grace

### Expanded Commentary

**Reward is grace, not a purchase**

Had the hypocrites obeyed, Allah would have given them a great reward by His grace. The wording keeps the balance between effort and gift. Obedience matters, but it does not make God indebted to the servant. The reward is generosity from the One who enabled the act and accepted it.

**Why the promise is placed after sacrifice**

The previous verse described obedience that might cost life or homeland. This one tells the hesitant heart that what is surrendered for Allah is not lost. The world measures sacrifice by what leaves the hand; divine grace measures it by what it opens in the soul and the everlasting account.

The verse also corrects the fear that obedience will make a person poor in every sense. Sometimes a duty does bring worldly loss, and the Qur’an does not promise that every sacrifice returns as money. It promises a greater reward, which may include guidance, honour, companionship, forgiveness, and the Hereafter. The believer acts for God, not as a bargain for a predictable earthly outcome.

**Sacrifice is not wasted**

The great reward may be hidden from the world’s accounting. A person who loses status by refusing corruption may gain integrity; a family that leaves oppression may gain safety; a soldier who protects rather than plunders may gain no headline but remain honoured by God. Grace measures what publicity cannot.

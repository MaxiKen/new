## Sūrah an-Nūr 24:54

> Say, “Obey Allah and obey the Messenger. But if you turn away, then he is only responsible for his duty and you are responsible for yours. And if you obey him, you will be ˹rightly˺ guided. The Messenger’s duty is only to deliver ˹the message˺ clearly.”

**Expanded Commentary**

**The Double Injunction to Obedience: aṭīʿū Allāha wa-aṭīʿū al-rasūl**

The prophetic voice is commanded to issue an unambiguous mandate to the entire community: *qul aṭīʿū Allāha wa-aṭīʿū al-rasūl*, Say: Obey Allah and obey the Messenger. The repetition of the imperative verb *aṭīʿū* (obey!) before both divine and prophetic titles carries profound jurisprudential significance.

In Islamic legal theory (*uṣūl al-fiqh*), repeating the imperative confirms that obedience to the Prophet ﷺ is an autonomous legislative obligation, not merely a redundant reflection of general scriptural commands. The Sunnah of the Prophet clarifies the Qur'an, establishes independent legal rulings, and provides the living, binding model of how divine law is actualized in human society. Obeying God requires adhering to the eternal principles of the revealed book, while obeying the Messenger requires unyielding submission to his judicial verdicts, personal examples, and executive commands without dispute, hesitation, or intellectual reservation, affirming his divine commission.

**The Principle of Individual Accountability: ʿalayhi mā ḥummila wa-ʿalaykum mā ḥummiltum**

The verse articulates the fundamental Qur'anic doctrine of moral individuality and divided responsibility: *fa-in tawallaw fa-innamā ʿalayhi mā ḥummila wa-ʿalaykum mā ḥummiltum*. The verb *ḥummila* signifies being entrusted with a solemn, sacred burden of cosmic weight that must be accounted for on Judgment Day.

The Prophet's burden (*mā ḥummil*) is to convey the divine message faithfully, explain its wisdom, embody its virtues, and establish justice among people without fear or favor. Once he delivers the revelation clearly, his cosmic mission is completely fulfilled; he carries zero spiritual liability for the rebellion, hypocrisy, or disbelief of those who reject him. Conversely, the community's burden (*mā ḥummiltum*) is to listen with humility, submit their egos, execute divine commandments, and maintain sincere fidelity. Neither party can carry the burden of the other. The hypocrites cannot excuse their moral treason by blaming leadership, nor can the Prophet force recalcitrant hearts into righteousness against their will, establishing personal culpability.

**The Inescapable Link Between Obedience and Guidance: wa-in tuṭīʿūhu tahtadū**

The conditional sentence establishes an immutable spiritual equation: *wa-in tuṭīʿūhu tahtadū*, and if you obey him, you will be rightly guided. True guidance (*al-hidāyah*) is not an elusive, esoteric secret accessible only to monastic recluses or intellectual elites; it is within reach of every sincere seeker who yearns for truth.

It is the direct, inevitable consequence of concrete obedience to prophetic guidance in daily life. To follow the Prophet ﷺ in his character, his humility, his family relations, his commercial fairness, his judicial equity, and his devotional life is to walk in the light of divine truth. Those who seek guidance while ignoring prophetic practice are chasing optical illusions in the desert. Divine favor does not descend upon theoretical philosophers who debate virtue in the abstract, but upon humble servants who translate prophetic commands into daily practice, illuminating their personal and communal existence with sacred light that guides every decision and purifies every intention.

**The Functional Limits of Prophetic Mission: mā ʿalā al-rasūli illā al-balāgh**

The verse defines the constitutional perimeter of prophetic authority: *wa-mā ʿalā al-rasūli illā al-balāghu al-mubīn*, and naught is incumbent upon the Messenger save the clear delivery. The noun *al-balāgh* denotes conveying a message so thoroughly, lucidly, and comprehensively that it reaches the core of the recipient's consciousness.

The qualifier *al-mubīn* indicates that the proclamation is clear, transparent, and entirely devoid of obscurity. The Prophet ﷺ was sent as an inviter (*dāʿī*), a warner (*nadhīr*), and a bearer of glad tidings (*bashīr*); he was not commissioned as an authoritarian tyrant who forces internal belief upon unwilling minds (*lasta ʿalayhim bi-muṣayṭir*). By stating that the Messenger's sole duty is clear delivery, the revelation relieves the Prophet of the crushing psychological grief he felt when observing the obstinacy of the hypocrites, reassuring him that human hearts belong exclusively to God and will be judged by Him alone.

**Emancipation from Moral Coercion**

This constitutional definition of prophetic duty establishes the foundational Qur'anic principle of religious liberty and moral autonomy. God could easily have compelled every human being on earth to submit through irresistible supernatural force.

However, divine wisdom created the terrestrial world as an arena of moral trial (*al-ibtilāʾ*), which requires genuine freedom of choice. Forced obedience is spiritually meaningless; virtue possesses moral value only when an autonomous human agent chooses righteousness over temptation through their own free will and sincere reflection. By restricting the prophetic mandate to *al-balāgh al-mubīn*, the Qur'an creates a society where truth is presented with irresistible clarity, while the individual remains entirely responsible before God for whether they accept guidance or choose the dark paths of ruin, preserving moral accountability and honoring human dignity.

**The Tragedy of Voluntary Blindness**

When the message has been delivered with immaculate clarity, rejection is stripped of every plausible defense. In the early Madinan community, the citizens lived in the physical presence of the Messenger of God ﷺ.

They witnessed his breathtaking compassion, observed his miraculous integrity, heard the uncreated speech of God flowing from his lips, and watched him lead the congregational prayers day and night. For an individual in such an environment to turn away in hypocrisy (*fa-in tawallaw*) was not an intellectual disagreement; it was deliberate, suicidal blindness. Verse 54 warns that when an individual turns away from clear truth, they do not harm God, nor do they diminish the glory of His Messenger. They merely destroy their own souls, choosing eternal darkness while standing directly beneath the midday sun of prophetic revelation, sealing their own eternal loss.

## Sūrah an-Nūr 24:12

> If only the believing men and women had thought well of one another, when you heard this ˹rumour˺, and said, “This is clearly ˹an outrageous˺ slander!”

**Expanded Commentary**

**The Rebuke of Passive Credulity: lawlā**

The divine address shifts from historical narrative to direct, piercing admonition: *lawlā idh samiʿtumūhu ẓanna al-muʾminūna wa-al-muʾminātu bi-anfusihim khayrā*. In classical Arabic grammar, the particle *lawlā* followed by a past-tense verb functions as an instrument of profound reproach (*tawbīkh*), demanding to know why a mandatory ethical duty was neglected. The Qur'an rebukes the community not merely for originating false speech, but for the moral passivity that permitted scandalous rumors to circulate unchecked.

Listening to slander with receptive indulgence or polite silence is treated by divine revelation as an active moral failure. A healthy society cannot survive if its citizens operate as passive conduits for character assassination. The text demands that believers exercise critical spiritual discernment the very moment a harmful report reaches their ears. When an allegation contradicts the established character of the accused, credulity ceases to be harmless naivety; it becomes complicity in evil, facilitating the spread of falsehood across the social fabric and eroding the communal peace.

**The Organic Unity of the Community: bi-anfusihim**

The verse employs an extraordinary phrase to describe the social bond of the faithful: *ẓanna al-muʾminūna wa-al-muʾminātu bi-anfusihim khayrā*—literally, that they should think well of their own selves. Classical exegetes, including Fakhr al-Dīn al-Rāzī and al-Qurṭubī, emphasized that the Qur'an portrays the community of believers as an indivisible, living organism. When a believer considers a fellow believer, they are gazing upon an extension of their own spiritual selfhood.

This principle echoes the authentic prophetic teaching in Ṣaḥīḥ Muslim 2586: The believers in their mutual love, mercy, and compassion are like a single body; when one limb aches, the entire body responds with sleeplessness and fever. To entertain malicious rumors about a brother or sister in faith is to harbor poison against one's own flesh. True solidarity demands that an attack on the honor of another believer be felt and resisted with the exact same protective instinct one would deploy to defend one's personal reputation, shielding one's brethren as one would shield oneself against disgrace.

**The Obligation of Ḥusn al-Ẓann (Positive Presumption)**

The verse codifies the foundational ethical duty of *ḥusn al-ẓann*—assuming the best of fellow believers. In Islamic legal and moral philosophy, innocence and integrity constitute the baseline reality of every Muslim (*al-aṣl barāʾat al-dhimmah*). Suspicion without definitive proof is strictly forbidden, as emphasized in 49:12. When an outrageous accusation is leveled against individuals whose lives are characterized by piety and moral excellence, the default assumption must always favor their purity.

To discard a proven lifetime of virtue in favor of an unverified rumor is an act of intellectual and spiritual corruption. Believers are commanded to anchor their judgments in established character rather than sensational whispers. The phrase *khayrā* (good) requires that when evidence is ambiguous or absent, the mind must immediately incline toward positive interpretations, shielding the honor of upright citizens from casual defamation and refusing to entertain vile speculation about their hidden lives.

**The Courage of Immediate Verbal Denunciation**

The revelation does not permit moral resistance to remain confined to internal thoughts; it commands outward verbal resistance: *wa-qālū hādhā ifkun mubīn*. The believers were required to actively articulate their rejection, declaring aloud: This is clearly an outrageous slander! Passive internal skepticism is insufficient to dismantle social hysteria. The voice of truth must be spoken boldly in the public square.

When gossip is met with open denunciation, its momentum is shattered. Slanderers rely upon polite silence and receptive smiles to spread their poison from ear to ear. When an upright believer interrupts the conversation to name the gossip for what it is—a manifest lie—the social atmosphere changes instantly. The command to speak out establishes an ethical duty of verbal intervention, requiring every believer to act as a guardian of communal truth, refusing to allow slander to masquerade as innocent conversation or harmless curiosity.

**The Historic Example of Abū Ayyūb al-Anṣārī**

Classical histories record an inspiring historical example of this verse in action through the conduct of Abū Ayyūb al-Anṣārī and his wife, Umm Ayyūb. When the slanderous rumors regarding ʿĀʾishah began circulating through Madinah, Umm Ayyūb approached her husband and mentioned what the people were whispering in the streets.

Abū Ayyūb turned to her and asked: O Umm Ayyūb, would you ever commit such an act of indecency? She replied with horror: By God, never! Abū Ayyūb then responded: By God, ʿĀʾishah is far better than you, and Ṣafwān is far better than me! This profound moral analogy captures the exact spirit of the verse. Abū Ayyūb measured the accusation against the demonstrated righteousness of the parties, rejecting the lie instinctively through the prism of mutual faith and established honor, providing an enduring template for spiritual integrity and familial defense.

**Information Ethics in an Age of Defamation**

The guidance of this verse provides an enduring framework for information ethics in every human society. Gossip and disinformation thrive whenever consumers of information suspend critical judgment, indulge salacious curiosity, and disseminate unverified claims. By placing the primary ethical obligation upon the *hearer*—*idh samiʿtumūhu*—the Qur'an attacks the economy of slander at its point of consumption.

If audiences refuse to consume, entertain, or pass along unverified attacks on human dignity, character assassination becomes impossible. The verse demands that believers cultivate intellectual discipline and moral courage, refusing to become algorithmic amplifiers of scandal. By demanding that every rumor be met with immediate positive presumption and public refutation, the Qur'an preserves the social fabric from the corrosive forces of division, suspicion, and communal decay, building an inviolable sanctuary of mutual honor where reputations are fiercely defended.

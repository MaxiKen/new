## Sūrah al-Naḥl 16:21

> **They are dead, not alive—not even knowing when their followers will be resurrected.**

**Expanded Commentary**

**Dead, Not Living**

Amwātun ghayru aḥyā'in — they are dead, not living. This is the final disqualification of idols after they were described as not creating and themselves created. Now they are described as dead, without life. Amwāt is plural of mayyit, dead. Ghayru aḥyā' emphasizes negation: not alive at all, not in any sense. Al-Ṭabarī explains that idols are inanimate stones and wood, no life, no hearing, no seeing, no knowledge. Even if they are given forms of living beings, they lack life. The contrast with Allah is stark: Allah is al-Ḥayy, the Living, who does not die, as in 2:255: *Allah, there is no god except Him, the Living, the Sustainer* (2:255) and 25:58: *And rely upon the Living who does not die* (25:58). A living worshipper calling upon dead objects is a reversal of reality. Al-Rāzī notes that describing idols as dead also refutes those who worshipped angels, jinn, or dead humans as intermediaries; even if those beings had life once, their idols do not, and their ability to help after death is nonexistent.

**Not Knowing When They Will Be Resurrected**

Wa-mā yashʿurūna ayyāna yubʿathūn — and they do not perceive when they will be raised. Yashʿurūn from sh-ʿ-r means to perceive, sense, be aware. Ayyāna means when, at what time. Yubʿathūn means they will be resurrected, raised. There are two main interpretations reported. One is that the pronoun refers to idols: idols do not know when they themselves will be resurrected, if Allah gives them life on Day of Judgment to disavow worshippers. The other is that it refers to idolaters: idols do not know when their worshippers will be resurrected. Al-Rāzī prefers the second because idols have no knowledge at all, so saying they do not know when worshippers will be raised emphasizes their ignorance of even what concerns them most. Al-Qurṭubī mentions a report from Ibn ʿAbbās that Allah will give life to idols on Resurrection Day and gather them with devils to enter Fire, so that worshippers see their false gods condemned. In either reading, the point is ignorance: a deity that does not know when resurrection will occur cannot prepare you for it.

**Why Life And Knowledge Are Required For Divinity**

A god, to be worthy of worship, must be living, knowing, powerful. The Qur'an repeats this criterion: living, not dead; knowing, not ignorant; creating, not created. Idols fail all three. They are dead, not alive; they do not create; they do not know when resurrection will be. Allah succeeds all three: He is Living, Creator, Knower of when Hour will come, as 31:34 says: *Indeed Allah alone has knowledge of the Hour* (31:34). The verse therefore completes the demolition of shirk that began in 16:17. Verse 17 asked can Creator be equal to non-creator? Verse 20 said non-creators are themselves created. Verse 21 adds they are dead and ignorant. The progression leaves no room for idols to claim divinity. Al-Zamakhsharī notes the rhetorical force of amwātun ghayru aḥyā' — dead, not living — as a deliberate redundancy to remove any metaphorical interpretation that idols might be alive in some spiritual sense. They are dead, period, not alive in any sense.

**The Psychological Appeal Of Dead Idols**

Why would anyone worship what is dead? The sūrah hints at reasons: habit, social pressure, desire for intercession, reluctance to accept accountability. Dead idols do not command, do not forbid, do not call to justice and mercy as living revelation does. They are convenient gods because they demand nothing. A living God who knows what you conceal and reveal, who will resurrect you and ask about blessings, is more demanding. Worshipping dead idols allows a person to feel religious while continuing injustice. The verse exposes this: you worship what is dead because you do not want to face the Living who will ask you when you will be raised. The idols' ignorance mirrors worshippers' chosen ignorance about resurrection. Both do not know when it will be, but one does not know because it is dead, the other because he refuses to reflect.

**A Verse That Prepares For Tawḥīd**

After demolishing false gods, verse 22 will affirm *Your God is one God*. The sequence is logical: first show what God is not, then show what He is. This is the method of lā ilāha illā Allah: negation then affirmation. Verse 21 completes negation: idols are non-creating, created, dead, ignorant. No one who hears this can reasonably place them beside Allah. The verse also prepares for the mention of arrogance in verse 22: those who do not believe in Hereafter, their hearts deny and they are arrogant. Why arrogant? Because they prefer dead gods that do not challenge their pride over a Living God who does. The dead cannot humble the proud; the Living can. By worshipping dead, the arrogant protect their arrogance.

**Linguistic Note On Amwāt And Shuʿūr**

Amwāt is plural of mayyit or mayt, dead. Ghayru aḥyā' reinforces that they are not alive in any way, not even metaphorically. Yashʿurūn from sh-ʿ-r means to perceive, feel, sense subtly, often used for knowledge that comes without obvious signs. Ayyāna is an interrogative noun for time, when, used only for future events of great importance, like Hour. Yubʿathūn from b-ʿ-th means to be raised, sent, resurrected, same root as baʿth, resurrection. The verse's shortness — seven Arabic words — makes it a sharp, memorable condemnation that could be quoted in debate. Its rhythm — amwātun ghayru aḥyā'in wa-mā yashʿurūna ayyāna yubʿathūn — with rhyme and balance, makes it easy to memorize and recite as proof against shirk.


**A Verse That Completes The Demolition Of False Gods**

This verse completes the argument that began in 16:17 and continued through 16:20. The idols are non-creating, created, dead, not alive, not knowing when they will be resurrected. Each step adds disqualification. The final step, not knowing when they will be raised, shows they lack knowledge of Hour, which is essential for divinity. Allah alone knows Hour, as 31:34 says. The verse therefore leaves no room for idols to claim divinity. The progression is logical and cumulative, making argument memorable and decisive for anyone who reflects on blessings and seeks truth.

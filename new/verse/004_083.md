## Sūrah al-Nisāʾ 4:83

> And when they hear news of security or fear, they publicize it. Had they referred it to the Messenger or their authorities, those with sound judgment among them would have validated it. Had it not been for Allah’s grace and mercy, you would have followed Satan—except for a few.

### Expanded Commentary

**News can become a public danger**

The verse criticizes those who broadcast reports of security or fear as soon as they hear them. Information can protect a community, but unverified information can create panic, expose operations, inflame suspicion, or encourage an enemy. The command is therefore about responsible circulation, not a blanket ban on communication.

**Refer matters to qualified authority**

The Messenger and those in authority are named as people to whom such reports should be referred. Those with sound judgment can assess authenticity, context, likely consequences, and whether publication serves the public interest. Authority here is not permission for rulers to conceal every inconvenient truth; it is a reminder that expertise and accountability matter in crises.

**A modern information ethic**

Social media makes the verse immediately recognizable. Sharing an alarming headline, private allegation, or fabricated emergency may feel like vigilance while actually serving Satan’s confusion. Verify before forwarding, distinguish fact from inference, and consider who may be harmed by disclosure. Divine grace saves a community from the consequences of every rumor; gratitude requires not manufacturing new ones.

**Verify before amplifying**

The modern equivalent of spreading news of security or fear may be forwarding an unverified message to thousands. Pause, identify the source, consider the risk, and let competent people assess it. A community protects itself not only with guards but with responsible information habits.

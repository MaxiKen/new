## Sūrah al-Nisāʾ 4:83

> And when they hear news of security or fear, they publicize it. Had they referred it to the Messenger or their authorities, those with sound judgment among them would have validated it. Had it not been for Allah’s grace and mercy, you would have followed Satan—except for a few.

**Expanded Commentary**

**When They Hear News of Security or Fear, They Publicise It**

The verse says *wa idhā jāʾahum amrun min al-amni aw al-khawfi adhāʿū bihi* — and when news comes to them of security or fear, they publicise it — broadcasting it, spreading it. *Amn* — security, safety — and *khawf* — fear, threat — were explained as reports from the front, rumours of victory or defeat, or news about expeditions like those the believers were told to go forth in groups or together with precautions (4:71). *Adhāʿū bihi* — to publicise, to spread wide — was read as hurrying the news to others without verification, driven by love of being first, by anxiety, or by the laggers’ wish to justify not being present (4:72). The sūrah has shown that news in Madinah could be a trust: the community’s safety depends on whether reports are true and on who hears them when. To spread security prematurely can breed complacency; to spread fear prematurely can breed the fear of people as God should be feared (4:77) that made a group ask *why have You ordered us to fight, delay us*. The verse therefore diagnoses a habit that harms the same community that was taught to return trusts and judge with fairness (4:58) and to obey and refer disputes (4:59). News, too, is a trust to be returned to rightful handlers before it is passed on.

**Had They Referred It to the Messenger or Their Authorities**

The corrective *wa law raddūhu ilā al-rasūli wa ilā ulī al-amri minhum la ʿalimahu alladhīna yastanbiṭūnahu minhum* — had they referred it to the Messenger or their authorities, those with sound judgment among them would have validated it — was read as the sūrah’s method for reports. *Radd* — to refer, to return — matches *raddūhu ilā Allāhi wa al-rasūl* — refer disputes to God and Messenger as best outcome (4:59). *Ulī al-amr* — those in authority — here are the leaders and those with insight, the same whose obedience within obedience was commanded and whose referral is best (4:59). *Yastanbiṭūnahu* — to draw out, to validate, to extract by sound inference — was explained as the work of those with *istinbāṭ* — expertise, the ability to test a report, to know its source, and to judge its implication before publicising. The sūrah has shown that purification requires knowing what is said in prayer (4:43), that fighting requires *ḥidhr* — precaution — and that judging requires hearing and seeing (4:58). Referring news for *istinbāṭ* is that precaution for information. The hypocrites who spend the night contradicting what was said (4:81) and who do not reflect on the Qur’an (4:82) are the type who would *adhāʿū bihi* — spread without referring — because the nightly plot and the hasty broadcast share the same root: bypassing the lawful handlers.

**Those With Sound Judgment Would Have Validated It**

The phrase *alladhīna yastanbiṭūnahu minhum* was stressed as showing that not every listener can validate news; it is those among the authorities with sound inference who can. The community was therefore taught to distinguish between hearing and validating, as it distinguishes between craving another’s portion and asking God for bounty (4:32). To validate — *istinbāṭ* — includes checking the transmitter, the context, and whether publicising serves security or fear as *amr* — affair — to be managed. Early reports linked the verse to the Prophet’s handling of the expedition to the Confederates, where news of security and fear about the Banū Qurayẓah and the approaching parties needed to be managed rather than broadcast. The sūrah generalises: whether news is good — *ḥasanah from God* — or evil — from the self where hands put forward (4:79) — its handling belongs to those who can draw out its meaning. The believers who were told *“do they not reflect on the Qur’an, had it been from other than God they would have found many inconsistencies”* (4:82) are here told to reflect on news as they reflect on the Book: refer it, extract, and then speak.

**Had It Not Been for Allah’s Grace and Mercy**

The reminder *wa law lā faḍlu Allāhi ʿalaykum wa raḥmatuhu la ittabaʿtumu al-shayṭāna illā qalīlā* — had it not been for Allah’s grace and mercy, you would have followed Satan, except for a few — was read as both history and warning. *Faḍl* and *raḥmah* — grace and mercy — were explained as the sending of the Messenger to be obeyed by God’s leave (4:64), the witness brought and the Day where no statement can be hidden (4:41–42), the earth that purifies where water fails (4:43), and the lightening because humankind was created weak (4:28). Without that, the community that heard security or fear would have followed Satan’s weak schemes (4:76) that the lagger and the fearful already followed when they feared people as God (4:77) and said *this is from you* at evil (4:78). The phrase *illā qalīlan* — except for a few — matches *few would have obeyed* the hard command to sacrifice or leave homes (4:66) and *few believed* among those given a portion (4:55). The few represent those who, by grace, did refer, did validate, and did not publicise. The verse therefore tells the broader community that the habit of broadcasting without referral is not a small slip; without grace and mercy, it is the path by which Satan is followed.

**A Verse That Guards Information as Trust**

Placed between the call to reflect on the Qur’an’s coherence (4:82) and the command to fight where the Prophet ﷺ is accountable only for himself but must motivate believers (4:84), this verse guards information as trust. The sūrah has commanded to return trusts to rightful owners and judge with fairness as a noble exhortation (4:58); news of security or fear is a trust to be returned to the Messenger and authorities before it is judged publicly. To publicise without referral is to handle a trust as devouring by falsehood (4:29) handles wealth — quickly and without mutual consent — while to refer for *istinbāṭ* is to handle it as trade by consent. For the weak for whom burdens were lightened, the verse is lightening: you need not decide every report alone; refer it to those with sound judgment, and the validation will be drawn out. The sūrah’s ethic of *iḥsān* — kind doing — to parents, relatives, neighbours, and travellers (4:36) becomes, at the level of news, not spreading fear that harms the weak and not spreading security that lulls the guard. Had the community not been graced, the broadcast of fear would have made it fear people as God; by grace, it can refer, validate, and then, if fighting is required, go forth with precautions, as commanded, rather than as rumour drives.

## Sūrah al-Naḥl 16:39

> **˹He will do that˺ to make clear to them what they disagreed on, and for the disbelievers to know that they were liars.**

**Expanded Commentary**

**Purpose Of Resurrection Is Clarification**

Li-yubayyina lahum al-ladhī yakhtalifūna fīhi — to make clear to them what they disagreed on, what they differed about. Li- indicates purpose: He will raise dead for this purpose, to clarify. Yubayyina from b-y-n means to make clear, manifest, explain, from same root as bayyinah, clear proof, and mubīn, clear. Al-ladhī yakhtalifūna fīhi, what they differed on, disagreed about, includes tawḥīd vs shirk, resurrection vs denial, lawful vs prohibited, truth of revelation vs fables. Al-Ṭabarī explains that resurrection will make clear truth of matters they disputed in world: whether Allah is One, whether resurrection is true, whether revelation is from Allah. In world, disputes remain unresolved because deniers refuse evidence; in Hereafter, truth becomes manifest and dispute ends. The same purpose appears in 16:64: *We sent down Book to you only to make clear to them what they differed about* (16:64), showing that both revelation in world and resurrection in Hereafter serve clarification.

**For Disbelievers To Know They Were Liars**

Wa-li-yaʿlama al-ladhīna kafarū annahum kānū kādhibīn — and for disbelievers to know that they were liars. Li-yaʿlama, so that they may know, from ʿ-l-m, to know. Al-ladhīna kafarū, those who disbelieved, covered truth. Annahum kānū kādhibīn, that they were liars, those who lied. Kādhbīn from k-dh-b means liars, those who called truth lie and told falsehood. Al-Ṭabarī explains that disbelievers will know on Day that they were liars in world when they said Allah has partners, resurrection will not happen, revelation is fables, had Allah willed we would not have worshipped others. Their lies will be exposed. Al-Rāzī notes that li-yaʿlama indicates that knowledge in Hereafter will be certain, not speculative, because they will see punishment and truth with own eyes, as 32:12 says *If you could see when criminals bow heads before Lord, Our Lord, we have seen and heard, so send us back* (32:12). Knowledge that they were liars will be part of humiliation described in verse 27.

**Why Clarification Requires Resurrection**

In world, disputes about unseen matters cannot be fully resolved because unseen is not seen. People differ about whether Allah is One, whether Hour will come, whether revelation is true, and each claims to be right. Resurrection resolves by making unseen seen: Allah's oneness becomes manifest, Hour arrives, revelation's truth becomes clear. Al-Qurṭubī notes that li-yubayyina lahum indicates that resurrection is necessary for justice: without it, disputes would remain unresolved, wrongdoers would not be exposed, and truth would not be made clear to all. The sūrah's blessings catalogue also needs resurrection for full clarification: blessings were given, but were they from Allah alone or from idols? Resurrection will make clear that they were from Allah alone, and those who attributed them to idols were liars.

**The Connection To Oaths Denying Resurrection**

Verse 38 said they swore solemn oaths that Allah will not raise dead, but it is true promise. Verse 39 says He will raise them to make clear what they disagreed on and for disbelievers to know they were liars. The connection is that their oath that there is no resurrection was lie, and resurrection will expose it as lie. They swore by Allah that Allah will not raise dead; resurrection will show they swore falsely. Their lie about resurrection is central lie that includes other lies: if no resurrection, then no accountability, then shirk and prohibitions they invented have no consequence. Resurrection exposes all as lies. The verse therefore answers oath with purpose: you swore no resurrection, but resurrection will happen to show you were liars.

**Liars In What Sense**

Kādhibīn, liars, can mean they lied when they said Allah has partners, when they said revelation is fables, when they said had Allah willed we would not have worshipped others, when they said Allah will not raise dead. All are lies. Al-Zamakhsharī explains that kādhibīn here means they were liars in their claims about Allah, not merely mistaken, because evidence was available via blessings, messengers, and clear delivery, so their denial was not honest error but willful lying. The Qur'an distinguishes between those who err out of ignorance and those who lie knowingly; disbelievers in this context are described as liars because they had access to truth but rejected and misrepresented.

**Linguistic Note On Bayyana And Kādhibīn**

Li-yubayyina from b-y-n means to make clear, clarify, from same root as bayān, clarification, and mubīn, clear. Lahum, to them, for them. Al-ladhī yakhtalifūna fīhi, what they differ in, from kh-l-f, to differ, disagree, be behind. Wa-li-yaʿlama, and for them to know, from ʿ-l-m. Al-ladhīna kafarū, those who disbelieved, from k-f-r, to cover. Annahum kānū kādhibīn, that they were liars, from k-dh-b, to lie. The verse's two purposes — to make clear what they differed on, and for disbelievers to know they were liars — are complementary: clarification for all, and specific realization for disbelievers that they were lying. The verse ends without mentioning believers, but implication is that believers will also know they were truthful, as contrast. The structure — purpose of resurrection is clarification and exposure of lies — shows that resurrection is not arbitrary but necessary for truth to be made manifest and for justice to be established.


**A Lesson That Connects To Gratitude**

This verse, like all verses in Sūrat al-Niʿam, ultimately calls to gratitude. Whether it describes Gardens, death, waiting, consequences, or divine command, the purpose is to make the reader recognize blessings and respond with thanks. Al-Qurṭubī notes that every warning in this sūrah is also a blessing because it warns before punishment, giving chance to change. Every promise is also a blessing because it encourages good. The sūrah therefore weaves warning and promise, blessing and accountability, to cultivate a heart that is mindful, humble, and grateful. The reader who reflects on this verse with that mindset will find both knowledge and motivation to be among those who say goodness when asked what Lord revealed, and who are greeted with peace at death.

## Sūrah al-Aʿrāf 7:51

> those who took this faith ˹of Islam˺ as mere amusement and play and were deluded by ˹their˺ worldly life.” ˹Allah will say,˺ “Today We will ignore them just as they ignored the coming of this Day of theirs and for rejecting Our revelations.”

**Expanded Commentary**

**Religion Taken as Amusement**

The verse describes the condemned as those who took their religion as amusement and play, and the Book attaches the same description to the deniers in several passages. The account of the Cattle instructs the Prophet to leave those who take their religion as play and amusement and are deluded by the life of this world (6:70), and the sūrah of the Ornament and the sūrah of the Heights' own later verses report the same charge. The phrase in front of us describes a settled attitude toward the message rather than a moment of levity.

The Book's descriptions of the audience's response to the revelation include mockery, and the sūrah's narratives of the prophets and their peoples present the messengers being treated as a subject for entertainment. The verse in front of us states the attitude in the terms the Book uses elsewhere for the world itself, and the parallel is the point: what the audience did with the religion was what the Book says the world is.

**The Delusion of the Life of This World**

The second description is that the condemned were deluded by the life of this world, and the Book makes the same statement in its accounts of the deniers. The Cattle's account uses the phrase in the instruction about those who take their faith as play, and the sūrah of Luqmān and the sūrah of Fāṭir warn against the deception of the present life. The verse in front of us names the world as the source of the delusion, and the world's own description in the Book explains how it works.

The Spider's account states that the present life is nothing but play and amusement and that the home of the Hereafter is the real life (29:64), and the sūrah of Iron expands the list to include boasting and competition in wealth and children (57:20). The account of the Cattle states the same in the form of a comparison with the home of the Hereafter (6:32). The verse in front of us applies the description to the people who mistook the amusement for the purpose.

**The Reciprocity of Forgetting**

The verse reports the divine statement that the condemned will be ignored as they ignored the meeting of this day and rejected the revelations, and the Book sets the same correspondence between the deed and the outcome in several passages. The sūrah of Ṭā Hā states that those who neglected the revelations will be neglected on the day (20:126), the sūrah of the Prostration states that those who forgot the meeting of their Lord will be forgotten (32:14), and the sūrah of the Crouching reports the same statement in the address to the condemned (45:34).

The Book's descriptions of the reckoning include several exchanges of this kind, and the principle behind them is the correspondence between the act and its return. The sūrah of the Cow states that the deceivers are themselves deceived (2:9), the sūrah of the Table states that the recompense of an evil is an evil like it (42:40), and the sūrah of the Cow states that those who spend do so for their own souls (2:272). This verse applies the principle to the audience that treated the meeting with its Lord as a thing of no consequence.

**What the Neglect Means**

The commentary tradition discusses what it means for God to neglect the condemned, since the Book states elsewhere that God neither errs nor forgets (20:52). The readings agree that the word does not describe a failure of knowledge on the part of God, since the Book denies that. What it describes is the treatment that the condemned receive: their plea is not answered, mercy is not extended to them, and they are left in the punishment they have entered.

The sūrah of the Hypocrites uses the same language for the relation between the hypocrites and God in this world, where the hypocrites are described as having forgotten God and being forgotten in turn (9:67). The verse here applies the same measure at the reckoning, and the two passages together show that the correspondence is a feature of the Book's account of the relation between conduct and outcome rather than a single verdict on one group.

**The Two Grounds of the Sentence**

The verse names two grounds for the treatment of the condemned: the neglect of the meeting of the day and the rejection of the revelations. The Book joins the two in its warnings throughout, since the denial of the last day and the denial of the message are the two halves of the same rejection. The sūrah's opening verses have stated the reckoning in detail, and the narratives that fill the chapter show the communities refusing the message that warned them of it.

The pair of grounds matches the two subjects of the sūrah's argument. The chapter states the obligations of the religion and the account of the reckoning, and it answers the practices of the audience and their expectation of the world's permanence. The present verse states the condemnation of the group that refused both, and the passage then turns from the two companies to the Book that the audience has received.

**Where the Verse Sits**

The verse stands fifty-first in the chapter, and it closes the scene of the two companies that began with the exclusion of the proud from the Garden. The verse that follows returns to the revelation and states that a Book has been brought to the audience, explained with knowledge and given as a guide and a mercy for those who believe (7:52). The passage therefore moves from the Hereafter to the Book in the hands of its hearers.

The reader who has been shown the two destinations and the words exchanged between them is now returned to his own situation. The Book that the sūrah's opening verses described is presented again, and the sūrah states that it is in the hands of the people whose letter is being recited.

**The Transition to the Next Verse**

The next verse states that a Book has been brought to the audience, explained with knowledge, and that it is a guide and a mercy for people who believe (7:52). The description of the condemned in front of us gives way to the account of the revelation, and the sūrah returns from the Hereafter to the message that the chapter has been presenting throughout.

The two verses together state the choice and the means. The people who took the religion as amusement and rejected the revelations have been shown their end, and the Book that they rejected is named in the next verse as the guide and the mercy that the audience may still accept.

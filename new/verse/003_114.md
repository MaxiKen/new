## Sūrah Āli ʿImrān 3:114

> They believe in Allah and the Last Day, encourage good and forbid evil, and race with one another in doing good. They are ˹truly˺ among the righteous.

**Expanded Commentary**

**Four Marks of the Upright Group**

The verse continues the description of the group named in the previous verse and gives four elements: belief in God and the Last Day, encouraging what is good, forbidding what is wrong, and competing with one another in good deeds. It closes by placing them among the righteous.

The list answers the question the previous verse left open about who the upright are. They are identified by what they hold and what they do, and the two halves of the description are as general as the Book's language allows: faith in God and in the final accounting, and conduct directed at both good and evil in the world.

**Why Belief Is Paired With the Last Day**

The pairing of God and the Last Day is the Book's shortest creed and one of its most frequent formulas. When the Book wants to describe belief in a way that covers both the present and the future, it names these two.

The reason is the difference the two make to conduct. Belief in God establishes who is being obeyed; belief in the Last Day establishes that obedience has a destination and that no deed is lost. The Book uses the pair when it speaks about the reward of those who do good, stating that whoever believes in God and the Last Day and acts rightly will have his reward with his Lord, without fear or grief (2:62). A verse in the sūrah of the Table repeats the same formula in the same terms (5:69).

**Practices the Sūrah Has Already Required**

The two practices in the middle of the list are the ones the sūrah commanded the believers to maintain. It told them to have a group that calls others to good, that encourages what is right and forbids what is wrong (3:104), and later it described what this community should be in the same words (3:110).

The vocabulary is not community-specific. The Book applies it to the Muslim community as its own obligation and to the upright among the People of the Book as their description, which shows that the terms name conduct rather than membership. What the sūrah presents here is the recognition of the same conduct in another community, and the recognition is stated as a fact rather than as a concession.

**"Racing With One Another in Doing Good"**

The final element of the description is the language of competition. The Book uses the same verb for the conduct of the righteous in another passage, where it says of Zachariah's family that they used to race one another in good deeds, calling on God in hope and fear and humbling themselves before Him (21:90).

The image is deliberate: two people running toward the same goal is a picture of shared effort rather than rivalry for a prize. The Book uses it to describe a community's internal energy, and it applies it here to the group whose conduct the sūrah has been describing. What the verse adds to the qualities of the upright is motion: the good they do is not a settled possession but an activity that draws them forward.

**What "Among the Righteous" Adds**

The closing phrase places the group among the righteous, using the Book's standard word for those whose conduct is sound and whose account is settled. The phrase is a verdict, and it is given without the qualifications that accompany descriptions of the two other groups in this passage.

The word's placement at the end of the list means that everything the verse has described is gathered into a single outcome. The Book's habit is to conclude such descriptions with a statement of the person's standing, and the sentence that follows this one confirms the same standing by stating that no good they do will be denied.

**The Two Passages That Make the Same Statement**

The sūrah of the Cow and the sūrah of the Table contain statements that are close to this passage in both content and purpose, promising reward to whoever believes in God and the Last Day and does good.

The commentators discussed whether those passages refer to the People of the Book as such or to the believers among them, and they noted the same question in connection with this group of verses. The difference between the readings does not affect the instruction the passage gives: the Book sets out the marks of sound belief and sound conduct and then states their reward, and the community reading it is being taught how to weigh what it sees in others.

**The Verse and the Community That Recites It**

The verse supplies the community with a working standard rather than a slogan. It answers the question of what to look for in a person or a community whose labels do not match one's own, and it answers with belief, practice, and effort.

The sūrah's passage began with the statement that the community is the best raised for humanity, and it has spent three verses describing people outside it who are described as upright. The two facts belong together: a community that is given a rank is also given the criteria by which it must recognise good wherever it occurs, and the criteria here are the ones its own revelation has set.

**Belief, Practice, and Effort in One Sentence**

The verse covers three dimensions of religion in one list, and the sūrah's own instructions run through all three. Belief in God and the Last Day is the doctrine; encouraging good and forbidding wrong is the ethics; racing in good deeds is the effort.

What the list shows is that none of the three is sufficient alone in the Book's account. A person may hold correct beliefs and remain inactive, and the verse pairs the belief with practices that require him to intervene in the world around him. He may act without competing to excel, and the verse adds the motion of racing. The description of the upright group is thus a full picture rather than a partial one, and the passage places it in front of a community that has just been given the same instructions.

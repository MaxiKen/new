## Sūrah al-Nisāʾ 4:141

> ˹The hypocrites are˺ those who wait to see what happens to you. So if Allah grants you victory, they say ˹to you˺, “Were we not on your side?” But if the disbelievers have a share ˹of victory˺, they say ˹to them˺, “Did we not have the advantage over you, yet we protected you from the believers?” Allah will judge between ˹all of˺ you on the Day of Judgment. And Allah will never grant the disbelievers a way over the believers.

**Expanded Commentary**

**Those Who Wait to See What Happens to You**

The verse states *alladhīna yatarabbaṣūna bikum* — those who wait, watch, lie in wait in expectation regarding you. *Yatarabbaṣūna* — to wait, to watch, to await outcome — was explained as the hypocrites’ posture between the community divided into two groups about them (4:88) and the gathering of hypocrites and disbelievers together in Hell (4:140). To wait regarding you — *bikum* — was read as waiting upon believers’ affairs, battles, and turns, as the lagger waited — *la yabṭiʾannakum* — to lag behind and say if you suffer *God blessed us for not being there* and if you gain *would we had been with them* (4:72–73), and as party waiting for the Prophet’s judgment to be deceived (4:113). The waiters here are the same who took disbelievers as allies seeking honour (4:139) and sat where revelations were mocked lest they be like them (4:140); waiting is sitting’s extension into war: not sitting in mosque where signs are denied, but lying in wait between armies to see who wins. The sūrah has shown that the emigrant finds many havens by moving (4:100), that the traveller shortens and guards prayer by moving in ranks (4:101–102); the waiter moves toward neither haven nor rank but stays to see.

**If You Win They Claim — Were We Not With You?**

The first face *fa in kāna lakum fatḥun mina Allāhi qālū a lam nakun maʿakum* — so if there is for you a victory, an opening, from God, they say to you — believers — were we not with you, on your side? — was explained with *fatḥ* — victory, opening — as God’s opening, the same opening invoked for the oppressed where *“appoint for us a saviour, helper”* from grace (4:75). *A lam nakun maʿakum* — were we not with you — was read as the claim of *walāyah* — alliance — after victory, as the sūrah later says they will say *“were we not on your side?”* to share gain. The claim was linked to *“as if there had been no bond between you”* (4:73) where the lagger says after gain *would we had been there* — the waiter here says after victory *were we not there* — both seek gain without sharing faith. Early reports noted that such waiters remained in Madinah, not going forth as commanded in groups or together (4:71), yet claimed share after. The sūrah has warned *“do not take them as allies unless they emigrate”* (4:89); the waiter’s *were we not with you?* is the emigration not done yet claimed.

**If Disbelievers Have Share They Say — Did We Not Have Advantage Yet Protected You?**

The second face *wa in kāna li al-kāfirīna naṣībun qālū a lam nastahwidh ʿalaykum wa namnaʿkum mina al-muʾminīn* — but if disbelievers have a share, a portion, they say to them — did we not have the advantage, gain mastery over you, yet we protected, prevented you from the believers? — was explained with *naṣīb* — share, portion — lesser than *fatḥ* — opening — showing even a small share for disbelievers makes the waiter turn. *A lam nastahwidh ʿalaykum* — did we not gain mastery, encompass you, have advantage over you — was read as claim of power over the disbelievers: we could have delivered you to believers, we had upper hand. *Wa namnaʿkum mina al-muʾminīn* — yet we protected you from the believers, we held believers back — was read as claim of protection: by not fighting with believers, by conveying information, by saying we held them off. The two claims mirror the lagger’s double speech and the double safety seekers who wish safety from you and from their people yet plunge into *fitnah* whenever returned (4:91). The sūrah that warned *Satan promises only delusion* (4:120) here shows human promisers who promise both sides delivery.

**God Will Judge Between You on Resurrection Day**

The adjudication *fa Allāhu yaḥkumu baynakum yawma al-qiyāmati* — then God will judge, decide, between you — all of you — on the Day of Standing — was explained as the sūrah’s gathering promise: He will gather hypocrites and disbelievers together in Hell (4:140) after being gathered on the Day about which there is no doubt (4:87) and after being asked at soul-taking *what were you doing?* (4:97). *Yaḥkumu baynakum* — will judge between you — was read as between believers, hypocrites, and disbelievers, between the waiter and both winners he courted, between the *fatḥ* and *naṣīb* claims. The sūrah has said the Book was sent with truth to judge between people by what He shows (4:105), and that He is sufficient as Trustee (4:132) and Witness (4:79); on the Day the Judge is not eloquence that might sway a human court where *“some may be more eloquent and I give his brother’s right as fire”* (Bukhārī 2680), but the All-Knowing who knows what was in hearts (4:63) and who is Fully Aware of what they do (4:108). The judgment separates the waiter’s *“were we not with you?”* from the true *with-ness* — *maʿiyyah* — that is with the believers for Gardens (4:122).

**And He Will Never Grant Disbelievers a Way Over Believers**

The assurance *wa lan yajʿala Allāhu li al-kāfirīna ʿalā al-muʾminīna sabīlā* — and God will never grant, make, for disbelievers over believers a way, a path, an argument — was explained with *sabīl* — way, path, proof, dominance — the same *sabīl* the sūrah said He will guide to noble ways (4:26), the path believers follow (4:115), and the *sabili al-muʾminīn* not to be left. To grant no *sabīl* for disbelievers over believers was read as promise that final argument and final dominance are not given to disbelief over faith, as *“He will judge between you”* ensures the argument is not the waiter’s double claim. Early authorities debated worldly reading — never granting disbelievers a way over believers in proof — versus eschatological — the Day — where gathering separates; the sūrah holds both: on the Day no *sabīl* remains, and in this world where God wills to make clear and guide to noble ways (4:26) the believer’s *sabīl* is protected where he stands for justice (4:135). The promise was also linked to *all honour belongs to Him* (4:139): *sabīl* over believers as honour is not granted to disbelievers, though *naṣīb* or *fatḥ* in battle may be. For the weak this is steadying: waiting between sides promises no *sabīl* over you, as He who owns heavens and earth (4:126) grants none.

**A Verse That Follows Gathering With Waiting and Judgment**

Placed after gathering hypocrites and disbelievers together in Hell (4:140), this verse follows gathering with waiting that made gathering inevitable and with judgment that decides waiting. The hypocrites who believed then disbelieved then increased (4:137), announced as painful punishment (4:138), who chose disbelievers as allies for honour (4:139), sat where revelations were mocked (4:140), now wait between armies to claim *were we not with you?* to whichever side wins. The sūrah that warns not to deviate by following desires (4:27) and not to let desires deviate from justice (4:135) here exposes the waiter’s desire: to belong without standing, to be *qawwām* nowhere yet claim *maʿiyya* everywhere. The next verses will anatomise that waiting: they seek to deceive God but He outwits them, they stand for prayer to be seen hardly remembering (4:142), torn between neither believers nor disbelievers, whoever He leaves to stray you will find no way (4:143).

## Sūrah al-Nisāʾ 4:141

> ˹The hypocrites are˺ those who wait to see what happens to you. So if Allah grants you victory, they say ˹to you˺, “Were we not on your side?” But if the disbelievers have a share ˹of victory˺, they say ˹to them˺, “Did we not have the advantage over you, yet we protected you from the believers?” Allah will judge between ˹all of˺ you on the Day of Judgment. And Allah will never grant the disbelievers a way over the believers.

### Expanded Commentary

**The two-sided speech of opportunism**

The hypocrites wait for the outcome, then claim loyalty to whoever appears victorious. To the believers they say they were on their side; to the disbelievers they claim they protected them. Their language has no centre beyond personal survival. They treat relationships as wagers and suffering as a price others must pay.

**Judgment belongs to Allah**

The final judgment will expose both claims. A person may persuade two audiences in the moment, but cannot make contradictory loyalties true before God. The verse therefore comforts those who were betrayed by people who later claimed credit for their work and warns the opportunist that history’s temporary ambiguity is not divine ambiguity.

The statement that Allah will never grant disbelievers a way over believers is read by scholars in light of the Qur’an’s command to justice and the realities of worldly defeat. It affirms that falsehood has no legitimate final authority over faith, not that believers are guaranteed permanent political dominance regardless of their conduct. When believers betray justice, they can suffer worldly defeat; Allah’s final authority remains.

**The politics of taking credit**

Opportunists often rewrite their role after an outcome is known. Good governance keeps records, honours actual service, and does not reward contradictory claims simply because the claimant is loud. Allah’s judgment offers the final correction, but earthly institutions should still resist rewarding duplicity.

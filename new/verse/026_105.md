## Sūrah al-Shuʿarāʾ 26:105

> The people of Noah rejected the messengers

**Expanded Commentary**

**Noah's Story Opens a New Prophetic Example**

The chapter moves from Abraham's confrontation with idolatry to the people of Noah. The first statement is blunt: they rejected the messengers. The immediate story concerns Noah, yet the plural messengers gives their refusal a wider meaning. Denying a true messenger is not an isolated disagreement with one person. It is rejection of the prophetic call as a class.

The transition also shows that the problem of false worship is not confined to Abraham's people. Noah's community will face the same essential choice: accept the Lord's warning and submit, or protect inherited practice and reject the messenger who challenges it. The names and arguments may change, but the structure of refusal remains recognizable.

**The Plural Holds One Messenger Within a Universal Call**

Some interpreters understand the plural because Noah's rejection amounts to rejecting all messengers. The prophets do not bring competing religions in the sense of rival truths. They call people to acknowledge God, live under His command, and answer for their choices. A person who rejects one messenger because he dislikes the message places himself against the common foundation of revelation.

Another interpretation allows the plural to include the people's denial of the future messengers Noah announced, or to use a category for one individual. These readings explain the grammar from different angles. The strongest shared point is that Noah's mission belongs to a universal chain. His people are not merely declining one local teacher; they are refusing the authority God sends through messengers.

**Rejection Begins Before the Argument Is Finished**

The verse names the verdict before recording Noah's speech. The people are already described as rejecting. Their problem is not that they lack an opportunity to hear the message in the next lines. They have made a settled response to a messenger. The narrative will show their reasons, but the opening alerts the reader that those reasons serve a refusal already chosen.

This pattern matters because people can confuse the number of objections with the depth of their search. A person may produce many arguments while protecting a conclusion he does not want to surrender. Noah's people will raise the social status of his followers as a barrier. The verse first identifies the deeper issue: rejection of the messenger and the truth he carries.

**The People Are Named Before Their Leaders**

The text speaks of the people of Noah, not only of a ruler or priesthood. False worship can be maintained by a whole society. Leaders may shape it, but ordinary members also inherit, perform, and defend the practice. Community identity becomes part of the question because the messenger addresses a people whose shared habits have turned against revelation.

This collective description does not erase individual differences. Some may have doubted quietly, some may have followed pressure, and some may have led the opposition. God's judgment can distinguish their roles. The opening simply shows that a society can acquire a common posture toward truth, and that posture can be called rejection even when it is expressed through many voices.

**A Verified Parallel Introduces Noah's Core Call**

The parallel account in 7:59 records Noah calling his people to worship Allah because they have no other god. That cross-reference confirms that the central issue in this story is monotheism, not a private dispute about Noah's personality. The people reject the messenger because his call threatens the authority of their inherited gods and the order built around them.

This also links Noah to Abraham. Both messengers return the community to the Lord alone, but the forms of resistance differ. Abraham questions the idols' capacity; Noah addresses a whole people over time. The prophetic call remains one even when the setting and audience change.

**Rejecting One Messenger Denies the Way of Guidance**

A messenger is not sent merely to deliver information. He embodies a path of response. To reject him is to refuse the guidance, warning, and moral correction that God has made available. The people may still possess intelligence, customs, and social order, but they have cut themselves off from the revealed direction that judges those things.

This is why the plural messengers has theological weight. The denial is not corrected by saying that the people accepted some general belief in God. Prophetic faith includes accepting the messengers God sends and obeying the truth they convey. Selective recognition that leaves the present messenger rejected is another form of resistance.

**The Opening Warns Against Socially Respectable Rejection**

Noah's people will not begin by saying that they love error. They will appeal to the people who follow him and treat social rank as evidence against the message. Their refusal may look refined or practical, yet the verse calls it rejection. A polished objection can still protect pride and inherited power.

The believer should therefore examine the reason beneath a refusal. Is the message unclear, or does it threaten a preferred hierarchy? Does the objection concern truth, or the status of those who accept it? Noah's story begins by naming the posture so that the later argument cannot disguise its spiritual source.

**The Story Repeats the Prophetic Test**

Abraham's account ended with most people refusing a sign. Noah's account begins with rejection. The repeated pattern is not redundancy. It shows that revelation continues to confront communities whose social strength makes them confident. A messenger's task remains to call, warn, and stand by truth even when a people decide against him.

The opening line therefore carries both history and warning. The people of Noah rejected the messengers, but later audiences can still choose differently. They can hear the message without measuring it by ancestry, wealth, or the social position of its followers. The new story begins with rejection so that the reader can recognize the choice before repeating it.

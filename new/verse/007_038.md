## Sūrah al-Aʿrāf 7:38

> Allah will say, “Enter the Fire along with the ˹evil˺ groups of jinn and humans that preceded you.” Whenever a group enters Hell, it will curse the preceding one until they are all gathered inside, the followers will say about their leaders, “Our Lord! They have misled us, so multiply their torment in the Fire.” He will answer, “It has already been multiplied for all, but you do not know.”

**Expanded Commentary**

**The Entry Alongside the Earlier Communities**

The verse reports the command given to those who fabricated lies and denied the signs to enter the Fire together with the communities of jinn and humanity that passed away before them. The Book's descriptions of the gathering of the condemned include the pairing of human beings with the jinn who misled them, and the sūrah of the Cattle states that the jinn will be gathered with their human associates. The entry in front of us places the communities of the present among all the communities that preceded them, and the sūrah's narratives of the destroyed peoples are completed by the scene.

The command describes the company into which the condemned are gathered, and the company includes every generation that followed the same path. The Book's treatment of the last day includes the gathering of each community with its leaders and its followers, and the sūrah's earlier verses on the questioning of the recipients and the messengers have prepared the ground for the assembly in front of us.

**Each Community Cursing Its Sister**

The verse describes the entry of the communities one after another, with each community cursing the one that preceded it. The commentary tradition offers two readings of the phrase, one of which takes the communities as the successive generations of a single religious community and the other as the successive communities of misguidance as a whole. Both readings describe the same feature of the scene: the condemned turn on one another as each group enters.

The curse in front of us marks the end of the solidarity that the communities had maintained in the world. The Book's descriptions of the Hereafter include the mutual disowning of the leaders and the followers, and the sūrah of the Spider, the sūrah of the Sheba, and the sūrah of the Cow describe the allies who will become enemies on the day. The verse in front of us presents the first stage of that estrangement, and the dialogue that follows shows it in full.

**The Blaming of the Earlier by the Later**

The verse reports that when the communities have all arrived, the last of them will say of the first that they misled them and will ask that the earlier communities receive a double punishment. The arrangement reverses the sequence of the world, since the later generations had followed the earlier ones and now hold them responsible. The sūrah of the Confederates reports the same complaint in other words, where the followers say that they obeyed their leaders and the leaders led them astray (33:67).

The commentary tradition reads the statement as an attempt to shift the burden, and the answer given in the following verse closes the attempt. The Book's treatment of the excuse of following the ancestors appears throughout, and the sūrah's own passage on the practices of the forefathers has already rejected the excuse for the world. The scene in front of us shows the rejection being confirmed at the reckoning.

**The Answer: Doubled for All**

The verse reports the answer given to the request, that the punishment has been doubled for all, and the answer grants the request while denying its purpose. The leaders who misled others carry their own guilt and the guilt of having misled them, and the followers who were misled carry their own guilt for having followed. The Book's statements about the burden of the leaders appear in the sūrah of the Spider, where the leaders promise to bear the sins of the followers and are told that they bear nothing of them, and in the sūrah of the Bee, where the leaders will bear their own burdens and other burdens with them (16:25).

The phrase that the punishment is doubled for all also closes the possibility of comparison between the two groups. Neither the leaders nor the followers escape by the doubling of the other's punishment, and the sūrah's opening verses on the weighing of deeds have already stated that no soul carries another's burden. The answer in front of us applies that principle to the most persistent excuse that the condemned offer.

**The Rancour of the Fire**

The verse's scene of mutual cursing is completed by the sūrah's later statement that God has removed rancour from the hearts of the people of Paradise, and the contrast between the two communities is one of the passage's purposes. The Book's descriptions of the two abodes include greetings of peace among the saved and recriminations among the condemned, and the sūrah of the Confederates states that friends will be enemies on that day except for the mindful (43:67). The verse in front of us supplies the picture of the second community.

The Book's teaching about the social relations of the Hereafter reflects its teaching about the loyalties of the world. The communities that supported one another in their denial are separated by the outcome they share, and the verse in front of us shows the separation beginning with the first entry into the Fire.

**Where the Verse Sits**

The verse stands thirty-eighth in the chapter, and it follows the taking of the souls and the confession and precedes the answer that the first will give to the last. The passage as a whole describes the fate of the communities that refused, and the verse in front of us presents the assembly in which the recriminations take place.

The reader who has followed the sūrah's account of the messengers and the peoples now sees the peoples gathered in the Fire and quarrelling. The sūrah then provides the last word of the leaders, and the passage closes with the image of the needle's eye.

**The Transition to the Next Verse**

The next verse reports that the first will say to the last that they were no better than them and that they should taste the punishment for what they earned (7:39). The quarrel in front of us is completed by the reply of the leaders, and the two speeches together present the full exchange of the condemned.

The two verses together state the mutual recrimination of the Fire, and the sūrah then turns to the fate of those who deny the signs and treat them with contempt, whose entry into the Garden is compared with the passage of a camel through the eye of a needle.

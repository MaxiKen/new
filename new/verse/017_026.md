## Sūrah al-Isrāʾ 17:26

> **Give to close relatives their due, as well as the poor and ˹needy˺ travellers. And do not spend wastefully.**

**Expanded Commentary**

**Give To Close Relatives Their Due**

Wa-āti dhā al-qurbā ḥaqqahu — and give possessor of closeness his due, his right. Wa-āti from a-t-y, to give, bring, from ātā, to give, to bring, from same root as ītā', giving. Dhā al-qurbā from q-r-b, closeness, nearness, relatives, kinship, from qaruba, to be near, close, and qurbā, closeness, kinship, nearness. Ḥaqqahu from ḥ-q-q, due, right, truth, incumbent, from ḥaqq, right, due, truth, from ḥaqqa, to be true, incumbent. Al-Ṭabarī explains that give to close relatives their due, their right, meaning give relatives what is due to them of kindness, support, maintaining ties, financial help if needed, as part of honouring ties after honouring parents, which was previous command. Close relatives, dhū al-qurbā, includes parents, children, siblings, uncles, aunts, cousins, extended family, those near in kinship. Ḥaqq, due, indicates they have right, not merely optional charity, but right, incumbent, as in wealth there is right for asker and deprived. Al-Qurṭubī says this includes both financial due and moral due: visiting, kindness, support, maintaining kinship, ṣilat al-raḥim, which is emphasized in Islam, as severing kinship is major sin.

**Poor And Needy Travellers**

Wa-al-miskīna wa-ibna al-sabīli — and the poor and son of way, traveller, needy traveller. Wa-al-miskīna from m-s-k, poor, needy, destitute, from maskana, poverty, stillness, from miskīn, poor, one who is still due to poverty, unable to move, needy. Wa-ibna al-sabīli from b-n-y and s-b-l, son of way, traveller, wayfarer, one whose way, journey, cut off, needy traveller, from sabīl, way, path, road. Al-Ṭabarī says give to poor and needy travellers their due as well, as part of using bounty that never withheld to help those in need, deserving. Poor, miskīn, is one who is poor, needy, unable to meet needs, destitute, even if not asking. Ibn al-sabīl, son of way, is traveller cut off from wealth, stranded, even if rich at home, needy on way, traveller who lost provision, as wayfarer. Al-Rāzī says giving to relatives, poor, traveller are three categories of giving that cover kinship and general need and temporary need, comprehensive, covering near and far, permanent and temporary, showing most upright spending.

**Do Not Spend Wastefully**

Wa-lā tubadh-dhir tabdhīran — and do not spend wastefully, squander with wasteful spending. Wa-lā, and not, prohibition. Tubadh-dhir from b-dh-r, to spend wastefully, squander, scatter, waste, from badhdhara, to waste, squander, scatter, as scattering seeds wastefully. Tabdhīran, wastefully, with emphasis, wasteful spending, squandering, scattering, from same root, intensive verbal noun, mafʿūl muṭlaq for emphasis. Al-Ṭabarī explains that do not spend wastefully, squander wealth in disobedience or in unnecessary extravagance, as wasteful spending is prohibited, is spending without right, without benefit, in sin or excess. Al-Qurṭubī says tabdhīr is spending in falsehood, disobedience, sin, or in extravagance beyond need, scattering wealth without benefit, which is characteristic of mutraf, affluent elite who rebelled when commanded, as previous verse 16 said elite rebelled. The prohibition follows command to give to relatives, poor, traveller, indicating give due but do not waste beyond due, be moderate, not wasteful.

**Why Relatives, Poor, Traveller Together**

Why these three together? Because they represent most deserving of giving, most entitled: relatives have right of kinship, womb, raḥim, poor have right of need, deprivation, traveller has right of temporary need, being cut off, stranger. Giving to them is using bounty never withheld properly, as part of most upright guidance, wisdom, that Qur'an guides to. The sūrah's theme of bounty appears: bounty never withheld, some favoured over others, provision abundant or limited by will, All-Aware All-Seeing, so use bounty to give due to those deserving, not to waste. The command to give due to relatives, poor, traveller is part of moral code that is wisdom, most upright, that leads to appreciated striving and mighty reward, and avoids painful punishment and being brother to devils.

**Connection To Not Being Tight-Fisted Nor Openhanded And Kind Word**

Verse 28 will say if you must turn down because you lack means while hoping for Lord's bounty, then give kind word, gentle. Verse 29 will say do not be tight-fisted, chained hand to neck, blameworthy, nor openhanded, fully extended, depleted in poverty. Verse 26 says give to relatives their due, poor, traveller, and do not spend wastefully. The connection is that giving due is commanded, wasteful spending prohibited, and being tight-fisted also not allowed, as withholding due, and being openhanded to poverty also not allowed, as wasteful. Moderation is required: give due to deserving, do not waste, do not be tight-fisted, do not be openhanded to poverty, and if lack means, give kind word while hoping for bounty. The balance is most upright, middle path, wasat.

**Link To Wasteful Are Brothers To Devils**

Next verse says wasteful are like brothers to devils, Devil ever ungrateful to his Lord. So tabdhīr, wasteful spending, makes one brother to devils, companion of devils, because devils are ungrateful, wasteful, spend in falsehood. The prohibition of wasteful spending is serious: makes one like brother to devils, companion in ungratefulness, disobedience. Therefore give due but do not waste, be moderate, grateful, not ungrateful like Devil.

**Linguistic Note On Qurbā And Miskīn And Tabdhīr**

Āti from a-t-y, to give, from ātā. Dhā from dh-w, possessor. Qurbā from q-r-b, closeness, kinship, nearness, from qaruba. Ḥaqq from ḥ-q-q, due, right, truth. Miskīn from m-s-k, poor, from sakana, to be still, poor still, unable. Ibn from b-n-y, son. Sabīl from s-b-l, way, path. Tubadh-dhir from b-dh-r, to waste, squander, from badhdhara, to scatter wastefully. Tabdhīran, wasteful spending, from same root, intensive verbal noun for emphasis. The verse's structure — give possessor of closeness his due and poor and son of way, and do not waste wastefully — with positive command to give three categories and negative prohibition of waste with emphasis, shows balance: give due to deserving, avoid waste, moderation, most upright.

**How This Verse Teaches Giving And Moderation And Gratitude**

This verse teaches giving due to relatives, poor, traveller, and avoiding wasteful spending, with gratitude. Al-Qurṭubī says believer should give to close relatives their due, maintaining kinship, helping financially if needed, visiting, kindness, and to poor and needy travellers, using bounty never withheld, provision abundant or limited, and should not spend wastefully, squander, as wasteful are brothers to devils, ever ungrateful. The practical lesson is to use provision, whether abundant or limited, All-Aware All-Seeing distribution, to give due to deserving, not to waste in disobedience or extravagance, and to be moderate, as most upright guidance teaches, wisdom. The verse also teaches that relatives have due, right, not optional charity, so maintain ties, give due, as gratitude to Lord who gave bounty.

**A Reflection On Due And Waste And Brotherhood To Devils**

Ḥaqqahu — his due, right — indicates relatives, poor, traveller have right in your wealth, as Qur'an says in wealth there is right for asker and deprived, and as zakat, charity, due. Giving due is justice, gratitude, not favour, not waste. Tabdhīran — wastefully — indicates spending without right, without benefit, in disobedience, extravagance, which makes one brother to devils, companion, as next verse says, because Devil ever ungrateful. The believer who reflects will give due to relatives, poor, traveller as right, using bounty never withheld, provision abundant or limited with awareness, and will avoid wasteful spending that makes one brother to devils, being moderate, grateful, not ungrateful, as part of most upright guidance that leads to appreciated striving, mighty reward, and avoids painful punishment and condemnation.

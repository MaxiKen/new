## Sūrah al-Anʿām 6:151

> Say, ˹O Prophet,˺ “Come! Let me recite to you what your Lord has forbidden to you: do not associate others with Him ˹in worship˺. ˹Do not fail to˺ honour your parents. Do not kill your children for fear of poverty. We provide for you and for them. Do not come near indecencies, openly or secretly. Do not take a ˹human˺ life—made sacred by Allah—except with ˹legal˺ right. This is what He has commanded you, so perhaps you will understand.

**Expanded Commentary**

**The Recitation Begins**

Say, Come! Let me recite to you what your Lord has forbidden to you. The verse opens the sūrah's summary of the law, and its form is a recitation rather than an argument. The audience has been examined on its dietary system and its claims about God, and the sūrah now states what has actually been forbidden, beginning with the foundations and moving through the commands about family, conduct, and life.

The Book has a parallel passage in the sūrah of the Night Journey, where God lists the commands given to the children of Israel and to the Prophet's community (17:22–39). The two passages share several of the same commands, and the version in front of us orders them as the terms of the covenant that the sūrah will mention at its close.

**The Command About the Association of Partners**

Do not associate others with Him in worship. The command stands at the head of the list, and the sūrah has made the same point in every part of its argument. The association of partners with God is the offence that the chapter has described in the audience's worship of the idols, their consultation of the jinn, their attribution of children to God, and their division of the harvests. The Book's placement of the command first reflects its standing among the prohibitions.

The sūrah of the Night Journey lists the same command at the beginning of its passage, and the sūrah of the Women states that God does not forgive the association of partners with Him while He forgives what is less than that for whom He wills. The sentence in front of us states the prohibition without qualification, and the sūrah has already explained why: the association divides the authority and gratitude that belong to the Creator alone.

**The Command About Parents**

Do not fail to honour your parents. The command follows immediately after the prohibition of association, and the Book's pairing of the two appears repeatedly. The sūrah of the Night Journey gives the two commands in sequence and describes the treatment of parents in old age, telling the believer not to repel them and to address them with words of honour (17:23–24); the sūrah of Luqmān places the command about parents after the warning against association; and the sūrah of the Sand Dunes states that God has commanded people to honour their parents and describes the mother's hardship (46:15).

The Book's treatment of the obligation includes the actions and the speech by which it is fulfilled. The sūrah of the Night Journey describes the prayer for mercy upon them and the humility with which they are to be treated (17:24), and the sūrah of Luqmān states that the obligation holds even when the parents are idolaters, with the qualification that the believer does not obey them in their association of partners with God (31:15). The verse in front of us states the command in its shortest form, and the fuller passages supply its content.

**The Command About Children**

Do not kill your children for fear of poverty. We provide for you and for them. The command takes up the practice the sūrah condemned in its account of the audience's religion, where the killing of children was described as the work of the evil associates (6:137) and where those who did it were declared lost (6:140). The verse in front of us states the prohibition and gives the reason, which addresses the fear that motivated the practice.

The Book's parallel passage in the sūrah of the Night Journey gives the same command with the reason that God provides for them (17:31), and the sūrah of the Cow includes the killing of children among the offences of the children of Israel. The sentence in front of us supplies the remedy for the fear the Book identifies: the provision is guaranteed for the parents and the children together, and the practice rested on an assumption about the future that the community had no grounds for making.

**The Command About Indecency and Life**

Do not come near indecencies, openly or secretly. Do not take a human life, made sacred by Allah, except with legal right. The two commands address the conduct of the individual and the safety of the community, and the Book attaches the same prohibitions to the covenant. The sūrah of the Heights records the Prophet's declaration that his Lord has forbidden open and secret indecencies, sinfulness, unjust aggression, and the association of others with God (7:33), and the sūrah of the Night Journey gives the command against taking life with the provision about the rights of the heirs (17:33).

The Book's phrase about the sacredness of life appears in the sūrah of the Night Journey and in the sūrah of the Distinguisher, where the servants of the Most Compassionate are described as those who do not invoke any other god, do not take a life except with right, and do not commit adultery (25:68). The verse in front of us states the two prohibitions in the same order, and the exception for legal right is the same in each passage.

**The Closing Formula**

This is what He has commanded you, so perhaps you will understand. The sentence closes the first half of the recitation with the Book's statement of purpose, and the phrase perhaps you will understand marks the passage as instruction rather than as bare prohibition. The sūrah's closing verses will use the same structure with two further endings that appeal to mindfulness and to consciousness of God.

The Book's habit of closing its command passages with such a clause appears in the sūrah of the Night Journey and in the sūrah of the Cow's account of the covenant. The verse in front of us ends the first list, and the recitation continues in the verse that follows with the commands about property, honest dealing, and speech.

**The Transition to the Next Verse**

The next verse continues the recitation with the commands about the wealth of the orphan, the giving of full measure, the requirement of justice in speech, and the fulfilment of the covenant with God, and it closes with the same formula addressed to mindfulness (6:152). The two verses together form the sūrah's statement of what the covenant requires, and the verse that follows them identifies the whole as the straight path (6:153).

The passage stands at the end of the sūrah's argument, after the examination of the audience's practices and before the closing passages about the Book and the reckoning. What the recitation establishes is the content of the religion that the sūrah has been commending, and the reader who has followed the argument now has the requirements set before him in a single list.

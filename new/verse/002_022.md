## Sūrah al-Baqarah 2:22

> ˹He is the One˺ Who has made the earth a place of settlement for you and the sky a canopy; and sends down rain from the sky, causing fruits to grow as a provision for you. So do not knowingly set up equals to Allah ˹in worship˺.

**Expanded Commentary**

**From Creating You to Settling You**

The summons to worship the Lord who created you and those before you (2:21) is immediately corroborated with the world that creation made habitable: *“Who has made the earth a place of settlement for you and the sky a canopy, and sends down rain … causing fruits to grow as a provision for you”* (2:22). *Firāsh* — a place of repose, a spread, a bedding — and *bināʾ* — a canopy, a built roof — are not mere nature descriptions; they are architecture. The earth is spread as a stable floor; the sky is raised as a sheltering ceiling. Between them, water descends and fruits emerge as *rizq* — provision — the same *rizq* from which the mindful were said to spend (2:3). The verse thus moves from genealogy (created you and those before) to cosmology (settled you under a canopy) to economy (provided for you), so that the prohibition that closes the verse — *do not set up equals to Allah* — will be heard as a violation not only of theology but of habitation.

**Earth as Firāsh, Sky as Bināʾ — Imagery That Recurs**

The earth-sky pairing recurs across the Qur'an with the same architectonic memory: *“We made the earth a cradle and the sky a guarded roof”* (21:31-32), *“We spread the earth and cast mountains therein”* (50:6-7), *“He raised the sky and set the balance”* (55:7), *“Are you harder to create or is the heaven? He built it”* (79:27-31). In each case the earth is made stable and the sky is made high, so that human life can be lived between them without being crushed from above or swallowed from below. That is why *firāsh* is more than “earth”; it is earth as furnished, as made fit for reclining, for tilling (*falāḥ*, 2:5), for settlement. The hypocrite’s earlier corruption *upon the earth* (*fī al-arḍ*, 2:11) is now set against the earth’s prior furnishing. To corrupt upon the earth after it has been made a *firāsh* is to spoil a bedding that was spread for you.

**Rain and Fruits as Provision — The Same Provision to Be Spent**

*“He sends down water from the sky and brings forth thereby fruits as a provision for you”* — *fa-akhraja bihi mina al-thamarāti rizqan lakum* — names the same *rizq* that (2:3) said the mindful spend from. The hydrological cycle — sky → water → earth → fruits — is presented not as an autonomous nature but as a conducted provision: He sends down, He brings forth, as provision *for you*. The fruits (*thamarāt*) are understood in the commentary as both literal produce — the generality of what the earth grows for food — and, by extension, the symbolic sustenance of the soul, since the same word is used for the fruits of Paradise (2:25). That duality prevents the verse from being heard as mere agronomy. The earth that was made a *firāsh* yields not only calories but signs, and the sky that was made a *bināʾ* yields not only rain but reminder. To receive provision and then to direct worship elsewhere is therefore to mislocate both the source and the gratitude that source requires.

**The Greatest Sin Named in a Prophetic Dialogue**

*“So do not set up equals to Allah, while you know”* — *fa-lā tajʿalū lillāhi andādan wa-antum taʿlamūn* — seals cosmology with prohibition. The source notes the prophetic definition of the greatest sin: when asked “What is the greatest sin in the sight of God?” the Prophet replied, *“To set up an equal to Allah though He created you”* (Ṣaḥīḥ al-Bukhārī 4477; Ṣaḥīḥ Muslim 86, verified). The wording matches this verse exactly: creation (*khalaqakum*, 2:21; *khalaqakum*, in the hadith) is made the ground of the prohibition. The sin is greatest not because it is the most exotic but because it is the most ungrateful: to take the provision that was grown for you between a floored earth and a roofed sky and to attribute it to a rival. The phrase *“while you know”* (*wa-antum taʿlamūn*) sharpens the charge. Knowledge is not lacking; the maker of the *firāsh* and the sender of the rain are known, and the equals are set up *knowingly*.

**Knows — What Is Known and Denied**

*Wa-antum taʿlamūn* — “while you know” — can point to two kinds of knowledge. It can point to the immediate knowledge that Allah created and provisioned, which the previous clauses have just refreshed; and it can point to the more general knowledge, attested even by the polytheists of the Jāhiliyyah, that Allah is the Creator while lesser intercessors are distributaries. The Qur'an records that even those who associated others would, if asked who created the heavens and earth, answer “Allah” (29:61; 31:25). The verse therefore does not accuse the setters-up of ignorance; it accuses them of a knowing misplacement. They know who spread the earth and raised the canopy, and they know who sent the water and brought forth the fruits, and they nevertheless set up equals *in worship* (*fī al-ʿibādah*, as the translation adds). That is why the same root *ʿilm* will later be used to summon them to fear a Fire whose fuel is humans and stones (2:24): knowledge without the worship that knowledge calls for becomes the evidence for the judgment.

**Fruits as Provision for You — The “For You” That Requires a Response**

*Rizqan lakum* — “as a provision for you” — repeats the *for you* that has structured the sūrah’s economy: the earth *for you*, the sky canopy *for you*, the fruits *for you*. The repetition is not rhetoric; it is entitlement language that creates responsibility. A provision that is *for you* is not merely *from* the earth; it is *to* you, addressed, and the addressee is expected to answer. That is why the verse moves without transition from cosmology to prohibition: the earth-for-you and the sky-for-you and the fruits-for-you are so many premises whose conclusion is *so do not set up equals*. The logical particle *fa-* — “so” — makes the prohibition the inference of the premises, not an additional command. To set up equals after such a furnishing is to draw a conclusion that contradicts its premises.

**A Canopy That Will Later Weep and a Bedding That Will Shake**

The earth as *firāsh* and the sky as *bināʾ* will not remain static images. Elsewhere the earth is made to quake and the sky to pour with a different intent — the rainstorm that terrified the hypocrites (2:19-20) showed the same sky as downpour and the same earth as exposure. The sūrah holds both images together: the sky that shelters and the sky that storms are one sky, and the earth that settles and the earth that is corrupted (2:11) is one earth. The difference is not in the creation but in the relation of those beneath it to the Creator who made it canopy and bedding. For the mindful who spend from what was provided (2:3), the earth remains a place of settlement; for those who trade guidance for error (2:16) and then kindle for light that is taken (2:17), the same earth becomes the stage of darkness and the same sky the source of a rain that exposes.

**Do Not Set Up Equals — The Practical Form of “Worship Your Lord”**

Finally, the prohibition — *do not set up equals* (*andādan*) — gives the positive command *“Worship your Lord”* (2:21) its practical edge. Worship is not defined here by ritual detail but by the refusal to divide the worship that creation requires. An *andād* is not only an idol in a shrine; it is any equal in devotion, reliance, or ultimate allegiance that is set alongside the One who made the bedding and the canopy. The sūrah will shortly test that definition with the challenge to produce a sūrah like what was sent down (2:23) and with the promise of gardens whose fruits resemble but do not replicate earthly fruits (2:25). The equals are to be set up neither in worship nor in rivalry with the Book that was sent to the servant (2:23) nor in preference for a garden that will be given. The earth-for-you and the sky-for-you are therefore also the Book-for-you and the Garden-for-you, and the prohibition gathers them all: having been created and settled and provided for, *so* do not set up equals, *while you know*.

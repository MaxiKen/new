## Sūrah al-Anʿām 6:125

> Whoever Allah wills to guide, He opens their heart to Islam. But whoever He wills to leave astray, He makes their chest tight and constricted as if they were climbing up into the sky. This is how Allah dooms those who disbelieve.

**Expanded Commentary**

**The Opening of the Heart**

Whoever Allah wills to guide, He opens their heart to Islam. The verse states the arrangement by which guidance occurs, and it does so in the physical language the sūrah has used before. The heart that is opened receives what is presented to it, and the same image appeared in the previous passage about the hearts of those who disbelieve in the Hereafter being receptive to the whisperings of the devils (6:113). The two verses describe the same faculty receiving two different things.

The Book uses the opening of the heart for the effect of the message on believers in other places. The sūrah of the Troops asks whether the person whose heart God has opened to Islam, so that he walks in light from his Lord, is like the person whose heart is hardened (39:22), and the sūrah of the Cattle earlier described the light by which the believer walks among people (6:122). The verse in front of us states the divine act that produces that condition.

**The Constricted Chest**

But whoever He wills to leave astray, He makes his chest tight and constricted as if they were climbing up into the sky. The second half of the verse describes the condition of the person who is left in his error, and the image is one of breath. The comparison with the climber who cannot draw air describes the difficulty such a person experiences when the message is presented to him, and the sūrah of the Troops asks the same question from the other side, contrasting the opened heart with the hardened one.

The Book uses the image of the chest in its accounts of the Prophet's own experience as well, and the sūrah of Tā-Hā records his request that his chest be expanded for the task given to him (20:25). The verse in front of us states the same faculty in its closed condition, and the two images together give the Book's picture of the inner life as a space that can be opened or narrowed.

**The Language of the Verse and Its Source**

The verse's closing sentence states that this is how God dooms those who disbelieve. The commentators noted that the description of the constricted chest was familiar to the Arabs as an image of extreme difficulty, and that the verse is one of the clearest statements in the Book of the relationship between God's will and a person's condition. The Book elsewhere describes God as the one who guides whom He wills and leaves astray whom He wills, and it presents the two outcomes without concealing either.

The sūrah of the Troops gives the fuller statement to which this verse belongs: those whose hearts God has opened walk in light, while those whose hearts are hardened against the remembrance of God are in manifest error (39:22). The Book's language in both passages joins the inner condition to the response, and it treats the guidance as a gift rather than as a reward for the person's prior merit.

**The Two Conditions and the Sūrah's Argument**

The verse completes the answer to the demand recorded in the previous verse, in which the elite said they would not believe until they received what God's messengers received. The answer given here is that belief is a matter of the heart's condition rather than of the messenger's standing, and the persons who make the demand are described as unable to breathe in the presence of the message.

The sūrah's account of the two conditions has run from the beginning of the chapter. It described the sealing of the hearts of those who denied the signs (6:46), the turning of the hearts and eyes after the first refusal (6:110), and the receptivity of the hearts of those who denied the Hereafter to the whisperings of the devils (6:113). The verse in front of us states the principle that governs all of those passages.

**What the Verse Says About the Prophet's Task**

The verse relieves the Prophet of a burden that the sūrah has been addressing throughout the passage. He had been told to follow what was revealed, to turn away from the polytheists, and that he was neither keeper nor maintainer over the people (6:106–107); now he is told that the opening of the heart is God's act. The messenger delivers; the response is produced by a cause beyond his control.

The Book states the same arrangement in the sūrah of the Stories, where the Prophet is told that he cannot guide whom he loves but that God guides whom He wills (28:56). The two verses together define the limit of the prophetic office, and the sūrah's later instructions to the Prophet rest on that limit.

**The Transition to the Following Verse**

The next verse names the path that is described in this passage and announces that it belongs to God: this is the path of your Lord, straight, and the signs have been detailed for people who remember (6:126). The two verses belong together, since the opening of the heart and the constriction of the chest describe the two responses to the path, and the sūrah states the path's character as the standard by which the responses are measured.

The sūrah then continues with the promise of the home of peace with God as a protector, the day when the wrongdoers will be gathered to the fire, and the final passages about the jinn, the livestock, and the dietary system. The verse in front of us completes the section on guidance, and the chapter returns to the theme of the signs and their explanation for people who remember.

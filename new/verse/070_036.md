## Sūrah al-Maʿārij 70:36

> So what is the matter with the disbelievers that they rush ˹head-long˺ towards you ˹O Prophet˺,

**Expanded Commentary**

**Question as Rebuke**

Fa-mā li-lladhīna kafarū qibalaka muhṭiʿīn — "what is with those who disbelieve, rushing headlong toward you?" Mā l... questions rebuke: "what's wrong with them?" Muhṭiʿīn means rushing headlong, necks craned forward, hastening heedlessly. Such questioning exposes: behavior questioned is behavior condemned. The Qur'an interrogates to indict — questions needing no answers. Rushing toward the Prophet to mock, dispute, demand: haste misdirected. Readers should hear rebuke-questions as mirrors: behavior questioned here warns behavior everywhere. What questions expose (heedless rushing), reflection corrects. Never be mā-l'd; always reflect. Questioned rushing; condemned haste.

Occasion clarifies: disbelievers crowded around the Prophet in groups, craning necks, scoffing loudly. Rushing toward, not away — circling to mock, pressing to dispute. Such rushing inverts guidance-seeking: proximity without reverence, nearness breeding mockery. Crowds gathered for spectacle, not salvation. Verse 37 will detail flanks: right and left, grouped. Mockery mobbed: numbers emboldening disrespect. Readers should examine approaches to truth: rushing to learn or rushing to scoff? Approach slowly, reverently, openly. What mockers rushed (spectacle), seekers walk (submission). Rush never to mock; hasten only to obey. Crowds mock; individuals believe.

**Headlong Haste, Blind Direction**

Muhṭiʿ describes headlong haste: bodies lunging, direction unconsidered. Haste itself is heedlessness's gait — rushing proves unthinking. Such haste contrasts commanded hastening: "flee to Allah" (paraphrased from 51:50) — haste Godward saves, haste mockward damns. Direction defines haste's moral: same speed, opposite destinies. The disbelievers' feet were fast, their compass broken. Readers should aim haste heavenward: rush to prayers, hasten to charity, flee to Allah. What haste hurries toward decides haste's worth. Hasten Godward; never mockward. Fast feet, fixed compass. Haste: vehicle, direction driver.

Qibalaka — "toward you," facing you, in your direction. The Prophet magnetized: crowds drawn, even mockers. Truth attracts before it convinces: nearness precedes either submission or scoffing. Such magnetism testifies: falsehood ignored, truth crowded. Disbelievers could not stay away — revelation's pull dragged even despisers. Presence proved power: the Prophet unavoidable in Mecca's conscience. Readers should feel the pull presently: revelation still magnetizes. Drawn? Submit, don't scoff. What magnetized crowds (truth's force), still draws hearts. Toward him reverently; never mockingly. Magnet: truth. Response: submit.

**Questions That Expose**

"What is wrong with them, turning away from the reminder?" (paraphrased from 74:49). Qur'anic mā-questions pattern: asked of deniers, answered by destiny. Such questions expose irrationality: rushing to mock what deserves reflection. Interrogation invites self-interrogation: listeners examine themselves. The Book questions to awaken: what ails you? Why haste past mercy? Readers should self-question regularly: what rushes me past prayers? What hastens me from reflection? What exposure questions, conscience must answer. Ask self; answer honestly. Exposed questions, examined souls. Mā-l: mirror held.

Adab of approach: slowly, thoughtfully, reverently. Revelation deserves reflection (tadabbur), not rushing. Haste past verses forfeits guidance; lingering harvests. Such adab corrects muhṭiʿīn: uncrane necks, still feet, open hearts. The rushing mockers modeled approach-anti-pattern: fast feet, shut minds. Seekers invert: still bodies, open minds. Readers should ritualize reflection: read slowly, pause frequently, ponder deeply. What reflection harvests (guidance), rushing tramples. Reflect, don't rush. Stillness: understanding's posture. Approach gently; receive greatly.

**Haste Repented Slows**

Repentance slows rushers: forgiven mockers become deliberate believers. Such slowing sanctifies: measured steps replace headlong lunges. Practice deliberation: pause before speaking, ponder before posting, reflect before reacting. Slowness signals reverence: stillness before revelation. The muhṭiʿ reformed walks gently to study circles. Readers hasty by nature should cultivate brakes: counted breaths, delayed replies, pondered verses. What haste trampled (guidance), slowness harvests. Slow repentedly; arrive surely. Brakes blessed; arrival certain.

**Mockery Backfires Eternally**

Mockers get mocked: "today the believers laugh at the disbelievers" (paraphrased from 83:34). Such reversal scripts eternity's theater: scoffers become spectacle. Earthly laughter at pulpits becomes hellish weeping; believers' patience becomes paradisal amusement. Backfiring fits: ridicule returns to ridiculer. Every sneer at revelation stored its echo; audit replays all. Readers tempted to mock piety should preview reversal: today's joke, tomorrow's wail. Respect believers, honor revelation, fear echoes. What mockery sows (laughter), audit reaps (lament). Never scoff; always revere. Backfire certain: laugh now at truth, be laughed at forever.

Mockers mobbed in groups, yet numbers never confer truth. "If you obey most of those on earth, they will mislead you" (paraphrased from 6:116) — majorities mislead more often than they guide. Such arithmetic steadies minorities: truth stands unvoted, falsehood falls uncheered. Crowd psychology explains rushing mockers: individuals hesitate, mobs surge. Each scoffer borrowed courage from neighbors; alone, most would have stayed silent. Believers must resist headcounts: popularity polls don't certify creeds, and trending falsehood remains falsehood. Readers should count truth, not heads: stand with evidence against crowds. What crowds confer (confidence), truth corrects (error). Never mob; always verify. Crowded broad ways end in fire; narrow truth ends in gardens.

**Deliberation Dignifies Believers**

Deliberation dignifies: measured believers contrast rushing mockers. Such dignity shows: calm speech, pondered judgments, unhurried worship. Haste undignifies — blurted words, rash verdicts, skipped reflections. Believers cultivate brakes: pause before posting, ponder before pronouncing, pray before deciding. Deliberation particularly suits revelation: verses deserve lingering. Study circles model it — unhurried recitation, repeated reflection, questioned understanding. Readers should schedule slowness: daily pondered verses, weekly reflected sermons. Resist outrage cycles; refuse rumor chains. Verify before voicing; understand before uttering. What deliberation dignifies (responses), haste degrades (into reactions). Dignify belief with deliberation; never disgrace it with rushing. Slow saints outrun hasty mockers eternally. Eternally vindicated: patience crowned with gardens forever.

## Sūrah al-Baqarah 2:186

> When My servants ask you ˹O Prophet˺ about Me: I am truly near. I respond to one’s prayer when they call upon Me. So let them respond ˹with obedience˺ to Me and believe in Me, perhaps they will be guided ˹to the Right Way˺.

**Expanded Commentary**

**The Question About God**

The verse reports that the servants ask about God and answers them directly, stating that He is near and that He responds to the caller. The sentence is unusual in the Book for the immediacy of its answer, since the question is not repeated in detail and the reply comes in the first person.

The community's teachers noted the placement of the sentence in the middle of the section on the fast. A passage about a month of discipline is interrupted by a statement about the nearness of God, and the arrangement gives the reader the inward subject of the practice before the passage returns to its rules.

**"I Am Near"**

The sentence states that God is near, and the community's scholars explained the term as describing a nearness that requires no intermediary and no journey. The Book's use of the divine name connected with nearness appears elsewhere, and the reports describe the Prophet ﷺ teaching the companions that the One they call upon is not distant.

The statement answers the question in a way that removes the distance the question presupposes. What the reader receives is the knowledge that the relation between the servant and his Lord is immediate, and the community's teachers used the sentence when they taught about the manner and the confidence with which a person might address God.

**The Response to the Caller**

The sentence states that God responds to the one who calls upon Him, and the community's scholars explained the response in a range of ways, including the granting of what is asked, the answering of a request in a better manner, and the acceptance of an act of worship. The Book presents the response as a promise that accompanies the act of calling.

The term used for the calling covers supplication and request, and the community's teachers described it as the conversation a servant holds with his Lord. What the sentence establishes is the reciprocity of the relation, and the reports preserve the Prophet's statement that the act of calling upon God is itself a form of worship.

**The Reports About the Occasion**

The commentators report that the sentence was revealed in response to questions from the companions, and the reports describe a Bedouin asking whether his Lord was near enough to whisper to or far enough that he should shout. Other reports describe the Prophet ﷺ gently reprimanding companions who were raising their voices in prayer on a journey, telling them that they were calling upon one who is hearing and near.

The variety of the reports reflects the ordinary curiosity of a community learning how to address its Lord, and the verse's answer addresses all of the questions at once. What the reader receives is the account of how a community's practice was settled by a sentence that names the nearness of God, and the passage shows the companions asking the questions that the sūrah answers.

**The Conditions Named**

The sentence instructs the servants to respond to God and to believe in Him, and the community's scholars read the pair as the two sides of the relation the verse describes. The response is the obedience that follows the call, and the belief is the condition of the whole relation.

The Book's use of the vocabulary of response connects the instruction with the summons that the revelation makes, and the community's teachers noted that the sentence's order places the human response after the divine answer. What the reader is given is a statement about what is asked of him alongside the promise of what is given, and the sentence leaves him with both.

**The Hope at the End**

The sentence closes with the statement that the servants may be led aright, and the Book's term for direction appears throughout the sūrah. The community's scholars read the ending as the purpose of the whole sentence, and they connected it with the request for guidance that opens the Book.

What the sentence establishes is that the nearness of God and the response to prayer are means by which a person arrives at the path the Book describes. The reader is left with an instruction that is also an assurance, and the passage returns to the rules of the fast with the community's relation to its Lord newly stated.

**The Verse as the Heart of the Section**

The sentence stands within a passage of legislation and supplies the inward dimension of the practices the passage describes. The community's teachers read the placement as a reminder that the fast, the recitation, and the supplication of the month belong to a relation that the verse has named, and the reader is shown that the discipline of the body and the conversation of the heart are parts of one practice.

## Sūrah al-Baqarah 2:14

> When they meet the believers they say, “We believe.” But when alone with their evil associates they say, “We are definitely with you; we were only mocking.”

**Expanded Commentary**

**Two Rooms, Two Creeds**

The verse is staged as a diptych of rooms. In the first, the hypocrites meet the believers and produce two words — *we believe*. In the second, alone with their own company, the profession is retracted in full: *we are definitely with you; we were only mocking*. The contrast is total — same speakers, same day, opposite creeds — and the verse lets both statements stand unedited, side by side, with no narrator between them. What emerges is a definition of nifāq more precise than any label: faith that exists only in an audience. In the believers' room the creed is a ticket; in the private room the ticket is described aloud as a fraud. The sūrah has been cataloguing this group's acts — the lie, the deception, the sickness, the corruption-claim, the sneer — and now it produces the group's own transcript, the one document that cannot be reinterpreted, because both versions of it were spoken by the same mouths.

**The Occasion That Sealed the Transcript**

The commentaries preserve the scene behind the verse. A hypocrite boasted to his circle: watch how I divert these fools from you. He went to certain Companions of the Prophet ﷺ, loaded them with praise, then returned to his own companions and instructed them — when you see them, do as I do. The Companions went to the Prophet ﷺ and reported what had happened, and this verse came down, exposing the intention behind the performance (recorded by Ibn Kathīr and others). The occasion explains the verse's documentary quality: revelation functioning as evidence in a live dispute. It also fixes a permanent feature of the prophetic community — its founder was given no police of hearts, but he was given a Book that overheard the back rooms. The boast "watch how I divert these fools" is the private text of which every public creed of verse 8 was the performance, and the two were now read aloud together.

**Shayāṭīn With Human Faces**

"With their evil associates" renders *shayāṭīnihim* — their satans, plural of *shayṭān*. The plural does most of the work. The singular in the Qur'an usually names the one tempter; the plural ranges widely, and exegetes read it here as the hypocrites' leaders and confederates, their fellow travelers in disbelief, or even the inner demons each carries into the private room. The Qur'an itself licenses the human application: *"And so We have made for every prophet enemies—devilish humans and jinn—whispering to one another with elegant words of deception"* (6:112). Shaitanic function, on that verse, is not a species marker but a job description, and humans hold the post. The possessive in "their satans" adds a final stroke: every man's company of deviation is somehow his own — chosen, cultivated, and addressed with the intimacy of *we are definitely with you*.

**“We Were Only Mocking”: The Clause That Convicts**

The private confession has three movements, and the last is the fatal one. *We are definitely with you* — loyalty reassured, the emphatic particle doing double duty after a public day of apparent betrayal. Then the disclosure: *we were only mocking*. Mockery, not doubt, is what the public profession concealed. The distinction matters theologically. The doubter withholds assent pending evidence; the mocker has already seen the evidence and chosen derision. Mockery is contempt in its purest verbal form — it does not argue with faith, it performs superiority over it, and it recruits the audience into the performance. The "only" — *innamā* — minimizes the act even while confessing it, the reflex of men for whom the mockery was entertainment rather than position. The verse records that self-minimizing tone verbatim because it is diagnostic: nothing feels cheaper than a joke, and the sūrah will show what the joke costs.

**The Taxonomy's Sixth Entry**

Read inside the passage that began at verse 8, this verse adds a new specimen to the collection. The lie stated a false creed; the deception organized it into a scheme; the sickness named its interior condition; the peace-making claim showed its social rhetoric; the fools-retort exposed its contempt for believers; now the mockery reveals its emotional core — not fear of the truth but amusement at it. The progression is from act to disposition, and mockery sits closest to the root. A man who lies to the believers still takes them seriously enough to manage them; a man who mocks them has placed them below seriousness entirely. That is why the sūrah treats this verse as the turning of the passage: after it, the description stops accumulating their sayings and starts pronouncing what will be done about them. The private room has said everything; the case is complete.

**A Revelation That Abolishes the Back Room**

The verse's permanent effect is on the geography of secrecy. Before it, the hypocrites possessed what every hidden type possesses: a space where the self is unobserved and the real creed can be spoken. This verse cancels that space — not by surveillance they can see but by a text that quotes their closed-door sentences with quotation marks intact. The community that received it learned that its Scripture hears rooms. For later readers the lesson generalizes past the historical hypocrites: every person maintains a private room where the day's professions are reviewed and revised, and the verse stands as the reminder that the room is not private in the only sense that finally matters. The believers in the first room could not tell the difference between the two creeds; the difference was nonetheless written down, preserved, and recited — and the next verse answers the mockery in its own idiom.

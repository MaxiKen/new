## Sūrah al-Aʿrāf 7:45

> those who hindered ˹others˺ from Allah’s Way, strived to make it ˹appear˺ crooked, and disbelieved in the Hereafter.”

**Expanded Commentary**

**The Conduct Behind the Curse**

The verse states the conduct of those on whom the curse was pronounced, and the description has three parts: turning others from the way of God, seeking to make that way crooked, and denying the Hereafter. The Book provides a nearly identical description in the sūrah of Hūd, where the same three features are attached to those who bar people from the path of God (11:19). The verse in front of us states the offense that the previous verse's proclamation answers, and the three clauses together describe an activity rather than a single act.

The conduct is presented as a campaign rather than as a private opinion. The people described do not merely fail to follow the way; they obstruct others from following it and work to make it appear defective. The sūrah's audience in Makkah knew that campaign from its own experience, since the leading families of the city opposed the message and used their influence over the tribes, the markets, and the pilgrimage to keep people away from it.

**Turning Others From the Way of God**

The first clause describes the obstruction of others, and the Book uses the language of barring for the leaders of the opposition in several passages. The sūrah of the Family of Imran states that a group of the people of the earlier books turns the believers from the way of God and seeks to make it crooked, and the sūrah of the Bee describes the leaders of the disbelievers as those who turn people from the path of God. The verse in front of us states the same activity in the summary of the cursed.

The commentary tradition notes that the clause may describe the turning of the person himself from the path as well as the turning of others, since the Arabic construction allows both readings. The two senses are joined in practice, because the person who abandons the way of God and the person who campaigns against it belong to the same category, and the sūrah's later passages describe both the leaders who mislead and the followers who accept their direction.

**Making the Path Crooked**

The second clause describes the distortion of the way itself, and the Book uses the image of crookedness for false teaching and for the misrepresentation of the revelation. The sūrah of the Family of Imran uses the same expression of those who seek to make the path crooked, the sūrah of Hūd uses it of the people who block the path and seek to make it crooked (11:19), and the sūrah of Abraham states that those who disbelieve and bar others from the way of God have gone far astray. The image in front of us presents the way of God as straight by nature, and the obstruction as an attempt to bend it.

The commentary tradition explains the crookedness as the introduction of alterations into the religion's teaching, whether by inventing practices and attributing them to God, by misquoting the message to those who might accept it, or by presenting the path as harder and less reasonable than it is. The sūrah's audience had its own practice of attributing to God commands that He had not given, and its leaders had an interest in presenting the messenger's teaching as a disruption of the order they controlled. The clause in front of us names the practice in the terms of the road.

**The Denial of the Hereafter**

The third clause states that those who act in this way disbelieve in the Hereafter, and the Book attaches the denial of the last day to the whole conduct of the opposition. The sūrah of the Family of Imran joins the phrase to the description of those who turn from the path, and the sūrah of the Bee states that those who do not believe in the Hereafter have had their deeds made to appear fair to them. The clause in front of us closes the description with the belief that makes the rest of the conduct possible.

The Book's treatment of the denial of the Hereafter frequently connects it to the denial of accountability, and the sūrah's opening verses have already stated the terms of the reckoning in full. The person who does not expect a reckoning has no reason to release the travelers he has barred from the road, and the clause in front of us states that connection in a single phrase. The sūrah's narratives of the communities show the same combination at work, since each of the peoples that rejected its messenger also denied the raising.

**The Audience and the Accusation**

The sūrah's audience included the men who controlled access to the sanctuary and to the tribes, and the description in front of us applies to the conduct of that group. The Book's later passages in this sūrah describe those who turned people from the path of God and threatened those who believed, and the sūrah of the Cattle reports the opposition's practice of sitting on every road to warn travelers away from the messenger. The verse in front of us states the offense in the form of a general description.

The Book's treatment of the offense as a wrong against others as well as against God appears in its warnings about those who mislead, and the sūrah of the Bee states that the leaders of misguidance will bear their own burdens and the burdens of those they misled (16:25). The clause in front of us names the activity that produces that double burden, and the passage that follows places the people who performed it on the wrong side of the barrier.

**Where the Verse Sits**

The verse stands forty-fifth in the chapter, and it completes the proclamation of the previous verse by naming the conduct that earned the curse. The verses that follow describe the barrier, the heights, and the people who stand on them, and the verse in front of us supplies the description of the group that the people of the heights address from above.

The reader who has been told about the people of the Garden, their words, and the announcement of the curse now receives the account of those who are cursed. The clause about the way of God returns the sūrah to the image with which it began, since the command to follow what has been sent down and the warning against other protectors have already placed the audience on a road.

**The Transition to the Next Verse**

The next verse states that a barrier will stand between the two groups, that on the heights of the barrier there will be people who recognize both parties by their marks, and that they will call out to the people of the Garden with the greeting of peace (7:46). The description of the cursed in front of us is followed by the account of the barrier and of those who stand upon it, and the sūrah's name is taken from that place.

The two verses together complete the scene of the Hereafter that the sūrah's account of the two outcomes has been building. The two groups face each other across the barrier, the people of the heights look on both, and the greeting and the warning are given from the elevated station that stands between the two destinations.

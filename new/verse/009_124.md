## Sūrah al-Tawbah 9:124

> Whenever a sûrah is revealed, some of them ask ˹mockingly˺, “Which of you has this increased in faith?” As for the believers, it has increased them in faith and they rejoice.

**Expanded Commentary**

**The Same Revelation Reveals Different Hearts**

The verse contrasts the mockery of hypocrites with the joy of believers. Revelation does not act like a neutral object producing the same response in every person. It meets hearts that are open, resistant, proud, fearful, or sincere. The words remain true, but the listener's condition shapes whether the truth becomes guidance or an occasion for ridicule.

This contrast calls the believer to examine the heart before blaming the message. If a passage unsettles him, he asks what attachment it is exposing. Discomfort can be a doorway to repentance. The hypocrite instead turns the discomfort into a joke and gathers others around the joke so that nobody has to face what the revelation demands.

The believers' faith increases when a new sūrah is revealed. Faith is not treated as a fixed possession that cannot deepen. Understanding, certainty, love, humility, and obedience can all grow through sincere engagement with Allah's words.

Increase requires more than hearing sounds. The believer listens, reflects, asks how the command applies, and acts upon what he understands. A person may know many passages while remaining unchanged if knowledge never reaches conduct. The increase is visible when revelation produces trust, patience, worship, and moral courage.

**Rejoicing Is a Sign of Recognition**

The believers rejoice because the revelation confirms and nourishes them. Their joy is not shallow excitement. It is the relief of receiving guidance from the One who knows what the heart needs. A difficult command may still bring joy because it gives the servant a clear path out of confusion.

This joy can coexist with fear. The believer may tremble at a warning and still rejoice that Allah warned him before the consequence arrived. He may feel the burden of an obligation and rejoice that Allah entrusted him with a way to serve. Joy is love recognizing the wisdom of the One who speaks.

**Mockery Protects Resistance**

The question asked by the hypocrites is not a sincere inquiry about faith. It is a way of making obedience look foolish. Mockery creates social pressure: if one person admits that the revelation changed him, others may accuse him of weakness. The group protects its pride by laughing before the message can be considered.

The believer should be careful with humor about sacred matters. Lightness can conceal an unwillingness to submit. A person does not need to pretend that every emotional reaction is profound, but he should never use ridicule to avoid a clear moral demand. Honest questions seek understanding; mocking questions seek permission to remain unchanged.

**Purity Affects Reception**

The Quran can remove the hardened crust around a heart when it is received with humility. The believer approaches the text as a servant, not as a judge looking for material to confirm his superiority. He asks what Allah is teaching and where he must change.

This does not eliminate the need for scholarship. Careful interpretation, language, context, and reliable teaching protect the believer from making the verse say whatever he already prefers. Humility and knowledge work together. A pure heart does not reject disciplined study, and disciplined study does not excuse a proud heart.

Every newly revealed sūrah gave the early community another opportunity to grow. The believer's joy therefore includes gratitude for timely guidance. Allah did not leave the community to invent its direction under pressure. He corrected, warned, consoled, and clarified as circumstances unfolded.

The modern believer receives the completed Quran but still encounters its guidance anew. A familiar verse can increase faith when a changed life makes its meaning more immediate. The servant returns to revelation in grief, responsibility, temptation, and hope. The text has not changed; the heart has become ready to hear another layer of its address.



The verse does not present the hypocrites as victims of a message that arbitrarily harmed them. Their hearts were already inclined to resistance, and their response deepened that inclination. Believers, by contrast, welcomed guidance and became more faithful. Each response trains the next response.

The servant therefore prays for a heart that rejoices in truth even when truth corrects him. He does not demand that revelation flatter him. He asks Allah to make the Quran a source of increase, then proves the request through listening, obedience, and gratitude.

A new encounter with the Quran can also increase faith by changing how the believer sees other people. He becomes slower to mock the weak, more willing to forgive, and more alert to the consequences of his choices. If reading produces only arguments and superiority, he asks whether he has received the words or merely collected them. Rejoicing in revelation means being grateful for correction. The servant welcomes a passage that removes an illusion, even when losing the illusion is uncomfortable.

The believer can make this response practical by setting aside time for recitation, reflection, and one concrete act of obedience. A passage about charity may lead to a gift; a passage about forgiveness may lead to an apology; a warning about hypocrisy may lead to a truthful disclosure. These responses are signs that faith has increased. The heart rejoices because revelation has not remained distant words. It has entered the choices by which the servant meets Allah.

A revelation that increases faith makes the servant more teachable, more grateful, and more ready to obey. That is the joy the verse praises.

A believer may therefore read the same sūrah many times and find that a different warning or promise reaches him each time. He thanks Allah for the renewed opening and responds with a deed that confirms his joy. Revelation is living guidance for the servant who approaches it with humility.

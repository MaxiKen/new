## Sūrah al-Aʿrāf 7:64

> But they rejected him, so We saved him and those with him in the Ark, and drowned those who rejected Our signs. They were certainly a blind people.

**Expanded Commentary**

**The Denial and Its Outcome**

The verse states that the people denied the messenger, that God saved him and those who were with him in the ark, and that those who denied the signs were drowned. The account of Noah in this sūrah reaches its end in a single sentence, and the two outcomes are stated without any description of the flood itself. The sūrah of the Moon supplies the missing detail in its account of the event, describing the opening of the gates of the sky, the pouring of the water, and the carrying of the ark on the waves.

The brevity of the verse matches the sūrah's method in its punishment accounts. The narratives are told for the pattern rather than for the scenery, and the details that are supplied concern the exchange between the messenger and his people. The verse in front of us states the conclusion that the sūrah's later accounts will repeat: the messenger and his followers are saved, and the community that denied the signs is destroyed.

**The Rescue of the Messenger and His Company**

The verse states that the messenger was saved along with those who were with him, and the Book presents the rescue of the messenger as a fixed feature of these accounts. The sūrah of the Cattle asks whether any would be destroyed except the wrongdoing people if the punishment came upon them suddenly or openly (6:47), and the sūrah's narratives of ʿĀd, Thamūd, Lot, and Shuʿayb all report the salvation of the messenger and the believers. The verse in front of us supplies the first instance of the pattern in the sūrah.

The company of the saved in the ark is described in the sūrah of Hūd, which reports the entry of the messenger's family and the believers into the vessel and the loss of one of the messenger's sons, who refused to board and was drowned. The sūrah of the Prohibition names the wife of Noah among those who disbelieved, and the two passages together show that the company of the saved was defined by belief rather than by kinship. The verse in front of us states the rescue without naming those who were left out, and the other accounts supply the detail.

**The Ark in the Book's Accounts**

The Book describes the ark in several passages, and the descriptions agree that the vessel was built by the messenger on God's instruction. The sūrah of the Moon states that it was made of planks and nails (54:13), and the sūrah of the Believers reports the instruction given to the messenger to build the vessel under the divine supervision and to take aboard a pair of every kind together with his family and the believers, and it states that those against whom the word had been passed would be left behind. This verse names the vessel and its company and passes on.

The ark in front of us serves the sūrah's argument about the reality of the warning. A community that had heard the call for years and refused it was removed from the world in a single event, and the vessel that carried the believers through the water was the evidence of the messenger's knowledge of what was coming. The Book's later accounts of the punishment of the communities present the same connection between the warning and the event.

**The Blindness of the People**

The verse closes with the statement that the community was a blind people, and the description names the condition that produced the denial. The Book uses blindness for the refusal to see the signs, and the sūrah of the Cattle describes the hearts and eyes that are covered, while the sūrah of the Pilgrimage states that the blind of the heart are the ones who can see with their eyes but not with their insight. The verse here states the condition of the community that watched the water rise.

The term in front of us also completes the contrast that the sūrah's account of the two kinds of land has supplied earlier in the chapter (7:58). The good land brings forth its produce and the poor land does not, and the community that refused the messenger is described as land that could not see what it received. The sūrah's next narratives apply the same diagnosis to the peoples that followed Noah.

**The Pattern Established**

The account in front of us establishes the shape that the sūrah's later narratives repeat, and the elements of the shape are the call to worship the one God, the refusal of the notables, the warning, the messenger's patience, the rescue, and the destruction. The sūrah of Hūd presents the same sequence for several peoples, and the sūrah of the Poets repeats it with the refrain that each people rejected the messengers. The present verse supplies the closing element of the first account.

The Book's use of the repeated sequence serves the audience's own situation. The Makkan listeners were being asked to see themselves in the position of the communities that had received a messenger and refused him, and the sūrah's series of narratives presents the outcome of that position without variation. The verse under discussion states the outcome for the first of those communities, and the next account begins immediately.

**Where the Verse Sits**

The verse stands sixty-fourth in the chapter, and it closes the account of Noah that began with the call in the fifty-ninth verse. The verse that follows opens the account of ʿĀd and their messenger Hūd (7:65), and the series continues in the same form. Here the verse supplies the conclusion of the first narrative and the model for the rest.

The reader who has followed the call, the charge, the reply, and the appeal now reads the outcome. The messenger is saved with his company, the deniers are drowned, and the community is described as blind; and the sūrah's following verses present the next people and its messenger with the same call and the same warning.

**The Transition to the Next Verse**

The next verse reports that to the people of ʿĀd God sent their brother Hūd, who called them to worship God alone and asked them whether they would not fear Him (7:65). The account of Noah ends and the second of the sūrah's narratives begins, and the call that Hūd makes is the same call that Noah made.

The two verses together carry the series forward. One community has been destroyed for its denial, and the messenger of the next people is introduced with the same message; and the verses that follow record the answer of the leaders of ʿĀd and the response of the messenger to their charge.

## Sūrah al-Nisāʾ 4:31

> If you avoid the major sins forbidden to you, We will absolve you of your ˹lesser˺ misdeeds and admit you into a place of honour.

### Expanded Commentary

**Major sins are not all the same in appearance**

The promise that Allah will remove major sins if lesser sins are avoided and the person enters the great sins calls attention to moral proportion. It does not trivialize lesser wrongdoing; persistent small sins can harden the heart and become major through contempt or repetition. Nor does it tell a person that one good deed mechanically cancels any deliberate crime. It teaches that the believer should recognize levels of danger and actively avoid the acts that destroy faith, life, property, and honour.

**The principle of serious avoidance**

The hadith of the seven destructive sins includes associating partners with Allah, magic, unjust killing, usury, consuming an orphan’s wealth, fleeing lawful battle, and accusing chaste believing women. The list is not exhaustive; the Qur’an and Sunnah identify many major sins. Its value is diagnostic: each item combines grave harm with a willingness to violate a protected boundary.

**A condition, not a bargain**

Avoiding major sins is joined to faith and righteous action. It is not a contract by which a person deliberately commits minor sins while counting on automatic pardon. Sincere vigilance includes seeking forgiveness, repairing harm, and refusing to normalize what one has been warned against. Allah’s promise is an encouragement to persevere, not a licence for calculation.

**“We will admit you to an honourable entrance”**

The closing promise gives the moral life a positive destination. The believer does not merely crawl between prohibitions; he or she is led into a dignified entry—into forgiveness, safety, and nearness to God. The verse therefore combines fear of destructive conduct with hope that disciplined obedience produces a better path. Its language is especially consoling for people who have left a major sin and are learning to live faithfully afterward.

**Major sins need active avoidance**

The believer should learn the signs of a major sin, avoid environments that normalize it, and seek help before temptation becomes action. The promise of forgiveness encourages disciplined hope, while the wording “if you avoid” requires a deliberate moral strategy. Reliance on mercy is not the same as relying on repeated exposure to danger.

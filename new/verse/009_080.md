## Sūrah al-Tawbah 9:80

> ˹It does not matter˺ whether you ˹O Prophet˺ pray for them to be forgiven or not. Even if you pray for their forgiveness seventy times, Allah will never forgive them. That is because they have lost faith in Allah and His Messenger. And Allah does not guide the rebellious people.

**Expanded Commentary**

**Intercession Cannot Override Settled Rejection**

The verse tells the Prophet that prayer for forgiveness will not benefit those who have lost faith in Allah and His Messenger. This does not diminish the mercy of prayer or the honor of intercession. It establishes a limit: no human being can force forgiveness for a person who persists in settled rejection. Allah's judgment is not manipulated by the number or status of the person who asks.

The statement also distinguishes between a sinful believer who seeks forgiveness and a hypocrite who refuses faith while using its public language. A believer may be weak, fall, and return. The people described here have made rebellion their condition. The barrier is not that Allah's mercy is small. The barrier is that they refuse the faith through which mercy is sought.

**Seventy Expresses a Complete Limit**

The number seventy is often understood as a large number rather than a limit to be counted mechanically. The point is that repeated requests, even in abundance, cannot change the judgment when the underlying disbelief remains. The Prophet's compassion cannot substitute for the sinner's repentance.

This protects the believer from magical ideas about quantity. A person may repeat words of forgiveness while protecting the same contempt and betrayal. Repetition becomes meaningful when it is joined to regret, faith, and changed conduct. Allah hears every sincere request, but the request must not be used as a ritual that avoids the return the request requires.

**Prayer for Others Must Be Joined to Truth**

The Prophet's desire for forgiveness shows mercy toward people who harmed him and the community. Yet mercy does not call falsehood truth. A believer can pray that a person be guided, repent, and be forgiven while still naming the person's harm and limiting his access to trust.

This balance matters in family and public life. Some people think compassion requires pretending that a repeated abuser is safe. Others think accountability requires wishing only destruction. The verse teaches that Allah's mercy and justice are not rivals. The servant hopes for repentance, protects the vulnerable, and accepts that forgiveness belongs to Allah's wisdom rather than to human pressure.

**The Cause of Refusal Is Named**

The verse gives the reason forgiveness will not occur: they have lost faith in Allah and His Messenger. Their condition is not a mystery and not a failure of the Prophet's prayer. They chose a direction that rejects the foundation of forgiveness. Allah's judgment is therefore connected to their own refusal.

This keeps believers from blaming themselves for another person's persistent rebellion. A teacher can explain, a parent can advise, and a friend can pray, but no human heart can be forced into sincerity. The servant does what is within his ability, then leaves the unseen to Allah. If the other person returns, he welcomes the return; if the person persists, he does not follow him into rebellion.

**Guidance Is Not Given to Those Who Protect Rebellion**

The closing says Allah does not guide rebellious people. Guidance is a gift, but people can resist the paths that lead to it. A person who asks for guidance while defending every obstacle is not yet ready to be guided in the way he claims. He must loosen his attachment to pride, falsehood, and the approval of corrupt companions.

This warning should create humility in the believer. He should not assume that present faith is a personal achievement that cannot be lost. He asks Allah to keep his heart firm and treats rebellion in himself before judging it in others. Guidance grows through obedience, and rebellion grows through the repeated protection of a known wrong.

**Mercy Has a Truthful Boundary**

The verse is severe because mercy without a boundary can become a lie. The Prophet's prayer is honored, but Allah says that prayer will not alter the final judgment of those who persist in disbelief. The believer accepts this boundary without becoming hard-hearted. He continues to hope for guidance before the end, but he does not promise forgiveness on Allah's behalf.

He can say that repentance is open while life remains, that Allah loves return, and that no sinner should despair. He can also say that refusal has consequences and that no intercession replaces faith. Such speech is both merciful and truthful. The servant prays for people, teaches them, protects others from their harm, and trusts Allah's perfect knowledge of every heart.

The verse therefore teaches the servant to combine hope with submission. He may pray for forgiveness for himself and others, but he does not treat prayer as a device that cancels the need for faith. He may hope that an enemy changes before death, but he does not promise that Allah has forgiven a person who persists in rejection. He may protect people from a rebellious person while still asking Allah to guide him. Mercy is truthful when it recognizes Allah's boundary. The number in the verse expresses the abundance of the Prophet's prayer and the certainty that quantity cannot replace a heart willing to return.

The boundary also protects the meaning of repentance. If repeated prayer from another person could forgive persistent disbelief without return, repentance would become unnecessary and faith would become a public formality. Allah's mercy is wider than every sin, but it is not a permission to prefer rebellion while demanding the reward of submission. The believer prays with hope, teaches with patience, and accepts Allah's judgment when a person refuses to change. He never despairs of a living person's ability to return, yet he never promises what Allah has withheld from the heart that persists in rejection.

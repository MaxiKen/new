## Sūrah al-Shuʿarāʾ 26:62

> Moses reassured ˹them˺, “Absolutely not! My Lord is certainly with me—He will guide me.”

**Expanded Commentary**

**Moses Rejects Despair Without Denying the Army**

Moses begins with an emphatic refusal: absolutely not. He does not accept the companions' conclusion that the pursuit has ended their hope. His answer is not a claim that the Egyptians are far away or that the sea is easy to cross. He rejects the meaning they have attached to the danger. The army may be near, but it is not the lord of the situation.

This is reassurance rather than rebuke. Moses does not shame the frightened people for seeing what he also sees. He speaks into their fear with a stronger truth. The purpose of courage is not to erase facts but to place facts under God's companionship. The sea and the army remain, yet neither is allowed to define the outcome before God has finished guiding His servants.

**My Lord Makes Faith Personal and Relational**

Moses says my Lord. The phrase is personal without being private. He knows the Lord who called him, supported him before Pharaoh, and led the Israelites out at night. That relationship gives him a basis for confidence when ordinary evidence points toward defeat. He does not rely on a general idea that some power might help. He relies on the Lord who has already acted in his mission.

The possessive also helps the companions. Moses is not claiming a special deity unavailable to them. The Lord who is with him is the Lord who brought the people out and who has commanded their journey. His personal trust becomes a public reminder that divine care has not stopped at the edge of the sea. The prophet's certainty is meant to steady the community, not to glorify the prophet.

**With Me Does Not Mean Away From Them**

Moses says that his Lord is with me, yet he is answering a group. The statement does not exclude the companions from divine care. It identifies the point from which Moses speaks. As the messenger responsible for the next command, he has confidence that God will guide him. That guidance will open the way for all who follow the command.

The phrase also avoids a shallow promise of comfort. Divine companionship is not described as removal of every obstacle. Moses stands with the sea before him and the army behind him. The Lord is with him within the crisis, not only after the crisis has become safe. This makes the reassurance stronger. God's presence is proved by His ability to guide through danger, not by the absence of danger.

**Guidance Is the Answer to Being Overtaken**

The companions have concluded that they are trapped because they can see no route. Moses answers with guidance rather than with a map. He does not yet know the visible form the rescue will take, but he knows that the Lord will direct the next step. Guidance means that the people do not have to invent an escape through panic or return to Pharaoh by despair.

This is a different kind of knowledge from predicting every detail. Moses does not announce how the sea will respond or what the Egyptian host will do. He states the principle that will govern the unknown: God will guide. Trust can be firm without possessing the full plan. The servant needs enough light to obey the next command, not a complete view of the road.

**The Prophet's Confidence Is Built on Earlier Evidence**

Moses has already experienced God's help in the court. His staff became a sign, the magicians recognized the truth, and Pharaoh's threats did not cancel the mission. The sea crisis is not his first encounter with overwhelming power. His reassurance grows from a history of divine acts, even though the present danger is new.

Memory gives confidence a rational foundation. Moses is not making a brave sound to keep the crowd calm. He has reasons to trust the Lord who has led him this far. A person may still feel fear while remembering those reasons. Confidence does not require that the heart become incapable of alarm. It requires that alarm no longer dictate the conclusion.

**Absolutely Not Protects the Community From False Certainty**

The emphatic opening interrupts the companions' sentence before it becomes the group's settled belief. In a crisis, one person's fearful interpretation can spread quickly and make obedience seem foolish. Moses stops that spread. He refuses to let the language of inevitable defeat become the community's only account of reality.

His answer offers a new sentence for the group to carry: the Lord is with us through His messenger, and guidance remains possible. That sentence does not solve the physical problem by itself. It changes the posture with which the people will receive the next command. They will need courage to follow when the path opens, and courage begins by refusing despair before the path is visible.

**Guidance Will Make the Impossible Specific**

The verse ends with a promise of guidance, not with a completed rescue. That ending preserves the role of obedience. God will show what to do, and Moses will have to act on what is shown. The community is reassured before it knows the method, because faith often begins at the point where method is still hidden.

The statement therefore holds together trust and movement. Moses does not tell the people to sit and wait for an abstract miracle. He tells them that the Lord will guide him, which means a command will follow and the people must be ready to obey. Despair says the road is closed. Moses says the Guide has not ceased to lead.

The assurance is therefore not a conclusion drawn from the landscape. It is confidence in the Lord who commands the landscape.

## Sūrah al-Nisāʾ 4:77

> Have you ˹O Prophet˺ not seen those who had been told, “Do not fight! Rather, establish prayer and pay alms-tax.”? Then once the order came to fight, a group of them feared those ˹hostile˺ people as Allah should be feared—or even more. They said, “Our Lord! Why have You ordered us to fight? If only You had delayed ˹the order for˺ us for a little while!” Say, ˹O Prophet,˺ “The enjoyment of this world is so little, whereas the Hereafter is far better for those mindful ˹of Allah˺. And none of you will be wronged ˹even by the width of˺ the thread of a date stone.

**Expanded Commentary**

**From Restraint to Command**

The verse recalls *a lam tara ilā alladhīna qīla lahum kuffū aydiyakum wa aqīmū al-ṣalāta wa ātū al-zakāta* — have you not seen those who had been told, do not fight, rather establish prayer and pay alms? — a reference to the early Makkan period when fighting was withheld and the community was commanded to worship and give. Early reports place this among believers in Makkah who asked permission to fight when oppressed, were told to restrain hands and to establish prayer and zakat instead, and then, after migration when the order to fight came, a group among them faltered. The sūrah has shown a similar pattern of lightening because humans were created weak (4:28) and of making things clear before imposing; the progression — *kiffu aydiyakum* then *qitāl* — is the sūrah’s method of preparing a community. To pray and to give are themselves preparation for fighting: prayer teaches awareness before standing (4:43) and giving teaches that wealth is trust to be circulated (4:37–38). Those who learned those two when fighting was barred would later fight for the oppressed who cry for a helper (4:75) with the right cause — for God, not for *ṭāghūt* (4:76). The verse therefore recalls a stage where restraint was obedience, so that later fighting would also be obedience, not desire for gain that the laggers show (4:73).

**A Group Feared People as Allah Should Be Feared**

The portrait continues *fa lammā kutiba ʿalayhimu al-qitālu idhā farīqun minhum yakhshawna al-nāsa ka khashyati Allāhi aw ashadda khashyah* — then once fighting was prescribed, a group of them feared people as Allah should be feared, or even more. *Khashyah* — fear that humbles and restrains — belongs to God; the sūrah elsewhere says to fear God and that He is sufficient as Guardian and Helper (4:45), and that Satan’s schemes are ever weak (4:76). To fear people with that fear is to place the creature where the Creator belongs, and to fear them more is the precise reversal of *“you are from one another”* and *“Allah is Ever-Pardoning”* where earth replaces water. The fear was explained as fear of the hostile forces’ numbers, of death in fortified towers (4:78), and of losing the little enjoyment of this world (4:77). The Qur’an elsewhere says that true fear of God leads to keeping limits about wealth and marriage, while fear of people leads to seeking false judges (4:60) and to distorting words (4:46). Here fear of people leads to asking why fighting was ordered and wishing it delayed.

**Why Have You Ordered Us to Fight? Delay Us**

The quoted plea *rabbanā li ma katabta ʿalaynā al-qitāla law lā akhkhartanā ilā ajalin qarīb* — our Lord, why have You ordered us to fight? If only You had delayed us for a little while — was read as the hesitant asking for postponement. *Law lā akhkhartanā* — if only You had delayed — matches the lagger’s *“Allah blessed us for not being there”* at disaster (4:72); both wish the time of action were later, when safety is surer. Early authorities noted that the delay sought was not for preparation — take precautions and go forth had already been commanded (4:71) — but for clinging to the *dunyā* whose enjoyment is little. The plea is addressed to *rabbanā* — our Lord — showing that even in hesitation the speakers know who commands, which makes the hesitation more revealing: they ask the Commander why He commanded. The sūrah has said that messengers were sent to be obeyed by God’s leave (4:64) and that faith is not complete until the Prophet ﷺ is accepted as judge without resistance (4:65); to ask why the order came and to request a little delay is the opposite of *wa yusallimū taslīmā* — submit wholeheartedly.

**The Enjoyment of This World Is Little**

The answer *qul matāʿu al-dunyā qalīl wa al-ākhiratu khayrun li man ittaqā* — say, the enjoyment of this world is little, whereas the Hereafter is far better for those mindful of God — reframes the delay request with proportion. *Matāʿ al-dunyā* — enjoyment, provision of this world — was read as safety kept by lagging, gain wished after bounty (4:73), and the heap of gold not reclaimed where the dowry was given (4:20). Each is *qalīl* — little — when set beside the Hereafter, where Gardens with rivers and vast shade await believers who do good (4:57) and where martyrdom or victory in God’s cause brings a great reward (4:74). *Li man ittaqā* — for those mindful of God — was stressed as the condition that links this verse to the sūrah’s opening *“be mindful of your Lord”* (4:1) and to *“be mindful of God and speak equitably”* (4:9). Mindfulness makes the Hereafter appear greater because the mindful see with the sūrah’s measure — *not an atom wronged but multiplied* (4:40) — rather than with the lagger’s measure — safety now. The Qur’an elsewhere says *“the Hereafter is better and more lasting,”* and the sūrah’s own division of shares — whether little or much (4:7) — is little beside the *ajr ʿaẓīm* promised for fighting.

**Not Wronged by a Thread**

The closing *wa lā tuẓlamūna fatīlā* — and none of you will be wronged even by the width of a thread of a date stone — seals the answer with exact justice. *Fatīl* was explained as the thin thread in the date stone’s groove, the same family as *naqīr* — speck — that those with no share of kingdom would not give (4:53). The sūrah has said none will be wronged by a *fatīl* where self-elevation is claimed (4:49), and that God never wrongs by an atom but multiplies (4:40). Here it tells the fearful that fighting as ordered will not diminish what is theirs by even a thread; the atom’s weight kept in a just division and the great reward for obedience both remain. The fear that going forth will cost too much is answered: the One who orders fighting is the One who measures by *fatīl* and atom, who never wrongs, and whose Hereafter is better for the mindful. For the weak for whom burdens were lightened, the verse is not a harsh rebuke but a reminder of scale: the little of this world, even if kept by delay, remains little, while the better of the next, approached with precautions and with God as sufficient Helper (4:45), is not diminished by even a thread. To fear people as God should be feared is to mistake the scale; to fear God, to fight where He commands for the oppressed, and to trust that no thread is lost is to see it rightly.

## Sūrah al-Nisāʾ 4:77

> Have you ˹O Prophet˺ not seen those who had been told, “Do not fight! Rather, establish prayer and pay alms-tax.”? Then once the order came to fight, a group of them feared those ˹hostile˺ people as Allah should be feared—or even more. They said, “Our Lord! Why have You ordered us to fight? If only You had delayed ˹the order for˺ us for a little while!” Say, ˹O Prophet,˺ “The enjoyment of this world is so little, whereas the Hereafter is far better for those mindful ˹of Allah˺. And none of you will be wronged ˹even by the width of˺ the thread of a date stone.

### Expanded Commentary

**From restraint to commanded defence**

The verse recalls people who were initially told not to fight, but to establish prayer and pay zakat. When fighting was later commanded, some became afraid of human opponents and asked for delay. The sequence shows that courage is not produced by excitement alone. A community is first formed through worship, charity, and discipline before it carries the burdens of armed defence.

**Fear exposes misplaced calculation**

Their fear is described as fearing people as Allah should be feared, or more. Fear of danger is natural; the problem is allowing it to outweigh obedience and justice. The request for a little more time reveals the endless postponement by which a person hopes the duty will disappear.

**Worldly enjoyment and the thread**

The world’s enjoyment is little compared with the Hereafter, and no one will be wronged even by the smallest amount. The verse does not deny the value of life or excuse reckless conduct. It places temporary safety and material comfort within a larger moral horizon. A believer must protect life, but cannot make survival the only good when others are being oppressed and a lawful duty is clear.

The response to fear is not shame for feeling it; it is training the fear to serve truth. Prayer, charity, consultation, and lawful preparation build the inner strength needed when the command arrives.

**Training precedes defence**

The sequence of prayer, zakat, and later fighting shows that a community is not prepared merely because it possesses weapons. It must learn discipline, generosity, restraint, and concern for the weak. Without that moral formation, power magnifies fear and revenge rather than justice.

## Sūrah Āli ʿImrān 3:170

> rejoicing in Allah’s bounties and being delighted for those yet to join them. There will be no fear for them, nor will they grieve.

**Expanded Commentary**

**The Joy Attributed to the Martyrs**

The verse continues the description of the men who were killed in the way of God and states that they are exulting in what God has given them from His bounty. The participle carries on from the previous verse's sentence, so that the reader is given a picture of an ongoing condition.

The Book's descriptions of the Hereafter often include the joy of those who have arrived, and the joy is always presented as a response to what God has given rather than as a possession. The men described in this verse are not said to be enjoying what they earned; they are said to be rejoicing over what has come to them from another's generosity.

**The Word for Bounty**

The noun rendered as bounty describes favour and generosity, and it appears in this sūrah at the end of the passage about the battle, where God is described as possessed of bounty toward the believers. The word's recurrence ties the reward of the martyrs to the same generosity that the sūrah had named for the community as a whole.

The choice of the term keeps the description from becoming a calculation. The Book does not present the state of the dead as the payment of a wage that they had coming; it presents them as recipients of a gift, and the joy the verse describes is the joy of people who have received far more than their deeds could have secured.

**The Second Joy in the Sentence**

The verse adds that they rejoice in those who have not yet joined them from among the believers who remain behind. The commentators explained this in a range of ways: that they are glad at the prospect of their brothers arriving where they are, that they are glad for the safety of those still alive, and that they take pleasure in knowing that the survivors will not be harmed by what frightened them.

The explanations differ in detail, and they agree about the direction of the joy. The men who have died are not described as absorbed in their own condition; their attention extends to the community they left, and the verse makes their concern for those behind them part of their state.

**The Pair About Fear and Grief**

The verse closes with the statement that no fear shall come upon them and no grief shall be theirs. The pair of terms appears throughout the Book as the description of the condition of those who are secure with God, and it answers the two anxieties that the community at Madinah was living with.

The Book repeats the promise to the believers, to those who follow guidance, and to those who do good in several passages. The two terms cover the future and the past: fear is the apprehension of what is coming, and grief is the weight of what has already happened. The verse announces that the men in question have been delivered from both.

**To Whom the Closing Statement Belongs**

The commentators discussed whether the clause about fear and grief applies to the martyrs or to the believers who are still alive. Some read it as belonging to the martyrs, whose state is described as wholly free of both. Others read it as describing the condition that the surviving believers are promised if they follow the same path.

The two readings are not far apart. The sentence's position at the end of a verse about the martyrs favors the first, and the community that heard it would have taken the second as well. The Book's promises in such passages are frequently read by the tradition in both directions, and the practice is consistent with the way the Book's language works.

**The Book's Uses of the Pair**

The promise that no fear will come upon a group and that they will not grieve appears in the Book's descriptions of the believers, of those who follow the guidance of God, and of the people of the earlier scriptures who believed and did good. The pairing is one of the Book's standard forms of assurance.

Its frequency means that the community reciting this passage had met the phrase before. The verse before the reader applies it to the men who had died at Uḥud, and the application is the point: what the Book promises to the believers in general is stated here for the particular people whose deaths the community was mourning.

**What the Passage Achieves**

The whole passage, from the direction of the day in the divine permission to this description of the dead, is arranged to answer the questions a community asks after a loss. The verses deny that the dead had died by mistake, refuse the argument that different conduct would have saved them, and then state where they are.

The consolation the Book offers is not a denial of the loss. The community had lost men it could not replace, and the verses do not say otherwise. What they give is a description of the state of the dead that makes the loss bearable without making it smaller, and the community is expected to carry both the grief and the description.

**The State of the Martyrs in the Sūrah's Teaching**

The sūrah's statements about those killed in God's cause make up a short body of teaching: they are alive, they are with their Lord, they are provided for, they are glad, they are free of fear and grief, and they are aware of those who remain. The Book assembles these statements in its passages on the subject, and the community's teaching has used them ever since.

The verses that follow this one return to the condition of the believers who are still alive and to the warning against the attitudes the battle had exposed. The account of the martyrs stands between, and it supplies the community with the fact it needed in order to continue its work.

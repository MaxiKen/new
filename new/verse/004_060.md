## Sūrah al-Nisāʾ 4:60

> Have you ˹O Prophet˺ not seen those who claim they believe in what has been revealed to you and what was revealed before you? They seek the judgment of false judges, which they were commanded to reject. And Satan ˹only˺ desires to lead them farther away.

**Expanded Commentary**

**Have You Not Seen Those Who Claim Belief**

The question *a lam tara ilā alladhīna yazʿumūna annahum āmanū bi mā unzila ilayka wa mā unzila min qablika yurīdūna an yataḥākamū ilā al-ṭāghūt* — have you not seen those who claim they believe in what was revealed to you and what was revealed before you, yet seek judgment of false judges — exposes a contradiction between claim and referral. *Yazʿumūna* — they claim, they assert — was stressed as mere profession; the sūrah has just said that true belief refers disputes to Allah and His Messenger where the Messenger’s clarification is available (4:59), and that true belief worships without association and is kind in circles (4:36). To claim belief in what was revealed to you — the Qur’an that confirmed what is with those given a portion of the Book (4:47) — and in what was revealed before — the Torah and Gospel in their true light — and then to desire judgment from *ṭāghūt* — the false judge, the overstepping authority followed beyond its limit — is to belie the claim at its first test. Early reports placed the verse among hypocrites in Madinah who, when a dispute arose between a believer and a Jew, the Jew accepted the Prophet ﷺ as arbiter while the hypocrite sought a soothsayer or a chief who judged by custom and bribe. The type was read broadly: any person who says he believes yet takes his case to a forum that judges by desire rather than by Book and Messenger.

**Which They Were Commanded to Reject**

The clause *wa qad umirū an yakfurū bihi* — though they were commanded to reject it — makes the referral doubly blameworthy. *Kufr bi al-ṭāghūt* — to deny, to reject the false authority — was commanded as the counterpart to belief in God, as in *“whoever rejects ṭāghūt and believes in Allah has grasped the firm handhold”* elsewhere. The sūrah has already described belief in *jibt* and *ṭāghūt* by those given a portion of the Book as trading guidance for misguidance (4:51) and has linked *ṭāghūt* to idols and false gods whose judgment is not *ʿadl* — fairness — as commanded for judges (4:58). To seek judgment from what you were commanded to reject is to reverse the sūrah’s ethic of referral — *refer it to Allah and His Messenger* is the best and fairest outcome (4:59) — and to choose the forum that follows desire and leads away. The sūrah elsewhere says *“do not devour wealth by falsehood”* and *“do not kill yourselves”* where *that is easy for God* (4:29–30); seeking *ṭāghūt* was read as the institutional form of that devouring, where property and life are taken by a judgment that is not mutual consent nor fairness but overstep.

**Satan Desires to Lead Them Farther Away**

The closing *wa yurīdu al-shayṭānu an yuḍillahum ḍalālan baʿīdā* — and Satan only desires to lead them farther away — names the companion behind the forum. *Ḍalālan baʿīdā* — far, distant straying — was explained as the opposite of the noble ways God wills to guide to (4:26) and of the clear path He wills to make plain. The sūrah has paired two wills before — *Allah wishes to turn to you, but those who follow desires wish you to deviate greatly* (4:27) — and here Satan’s will is the deeper source of that wish. The false judge is not neutral; he is the instrument of a straying that grows with each step, as the sūrah showed where trading a portion for misguidance and reassuring disbelievers they are better guided (4:51) also had no helper once condemned (4:52). The distance — *baʿīd* — was heard as both moral and spatial: far from the limits set as *ḥudūd* (4:13–14), far from the trust returned to rightful owners (4:58), and far from the Garden whose rivers flow and whose shade is vast (4:57). The hypocrites who seek *ṭāghūt* while claiming belief think they are choosing a convenient forum; the verse says they are being led farther from the path that was made clear and lightened because they are weak (4:28).

**Claim, Referral, and Far Straying as a Sequence**

The verse traces a sequence that the sūrah has exposed in stages. First, the claim — *yazʿumūna annahum āmanū* — where belief is professed but not practiced as referral. That claim was already diagnosed in *“we hear and we disobey”* said as though it were *we hear and obey* (4:46). Second, the referral — *yurīdūna an yataḥākamū ilā al-ṭāghūt* — where the dispute is taken to the wrong authority, the opposite of *fa ruddūhu ilā Allāhi wa al-rasūl* (4:59). Third, the straying — *yuḍillahum ḍalālan baʿīdā* — where Satan’s desire moves the first detour into far wandering. The sūrah has shown that far wandering ends in wishes to be dust and no speech hidden from God (4:42), in Fire with skin renewed for tasting (4:56), and in condemnation with no helper. The distance is therefore not an abstraction; it is the measured gap between Gardens and Fire that the sūrah keeps open. To seek *ṭāghūt* is to step deliberately onto the side where *rāʿinā* is played with tongues and faith is discredited, rather than the side where *unẓurnā* is said and it would have been better and more proper (4:46).

**A Test of Belief That Is Practical**

Placed after the command to return trusts and judge with fairness and the declaration that such is a noble exhortation from the All-Hearing, All-Seeing (4:58), and after the command to obey and refer disputes (4:59), this verse tests belief at the point where it costs. It is easy to claim belief in what was revealed to you and before you while there is no dispute; the test comes when a dispute arises over property, marriage, or honour — the very matters this sūrah has regulated — and the claimant must choose the judge. To choose the Book and the Messenger is to accept that inheritance shares after debts (4:11–12), dowry not reclaimed (4:20–21), and trade by mutual consent (4:29) will be the standard; to choose *ṭāghūt* is to seek a standard that follows desire, as those who follow desires wish you to deviate greatly (4:27). The sūrah therefore makes referral the measure of sincerity. The one who claims belief and then seeks the false judge has claimed one thing and willed another — *yazʿum* and *yurīd* — and the will exposes the claim. Satan’s desire to lead farther away is already at work in that will, which is why the sūrah follows this exposure with the hypocrites’ turning away stubbornly when called to the revelations and the Messenger (4:61), and with the disaster that later brings them back swearing they intended only good.

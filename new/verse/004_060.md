## Sūrah al-Nisāʾ 4:60

> Have you ˹O Prophet˺ not seen those who claim they believe in what has been revealed to you and what was revealed before you? They seek the judgment of false judges, which they were commanded to reject. And Satan ˹only˺ desires to lead them farther away.

### Expanded Commentary

**A claim of faith tested by arbitration**

The verse exposes people who claim belief in revelation but seek judgment from false authorities. Their problem is not a lack of religious vocabulary; it is that they want a decision that will protect their interest. When revelation and conscience may limit them, they look for a judge, custom, or power willing to authorize the desired result.

**False judges in every age**

The term *ṭāghūt* includes idols and satanic powers but can also describe a human or institution that is given authority to rival God’s guidance. Courts themselves are not false merely because they are human; justice requires human judges. The danger arises when a decision-maker is treated as entitled to legalize oppression, or when a believer deliberately bypasses a fair process for a corrupt one.

**Satan’s gradual redirection**

The verse says Satan wants to lead them far astray. The movement usually begins with a small exception: “this case is special,” “everyone does it,” or “the result matters more than the rule.” Once the principle is surrendered, the person can be moved farther without noticing. Returning disputes to Allah and His Messenger interrupts that gradual slide.

The verse also calls for intellectual honesty. If a person seeks advice, he should be willing to accept an inconvenient answer; if he goes to court, he should not manipulate the process through bribery or influence. Faith is tested less by the ruling one quotes when it serves one’s interest than by the ruling one accepts when it restrains it.

**Choosing the judge who flatters us**

A person may seek a corrupt authority not because he doubts the truth but because he knows the truth will restrain him. The cure is to choose counsel before desire hardens, disclose one’s interest, and accept an answer that could be inconvenient. The integrity of a dispute is tested by the judge one is willing to hear.

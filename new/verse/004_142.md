## Sūrah al-Nisāʾ 4:142

> Surely the hypocrites seek to deceive Allah, but He outwits them. When they stand up for prayer, they do it half-heartedly only to be seen by people—hardly remembering Allah at all.

### Expanded Commentary

**The illusion of deceiving God**

The hypocrites seek to deceive Allah, but He turns their deception against them. They may deceive people because people see the outward act; God knows the inward refusal. The “outwitting” is divine justice: the space they use for performance becomes evidence of their insincerity.

**Prayer as performance**

They stand for prayer lazily, seeking to be seen and remembering Allah little. The problem is not physical tiredness or a moment of distraction; it is making public appearance the purpose of worship. A person may pray with an imperfect heart and still strive sincerely. Hypocrisy turns the imperfect effort into deliberate theatre.

The verse invites an audit of intention. Would the act continue if no one praised it? Is the delay caused by fatigue or by resentment of worship? The cure is private prayer, honest repentance, and remembrance that returns the heart to God rather than to the audience.

**Private worship repairs public performance**

A person who fears that prayer has become performance can protect some acts from public view, slow down the recitation, and remember the meaning of standing before Allah. Imperfect concentration is not the same as hypocrisy. The danger is making visibility the purpose and remembrance an inconvenience.

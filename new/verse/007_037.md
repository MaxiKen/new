## Sūrah al-Aʿrāf 7:37

> Who does more wrong than those who fabricate lies against Allah or deny His revelations? They will receive what is destined for them, until Our messenger-angels arrive to take their souls, asking them, “Where are those ˹false gods˺ you used to invoke besides Allah?” They will cry, “They have failed us,” and they will confess against themselves that they were indeed disbelievers.

**Expanded Commentary**

**The Greatest Wrongdoers**

The verse opens with a question about who does greater wrong than the person who fabricates lies against God or denies His signs, and the Book uses the question in several passages. The sūrah of the Cattle puts the same question twice in its accounts of the idolaters and of the prohibition of what God has permitted, the sūrah of Yūnus and the sūrah of Hūd use it of those who invent falsehood, the sūrah of the Cave and the sūrah of the Spider use it of the fabricators generally, and the sūrah of the Ranks uses it of those who invent lies about God. The question in front of us gathers the two offenses that the sūrah's passage on the forbidden things has already named.

The fabrication in question includes the claims the audience made about what God had commanded and forbidden, and the denial includes the rejection of the revelations that the messengers recited. The verse names the combination as the height of wrongdoing, and the sūrah's argument against the invented rules and the false attributions has prepared the reader to recognize the offense.

**The Portion That Reaches Them**

The verse states that those who fabricate and deny will receive their portion of what has been written for them, and the commentary tradition offers several readings of the phrase. The portion may be the share of worldly provision and longevity that has been decreed, the share of good or ill that befalls a person, or the share of the punishment that has been assigned. The readings agree that the phrase describes something measured and delivered in full.

The Book's use of the language of portion appears in its descriptions of the provision that each creature receives, and the sūrah of Hūd states that there is no creature on the earth whose provision is not with God. The verse in front of us applies the language to the end of the deniers, and the arrival of the angels follows the receipt of the portion.

**The Messengers Who Take the Soul**

The verse describes the arrival of the messengers of God who take the deniers away, and the Book identifies them as the angels charged with the collection of souls. The sūrah of the Cattle describes the same messengers and their work, and the sūrah of the Prostration states that the angel of death who has been charged with the people takes them in full. The scene in front of us follows the person to the moment of death, and it places the questioning there.

The question the angels put to the deniers concerns the beings they used to invoke besides God, and the answer they receive is that those beings have failed them. The Book's descriptions of the Hereafter include the same abandonment, and the sūrah of the Cattle states that the partners of the idolaters will say that they were not being worshipped by them. The verse in front of us presents the abandonment at the taking of the soul, and the sūrah's later verses show the same parties addressing one another in the Fire.

**The Witness Against Themselves**

The verse closes with the statement that the deniers bear witness against themselves that they were disbelievers, and the Book uses the language of self-witness in several passages. The sūrah of the Cattle and the sūrah of the Bee report the confessions of the condemned, and the sūrah of Fussilat describes the testimony of the skin and the limbs of the wrongdoers against them. The witness in front of us is given by the persons themselves, and the acknowledgment is complete.

The Book's descriptions of the reckoning include the closing of the mouths and the testimony of the limbs, and the two arrangements serve the same purpose in the narrative. What the verse in front of us establishes is that the deniers recognize their condition at the moment when recognition can no longer change it, and the sūrah's opening verses have already described the same kind of late confession in the case of the destroyed communities.

**The Sūrah's Account of the Denial**

The verse's details gather the elements that the sūrah has presented in its earlier passages. The fabricated claims about God appear in the audience's defense of its practices; the denial of the signs appears in the treatment of the messenger's recitation; and the abandonment by the beings invoked appears in the sūrah's warnings about the protectors taken in place of God. The verse in front of us shows the working out of all three at the moment of death.

The Book's placement of such scenes at the point of death rather than at the resurrection appears in several passages, and the sūrah of the Women describes the condition of those whom the angels take while they are wronging themselves. The verse in front of us belongs to that family, and the sūrah's following verse continues the scene into the Fire itself.

**Where the Verse Sits**

The verse stands thirty-seventh in the chapter, and it follows the assignment of the deniers to the Fire and precedes the command to enter it alongside the communities that went before. The transition from the verse before it to the verse after it is a transition from the taking of the soul to the entrance into the punishment, and the details in front of us fill the interval.

The reader has been shown the fabrication, the denial, the portion, the taking, the abandonment, and the confession. The sūrah then describes the entry into the Fire and the recriminations among the communities that are gathered there.

**The Transition to the Next Verse**

The next verse reports the command to enter the Fire among the communities of jinn and humanity that passed away before, and it describes each community cursing its sister, the blaming of the earlier generations by the later, the request that the leaders receive a double punishment, and the answer that the punishment has been multiplied for all (7:38). The confession of the previous verse is followed by the communal scene of the Fire.

The two verses together complete the account of the end of the deniers. The individual is taken and confesses, and the communities are gathered and quarrel, and the sūrah's teaching about responsibility is stated through the exchange that follows.

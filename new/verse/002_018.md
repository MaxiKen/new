## Sūrah al-Baqarah 2:18

> They are ˹wilfully˺ deaf, dumb, and blind, so they will never return ˹to the Right Path˺.

**Expanded Commentary**

**Three Adjectives and No Verb**

The verse is a nominal sentence — Arabic's construction for states rather than events. It does not say they *became* deaf, dumb, and blind, or that something *was done* to them; it says: deaf, dumb, blind — full stop. The nominal form in Arabic carries the sense of fixity and continuance, and the three adjectives arrive without a single verb to soften them into process. What the syntax states is a achieved condition: the disabilities have hardened from history into nature. The bracketed *˹wilfully˺* in the translation marks the exegetical consensus that supplies the missing history — these are not afflictions that befell innocent organs but disabilities manufactured by their owners' choices, now past the point where choice is needed to maintain them. The verse thus stands between the parable of verse 17 and the storm of verse 19 as the passage's one-line medical report: what began as conduct has completed itself as constitution.

**Words Whose Sound Enacts Their Sense**

The commentaries note a feature that survives recitation better than translation. Read aloud in Arabic, *ṣummun bukmun ʿumyun* performs what it says: three heavy, closed-syllable words, each ending in a doubled consonant that shuts the mouth — no open vowels to release them, no verbs to move them along. The line sounds the way the condition feels: sealed, weighted, without exit. The Qur'an's reciters have always treated sound as semantic material, and this triad is one of its clearest cases — a diagnosis delivered in phonetics as well as lexicon, so that the ear receives the closure before the mind parses it. In the prayer where these verses are recited, the congregation's own mouths form the words; the description of the hypocrites is uttered in the voices of the people warned against resembling them. Few verses so completely fuse their form to their content.

**Each Organ and the Faculty It Abandoned**

The triad maps onto the channels of guidance with clinical neatness. Deaf: the ear is closed, and revelation in the Qur'an is above all a heard thing — the Prophet ﷺ received it as recitation, and faith enters through listening. Dumb: the tongue is disabled, and the tongue is the organ of the *shahādah* — the very instrument that lied in verse 8 can now speak nothing true even if it wished. Blind: the eye is shut, and the Qur'an's world is dense with *āyāt*, signs to be seen in the sky, the earth, and the self. The three losses answer the three closures of verse 7 — the sealed heart, the sealed hearing, the covered sight — and complete them outward: what was locked inside is now dead at the surface. The order in Arabic puts deafness first, matching the priority of hearing in a religion of recited revelation; the translation's "deaf, dumb, and blind" preserves the sequence, and the sequence itself is an anatomy lesson in how guidance arrives and how it can be refused.

**A Signature the Qur'an Signs Twice More**

The triad is not improvised here; it is a recurring formula the Book applies to the same human type in two other settings, and the recurrences fix its meaning. Later in this sūrah, the disbelievers who will not respond to the messenger are compared to livestock: *"˹They are wilfully˺ deaf, dumb and blind so they have no understanding"* (2:171) — the same three words, now attached to a flock that hears the shepherd's call as noise. In al-Isrāʾ the formula is projected onto the Resurrection: *"And We will drag them on their faces on the Day of Judgment—deaf, dumb, and blind. Hell will be their home"* (17:97). The two extensions do real interpretive work. Al-Baqarah 171 confirms the disabilities are spiritual insensibility, not bodily fate — the organs work; the understanding is elsewhere. Al-Isrāʾ 97 shows the condition's terminus: what was acquired by choice in this life is ratified as the body's posture in the next. Between acquisition and ratification, this verse holds the hinge.

**“They Will Never Return”: The Last Door**

The closing clause — *fa-hum lā yarjiʿūn* — names what the triad forecloses: the return, *rujūʿ*, the Qur'an's word for repentance as a turning back. The exegetes specify the object variously: they will not return to guidance, to Islam, or to repentance and heed — and the three readings collapse into one, since each names the same lost motion. The finality particle *lan* is not used here; the negation is the plain *lā* with the imperfect, stating continuation rather than decree — they do not return, and the not-returning is their present tense. The clause explains itself by the triad before it: return requires hearing the call, speaking the confession, seeing the road, and the organs of return are precisely the organs disabled. This is the verse's most severe claim — not that return is forbidden but that it has become unperformable, the way a burned-out bridge is not prohibited, only absent. The door did not close on them; they dismantled it from inside.

**Acquired Disability, Preserved as Warning**

Everything in the verse's placement argues against fatalism. These are not men born deaf, dumb, and blind — the passage has just narrated the manufacturing, step by step: the lie, the scheme, the sickness increased, the mockery, the trade. The triad is the scar tissue of those acts, which is why the translation's "wilfully" belongs to the sense and not merely to the gloss. And the verse exists to be heard by the un-afflicted. In the liturgy of the mosque, in the private recitation of al-Baqarah, the description passes over the tongues of people whose organs all function — the warning operating on the principle that a disability is preventable only while it is still recognized as a choice. The hypocrites of Madina are long dead; the triad is not, because the acts that build it are perennial. Every refused truth deposits something; the verse states the endpoint of the deposits so that the first refusal can be recognized for what it is — the down payment on a silence that will not end by itself.

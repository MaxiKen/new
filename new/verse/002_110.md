## Sūrah al-Baqarah 2:110

> Establish prayer, and pay alms-tax. Whatever good you send forth for yourselves, you will ˹certainly˺ find ˹its reward˺ with Allah. Surely Allah is All-Seeing of what you do.

**Expanded Commentary**

**The Community's Program at the Pivot**

The verse stands at a hinge, and its placement is its first instruction. Verse 109 filed the opposition's wish — the reversal-work of envy, the taunt timed to weakness — and commanded pardon and bearing until Allah's decision. Verse 110 answers with the community's own agenda: *establish prayer, and pay alms-tax*. The pivot is from what is done to the believers to what the believers do; from the envy-war's front to the community's interior construction. The two commands are the sūrah's standing pair — joined since verse 43's establish prayer, pay alms-tax, and bow with those who bow, and built into the charter quoted at verse 83 as the covenant's ritual seal — and their reappearance at this exact point is deliberate. A community under reversal-pressure is tempted to two errors: to spend its whole attention on the pressure, or to answer it in the pressure's own currency. The verse forecloses both by redirecting the community to its constitutive acts — the prayer that holds the vertical bond in the body's daily rhythm, the alms-tax that holds the horizontal bonds in the property's circulation. The taunt operates on the community's morale; prayer and zakāh operate on its structure. The verse's pastoral logic is that of every durable movement under pressure: the answer to the wish for your reversal is the deepening of the practices whose reversal would actually constitute your loss.

**Sending Forth, Finding With**

The verse's second sentence is its economics, in two verbs: *whatever good you send forth for yourselves, you will find its reward with Allah*. The first verb — *tuqaddimū*, you send ahead — carries the commercial and postal sense of forwarding: the good is not spent but dispatched, transmitted from the world's ledger to another's, with the sender named on the package (*for yourselves* — the forwarding address is the sender's own future self). The second verb — *find* — completes the transaction's guarantee: what is sent arrives, is held, and will be located by its sender at the finding-place, the Day when the accounts open. The exegetes note the sentence's grammatical character: a promise in the form of a description — the verse does not argue that the good will be rewarded but describes the reward's finding as a standing fact, the way one describes where a thing is kept. The parallel passage in Sūrah al-Muzzammil runs the same sentence with the same verbs, connecting the found good specifically to prayer and charity — the two commands this verse opens with — and fixing the finding's time at the Day when the fruits of action are reaped. The economy the verse installs is total in its coverage: *whatever good* — no minimum, no specified channel beyond the good's goodness, no exclusion of the secret and small. The sūrah's transaction-language, which has documented sale after disastrous sale — signs for a cheap price, souls for magic, the Hereafter for the world — receives here its counter-transaction: good sent forward, reward found waiting, the only trade in the recital whose terms improve with the sender's ignorance of the details, because the details are held by the Finding-Place's keeper.

**The Pause That Answers the Verse**

The tradition preserves a practice of the Prophet ﷺ at exactly this verse, and the practice is its lived commentary. A Companion reported that when the Prophet ﷺ recited this verse in prayer, he would follow it with the words *He sees all things* — the recitation pausing so that the reciter could answer the verse in its own register, and the tradition notes the practice's general form: the Prophet ﷺ would respond to what he recited, glorifying at verses of glorification, asking at verses of asking, prostrating where prostration was mentioned. The pause is small and its instruction is large. Recitation in this practice is not performance but conversation — the verse addressed to the reciter, and the reciter's answer returned from the same heart the verse targets. At verse 110 specifically, the answer *He sees all things* takes up the verse's own closing clause — Allah All-Seeing of what you do — and speaks it back as acknowledgment: yes, He sees; the sending is witnessed, the finding guaranteed, the audit running. The practice also models the verse's central reassurance for the case that needs it most: the good sent in secret, unobserved, unthanked, apparently lost — the prayer at an empty hour, the alms nobody counted. The All-Seeing clause exists for that good specifically, and the Prophet's ﷺ pause teaches the community to answer the clause not with information but with trust: the verse says the good is seen, and the reciter says *He sees all things*, and between the two sentences the secret dispatch completes its journey — sent, received, held, filed under a sight that misses nothing.

**All-Seeing: The Clause That Guarantees the Finding**

The closing sentence — *surely Allah is All-Seeing of what you do* — repeats the audit-formula the recital has filed at every station, and its function shifts with its placement. At verse 74 the clause closed a diagnosis of hardening; at verse 96 it recorded the clinging of life; here it underwrites a promise. The seeing named is *Baṣīr*'s seeing — penetration rather than observation, the sight that reaches the deed's interior: its amount, its manner, its intention. For the sending-economy the clause is the guarantee's mechanism. A transaction whose counterparty is unseen requires an unseen witness to be secure; the good dispatched into the hereafter travels a route no human ledger tracks, and the verse stations its assurance at the route's only reliable point — the sight that watches the dispatch at its origin. The clause also does its double work on the deeds the verse does not describe: the good sent is seen, and the evil not sent — the withholding, the taunt returned, the envy answered with envy — is seen on the same sight. The verse's structure thereby holds both the promise and the audit in one sentence, and the believer's posture between them is the recital's whole requirement: send freely, because the finding is guaranteed; send carefully, because the seeing is total. The Muslims of Madina, under the reversal-pressure verse 109 described, received in this clause their morale's actual ground — not the visibility of their virtue to their taunters, who could not see it and did not matter, but its visibility to the One with whom it is all deposited.

**The Two Economies, Side by Side**

Read against the verses around it, verse 110 completes a contrast the recital has been building for a hundred verses. The opposition's economy runs on withholding: the wish that no good descend (105), the envy that wants belief reversed (109), the counsel that managed knowledge against the believers (76), the Scripture rationed by cost (85). The community's economy runs on dispatch: prayer established, alms paid, every good sent forward for the self that will collect it. The two economies are not merely opposed in morality but in structure — one organized around keeping, the other around giving — and the verse's placement says which structure survives its own audit. The withholders' good stays with them and stays finite; the senders' good leaves them and becomes found, held with Allah, secured by the sight that guarantees the depot. The envy of verse 109 now reads as an economic error before it reads as a moral one: the envier resents descent he cannot dam and withholds dispatch he cannot recover, double loss in a single posture. The community's answer to being envied is not counter-envy but increased sending — the prayer deeper, the alms wider, the good dispatched at exactly the moments the taunt expects retrenchment. This is the verse's permanent instruction under pressure: the reversal-agents measure the community by its visible morale, and the community is being taught to measure itself by its dispatches — because on the Day the verse describes, the finding will be of what was sent, and nothing the taunt did or undid will appear in that inventory at all.

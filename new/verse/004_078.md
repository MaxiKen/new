## Sūrah al-Nisāʾ 4:78

> Wherever you may be, death will overcome you—even if you were in fortified towers.” When something good befalls them, they say, “This is from Allah,” but when something evil befalls them, they say, “This is from you.” Say, ˹O Prophet,˺ “Both have been destined by Allah.” So what is the matter with these people? They can hardly comprehend anything!

**Expanded Commentary**

**Wherever You May Be, Death Will Overtake You**

The statement *aynamā takūnū yudrikkumu al-mawtu wa law kuntum fī burūjin mushayyadah* — wherever you may be, death will overtake you, even if you were in fortified towers — answers the plea *“if only You had delayed the order for a little while”* (4:77). The fear that made a group fear people as God should be feared was, at root, fear of death in fighting. The verse says death is not avoided by staying behind, as the lagger imagined his absence at disaster was a blessing (4:72). *Burūj mushayyadah* — fortified, high, plastered towers — were explained as the strongest shelters the Arabs knew, high forts that seemed to put death at distance. *Yudrikkumu* — will overtake, will reach — was stressed as pursuing, not merely occurring. The One who created from a single soul and spread countless (4:1), who is Ever-Pardoning and All-Forgiving in purification (4:43), and who is sufficient as Guardian and Helper (4:45) is also the One who brings death wherever the person is. The sūrah elsewhere says *“every soul will taste death”* and that the term is fixed — *appointed* — as inheritance shares are fixed after debts (4:11–12). To take precautions and go forth as commanded (4:71) is not to hasten death; to lag is not to delay it. The towers that seem to delay it are still within the appointment, so the wish for a little delay (4:77) mistakes the nature of the term.

**When Something Good Befalls Them, This Is From Allah**

The portrait shifts to speech after events: *wa in tuṣibhum ḥasanatun yaqūlū hādhihi min ʿindi Allāhi* — when something good befalls them, they say, this is from Allah. *Ḥasanah* — good, beauty — was read as victory, bounty, safety, the *faḍl* that the lagger envied as great gain (4:73). To say *from Allah* at good is not false; the sūrah has just taught *“whatever good befalls you is from Allah”* (4:79). The fault here is not the attribution at good but the inconsistency that follows, where attribution changes at harm. The sūrah has shown that bounty is *faḍl* to be asked from God rather than craved (4:32) and that donation from what was provided would have harmed no one (4:39); to acknowledge good from Him when it arrives is therefore the beginning of gratitude the sūrah invites when it says *“Allah does not wish to place hardship upon you”* elsewhere. The persons described acknowledge that beginning, which makes their next turn more revealing.

**But When Evil Befalls Them, This Is From You**

The counterpart *wa in tuṣibhum sayyiʾatun yaqūlū hādhihi min ʿindika* — but when something evil befalls them, they say, this is from you — was read as the hypocrites and weak blaming the Prophet ﷺ for setbacks in battle, as though the order to fight (4:77) he conveyed had caused the harm. *Sayyiʾah* — evil, hardship — was explained as defeat, injury, or drought, the *muṣībah* that hits because of what hands put forward (4:62). To say *from you* — *min ʿindika* — to the Messenger ﷺ is to place the cause in the command rather than in the deed, desire, and divine decree. The sūrah has already corrected the lagger who called absence at disaster a blessing (4:72) and who will later say *“Our Lord, why have You ordered us to fight?”* (4:77); this saying — *this is from you* — is the same deflection in another form: good is from God, evil is from the Messenger’s order. The Qur’an elsewhere says the hypocrites’ failure to refer news to the Messenger and authorities led them to publicise fear (4:83), which spreads the same *from you* logic.

**Both Have Been Destined by Allah**

The correction *qul kullun min ʿindi Allāh* — say, both have been destined by Allah — was explained as both good and evil befalling as part of divine decree, not as the Messenger’s authorship. *Min ʿindi Allāh* — from Allah — was read as from His decree, creation, and knowledge, the same knowledge that never wrongs by an atom but multiplies good (4:40) and that fully knows who deserves favour (4:70). That does not remove human responsibility; the sūrah will immediately add *“whatever evil befalls you is from yourself”* (4:79) to show the secondary cause. Here the corrective is for those who split the source — good from God, evil from you — to tell them the source of decree is one. The sūrah has said God wills to make things clear and guide to noble ways (4:26) and that He is sufficient as Guardian and Helper (4:45); to say *both from God* is to return the fearful to that clarity: the Prophet ﷺ is the one sent to be obeyed by God’s leave (4:64) and to be accepted as judge without resistance (4:65), not the author of the decree that the dispute tests. To attribute evil to him is to confuse the messenger with the sender.

**They Can Hardly Comprehend Anything**

The reproach *fa mā li hāʾulāʾi al-qawmi lā yakādūna yafqahūna ḥadīthā* — so what is the matter with these people? They can hardly comprehend any statement — was read as amazement at the split attribution and at the fear that made them request delay. *Ḥadīth* — statement, discourse — was explained as any statement that would make them understand — that the enjoyment of this world is little and the next better for the mindful (4:77), that not a thread is wronged (4:77), that Satan’s schemes are ever weak (4:76), and that both good and evil are from God’s decree. The phrase *lā yakādūna yafqahūna* — they hardly understand — was read as they barely grasp, as though understanding is a surface they skim. The sūrah elsewhere asks *“do they not reflect on the Qur’an? Had it been from other than God they would have found many inconsistencies”* (4:82), and *“when they hear news of security or fear they publicise it”* (4:83) where referral would have validated. The same lack of comprehension operates: good and evil are both from God’s decree, yet the means — taking precautions, asking for bounty, referring disputes — are entrusted to people, so that evil that befalls through a person’s hand is from that hand as secondary cause while remaining from God as decree. To fail to see that is to be the people who can hardly comprehend any *ḥadīth*, even when death in towers is promised wherever you are.

## Sūrah al-Nisāʾ 4:78

> Wherever you may be, death will overcome you—even if you were in fortified towers.” When something good befalls them, they say, “This is from Allah,” but when something evil befalls them, they say, “This is from you.” Say, ˹O Prophet,˺ “Both have been destined by Allah.” So what is the matter with these people? They can hardly comprehend anything!

### Expanded Commentary

**Death reaches every fortress**

Wherever people are, death overtakes them, even in fortified towers. The image addresses those who imagine that political power, architecture, medical wealth, or physical distance can cancel mortality. Precautions are commanded, but precautions do not create sovereignty over the appointed term.

**Attributing good and evil**

The hypocrites attribute good to Allah and evil to the Messenger. The response—both are by Allah’s decree—rejects their attempt to make blessing evidence of alliance and misfortune evidence of prophetic failure. Events occur within divine knowledge and decree, though human beings remain responsible for the choices that produce wrongdoing.

Verse 79 clarifies the moral distinction: good is from Allah, while evil that befalls a person is from himself in the sense of human fault and moral cause. The two verses together avoid both fatalism and arrogance. God’s decree encompasses the world; people cannot blame destiny to excuse sin, nor claim success as proof of independent merit.

The final question about their lack of understanding is a call to intellectual honesty. Tragedy is not automatically evidence against truth, and success is not automatically evidence for it. The believer evaluates events by revelation, responsibility, and the final account.

**Events are not simple verdicts**

Success may test a person more dangerously than failure, and disaster may expose a weakness without proving that the entire cause was false. The verse asks believers to read events with revelation and responsibility rather than with immediate emotional attribution. This protects both gratitude and humility.

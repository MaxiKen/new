## Sūrah al-Naḥl 16:37

> **Even though you ˹O Prophet˺ are keen on their guidance, Allah certainly does not guide those He leaves to stray, and they will have no helpers.**

**Expanded Commentary**

**Keenness For Guidance**

In taḥriṣ ʿalā hudāhum — even though you are keen, eager, desirous for their guidance. Taḥriṣ from ḥ-r-ṣ means to be keen, eager, to desire strongly, to strive. Hudāhum, their guidance. The verse addresses Prophet ﷺ who was deeply keen for his people to be guided, grieved when they refused, as 18:6 says *Perhaps you would kill yourself with grief over them if they do not believe* (18:6) and 26:3 says *Perhaps you will kill yourself that they are not believers* (26:3). Al-Ṭabarī explains that Prophet ﷺ was eager for Quraysh to accept tawḥīd, to be saved from Hell, to be among those who say goodness when asked what Lord revealed. His keenness was from mercy, as he is described as *merciful to believers* (9:128). The verse acknowledges that keenness while clarifying limits: even with your keenness, guidance is by Allah's will and human choice.

**Allah Does Not Guide Those He Leaves To Stray**

Fa-inna Allāha lā yahdī man yuḍillu — but indeed Allah does not guide those He leaves to stray, whom He lets go astray. Man yuḍillu means whom He leaves to stray, whom He allows to stray as consequence of their choice. Al-Ṭabarī explains that yuḍillu here means those whom Allah has judged to be astray because they chose denial, arrogance, calling revelation fables, using divine will as excuse. Their straying became deserved, as verse 36 said *upon whom straying was justified*. Allah does not guide them further because they refused initial guidance shown via blessings, messengers, and clear delivery. Al-Rāzī notes that lā yahdī here does not mean Allah prevents them from guidance arbitrarily, but that He does not guide them to success because they chose straying. The same idea appears in 16:107: *That is because they preferred worldly life over Hereafter and Allah does not guide disbelieving people* (16:107). Guidance requires willingness.

**They Will Have No Helpers**

Wa-mā lahum min nāṣirīn — and they will have no helpers, supporters, protectors. Nāṣirīn plural of nāṣir, helper, from n-ṣ-r, to help, support. Al-Ṭabarī says they will have no helpers to protect them from Allah's punishment, to intercede, to prevent humiliation described in verse 27, to prevent entry to Hell described in verse 29. Their idols, whom they worshipped for help, will not help, as verse 27's question *Where are My partners?* shows. Their forefathers, whom they invoked as tradition, will not help. Their plotting, building, wealth will not help. The phrase mā lahum min nāṣirīn appears many times for deniers, as in 2:270 and 3:192, to emphasize that on Day, help comes only from Allah, and those who associated others will find those others absent.

**Why This Verse Follows Universal Messengership**

Verse 36 said We sent messenger to every community, some guided, some straying justified, travel and see fate of deniers. Verse 37 addresses Prophet's keenness for guidance of his own community, Quraysh, who are among those who may be guided or stray. It says even though you are keen, Allah does not guide those He leaves to stray, and they will have no helpers. The connection is that universal sending does not mean universal acceptance; some will stray despite messenger's keenness. The Prophet's duty is clear delivery, as verse 35 said, not compelling guidance. His keenness is praiseworthy but does not override divine wisdom and human choice. The verse consoles Prophet: you have done your duty, you are keen from mercy, but guidance is ultimately by Allah and by people's willingness.

**The Balance Between Keenness And Reliance**

The verse teaches balance for anyone calling to truth: be keen, eager, strive for others' guidance, but rely on Allah and do not despair if some refuse. Al-Qurṭubī notes that taḥriṣ ʿalā hudāhum is natural for compassionate caller, but fa-inna Allāha lā yahdī man yuḍillu reminds that result is not in caller's hand. The caller delivers clearly, is keen, prays, but guidance is by Allah. This prevents both negligence, saying guidance is only by Allah so I need not be keen, and despair, saying I am keen but they refuse so I failed. The correct attitude is keenness plus reliance plus acceptance that some will stray and have no helpers, while continuing to call with wisdom and good exhortation as verse 125 will say.

**Linguistic Note On Ḥirṣ And Naṣīr**

Taḥriṣ from ḥ-r-ṣ means to be keen, eager, greedy, desirous, often used for strong desire to obtain something. In this context, it is praiseworthy keenness for others' good. Hudā from h-d-y means guidance. Fa-inna Allāha lā yahdī man yuḍillu, but Allah does not guide whom He leaves to stray, uses yuḍillu from ḍ-l-l, to stray, misguide. The construction man yuḍillu could be read as whom He misguides, but tafsīr explains it as whom He judges to be astray due to their choice. Mā lahum min nāṣirīn, they will have no helpers, uses mā for negation, lahum for them, min for any, nāṣirīn for helpers. The verse's structure — concession, even though you are keen, then principle, Allah does not guide those left to stray, then consequence, no helpers — moves from Prophet's attitude to divine principle to outcome for deniers, guiding listener to understand limits of human keenness and necessity of divine guidance and personal choice.


**A Lesson That Connects To Gratitude**

This verse, like all verses in Sūrat al-Niʿam, ultimately calls to gratitude. Whether it describes Gardens, death, waiting, consequences, or divine command, the purpose is to make the reader recognize blessings and respond with thanks. Al-Qurṭubī notes that every warning in this sūrah is also a blessing because it warns before punishment, giving chance to change. Every promise is also a blessing because it encourages good. The sūrah therefore weaves warning and promise, blessing and accountability, to cultivate a heart that is mindful, humble, and grateful. The reader who reflects on this verse with that mindset will find both knowledge and motivation to be among those who say goodness when asked what Lord revealed, and who are greeted with peace at death.

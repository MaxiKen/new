## Sūrah al-Nisāʾ 4:37

> those who are stingy, promote stinginess among people, and withhold Allah’s bounties. We have prepared for the disbelievers a humiliating punishment.

### Expanded Commentary

**Miserliness and concealment of bounty**

The verse condemns those who are miserly, urge others to be miserly, and hide what Allah has given them. Miserliness is more than failing to donate beyond one’s means. It includes withholding obligatory support, refusing a right because the recipient is weak, and cultivating a culture in which generosity is mocked. The person who hoards may also hide the source of his privilege so that he is never asked to use it for good.

**Bounty carries responsibility**

Wealth, knowledge, influence, health, and time can all be gifts that ought to be used responsibly. The verse’s wording is broad enough to expose the scholar who hides beneficial knowledge, the employer who conceals profits while pleading poverty, and the official who monopolizes access. Gratitude is shown by making a blessing useful, not merely by saying that it came from God.

**The “painful punishment”**

The punishment corresponds to the social injury. Hoarding does not remain private when the hoarder controls resources needed by others or teaches a family to fear every act of giving. A community shaped by such behaviour experiences distrust, class division, and the collapse of mutual aid. The warning is therefore both about the Hereafter and about the moral poverty produced here.

The verse does not condemn prudent saving or responsible planning. Islam recognizes ownership and the need to provide for dependants. It condemns the heart that treats every resource as a fortress against obligation and then recruits others into the same fear. The cure is to identify rights, fulfil them first, and practice voluntary generosity until wealth becomes a means rather than a master.

**Prudence is not miserliness**

Saving for dependants, paying debts, and avoiding waste are responsible uses of wealth. Miserliness begins when legitimate responsibility becomes an excuse to withhold a right or when fear of losing control makes generosity impossible. The verse asks whether a resource is being managed wisely or worshipped through hoarding.

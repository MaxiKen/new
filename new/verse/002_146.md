## Sūrah al-Baqarah 2:146

> Those We have given the Scripture recognize this ˹Prophet˺ as they recognize their own children. Yet a group of them hides the truth knowingly.

**Expanded Commentary**

**Recognition Described**

The verse states that the people who had been given a scripture recognised the Prophet ﷺ the way a man recognises his own children, and the comparison is the strongest the Book uses for the certainty of identification. No one requires evidence to recognise his own son, and the sentence attributes that kind of knowledge to the community being addressed.

The sūrah has made the same claim before in the passage about the direction of prayer, and it makes it again in the context of the Prophet's mission. The Book's argument throughout is that the earlier scriptures contained the marks by which the messenger would be known, and the verse states that the marks were recognised rather than that they were discovered.

The comparison to a man’s knowledge of his own children was chosen for its freedom from doubt. A father may be mistaken about many things, but he is not mistaken about the identity of his son, and the Book uses that certainty to describe what the people of the scripture possessed. The sentence is therefore a charge rather than a compliment, since knowledge of that kind leaves no room for honest confusion.

**What Was Recognised**

Commentators differed on whether the object of recognition is the direction of prayer or the Prophet himself. Some connected the phrase with the previous verses about the qiblah and read it as the knowledge that the Sacred Mosque was the true direction. Others took it as the recognition of the Prophet, whose description the earlier books contained and whose character and message confirmed what those books had announced.

The two readings are not in conflict, since the recognition of the direction depends on the recognition of the one who was commanded to face it. What the sūrah establishes is that the community possessed the means to identify what God had sent, and the sentence states that it did so.

**A Group, Not a People**

The verse says that a group hides the truth, and the sūrah has used the same qualification repeatedly in its treatment of this community. Earlier passages named those who kept faith, and the Book's habit is to specify rather than to generalise, so that its judgments fall on the conduct described.

The precision also protects the reader from the conclusion that a whole people is being condemned. The sūrah has stated that some of the People of the Book are upright, that some of them recognise the truth and weep over it, and that among them are those who believe in God and in what has been sent down. The group named here is those who conceal.

**The Recurring Charge of Concealment**

The Book charges this community with concealment in several places. It states that they were bound by a covenant to make the scripture known and not to hide it, that they bought a small price with it, and that they distort words from their proper places. The concealment described in this verse belongs to that series, and the sūrah has already used the image of a book cast behind the back.

The offence is presented as deliberate rather than inadvertent, since the verse says the group hides the truth knowingly. The Book's treatment of knowledge in this passage consistently holds those who possess it to a higher standard, and the sentence identifies the moment at which the standard was broken: understanding was present, and the truth was withheld anyway.

**The Prophet's Confidence**

The verse is addressed to the Prophet ﷺ in a situation where his community's practice was being contested by people who possessed earlier scriptures. The sentence supplies him with an account of the opposition that does not depend on his opponents' sincerity, and it explains why their objections to the direction of prayer did not indicate a weakness in his position.

The Book's statements of this kind are not gloating. They give the messenger and the community a footing, and they help the readers of the Book understand what they will meet when they carry the same message. What the verse establishes is that the recognition has already occurred, and that what follows from it is a matter of honesty.

The description also places the objections in a perspective the Prophet ﷺ could work with. A person who is told that his opponents have recognised him and withheld what they know is not left wondering whether the fault lies in his message. The sentence supplies the messenger with a true account of the situation, and the account frees him from the need to prove what the other communities already see.

**The Standard for Holding a Book**

The sentence states a standard that the sūrah has been setting out for several passages, and it applies to the community reading it as much as to the groups being described. A people that has been given a scripture is expected to make known what it contains and to act on what it knows, and the failure described here is the failure of a community that possessed the answer.

The Book's warnings to the Muslims in this sūrah use the same terms. The community is told to hold firmly to what it has received, to study and to teach the revelation, and to be witnesses rather than concealers. The verse's charge against the earlier community therefore becomes an instruction for the community that holds the final revelation.

**What Follows in the Passage**

The next verse states that this is the truth from the Lord and commands the Prophet not to be among those who doubt. The two sentences fit together: the recognition of the earlier community is established, and the community that receives the revelation is told to hold what it has without wavering. The passage continues with reminders to remember God and to seek help through patience and prayer, and the reader is carried from the controversy about the direction into the practices that sustain a community.

The verses that follow turn from recognition to practice. The community is commanded to remember God and to be grateful, and then it is told to seek help through patience and prayer. The charge of concealment stands at the end of a passage of controversy, and the practices that follow supply the community with what it needs in order to hold its position without bitterness. The next passage then describes the trials that would test it, and the reader sees the sequence: a disputed claim, a settled conviction, and the discipline that sustains it.

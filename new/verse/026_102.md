## Sūrah al-Shuʿarāʾ 26:102

> If only we could have a second chance, then we would be believers.”

**Expanded Commentary**

**Regret Asks for a Return That Cannot Be Granted**

The condemned wish for another turn. They imagine returning to the world and living differently, believing after seeing the consequence that they previously denied. The request reveals that their problem was not a lack of desire for safety. They wanted safety, but they postponed the faith and obedience that would have shaped their safety before judgment.

A second chance here is not an ordinary opportunity to repair a mistake. It is a request to reopen the period of earthly choice after its appointed end. The barrier is not that they have failed to explain themselves. The barrier is that the life in which belief could become action has passed.

**The Promise of Belief Appears Only After Proof**

They say they would be believers if returned. The statement may be sincere as an expression of regret, but it comes after the unseen has become visible and the result can no longer be denied. Belief that arises from unavoidable exposure is different from faith that trusts God's warning while the choice remains open.

This does not make evidence unimportant. God sends signs and arguments so that people can respond responsibly. The danger is waiting for a kind of proof that removes every possibility of refusal. The condemned now possess certainty, but certainty forced by final judgment cannot be used to rewrite the choices that produced their condition.

Quran 23:99–100 describes a person asking to be sent back so that he may do good with what he left behind, followed by the statement that the appeal is only a word and that a barrier stands until resurrection. That parallel confirms the moral point of this verse: the request to return is recognized as regret, not granted as a new earthly life.

The barrier gives the refusal a settled shape. It prevents the condemned from treating death as a doorway through which they can bargain for extra time. The life of choice was a trust with an end. Once the end arrives, the request for a new beginning cannot create one.

**Regret Reveals That the Truth Was Understandable**

The condemned do not ask for a different message. They ask for another opportunity to believe. Their request implies that the problem was not that the truth was impossible to grasp. They now understand enough to want a changed life. The tragedy is that understanding arrived after action could no longer follow it.

This is why procrastination in matters of faith is dangerous. A person may say that he will repent when circumstances improve, when age advances, or when the heart feels more ready. None of those future conditions is promised. The desired second chance can disappear before the person reaches the time he imagined.

**Belief Must Become a Present Allegiance**

The word believers in the request cannot mean only that the condemned would verbally agree with the truth. In the context of judgment, they want to return and live as people whose conduct reflects belief. Faith includes worship, moral repair, and obedience. The wish exposes the gap between recognizing a doctrine and ordering a life by it.

A person should therefore test belief by what it changes now. Does it make worship sincere, wealth responsible, speech truthful, and relationships just? The final request teaches that belief postponed is not yet the faith that saves. The heart must answer God while response remains possible.

The condemned remember their earlier choices, but memory cannot become action. They can name what they would have done, yet they cannot perform it. This is a severe distinction between intention and obedience. A good intention that is never acted upon may be meaningful in some circumstances, but at the final boundary it cannot replace the deeds that were deliberately delayed.

The regret also shows that people often understand the value of time only after time has ended. While life continues, ordinary days can seem expendable. In the Hereafter, every lost opportunity becomes visible. The request for another turn is a confession that the days they treated lightly were a mercy they cannot recover.

The verse is frightening, but it is also addressed to those who can still respond. While a person is alive, repentance, restitution, and renewed worship remain possible. The call to change does not require waiting for a perfect past or a dramatic sign. It requires turning from the false direction and acting on the truth already made clear.

That present opportunity should not be confused with confidence that life will continue long enough to use it. The wise person treats today as the available turn. He does not demand a future in which obedience will become easier. The condemned want the moment that the living already possess.

**The Request Separates Hope From Wishful Thinking**

Hope in God's mercy leads to work, while wishful thinking postpones work and assumes that mercy will be available on demand. The condemned express a wish for belief without showing the life that belief would have produced. Their words cannot create the obedience they now desire.

True hope says that God can forgive and guide, then moves toward Him in the present. It accepts that the final decision belongs to God and refuses to use that dependence as an excuse for delay. The request for a second chance reveals the difference. Hope used well changes a life before judgment; hope abused becomes regret after it.

**The Final Appeal Leaves the Reader a Choice**

The line ends with the promised identity of believers, but the promise remains unfulfilled because the return is impossible. The reader is left with a choice that the condemned no longer have: respond now or continue postponing. The verse does not invite morbid fascination with their regret. It asks the living to make their belief visible while the gift of time remains.

A second chance cannot be demanded after the final boundary. The mercy is the present chance to believe, repent, and obey. Whoever uses it does not need to make the desperate request recorded here.

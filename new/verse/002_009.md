## Sūrah al-Baqarah 2:9

> They seek to deceive Allah and the believers, yet they only deceive themselves, but they fail to perceive it.

**Expanded Commentary**

**Attempting What Cannot Be Done**

The sentence begins with an impossibility. To deceive is to produce in someone a belief that is false; God holds no false beliefs. The verb the Quran uses, *yukhādiʿūna*, is built on the pattern that expresses mutual action — each side working on the other — which makes the problem sharper rather than softer, since a mutual relation with God is exactly what cannot exist here.

Commentators resolved it by reading the form as the attempt rather than the achievement: they *try* to deceive, and the trying is the crime. The Quran states the same thing without the grammatical difficulty in another sūrah: *Surely the hypocrites seek to deceive Allah, but He outwits them* (4:142). Elsewhere the pattern is named as a general law of the world, applied to people who plotted against a prophet: *And the disbelievers made a plan ˹against Jesus˺, but Allah also planned —and Allah is the best of planners* (3:54); *Those ˹disbelievers˺ before them ˹secretly˺ planned, but Allah has the ultimate plan. He knows what every soul commits* (13:42). The plotting is real, it is recorded, and it never reaches its object.

**The Rebound**

The middle clause states where the deception does land: on the people who produced it. *They only deceive themselves* (2:9). The mechanism is not difficult to state. A person who professes what he does not hold must, from that point, organise his life around maintaining the gap between what he says and what he is — and in maintaining it he removes himself from the very thing he is claiming. He has the vocabulary of faith and none of its substance, and the arrangement costs him the audience, the instruction, and the assistance that would have gone with the claim if it had been true.

The Quran states this as a law that applies beyond the hypocrites, in a sentence almost identical to this one: *And so We have placed in every society the most wicked to conspire in it. Yet they plot only against themselves, but they fail to perceive it* (6:123). Another sūrah puts it as a general rule for anyone: *evil plotting only backfires on those who plot* (35:43). The deception is not punished from outside; it fails in the way an unsound structure fails, by collapsing under what it was built to carry.

**The Part They Cannot See**

The last clause is the one that makes the verse a diagnosis rather than a threat: *but they fail to perceive it* (2:9). The harm is being done and the person sustaining it does not register it. This is not incidental to the description — the sūrah repeats the formula twice more within a few lines. Of their claim to be reformers: *Indeed, it is they who are the corruptors, but they fail to perceive it* (2:12). Of their contempt for the believers: *Indeed, it is they who are fools, but they do not know* (2:13).

The repetition establishes what the condition consists of. The hypocrite's difficulty is not that he lacks intelligence or that he lacks information about what the believers do; it is that his assessment of himself is unreliable. Everything he has built his self-judgement on — his own pronouncements, his own attendance, his own public standing — confirms him, so the one thing he cannot check is the thing that is wrong.

**A Tension Inside the Sūrah's Own Description**

Later in this passage the sūrah records the hypocrites saying something that implies full awareness: *When they meet the believers they say, “We believe.” But when alone with their evil associates they say, “We are definitely with you; we were only mocking”* (2:14). That is not the speech of someone who does not know what he is doing. It looks like the plainest possible contradiction of the clause before us.

The reconciliation the commentators propose is a distinction of objects, and the text supports it. What the hypocrites perceive is what they are doing *to the believers* — mocking them, managing them, keeping their standing while withholding their commitment. That part is deliberate and they enjoy it. What they do not perceive is what they are doing *to themselves*. The deception that succeeds is the one aimed at the deceiver. On this reading the two verses are not in conflict: a person can know exactly what he is doing to other people and be entirely mistaken about what he is doing to his own soul.

**Why Self-Deception Is the Worse State**

A person who lies knowingly can, in principle, stop. The calculation that made him lie can change; the fear that made him lie can lift; someone can present him with an argument he cannot answer. The self-deceived person has no such access point, because there is nothing in his own report of himself that he distrusts.

This is why the sūrah's language about them escalates so quickly from mockery to sealing. Their oaths become a shield that protects them from exactly the exposure they need (63:2), and the sūrah of the Hypocrites gives the sequence plainly: *This is because they believed and then abandoned faith. Therefore, their hearts have been sealed, so they do not comprehend* (63:3). The condition described here is not the first step in a deception; it is a late one, in which the instrument for noticing the truth has already gone.

**Intention, and the Gap It Closes**

The tradition's remedy for this condition is a single sentence, and it is the first hadith in the most rigorously compiled collection of reports: *Actions are judged by intentions, and every person will have what he intended* (Ṣaḥīḥ al-Bukhārī 1; Ṣaḥīḥ Muslim 1907, from ʿUmar ibn al-Khaṭṭāb). The report was occasioned by a man who emigrated for the sake of marrying a woman rather than for God, and the Prophet's answer was that the man would get what he actually intended.

That is the precise opposite of the state described in this verse. Hypocrisy is the condition of a person whose action and intention have come apart and who benefits from their staying apart. Sincerity is not a stronger feeling attached to the same action; it is the closing of the gap that this verse names. What the verse asks of anyone reading it is not an assessment of the hypocrites — that is God's business, as the previous verse established — but an examination of the distance between what he says and what he wants.

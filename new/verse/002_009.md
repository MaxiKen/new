## Sūrah al-Baqarah 2:9

> They seek to deceive Allah and the believers, yet they only deceive themselves, but they fail to perceive it.

**Expanded Commentary**

**Deception Aimed at the Undeceivable**

The verse names the operation behind the costume: *yukhādiʿūn* — they deal in deception, in a verb form that carries attempt and reciprocity, deceiving or trying to deceive in exchange. The first object of the attempt is Allah. The absurdity is the diagnosis. Deception is a technique that works on limited perceivers — you conceal what another cannot see — and Allah is the Knower of the unseen, from whom nothing is concealed in the heavens or the earth. To attempt deception against omniscience is not merely to fail; it is to demonstrate what the attempter actually believes about his target. The classical commentaries draw the inference cleanly: you only scheme against someone you think can be fooled, and the hypocrite's scheme exposes a practical atheism inside his professed creed. He says he believes in Allah and the Last Day (2:8) and behaves as though neither were watching. The verse treats the attempt itself as evidence — the deception declares the deceiver's real theology better than his testimony does.

**The Trick That Lands Only on the Trickster**

The second clause delivers the reversal in the tightest possible grammar: *wa-mā yakhdaʿūna illā anfusahum* — they deceive *none but* themselves. The particle of exception confines the entire effect of the scheme to the schemer. Every hour of performance, every calculated creed, every managed appearance subtracts from the one self the deceiver actually owns; the mask is worn at the cost of the face. The Qur'an states the same reversal elsewhere with the verb turned around: *"Surely the hypocrites seek to deceive Allah, but He outwits them"* (4:142) — the attempted deception returns as their being outwitted, and the sūrah al-Nisāʾ adds the detail of prayer performed half-heartedly, purely to be seen. The mechanism deserves precision: the self-deception is not an accidental side effect but the substance of the act. To live inside a false self-presentation is to lose access to the true self; the hypocrite's most successful deception always has exactly one victim, and he is standing behind the mask.

**The Recurring Grammar of the Counter-Plan**

Where creatures plot, the Qur'an repeatedly answers with divine plotting, and the pairing is a fixed motif: *"They planned, but Allah also planned. And Allah is the best of planners"* (8:30). The exegetical lists run the motif through the Book — in Yūnus, al-Raʿd, Ibrāhīm, al-Naḥl, al-Naml — always in the same shape: a human scheme, a divine response, the response described as better rather than greater. The comparison is qualitative. Human planning works by concealment and speed; divine planning needs neither, since it operates through truth taking its time, letting the scheme unfold until its own logic undoes it. To call Allah the best of planners is not to accuse Him of trickery — the word in these verses carries the sense of arranging outcomes the arranger of evil did not intend. The hypocrites' deception in Madina did eventually undo itself exactly this way: no counter-mask was needed, only events, and each event pulled another thread from the costume.

**Wa-lā Yashʿurūn: The Blindness Built Into the Trick**

The verse closes on a verb of perception: *yashʿurūn*, from *shuʿūr* — the finest grade of awareness, sensation so subtle that Arabic connects it with *shaʿr*, hair, the sensitivity of skin that feels a hair's touch. Its negation is therefore not "they do not know" but "they do not feel it" — the reversal is happening at a level below their noticing. This is the built-in blindness of self-deception: the trick requires the trickster to half-believe the performance in order to sustain it, and the half-belief anesthetizes precisely the faculty that could catch him out. The closing clause reads as diagnosis rather than insult. A deceiver who perceives his own deception is in torment and usually stops; the hypocrite's peace of mind is the symptom of the disease. The verse has located the mechanism by which a person can spend years defrauding himself and call the years successful — perception itself has been spent in buying the mask.

**Concealment, Lying, and Deception: The Passage's Taxonomy**

Read against its neighbours, this verse advances the exposure by one precise step, and the steps form a taxonomy. Verse 8 recorded the lie — saying with the tongue what the heart withholds; that is *kidhb*, falsehood in speech. Verse 9 records the operation — active double-dealing directed at Allah and the community; that is *khidāʿ*, deception as a practice with objects and goals. Between and beneath both runs *kitmān*, concealment: hiding what one knows, which the sūrah will later charge against People of the Book who suppress revelation. The distinction matters because the sins have different centers. The lie is a sentence; the deception is a career. A person may lie once from fear and remain sound; a person who organizes his life around managing other people's beliefs has built a structure that now builds him. The passage proceeds act by act — claim, operation, disease — assembling the type out of behaviors before it ever needs the name.

**What the Community Is Owed by All This**

The believers were the second object of the deception, and the verse quietly assigns them their posture. No community can fully screen hearts — the interior belongs to Allah, who alone bears witness that the hypocrites are liars (63:1). What the community receives instead is pattern recognition: the signs by which the hidden type leaks — the claim without cost, the deception whose only victim is the deceiver, the absence of felt awareness. With those signs the believers are neither naive nor inquisitorial; they hold the outward by the profession and leave the inward to its Knower, while Allah undertakes the counter-plan. The verse's final irony arms them with patience: a deception that devours only its author needs no hurried exposure. It will ripen. The community's task is to remain what the deceiver cannot fake — and the following verse explains why the faking itself is a sickness that grows.

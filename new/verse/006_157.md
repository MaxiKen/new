## Sūrah al-Anʿām 6:157

> Nor can you say, “If only the Scriptures had been revealed to us, we would have been better guided than they.” Now there has come to you from your Lord a clear proof—a guide and mercy. Who then does more wrong than those who deny Allah’s revelations and turn away from them? We will reward those who turn away from Our revelations with a dreadful punishment for turning away. Waiting for the Apocalypse?

**Expanded Commentary**

**The Clear Proof, the Guide, and the Mercy**

Now there has come to you from your Lord a clear proof — a guide and mercy. The sentence states what the audience has received, and the three terms it uses describe the revelation in the language the sūrah has already applied to the earlier scriptures. The Book of Moses was called a guide and a mercy (6:154), and the Book revealed to the Prophet was called blessed in the verse before this one (6:155). What the sentence states is that the proof has come from the Lord and that it carries both guidance and mercy.

The Book describes its own revelation in the same terms elsewhere. The sūrah of the Table opens its address to the People of the Book with the announcement that a light and a clear Book have come from God (5:15), and the sūrah of the Story calls the book given to Moses an insight, a guide, and a mercy. It belongs to that family of statements, and it places the Qur'an within the same provision that the earlier communities had received.

**The Question About the Greatest Wrong**

Who then does more wrong than those who deny Allah's revelations and turn away from them? The question is the sūrah's repeated formula, and it appears in the passages about fabricating lies against God (6:21), about claiming to have received revelation (6:93), and about inventing dietary rules (6:144). The form in front of us attaches the formula to the denial of the signs and to the act of turning away from them.

The Book uses the same question in other sūrahs. The sūrah of the Adoration asks who does more wrong than the person who is reminded of the revelations of his Lord and then turns away from them, and it states that God will take retribution from the guilty (32:22); the sūrah of the Cave asks the same question about those who turn away from the signs and forget what their hands have sent ahead (18:57). The offence is stated here in the context of the Book that has just come.

**The Punishment Attached to Turning Away**

We will reward those who turn away from Our revelations with a dreadful punishment for turning away. The sentence states the consequence, and it attaches the punishment to the act by which the offence is committed. The Book's account of recompense follows the conduct it answers, and the sūrah of Consultation states that the reward of an evil deed is its equivalent (42:40).

The Book's statements about turning away from the signs run through the sūrah. It has described the people who turn away from the signs as unable to see them (6:46), it has described the condition of those whose hearts are turned aside after their first refusal (6:110), and it has stated that the evil of the people who turn away will be answered. The arrangement is stated here in its final form, and the phrase for turning away names the offence twice in a single sentence.

**What the Verse Says About the Audience's Refusal**

The verse answers the excuse that the previous verse removed. The audience had claimed that revelation had been given to others and that they had no access to it; the verse in front of us states that the proof has now come from its Lord and that the people who deny it and turn from it are the ones who do the greatest wrong. The two verses together leave the audience with the Book in its hands and the responsibility for its response.

The Book's treatment of the matter elsewhere states the same conclusion. The sūrah of the Cattle has said that the signs have been made clear for people who know (6:97) and that the insights have come to the audience from its Lord (6:104), and it has stated that whoever chooses to see does so for his own good while whoever chooses to be blind does so to his own loss. The consequence of the refusal is stated here in the strongest terms the sūrah uses.

**The Turning Away as the Central Offence**

The sūrah's account of the audience's refusal has been built on the language of turning. The verse about the signs said that they turned away from the signs while they were able to see them (6:46), the verse about the hearts said that God turned their hearts and eyes aside after their first refusal (6:110), and the sentence in front of us names turning away as the offence for which the punishment is given. The three passages describe the same movement in the audience's relation to the message.

The Book's use of the term in its other sūrahs gives the same picture. The sūrah of the Cave describes those who turn away from the signs and forget what they have sent ahead, and the sūrah of the Adoration describes the person who is reminded and turns away. The verse in front of us completes the sūrah's account of that movement by stating its outcome.

**The Place of the Verse in the Passage**

The verse completes the passage that began with the declaration that the Book is blessed and the command to follow it (6:155), and it hands the reader into the final verses in which the coming of the signs is described. The sūrah's argument with the audience is now closed: the Book has come, the excuse has been removed, and the consequence of refusing has been stated.

The verses that follow describe the people who wait for the angels or for the Lord or for some of the major signs, and they state that belief will not benefit those who did not believe before that moment (6:158). The verse in front of us supplies the ground on which the warning rests, and the passage that follows states the terms in which the waiting is answered.

**The Transition to the Next Verse**

The next verse asks whether the audience is waiting for the coming of the angels, or for the Lord Himself, or for some of the major signs, and it states that on the day the signs arrive the belief of those who did not believe earlier will not benefit them, before instructing the Prophet to say that both sides are waiting (6:158). The passage continues the sūrah's treatment of the demand for signs, and it states the point at which the opportunity closes.

The sūrah then moves to the verses about the divisions among the communities and about the reward of deeds (6:159–160), and it closes with the Prophet's declaration that God has guided him to the straight path (6:161). This sentence stands at the beginning of that closing movement, and the reader who has followed the argument now has the whole case before him.

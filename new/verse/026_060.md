## Sūrah al-Shuʿarāʾ 26:60

> And so they pursued them at sunrise.

**Expanded Commentary**

**The Pursuit Begins in the Fullness of Day**

The Egyptians pursue the Israelites at sunrise. The timing gives the chase a clear visual setting. The Israelites have left at night under God's command, while the pursuing force moves when daylight makes tracking easier. Pharaoh's host is no longer searching through darkness. It advances with the confidence that visibility, numbers, and speed will return the fugitives to its control.

Sunrise also creates a contrast between the two movements. The Israelites depart quietly under divine instruction; the Egyptians follow openly under the authority of a ruler who has refused to release them. Night protected the vulnerable community, while day reveals the scale of the force behind it. The verse is brief because the pursuit itself is the point: Pharaoh's refusal has become physical and direct.

**Daylight Does Not Give the Pursuers Divine Sight**

The Egyptians can see the route and organize their movement, but physical visibility is not the same as understanding. They know where the Israelites have gone, yet they do not understand what the departure means or who governs its outcome. Sunrise illuminates the road without illuminating the heart of the host.

This distinction continues the chapter's contrast between appearance and truth. Pharaoh's people have watched signs in the court and still chosen a false interpretation. Now they see the fleeing community and read the sight as an opportunity to recover property. The light of day cannot correct a moral blindness that the ruler has taught them to share.

**Pursuit Shows That Pharaoh Has Chosen Possession Over Mercy**

The Egyptians do not follow because the Israelites have attacked them or threatened their homes. They follow because Pharaoh will not accept their departure. The movement of the army therefore reveals the ruler's priority. He values control over the people more than peace with the people. The possibility of letting them go has been rejected, and pursuit becomes the proof.

A ruler may call such pursuit security, but the sequence tells another story. The Israelites were commanded to leave, Pharaoh mobilized the cities, and the host now follows. Each step arises from the refusal to recognize God's ownership. The army's movement is not a neutral reaction. It is the enforcement of an unjust claim.

**Sunrise Makes the Power Imbalance Visible**

At dawn the Israelites can be seen by the pursuing force, but they can also be seen in their vulnerability. The contrast between a departing community and an organized host becomes plain. Pharaoh's side has the apparent advantage of equipment, numbers, and daylight. The verse does not hide that advantage or pretend that deliverance feels easy to those being chased.

This honesty strengthens the meaning of trust. Faith does not require the Israelites to imagine that the army is small or harmless. It requires them to know that visible advantage is not the final measure of power. The scene will soon place the two groups in direct relation, but already the reader knows that the pursuit is moving within the plan announced to Moses.

**The Pursuit Fulfills the Warning**

God had told Moses that the people would be pursued. The sunrise pursuit fulfills that warning. Foreknowledge does not remove fear, but it prevents the people from interpreting the danger as proof that the night departure was mistaken. The event occurs as God said it would, which means the next stage must also be understood within His promise.

A warning can serve mercy by preparing people for what they will face. The Israelites are not promised a journey without resistance. They are told that resistance will come, and they leave anyway. That obedience matters. They do not turn back simply because Pharaoh's host appears behind them. The pursuit becomes part of the path rather than evidence that the path was wrong.

**The Chasers Move Toward the Consequence of Their Choice**

Pharaoh's army believes it is moving toward victory. In the narrative's deeper structure, it is moving toward judgment. The host's confidence does not make its direction false. It has followed the ruler's command into a situation where the resources that protected the tyrants—gardens, springs, treasure, and rank—cannot help them.

This does not turn the Egyptians into helpless objects. They chose to pursue, and their choice carries consequence. Divine sovereignty and human responsibility meet in the movement. God knows where the pursuit leads, while the pursuers remain accountable for why they entered it. Sunrise makes their route visible; it does not make their decision just.

**The Time of Day Highlights the Difference Between Escape and Chase**

Night departure and sunrise pursuit create a rhythm of concealment followed by exposure. The Israelites are allowed to leave before the full force of Pharaoh can gather. The Egyptians follow when the light favors their search. That difference shows the practical care in God's command and the urgency of the ruler's response.

The verse does not praise darkness as a moral good or daylight as a moral evil. It uses time to show how God protects the weak and how the powerful mobilize. The oppressed receive a window in which to move, while the oppressor uses the morning to reclaim them. Both groups are now on the road, but they are not moving under the same authority.

**A Visible Pursuit Cannot Cancel an Invisible Promise**

The Israelites can see the army, while the promise of deliverance must be held in faith. That tension is the spiritual center of the verse. The immediate sight is frightening and seems to contradict freedom. Yet God's command has not been withdrawn. The pursuit is visible; the divine plan is known through revelation.

The line ends before describing the people's reaction, preserving the pressure of the moment. The reader is left with the army at sunrise and the vulnerable community ahead. What follows will show how fear speaks when visible power closes in. For this verse, the essential truth is that Pharaoh's pursuit has begun, but pursuit is not possession and daylight is not victory.

## Sūrah al-Ḥajj 22:61

> That is because Allah causes the night to merge into the day, and the day into the night. Indeed, Allah is All-Hearing, All-Seeing.

**Expanded Commentary**

**What "That" Refers To**

*Dhālika bi-anna Allāha yūliju al-layla fī al-nahāri* — that is because God makes the night enter into the day.

The demonstrative points back to the promise of verse 60: God will help the person who has been wronged twice. The ground given for that promise is not military, political or even moral. It is the daily alternation of light and darkness.

The logic is worth stating plainly. A God who can reverse the condition of the world twice a day, without interruption and without effort, is not going to find the reversal of a community's fortunes difficult. The promise is being grounded in a power that is demonstrated on schedule, in front of everyone.

**The Structure of the Four Causal Verses**

Verses 61 to 64 each begin with the same particle, *anna*, and each supplies a ground for the promise that precedes them. The sequence is worth seeing whole before taking the verses one at a time.

The first ground is the alternation of night and day (v. 61). The second is that God is the Truth and everything invoked besides Him is void (v. 62). The third is the rain that greens the earth (v. 63). The fourth is God's ownership of everything and His freedom from need (v. 64).

Two of the four are about power and two are about what God is in Himself. The pairing is deliberate: a promise grounded only in power would leave a reader wondering about the will to use it, and a promise grounded only in God's nature would leave him wondering about the capacity. The sūrah supplies both, in alternation, the way the night enters the day.

**The Verb: Entering, Not Replacing**

*Yūliju* means to insert, to cause to enter, to make one thing pass into another. It is not the verb for replacing or for following.

The choice is precise, and the parallel verses use the same word: *“Do you not see that Allah causes the night to merge into the day and the day into the night, and has subjected the sun and the moon, each orbiting for an appointed term”* (31:29), and in the fuller statement, *“He wraps the night around the day, and wraps the day around the night”* (39:5).

What is described is a continuous interpenetration rather than a switch. The night does not end and the day begin; each is always entering the other, and the boundary between them is a gradient that is constantly moving. The same verb appears in the verse that describes the provision of life from death, which is the larger point: the God who blends night into day is the God who brings the living out of the dead.

Sūrah Āl ʿImrān makes the connection explicit: *“You cause the night to pass into the day and the day into the night. You bring forth the living from the dead and the dead from the living. And You provide for whoever You will without limit”* (3:27).

**Why This Sign Above All Others**

The Qur'an returns to the alternation of night and day more than to any other natural phenomenon, and the reason is not that it is the most spectacular sign available.

It is the most *available*. Nobody is excluded from it by geography, wealth, literacy or health. A person who has never seen the sea nonetheless experiences this twice daily. The sign cannot be dismissed as local, obscure or reserved for specialists, and it cannot be forgotten, since it structures every human life.

Sūrah Yūnus states the argumentative function in a single line: *“Surely in the alternation of the day and the night, and in all that Allah has created in the heavens and the earth, there are truly signs for those mindful ˹of Him˺”* (10:6). Sūrah al-Furqān adds the purpose: *“And He is the One Who causes the day and the night to alternate, ˹as a sign˺ for whoever desires to be mindful or to be grateful”* (25:62).

**What the Sign Establishes**

The transition from night to day is not a proof of the Resurrection in the way that the reviving field of verse 5 is. It establishes something prior: that the conditions of the world are not fixed.

A person being oppressed is apt to experience his situation as permanent — as though the present arrangement were the nature of things rather than an arrangement. The daily reversal is the standing refutation of that. The world's most reliable structure is one in which darkness is always being replaced by light, and it happens whether or not anyone deserves it.

That is the specific comfort the verse offers. The promise of help in verse 60 is attached to a demonstration that conditions reverse.

**"Hearing, Seeing"**

*Wa-anna Allāha samīʿun baṣīr* — and God is Hearing, Seeing.

The two attributes are the ones the Qur'an invokes when it is talking about justice, and their presence here is not generic. The verse's subject is a person who has been wronged, retaliated proportionately, and been wronged again.

Such a person's first need is not power but witness. He needs the injury to have been registered, and he needs it registered accurately — the full extent, the sequence, who started it. *Samīʿ* covers what was said and *baṣīr* covers what was done, and between them nothing in the case is missing from the record.

**The Verse in the Chain**

Verses 61 to 64 are a chain of four verses each beginning with a causal particle: because God merges night into day (v. 61), because God is the Truth (v. 62), because God sends rain (v. 63), because to Him belongs everything (v. 64).

Each supplies a ground for what was promised before it, and the four together rest the sūrah's argument on the divine attributes rather than on circumstances. The help promised to the wronged is not a forecast about the balance of power. It is a deduction from what God is.

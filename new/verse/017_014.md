## Sūrah al-Isrāʾ 17:14

> **˹And it will be said,˺ “Read your record. You ˹alone˺ are sufficient this Day to take account of yourself.”**

**Expanded Commentary**

**Read Your Record**

Iqra' kitābaka — read your book. Iqra' from q-r-ʾ, to read, recite, same root as Qur'an, recitation, and iqra' first revealed to Prophet Muhammad in cave, command to read. Kitābaka, your book, your record, from k-t-b, to write, book. The command iqra' kitābaka is said to each person on Day of Judgment, as if it will be said, read your record. Al-Ṭabarī explains that on Day We say to person read your book, your record of deeds that was bound to neck and brought forth laid open. Al-Qurṭubī says this is address to each person to read own book, meaning to examine deeds, to confront own actions. The imperative iqra' indicates that person is able to read, even if illiterate in worldly life, on Day he reads his record, because record laid open is made readable by Allah. The fact that first revelation iqra' and Day command iqra' kitābaka both about reading links worldly reading of Qur'an to Hereafter reading of record.

**Sufficient Yourself As Reckoner**

Kafā bi-nafsika al-yawma ʿalayka ḥasīban — sufficient yourself today against you as reckoner. Kafā from k-f-y, to suffice, be enough, as in verse 17 sufficient Lord as Aware Seeing. Bi-nafsika, with yourself, yourself, from n-f-s, self, soul. Al-yawma, today, this Day, Day of Judgment. ʿAlayka, against you, upon you, indicating reckoning against self. Ḥasīban from ḥ-s-b, to reckon, calculate, account, same root as ḥisāb, calculation, and ḥasīb, reckoner, as verse 12 about knowing number years and calculation. Al-Ṭabarī says sufficient yourself today as reckoner against yourself, meaning you are sufficient to reckon yourself, you do not need other reckoner, your own self knows what you did when you read book laid open. Al-Rāzī explains that kafā bi-nafsika ʿalayka ḥasīban indicates justice and self-conviction: you yourself reckon, you cannot claim you did not know, because you read your own book laid open, spread open, and you know deeds.

**Why Self Reckoning Is Most Just**

Why self reckoning? Because it is most just and most convincing: when person reads own record laid open and reckons himself, he cannot deny, cannot claim injustice. Al-Zamakhsharī notes that phrase kafā bi-nafsika indicates that self is sufficient as reckoner, no need for witnesses beyond self, because book is laid open and self knows deeds intimately. This is also mercy: person sees own deeds clearly and understands justice of judgment. The verse also implies that in worldly life, person should reckon self before being reckoned, as ʿUmar ibn al-Khaṭṭāb said reckon yourselves before you are reckoned, weigh yourselves before you are weighed, because on Day you will be sufficient as reckoner, so prepare by self-accountability now. This practice of muḥāsabah, self-reckoning, is central to spiritual training.

**Connection To Destiny Bound To Neck**

Verse 13 said every person's destiny bound to neck, record laid open brought forth on Day. Verse 14 says read your book, sufficient yourself today as reckoner. The connection is that record bound to neck in world becomes book laid open in Hereafter that person reads and reckons himself. The destiny that was bound, ṭā'ir, becomes readable book. The sequence shows responsibility and justice: your deeds bound to neck, recorded, laid open, you read, you reckon. No injustice: you see own book, you are sufficient as reckoner. This also links to principle no bearer bears burden of another: each reads own book, reckons own self.

**Link To Previous Chapter About Book Not Omitting**

Previous chapter said book placed and criminals fearful saying woe this book does not omit small nor great but has counted, they find what they did present, and Lord wrongs not. This verse says read your book, sufficient yourself today as reckoner, similar theme: book does not omit, you read, you reckon, you cannot deny. The sūrah emphasizes record and self-reckoning to encourage following most upright guidance that leads to good record and mighty reward, and avoiding evil that leads to painful punishment. The fact that book does not omit small nor great means self-reckoning will be detailed.

**Linguistic Note On Iqra And Hasib**

Iqra' from q-r-ʾ, to read, recite, from same root as Qur'an, recitation, and qirā'ah, reading, and maqra'ah, place of reading. Kitāb from k-t-b, book, record, writing, from kataba, to write. Nafs from n-f-s, self, soul, from nafasa, to breathe. Yawm from y-w-m, day. Ḥasīb from ḥ-s-b, to reckon, calculate, account, from same root as ḥisāb, calculation, as in verse 12 about knowing number years and calculation, and as in reckoning deeds. Kafā bi-, sufficient with, enough, from kafā, to suffice. The verse's brevity — read your book, sufficient yourself today against you as reckoner — with command and statement of sufficiency, conveys justice and self-awareness: you read, you reckon, you are enough as reckoner against yourself, no need for other.

**How This Verse Teaches Self-Accountability In World**

This verse teaches self-accountability in worldly life: in world, reckon yourself before you are reckoned on Day when you will be sufficient as reckoner. Al-Qurṭubī says believer should daily read his own book of deeds in worldly sense, i.e., reflect what he did, and reckon himself, repenting for evil and increasing good, so that when book laid open on Day he is pleased to read it. The sūrah's moral code that follows provides what good deeds to do that will make self-reckoning easy: honoring parents, giving relatives due, not spending wastefully, not killing children, not approaching adultery, honoring pledges, measuring fully, etc. The verse also teaches that Qur'an guides to most upright, so reading it in world helps you read your record with ease in Hereafter, because it teaches what to write in book.

**A Lesson On Reading And Reckoning**

Iqra' — read — first word revealed to Prophet, and here addressed to every person on Day to read own record. The parallel suggests that reading Qur'an in world, which guides to most upright and explains everything in detail, prepares you to read your record in Hereafter with success. The person who reads Qur'an and follows it will have book laid open showing good deeds and will reckon himself with ease, while who does not believe in Hereafter and prays for evil out of haste will find painful record and self-reckoning difficult. The command read your book therefore invites reflection: what are you writing in your book today via deeds bound to neck? Are you preparing for easy self-reckoning by following most upright guidance, seeking bounty with gratitude, knowing calculation, being patient not hasty?

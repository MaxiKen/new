## Sūrah an-Naml 27:83

> ˹Watch for˺ the Day We will gather from every faith-community a group of those who denied Our revelations, and they will be driven in ranks.

**Expanded Commentary**

**A Selection, Not a Sweep**

The verse describes a gathering, and the striking thing about it is how narrow the selection is. From every community a *fawj* is assembled — a contingent, a party — consisting of those who denied the signs.

The Qur'an elsewhere describes the resurrection as universal, with everyone raised and everyone present. What this verse depicts is something different and more specific: a delegation drawn out of each nation for a particular purpose. The scene that follows in the next verse makes the purpose clear. They are brought forward to be questioned, one group at a time, and the questioning is about what they did with the evidence they were given. This is a procedure, not a stampede.

**"From Every Community"**

*Min kulli ummah* — from every nation. No community is exempt from producing such a contingent, and the phrase includes the ones that received prophets and the ones the chapter has just been discussing.

That universality does two things. It rules out the idea that denial was a peculiarity of Arabian paganism, which would let the Meccan audience treat the whole passage as a description of themselves and nobody else. It also rules out the inverse comfort, that membership in a community with a scripture provides cover. The previous verses addressed the Children of Israel and their disputes; this one says that every nation, the scriptural ones included, contributes to the group being assembled. The chapter has already made the point in narrative by pairing Thamūd with Lūṭ's people — one sent a prophet from among them, one visited by messengers — and giving both the same outcome.

**"Driven in Ranks"**

The verb is *yūzaʿūn*, and readers of this chapter have met it before. It was used in verse 17 for Sulaymān's hosts of jinn, men and birds, who were marshalled into ordered ranks before him.

Al-Ṭabarī glosses it in both places as corralling or grouping together, holding the front back so the rear can catch up. The reuse of a rare word across one chapter is unlikely to be accidental, and the effect is a deliberate parallel with the difference left visible. Sulaymān's ranks were an army assembled in glory, marching under a king who thanked God for his share of all things. These ranks are assembled for interrogation. The same formation, the same word, and two entirely different meanings depending on who is doing the marshalling and where the column is headed.

**Order as the Characteristic of the Scene**

The image is not chaotic. Nothing in the verse suggests panic or confusion; the deniers are collected, sorted by community, and moved in formation. That orderliness is worth pausing on.

Descriptions of judgment in popular imagination tend toward tumult, and the Qur'an does use such imagery elsewhere — mountains moved, seas boiling, people scattered like moths. This verse does the opposite. It depicts an administrative process conducted with precision, which is in keeping with what the chapter has just said about a record in which everything hidden is written. A proceeding based on a complete document does not need to be frantic. The calm of the description is itself an argument about the quality of the information behind it.

**"Watch for the Day"**

The translation supplies an imperative that the Arabic carries through its particle: *wa yawma naḥshuru* — and the Day We gather. The construction points forward to an appointed time and asks the hearer to hold it in view.

That is what the chapter has been asking since verse 3, where the believers were identified as those certain about the Hereafter. Between then and now the sūrah has described four nations that did not hold the day in view, an idolatrous queen who came to, and a Meccan audience demanding its date. This verse gives them a picture of the date they asked about. They wanted to know when; they are shown what, and the what is a scene in which they are among those being counted out of their own nation and put in line.

**A Scene Built for the People Who Demanded a Date**

Twelve verses earlier the Meccans asked when the promise would arrive. The chapter has answered them in stages: perhaps part of it is close behind you, then a statement about the record of hidden things, and now a picture of the occasion itself.

What the sequence does is replace the question. They wanted a position on a calendar, which the chapter has consistently refused to supply. What they are given is a description of their own place in the event, which is information of a more useful kind. A man told the date of a trial learns something about his schedule; a man shown himself in the dock learns something about his situation. The chapter has used this substitution before, answering the demand for a proof with a demand for theirs, and answering the request for timing with a statement about nearness.

**The Contingent Is Drawn From Their Own People**

There is a detail in the phrasing that would have landed sharply on the first audience. The group is taken *from* a community — pulled out of it, separated from the rest of their nation.

Tribal Mecca understood identity as collective. A man's standing, protection and fate ran with his clan, and the worst thing that could happen to him was to be cut off from it. The chapter has already played on this at verse 56, where Lūṭ's opponents proposed expelling his household from the town, and at verse 57, where the rescue was of a household as a unit. This verse describes a separation that runs the other way and that no clan can prevent. Belonging, which was the ultimate security in that society, does not determine which contingent a person is counted into.

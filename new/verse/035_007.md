## Sūrah Fāṭir 35:7

> Those who disbelieve will have a severe punishment. But those who believe and do good will have forgiveness and a great reward.

**Expanded Commentary**

**Two Allegiances Receive Two Outcomes**

The verse sets two groups beside each other. Disbelief is paired with severe punishment, while belief is joined to good action, forgiveness, and a great reward. The arrangement is not a claim that every human story can be understood by a quick glance at worldly fortune. It is a statement about the final moral structure of reality. Allegiance to falsehood and allegiance to Allah do not lead to the same destination, even when their earthly circumstances temporarily overlap.

The contrast follows the warning about Satan. A person takes Satan as an enemy by turning toward faith and letting that faith govern conduct. Belief is not an inward label detached from the limbs. Nor are actions presented as a substitute for belief. The two are joined because faith gives action its direction and action gives faith visible truth. The Qur'an frequently places belief beside righteous deeds, making the combination a settled pattern rather than an exceptional formula.

**Severe Punishment Answers Deliberate Refusal**

The punishment is described as severe, but the verse does not portray Allah's judgment as arbitrary. Disbelief here is persistent refusal of the truth, not an accident of vocabulary or a passing moment of confusion. It includes a will that turns away after signs, rejects accountability, and treats created powers as rivals to Allah. The severity of the outcome corresponds to the depth of the rupture: a servant refuses the One who gave existence and then chooses to live as though no return were coming.

This warning keeps theology from becoming a decorative subject. What a person believes about Allah shapes what he permits himself to do, whom he fears, and what he regards as success. Denial may produce visible wealth or influence, but those goods cannot erase the spiritual meaning of refusal. The verse does not command believers to pronounce final judgment on individuals. It establishes Allah's judgment as serious and certain, leaving the hidden details of each case to His knowledge.

**Faith Must Take the Form of Good Work**

The believers are not described by belief alone. They believe and do good. Good work includes worship, justice, honesty, patience, mercy, and the fulfillment of obligations. Its range is wide because faith addresses the whole person. A prayer that trains no humility, a claim of devotion that leaves injustice untouched, or a religious identity used to excuse harm has failed to display the fruit named here.

The pairing also protects deeds from becoming self-advertisement. A good act is not merely something that earns social approval; it is something aligned with Allah's commands and offered with sincerity. The Qur'an says in 29:7 that those who believe and act rightly will have their wrongs covered and will be rewarded according to the best of what they did. Action therefore matters both as obedience and as evidence that belief has entered the will. The act does not purchase Allah's favor as a commercial exchange; it is the servant's response to grace.

**Forgiveness Comes Before the Great Reward**

Forgiveness is mentioned before reward. The order speaks to the human condition. Even a believer who does good remains in need of Allah's pardon for weakness, error, mixed motives, and neglected duties. The reward is not founded on a record that has no stain. Allah covers what would otherwise expose the servant and then gives beyond what the servant could claim by merit.

This ordering also changes the emotional tone of obedience. A person does not have to choose between fear of sin and hope for generosity. Forgiveness removes the barrier of past failure, while reward opens a future larger than bare acquittal. The Qur'an describes the reward of believers as gardens in 18:107, but the verse here leaves its greatness unbounded. The phrase allows the believer to expect not only safety from punishment but a generosity whose measure belongs to Allah.

No good act is too small when it is directed to Allah, and no claim of faith is safe when it is used to excuse deliberate harm. The promise of forgiveness encourages a servant who has fallen to return rather than to abandon obedience. It also teaches that the path is renewed through repeated choices, not secured by a single public declaration.

Forgiveness is not a license to postpone reform. It is an invitation to begin, repair what can be repaired, and keep asking Allah for help. The great reward belongs to a path of belief and action that continues through weakness, correction, and renewed effort.

The promise therefore meets both the hopeful beginner and the struggling believer.

**The Contrast Is a Call to Choose Now**

The two outcomes are stated in the present tense of certainty, but the choices are made within time. The verse therefore does not invite passive speculation about one's final category. It calls the hearer to examine allegiance, repent from denial, and make faith visible through good action. The warning is not a reason to despair; it is a mercy that identifies the road before its end. The promise is not a reason for complacency; it is an encouragement to keep doing good while asking for forgiveness.

In this balance, the verse offers a complete moral map. The enemy's road ends in the Blaze because it separates the creature from the Creator. The road of faith joins acknowledgment to conduct and receives both pardon and gift. Worldly appearances may conceal the difference for a time, but Allah's judgment will uncover it. The wise response is neither pride in deeds nor indifference to them, but grateful effort under the shelter of divine forgiveness.

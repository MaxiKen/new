## Sūrah al-Nisāʾ 4:63

> ˹Only˺ Allah knows what is in their hearts. So turn away from them, caution them, and give them advice that will shake their very souls.

### Expanded Commentary

**Advice that reaches the conscience**

Allah knows what is in their hearts, so the Prophet is instructed to turn away from their immediate performance, caution them, and address them with words that penetrate their souls. The three movements are balanced. Turning away avoids rewarding manipulation; warning makes the moral danger clear; penetrating advice leaves a door open for repentance.

**The limits of human diagnosis**

Because God alone knows the heart, believers must not claim certainty about hidden hypocrisy. They may identify public contradiction and protect the community from harm, but they should avoid declaring an individual’s final spiritual state. The Prophet was given revelation about particular cases; ordinary people do not possess that access.

**Speech with moral force**

Advice that “reaches” a person is not necessarily loud or humiliating. It names the wrong accurately, explains its consequence, and speaks in a way capable of awakening responsibility. Public shaming often hardens the heart; private counsel can create the space in which truth is admitted. The verse’s aim is reform and protection together.

**Advice should preserve a route back**

A warning can be firm without making repentance socially impossible. Correct the conduct, protect those at risk, and speak words that allow the person to recognize himself honestly. Public humiliation may satisfy anger while private truth has a better chance of producing reform.

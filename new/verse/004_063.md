## Sūrah al-Nisāʾ 4:63

> ˹Only˺ Allah knows what is in their hearts. So turn away from them, caution them, and give them advice that will shake their very souls.

**Expanded Commentary**

**Allah Knows What Is in Their Hearts**

The clause *ulāʾika alladhīna yaʿlamu Allāhu mā fī qulūbihim* — only Allah knows what is in their hearts — follows the oath of the hypocrites that they intended nothing but goodwill and reconciliation after disaster struck because of what their hands put forward (4:62). The oaths were *yaḥlifūna bi Allāhi* — swearing by God — yet the verse says the hearts behind the oaths are known to Him. The sūrah has prepared that knowledge: He is ever Watchful over you (4:1), sufficient as Reckoner (4:6), Witness over all things (4:33), All-Knowing, All-Aware where mediators desire harmony (4:35), All-Hearing, All-Seeing over trusts and judgments (4:58), and has perfect knowledge of them (4:39). To say He knows what is in hearts is to say oaths do not change the record; the hands’ referral to false judges (4:60) and the stubborn turning from the Book and Messenger (4:61) are already entered. The Qur’an elsewhere says that God knows the treachery of eyes and what breasts conceal, and that He knows the hypocrites’ excuse before it is spoken. Here that knowledge is placed at the head of the counsel, so that what follows is done with clarity: the Prophet ﷺ and the community are not told to uncover hearts by force; they are told that hearts are already known to the One who will reckon.

**Turn Away From Them**

The first instruction *fa aʿriḍ ʿanhum* — so turn away from them — was read as turning away from their oaths, their company where it seeks *ṭāghūt*, and their attempt to draw the Prophet ﷺ into endorsing their excuse. *Iʿrāḍ* — to turn aside, to withdraw face — matches the earlier scene where they turned away stubbornly — *yaṣuddūna ʿanka ṣudūdā* (4:61) — and answers it with a measured withdrawal rather than with harsh exposure. The sūrah has used *aʿriḍ* for those who repent and mend after discipline — *relieve them* (4:16) — showing that turning away can be mercy where amendment appears. Here turning away is discipline for those whose oaths are known to be hollow; it denies the social reward of being taken at their word while disaster is still fresh, without yet removing the chance to hear. The community that was told to refer disputes to God and Messenger as best outcome (4:59) is here told not to refer to the hypocrites’ oaths as evidence; the referral goes upward, not outward to their sworn excuses. Turning away therefore protects the community from being softened by oaths that the All-Knowing has already weighed.

**Caution Them**

The second instruction *wa ʿiẓhum* — and caution them, exhort them — adds the voice of warning. *Waʿẓ* — admonition that reaches the heart, reminding of limits, Gardens, and Fire — was explained as private caution that names what was done — seeking false judges though commanded to reject them, turning from the revelations and the Messenger — and what follows if it persists — condemnation with no helper (4:52), blaze sufficient (4:55), Fire with skin renewed (4:56). The sūrah has shown caution as part of handling ill-conduct in the home — advise them first (4:34) — and as part of the noble exhortation to return trusts and judge with fairness (4:58). Here caution is directed at the community’s internal ill-conduct, where hypocrisy seeks to make the Book one judge among others. To caution is to bring the hypocrite back to the referral that defines belief: come to what God revealed and to the Messenger (4:61). The caution is not public shaming but moral alert, because the One who knows hearts also wills to turn toward those who turn (4:26–27) and to lighten because weakness is real (4:28). Caution keeps that turn possible.

**Advice That Will Shake Their Souls**

The third instruction *wa qul lahum fī anfusihim qawlan balīghā* — and give them advice that will shake their very souls — was read as speech that reaches inside, *fī anfusihim* — in their selves — and is *balīgh* — reaching, penetrating, eloquent in the sense of arriving. It is not a slogan for crowds; it is the word that finds the hypocrite alone and tells him what God knows is in his heart. Early authorities described *qawlan balīghā* as speech that makes the hearer fear the standing before witnesses where no statement can be hidden (4:41–42), that recalls the atom’s weight that is never wronged but multiplied (4:40), and that reminds that soon repentance for ignorance is accepted while deferral until death is present is not (4:17–18). The sūrah has praised *qawlan maʿrūfā* — kind speech at distribution (4:8) — and *qawlan sadīdā* — straight speech (4:9), and here it adds penetrating speech that moves the private self. The progression of three — turn away, caution, penetrate — was stressed as graduated response: withdraw social endorsement, warn of limits and recompense, then speak to the soul that alone can choose belief before the disaster becomes the greater one. *Qawlan balīghā* is not harsh for harshness; it is precise, as a physician’s word that reaches the illness, because the illness is in the heart.

**A Method for Hearts That Swear**

Placed between the oath of goodwill after disaster (4:62) and the promise that those who wronged themselves and came seeking forgiveness would find God ever Accepting, Most Merciful if the Messenger prayed for them (4:64), this verse gives the method for hearts that swear. It tells the Prophet ﷺ not to be taken by the oath, not to expose vindictively, but to combine distance, warning, and inward speech. The sūrah has shown that God is sufficient as Guardian and Helper (4:45) and that He knows best who enemies are; here that sufficiency becomes practice: the community leans on the One who knows hearts while it turns, cautions, and speaks penetratingly. The three steps keep the door open where the hypocrites’ own turning had closed it stubbornly (4:61), but they keep it open only as a path back to the Book and Messenger, not as a path back to *ṭāghūt*. For the weak for whom burdens were lightened, the verse is also a mercy: the advice that shakes the soul is the same that invites the soul to be among those who believe and do good and are admitted to Gardens with pure spouses and vast shade (4:57), after being told that avoidance of major sins covers lesser ones and opens honour (4:31).

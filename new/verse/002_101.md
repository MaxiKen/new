## Sūrah al-Baqarah 2:101

> Now, when a messenger from Allah has come to them— confirming their own Scriptures—some of the People of the Book cast the Book of Allah behind their backs as if they did not know.

**Expanded Commentary**

**The Image in the Verse**

The sentence describes an action with a physical picture attached: a book thrown behind the back, out of sight and out of mind. The Book uses the image for the deliberate setting aside of something a person is fully aware of, and it will use it again in the same sūrah when it describes people who were entrusted with the Torah and failed its demands.

The clause that follows explains the picture. The community behaves as though it did not know what it had rejected, and the comparison is a statement about the gap between knowledge and conduct rather than about literal ignorance. **Which Book Was Cast Aside**

Commentators discussed whether the Book referred to here is the Torah or the revelation just delivered. Al-Ṭabarī restricted the phrase to the learned among the community, while other commentators applied it to everyone who takes the Torah as his book and then disregards what it says.

The context favours the Torah, since the verse describes people who had been given a book and then treated it as something to be put away. The point the sentence makes is that the community's own scripture contained the marks and the announcement that the messenger's coming would fulfil, and it is that scripture that was set aside when the confirmation arrived. **Confirmation as the Ground of the Charge**

The verse's middle clause states that the messenger came confirming their scriptures, and the whole weight of the accusation rests there. A messenger who contradicted the earlier book would have been easy to refuse. A messenger who confirmed it required the community to accept what its own text had led it to expect, and the sentence states that the confirmation had arrived.

The sūrah has made this claim repeatedly in the chapter, describing the revelation as confirming what came before it. The argument being built is not that this community should abandon its scripture but that it should follow the implications of its own book, and the verse's image of a discarded text makes that argument concrete.

**Behind the Backs**

The Arabic expression for placing something behind the back carries the sense of treating an obligation as a matter for the past rather than the present. The community did not deny that it possessed the scripture; it kept it as a possession and stopped treating it as an authority, and the Book's image captures the difference exactly.

That distinction recurs in the sūrah's treatment of the community, which repeatedly describes them as people who had the text and did not follow it. It appears again in the parable of the donkey carrying books later in the chapter, where the contrast between possession and understanding is drawn even more sharply.

**As If They Did Not Know**

The final clause is a comparison rather than a statement about their information. The community knew; the following verses will describe what its members did with the knowledge, and the sūrah has already narrated their recognition of the revelation. What the clause describes is a posture adopted in public, and the Book uses the same kind of language when it reports the excuses people offer for conduct they understand.

The comparison also serves the verse's argument by removing the community's leading defence in advance. A group accused of rejecting revelation could answer that it did not recognise the message; the sentence forecloses that answer before the next verses outline what they did instead.

**The Deliberateness of the Act**

The verb describes throwing away, which implies a decision rather than a failure to read. The Book's accounts of this community's relation to its scripture are consistently framed in terms of choice, and the sūrah's earlier verses described a group that heard the word of God, understood it, and then altered it knowingly.

Read with those verses, this sentence gives a second instance of the same pattern. The community's problem was never a shortage of information or a defect in the text; it was what its members did after understanding, and the verse's image is chosen to display exactly that. **What the Sentence Holds for Later Readers**

The verse is addressed to a specific community in a specific dispute, and the warning it carries is a general one. A community that inherits a book and keeps it out of sight has repeated the act described here, whatever the book it holds. **Possession and Authority**

A book can be owned without being obeyed, and the verse's image separates the two. The community kept its scripture in the sense that it retained custody of the text, quoted it in argument, and took its standing from it, and all of that was compatible with disregarding what the text required. The Book's answer to the separation is not another text but a reminder that possession is not authority. The parable of the donkey carrying books later in the chapter makes the same point in a harsher form, and the sūrah's commands to the believers about reciting and studying the revelation are aimed at preventing the same gap in its own community. What the verse establishes is that the offence is not the loss of a scripture but the refusal to be governed by one.

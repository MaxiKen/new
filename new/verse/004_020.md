## Sūrah al-Nisāʾ 4:20

> If you desire to replace a wife with another and you have given the former ˹even˺ a stack of gold ˹as a dowry˺, do not take any of it back. Would you ˹still˺ take it unjustly and very sinfully?

### Expanded Commentary

**The dowry cannot be reclaimed through replacement**

A husband who wishes to marry another woman is prohibited from taking back the dowry he gave to the first, even when it was a large amount. The image of a “stack of gold” emphasizes that the size of the gift does not change its legal character. Once given, it belongs to the wife. Divorce does not retroactively turn her property into a loan.

This rule prevents a powerful form of financial pressure. A husband could otherwise threaten to replace his wife and demand repayment, leaving her with the choice between surrendering wealth and enduring a marriage in which she is unwanted. The Qur’an refuses to let a new desire rewrite an old obligation.

**Calumny as a tool of confiscation**

The verse asks whether the husband would take the property through slander and manifest sin. Accusing the wife of sexual misconduct, inventing defects, or publicly shaming her in order to make her relinquish the dowry is doubly wrong: it harms her reputation and steals her asset. The preceding verse’s exception cannot be activated by a private allegation; evidence and legitimate adjudication are required.

**Marriage, wealth, and moral memory**

The ordinance applies even when the husband has spent heavily on the marriage or feels that the relationship has failed. Financial disappointment does not erase the wife’s right. If she freely chooses to return some of it in a lawful separation arrangement, that is a different transaction; coercion is the dividing line.

For contemporary practice, the verse calls for clear contracts, independent access to the bride’s property, and a divorce process that does not make the woman bargain under fear. A new relationship may be permissible, but it cannot be financed by the unjust recovery of an old wife’s entitlement. God’s question—would you take it through falsehood and open sin?—turns an apparently private financial decision into a matter of conscience.

**Divorce must not become financial punishment**

A fair separation process tells each spouse what is owed, protects the waiting period, and prevents relatives from using shame to recover the dowry. The husband’s wish for a new marriage does not make the former wife responsible for financing it. The question in the verse should be asked before any demand is made: is this a lawful settlement, or is fear being used to disguise theft?

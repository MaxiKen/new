## Sūrah al-Tawbah 9:94

> They will make excuses to you ˹believers˺ when you return to them. Say, “Make no excuses, ˹for˺ we will not believe you. Allah has already informed us about your ˹true˺ state ˹of faith˺. Your ˹future˺ deeds will be observed by Allah and His Messenger as well. And you will be returned to the Knower of the seen and unseen, then He will inform you of what you used to do.”

**Expanded Commentary**

**A Late Explanation Cannot Undo an Earlier Choice**

The hypocrites offer excuses after the believers return. Their timing matters. They did not speak honestly before the mission, when an accurate report could have helped planning. They speak afterward, when the immediate danger has passed and the community is more likely to be pressured into accepting a convenient story.

The instruction to make no excuses rejects this attempt to manage the record. It does not teach believers to refuse every explanation. It teaches them not to accept explanations from people whose conduct has already exposed a settled pattern of deceit. Truthful accountability occurs before the duty when possible, not after the result has made the excuse necessary.

**Trust Can Be Lost Through Conduct**

The believers are told that they will not believe the excuses because Allah has informed them about the hypocrites' true state. This does not mean ordinary people possess the unseen. It means that revelation and conduct have provided enough evidence for the community to stop treating these speakers as reliable.

Trust is a moral asset. It can be damaged by repeated lies, broken promises, and refusal to accept responsibility. A person cannot demand that every new statement be treated as credible while rejecting the evidence that made him untrustworthy. Repentance can rebuild trust, but rebuilding begins by accepting that trust was lost rather than blaming the people who lost confidence.

**Future Deeds Will Continue the Test**

The verse says their future deeds will be observed by Allah and His Messenger. This shifts attention from arguments to conduct. A person may produce a moving explanation, but his later actions will show whether the explanation was true. Deeds are not a substitute for intention, yet they are evidence of what intention has become.

The believer should apply this principle to himself before applying it to others. If he claims repentance, what changes in his habits? If he claims loyalty, does he prepare, give, and speak truthfully? Allah gives time for a person's direction to appear. The servant should use that time for repair rather than for better public relations.

**The Seen and Unseen Belong to One Judge**

The verse calls Allah the Knower of the seen and unseen. Human beings judge from visible reports and later conduct; Allah knows what was hidden at the beginning and what will be revealed at the end. This protects the community from pretending that its judgment is complete.

It also comforts the person whose sincere intention was misread. Allah knows the limit he faced, the help he sought, and the grief he carried. Human beings may need boundaries based on evidence, but Allah's judgment reaches deeper than their evidence. The servant tells the truth and leaves the unseen account to the One who cannot be deceived by performance.

**Accountability Includes What Was Done in Secret**

The promise that Allah will inform them of what they did places every hidden choice into the final record. The private agreement, concealed resentment, and false explanation will not disappear because no person wrote them down. This knowledge makes the present morally serious even when no audience is watching.

A believer can use that seriousness positively. He performs hidden good, corrects hidden intention, and stops hidden harm before it grows. He does not wait for public exposure to become honest. Allah's awareness is already complete, and the wise servant makes his private conduct worthy of the final disclosure.

**Mercy Does Not Require Believing a Lie**

The community can forgive a person without trusting every statement he makes. It can avoid revenge while still refusing access to sensitive responsibilities. It can hope for repentance while telling the truth about past conduct. These distinctions are part of mercy because they protect people from being deceived again.

The verse therefore gives leaders permission to end a debate when evidence is sufficient. They should remain fair and open to real change, but they need not surrender discernment to a late oath. The ultimate return is to Allah, and that return should make every person prefer a truthful account now over an excuse that will fail before the Knower of all things.

The final return to Allah also keeps human refusal from becoming absolute. The believers may stop believing the excuses because the evidence is clear, but Allah remains the One who knows the hidden intention and the final repentance. A person who has lost trust cannot demand immediate access, yet he can begin the slow work of becoming trustworthy. He tells the truth before being questioned, accepts consequences without theatrical oaths, and lets future deeds confirm the change. This is how a late excuse is replaced by a long record of honesty. The Knower of the seen and unseen judges the entire record, not just the speech offered at the door.

The verse also teaches the difference between evidence and omniscience. The believers can say that they do not believe the excuses because the conduct and revelation have made the matter clear. They do not claim to know every hidden movement of the heart. Allah alone knows the unseen and the seen together. This humility keeps accountability from becoming gossip. It also gives hope to the person who truly changes, because a long record of honest deeds can show that the earlier pattern has ended. Trust returns through evidence over time, while final judgment remains with Allah.

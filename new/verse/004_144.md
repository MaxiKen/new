## Sūrah al-Nisāʾ 4:144

> O believers! Do not take disbelievers as allies instead of the believers. Would you like to give Allah solid proof against yourselves?

**Expanded Commentary**

**O Believers — Do Not Take Disbelievers as Allies Instead of Believers**

The verse states *yā ayyuhā alladhīna āmanū lā tattakhidhū al-kāfirīna awliyāʾa min dūni al-muʾminīna* — O believers, do not take disbelievers as allies, guardians, protectors, instead of, apart from, believers. *Lā tattakhidhū awliyāʾ* — do not take as allies — repeats the same prohibition as *who choose disbelievers as allies instead of believers seeking honour* (4:139) where honour belongs to Him entirely, but now as direct command after the portrait of the torn between neither these nor those for whom no way is found (4:143). *Min dūni al-muʾminīn* — instead of believers, apart from them — was explained as choosing the other company while believers are available, as earlier *do not take them as allies unless they emigrate* (4:89) conditioned alliance on hijra, and as *those who are allies of a treaty people excepted* (4:90) honoured treaty, but this alliance is apart from believers altogether. The sūrah has traced *awliyāʾ* from the single soul’s family ties (4:1), to dowry and inheritance ties, to the emigrant’s haven, to Satan taken as guardian instead of Him suffering tremendous loss (4:119); to take disbelievers as guardians instead of believers is to take the cursed guardian’s share (4:118). Early reports illustrated the command where hypocrites cultivated disbelievers for protection against believers, assuming the believers’ trusteeship — *kafā bi Allāhi wakīlā* (4:132) — was insufficient; the verse says trusteeship suffices, do not seek other.

**Would You Give God Solid Proof Against Yourselves?**

The question *a turīdūna an tajʿalū li Allāhi ʿalaykum sulṭānan mubīnā* — do you want to give, to make, for God against you a clear, manifest warrant, proof, authority? — was explained with *sulṭān mubīn* — clear warrant, manifest proof, evident authority — the same *sulṭān mubīn* given to you over the double-faced who do not withdraw or offer peace (4:91) — *We have given you clear warrant over such people*. Here the *sulṭān* is inverted: instead of clear warrant for you against the hostile turners, you give God clear warrant against yourselves by allying with disbelievers instead of believers. *Tajʿalū li Allāhi ʿalaykum* — to make for God against you — was explained as furnishing the prosecution evidence by act: to take allies instead of believers while the Book with truth judges between people (4:105) and while standing firm for justice though against yourselves is commanded (4:135) is to write your own proof. The sūrah has shown that association is not forgiven (4:116) and leaves far straying, and that the gathered hypocrites and disbelievers together in Hell (4:140) is the gathering of the like — *mithluhum*; to give *sulṭān mubīn* against yourselves is to make that gathering just, because the proof — *ʿizzah* sought among disbelievers (4:139), sitting where signs are mocked (4:140), waiting to claim with winners (4:141), deceiving while being outwitted (4:142) — is manifest.

**A Verse That Follows Torn Between With Choice**

Placed after *torn between neither these nor those, whoever He leaves to stray you will never find a way* (4:143), this verse follows the frozen between with a choice and its evidence. The torn between is told: do not take disbelievers as allies instead of believers — decide the between. The sūrah has shown that guidance was made clear, noble ways taught, turning offered (4:26–28), and that He can remove you O humanity and bring others (4:133) — removal is the consequence of giving Him clear warrant against yourselves by choosing the other alliance. The sūrah has also shown that *with Him are both rewards He hears and sees* (4:134) — to seek reward from disbelievers as allies is to seek little where both are with Him whose awareness is *Khabīr* of what you do (4:135, 4:128). The question *a turīdūna* — do you want? — was read as the Book’s appeal to will: do you intend to give proof against yourselves, or would you rather stand for justice even against yourselves and find Him All-Forgiving where you set right and are mindful (4:129–130)? The choice is not between honour and humiliation invented, but between proof for you — *sulṭān* over hostile turners (4:91) — and proof against you — *sulṭān* for Him over you.

**Alliance Instead of Believers as Proof**

The phrase *min dūni al-muʾminīn* — instead of believers — was explained to show that alliance with disbelievers is not forbidden where treaty or haven requires intercourse — the sūrah excepted those allied to treaty people (4:90), allowed laying aside weapons in rain yet keep precaution (4:102), and promised many havens among people (4:100); the prohibition is taking them as *awliyāʾ* — intimate guardians — instead of believers, as the oppressed cry for guardian and helper from grace (4:75) — guardian is to be from Him. Early notes added that the *sulṭān mubīn* against yourselves is not a metaphorical phrase but the record: night plotting with them (4:108) while He is with you, advocating for self-wrongers among them (4:107), hiding from people not from the Encompassing (4:108), will be brought as witness. Where the emigrant’s reward falls upon God secure (4:100), the ally-taker’s proof falls against himself secure, because the Trustee sufficient (4:132) keeps record that encompasses everything (4:126).

**A Short Verse That Anchors Proof in Alliance**

Though only *“do not take disbelievers as allies instead of believers, would you give God clear warrant against yourselves?”* the verse anchors proof in alliance. Immediately after: *hypocrites will be in lowest depths of Fire and you will never find helper* (4:145) — except those who repent, mend, hold fast and are sincere will be with believers and God will grant believers great reward (4:146) — proof either stays or is erased by repentance and holding fast. The sūrah that warns not to let desires deviate from justice (4:135) names this alliance as the desire that deviates: to seek protection or honour from those who disbelieve is to deviate from *qawwāmīn bi al-qisṭ* — ever standing for justice. For the weak this is the lightest protection: where you feared rising or turning away in household and were given settlement best (4:128), here you fear alliance with disbelievers for honour; the lightest is to belong with believers, whose path is submission following Abraham — best faith — whose friend is the Owner of heavens and earth, and not to give against yourselves the manifest proof whose gathering is together in Hell.

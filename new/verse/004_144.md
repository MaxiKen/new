## Sūrah al-Nisāʾ 4:144

> O believers! Do not take disbelievers as allies instead of the believers. Would you like to give Allah solid proof against yourselves?

### Expanded Commentary

**Do not exchange the community for power**

Believers are told not to take disbelievers as allies instead of believers and are asked whether they wish to give Allah a clear case against themselves. The prohibition concerns political and military loyalty that harms believers, not ordinary relations, commerce, justice, or cooperation in good. The Qur’an’s treaty verses prove that ethical dealings with non-Muslims remain possible.

**“Proof against yourselves”**

A person who uses his authority to strengthen those committed to persecuting believers supplies evidence of betrayal through his own choice. The phrase does not mean Allah needs proof; it means the deed makes the case manifest. Hidden intention becomes visible in the alliance’s consequences.

The verse asks believers to seek honour through principled relationships. Cooperation should be judged by justice and harm, not by tribal hostility or naïve trust. Loyalty to the believing community includes protecting its vulnerable members, while faith also requires fairness toward everyone.

**Alliance and ordinary neighbourliness**

The command does not prevent Muslims from cooperating with non-Muslims in justice, medicine, trade, or public welfare. It prevents a loyalty that strengthens persecution of believers or makes falsehood the community’s protector. Distinguishing these categories is necessary to obey the verse without contradicting the chapter’s treaty ethic.

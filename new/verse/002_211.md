## Sūrah al-Baqarah 2:211

> Ask the Children of Israel how many clear signs We have given them. And whoever trades Allah’s favour—after receiving it—˹for disbelief˺ should know that Allah is indeed severe in punishment.

**Expanded Commentary**

**The Instruction to Ask**

The verse instructs the Prophet ﷺ to ask the Children of Israel how many clear signs they were given, and it then states that whoever exchanges the favour of God after it has come to him will find that God is severe in punishment. The sentence turns the passage's attention to the record of an earlier community.

The Book's use of a question addressed to a community about its own history appears in several passages, and the community's teachers read it as an appeal to what the people addressed already know. What the reader receives is an instruction that draws the audience's attention to the evidence it possesses.

**The Signs Named**

The commentators identified the signs as the wonders granted to Moses, including the staff, the hand, the parting of the sea, and the deliverance of the community from its pursuers. The Book recounts the succession of these events in its account of the exodus.

The list shows the kind of evidence the sentence refers to, and the community's scholars noted that the signs were given to the people as a whole rather than to a single individual. What the reader receives is the account of a community whose history provides the proof of what the Book states, and the sentence's question invites the audience to count what it has received.

**The Favour and Its Exchange**

The sentence states that a person who exchanges the favour of God after it has come to him will face a severe punishment, and the commentators explained the favour as the trust and the covenant that God made with the community. The exchange is described as an alteration of what was received.

The Book's use of the language of exchange connects the sentence with its earlier account of those who traded guidance for error, and the community's teachers read the passages together. The reader carries away the account of a transaction that turns a gift into a loss, and the sentence shows that the Book treats the favour of revelation as something for which a person is held responsible.

**The Alteration of the Text**

The commentators connected the sentence with the reports about the alteration of the scriptures, and they noted that the verb used in the sentence carries the sense of substitution. The community's scholars gathered the passages in which the Book describes the distortion of the earlier texts and read them with this verse.

The reading gives the sentence a particular application, and the community's teachers used it when they spoke about the handling of a revealed text. The sentence leaves him with the account of a charge that the Book makes against the communities that received earlier scriptures, and the sentence's wording supports both the general and the particular readings.

**The Punishment Named**

The sentence closes with the statement that God is severe in punishment, and the community's scholars read the clause as the counterpart of the favour the sentence has described. The Book pairs the two in several passages about the communities that received revelation.

The wording states the consequence without elaboration, and the community's teachers noted that the Book's warnings to the earlier communities are recorded for the benefit of the community that reads them. The passage supplies him with the statement that the gift of revelation carries an account, and leaves him with the knowledge that the same standard applies to what the community has received.

**The Passage's Address to the Prophet**

The sentence is addressed to the Prophet ﷺ, and the instruction to question the community is given to him in the setting of the dispute about his mission. The community's teachers noted that the question is rhetorical in its force, since the answer is known to the people addressed.

## Sūrah al-Tawbah 9:96

> They will swear to you in order to please you. And even if you are pleased with them, Allah will never be pleased with the rebellious people.

**Expanded Commentary**

**Human Approval Cannot Repair Rebellion**

The hypocrites swear in order to please the believers. Their purpose is the audience's satisfaction, not Allah's satisfaction. The verse does not deny the value of reconciliation between people. It exposes reconciliation built on a false story. A community may feel calmer after hearing an oath, but calm is not the same as truth.

This teaches believers to distinguish social peace from moral peace. A false apology can end an argument while leaving the wrong intact. A truthful apology may create discomfort because it names the harm. Allah's pleasure is not measured by how quickly a room becomes comfortable. It is measured by truth, repentance, and conduct that no longer supports rebellion.

**A Leader's Contentment Has a Limit**

The verse says that even if the believers become pleased, Allah will not be pleased with the rebellious. This limits human authority. Leaders may forgive, reconcile, and decide not to pursue a matter, but they cannot declare rebellion acceptable before Allah. Human contentment can remove a worldly consequence; it cannot rewrite divine judgment.

This also protects people from becoming dependent on human approval. A servant can be forgiven by a leader and still need to repent before Allah. Conversely, a leader can remain displeased while Allah accepts a sincere servant's repentance. The two forms of contentment must not be confused. Human judgment matters in social dealings, but Allah's pleasure is the final measure.

**Pleasure Should Follow Evidence of Change**

A person may reasonably become pleased when the truth is established, the harm is repaired, and conduct changes. Pleasure should not be purchased by a new oath alone. The believer can welcome a repentant person while still keeping appropriate boundaries until reliability returns.

This is not suspicion without end. It is patience with the process of rebuilding. The repentant person accepts that his past affects present trust. He does not demand immediate celebration as proof that he has been forgiven. He serves quietly, tells the truth when it costs him, and allows people to see that the rebellion has ended.

**Rebellion Is More Than a Political Act**

The word rebellious includes the refusal to submit Allah's guidance to personal preference. A person can rebel through public opposition, hidden mockery, hoarding, or the repeated use of religious words to avoid duty. The common feature is that the servant wants the benefits of order while rejecting the authority that defines the order.

The believer watches for this in himself. He may seek approval from people by making worship less demanding than Allah made it. He may call compromise wisdom when it is actually fear. Human pleasure can hide these movements for a time, but it cannot make them safe. Allah's displeasure is a warning that the heart must return to submission.

**Oaths Should Point Toward Allah**

A true oath should remind the speaker that Allah hears. It should make false speech frightening and truthful speech humble. The hypocrites reverse the purpose by using Allah's name to please people. Their oath becomes evidence against them because it shows how easily the sacred is placed beneath the audience.

The believer uses speech differently. He seeks Allah's pleasure first, then speaks to people with courtesy. If they are pleased, he thanks Allah. If they are displeased, he checks his manner and remains truthful if the content was right. This order saves the heart from being pulled by every reaction around it.

**True Reconciliation Includes Moral Repair**

The verse does not require the community to remain angry forever. It requires the community not to call rebellion acceptable simply because an oath is soothing. Reconciliation can include a boundary, a repayment, a public correction, or a period of supervised trust. Its purpose is restored truth, not restored appearances.

Allah's displeasure teaches the servant that real peace begins with Him. When the heart submits, human relationships can be repaired honestly. When the heart remains rebellious, even a pleased audience cannot provide lasting safety. The wise believer seeks both human reconciliation and divine approval, but when the two are placed in conflict, Allah's pleasure has priority.

A leader may therefore accept a person's apology without declaring the original conduct sound. A family may resume kindness without handing back every trust at once. An institution may forgive a breach while requiring supervision before access is restored. These are not contradictions. They reflect the difference between mercy toward a person and approval of rebellion. Allah's pleasure is the final horizon for both the one who forgives and the one who seeks forgiveness. The servant who wants people to be pleased with him should first make his speech and conduct pleasing to Allah, then accept whatever human response follows.

The believer should also beware of the desire to be pleased by everyone. A leader who needs universal approval will eventually call wrongdoing acceptable. A family member who cannot bear another person's displeasure may protect a lie. A teacher who wants applause may remove every demanding part of the lesson. Allah's pleasure gives courage to disappoint people when disappointment protects truth. It also gives tenderness when correction is required, because the goal is not to win a quarrel but to restore obedience. Human contentment is welcome when it follows truth, never when it purchases permission to rebel.

The servant seeks human peace only after placing the pleasure of Allah above the comfort of the audience.

A person may accept human displeasure rather than buy peace with a lie. Allah's pleasure is the steadier refuge, and sincere repair begins when the audience no longer controls the truth.

## Sūrah al-Anʿām 6:106

> ˹O Prophet!˺ Follow what is revealed to you from your Lord—there is no god ˹worthy of worship˺ except Him—and turn away from the polytheists.

**Expanded Commentary**

**The Command to Follow What Is Revealed**

Follow what is revealed to you from your Lord. The command stands at the head of a passage in which the sūrah turns from the argument with the disbelievers to the instructions given to the Prophet himself, and its position is significant. After many verses of signs, refutations, and accounts of what the audience said, the sūrah states the one thing the messenger is to do with all of it, which is to follow the revelation.

The Book's use of this command elsewhere gives the phrase its force. The sūrah of the Cattle ends its long list of prohibitions with the instruction to follow the straight path and with the declaration that this is what God has commanded (6:153), and the sūrah of the Bee tells the Prophet that God has revealed to him to follow the way of Abraham, who was a man of pure faith (16:123). The command is the practical counterpart of the arguments that precede it.

**The Declaration That Accompanies It**

There is no god worthy of worship except Him. The sūrah inserts the confession in the middle of an instruction, and the arrangement shows how the Book treats the revelation and the declaration as one subject. To follow what is revealed is to act on the content of the message, and the content begins with the statement about God that the sūrah has been arguing for since its opening verses.

The sūrah has attached this sentence to several of its conclusions: to the account of the created order (6:102), to the description of the messenger's own position (6:19 context), and to the declaration of the truth that has come from the Lord. Here it accompanies the command to follow, and the two together state the terms of the Prophet's own obedience. What he is told to do is what every other person addressed by the sūrah was told to do, with the difference that his following includes the delivery of the message.

**The Instruction to Turn Away**

And turn away from the polytheists. The sūrah has issued a similar instruction at earlier points: it told the Prophet to leave those who took their religion as play and amusement (6:70) and it told him to say that he is not a keeper over the people (6:104). The command to turn away in this verse is not a command to abandon the argument, since the sūrah continues to address the audience's objections after it.

The Book's habit with this instruction is to distinguish between the prophet's responsibility for the message and his responsibility for the response. The sūrah of the Ranged tells him to turn away from them and to say peace, for they will come to know; the sūrah of the Pen tells him not to be distressed by those who deny. The turning away in each case belongs to the prophet's own conduct rather than to the mission, and the sūrah's next verse explains what the Prophet is not.

**What the Verse Establishes About the Messenger**

The verse places the messenger under the revelation in the plainest terms. He does not choose the content of what he teaches, and the instruction is given to him rather than to the audience, which is a pattern the sūrah keeps in the passages that address his situation directly. The Book records the same ordering in the sūrah of the Star, where the Prophet's utterance is described as nothing other than revelation sent to him (53:4).

The verse's second half then tells the Prophet how to live in a city that would not accept the message. The command to turn away is attached to a situation in which the Prophet had no power to compel belief and no responsibility for the failure of others to accept it. The sūrah treats that as a fact to be recognised rather than as a problem to be solved.

**The Verses That Define the Limits**

The verse that follows states the limits in a form that leaves no ambiguity: had God willed, they would not have been polytheists, the Prophet has not been appointed as their keeper, and he is not their maintainer (6:107). The two verses belong together, and they answer the question of how a person should act toward a community that has rejected the message he carries.

The Book uses the same structure in other sūrahs, joining a command to the Prophet with a statement about its scope. The sūrah of the Bee says God has guided whom He wills and that the Prophet's duty is to call people to the way of the Lord with wisdom and good instruction (16:125). This sūrah's version of the instruction is to follow, to declare, and to keep his own course, which is what the conclusion of the passage states.

**The Place of the Verse in the Chapter**

The sūrah has been moving through a section about the signs and the responses to them, and this verse begins the closing movement of that section. The arguments against the audience's theology have been made; the created order has been described; and the sūrah now turns to the practical form the message takes in the life of the man who receives it.

That turn gives the chapter its shape. What follows concerns the limits of the messenger's responsibility, the prohibition on insulting the objects the idolaters venerate, the oaths sworn by those who demand a sign, and the nature of what the witnesses among them testify to. Each passage continues the same argument from a different angle, and the instruction to follow what has been revealed remains the point from which they proceed.

## Sūrah al-Baqarah 2:54

> And ˹remember˺ when Moses said to his people, “O my people! Surely you have wronged yourselves by worshipping the calf, so turn in repentance to your Creator and execute ˹the calf-worshippers among˺ yourselves. That is best for you in the sight of your Creator.” Then He accepted your repentance. Surely He is the Accepter of Repentance, Most Merciful.

**Expanded Commentary**

**Moses Names the Wrong Before Prescribing the Return**

“And remember when Moses said to his people, ‘O my people! Surely you have wronged yourselves by worshipping the calf, so turn in repentance to your Creator and execute the calf-worshippers among yourselves. That is best for you in the sight of your Creator.’ Then He accepted your repentance. Surely He is the Accepter of Repentance, Most Merciful” (2:54). Qāla Mūsā li-qawmihi — Moses speaks to his own people, not to outsiders. Yā qawmi — “O my people!” — carries belonging even while naming wrong. Innkum ẓalamtum anfusakum — you wronged yourselves. As with the calf act earlier named ẓulm while still in it (2:51), wrong is stated as self-wrong: to take the calf as god is to misplace the self.

The opening echoes the pardon and the book: after deliverance (2:49–50), calf (2:51), pardon (2:52), and Book and Criterion (2:53), the criterion now speaks. Moses’ speech is the criterion applied: what was given to distinguish now distinguishes.

**“Turn to Your Creator and Execute Yourselves” — What Was Commanded?**

Fa-tūbū ilā Bāriʾikum fa-uqtulū anfusakum — “turn in repentance to your Creator and slay yourselves.” Bāriʾ — the Creator, the Maker who formed, who cut out and proportioned — appears here instead of the more common Khāliq, emphasizing the One who originated without flaw. To have worshipped a lowing image when the Bāriʾ had just parted the sea is to have insulted the making.

Fa-uqtulū anfusakum — literally “kill yourselves” — has been read in tafsīr in three ways held together by the source:

- The severe, outward reading: the guilty among the Israelites were to be killed by their fellows — the repentance’s cost borne in blood. The source cites 2:85; 4:29 as parallels for “kill yourselves” as killing among yourselves, and Exodus 32:27 as scriptural parallel. On this reading the severity reflects the severity of collective apostasy after witnessed deliverance, and the narrative adds that the killed were counted martyrs and the killers were forgiven, so that all were covered by pardon, though through a grievous discipline.

- The inward reading: “slay yourselves” means slay your egos, oppose passions, mortify the self that took the calf — a spiritual killing of caprice (IA, Iṣ). This reading keeps the literal killing as type while drawing its lasting moral: repentance kills what caused the sin.

- The collective-punishment reading: the episode as a whole — slain and slayers — was a shared chastisement that was itself the acceptance, not merely its prerequisite.

The Qur’anic text states the command and then states relenting: “Then He accepted your repentance” — fa-tāba ʿalaykum. The relenting follows the turning. Whether the slaying was fully enacted as history records or was commanded and then lightened by divine mercy, the verse’s gist is severity made merciful: that is best for you with your Creator (khayrun lakum ʿinda Bāriʾikum).

**“That Is Best for You with Your Creator” — Best by Whose Measure?**

Dhālikum khayrun lakum ʿinda Bāriʾikum (2:54). “Better” is not by tribal measure but with the Bāriʾ. What looks excessive by lenient measure is “best” when the offended is the Maker who spared sons and parted waters. The criterion (furqān) given in 2:53 now declares value: better to lose the guilty than to keep calf-worship, better to be severed than to stay mixed.

**“Then He Accepted Your Repentance. Surely He Is the Accepter of Repentance, Most Merciful” — Seal as in Adam’s Return**

The seal repeats Adam’s seal: “He accepted his repentance. Surely He is the Accepter of Repentance, Most Merciful” (2:37). The same two names — al-Tawwāb al-Raḥīm — close a people’s return as they closed a father’s return. The parallel is deliberate: the first human slip and the first collective apostasy are both met by taught turning and by relenting mercy, named identically. The sūrah thus frames Israel’s history inside Adam’s: to fall, to be told how to turn, to be turned to.

**Severity Framed by Mercy**

The verse holds severity and mercy without softening either. It does not minimize “execute” into metaphor only, nor does it maximize it into unending wrath. It states command, states value — better with your Maker — and states outcome — He relented. The discipline is real; the relenting is real; the names that close are real: ever-accepting, ever-merciful.

**Living the Verse Without Presuming Its Severity**

For later communities, the specific law of “slay yourselves” is not legislated as ongoing practice. Its lesson is not to imitate the penalty but to imitate the principle: repentance costs the self. To turn to the Bāriʾ is to kill the image taken, the gain sold, the truth mixed or hidden (2:41–42). The “calf” to be executed is the lowing form that promises help while delivering none. Its execution is better with the Maker than its keeping.

Details about numbers slain, duration of killing, or whether Moses’ Levites alone carried it out should be marked UNVERIFIED when lacking Sahih prophetic wording; they are tafsīr and Isrāʾīliyyāt expansions. What is firm is Moses’ speech as quoted, the command to turn to the Bāriʾ and slay yourselves as better with Him, and the subsequent acceptance sealed with al-Tawwāb al-Raḥīm (2:54).

## Sūrah al-Baqarah 2:68

> They said, “Call upon your Lord to clarify for us what type ˹of cow˺ it should be!” He replied, “Allah says, ‘The cow should neither be old nor young but in between. So do as you are commanded!’”

**Expanded Commentary**

**A Question That Adds Nothing**

The community asks Moses to consult his Lord about the kind of cow required, and the answer supplies the animal's age: not worn out, not a calf, but between the two. The Book then reports the instruction that accompanies the answer, and the instruction is the one they had already received. Nothing in the clarification changes the task.

The word the community uses for their request places God at a distance from them and Moses at the centre: call upon your Lord. The sūrah's account of the exchange keeps that phrasing, and it uses the same construction for the following questions. The community is addressing a prophet about a command from God while keeping its own access to the command indirect, and the Book reports the arrangement without correcting it yet.

**Why the Detail Was Not Necessary**

The Book's account of the incident makes clear that a cow of any description would have fulfilled the command, and the reports attach a saying to the effect that the first animal offered would have sufficed. What the community's question produced was a narrower requirement, which is the opposite of what the question was intended to achieve.

The Book states the principle in the exchange itself, in the instruction attached to the answer: do what you are commanded. That clause is the substance of the reply, and the detail about the animal's age is secondary to it. The reader is being shown a community that asked for precision and received a smaller target than the one it began with.

**The Pattern of the Interrogation**

This is the second question in the exchange, and the sūrah will report two more. Each one asks for a further specification, and each answer narrows the description of the animal. The structure of the passage is therefore cumulative: the community's carefulness increases the difficulty of the task, and the Book lays the process out over four verses so that the reader can watch it happen.

The narrative's effect depends on the reader's knowledge that the command could have been satisfied at once. Without that knowledge the questions look like the diligence of a conscientious community, and the Book supplies the knowledge through the instruction that keeps appearing in the answers. Each reply ends by telling them to do what they were commanded, which is the answer to a question they had not asked.

**"Between the Two": a Middling Animal**

The description given in this verse excludes the extremes and leaves the ordinary. A cow that has not reached the end of its working life and has not yet begun it is a beast in its prime, and the requirement therefore points at the most usable and most valuable animal in a herd. The community had asked for a category; the answer removed the two cheapest options.

The Book's accounts of sacrifices are consistently concerned with the quality of what is offered rather than with the quantity, and it states elsewhere that God does not accept the worthless. The detail in this verse belongs to that teaching, and it also functions inside the narrative as the first consequence of the community's insistence on being told exactly what was wanted.

**Moses as Intermediary**

The verse shows Moses relaying each answer, and his role in the exchange is worth noticing. He does not rebuke the community for asking, and he does not attempt to reason with them; he delivers the clarification and repeats the command. The Book's messengers are consistently described as transmitters of what they receive, and the sūrah's account of this episode keeps Moses inside that description.

That treatment protects the reader from an obvious misreading. The narrative is not a story about a prophet who loses patience; it is a story about a community that keeps returning to the market with a divine command. Moses' position between the two parties is stated in every verse of the exchange, and the sūrah will report the outcome in the sixth of them.

**What the Exchange Cost the Community**

The community's request in this verse is not treated as sinful, and the Book does not describe a punishment for asking. What the sūrah reports is a consequence that follows naturally: the search for the animal became progressively harder, and the praise hidden in the eventual answer about its rarity is bought at a price the community did not anticipate.

The Book's teaching about questioning revelation is not that questions are forbidden. Its objection is to the kind of inquiry that delays obedience while it is being conducted, and the sūrah's narrative shows the cost of that delay in a series of tightening specifications. The reader is left with the smallest animal and the largest bill, and the instruction to do what was commanded has been repeated twice in the exchange.

**Answers That Narrow Rather Than Explain**

The clarification in this verse is a good example of how the Book answers requests for detail. The community asked for a category and received a range with both ends closed: not old, not young. The information is accurate, and it leaves the community less room to manoeuvre than it had before, which is the opposite of what a request for specification normally produces.

That pattern continues through the exchange and is completed at its end. Each answer removes options from the search rather than supplying a solution to it, and the community's own questions are the instrument by which the task becomes harder. The Book records the process without commentary, and the reader who follows it to the final verse sees the effect of pressing a command for particulars.

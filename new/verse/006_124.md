## Sūrah al-Anʿām 6:124

> Whenever a sign comes to them, they say, “We will never believe until we receive what Allah’s messengers received.” Allah knows best where to place His message. The wicked will soon be overwhelmed by humiliation from Allah and a severe punishment for their evil plots.

**Expanded Commentary**

**The Demand Made at Every Sign**

Whenever a sign comes to them, they say, We will never believe until we receive what Allah's messengers received. The verse records the demand of the elite described in the previous verse, and the demand is not for evidence but for standing. What the messengers received was the message itself and the authority that accompanied it, and the audience's position was that they would accept revelation only if it came down to them personally.

The Book records the same disposition in other places. The sūrah of the Night Journey records the demand of the audience that the Prophet bring down a book they could read (17:93), and the sūrah of the Family of Imran records the claim of those who said that God had commanded them not to believe in any messenger unless he brought an offering consumed by fire from the sky (3:183). The verse in front of us states the demand in its clearest form, since the audience wanted the office rather than the proof.

**The Answer Given to the Demand**

Allah knows best where to place His message. The sentence answers the demand by referring the matter of appointment to the One who makes it, and the Book states the same principle elsewhere in its accounts of the prophets. The sūrah of the Pilgrimage says that God selects messengers from both angels and people, for He is All-Hearing and All-Seeing (22:75), and the sūrah of the Cow records God's testing of Abraham and His promise to make him a leader for the people (2:124).

The answer also addresses the assumption behind the demand, which was that the leading families of a city were the natural candidates for any great mission. The Book's choice of messengers from among the shepherds, the traders, and the communities without standing was a standing provocation to the elite, and the sūrah of the Ornament records their complaint that the Qur'an should have been sent down to a great man of one of the two cities. The verse in front of us states that the placement of the message belongs to God's knowledge.

**The Humiliation and the Punishment**

The wicked will soon be overwhelmed by humiliation from Allah and a severe punishment for their evil plots. The verse closes by stating what the demand will produce, and the terms it uses are the ones the sūrah has applied to the opponents of the message throughout. Humiliation is the condition of the people who set themselves against the message, and the punishment is attached to the plotting described in the previous verse.

The Book's statements about the outcome of the plotters appear in several passages. The sūrah of Fāṭir states that evil plotting only backfires on those who plot (35:43); the sūrah of Tā-Hā records the magicians falling in prostration and believing after the sign was shown to them (20:70); and the sūrah of the Cattle has already described the wrongdoers in the throes of death and the torment that follows (6:93). The sentence in this verse attaches the same outcome to the demand for the messenger's office.

**The Reasoning in the Verse**

The verse sets two kinds of knowledge side by side. The audience claimed to know who should receive the message, and the Book states that God knows where to place it. The difference between the two is the difference between assumption and knowledge that the sūrah has been stating since its warning about following the majority.

The verse's arrangement also explains the audience's persistence. A people who believed that the position of their families entitled them to the leadership of any movement would not accept the message of a man from outside that position, and the Book presents their rejection as a response to the messenger's standing rather than to the content of what he brought. The verse states the correction directly, and it leaves the outcome with God.

**What the Verse Says About the Messengers**

The sentence about placing the message is the Book's answer to every objection based on the messenger's social position. The Book's narratives of the earlier prophets consistently present the same objection from the elites: that the messengers were ordinary men, that they ate food and walked in the markets, that they were not accompanied by angels, and that their followers were the weak. The verse in front of us treats all such objections under one principle.

The Book's own account of the Prophet's standing belongs to the same argument. The sūrah of the Family of Imran tells the believers that God has favoured them by sending a messenger from among themselves, and the sūrah of the Repentance states that a messenger has come to them from among their own number who is pained by their hardship. The message's placement among a particular people is presented as a mercy rather than as an accident.

**The Transition to the Next Verse**

The next verse states the arrangement by which the two responses are decided: whoever God wills to guide, He opens his heart to Islam, and whoever He wills to leave astray, He makes his chest tight and constricted as though he were climbing into the sky (6:125). The two verses answer the same question from different sides, and the sūrah completes its account of guidance and its refusal.

The passage therefore ends with the division it has been describing throughout: a demand answered by God's knowledge, and a response settled by God's will. The sūrah's next verses return to the created order and to the signs, and they bring the chapter's argument back to the evidence that was available to the audience all along.

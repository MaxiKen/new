## Sūrah al-Aʿrāf 7:46

> There will be a barrier between Paradise and Hell. And on the heights ˹of that barrier˺ will be people who will recognize ˹the residents of˺ both by their appearance. They will call out to the residents of Paradise, “Peace be upon you!” They will have not yet entered Paradise, but eagerly hope to.

**Expanded Commentary**

**The Barrier Between the Two Groups**

The verse names a barrier set between the people of the Garden and the people of the Fire, and the Book describes a similar partition in the sūrah of the Iron, where a wall with a gate is set down between the believers and the hypocrites, with mercy on its inner side and punishment on the outer (57:13). The barrier in front of us separates the two outcomes of the reckoning, and the commentary tradition identifies it with the wall described in that passage.

The two groups in front of us have just exchanged the question and the answer about the truth of the promise (7:44–45), and the barrier is placed between them without preventing the exchange. The Book's descriptions of the Hereafter present the two abodes as adjacent and mutually visible, and the sūrah's later verses continue the dialogue across the divide with the request for water and the refusal (7:50). The barrier in front of us is therefore a boundary of status rather than of distance.

**The Heights and the Name of the Sūrah**

The verse places a group of people on the heights of the barrier, and the chapter takes its name from that place. The Arabic word *aʿrāf* denotes the elevated edge of a thing, and the commentary tradition describes it as a high place above the barrier from which both sides can be seen; some commentators identify it with the bridge that stretches over the Fire and leads to the Garden, and others describe it as a sand dune raised above the two abodes.

The name is connected in the tradition with the ability to recognize, since the same root produces the verb in the verse where the people on the heights know both groups by their marks. Those who occupy the height are therefore described by what they can do there: they identify the people of both sides by their appearance, and the chapter's title records the station rather than a doctrine.

**Recognized by Their Marks**

The verse states that the people of the heights recognize both groups by their marks, and the phrase describes a visible distinction that requires no announcement. The commentary tradition explains the marks as the features that identify the people of the Garden and the people of the Fire, whether the brightness of the faces of the saved or the darkness of the faces of the condemned. The Book's descriptions of the day include the light that runs before the believers and the signs by which the two groups are known.

The recognition in front of us establishes the knowledge of the people on the heights, and the chapter's later verses state what they do with it. They address the people of the Garden with the greeting of peace, and they address some of the people of the Fire with the observation that their numbers and their arrogance have not helped them (7:48). The station on the heights is therefore a place of observation and address rather than of judgment.

**The Greeting of Peace**

The verse reports that the people of the heights call out to the people of the Garden with the greeting of peace, and the greeting is the standard salutation of the saved. The Book reports it among the people of the Garden themselves, where they greet one another with peace, and the sūrah of the Heights will state that the people of the Garden have had rancour removed from their hearts (7:43). The greeting in front of us is given from the barrier, and it is answered by the state of those who receive it.

The people who give the greeting have not entered the Garden at the moment of the scene, and the verse states that they hope to. The commentary tradition draws from this the conclusion that their position is neither that of the saved who have entered nor that of the condemned. They are close enough to greet the first group and far enough to fear the second, and the verse that follows records their prayer when they look at the Fire.

**Who the People of the Heights Are**

The commentary tradition reports several identifications of the people on the heights, and each identification rests on a reading of the verse. One reading holds that they are those whose good and evil deeds are equal, so that the balance does not carry them into the Garden and does not consign them to the Fire; a second holds that they are believers who fell short in their works; and a third holds that they are the martyrs who were killed in the way of God while owing an obligation that was not discharged. The reports differ, and the disagreement concerns who is placed at the barrier rather than whether the barrier exists.

The weighing of the identifications turns on the rest of the passage. The people on the heights greet the people of the Garden and hope to enter, and they pray not to be placed among the wrongdoers when they look at the Fire. The two actions describe a group that belongs to the company of the saved in its hope and shares nothing with the condemned in its fear, and that description is the one the passage supports.

**Where the Verse Sits**

The verse stands forty-sixth in the chapter, and the sūrah's name is drawn from the place it names. It follows the proclamation of the curse on the wrongdoers and precedes the prayer of the people on the heights and their address to the dwellers of the Fire. The scene in front of us places a third station between the two that the previous verses have described.

The reader who has followed the sūrah from its opening has been given the Book, the command, the reckoning, the account of the first pair, the warnings against the practices of the audience, and the two ends of the judgment. The verse in front of us adds the station above the barrier, and the remaining verses of the passage complete the scene with the prayer and the address that are made from it.

**The Transition to the Next Verse**

The next verse reports that when the eyes of the people on the heights turn towards the people of the Fire, they pray that their Lord not place them among the wrongdoing people (7:47). The greeting in front of us is followed by the prayer that the sight of the Fire produces, and the speech of the station between the two outcomes is given both of its parts.

The two verses together complete the description of the people on the heights. They see both sides, they greet the first, and they pray to be spared the company of the second; and the sūrah's following verses report the address they make to the proud among the condemned.

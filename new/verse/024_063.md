## Sūrah an-Nūr 24:63

> Do not treat the Messenger’s summons to you ˹as lightly˺ as your summons to one another. Allah certainly knows those of you who slip away, hiding behind others. So let those who disobey his orders beware, for an affliction may befall them, or a painful torment may overtake them.

**Expanded Commentary**

**Revering the Prophetic Summons: lā tajʿalū duʿāʾa al-rasūl**

The divine speech issues an urgent constitutional command governing decorum and reverence toward prophetic leadership: *lā tajʿalū duʿāʾa al-rasūli baynakum ka-duʿāʾi baʿḍikum baʿḍā*. Classical commentators, including Ibn ʿAbbās, al-Ṭabarī, and al-Qurṭubī, articulated two complementary and mutually reinforcing interpretations of *duʿāʾ al-rasūl*.

First, it refers to the community addressing the Prophet ﷺ: believers are strictly forbidden from calling out to him casually by his bare personal name—such as saying "O Muḥammad!" as they would address a peer in the marketplace. Instead, they must address him with reverent titles of honor, saying "O Messenger of God!" or "O Prophet of God!" Second, it refers to the Prophet calling the community to action: when the Messenger of God summons the people to an assembly, a defensive mobilization, or a judicial hearing, his summons carries absolute, divine authority. It cannot be treated casually, postponed for convenience, or ignored like an ordinary social invitation from an acquaintance. To heed the Prophet's call is an immediate, non-negotiable religious obligation.

**The Anatomy of Cowardly Evasion: yatasallalūna minkum liwādhā**

The verse provides an incisive exposure of the subterranean tactics practiced by the hypocrites: *qad yaʿlamu Allāhu alladhīna yatasallalūna minkum liwādhā*. The verb *yatasallalūn* depicts sneaking away stealthily, creeping out of sight, and slipping through exits one by one without drawing attention.

The adverbial noun *liwādhā* derives from *lādha bi-al-shayʾ* (to take shelter or seek cover behind something). During critical public assemblies or difficult defensive labors—such as digging the trench in freezing winds—the hypocrites lacked the courage to ask the Prophet ﷺ for permission, knowing their excuses were fraudulent. Instead, they practiced physical deceit: an individual would position himself directly behind the broad back of a sincere companion, and when the Prophet looked elsewhere, he would slip behind another companion, gradually edging his way toward the perimeter until he bolted into the shadows. This behavior demonstrated their complete moral bankruptcy, cowardice, and contempt for communal solidarity.

**The All-Seeing Eye of Divine Surveillance: qad yaʿlamu Allāh**

The hypocrites' calculated evasions are shattered by an absolute assertion of divine omniscience: *qad yaʿlamu Allāh*, Allah certainly knows! The particle *qad* combined with the imperfect verb *yaʿlam* communicates absolute, continuous, and inescapable awareness.

The conspirators imagined that by shielding their bodies behind others, they had successfully deceived the Prophet ﷺ and protected themselves from embarrassment. The Qur'an reminds them that while they might conceal their movements from human eyesight, they stand completely exposed before the Lord of the worlds. God watches every furtive glance, tracks every creeping step, and knows the cowardly calculations festering within their chests. The declaration of divine surveillance reassures the sincere believers who remained steadfast at their posts that their loyalty is seen and cherished, while serving as a terrifying warning to the deserters that their treachery is recorded in full, awaiting eschatological reckoning.

**The Strict Prohibition of Defying Prophetic Decrees: yukhālifūna ʿan amrih**

The verse transitions into an ominous, universal warning that forms a cornerstone of Islamic constitutional law: *fa-lyaḥdhari alladhīna yukhālifūna ʿan amrih*. The verb *yukhālifūn ʿan amrih* denotes deviating from, opposing, contradicting, or turning away from the commands of the Prophet ﷺ.

In Islamic jurisprudence, this phrase establishes the supreme legal authority of the Sunnah. Prophetic commands are not advisory suggestions or optional moral recommendations; they are binding legal statutes backed by divine authority. To deliberately contradict or abandon an explicit prophetic command is an act of spiritual rebellion that endangers both the individual soul and the collective stability of the community. The imperative *fa-lyaḥdhar* (let them beware!) sounds an alarm across history, commanding every generation to audit their laws, policies, and personal choices against the unyielding standard of prophetic guidance, preserving the community from deviation.

**Temporal Catastrophe: an tuṣībahum fitnah**

The divine warning delineates two distinct categories of retribution awaiting those who defy prophetic leadership, beginning with temporal calamity: *an tuṣībahum fitnah*, lest an affliction/trial befall them. In Qur'anic semantics, *fitnah* encompasses severe worldly trials, social chaos, political corruption, and internal spiritual blindness.

Classical authorities explained that when a society abandons prophetic guidance, divine protection is withdrawn, exposing the community to catastrophic worldly consequences and spiritual blindness. It manifests as tyrannical misrule, civil warfare, economic collapse, moral degeneration, and the creeping hardness of hearts that can no longer distinguish truth from falsehood. On an individual level, the person who persistently defies prophetic commands suffers the *fitnah* of doubts, emotional misery, and the loss of faith. Contradicting prophetic wisdom inevitably unleashes destructive societal forces that tear communities apart from within, plunging them into chaos.

**Eschatological Retribution: aw yuṣībahum ʿadhābun alīm**

If the temporal calamity does not awaken the defiant soul, an infinitely worse fate awaits them in the Hereafter: *aw yuṣībahum ʿadhābun alīm*, or a painful torment may overtake them. The disjunctive particle *aw* indicates that defiance brings either worldly devastation, eschatological ruin, or both.

The adjective *alīm* conveys excruciating, agonizing pain that pierces the soul and body in the blazing fires of Hell. The hypocrites who sneaked away behind their companions to avoid a few hours of physical labor or military service will find that their brief worldly comfort was purchased at the cost of unimaginable, eternal torment. Verse 63 stands as a monumental pillar of Sūrah an-Nūr, establishing that true civilizational survival, moral sanity, and spiritual salvation are found solely in unwavering, reverent submission to the commands of the Messenger of God ﷺ, honoring his prophetic station across all generations.

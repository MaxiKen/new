## Sūrah al-Shuʿarāʾ 26:100

> Now we have none to intercede for us,

**Expanded Commentary**

**The Condemned Discover That Their Intercessors Are Absent**

The speakers say that they have no intercessors. Their confession exposes a hope that had supported idolatry: the belief that the objects they worshipped could plead for them before God. In the final scene, the supposed advocates are absent. The people who trusted them are left without the protection they imagined.

Intercession is not simply a word for help. It means another standing before the Judge on behalf of the accused. The idolaters assigned that role to created objects without proof. Their expectation collapses because the objects cannot hear, speak, or possess authority. A claim to mediation cannot create a mediator.

**The Verse Rejects False Intercession**

This verse should be read as a judgment on the intercessors claimed by the idolaters, not as a careless denial of every form of intercession in every context. The Qur'anic doctrine places intercession under God's ownership and permission. What is rejected here is the assumption that idols possess an independent right to secure a person's forgiveness.

That distinction matters. A servant cannot demand that God accept a created advocate, and no object can make an unauthorized claim on God's judgment. True intercession, where granted, does not compete with divine sovereignty. The false intercession of the idolaters tried to turn worship of created things into a guaranteed shelter, and this verse shows that shelter to be empty.

**A Mediator Cannot Replace the Lord**

The worshippers treated the idols as a bridge to God, but the bridge had no authority at either end. The objects could not receive their prayers and could not present a case before the Lord. Their supposed position between God and humanity was created by human imagination and maintained by ritual.

A created person may convey a message, teach, advise, or pray for another within ordinary limits. None of these acts makes that person a divine partner. The line is crossed when the mediator is worshipped or treated as an independent controller of forgiveness. The condemned discover that the role they assigned to idols was never theirs to exercise.

**The Loss Is the Loss of a False Hope**

The words now we have none carry a sense of sudden emptiness. The people once believed they had powerful allies. Their loss is not the removal of a real protection but the exposure of a protection that never existed. The pain comes from discovering that their confidence was built on a false account of God and the world.

False hope can be comforting for a time because it allows a person to avoid direct responsibility before God. If an object can always intercede, repentance can be delayed. If a shrine can absorb guilt, the heart need not change. The final absence of intercessors shows the cost of postponing a direct return to the Lord.

**God's Permission Is the Measure of Intercession**

The verified parallel in 2:255 places any intercession under God's permission. That principle keeps the subject within monotheism. An intercessor does not act as a rival power or force God's decision. The intercessor's ability, speech, and acceptance are all granted by the Lord.

This verse therefore teaches caution in religious hope. A person should not invent an advocate, attach salvation to an object, or treat a ritual as a guarantee. He should ask God directly for mercy and accept that any aid in the Hereafter belongs to God's wisdom. Hope remains possible, but it must be directed through the Lord rather than around Him.

**Family and Status Cannot Become Intercessors by Themselves**

The surrounding prayer has already removed wealth and children as final supports. This verse removes another support: the imagined advocate. A person cannot rely on family name, social rank, or a sacred object to perform the moral work that belongs to his own response to God.

This does not make prayer for others meaningless. Abraham prays for his father and for believers, but he asks God to forgive; he does not claim power to forgive. The distinction is between supplication offered to God and worship offered to a supposed intermediary. The first acknowledges divine authority, while the second invents a rival refuge.

Quran 29:25 describes idolaters forming worldly bonds around their idols, followed by mutual disowning and cursing on the Day of Judgment. That parallel explains why the absence of intercessors is so painful. The alliances that seemed to secure belonging cannot survive the final truth. The object, the leader, and the follower cannot save one another.

The verse before us narrows that collapse to one desperate need: someone to speak for them. No one is available because the people invested the power of advocacy in beings who never possessed it. Their social bond was strong enough to shape life, but not strong enough to change judgment.

**The Lack of Intercessors Reveals Misplaced Worship**

The problem is not simply that the people have no helpers. It is that they sought help from the wrong place. If they had turned to God, confessed dependence, and pursued a sound heart, their hope would have been rooted in reality. Instead, they gave worship to objects and expected those objects to repair the result.

A person can repeat this mistake in subtler forms. He may treat a teacher, institution, lineage, or public identity as though association with it guarantees acceptance. The verse asks what is being trusted to stand between the servant and God. If the answer is a created thing, the heart has begun to seek an intercessor where only God's permission can establish one.

**The Sentence Is an Invitation Before It Becomes a Verdict**

The condemned say that they have none. The living still have the opportunity to call upon Allah, seek forgiveness, and place hope in His mercy without inventing a partner. The warning is therefore not meant to make believers abandon hope. It is meant to purify hope from false guarantees.

Direct dependence on God is not spiritual isolation. It includes lawful means, sincere prayer, righteous companionship, and the hope that God may accept the supplication of His servants. But every means remains a means. The final refuge is the Lord, and no intercessor can be treated as independent of Him.

**Only Truthful Hope Survives the Final Day**

The absence of intercessors completes the exposure of idolatrous security. The idols could not hear, help, or protect themselves, and they cannot speak for those who worshipped them. What remains is the servant's relation to God, formed through faith, repentance, and obedience.

The verse therefore directs the present heart toward a better hope. Do not wait for an object to carry a devotion it cannot receive. Do not mistake family, wealth, or reputation for a shield. Seek God's mercy directly, and accept that every accepted aid in the Hereafter comes by His permission. A hope built on Him will not disappear when judgment arrives.

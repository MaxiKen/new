## Sūrah al-Naḥl 16:23

> **Without a doubt, Allah knows what they conceal and what they reveal. He certainly does not like those who are too proud.**

**Expanded Commentary**

**Certainty Of Divine Knowledge**

Lā jarama anna Allāha yaʿlamu mā yusirrūna wa-mā yuʿlinūn — without doubt, Allah knows what they conceal and what they reveal. Lā jarama is an emphatic expression meaning no doubt, certainly, without question. It introduces a statement that is definitive. The phrase repeats verse 19 almost verbatim: *Allah knows what you conceal and reveal*, but now with lā jarama and with third person yusirrūna, what they conceal, referring to those who deny Hereafter and are arrogant in previous verse. Al-Ṭabarī explains that this is to stress that Allah knows their denial and arrogance even when they hide it with polite words or public justifications. What they conceal includes hidden motives: pride, desire to keep status, fear of losing customers for idols. What they reveal includes open mockery: *ancient fables* (16:24). Both are known. The repetition from verse 19 to 23 shows that knowledge was mentioned first as general principle, now as specific warning to deniers.

**He Does Not Like The Arrogant**

Innahu lā yuḥibbu al-mustakbirīn — He certainly does not like those who are too proud. This is the first explicit statement of divine dislike in the blessings passage. After describing blessings and idols, the sūrah now states Allah's attitude toward arrogance. Lā yuḥibbu means does not love, does not like, does not approve. Al-mustakbirīn are the arrogant, those who deem themselves great and refuse truth. Al-Zamakhsharī notes that divine love and dislike in Qur'an are tied to qualities and deeds, not arbitrary: Allah loves the grateful, the just, the patient; He does not love the arrogant, the unjust, the wasteful. Arrogance is singled out here because it was identified as root of denial in verse 22. The Makkans' refusal to accept tawḥīd was not due to lack of evidence but to istikbār, considering themselves too great to follow a human messenger and abandon ancestral worship. The verse says Allah does not like that quality, which implies threat: those who persist in what Allah does not like will face consequence.

**Why Knowledge And Dislike Are Paired**

The pairing of knowledge and dislike is logical: because Allah knows what they conceal and reveal, He knows who is arrogant, and because He knows, His dislike is just, not based on assumption. Human dislike can be based on incomplete knowledge; divine dislike is based on complete knowledge of hidden and open. Al-Rāzī explains that lā jarama emphasizes that Allah's knowledge of their arrogance is certain, so His dislike is certain and justified. The verse also serves as warning that concealment does not protect from dislike. A person may hide arrogance behind humility, but Allah knows hidden arrogance. Conversely, a person may appear arrogant but have humble heart, and Allah knows that too. The knowledge covers both directions, ensuring justice. This is why the verse comes after describing hearts as munkiratun, denying: Allah knows that denial inside hearts, even if tongues say otherwise.

**The Connection To Next Verse About Fables**

Verse 24 will say when they are asked what has your Lord revealed, they say ancient fables. That answer is an example of what they reveal openly, but its root is concealed arrogance. They say fables because they deem themselves too great to accept revelation as guidance. The verse 23 therefore prepares for 24 by stating Allah knows both hidden arrogance and open dismissal. Al-Qurṭubī notes that lā yuḥibbu al-mustakbirīn is both statement and threat: if Allah does not love arrogant, then arrogant will not be guided, as 16:37 will say *Allah does not guide those He leaves to stray*, and will end in Hell as 16:29 says *evil abode of arrogant*. The sequence from knowledge to dislike to punishment is consistent.

**Arrogance As Barrier To Gratitude**

In Sūrat al-Niʿam, arrogance is opposite of gratitude. Grateful person recognizes blessing comes from above and humbles self before Giver. Arrogant person attributes blessing to self or denies Giver, refusing to humble. The sūrah's blessings catalogue is meant to humble: you were a drop, you were given cattle, rain, crops, sea, mountains, all for you, yet you cannot even count blessings. Arrogance refuses this humbling. It says, I deserve this, or I made this, or no one gave this. Allah does not like that attitude because it falsifies reality and prevents gratitude, which is purpose of creation. The verse therefore is not only about belief in Hereafter but about character that makes belief possible: humility vs arrogance.

**Linguistic Note On Lā Jarama And Mustakbirīn**

Lā jarama is an idiom meaning no doubt, certainly, from j-r-m meaning to cut, to decide, so lā jarama means no cutting off of certainty, i.e., undoubtedly. Some grammarians treat it as meaning inevitably, with no escape. Innahu refers to Allah. Lā yuḥibbu is negated love, indicating dislike, disapproval. Al-mustakbirīn from k-b-r, to be great, in form X meaning to seek greatness, to deem self great, to show arrogance. The form X indicates effort to be great, not merely being great. This nuance matters: arrogance is not accidental greatness but sought, performed greatness. The verse's two parts — knowledge and dislike — are both in present tense, indicating ongoing reality: Allah knows ongoing concealment and revelation, and He does not like ongoing arrogance. The warning is therefore current, not only future.

**Why Dislike Of Arrogance Is Mercy To Others**

Allah's dislike of arrogance protects the humble. When arrogant are left unchecked, they oppress, mock revelation, and mislead others, as verses 24-25 show. By stating He does not like the arrogant, Allah warns that arrogance will not be allowed to dominate forever. The oppressed who hear this find comfort: the One who knows what arrogant conceal and reveal does not love their arrogance, so He will eventually hold them accountable. Al-Rāzī notes that divine dislike implies withdrawal of support and guidance, as 16:37 says Allah does not guide those He leaves to stray, and that withdrawal is itself consequence of arrogance, not arbitrary. The person who insists on being great will be left to his own smallness. The humble who recognize blessings and accept Oneness will find love, as Allah loves the grateful and patient.

**The Repetition Of Knowledge Phrase**

The phrase yaʿlamu mā yusirrūna wa-mā yuʿlinūn appears in 16:19 and again here in 16:23, almost identical. Repetition in Qur'an is never without purpose. First occurrence came after uncountable blessings and forgiveness, to remind that forgiveness requires sincerity. Second comes after describing hearts that deny and are arrogant, to warn that denial and arrogance are known even when hidden. The repetition frames the argument against idols: between two statements of divine knowledge, idols are shown to be ignorant and dead. The contrast is intentional: Allah knows hidden and open; idols know nothing, not even when they will be raised. Which deserves worship? The repetition also emphasizes that knowledge is central to divinity. A god who does not know cannot guide, cannot judge, cannot forgive. Allah's knowledge is what makes His forgiveness meaningful and His dislike just.

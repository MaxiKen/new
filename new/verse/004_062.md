## Sūrah al-Nisāʾ 4:62

> How ˹horrible˺ will it be if a disaster strikes them because of what their hands have done, then they come to you swearing by Allah, “We intended nothing but goodwill and reconciliation.”

**Expanded Commentary**

**How Will It Be When a Disaster Strikes Them**

The question *fa kayfa idhā aṣābathum muṣībatun bi mā qaddamat aydīhim* — how will it be if a disaster strikes them because of what their hands have done — mirrors the sūrah’s earlier *how will it be when We bring a witness from every community* (4:41) and *how will it be if they are struck*. *Muṣībah* — disaster, affliction that hits the mark — was read as any calamity that exposes the hypocrite’s referral to false judges (4:60) and his stubborn turning from the revelations and Messenger (4:61): defeat, exposure, punishment, or the failure of the alliance with *jibt* and *ṭāghūt* that did not help (4:52). *Bi mā qaddamat aydīhim* — because of what their hands have done — ties the disaster to their own prior acts, as the sūrah has tied Fire to deliberate devouring where *that is easy for Allah* (4:30) and humiliating punishment to concealing bounty (4:37). The question *kayfa* — how — is left hanging, as it was with the witness scene, so the listener feels the shame before it is described. Those who sought the false judge for advantage will find advantage become affliction, because the sūrah has said that the false judge’s path is Satan’s desire to lead farther away (4:60) and farther away ends in no helper.

**Then They Come to You Swearing Goodwill**

The scene that follows *thumma jāʾūka yaḥlifūna bi Allāhi in aradnā illā iḥsānan wa tawfīqā* — then they come to you swearing by Allah, we intended nothing but goodwill and reconciliation — was read as the hypocrites returning after the disaster with oaths that they meant only good. *Iḥsān* — kindness, doing good — and *tawfīq* — reconciliation, bringing together — sound like the sūrah’s own aims where it said appoint mediators from families, and if they desire reconciliation God will bring harmony (4:35). The hypocrites borrow that language after the fact, claiming their request to *ṭāghūt* was an attempt to reconcile rather than to deviate. Early reports placed this among hypocrites who, after being exposed, excused their resort to a soothsayer as seeking amicability between factions. The sūrah presents the excuse as oath — *yaḥlifūna bi Allāhi* — swearing, the same form the Qur’an elsewhere says the hypocrites use as shield. To swear by God that only goodwill was intended while the Book and Messenger were turned from (4:61) is to use the Name that the sūrah said is ever Watchful (4:1) and All-Hearing, All-Seeing (4:58) as cover for what the hands already did. The sūrah has said that on the Day when witnesses are brought, no statement can be hidden from God (4:42); this oath anticipates that Day on a smaller stage, where the speaker thinks words can hide hands.

**Because of What Their Hands Have Done**

The phrase *bi mā qaddamat aydīhim* was stressed as tying the disaster to prior choice, not to fate alone. The hands that sought *ṭāghūt* — the hands that signed, paid, or promised alliance — are the same hands to which the disaster is traced. The sūrah has tracked hands throughout: the hand that mixes orphan wealth (4:2), that reclaims a heap of gold by slander (4:20), that trades a portion of the Book (4:44), that distorts words with tongues (4:46), and that fabricates lies against God as blatant sin (4:50). Each act was done by hands that later claim goodwill. To say disaster strikes because of what hands put forward is to say recompense is continuous, as skin renewed for tasting is continuous (4:56) and as an atom’s weight of good is multiplied continuously (4:40). The hypocrites’ oath *we intended nothing but goodwill and reconciliation* tries to separate intention from act, claiming a good will behind a bad referral. The verse keeps intention and act together by attributing the *muṣībah* to what hands did, not only to what hearts wished. A will that chose *ṭāghūt* over the Book and Messenger is already known — *Allah knows what is in their hearts* as the next verse will say (4:63) — so the hands’ work is the evidence of it.

**Goodwill and Reconciliation as Borrowed Language**

The words *iḥsān* and *tawfīq* in the oath are not condemned in themselves; the sūrah commends both where they are real — *iḥsān* to parents, relatives, orphans, poor, neighbours, companions, travellers, and those in possession (4:36), and *tawfīq* between spouses where mediators desire harmony and God brings it (4:35). What the verse exposes is borrowed goodness after disaster, not goodwill before. To seek *ṭāghūt* for judgment is not *iḥsān* toward the community, because *iḥsān* in judgment is to return trusts and judge with fairness as a noble exhortation (4:58) and to refer disputes to God and Messenger as best outcome (4:59). To seek *ṭāghūt* is the opposite of *tawfīq*; it leads farther away, as Satan desires (4:60). When disaster follows — exposure, loss of trust, or defeat — to come swearing goodwill is to ask that the borrowed words cover the referral. The sūrah has already said that failure to repent until death is present is not accepted (4:18) and that turning soon is accepted (4:17); the oath after disaster is closer to the first than the second, because it comes after the hands’ work and only when the cover is needed.

**How Will It Be — The Unanswered Question As Warning**

As with 4:41’s *how will it be when witnesses are brought*, the question *how will it be if a disaster strikes* is left open, inviting the listener to imagine the hypocrites’ standing before the Prophet ﷺ with oaths, and more broadly before God with perfect knowledge of them (4:39) and before the Day when no speech can be hidden (4:42). The horror — *how horrible will it be* as rendered — lies in the combination: a disaster earned by hands, and oaths that try to rename the hands’ work as goodwill. The sūrah prepares the answer with counsel that God knows their hearts and directs turning away, cautioning, and penetrating inner advice (4:63). That counsel treats the oaths as known and insufficient; what is needed is not more oaths but an admonition that reaches the soul. For the believers the question is also a mirror: to avoid being those who must come swearing after disaster, refer disputes while they are small to the Book and Messenger, keep trusts, judge fairly, and desire reconciliation sincerely, so that when hardship comes it does not find hands that had already put forward a referral to false judges, but hands that had put forward belief, good, and a great reward from grace.

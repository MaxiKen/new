## Sūrah al-Nisāʾ 4:62

> How ˹horrible˺ will it be if a disaster strikes them because of what their hands have done, then they come to you swearing by Allah, “We intended nothing but goodwill and reconciliation.”

### Expanded Commentary

**The excuse after consequences arrive**

The verse imagines the hypocrites after a disaster caused by their own choices. They return swearing that they intended only goodwill and reconciliation. The timing exposes the weakness of the claim: when the cost was distant, they resisted; when the consequence was visible, they offered a pious explanation.

**Good intentions cannot sanctify betrayal**

Islam values intention, but an intention cannot be proven by a slogan after harm has occurred. A person who undermines a just process and later says he meant peace must examine whether “peace” was being used to protect status or avoid accountability. Reconciliation requires truth about what happened, repair, and changed conduct.

**A warning for crisis leadership**

Communities often hear claims of good will from those who opposed necessary action until success became likely. The verse teaches leaders to assess patterns, evidence, and responsibility rather than accepting every late declaration. It also warns the accused not to become cynical: Allah knows the heart, and a sincere person may still repent. The final judgment does not belong to public suspicion.

**Late explanations cannot erase early choices**

When consequences reveal a plan’s harm, the person who claims good intentions should be asked to repair the damage, not merely repeat the claim. Reconciliation is proved by changed behaviour and restitution. The verse warns against the habit of using pious vocabulary only after an alliance has failed.

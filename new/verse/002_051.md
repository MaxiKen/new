## Sūrah al-Baqarah 2:51

> And ˹remember˺ when We appointed forty nights for Moses, then you worshipped the calf in his absence, acting wrongfully.

**Expanded Commentary**

**Forty Nights Appointed — And a People Unappointed**

“And remember when We appointed forty nights for Moses, then you worshipped the calf in his absence, acting wrongfully” (2:51). Wa idh wāʿadnā Mūsā arbaʿīna laylah — “We made an appointment with Moses” for forty nights, also read as “We promised Moses” (another qirāʾah), as the source notes. The referent is the seclusion on Mount Sinai — the forty-night retreat where revelation was received — paralleled in 7:142 where thirty nights plus ten complete the term.

The appointment is measured in nights (layālī), not days: in the Semitic calendar night precedes day, and revelation’s retreat is kept by night watch. The precise forty recalls formation through waiting: the deliverance through water (2:50) is followed not by immediate law but by interval, so that the delivered learn to wait. What follows shows they had not yet learned: thumma ittakhadhtumu al-ʿijla min baʿdihi — “then you took up the calf after him” — in his absence, in his wake, while he was away.

**“You Worshipped the Calf” — Taking Up What Was Already There**

Ittakhadhtumu al-ʿijla — you took the calf as object of worship. The calf (ʿijl) appears elsewhere as the act clarified in 7:148–54, and is recalled in 2:92–93 and 4:153 and in 20:88 where the calf is said to low. The sūrah does not here name the maker or the art; it names the act: you took it up. The verb suggests not invention from nothing but taking up what Egyptian sojourn and impulse had made available: a familiar form made god when the familiar mediator — Moses — was absent.

The absence matters. “After him” — min baʿdihi — can mean after his departure and after his deliverance. The one through whom the sea parted is not yet out of sight before the people find another image to follow. Gratitude for deliverance (2:52: “that you may be grateful”) is tested precisely where presence is withdrawn.

**“Acting Wrongfully” — Ẓālimūn Even Before Punishment Falls**

Wa antum ẓālimūn — “while you were wrongdoers” (2:51). The clause states the act’s name before its consequence. Ẓulm — to place a thing where it does not belong — captures calf-worship perfectly: the heart places dependence on a lowing image that cannot hear (cf. 7:148: “did they not see it did not speak to them nor guide them?”). The people are called wrongdoers while the calf still stands, not only after Moses returns and shatters it. The sūrah names before it narrates retribution.

**Why Forty Nights Matters**

The term is not incidental duration but theological spacing: thirty plus ten (7:142) as fast and completion, or simply forty as fullness. It mirrors Moses’ formation for reception: revelation is not downloaded on demand but awaited. The Children of Israel’s failure is therefore not that they waited, but that they filled waiting with an image. Patience (ṣabr), which the sūrah only recently counselled to seek help through (2:45), fails here: to seek help in patience and prayer would have kept the interval; to refuse patience was to manufacture help.

**From Rescue to Apostasy — How Quickly “After” Arrives**

Thumma — “then” — in “then you worshipped the calf” carries bluntness: after the sea, after sight, after deliverance before eyes (2:50), then the calf. The recital’s bluntness is pedagogical. The sūrah keeps the community’s history unvarnished: the same people who watched water stand watched a golden calf low, and chose it. Later verses will show the same people granted pardon after this very act (2:52) and then given the Book and the Criterion (2:53), so that mercy and guidance are set against stark wrong.

**The Calf as Perennial Type**

While calf-worship is historically Egyptianized — Hathor, Apis, and the memory of a bovine deity of increase — the type recurs whenever a community, deprived of a trusted mediator, elevates a glittering form that promises immediacy without obedience. The source notes the calf’s low — a sound that simulates life — as part of its deceit: an image that seems alive because it sounds. The Qur’an’s critique is not of art as such but of taking art as lord.

**Living the Forty Nights**

- Keep appointments when presence is withdrawn. The test given to Israel — forty nights without the leader — is given in smaller forms to every community between a teacher’s visits: will you hold the appointment or fill it?
- Measure ẓulm by placement. To ask “are we wrongdoers?” (2:51) is to ask “have we placed ultimate reliance where it cannot speak or guide?”
- Convert waiting into prayer. The one who sought help in patience and prayer (2:45) would not have taken the calf; the forty nights were given so that help could be sought.

This verse does not name the calf’s maker here (elsewhere Sāmirī in 20:85–96), nor detail the gold’s source, nor narrate Aaron’s role. Those are supplied elsewhere and should not be imported as if stated here. What is firm is the appointment of forty nights for Moses, the taking up of the calf in his absence, and the naming of that taking as wrongdoing while still in it (2:51).

## Sūrah al-Anʿām 6:153

> Indeed, that is My Path—perfectly straight. So follow it and do not follow other ways, for they will lead you away from His Way. This is what He has commanded you, so perhaps you will be conscious ˹of Allah˺.”

**Expanded Commentary**

**The Commands Gathered Into a Path**

Indeed, that is My Path, perfectly straight. The sentence gathers the commands of the two preceding verses into a single image, and the demonstrative reaches back over the whole recitation. What the sūrah has listed — the worship of God alone, the honouring of parents, the protection of children, the avoidance of indecency, the sanctity of life, the care of orphans, honest measure, just speech, and the keeping of covenants — is presented as one road rather than as a set of separate rules.

The image has been prepared throughout the chapter. The sūrah asked the Prophet to say that his Lord has guided him to a straight path in the verse that will appear shortly after this one (6:161), and its earlier passage on guidance described the path of the Lord as straight and stated that the signs had been made clear to those who are mindful (6:126). The Book's use of the image at the opening of the Qur'an, where the community prays for guidance upon the straight path (1:6), gives the metaphor its place in the community's daily worship.

**The Command to Follow and the Warning**

So follow it and do not follow other ways, for they will lead you away from His Way. The sentence joins the command to the warning, and the Book's term for the other ways describes paths that branch off from the main road. The image is that of a traveller who has been shown the way and is told that the alternatives lead in another direction, and the Book uses the same figure in its warnings against division.

In the sūrah of the Family of Imran, the community is commanded to hold firmly to the rope of God and not to be divided (3:103), and the sūrah of the Romans describes those who have divided their faith and split into sects, each rejoicing in what they have (30:32). The sūrah of the Cattle itself states that the Prophet bears no responsibility for those who have divided their religion into sects (6:159), and the verse in front of us supplies the command that keeps the community from that condition.

**The Path and the Covenant**

The path is identified with the content of the covenant that the two previous verses have stated. To follow the path is to keep the commands; to leave it is to break them. The Book's habit of presenting its requirements as a road gives the reader a way of understanding what the rules are for, and the verse in front of us completes the recitation by naming its destination.

The Book uses the same connection in other passages that summarize its requirements. The declaration attributed to Jesus in the sūrah of the Family of Imran closes with the statement that God is his Lord and theirs, that they should worship Him, and that this is the straight path (3:51), and the sūrah of the Consultation says that the revelation is a light by which God guides whom He wills to a straight path (42:52). The verse in front of us belongs to that family, and it stands at the end of the sūrah's list of commands.

**The Third Closing Appeal**

This is what He has commanded you, so perhaps you will be conscious of Allah. The sentence closes the third of the appeals that end the recitation, and the three together address understanding, mindfulness, and the fear of God. The Book's closing formulas in these passages are not decorative; they state the response the commands are meant to produce, and the progression from understanding to consciousness of God describes a deepening of that response.

The comparable list in the sūrah of the Night Journey ends with a single appeal, while the sūrah of the Cattle distributes the appeals across its three verses. The arrangement lets the reader meet the same requirements three times with a different invitation attached to each, and the verse in front of us supplies the last of them.

**What the Verse Says About the Religion's Content**

The verse states that the religion the sūrah has been presenting is a way of conduct rather than a collection of doctrines alone. The commands listed in the two previous verses concern the family, the market, the court, and the neighbour, and the sentence in front of us treats them as the road itself. The sūrah's argument against the audience's religion has concerned both its theology and its practices, and the positive statement that follows concerns the same two things.

The Book's treatment of the community's obligations elsewhere follows the same pattern. The long passage of legislation in the sūrah of the Cow covers worship, family law, commerce, and the treatment of the weak, and it ends with the declaration that God does not require of any soul more than it can afford. The verse in front of us states the principle that unites those requirements, and the image of the path gives them their direction.

**The Place of the Verse in the Sūrah**

The verse closes the passage that began with the demand for witnesses and the recitation of what God has forbidden (6:150–152). The sūrah's survey of the audience's system is finished, its argument against the audience's excuses is complete, and the requirements of the covenant have been stated. What remains in the chapter is the closing section, which returns to the Book itself and to the testimony that the earlier scriptures bear to it.

The verse that follows this one turns to Moses and the Scripture given to him, describing it as complete for those who do good, detailed in everything, and a guide and a mercy (6:154). The transition from the path to the earlier scripture is deliberate: the way that the community is commanded to follow is the same way that the earlier books marked out.

**The Transition to the Next Verse**

The next verse states that God gave Moses the Scripture as a completion of the favour upon those who do good, as a detailed explanation of everything, and as a guide and a mercy, so that the people might be certain of the meeting with their Lord (6:154). The account of the earlier revelation follows the recitation of the commands, and it prepares the reader for the passage in which the community is told that the Book has now come to it.

The verses that follow describe the blessing of the Book that has been revealed and answer the excuses of the people who claimed that the scriptures had been given only to the communities before them (6:155–157). The verse in front of us supplies the image that unites the sūrah's commandments, and the reader who takes it up is placed on the road at the end of the argument.

## Sūrah al-Tawbah 9:109

> Which is better: those who laid the foundation of their building on the fear and pleasure of Allah, or those who did so on the edge of a crumbling cliff that tumbled down with them into the Fire of Hell? And Allah does not guide the wrongdoing people.

**Expanded Commentary**

**Every Building Rests on a Moral Foundation**

The verse compares two builders. One lays a foundation on reverence for Allah and the desire for His pleasure. The other builds on the edge of a crumbling cliff and falls with it into the Fire. The image asks the reader to evaluate not the height of the building but the ground beneath it.

A project may be large, efficient, and admired while resting on falsehood. A family, charity, school, or public movement can look strong yet depend on deception, injustice, or factional hatred. The first question is therefore not “How impressive is it?” but “What will remain when pressure tests the foundation?” Reverence gives an institution a reason to serve beyond its own survival.

**Fear and Pleasure Belong Together**

The foundation is built on fear and pleasure of Allah. Fear keeps the builder from crossing limits, while desire for Allah's pleasure gives the work a positive aim. Fear without hope can produce paralysis or harshness. Hope without fear can produce careless claims of good intention.

A sound institution needs both. It fears misuse of funds, pride in leadership, and harm to people. It seeks Allah's pleasure through clear benefit, truthful teaching, and protection of the vulnerable. This balance should govern small personal projects as well. The servant asks what Allah forbids, what He loves, and how the work can remain within those boundaries.

**A Crumbling Cliff Gives False Security**

The second foundation is compared to a cliff edge ready to collapse. Such ground may look firm from a distance, but it cannot bear weight. The image captures a project founded on unstable motives. As long as no pressure comes, the builders may praise their structure. When truth or accountability arrives, the ground gives way.

False foundations include secrecy, inflated reputation, hatred of sincere believers, and the claim that a good goal excuses every means. They also include dependence on one powerful person rather than on principles that survive personal change. A wise builder looks for cracks before the building is occupied. Repentance and reform are cheaper before collapse than after people have been harmed.

**Collapse Carries the Builders With It**

The cliff does not merely fail beneath the structure; it takes the builders into the Fire. The image links institution and character. People who build a harmful project are also being shaped by it. Every meeting in which they defend falsehood, every donation used for division, and every oath used to protect the scheme deepens their investment in the wrong foundation.

This warns leaders not to treat an organization as morally separate from themselves. A project can outlive its founders, but the founders remain accountable for what they knowingly built. The believer therefore welcomes review and correction. A structure that survives only because no one is allowed to question it is already near the cliff.

**The Better Building Is Chosen by Its Ground**

The question “which is better?” expects an obvious answer, but it also challenges the viewer's priorities. People often compare size, visibility, attendance, and income. Allah compares reverence and purpose. A small place where people pray, learn, and purify themselves may be better than a large place that feeds division.

This does not make excellence or beauty wrong. A strong building can serve a strong purpose. The issue is what the beauty serves. Resources should improve safety, access, and worship, not conceal a corrupt foundation. The believer asks Allah to let his work be both sound in means and useful in effect.

**Guidance Requires Leaving Wrongdoing**

The verse ends by saying Allah does not guide wrongdoing people. Guidance is not given to confirm a harmful plan. The wrongdoing must be abandoned before the project can be made sound. A person cannot ask Allah to bless a foundation while refusing to remove the injustice built into it.

Yet the warning does not make reform impossible. Tear down the false support, return rights, expose the division, and rebuild on reverence. The work may be painful, and the public image may be lost, but a truthful foundation is worth more than a structure that carries its builders toward ruin. Allah guides the servant who chooses to stop building on the cliff.

Every serious undertaking can ask the same question: what will happen when pressure reaches the foundation? If the answer depends on secrecy, personal loyalty, or the fear of criticism, collapse has already begun. If the answer rests on Allah's pleasure, truth can survive a change of leader or a period of loss. The believer builds patiently, welcomes review, and accepts that a smaller sound work is better than an impressive project that harms people. Allah's guidance is not a decoration placed on a building after it is complete. It is the ground on which the work must stand from its first intention.

The cliff image also describes a personal life built on borrowed approval. A person may seem stable while everyone around him praises the same falsehood, but one honest question can expose the weakness. He should therefore build his character on practices that survive solitude: prayer, truthful speech, lawful earning, and care for people who cannot increase his status. These foundations may look less impressive than public success, but they bear weight. When hardship arrives, the servant does not need to invent a new identity. He returns to the ground on which he built from the beginning.

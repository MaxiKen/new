## Sūrah al-Tawbah 9:22

> to stay there for ever and ever. Surely with Allah is a great reward.

**Expanded Commentary**

**Eternity Completes the Promise**

Verse 22 completes the previous promise by adding the quality that earthly reward cannot provide: permanence. The believers will stay in the Gardens for ever and ever. The repeated expression closes the door on the anxiety that follows every worldly pleasure. A person may possess something beautiful and still fear its loss. The Garden promised here is not held on a temporary lease. It is a home whose enjoyment does not move toward an ending.

The Qur'an often pairs Paradise with words of abiding. This repetition is not merely poetic emphasis. It answers a human problem. Created beings are aware that time takes everything from them. Even a long life is short against the horizon of death. The promise of eternal residence changes the meaning of the reward. It is not a large share of a temporary world; it is a different order of existence in which the faithful are no longer threatened by departure.

**The Great Reward Is Kept With Allah**

The final clause says that with Allah is a great reward. The preposition gives the promise a secure location. The reward is not stored in a place where another power can seize it, nor is it dependent on the changing judgment of people. It is with Allah, the One who promises, preserves, and delivers. The believer's confidence rests on the character of the Giver before it rests on the description of the gift.

Greatness here also resists calculation. A person can count wealth, land, or years, but he cannot measure the reward that Allah has prepared. The Qur'an uses the same phrase in other passages, including 4:95 and 48:29, after describing faith and sacrifice. The recurring formula teaches that the reward's scale matches the Lord's generosity, not the narrow scale by which people assess a believer's worldly standing.

**The Promise Is Not a Wage Extracted From God**

The language of reward can mislead if it is treated as though Allah were a debtor forced to pay a servant who has made an equal exchange. The previous verse began with mercy, and mercy remains the ground of the promise. The believer works because Allah commands and hopes because Allah has promised. No creature can place Allah under compulsion. Even the ability to obey is a gift.

This does not make the promise uncertain. Allah's generosity is free, and His promise is true. The believer's humility and confidence therefore belong together. He does not boast that he purchased Paradise by his own strength, but he also does not doubt the word of the One who promised it. The verse's ending joins both ideas: the reward is great, and it is with Allah.

**Forever Changes the Meaning of Loss**

Emigration had involved real losses. Some believers left property, family protection, familiar streets, and a place in the old social order. Striving could involve injury or death. The promise of everlasting residence does not tell the believer that these losses were unreal. It tells him that they are not the end of the account. A person may surrender a house for Allah and receive a home whose security cannot be broken.

That contrast appears throughout the Qur'an. The enjoyment of this life is described as little compared with the Hereafter (9:38), not because created blessings have no value, but because their duration is limited. Verse 22 gives the missing term in the calculation. When the reward is everlasting, a temporary sacrifice can be understood without being trivialized. The believer may grieve what was lost while still knowing that Allah's replacement is not merely equal. It is beyond the reach of loss.

**The Promise Guards Against Empty Heroism**

The context also prevents a false glorification of sacrifice. People can suffer for nationalism, pride, revenge, or the desire to be remembered. Verse 22 does not promise eternal reward to every person who undergoes hardship. It follows the description of those who believed, emigrated, and strove in Allah's cause. The reward is tied to faith and purpose. Pain alone is not a path to Paradise.

This distinction protects the believer from two errors. He does not avoid every cost because comfort is good, and he does not seek pain as proof of spiritual worth. He asks what Allah requires, then accepts the cost attached to obedience. If hardship comes for another reason, he turns it into patience and trust. If an apparent sacrifice is driven by ego, he cannot cover that intention with religious language.

**A Great Reward Produces Steady Work**

The believer who trusts this verse does not become passive while waiting for the Garden. Eternal reward makes present obedience meaningful. Each prayer, act of generosity, truthful word, and difficult restraint is part of a life moving toward Allah's promised home. The reward is not an excuse to neglect the world. It is the reason to use the world without being owned by it.

The verse offers particular strength when results are hidden. A teacher may not see the fruit of years of work, a parent may not see the effect of patient guidance, and a person who refuses corruption may lose status without immediate vindication. Allah does not lose the account. The reward remains with Him. What is unseen by people is not absent from the divine store. Everlasting residence and a great reward turn unnoticed faithfulness into a secure investment. The servant may not see the return today, but Allah's keeping is more reliable than every worldly account. A delayed reward is not a lost reward when the promise rests with the One who never forgets. This certainty steadies people whose obedience seems to produce no immediate advantage. They continue because the ledger is held by Allah, not by public memory, and because the Garden's permanence makes every faithful step meaningful even when the step is hidden from everyone else.

## Sūrah al-Anʿām 6:129

> This is how We make the wrongdoers ˹destructive˺ allies of one another because of their misdeeds.

**Expanded Commentary**

**The Alliance of the Wrongdoers**

This is how We make the wrongdoers allies of one another because of their misdeeds. The verse states a general principle after the account of the partnership between the jinn and their human associates, and it describes the companionship of the wicked as something God brings about. The sentence presents the alliance as an arrangement rather than as an accident of temperament, and it attaches the arrangement to the deeds of the people involved.

The Book's word for the ally covers the patron who takes charge of another's affairs and the friend who stands with him. The sūrah of the Kneeling uses the pair of terms in a single sentence, saying that the wrongdoers are patrons of one another while God is the patron of the righteous (45:19), and the verse in front of us states the first half of that contrast as a fact of the present order.

**The Book's Descriptions of the Wicked Company**

The Book returns to the company of the wicked in its accounts of the day of judgment, and it describes the company dissolving there. The sūrah of the Ornament states that close friends will be enemies to one another on that day except the righteous (43:67), and the sūrah of the Heights describes the groups of jinn and humans entering the fire together and cursing one another as they follow one another in. The sūrah of the Cattle has already described the partners in deception arriving at their term and being told that the fire is their home (6:128).

The companionships described in these passages share a feature: those who supported one another in the world are described as turning on one another when the support is no longer useful. The verse in front of us states the arrangement that produces those companionships, and the account of their end appears in the sūrahs that describe the reckoning.

**The Reason Attached to the Sentence**

Because of their misdeeds. The clause attaches the alliance to the conduct of the people, and the Book's statement is that the companionship of the wicked is itself a consequence of their wrongdoing. The sūrah has described the whisperings of the satans among men and jinn as an activity that targets the hearts of those who disbelieve in the Hereafter (6:113), and the sentence in front of us states that the participants in that activity are joined to one another.

The Book's reasoning follows the pattern it has established throughout the chapter. The people who choose a course are confirmed in it, and the confirmation includes the company they keep. The verse states the arrangement as one of the ways in which a choice is made to hold, and it presents the company of the wicked as a feature of the condition they have chosen.

**What the Verse Says About the Strength of the Wicked**

The sentence describes an alliance that is real but ruinous, and the translation's word for it carries the sense of mutual destruction. The Book's accounts of the powerful in the world do not deny their power; the sūrah of Saba' and the sūrah of the Ornament describe the elites of the communities as established and confident. What the Book denies is that their alliance can secure them against the judgement that follows.

The sūrah has made the point in its passages about the plotters who did not perceive that they plotted against themselves (6:123) and about the elite who demanded the messenger's office (6:124). The verse in front of us states the same fact about the alliances those people formed, and it describes the mutual support as a mutual harm that they did not see.

**The Verse in the Sūrah's Argument**

The verse stands between the account of the partnership between the jinn and the people and the address to both assemblies at the resurrection, and it supplies the general statement that binds the two passages together. The jinn misled human beings and the human beings benefited from the jinn, and the alliance that grew from that arrangement belongs to the wider pattern of alliances among the wrongdoers.

The Book's presentation is therefore not limited to the case of the jinn. The same sentence applies to every company formed around a course that the revelation condemns, and the sūrah's audience, organised as it was around clans and interest, is addressed by the principle in its own terms.

**The Contrast With the Community of the Believers**

The Book sets the mutual support of the wrongdoers against another kind of company, and the contrast shows what the verse is describing. The sūrah of the Chambers states that the believers are but one brotherhood and commands them to make peace among themselves (49:10); the sūrah of the Family of Imran tells the community to hold firmly to the rope of God and not to be divided, and it recalls the favour by which their hearts were united after enmity (3:103); and the sūrah of the Spoils says that God brought the hearts of the believers together when no amount of wealth could have done it (8:63).

The difference between the two companies lies in what holds them together. The alliance described in this verse rests on misdeeds and dissolves when its usefulness ends, while the brotherhood described in the other passages rests on a shared response to the message and is described as a gift from God. The sūrah's audience knew both kinds of bond, since the message had begun to create a community out of the tribes of a city organised around rivalry.

**The Transition to the Next Verse**

The next verse records the address to the assembly of jinn and humans, asking whether messengers did not come to them from among their own people, proclaiming the revelations of God and warning them of the coming of the day, and it records their answer that they testify against themselves (6:130). The address follows the principle stated here, since the alliance of the wrongdoers does not remove the warning that was given to every community.

The sūrah thus completes its account of the responsibility of the people who were gathered. The partnership, the term, the fire, the alliance, and the confession follow one another as a sequence, and the verse in front of us states the law that governs the company of the people who chose the path the sūrah has been describing.

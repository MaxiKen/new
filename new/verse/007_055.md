## Sūrah al-Aʿrāf 7:55

> Call upon your Lord humbly and secretly. Surely He does not like the transgressors.

**Expanded Commentary**

**The Command to Call Upon God**

The verse commands the audience to call upon their Lord, and the command follows the description of the Creator in the previous verse. The Book issues the same command in several passages, and the sūrah of the Forgiver states that the Lord has proclaimed that His servants should call upon Him and that He will respond, adding that those who are too proud to worship will enter Hell in humiliation (40:60). The account of the Cow states that God is near and answers the call of the one who calls upon Him (2:186). The verse in front of us states the command in its shortest form and attaches two conditions.

The address is made to the audience of the sūrah, and the Lord named in it is the One whose works the previous verse has just described. The pair of verses joins the evidence to the response that the evidence requires, and the command in front of us supplies the act of worship that follows from the recognition of the Creator.

**Humbly and in Secret**

The two conditions in the verse are humility and concealment, and the Book attaches both to the supplication of the sincere. The Cattle's account describes people in distress calling upon God with humility and in secret, and it reports their abandonment of the practice once they reach dry land (6:63). The sūrah of Maryam describes Zechariah crying out to his Lord privately (19:3), and the sūrah of the Heights itself will state later in the chapter that the remembrance of God is to be kept inward, with humility and reverence and without loudness (7:205).

The concealment in front of us distinguishes the supplication from display, and the Book's criticisms of those who pray to be seen supply the contrast. The sūrah of the Small Kindnesses describes those who only show off in their worship (107:6), and the sūrah of the Cow warns against the alms that are given and then followed by reminders and injury. The verse in front of us states the two conditions that keep the act between the servant and his Lord.

**He Does Not Love the Transgressors**

The verse closes with the statement that God does not love those who transgress the limits, and the term covers excess in supplication as well as the wider sense of transgression. The commentary tradition reads the statement as a warning against the extremes in the act that has just been commanded, whether by raising the voice beyond what the setting requires, by making the petition so elaborate that it turns into display, or by demanding what lies outside the worshipper's proper concern. The Book's instructions about the tone of the remembrance in the later verses of the same chapter belong to the same teaching.

The Prophet's guidance on the subject is reported in the collections, and the tradition records that he corrected those who carried the practice to excess. A report transmitted by al-Tirmidhī states that nothing is more honourable with God than supplication (al-Tirmidhī 3370), which the collector graded as a good narration of a single chain, and the report places the act at the centre of the worshipper's relation with his Lord while leaving the manner of it to the rules the Book and the practice state.

**The Manners of Supplication in the Book**

The Book states the manner of the call in several passages, and the two conditions in front of us are part of a larger set of instructions. The sūrah of the Heights states that the remembrance is to be kept within oneself, with fear and without loudness, in the mornings and the evenings (7:205), and the sūrah of the Night Journey instructs the Prophet not to raise his voice too high in his prayer nor to be silent altogether but to seek a middle way (17:110). The verse in front of us states the two conditions in a single phrase.

The Book's instructions about the call include the address used in it and the confidence with which it is made, and the sūrah of the Cow states that God answers the one who calls on Him and that the callers should respond to Him and believe in Him (2:186). This verse supplies the posture of the call, and the passage that follows states the two other attitudes that accompany it: hope and fear.

**Sincerity Against Display**

The conditions of humility and concealment address the danger of the act becoming a performance, and the Book's teaching about the worship of the heart supplies the alternatives. The sūrah of the Cave commands the Prophet to be with those who call upon their Lord morning and evening seeking His face, and the sūrah of the Family of Imran praises those who stand and sit and lie in prayer while reflecting on the creation. The verse here places the same requirement at the head of the passage on the created order.

The Book's warnings about the display of good deeds appear in the sūrah of the Cow in the parable of the rock with dust upon it that a heavy rain leaves bare (2:264), and the metaphor states that the deed and its appearance diverge. The present verse states the alternative in the conditions of the call.

**Where the Verse Sits**

The verse stands fifty-fifth in the chapter, and it follows the account of the creation and precedes the command against spreading corruption in the land and the instruction to call upon God with hope and fear (7:56). The three verses form a unit on the worship of the Creator, and the verse in front of us supplies the command that begins it.

The reader who has been given the account of the heavens and the earth is now told how to address their Maker. The call is to be humble and hidden, and the excess that would turn it into a display is excluded by the warning that God does not love those who go beyond the limits, and the next verses state the other conditions of the call and the corruption that the audience is to avoid.

**The Transition to the Next Verse**

The next verse commands the audience not to spread corruption in the land after it has been set in order and to call upon God with hope and fear, and it states that the mercy of God is near to those who do good (7:56). The instruction about the manner of the call in front of us is followed by the two attitudes that accompany it and by the land's condition, and the passage then turns to the winds and the rain.

The two verses together form the worship that the sūrah asks of its audience. The call is made with humility and concealment, the land is left in the order in which it was set, and the call is offered with hope and fear; and the verses that follow present the rain and the good land as the signs of the One who is called upon.

## Sūrah Āli ʿImrān 3:103

> And hold firmly to the rope of Allah and do not be divided. Remember Allah’s favour upon you when you were enemies, then He united your hearts, so you—by His grace—became brothers. And you were at the brink of a fiery pit and He saved you from it. This is how Allah makes His revelations clear to you, so that you may be ˹rightly˺ guided.

**Expanded Commentary**

**What the Rope of God Means**

The verse commands the believers to hold firmly to the rope of God together and not to be divided, and then reminds them of what God had done for them. The image of the rope has been explained in several ways, and the explanations are not far apart.

The community of believers is the reading many commentators preferred, since the sentence goes on to forbid division. Others took the rope to be the Book itself, and some traced the image to a report in which the Qur'an is described as a rope stretching between heaven and earth. A further reading, noted by Ibn Kathīr, understands the rope as a covenant or pact, because the same word appears later in this sūrah for the treaties by which a people may be protected.

**"All Together" Is Part of the Command**

The verse does not say only that the rope must be held; it says that the believers must hold it together. The two instructions are one act described from two sides, and the second requires the first.

The Book's treatment of unity is not a general commendation of harmony. It is a command attached to a specific object, so that agreement is required about something rather than for its own sake, and the object is the revelation and the religion it establishes. That is why the same sūrah can forbid division in the next verses and also command a group to call others to what is good: unity in the Book's sense is not the absence of activity but the shared direction of it.

**Remembering What God Did**

The verse turns from command to memory, directing the community to consider its own recent past. It names three things: the enmity that existed before, the joining of hearts that followed, and the nearness of a pit of fire from which the believers were delivered.

The enmity refers to the tribal wars among the Arabs in the time before Islam, and more particularly to the long feud between the two Madinan tribes of Aws and Khazraj, who had fought each other for years and were separated only by the arrival of the Prophet and the faith he brought. The Book states elsewhere that the joining of hearts was God's work and could not have been bought with any amount of wealth (8:63), which makes the memory a statement about divine action rather than about diplomacy.

**The Brink of a Pit of Fire**

The image of standing at the edge of a fire is drawn from the community's condition before Islam, and the commentators explained the delivery as guidance that came before death, since a person who is on the brink and has not yet fallen is separated from it only by the time he has left.

The metaphor gives the memory its force. A community that had been on the edge, and was drawn back by the message it now held, has a concrete reason to remain where it was placed. The Book's use of this argument in the sūrah is not nostalgia; the sūrah has just been warning the believers about being pulled away, and the reminder of the rescue is the ground of the warning's force.

**The Book's Standing Command Against Division**

The instruction not to be divided is repeated in the Book in ways that show how the theme is handled. The command was given before to Noah, Abraham, Moses, and Jesus, together with the instruction to uphold the faith and make no divisions in it (42:13). Another passage states that those who were given the Scripture did not split into sects out of mutual envy until knowledge came to them (42:14).

That order — knowledge first, then division — is the pattern the Book keeps returning to, and it appears again where the People of the Book are said to have become divided only after the clear proof came to them (98:4). The sūrah's own version of the command follows the same logic: the community has received what it received, and the warning is against coming apart in the presence of the truth rather than in ignorance of it.

**Brotherhood as the Fruit of the Favour**

The verse's reminder ends with the community described as brothers, and the Book elsewhere builds that description into a rule. The believers are described as a single brotherhood, and the instruction that follows is to make peace between brothers, with mindfulness of God named as the condition for receiving mercy (49:10).

The sequence in this verse matches it. The joining of hearts comes first, and brotherhood is the name given to the result; what the community is then asked to do is not to create the bond but to stop dividing what God had joined. That is why the verse's command is phrased as it is: the effort required is not the manufacture of unity but the refusal to break it.

**A Verse the Community Reads About Itself**

This verse has served the community as its charter on the subject of division and as a reminder of what the alternative cost. Its mention of the pit of fire and the rescue gives the instruction a history rather than an abstraction.

The closing sentence says that God makes His revelations clear so that the community may be rightly guided, which places the instruction within the Book's general purpose. What the verse describes is not a rule imposed on people who had never seen its value; it is the statement of a policy for a community that had already experienced both conditions and knew which one it had been rescued from. The verses that follow set out what the community is to do with the unity it has been given.

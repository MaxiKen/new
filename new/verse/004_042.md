## Sūrah al-Nisāʾ 4:42

> On that Day, those who denied ˹Allah˺ and disobeyed the Messenger will wish they were reduced to dust. And they will never be able to hide anything from Allah.

### Expanded Commentary

**The wish to become dust**

Those who denied Allah and disobeyed the Messenger will wish to be reduced to dust. The wish expresses total escape from moral personhood: if they were dust, they would not have to answer, remember, or endure the consequences of chosen denial. But the wish cannot be fulfilled. The human being cannot hide from the identity in which deeds were performed.

The phrase “they will not be able to hide anything” closes the possibility of courtroom evasion. In this world a person can conceal a motive, recruit a witness, delete a record, or persuade himself that a wrong was harmless. In the Hereafter the concealed and the public are one account. The tongue may be silent or may attempt a defence, but no hidden fact is absent from Allah.

**Why the verse follows testimony**

The preceding verse established witnesses. This one shows why witness matters: judgment will not rely on a vague impression of humanity but on a complete record of message and response. The denial that seemed clever in life becomes exposed as an attempt to outrun truth. The wish for dust is the final recognition that excuses have no power when knowledge is perfect.

The verse is a warning but also a source of confidence for the oppressed. A victim whose testimony was dismissed, an orphan whose account was denied, or a believer mocked for speaking truth is not dependent on the completeness of human memory. Allah’s court does not lose evidence. The proper response is neither despair nor revenge, but truthful action while the account remains open.

**No hidden record is lost**

The wish to become dust also expresses the desire to escape responsibility by ceasing to be a person. The verse denies that escape. Its practical comfort is that forgotten injuries and concealed acts remain known to God, while its warning is that a person should not postpone repair in the hope that time will erase the account.

## Sūrah al-Nisāʾ 4:42

> On that Day, those who denied ˹Allah˺ and disobeyed the Messenger will wish they were reduced to dust. And they will never be able to hide anything from Allah.

**Expanded Commentary**

**Wishing to Be Dust**

The verse answers the hanging how of the previous scene. *Yawmaʾidhin yawaddu alladhīna kafarū wa ʿaṣaw al-rasūla law tusawwā bihimu al-arḍ* — on that Day, those who denied and disobeyed the Messenger will wish they were reduced to dust, that the earth were levelled over them. The wish *law tusawwā bihimu al-arḍ* — that the earth were made even with them, that they became dust within it — was explained as the desire to be nothing rather than to face the witness brought from every community and the Prophet ﷺ as witness over his own (4:41). The sūrah has shown what was denied and disobeyed: limits about orphans’ wealth (4:10), inheritance after debts (4:11–12), guarding marriage and dowry (4:19–21), and trade by mutual consent (4:29). To have denied that those limits came from God and to have disobeyed the Messenger who clarified them was to choose custom over revelation. On the Day when witnesses confirm that the message was made clear (4:26) and that burdens were lightened because humankind was created weak (4:28), that choice stands exposed. The wish to be dust is not repentance; it is the final awareness that disobedience was not hidden, as the next clause says, and that being nothing would be easier than being seen as one who was shown clarity and turned away.

**Those Who Denied and Disobeyed the Messenger**

The pairing *kafarū wa ʿaṣaw al-rasūla* gathers two groups and two modes. *Kafarū* — denied — covers those who covered the bounty and the sign, as the stingy concealed what God gave and the ostentatious spent for eyes without belief (4:37–38). *ʿAṣaw al-rasūla* — disobeyed the Messenger — covers those who heard the shares, the bar on reclaiming dowries, and the command to appoint mediators yet followed desires to deviate greatly (4:27). The sūrah has linked disbelief and disobedience throughout: *“whoever disobeys Allah and His Messenger and exceeds His limits will be cast into Fire”* (4:14), *“whoever takes Satan as associate, what an evil associate”* (4:38) where the associate’s counsel is disobedience, and *“obey Allah and obey the Messenger and those in authority among you, and refer disputes to them”* (4:59). The Day groups them because their outcome converges. To deny is to refuse the source; to disobey the Messenger is to refuse the route that makes the source practicable. Both leave the person without a limit to keep and without a turn to take, because turning depends on believing and on following the one sent to make things clear. The wish to be levelled with earth therefore follows both paths, because both end at the same court without an answer.

**And They Will Never Be Able to Hide Anything from Allah**

The clause *wa lā yaktumūna Allāha ḥadīthā* — and they will never be able to hide anything from Allah — closes the scene with knowledge. *Ḥadīth* — speech, statement, narration — was read as any statement they might wish to hide — the hidden mixing of orphan wealth (4:2), the pressured dowry return (4:19–20), the fraudulent trade (4:29), the words of distortion such as *“we hear and we disobey”* (4:46). On the Day when witnesses are brought (4:41), hiding fails because the One who is ever Watchful (4:1), sufficient as Reckoner (4:6), Witness over all things (4:33), and All-Knowing, All-Aware (4:35) is the One to whom no *ḥadīth* is concealed. The Qur’an elsewhere says that hands, feet, and skin will testify and that records will not leave small or great except counted. Here the same certainty is stated simply: no hiding. The sūrah has warned that God has perfect knowledge of them (4:39) and that He never wrongs even by an atom’s weight but multiplies good (4:40); that atom-level accounting now becomes the reason no speech can be hidden. What was concealed as bounty (4:37) will be known as concealed; what was spent for show (4:38) will be known as shown. The Day does not need new evidence; it reveals what perfect knowledge already contained.

**Dust as the Desired Alternative to Exposure**

To wish to be dust is the final form of the sūrah’s earlier wish to deviate. Those who follow desires wished you to deviate greatly (4:27), to tilt away from the clear path; here the deviators wish the tilt had never happened and that the earth would cover them. The image of dust recalls the creation from a single soul and the spreading as countless men and women (4:1) and the reminder that humankind was created weak (4:28). Dust is the beginning of that creation — *“from dust He created you”* — and the end to which the defiant would rather return than face the witness of the Messenger. The Qur’an elsewhere says that the disbeliever will say *“O would that I were dust”* (78:40), and this verse places that cry at the specific court of witnesses the previous verse opened. The cry does not avail, because the One who spread humanity from one soul is also the One who brings each soul with its witness. To be reduced to dust would be to escape accounting, but the sūrah has already said that account is prepared — Fire prepared for disbelievers as humiliating punishment (4:37) and Gardens prepared for those who keep limits (4:13) — and that preparation assumes presence, not absence. The wish remains a wish.

**A Scene That Holds the Sūrah’s Warnings Together**

Read with 4:41, this verse completes the witness tableau that the sūrah will use for its pivot to purification and to the Children of the Book. The court is assembled with a witness from every community and the Prophet ﷺ over his own; those who denied and disobeyed are present and wish to be levelled; no speech can be hidden from God. The tableau therefore gathers the three threads that have run together: God’s will to make clear and turn toward you (4:26–27), the limits whose keeping is obedience and whose exceeding is Fire (4:13–14, 4:30), and the mercy that covers lesser faults for those who avoid major sins (4:31). Those who kept the major limits and turned soon after ignorance (4:17) will stand differently under that sky than those who persisted until death was present (4:18). The verse does not name the believers’ recompense here; it leaves their standing implied by the sūrah’s larger pattern of honourable entry (4:31) and vast shade with pure spouses (4:57). For the deniers and disobedient, the recompense is named as a wish that cannot be granted and a concealment that cannot be kept, which is itself a form of exposure. The Day that begins with how will it be ends here with they will wish they were dust, showing that the community’s choice between clarity and desire, made in matters as small as a sixth and as serious as *shirk*, will have a weight that even the earth cannot cover.

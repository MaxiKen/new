## Sūrah al-Isrāʾ 17:7

> **If you act rightly, it is for your own good, but if you do wrong, it is to your own loss. And when the second warning would come to pass, your enemies would ˹be left to˺ totally disgrace you and enter the Temple ˹of Jerusalem˺ as they entered it the first time, and utterly destroy whatever would fall into their hands.**

**Expanded Commentary**

**If You Do Good, For Yourselves; If You Do Wrong, Against Yourselves**

In aḥsantum aḥsantum li-anfusikum wa-in asa'tum fa-lahā — if you do good, you do good for yourselves, if you do wrong, it is against yourselves, for yourselves. Aḥsantum from ḥ-s-n, to do good, excellence. Li-anfusikum, for yourselves, for your own souls. Asa'tum from s-w-ʾ, to do wrong, evil. Fa-lahā, then for it, against it, meaning against yourselves, your own loss. Al-Ṭabarī explains that doing good benefits doer, not Allah, because Allah is free from need; doing wrong harms doer, not Allah. This principle appears throughout Qur'an, as in 41:46: *Whoever does righteousness, it is for his own soul, and whoever does evil, it is against it* (41:46). The verse is placed after restoration with upper hand, wealth, offspring, numbers, to remind that restoration is opportunity: if you do good after restoration, it benefits you; if you do wrong, it harms you. The choice determines next stage.

**Second Warning And Total Disgrace**

Fa-idhā jā'a waʿdu al-ākhirati li-yasū'ū wujūhakum — then when second warning comes to pass, to disgrace your faces, to make faces gloomy. Fa-idhā, then when, indicating second corruption. Jā'a waʿdu al-ākhirati, second warning, last warning came to pass. Li-yasū'ū wujūhakum, to disgrace your faces, make faces bad, gloomy, from s-w-ʾ, evil, bad, and w-j-h, face. Al-Ṭabarī says li-yasū'ū wujūhakum means enemies will disgrace you, make your faces gloomy with humiliation, as happened when Romans destroyed Jerusalem second time. Al-Qurṭubī says second punishment was by Romans under Titus in 70 CE, who destroyed Second Temple, killed many, enslaved others, causing faces to be disgraced.

**Entering Temple As First Time**

Wa-li-yadkhulū al-masjida kamā dakhalūhu awwala marratin — and to enter Temple as they entered it first time. Yadkhulū, they enter, from d-kh-l. Al-masjid, Temple, Mosque, meaning Temple in Jerusalem, Farthest Mosque. Kamā dakhalūhu awwala marratin, as they entered it first time, meaning as first invaders entered and destroyed Temple first time, second invaders will enter similarly. Al-Ṭabarī explains that second entry to Temple was by Romans who entered and destroyed as Nebuchadnezzar did first time. The phrase shows pattern: first corruption punished by entry and destruction of Temple, second corruption punished similarly, showing that sacred place does not protect corrupt people.

**Utterly Destroy Whatever Falls Into Hands**

Wa-li-yutabbirū mā ʿalaw tatbīran — and to utterly destroy whatever they rise over, whatever falls into their hands, with total destruction. Yutabbirū from t-b-r, to destroy, crush, from same root as tabār, destruction. Mā ʿalaw, whatever they rise over, overcome, dominate. Tatbīran, utterly, with emphasis, complete destruction. Al-Ṭabarī says this means second invaders will utterly destroy whatever they overcome, including buildings, people, etc., showing severity of second punishment. Al-Rāzī notes that tatbīran indicates total, not partial, destruction, fitting second corruption that was worse or repeated.

**Why Second Punishment More Severe**

Second punishment includes disgracing faces, entering Temple as first time, and utter destruction, which is more severe than first that was ravaging homes, scouring houses. The increase in severity shows that repeated corruption leads to greater punishment, as sin after being warned and restored is worse than first sin. Al-Qurṭubī says second punishment was more humiliating because it included disgracing faces, li-yasū'ū wujūhakum, indicating public humiliation, faces made gloomy with shame, not only private homes ravaged. Romans under Titus in 70 CE entered Temple, destroyed it, killed, enslaved, causing faces to be disgraced, as history records. The pattern warns that if you are restored after first punishment and you corrupt again, second punishment will be worse, with total disgrace and Temple entry and utter destruction, showing that repeated rejection after mercy leads to severe consequence.

**A Universal Principle Of Self-Benefit And Self-Harm**

In aḥsantum li-anfusikum wa-in asa'tum fa-lahā — if you do good for yourselves, if you do wrong against yourselves — is universal principle that applies to all, including Israelites and Makkans. Al-Rāzī explains that doing good benefits self in world and Hereafter, as verse 9 will say Qur'an gives good news to believers who do good of mighty reward, and doing wrong harms self in world and Hereafter, as verse 10 will say those who do not believe in Hereafter have painful punishment. The principle also appears in previous chapter: those who do good in world have goodness, but Hereafter better. The verse therefore teaches responsibility: your deeds return to you.

**Connection To Night Journey And Temple**

Second warning involves entering Temple, al-masjid, same word as Farthest Mosque, al-masjid al-aqṣā, in verse 1. The Temple that was entered and destroyed twice is same Temple whose surroundings blessed and to which Prophet was taken by night. The connection shows that Temple's sanctity does not prevent punishment of corrupt inhabitants; instead, its entry by enemies is part of punishment. The Night Journey to Farthest Mosque therefore is not only honor for Prophet but also reminder that sacred places require obedience. The Temple entered twice by invaders will be visited by Prophet in Night Journey as honored servant, showing transfer of custodianship from corrupt to grateful servant.

**Linguistic Note On Sā'a And Tabbār**

Aḥsantum from ḥ-s-n, to do good. Asa'tum from s-w-ʾ, to do evil. Li-anfusikum, for yourselves, from n-f-s. Fa-lahā, then for it, against it. Jā'a waʿdu al-ākhirati, second warning came. Li-yasū'ū from s-w-ʾ, to make bad, disgrace. Wujūhakum, your faces, from w-j-h. Yadkhulū from d-kh-l, to enter. Al-masjid, Temple, from s-j-d. Kamā dakhalūhu awwala marratin, as they entered it first time, from d-kh-l and a-w-l and m-r-r. Yutabbirū from t-b-r, to destroy. Mā ʿalaw, what they rise over, from ʿ-l-w. Tatbīran, destruction, from t-b-r. The verse's structure — if you do good for yourselves, if you do wrong against yourselves, then when second warning comes, they will disgrace faces and enter Temple as first time and utterly destroy — with principle then history, teaches that deeds benefit or harm self and that second corruption leads to severe punishment.

**How This Verse Teaches Accountability**

This verse teaches accountability: your good is for you, your wrong against you. It removes idea that you can do good for Allah's benefit or wrong that harms Allah; Allah is free from need. It also teaches that second chances exist, as restoration after first punishment, but repeated corruption leads to worse punishment. The practical lesson is to do good after being given upper hand, wealth, offspring, numbers, not to become arrogant and corrupt again. For Makkans who were given many blessings and were being warned, it says if you do good after being warned, it is for your own good, if you do wrong, it is your own loss, and if you corrupt again after being given chance, punishment will be severe with disgrace and destruction.

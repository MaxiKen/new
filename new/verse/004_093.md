## Sūrah al-Nisāʾ 4:93

> And whoever kills a believer intentionally, their reward will be Hell— where they will stay indefinitely. Allah will be displeased with them, condemn them, and will prepare for them a tremendous punishment.

### Expanded Commentary

**The exceptional severity of intentional killing**

The verse gives one of the Qur’an’s strongest warnings for intentionally killing a believer: Hell, divine anger, condemnation, and tremendous punishment. The accumulation of terms reflects the destruction of a protected life and the betrayal of the community’s covenant. Intentional murder cannot be treated as an ordinary dispute that a fine automatically resolves.

**Theological discussions and practical duty**

Classical theologians discussed whether the promise of permanence means that every intentional killer is eternally in Hell or whether it is a threat subject to divine will for a believer who repents. The Qur’an elsewhere opens repentance and affirms that Allah may forgive sins, while the present verse ensures that no one treats repentance as a predictable escape from accountability. A murderer must repent, submit to lawful judgment, and seek restoration where possible.

The verse does not authorize families to exact private vengeance. Its severity supports due process because only a competent authority can establish intention, evidence, defences, and punishment. The sanctity of life is served by law, not by cycles of retaliation.

**No private blood vengeance**

The severity of the warning supports the need for lawful investigation, not personal retaliation. Intent, evidence, and circumstances must be established by competent authority. Families may seek justice and may forgive, but they should not be pushed into cycles of revenge by those who confuse anger with the sacred protection of life.

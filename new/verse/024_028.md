## Sūrah an-Nūr 24:28

> If you find no one at home, do not enter it until you have been given permission. And if you are asked to leave, then leave. That is purer for you. And Allah has ˹perfect˺ knowledge of what you do.

**Expanded Commentary**

**The Prohibition of Entering Empty Homes**

The divine legislation addresses situations where a residence appears vacant or devoid of responding inhabitants: *fa-in lam tajidū fīhā aḥadan fa-lā tadkhulūhā ḥattā yuʾdhana lakum*. The absence of occupants does not create a license for uninvited entry. Even if an entrance gate stands wide open, a door is unlocked, or the dwelling appears completely empty, unauthorized intrusion remains strictly forbidden until explicit permission is granted by the lawful owner or resident.

This statute establishes an absolute property right and privacy right in Islamic jurisprudence. The sanctity of a home is not defined by physical locks or barricades; it is rooted in the moral and legal authority of its occupants. A person has no right to wander through another's private space, examine their possessions, or seek shelter without direct authorization. Divine law protects the domestic sphere from casual trespassing, ensuring that an empty home remains as inviolable as an occupied one.

**The Etiquette of Refusal: wa-in qīla lakumu irjiʿū farjiʿū**

The verse introduces a remarkable and counter-cultural social instruction: *wa-in qīla lakumu irjiʿū farjiʿū*, and if it is said to you, “Turn back,” then turn back. In virtually every human culture, being explicitly turned away from a door is perceived as an acute social insult, often provoking deep offense, lingering resentment, and fractured relationships between friends and kin.

The Qur'an fundamentally de-stigmatizes the act of refusal, commanding the visiting believer to accept being turned away with complete equanimity and grace. Household residents may be unwell, engaged in private intimacy, grieving a personal loss, resolving a domestic dispute, or simply exhausted and unprepared to receive guests. They possess the sovereign right to decline visitation without offering an elaborate justification or apology. The visitor is commanded to turn back immediately without harboring resentment or demanding explanations, honoring the host's autonomy.

**Purity of Soul Through Respecting Boundaries: huwa azkā lakum**

The divine voice provides a profound spiritual evaluation of this etiquette: *huwa azkā lakum*, that is purer for you. Turning back gracefully upon refusal cultivates deep spiritual purity in both parties. For the host, it preserves their domestic peace and relieves them of the oppressive burden of hosting guests when they lack the physical or emotional capacity to do so, shielding them from hypocrisy or resentment.

For the visitor, turning back without offense purifies the ego (*tazkiyat al-nafs*) of pride, entitlement, and intrusive curiosity. It trains the believer to respect the privacy of others as they would want their own privacy respected. Early Muslims understood the immense spiritual elevation promised by this phrase. A famous scholar from the righteous predecessors remarked: I spent my entire life hoping that someone would say to me at their door, “Turn back,” so that I could turn back with a joyous heart and attain the divine purity mentioned in this verse!

**The Prophetic Protocols of Seeking Permission**

The practical application of this verse is meticulously detailed across authoritative traditions of the Sunnah. In Ṣaḥīḥ al-Bukhārī 6245 and Ṣaḥīḥ Muslim 2153, the Prophet ﷺ established the golden rule of visitation: Permission should be sought three times; if you are granted permission, enter; otherwise, depart. A visitor knocks or calls out with the greeting of peace at reasonable intervals, allowing the occupants time to respond.

If no response is received after the third attempt, the visitor must walk away without lingering, peering, or expressing frustration. Furthermore, the Prophet ﷺ commanded that visitors never stand directly in front of the center of a doorway, but position themselves to the right or the left side of the entrance. This protocol ensures that when the door is opened, the interior of the home and its female occupants remain completely shielded from the visitor's initial gaze, fulfilling the spirit of modesty.

**The Criminalization of Peeping and Voyeurism**

Islamic jurisprudence treats the visual violation of domestic privacy with extraordinary severity. In Ṣaḥīḥ al-Bukhārī 6888, the Prophet ﷺ articulated the foundational principle: The seeking of permission was legislated entirely for the sake of the gaze. An individual who stands outside a home and deliberately peers through windows, cracks, or door keyholes commits a major legal and moral transgression.

When a man once peered through an opening into the Prophet's ﷺ private quarters while he was combing his hair, the Prophet ﷺ pointed an iron instrument toward him, declaring: Had I known you were looking, I would have pierced your eye with it! Classical jurists across all four schools of law concluded from this and related traditions that if an intruder deliberately peeps into a private residence without permission, and the occupants throw a stone or object that injures or blinds the intruder's eye, the occupants bear no financial or legal liability (*lā diyata lah*), because the intruder stripped himself of legal protection by violating the domestic sanctuary.

**The Divine Gaze and the Transparency of Intent: Allāhu bimā taʿmalūna ʿAlīm**

The verse concludes with a sobering reminder of divine surveillance: *wa-Allāhu bimā taʿmalūna ʿalīm*, and Allah has full knowledge of what you do. While human beings cannot see through walls or police secret intentions at the doorstep, God possesses exhaustive, real-time knowledge of every movement and motivation.

God knows whether a visitor angled their eyes to catch a glimpse into a private room, whether a host refused entry out of genuine necessity or petty arrogance, and whether an individual departed with a clean heart or a mind festering with spite. By sealing the legislation with divine omniscience, the revelation elevates the ordinary mechanics of neighborhood visitation into an arena of spiritual testing, reminding every believer that true modesty and courtesy are lived under the all-seeing gaze of the Creator.

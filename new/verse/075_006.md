## Sūrah al-Qiyāmah 75:6

> asking ˹mockingly˺, “When is this Day of Judgment?”

**Expanded Commentary**

**A Question That Is Not an Inquiry**

The verse gives the denier's words directly, and the form is interrogative. The content is not a request for information. *Ayyāna* asks after time — at what moment? — and the question is asked in a tone the Qur'an describes elsewhere as ridicule, since its purpose is not to learn but to display the asker's confidence that there is nothing to learn. A person who genuinely wants to know whether an event will happen asks *whether*; this question concedes nothing and demands a date. The move is a familiar one in argument: convert a claim you cannot refute into a logistical detail, and then treat the missing detail as the refutation. Whoever has no answer about the calendar appears, to the crowd, to have no answer at all.

**The Question Rehearsed Across the Qur'an**

The wording recurs so often, and in such similar form, that it reads as a fixed piece of the Makkan repertoire. *“They ask ˹mockingly˺, ‘When is this Day of Judgment?’”* (51:12). *“˹Still˺ they ask ˹the believers˺, ‘When will this threat come to pass, if what you say is true?’”* (67:25). The same sentence appears with small variations in 10:48, 21:38, 27:71, 34:29 and 36:48. The repetition tells us something about the audience rather than about the Day. This was not an objection raised once by a thoughtful questioner; it was a slogan, repeated in assemblies, whose function was social — to mark the believers as people making claims they could not substantiate. That is why the Qur'an answers it so many times and in so many different ways: not because the question was hard, but because it kept being asked in bad faith.

**Why the Date Is Withheld**

The reply given to this question elsewhere in the Qur'an is consistent and unusually direct: the time belongs to God, and the Messenger was not permitted to disclose it. *“They ask you ˹O Prophet˺ regarding the Hour, ‘When will it be?’ Say, ‘That knowledge is only with my Lord. He alone will reveal it when the time comes. It is too tremendous for the heavens and the earth and will only take you by surprise.’”* (7:187). The same instruction is repeated in 79:42–43, and the list of five hidden things includes the Hour alongside knowledge of what the wombs carry and what a soul will earn tomorrow (31:34). If the date were published, the life lived in front of it would be transformed into a countdown, and conduct would be governed by arithmetic rather than by love, fear, and loyalty. The withholding is the condition of the test, not a gap in the revelation. A second wisdom sits behind it. A published date would change conduct, but it would also end the condition in which conduct means anything, since a person who knows exactly when the audit falls can arrange his affairs for the day before it and live as he pleases until then. The hiddenness of the hour is what makes every hour the possible one. It is no accident that the Qur'an names knowledge of the Hour alongside the sending of rain and the contents of the wombs: these are things no creature manages, and not managing them is what keeps a creature dependent.

**What Was Actually Being Demanded**

Behind the demand for a date sat a demand for a different kind of proof. The Makkan audience wanted the threat cashed immediately, in this world, on a schedule they could check. *“And if We delay their punishment until an appointed time, they will definitely say, ‘What is holding it back?’”* (11:8). A prophecy that arrives late looks, to someone keeping score, like a prophecy that failed, and the delay was therefore taken as evidence. The Qur'an's response is to re-locate the whole matter: the Hour is not late, it is near in a sense that has nothing to do with clocks, *“Bringing about the Hour would only take the blink of an eye, or even less.”* (16:77). Nearness here belongs to the One who will bring it, not to the interval that remains.

**How the Qur'an Answers a Question It Refuses to Answer**

The sūrah does not date the Day; it describes what the Day will do to the people who asked. That is the shape of the answer throughout the Qur'an, and it is worth stating why. A date would have settled a dispute about information and left the dispositions of the disputants untouched. A description of the Day addresses the will, which is where this sūrah has already located the problem in v. 5. Seen in that light, the mockery is not dodged but answered on its own ground: the questioner wanted to know when, and he is told, in effect, that by the time he knows, the knowing will be part of the terror. *“In fact, the Hour will take them by surprise, leaving them stunned. So they will not be able to avert it, nor will it be delayed from them.”* (21:40).

**Hastening, and the Discipline Against It**

The impulse behind the question — the demand that God produce His threat on the asker's timetable — is one the Qur'an treats as a recurring disease, and it prescribes patience as its cure: *“And do not ˹seek to˺ hasten ˹the torment˺ for the deniers. On the Day they see what they have been threatened with, it will be as if they had only stayed ˹in this world˺ for an hour of a day.”* (46:35). The warning about the Day is not softened by the delay, and it is not strengthened by it either. What the delay does is expose the questioner: whether he is waiting to be convinced or waiting to be caught. Asked in that tone, the question also performs a second function: it shields the asker from the content of the claim. A date can be laughed at, while a claim about justice and accountability can only be answered in conduct. The sūrah will now show what being caught looks like, beginning not with the ground or the sky but with the eyes of the man who asked.

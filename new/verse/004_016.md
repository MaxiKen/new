## Sūrah al-Nisāʾ 4:16

> And the two among you who commit this sin—discipline them. If they repent and mend their ways, relieve them. Surely Allah is ever Accepting of Repentance, Most Merciful.

### Expanded Commentary

**“The two among you” and the early communal response**

The verse refers to two people who commit the sexual offence and instructs the community to discipline them, then release the disciplinary response when they repent and reform. Early exegetes differed over whether the wording refers to a man and a woman, two men, or the two parties to the act in a general sense. The later legal verses refine the punishments, but this passage makes clear that public response must have a moral and judicial framework rather than becoming private revenge.

**Repentance is demonstrated by reform**

The verse does not treat an apology as a magic eraser. Repentance is joined to amendment: the wrongdoing must stop, its causes must be confronted, and any person harmed must receive restitution where possible. For a community, this means that discipline can have a rehabilitative purpose. It is not permitted to enjoy another person’s disgrace after the person has genuinely returned and repaired what can be repaired.

**Mercy within accountability**

The release from discipline is tied to Allah’s acceptance of repentance and mercy. That pairing prevents two distortions. One is permissiveness that calls every consequence oppression; the other is punitive cruelty that refuses to recognize moral change. Legitimate authority must assess evidence, repentance, and public safety. Individuals must not take the verse as permission to expose sins that Allah has concealed or to appoint themselves as executioners of justice.

The verse’s place beside 4:17–18 is important. The chapter will define when repentance is likely to be accepted and when a person has postponed it until the moment of death. Here, the emphasis is on a living reformer who can turn conduct around. The community’s task is to stop the harm, preserve justice, and leave a genuine path back to God.

**From discipline to restoration**

A response to sexual wrongdoing should ask how further harm will be prevented, how the parties can return to lawful conduct, and how private repentance can occur without public spectacle. The verse’s instruction to relieve them after reform resists the human appetite to keep punishing someone after the stated purpose of discipline has been fulfilled.

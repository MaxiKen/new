## Sūrah al-Baqarah 2:153

> O believers! Seek comfort in patience and prayer. Allah is truly with those who are patient.

**Expanded Commentary**

**An Address to the Believers**

The verse addresses the community by the name it acquired through its faith, and the sūrah uses the same address at several points where a general instruction is given to the believers. The command that follows belongs to the whole community rather than to particular persons, and its subject is the means by which difficulty is to be met.

The address opens a passage that continues with the account of the trials the community would face and the good news given to those who endure them. The sentence in this verse supplies the provision before the passage describes the difficulty, and the order is deliberate.

The address names the community by its faith rather than by its lineage or its location, and the Book uses that name where an instruction applies to everyone who holds the position described. The reader is therefore included in the command whatever his origin, and the sentence leaves no room for the assumption that endurance is the concern of a particular group within the community. The same instruction is given to the whole body of the believers, and the promise at its end is given on the same terms.

**Patience in the Book's Usage**

The Book's term for patience carries the meanings of restraint, endurance, and persistence, and the commentators explained it as holding oneself to what is right and away from what is wrong without complaint against God. It is described in the Book as a quality that God loves, that the angels greet the patient for, and that is given without measure.

The sūrah uses the term in several contexts: in the account of Moses and his people, in the description of the believers who endure, and in the commands that follow the present verse. The Book's vocabulary treats patience as an active condition rather than a passive one, and the reports describe it as the quality that accompanies the performance of difficult duties.

**Prayer as the Second Resource**

The second resource named is the prayer, and the Book pairs the two in several passages. The commentary on the pairing notes that patience involves the effort of the self while the prayer involves turning to God, so that the two together cover the community's duty and its hope. The Book's earlier verses about the community's response to hardship repeat the same instruction.

The Book describes the prayer as a burden except for those who are humble in spirit, and that observation is relevant to the present command, since the community is being told which resource to use when the burden is greatest. The sūrah has already instructed the community to seek help through patience and prayer in a passage about the treatment of the orphaned and the righteous, and the repetition of the instruction shows how central it is to the Book's teaching on hardship.

**The Community's Early Difficulties**

The community addressed by this verse was living through a period of struggle, with the opposition of the Makkan leadership behind it and the confrontations of Madinah ahead. The verses that follow describe trials of fear and hunger, of loss of property and life, and the reports about the revelation's circumstances connect the passage with the campaigns and the hunger of the early years.

The verse's instruction is therefore addressed to a community that would need it, and the Book names the resources before it names the trials. The reader is given the means of endurance in advance, and the passage's framing assumes that difficulty is a normal part of the community's life rather than an exception to it.

The passage continues with the description of trials that the community could measure against its own experience, and the commentators connected the verses with the campaigns and the privations of the early years in Madinah. The Book also prepares the community for losses that would occur among the believers themselves, and it describes the patient as those who, when misfortune strikes, acknowledge that they belong to God and that they will return to Him.

**"Allah Is With Those Who Are Patient"**

The sentence closes with the assurance that God is with those who are patient, and the community's scholars noted that this companionship is of a particular kind, involving support, guidance, and care rather than the general presence of God with His creation. The wording distinguishes the promise made to the patient from the statement that God is with everyone.

The Book uses the same assurance elsewhere in connection with victory and with the absence of fear, and it is the strongest comfort the sentence could give. The community is told not that its difficulties will disappear but that it will not meet them alone, and the assurance is repeated in the following verses where the patient are given news of mercy, of blessing, and of guidance.

**The Relation to the Self and to God**

The two resources named in the sentence form a pair that covers both halves of the religious life. The community works to endure and is told to bring its need before God, and the Book's instruction is that neither half can be omitted. A community that only exerted itself would be left without the help it needs, and a community that only called on God would not meet its duty.

The pairing also answers a question about how the religious life is divided. Effort belongs to the servant, and help belongs to God; the verse instructs the community to bring both, and it makes neither sufficient on its own. The community’s teachers used the pair to warn against an activism that forgets its dependence and against a devotion that neglects its duty, and the Book states the combination in the plainest possible words.

**The Verse as a Foundation for the Passage**

The sentence introduces the passage about trials, and its position at the head of that passage gives the community its footing before the difficulties are described. The verses that follow state that the patient will be given news of mercy and blessing, and the reader who has begun with the instruction reaches the promise already knowing what the instruction was for.

The instruction stands at the head of a passage about hardship, and the order in which the Book gives things matters. The community is told what to do before it is told what it will face, and the resources named are the same ones the Book prescribes wherever a community is under strain. The verses that follow describe fear, hunger, and loss, and they end with the promise of mercy, blessing, and guidance for those who endure. The reader who has heard the command understands what the promise is attached to.

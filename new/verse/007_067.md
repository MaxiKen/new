## Sūrah al-Aʿrāf 7:67

> Hûd replied, “O my people! I am no fool! But I am a messenger from the Lord of all worlds,

**Expanded Commentary**

**The Denial of the Charge**

The verse reports the reply of Hūd, and the reply begins with the denial of the charge that the leaders had brought against him in the previous verse. He states that there is no folly in him, and the denial answers the accusation in the terms in which it was made. The sūrah's earlier account contains the same structure, since Noah answers the charge of error with the statement that he is not misguided (7:61). The verse in front of us repeats the exchange with the second messenger.

The denial is stated as a fact about the messenger's person, and the second half of the sentence supplies what he actually is. The two clauses belong together in the way they did in the first account, and the sūrah's arrangement places the messengers' self-description at the point where the community's judgment has been delivered. The Book's narratives use the same answer in each case, and the repetition establishes the office rather than the individual.

**A Messenger From the Lord of All Worlds**

The verse states the messenger's office in the words that the sūrah has used for Noah, and the title of the sender is the same in both accounts. The sūrah of Hūd reports the same identification in its own version of the exchange (11:50), and the sūrah of the Poets presents Hūd among the messengers who were rejected by their people. The phrase Lord of all worlds in front of us states that the mission belongs to the Lord of everything rather than to a tribal patron.

The title carries the argument of the sūrah in miniature. The audience is asked to worship the One whose lordship covers everything, and the messenger presents himself as the servant of that Lord. The Book's use of the same title for the sending of each messenger shows that the communities were addressed by the same authority, and the sūrah's series of narratives makes the point by repetition.

**The Shared Structure of the Two Answers**

The reply in front of us is the reply of Noah with the names changed, and the similarity is one of the sūrah's deliberate effects. The same denial, the same claim, and the same description of the work follow in each account, and the sūrah's later narratives of Ṣāliḥ and Shuʿayb use the same form. The Book's presentation of the messengers as a single company with a single message is the argument that the series carries.

The tradition of the community preserves the distinguishing features of each prophet, and the sūrah's narratives supply them in the reminders that each messenger gives his people. Hūd reminds his people of their establishment in the land and of their increase in stature (7:69), and the reminder in front of us is followed by that history. The reply states the office, and the reminder states the evidence.

**What the Messenger Does Not Claim**

The reply claims nothing for the messenger beyond the office, and the Book states the limits of the messengers' claims in other passages. The sūrah of the Sand Dunes states that the Prophet is told to say that he does not know what will happen to him or to his people and that he follows only what is revealed to him (46:9), and the sūrah of the Spider reports the answer that the messenger is only a clear warner (29:50). The verse in front of us states the claim and stops there.

The restraint in front of us answers the leaders' charge by refusing the terms of it. The leaders had judged the messenger as a man seeking standing in the community, and the reply places him in an office that carries no standing of its own. The Book's accounts of the messengers' poverty and their treatment by their peoples state the same fact in the language of the narratives, and the sūrah's later verses add the reminders that the people had received.

**The Reply in the Sūrah's Series**

The verse belongs to the fixed structure of the sūrah's punishment accounts, and the structure has now appeared twice in the same form. The call, the refusal of the leaders, the charge, and the reply repeat, and the effect of the repetition is to turn the history of the communities into a single account of the response to one message. The verse in front of us supplies the fourth element of the second version.

The Book's arrangement of the series in the sūrah of Hūd and the sūrah of the Poets follows the same order, and the audience of the revelation was familiar with the pattern. The sūrah's use of it in front of us places the Makkan listeners in the line of the communities that had answered the call in the same manner, and the sūrah's later verses state the reminders that the messengers gave and the destruction that followed the refusal.

**Where the Verse Sits**

The verse stands sixty-seventh in the chapter, and it answers the charge of the leaders in the previous verse. The verse that follows states the messenger's work of delivery and counsel (7:68), and the passage then reports the reminder he gives his people of the favours they had received (7:69). This verse supplies the reply at the centre of the second account.

The reader who heard the leaders of ʿĀd dismiss their messenger now hears the messenger's answer. The folly is denied, the office is claimed, and the account proceeds to the description of the work; and the sūrah's next verse states what the messenger does among the people who have rejected him.

**The Transition to the Next Verse**

The next verse states that the messenger conveys his Lord's messages and that he is the sincere adviser of his people (7:68). The claim of the office in front of us is followed by the description of its exercise, and the account then turns to the reminder of the favours that the people had received.

The two verses together state the messenger's position and his work. He is not foolish but sent, and what he brings is the message of his Lord together with the counsel that belongs to it; and the sūrah's next verses carry the second account towards its conclusion in the destruction of the people.

## Sūrah al-Baqarah 2:241

> Reasonable provisions must be made for divorced women—a duty on those mindful ˹of Allah˺.

**Expanded Commentary**

**The Provision Repeated**

The verse instructs the community to make a provision for divorced women on a reasonable basis and describes it as a duty upon those who are mindful of God. The sentence follows the rules about the widow's bequest and states the obligation that accompanies every divorce.

The Book's concern is the position of the woman whose marriage has ended, and the community's teachers read the sentence as the general rule that gathers the provisions of the passage. Readers are shown that the legislation does not leave a separated woman without support, whatever the terms of the marriage contract.

**The Relation to the Earlier Verses**

The sentence follows the verses that treated the cases in which a divorce occurs before the consummation and before the fixing of a dowry, and the community's jurists discussed its relation to those provisions. Some held that the instruction states the general obligation of which the earlier verses supply the particulars, and others held that it confirms what has already been established.

The wording's generality gave the community the framework in which the cases were arranged, and the teachers used the sentence when they set out the law. Readers are given the account of a rule that repeats a provision in a form that covers every case, and the passage's arrangement places the general statement after the particulars.

**The Standard Named**

The sentence describes the provision as being made in the way that is commonly recognised, and the community's teachers read the phrase as the standard by which the amount and the manner are determined. The same standard appears in the rules about the waiting period and the maintenance of the nursing mother.

The Book's practice of referring its community to the custom that is approved among them appears throughout its legislation, and the community's scholars used the phrase when they discussed the measure of the provision. The passage shows that the sentence leaves the detail to the community's judgment while requiring that the judgment be sound, and the wording supplies the standard.

**The Duty Stated**

The sentence describes the provision as a duty upon those who are mindful of God, and the community's scholars read the clause as the statement of the obligation that accompanies the command. The Book's practice of attaching the obligation to the awareness of God appears in its treatment of the household and elsewhere.

The wording places the provision within the community's obligations rather than among its optional good deeds, and the community's teachers used the clause when they spoke about the treatment of the divorced woman. The passage shows that the Book's legislation on the subject rests on the mindfulness of the one who provides, and the sentence supplies the ground of the duty.

**The Reports and the Applications**

The commentaries record the cases in which the companions divorced their wives and the provision that was made, and the reports show the community applying the sentence to the circumstances it met. The community's jurists discussed the amount that is due in the various cases and recorded the positions.

The material also shows the differences between the schools about the woman who received nothing at the time of the contract and about the one whose marriage had been consummated. The account describes a body of law that the community built on the sentence together with the surrounding verses, and the sentence in this place supplies the general rule.

**The Passage's Concern**

The sentence stands near the end of the section that treats the household, and it follows the rules about the waiting periods, the nursing of children, the dowry, and the bequest to the widow. The Book has arranged its provisions in a sequence that moves from the marriage contract to its conclusion, and the community's teachers read the verses as a single body of instruction.

The passage's concern is the fair treatment of the parties at every stage, and the reader is shown that the legislation attends to the position of the woman throughout. The verse that follows closes the section with a statement about the signs that God makes clear, and the sentence in this place supplies the obligation that completes the sequence.

**The Gift That Closes the Marriage**

The provision the sentence requires of the divorced woman stands at the end of the section that treats the household, and the community's jurists read it as the general statement that gathers the cases the earlier verses describe. It is owed whether the marriage was consummated or not, and the amount is measured by the standard of the community and by the means of the man.

The schools differ about the standing of the gift in the various cases, and the discussion reflects the relation of the sentence to the provisions that precede it. The teachers noted that the wording places the provision among the duties rather than among the acts of supererogation, since the verse describes it as a duty upon those who are mindful of God.

The community's practice followed the sentence in the separations of the early believers, and the judges of the towns applied the standard of what was known among the people to the disputes that came before them. The passage therefore closes the section on the household with an obligation that reaches the woman whose marriage has ended, whatever the terms its contract contained.

**What the Verse Establishes**

The sentence establishes the duty of providing for divorced women, the standard of the customary and reasonable, and the mindfulness of God that the duty requires. The community's teachers used the verse when they set out the law on the subject, and the reader is shown that the Book's legislation on the household concludes its treatment of the married and the separated with an obligation that covers every case.

The teachers read the sentence as the statement that no woman leaves a marriage without support, and the community's law of the provision was built upon it. The verse also shows the manner in which the Book's legislation refers the community to its own recognised practice, since the amount is left to the standard of the people rather than fixed in the text.

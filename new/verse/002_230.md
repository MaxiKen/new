## Sūrah al-Baqarah 2:230

> So if a husband divorces his wife ˹three times˺, then it is not lawful for him to remarry her until after she has married another man and then is divorced. Then it is permissible for them to reunite, as long as they feel they are able to maintain the limits of Allah. These are the limits set by Allah, which He makes clear for people of knowledge.

**Expanded Commentary**

**The Third Divorce**

The verse states that if the husband divorces his wife a third time she is not lawful for him until she has married another man, and it then states that the pair may return to one another if they believe they can keep within the limits of God. The sentence supplies the consequence of the third declaration.

The Book's treatment of the subject elsewhere supplies the details of the procedure, and the community's jurists derived the rule from the sentence and the surrounding verses. Readers are shown that the legislation places a barrier at the end of the process, and the sentence states the conditions under which the marriage may later be renewed.

**The Reason for the Provision**

The commentators explained the rule as the completion of the limit that the previous verse established, and they noted that a man who could divorce and remarry without restriction would be able to keep his wife in a cycle of declarations and waiting periods. The provision removes that possibility.

The community's teachers read the sentence as a measure that protects the woman from an arrangement she could not escape, and they described the effect it has on the seriousness with which a man declares a divorce. Readers are given the account of a ruling whose purpose is the protection of the household, and the sentence's wording places the matter within the framework the preceding verses have built.

**The Intervening Marriage**

The sentence states that the woman must marry another man before the first husband may marry her again, and the community's jurists discussed the character of that marriage and the conditions attached to it. The commentaries record the positions, and most held that the marriage must be a real union rather than a formality.

The discussion reflects the care with which the community handled a provision that could be manipulated, and the reports show the teachers guarding the ruling's purpose. The passage shows that the Book's legislation leaves a door open while preventing the door from becoming a device, and the sentence in this place supplies the condition that the community applied.

**The Return Permitted**

The sentence states that if the second marriage ends the original pair may return to one another if they believe they can keep within the limits of God, and the community's teachers read the clause as the statement of the conditions that govern the renewal. The Book's concern is the observance of the limits rather than the mere resumption of the union.

The wording places the judgment of the parties at the centre of the provision, and the community's scholars noted that the sentence requires an honest assessment from the two who wish to return. The account describes a permission that is bounded by the conditions the passage has described, and the sentence leaves the decision with the parties and the outcome with God.

**The Limits Named**

The sentence closes with the statement that these are the limits that God makes clear to people who know, and the phrase completes the series of rulings that runs through the passage. The community's teachers read the ending as the statement that the legislation is addressed to people who understand what it requires.

The Book's practice of closing a legal passage with a reference to knowledge appears throughout the sūrah, and the sentence in this place supplies the disposition in which the rules are to be received. The passage shows that the provisions about the household belong to the body of guidance the sūrah has been setting out, and the sentence's closing words connect the arrangement with the understanding of those who observe it.

**The Passage's Treatment of the Household**

The verse stands among the rules about the concluding of a marriage, and it follows the passage on the waiting period and the number of declarations. The Book has described the arrangements in sequence, and the community's teachers taught the verses as a single body of instruction.

The section's concern is the orderly conduct of the community's households, and the reader is shown how the legislation addresses each stage of the process. The passage that follows returns to the waiting period and to the treatment of the wife at its end, so that the reader is carried from the limit on the declarations back to the practical conduct of the couple.

**The Barrier and the Door**

The sentence closes the cycle that the earlier verses describe, and the community's teachers read the provision as the measure that made a man consider the weight of his declaration. Reports describe the practice of the formal intervening marriage, and the Prophet ﷺ spoke against the arrangement in which a union is contracted without its conditions, so that the man who is taken back and the one who makes the arrangement were both named in the warning.

The jurists derived the conditions of the intervening marriage from the reports. A genuine union is required, with its own contract and its ordinary consequences, and a marriage designed for the sole purpose of allowing a return does not satisfy the requirement in the view of the schools.

The sentence also leaves the door open, since the pair may return to one another after the second marriage has ended when they believe that they can observe the limits. The closing words address the community's understanding, and the passage's concern is the seriousness of the bond that the couple entered.

**What the Verse Establishes**

The sentence establishes the effect of the third declaration, the requirement of an intervening marriage, the possibility of a later return under the conditions named, and the closing statement about the limits. The community's teachers used the verse when they set out the law of divorce, and the reader is shown that the Book's legislation closes the process at a definite point while leaving room for a renewed marriage undertaken with the observance of the limits.

The teachers used the sentence when they explained why the Book placed a barrier at the end of the process, and they described its purpose as the protection of the marriage from a treatment that would make its declarations idle. The door that remains open shows that the rule is not a punishment, and the conditions attached to the return place the decision with the two who wish to resume their life together.

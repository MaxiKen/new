## Sūrah an-Nūr 24:37

> by men who are not distracted—either by buying or selling—from Allah’s remembrance, or performing prayer, or paying alms-tax. They fear a Day when hearts and eyes will tremble,

**Expanded Commentary**

**True Nobility and Divine Archetypes: rijālun lā tulhīhim**

The divine command identifies the noble inhabitants who populate the elevated houses of worship: *rijālun lā tulhīhim tijāratun wa-lā bayʿun ʿan dhikri Allāh*. The designation *rijāl* (men) in Qur'anic usage does not merely denote biological masculinity; it conveys moral fortitude, spiritual maturity, integrity, chivalry, and unyielding steadfastness in faith.

These are mature souls who have conquered the intoxicating distractions of material existence. They participate actively in worldly affairs, running businesses, managing trades, negotiating markets, and engaging in agriculture, yet their hearts remain unenslaved by commerce. When the call to prayer reverberates across the city, they immediately abandon ledgers, contracts, and clients, demonstrating that their highest loyalty belongs exclusively to the Creator. They are not ascetics who fled into monastic isolation; they are righteous actors whose worldly involvement never dims their inner spiritual vigilance, illustrating the perfect synthesis of active civic life and contemplative devotion across every arena of daily responsibility, proving that worldly involvement can coexist with profound holiness.

**Commercial Engagement Without Spiritual Compromise: tijāratun wa-lā bayʿ**

The revelation distinguishes between two forms of economic transaction: *tijārah* (general trade or business ventures) and *bayʿ* (immediate buying and selling contracts). Classical commentators noted the profound subtlety of mentioning both terms.

*Tijārah* represents large-scale, ongoing business enterprises that consume long-term cognitive focus, planning, and capital investment. *Bayʿ* denotes individual, quick retail transactions where immediate profit is exchanged hands-on. By declaring that neither long-term commercial ambition nor immediate cash profit distracts them, the text covers the full spectrum of economic temptation. These individuals operate within the commercial world without allowing the world to occupy their hearts. They handle silver and gold with their hands, but keep their souls entirely consecrated to the transcendent remembrance of God, remaining upright in both large contracts and petty exchanges, refusing to let material gain compromise ethical virtue, degrade personal integrity, or dim their inner spiritual vision, living as shining examples of righteousness.

**Tripartite Pillars: Dhikr, Ṣalāh, and Zakāh**

The verse delineates the three fundamental spiritual disciplines that safeguard the soul against material corruption: *ʿan dhikri Allāhi wa-iqāmi al-ṣalāti wa-ītāʾi al-zakāh*.

First, *dhikr Allāh*—the continuous remembrance of God—anchors the conscious mind in divine presence, preventing moral blindness during negotiations and shielding the conscience from deceit. Second, *iqām al-ṣalāh*—the timely establishment of canonical prayer—imposes sacred temporal pauses throughout the business day, breaking the relentless momentum of profit-seeking and reminding the merchant of ultimate servitude before the King of kings. Third, *ītāʾ al-zakāh*—the cheerful payment of obligatory alms—purifies accumulated capital from greed, hoarding, and stinginess, affirming that wealth is a divine loan meant to be shared with the vulnerable. Together, these three disciplines immunize the faithful from economic idol worship and social callousness, establishing a healthy balance between spiritual devotion and communal generosity, ensuring that wealth circulates justly.

**Eschatological Tremor: tataqallabu fīhi al-qulūbu wa-al-abṣār**

The inner engine that powers their steadfastness is a profound awe of divine accountability: *yakhāfūna yawman tataqallabu fīhi al-qulūbu wa-al-abṣār*, they fear a Day when hearts and eyes will tremble and turn about. The verb *tataqallab* depicts violent, erratic overturning, instability, and convulsions.

On the Day of Judgment, human hearts will palpitate uncontrollably between sheer terror and desperate hope for salvation, while eyes will dart restlessly, transfixed by overwhelming cosmic upheavals and celestial ruptures. The superficial illusions of worldly security, commercial wealth, and social prestige will evaporate instantly. Those who kept their eyes lowered and their hearts tranquil in devotion on earth will be sheltered on that Day, while those who allowed their eyes and hearts to wander in heedless pursuit of worldly gain will experience agonizing, unquenchable panic as reality is unveiled, realizing too late that their accumulated wealth cannot purchase deliverance from divine reckoning.

**Psychological Resilience in the Modern Marketplace**

This verse articulates an enduring philosophy of work and ethics that directly addresses contemporary economic pressures. Modern commercial life constantly demands total cognitive absorption, conditioning professionals to measure human worth purely through quarterly profits, career advancement, consumer prestige, and accumulated assets.

The Qur'an shatters this commercial absolutism, establishing that true human dignity is found in maintaining moral autonomy amidst economic competition. The believer does not allow career demands to devour spiritual duties, nor do they compromise honesty for financial advantage. By placing the remembrance of God at the pinnacle of their daily schedule, they achieve exceptional psychological equilibrium. They operate in high-pressure financial environments without succumbing to the anxiety, moral burnout, loneliness, and cynicism that routinely ravage purely materialistic societies, preserving their inner tranquility, ethical integrity, and moral clarity across every life trial.

**The Courage of Spiritual Detachment**

The spiritual profile drawn in verse 37 demands extraordinary moral courage. To walk away from a lucrative negotiation simply because the call to prayer has sounded requires an absolute conviction that sustenance (*rizq*) comes from God alone, not from aggressive commercial maneuvers.

The early companions exemplified this sublime detachment. Commentators recorded that when the blacksmiths among them raised their hammers to strike iron and heard the *adhān*, they would not even bring the hammer down upon the anvil; they would lay the tool aside immediately to hasten to prayer. Similarly, merchants weighing goods would leave their scales balanced and walk directly to the mosque. This unyielding prioritization of the eternal over the ephemeral transformed an obscure group of desert traders into the moral leaders of civilization, showing that worldly success reaches its peak when subordinated to sacred obedience, elevating commerce into an arena of spiritual integrity and selfless service, lighting up the marketplace with divine remembrance and ethical distinction.

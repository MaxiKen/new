## Sūrah Sabaʾ 34:54

> They will be sealed off from whatever they desire, as was done to their counterparts before. Indeed, they were ˹all˺ in alarming doubt.

**Expanded Commentary**

**A Barrier, Not a Refusal**

*Wa ḥīla baynahum wa bayna mā yashtahūn* — a separation was made between them and what they desire. The verb *ḥāla* means to come between, to intervene, to block a passage; the related noun *ḥāʾil* is a partition or screen. What the verse describes is therefore not a petition denied but a structure put in place. A refusal implies someone still listening and deciding; a barrier implies the matter has passed out of the realm of decision entirely. The passive construction reinforces this, since no agent is named — the separation simply exists between them and the object. The sūrah has spent its last three verses removing possibilities in sequence: no escape (34:51), no faith available at that distance (34:52), no basis for the confidence that brought them there (34:53). This verse makes the closure architectural.

**What Exactly They Want**

The verse does not specify the object of the desire, and the readings offered for it all fit the scene. Most obviously it is a return to the world in order to repent, which is the request the Qur'an records at al-Sajdah: *"send us back and we will do good"* (32:12). It may be repentance itself, wanted directly rather than as something to be enacted elsewhere. It may be escape from the punishment. Or it may be the pleasures they had actually spent their lives desiring — the wealth and sons they had boasted about (34:35), now cut off permanently from people who no longer have any use for them. The verb *ishtahā*, to crave or covet, supports the last reading as much as the first. Its ordinary range in Arabic is appetite rather than repentance, which gives the sentence a second and colder meaning: the barrier stands between them and everything they ever wanted.

**"As Was Done to Their Counterparts Before"**

*Kamā fuʿila bi-ashyāʿihim min qabl* — as was done with their like before. *Shīʿah* means a party, a faction, a group of the same kind; *ashyāʿ* is its plural. The word classifies rather than names, and the classification is the sūrah's final use of a device it has employed throughout. Every warned town's luxury class said the same thing (34:34). Those before them denied and had ten times their resources (34:45). Now their end is the same as the end of their type. What this removes is the last trace of exceptionalism — the assumption, stated openly in verse 35 and implied everywhere else, that these particular people would be the case that came out differently. The chapter has been dismantling that assumption since its first quotation of their speech, and it closes by filing them with the others.

**The Doubt That Was There All Along**

*Innahum kānū fī shakkin murīb* — they were in doubt, disquieting doubt. The construction is emphatic in a way English struggles to reproduce, because *murīb* comes from a second root for doubt, *rayb*, so the phrase stacks two doubt-words together: doubt that is itself doubt-inducing, unsettled and unsettling. What makes this the right note to end on is that it contradicts the public posture the sūrah has recorded for fifty-four verses. These men spoke with total confidence — the Hour will never come to us, we will never be punished, we will never believe in this Qur'an. The final verse says that underneath the certainty there was doubt, and that the doubt was never resolved, only managed. Loud assertion, on this diagnosis, was the management technique rather than the conviction.

**Doubt Named as the Condition, Not Disbelief**

It is worth registering what the chapter does not say here. It could have ended by calling them deniers, idolaters, or liars, and it has used all three descriptions earlier. It ends instead with *doubt* — the mildest available term, and the one the sūrah identified in the middle of the chapter as the line of division: the arrangement exists so that those who believe in the Hereafter may be distinguished from those *in doubt about it* (34:21). The two verses frame the whole book of the chapter. Doubt is where the separation was drawn at the start and doubt is the verdict at the end, which means the sūrah has been consistent about what it was actually arguing against. Not a rival theology. An unresolved uncertainty that was never examined, because examining it was uncomfortable and mocking it was easy.

**Doubt Described Elsewhere in the Same Words**

The expression *shakk murīb* is not unique to this verse, and tracking it shows what kind of condition the Qur'an means by it. Hūd uses it twice of communities that would not accept their prophets' message, and Ibrāhīm applies it to peoples who told their messengers they were in disquieting doubt about what they were called to. Fuṣṣilat and al-Shūrā both use it of those who dispute the Book. In every case the phrase describes not a temporary uncertainty on the way to a conclusion but a settled refusal to reach one — doubt maintained as a position, because reaching a conclusion would require acting on it. That is the condition the sūrah ends on, and it explains why the chapter's central prescription was an instruction to stand apart and think rather than an instruction to believe.

**A Chapter That Began With Praise and Ends With a Partition**

Sabaʾ opened by saying that all praise belongs to God in this world and the next, and that everything in the heavens and on the earth is His (34:1). It closes with a group of people sealed away from everything they wanted. The two ends of the chapter are connected by the argument that runs between them: if ownership is total, then nothing a person holds is held independently, and the only question is whether he acknowledged the terms. Sheba was given two orchards and a safe road and asked for one thing in return (34:15). David was given iron and told to make protection and work well (34:11). Solomon's household was told to work gratefully (34:13). The people in this final verse were given a warner, an argument, a history, and an instruction to stand apart and think (34:46), and the barrier at the end stands exactly where they stopped.

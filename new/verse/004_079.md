## Sūrah al-Nisāʾ 4:79

> Whatever good befalls you is from Allah and whatever evil befalls you is from yourself. We have sent you ˹O Prophet˺ as a messenger to ˹all˺ people. And Allah is sufficient as a Witness.

**Expanded Commentary**

**Good From Allah, Evil From Yourself**

The verse states *mā aṣābaka min ḥasanatin fa min Allāhi wa mā aṣābaka min sayyiʾatin fa min nafsika* — whatever good befalls you is from Allah and whatever evil befalls you is from yourself. This corrects the split attribution just described, where good was said to be from God but evil was said to be from the Messenger (4:78). Both good and evil befall by divine decree — *both from Allah* (4:78) — yet the secondary cause differs. Good as bounty, victory, safety, and guidance is from God as favour — *faḍl min Allāh* (4:70, 4:73) — and even an atom’s weight of it is multiplied many times (4:40). Evil as *muṣībah* because of what hands put forward (4:62) — defeat where precaution was abandoned (4:71), withholding of a share, or seeking false judges (4:60) — is from the self as deed and desire. The Qur’an elsewhere says *“whatever affliction strikes you is because of what your hands have earned”* and the sūrah has tied disaster to *bi mā qaddamat aydīhim* repeatedly. To say evil is from yourself is therefore not to deny decree; it is to name the path by which decree arrives where the servant’s choice was the vessel. The sūrah has said humankind was created weak (4:28) and burdens lightened; where the weak follows desire — craving another’s portion (4:32), believing in *jibt* (4:51) — the evil that befalls through that following is from that self, while the good that befalls when the weak does what was advised — better, more reassuring, great reward, straight path (4:66–68) — is from God’s grace.

**We Have Sent You as Messenger to All People**

The clause *wa arsalnāka li al-nāsi rasūlan* — We have sent you as a messenger to all people — follows the correction to show the Messenger’s role in both good and evil that befalls. He is *rasūl* — sent, messenger — not the source of decree, as those who said *this is from you* (4:78) implied. The sūrah has said messengers were sent to be obeyed by God’s leave (4:64) and that whoever obeys the Messenger has obeyed God (4:80); to be sent to all people — *li al-nāsi* — matches the sūrah’s opening *“O humanity, be mindful of your Lord who created you from a single soul”* (4:1) and its witness scene where a witness is brought from every community and the Prophet ﷺ as witness over his (4:41). The message he brings includes the limits about inheritance after debts (4:11–12), the bar on consuming by falsehood and the permission of trade by consent (4:29), and the purification through earth where water fails (4:43). Good that befalls when those limits are kept is from God through the messenger’s clarification; evil that befalls when they are rejected is from the self that turned away stubbornly — *yaṣuddūna ṣudūdā* (4:61) — and sought *ṭāghūt*. To send him to all people is to make the means of good universally available, so that no soul can say the call to *“come to what God revealed and to the Messenger”* (4:61) was not addressed to it.

**And Allah Is Sufficient as Witness**

The closing *wa kafā bi Allāhi shahīdā* — and Allah is sufficient as a Witness — seals both attributions. *Shahīd* — witness, present testifier — was read as God witnessing that the Messenger is truly sent, as He witnessed over all things (4:33), heard and saw trusts and judgments (4:58), and knew best who enemies are though sufficient as Guardian and Helper (4:45). To say He suffices as witness is to say the Messenger’s truth does not depend on the hypocrites’ claim that good is from God but evil from you; God witnesses that both are from His decree and that good’s favour and evil’s responsibility are as stated. The phrase *kafā bi Allāhi* — sufficient is God — appears elsewhere where the community fears enemies’ schemes are strong (4:76) and where the Prophet ﷺ is told to put trust in Him as Trustee (4:81). Here sufficiency as Witness tells the community that the correction — good from God, evil from self — is not a slogan to be debated but a witnessed truth. The One who never wrongs even by an atom and who will bring a witness from every community to testify (4:41) witnesses that where good befalls, it is from His bounty, and where evil befalls through a hand’s doing, it is from that hand, while both remain within His decree.

**A Balance Between Decree and Responsibility**

Placed after *both from Allah* (4:78), this verse holds decree and responsibility together without merging them. The sūrah maintains that nothing befalls except by God’s leave — towers do not prevent death (4:78) — and at the same time maintains that hands earn — laggers’ disaster talk, distorters’ *rāʿinā*, and deviators’ wish to misguide are from selves. Early authorities explained the balance as: evil as affliction is decreed, but evil as sin is from the self that chose the scheme Satan made to seem fair. The sūrah has shown that choice repeatedly: trading a portion for misguidance (4:44), fabricating lies as blatant sin (4:50), believing in *jibt* (4:51), and seeking false judges (4:60). Each is a self’s doing that decree allowed but did not compel as worship. Good, by contrast, is from God even as secondary cause, because the sūrah’s good — guidance to the straight path (4:68), honourable company (4:69), vast shade (4:57) — is given as *faḍl* and *ajr ʿaẓīm* from grace (4:67). To say good from God and evil from self is therefore to teach the community to thank Him for the first and to turn from the self in the second, rather than to blame the Messenger for either. The next verse will make that turning practical: whoever obeys the Messenger has obeyed God, and whoever turns away, the Prophet ﷺ is not a keeper over them (4:80).

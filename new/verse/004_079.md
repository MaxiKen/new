## Sūrah al-Nisāʾ 4:79

> Whatever good befalls you is from Allah and whatever evil befalls you is from yourself. We have sent you ˹O Prophet˺ as a messenger to ˹all˺ people. And Allah is sufficient as a Witness.

### Expanded Commentary

**Good as grace, evil as consequence**

The verse attributes whatever good befalls a person to Allah and whatever evil befalls him to himself. Good ultimately comes as provision, opportunity, guidance, and enabling grace from God. Evil is “from yourself” because people introduce injustice, negligence, and disobedience into the world, even though nothing escapes Allah’s decree.

This distinction protects two truths. Gratitude prevents success from becoming self-worship, while responsibility prevents suffering from being used as an excuse for wrongdoing. It does not mean that every illness or disaster is a direct punishment for a particular sin. The verse addresses moral accountability, not permission to blame victims for every hardship.

**The Messenger’s universal mission**

The Prophet is sent to all people, and Allah is sufficient as Witness. The statement establishes the scope of the message and the certainty of its testimony. The Messenger conveys; Allah judges how people respond. A believer who follows him should therefore carry the message through justice and mercy rather than through coercion or contempt.

The verse is also a correction to the idea that prophetic guidance is responsible for the evil people choose after rejecting it. Revelation offers a path; people remain answerable for walking away. Allah’s witness is sufficient even when the public record is distorted.

**Do not blame victims for every hardship**

The statement that evil is from oneself must not become a tool for telling the sick, displaced, or abused that they caused their suffering. It addresses culpable choices and moral consequences. Compassion requires distinguishing personal wrongdoing from trials that befall a person without his choice.

## Sūrah al-Baqarah 2:117

> ˹He is˺ the Originator of the heavens and the earth! When He decrees a matter, He simply tells it, “Be!” And it is!

**Expanded Commentary**

**Badīʿ: Origin Without Model**

The verse opens with a divine name whose force is creation's absolute beginning: *the Originator of the heavens and the earth* — *badīʿ*, the one who originates without precedent, without material, without model. The word marks the difference between making and originating: the maker arranges what exists, following a pattern he did not invent, from stuff he did not produce; the badīʿ brings the stuff, the pattern, and the arrangement together from nothing, first. Sūrah al-Shūrā uses the same name in the same office: *the Originator of the heavens and the earth — He has made for you spouses from among yourselves*, proceeding immediately to the transcendence-clause *there is nothing like Him* (42:11) — origination and unlikeness held in one sentence, because a true originator cannot resemble what he originates. The name's placement after verse 116 completes the rebuttal's positive half. The child-claim had tried to place a creature inside Allah's nature as kin; the Originator-name places every creature inside His act as origination — the heavens and the earth themselves, the largest of effects, stand as originated, and whatever stands inside the two horizons stands inside their dependency. The verse's structure is thus argument by substitution: not fatherhood but origination, not a second of a kind but the first cause of every kind, and the substitution is announced with the exclamation the recital reserves for its most compressed theology.

**The Decree and the Command**

The second sentence describes origination's manner, in two verbs. *When He decrees a matter* — *qaḍā*, a verb whose range runs from decreeing through accomplishing and completing to judging, so that the decree carries its execution inside its own meaning: what Allah qaḍā is decided and done in the deciding. *He simply tells it, "Be!" — and it is*: the creative command in its Qur'anic formula, *kun fa-yakūn*, the word-family the Book deploys as creation's standing grammar (6:73, 16:40, 36:82, 40:68). Sūrah Yāsīn states the formula at its most economical: *all it takes, when He wills something to be, is simply to say to it: "Be!" And it is!* (36:82); Sūrah Ghāfir adds the formula's jurisdiction over life and death themselves: *He is the One Who gives life and causes death. When He decrees a matter, He simply tells it, "Be!" And it is!* (40:68). The formula's content is immediacy: between the Will and the being there is no workshop, no interval, no instrument, no resistance — the matter does not negotiate, the material does not wait, and the sequence from decree to existence has exactly two terms. The commentators draw the formula's first lesson explicitly: al-Rāzī reads *Be!* as conveying the ease and power of divine creation, requiring no preparation, no practice, no effort — the opposite of every human making, which is all preparation, practice, and effort, and which produces not being but arrangement. The verse installs that contrast as permanent: human beings cannot bring anything into being out of nothing, and the formula is the statement of what only Allah does.

**A Word That Is Not an Event in Time**

The formula provoked the tradition's most careful metaphysics, because read literally it hides a puzzle: a command addresses something, and the thing commanded to be does not yet exist — so to what is *Be!* spoken? Al-Rāzī's resolution refuses the temporal sequence outright. There is no order of first nonexistence, then the saying, then existence: nothing cannot be addressed, and what exists does not need the address. The *Be!* neither precedes nor follows the created thing — al-Qushayrī's formulation — but is coterminous with it, the command and the creature one event seen from its two sides, the divine speech and the divine effect simultaneous in the act. The formula is therefore not a report of words spoken at things but the description of a relation: whatever comes to be, comes to be by a Will whose effectiveness is immediate, and the imperative mood names that effectiveness in the only grammar creatures possess. The tradition presses the point further into knowledge: the verse confirms that Allah knows things before their creation, before their existence — the object of the decree is known in the knowing that decrees it. Ibn ʿArabī's school developed the doctrine into its fullest form: the *Be!* is addressed to the immutable essences — *al-aʿyān al-thābitah* — the forms of things as they stand in divine Knowledge, which is Allah's knowledge of His own qualities and attributes. The thing-to-be is not sheer nothing, since it is known; and not yet created, since it does not exist; and the command brings Will and Power to bear on the known object, which thereupon is. The school's views converge, in its own summary, on the inseparability of Allah's Knowledge, Will, and Power, and on His complete self-sufficiency toward everything He creates — the formula's deepest reading: *Be!* is not a transaction with anything outside, but the outward face of a single undivided act.

**The Formula Against the Filiation**

Read in its place after verse 116, the formula completes the child-claim's demolition with the Qur'an's own most explicit applications. Sūrah Maryam joins the two verses' contents in one sentence: *it is not for Allah to take a son! Glory be to Him. When He decrees a matter, He simply tells it, "Be!" And it is!* (19:35) — the tasbīḥ of impossibility followed immediately by the creative command, so that the command itself functions as the argument: what Allah wills into being, He originates, and what is originated is creature, not child. Sūrah Āl ʿImrān applies the formula to the claim's most honored name: *the example of Jesus in the sight of Allah is like that of Adam — He created him from dust, then said to him, "Be!" And he was!* (3:59). The comparison is the formula's polemical masterstroke: if miraculous origin proved filiation, Adam — without father or mother, made from dust and a word — would be the greater son; since Adam is agreed to be creature, the word-made man beside him is creature by the same grammar. The tradition also notes the formula's scriptural kinship across the revelations: *Be!* corresponds to the Mosaic *let there be light* of Genesis's first day, the fiat by which the biblical account also grounds all existence in a divine command — and the correspondence is the verse's ecumenical reach, since both revelations teach the same dependence: everything that is, is by a word it did not earn and cannot refuse. Filiation needs nature shared; origination needs only a word — and the two verses together leave the claim no grammar to stand in.

**The Decree That Carries the Promises**

The formula's final register is its comfort, and the register belongs to the recital's whole argument with its audience. The same *kun* that originates galaxies governs the matters the sūrah has been legislating about: the provision promised the charitable (whatever good you send ahead is found with Allah), the relief promised the patient, the resurrection promised the dead, the guidance promised the sincere — every divine commitment is a decree, and every decree stands under the formula's immediacy. The creature's projects move through means, delays, intermediaries, and resistances; the promised good moves through a word. This is why the recital can ask its community to spend wealth it fears losing, to pardon taunts it feels keenly, to hold directions it cannot verify: the counterparty in every one of those transactions is the Originator whose decrees need nothing but their own utterance. The verse also disciplines the believer's prayer-life at its root — the petition is addressed to a Will that does not deliberate, to a Power that does not assemble means, to a Knowledge that already holds the thing asked for among its known objects. Between the child-claim of verse 116 and the demand for signs of verse 118, the formula stands as the recital's statement of what divine action actually is: not kinship to be claimed, not spectacle to be ordered, but origination to be received — the heavens and the earth as the standing exhibit, and every matter He decrees, from the first dust to the last resurrection, as its continuing proof.

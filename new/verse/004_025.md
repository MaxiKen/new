## Sūrah al-Nisāʾ 4:25

> But if any of you cannot afford to marry a free believing woman, then ˹let him marry˺ a believing bondwoman possessed by one of you. Allah knows best ˹the state of˺ your faith ˹and theirs˺. You are from one another. So marry them with the permission of their owners, giving them their dowry in fairness, if they are chaste, neither promiscuous nor having secret affairs. If they commit indecency after marriage, they receive half the punishment of free women. This is for those of you who fear falling into sin. But if you are patient, it is better for you. And Allah is AllForgiving, Most Merciful.

### Expanded Commentary

**A dispensation tied to real constraints**

The verse addresses a man unable to marry a free believing woman and permits marriage to a believing bondwoman with her owners’ permission. This belongs to the historical institution of slavery and to a world in which emancipation was being encouraged but not completed at once. The passage does not invite the powerful to acquire women for pleasure; it places marriage, faith, consent through the lawful authority of the time, and a dowry within the arrangement, while describing the woman as belonging to another household in a way modern readers must treat as a historical legal category rather than a moral ideal.

**Equal spiritual worth**

The verse makes the crucial point that believing bondwomen are of the same human community as free women. Social status is not the measure of acceptance with Allah. Their Lord knows their faith, and the marriage is not a second-class spiritual union. The Prophet’s instruction to care for servants and feed and clothe them as one does oneself expresses the wider direction in which this law operates.

**A protected marriage**

The woman receives a dowry and enters marriage in chastity, not as a secret concubine. If she commits the specified offence, the legal penalty was half that of a free woman in the classical law because of her status, but the verse’s legal mechanics should never be detached from the prohibition of sexual exploitation and the possibilities of emancipation. It also warns that patience and chastity are better than treating the dispensation casually.

**Reading a historical text without erasing its limits**

The verse is neither a licence to revive trafficking nor a reason to deny the Qur’an’s historical legal address. It regulated a pervasive institution, recognized the faith and rights of enslaved people, and directed conduct toward lawful marriage and eventual freedom. Contemporary law has abolished the institution in the societies to which Muslims belong; the category therefore has no legitimate modern application. The lasting principle is that sexual access must not be built on coercion, concealment, or contempt for a person’s faith and dignity.

**The direction of the law**

The historical category addressed here should be studied with the Qur’an’s emancipation measures, the Prophetic commands of humane treatment, and the contemporary abolition of slavery. It cannot be extracted to validate ownership of persons today. What remains applicable is the insistence that vulnerability never makes a person available for secret or coercive intimacy.

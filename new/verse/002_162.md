## Sūrah al-Baqarah 2:162

> They will be in Hell forever. Their punishment will not be lightened, nor will they be delayed ˹from it˺.

**Expanded Commentary**

**The Permanence Stated**

The verse states that those described in the previous sentence will remain in the punishment forever, that it will not be lightened for them, and that they will not be granted respite. The three statements together rule out every form of relief that a person might imagine.

The Book describes the state of the people of the Fire in these terms in several passages, and the sūrah has already spoken about those who remain in the fire in the account of the calf worshippers. The wording here is brief and complete, and the reader receives the statement without qualification.

**The Recurrence of the Phrase in the Book**

The statement that the punishment will not be lightened appears in other chapters with the same wording, and the Book repeats it in passages that describe the fate of those who persisted in disbelief. The repetition gives the community its vocabulary for the subject, and the consistency of the wording across the chapters indicates that the Book presents a single account.

The Commentators read the repetitions together and connected them with the passages about the Fire in which the people ask for relief and the passages in which the duration of the punishment is described. What the community received from the Book is a settled statement rather than a collection of images, and the verses in this sūrah belong to that statement.

**The Meaning of Abiding**

The word rendered as abiding describes a settled residence, and the Book uses it for the state of the people of the Garden as well as for the people of the Fire. The community's scholars discussed at length how the term is to be understood, and their discussions are connected with the passages in which God's will and mercy are described.

The sūrah's earlier verses about the calf worshippers and about those who claimed that the fire would touch them only for a limited number of days show that the Book corrects the assumption that punishment in the hereafter is temporary. The reader is given a statement about permanence in a passage that has been dealing with communities that postponed the reckoning in their own minds.

**The Absence of Respite**

The closing words state that those in the punishment will not be given time, and the term describes a delay granted so that a person may change his condition. The Book's use of the term elsewhere includes the respite granted to those who are warned in this life, so that the removal of the respite marks the end of the period in which change was possible.

The sentence therefore tells the reader where the boundary lies. While a person lives, he is given time and the warning that accompanies it; the verse describes what follows when the time has run out. The community's teachers used the passage when they urged the reader to act while he could, and the placement of the verse after the offer of forgiveness in the preceding verses gives the warning its context.

**How the Verse Follows the Offer**

The Book places the statement of permanence after the verse that describes the acceptance of those who repent, and the sequence gives the passage its force. The reader is shown that the door was open, that the terms were stated plainly, and that what follows the closing of the door is described without softening.

The Book’s method in this passage is to present the two possibilities and to leave the choice in the reader's hands. The verse does not tell the reader what his own end will be; it tells him what the end of persistence is, and it does so after having told him what follows repentance. The two statements belong together, and the community's preachers have always used them in that pairing.

**The Sūrah's Treatment of Duration**

Earlier in the sūrah, the Book addressed a claim that the Fire would touch a certain community for a limited number of days, and it asked on what authority the claim rested. The present verse supplies the Book's own statement about duration, and the reader who has followed the passage has already been shown which account he is to trust.

The sūrah's treatment of the subject is therefore part of a longer conversation with the communities who held expectations about their own futures. The Book states the conditions that belong to its own account of the hereafter, and it leaves the reader with a description that applies to anyone who dies in the condition the previous verse named.

The community’s teachers used the passage to answer those who held that punishment in the hereafter would be brief, and they cited the Book’s own question about the basis of such a claim. What the sūrah establishes is a settled account of the matter, and the verses that repeat the wording in other chapters confirm it. The reader is therefore given a statement about duration that does not depend on a single verse, and the teachers of the community treated the consistency of the Book as part of its argument.

**What the Verse Contributes to the Passage**

The sentence adds permanence, the absence of relief, and the absence of delay to the account of the punishment, and it does so in three short clauses. The reader is given a complete picture in a few words, and the verses that follow turn to the oneness of God and to the signs in creation.

The contrast between the brevity of the statement and the weight of its subject is characteristic of the Book's treatment of the hereafter. What the passage asks of the reader is that he take the statement as it stands and that he act on the invitation that the previous verse has provided.

The sentence also completes the reader’s picture of the two outcomes that the passage has set side by side. The community has been shown what is granted to those who turn to God and what is stated of those who do not, and the details of permanence and of the absence of delay give shape to the second. The Book’s method in this section is to describe the alternatives in full rather than to leave them general, and the reader who follows the passage receives both accounts before the verses turn to the signs in the creation.

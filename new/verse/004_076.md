## Sūrah al-Nisāʾ 4:76

> Believers fight for the cause of Allah, whereas disbelievers fight for the cause of the Devil. So fight against Satan’s ˹evil˺ forces. Indeed, Satan’s schemes are ever weak.

**Expanded Commentary**

**Two Causes, Two Fightings**

The verse states *alladhīna āmanū yuqātilūna fī sabīli Allāh wa alladhīna kafarū yuqātilūna fī sabīli al-ṭāghūt* — believers fight for the cause of Allah, whereas disbelievers fight for the cause of the Devil, the false authority. The pairing gathers the sūrah’s long contrast between the path God wills to make clear (4:26) and the far straying Satan desires when people seek false judges (4:60). To fight *fī sabīli Allāh* was explained as fighting to remove oppression, to deliver the weak who cry for a guardian and helper (4:75), to return trusts and judge with fairness (4:58), and to secure the limits about wealth and marriage the sūrah has set. To fight *fī sabīli al-ṭāghūt* — in the way of the overstepping — was explained as fighting to keep the land of oppressors as it is, to keep idols and false gods believed (4:51), to keep women inherited against will, and to keep wealth devoured by falsehood (4:29). The sūrah has shown that those who follow desires wish you to deviate greatly (4:27) and that those given a portion who trade it for misguidance wish you to deviate from the path (4:44); their fighting is that wish armed. Believers and disbelievers are therefore not distinguished here by tribe but by cause, and cause determines whose fighting has a future.

**So Fight Against Satan’s Forces**

The command *fa qātilū awliyāʾ al-shayṭān* — so fight against Satan’s evil forces — follows as corollary. *Awliyāʾ al-shayṭān* — allies, close friends of Satan — was read as those who take him as associate — *qarīn* — what an evil associate (4:38) — and who take him as guardian instead of God who suffices as Guardian and Helper (4:45). The verse tells believers not to fear the name that accompanies opposing fighting; the forces that fight for *ṭāghūt* may appear numerous — the polytheists, their allies among those given a portion who reassure they are better guided (4:51), and the laggers who wish for gain (4:73) — but they are called *awliyāʾ al-shayṭān*, a party whose patron is already the defeated. The command is given to believers who were told to take precautions and go forth in groups or together (4:71); to fight the forces is to fight prepared, not heedless. The sūrah elsewhere says *“take your precautions”* before *“go forth,”* showing that the community that fights for God does not fight with the Devil’s method — deceit, wordplay, and coercion — but with guard, referral to Book and Messenger (4:59), and acceptance of judgment without resistance (4:65). To fight Satan’s allies is therefore not to mimic them but to oppose them where they make oppression.

**Indeed Satan’s Schemes Are Ever Weak**

The reassurance *inna kayda al-shayṭāni kāna ḍaʿīfa* — indeed, Satan’s schemes are ever weak — was explained as the reason not to fear. *Kayd* — scheme, plot, stratagem — was read as the whisper that makes stinginess look like prudence (4:37), ostentation look like honour (4:38), word distortion — *rāʿinā* for *unẓurnā* — look like cleverness (4:46), and belief in *jibt* look like guidance (4:51). Each was a scheme to keep a limit blurred. The sūrah has said that God is sufficient as Guardian and Helper (4:45), that He never wrongs even by an atom but multiplies good (4:40), and that He multiplies and gives a great reward for fighting whether martyred or victorious (4:74). Against that sufficiency and multiplication, the Devil’s schemes are *ḍaʿīf* — weak, frail — not because they are few but because they have no weight where God’s cause is fought. Early authorities noted that the weakness is intrinsic: Satan promises and his promise is delusion, he commands stinginess and his command impoverishes. The believers who fight for God fight for the oppressed who ask for a guardian and helper from God’s grace (4:75); the disbelievers who fight for the Devil fight to keep that helper from arriving. The first fight is supported by the sufficient Helper; the second is supported by a weak plot. That is why the verse ends where it does: the weakness is not a prediction but a standing truth — *kāna ḍaʿīfa* — it was ever weak, is weak.

**A Verse Between the Oppressed’s Cry and the Fearful’s Delay**

Placed between the cry of the oppressed (4:75) and the exposure of those told *do not fight, establish prayer and give alms* who then feared people as God should be feared when fighting was ordered (4:77), this verse is the sūrah’s pivot from motive to courage. The oppressed need a fight in God’s cause; the fearful delay because they see the enemy as strong. The verse says the enemy’s camp fights for a weak cause whose patron’s schemes are ever weak, while you fight for the cause whose Guardian suffices. The lagger who lagged at disaster and wished after bounty (4:72–73) measured strength by outcome — safety or spoils — and therefore saw the Devil’s forces as strong when they were many. The sūrah measures strength by cause and patron, and therefore sees them as weak even when they are many, because their patron cannot give even a speck — *naqīr* — if he controlled kingdom (4:53). For the believers told that humankind was created weak and burdens lightened (4:28), the reassurance is not that they are strong in themselves but that the opponent’s scheming is weak before the Helper who suffices. To fight the forces of the weak schemer is therefore not a gamble but the straight path walked. That straight path is the one He promised to make clear while lightening burdens (4:27–28).

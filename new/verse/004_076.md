## Sūrah al-Nisāʾ 4:76

> Believers fight for the cause of Allah, whereas disbelievers fight for the cause of the Devil. So fight against Satan’s ˹evil˺ forces. Indeed, Satan’s schemes are ever weak.

### Expanded Commentary

**Two causes, two moral orientations**

Believers fight for Allah’s cause while disbelievers fight for the cause of Satan. The contrast is ethical rather than a claim that every opponent is personally identical or that every conflict with a Muslim side is holy. The question is what a force serves: justice, protection, and obedience, or oppression, vanity, and domination.

**The weakness of Satan’s schemes**

Satan can make falsehood appear powerful, but his schemes are weak in the final sense. They depend on fear, fragmentation, exaggeration, and the willingness of people to cooperate with their own corruption. A believer should not be paralysed by the size of an unjust power. Yet confidence in Satan’s weakness does not eliminate planning; the previous verse commanded precautions.

The verse also prevents a simplistic geography of good and evil. A community calling itself Muslim can fight for Satan if it massacres innocents, steals, or pursues pride. The label of the cause must be tested by its means and ends. The real struggle begins with the self’s willingness to obey God rather than anger.

**The cause must be examined**

A slogan about God does not purify a violent cause. Examine whether the action protects the vulnerable, whether peaceful alternatives were considered, and whether means remain within divine limits. Satan’s scheme often persuades people that their anger is already a sacred mandate.

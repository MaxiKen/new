## Sūrah al-Nisāʾ 4:34

> Men are the caretakers of women, as men have been provisioned by Allah over women and tasked with supporting them financially. And righteous women are devoutly obedient and, when alone, protective of what Allah has entrusted them with. And if you sense ill-conduct from your women, advise them ˹first˺, ˹if they persist,˺ do not share their beds, ˹but if they still persist,˺ then discipline them ˹gently˺. But if they change their ways, do not be unjust to them. Surely Allah is Most High, All-Great.

### Expanded Commentary

**Responsibility, not superiority**

The verse describes men as maintainers and protectors of women because Allah has given some people different capacities and because men spend from their wealth. The statement belongs to a household system in which the husband bears material maintenance. It establishes responsibility and accountability, not a licence for domination. A person cannot claim authority while refusing provision, safety, consultation, and good treatment.

The verse praises women who are devout and protect what should be protected in the husband’s absence. The phrase includes fidelity, property, privacy, and the trust of the marriage. It does not erase the wife’s independent ownership or make her a servant. Both spouses have duties; the verse identifies the husband’s financial and protective burden as the basis for a particular household responsibility.

**Nushūz and a graduated response**

The passage addresses serious marital discord, not ordinary irritation or a husband’s desire for obedience in every preference. The steps—admonition, separation in beds, and the final term often translated as striking—have been the subject of extensive legal and ethical discussion. They cannot be read apart from the Prophet’s prohibition of harming women, his statement that the best believers are best to their families, his own practice of never striking a wife, and the following verse’s command to seek arbitration.

Many contemporary scholars and Muslim legal authorities emphasize that any act causing injury, humiliation, or fear violates the Qur’anic requirement of honourable association; domestic violence is a crime and cannot be justified by this verse. The Arabic term has a range of uses, and the Prophetic model, the general ban on harm, and the purposes of marriage constrain any interpretation. A reading that turns the verse into permission for abuse contradicts the chapter’s protection of women.

**The conclusion is mercy and knowledge**

Allah is Most High and Great: the husband who has a limited household responsibility remains under the authority of God. He must not imitate divine sovereignty. If the wife reforms, the husband has no right to continue searching for punishment or leverage. The verse ends by closing the door to retaliation and reminding the powerful that the One above them knows every hidden act.

**Authority is measured by care**

Any reading of household responsibility that produces fear, injury, or humiliation has failed the verse’s own standard of honourable conduct. A husband’s responsibility should make him more accountable to provide and protect. It cannot make him less accountable to the wife, the law, or Allah, who is higher than every household head.

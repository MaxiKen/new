## Sūrah al-Anʿām 6:18

> He reigns supreme over His creation. And He is the All-Wise, All-Aware.

**Expanded Commentary**

**The Summit of the Argument So Far**

The sūrah has been climbing through its first verses, and this short sentence states the result. Ownership of the heavens and the earth (6:12), control of harm and benefit (6:17), and now unqualified dominance: He reigns supreme over His creation. The Arabic root behind the phrase carries the sense of overpowering and subduing, the way a thing is held down by something stronger than itself. Nothing in the created order stands outside that grip.

The word for creation here is the word for servants, which links the claim to loyalty rather than to raw force. A maker has authority over what he made; the verse states that authority in the language of ownership over those who serve, and the two senses run together deliberately. The sūrah is arguing with a city that had assigned shares of divine authority to other beings, and the sentence leaves no gap in which such a share could be lodged.

**The Same Claim Beside the Recording Angels**

Five verses later the phrase returns with an addition that shows what kind of dominance is meant. He reigns supreme over all of His creation, and sends recording-angels watching over you, and when death comes the angels take the soul without neglecting the duty (6:61). The next sentence reports that people are returned to Allah, their True Master, to whom judgment belongs alone, and that He is the swiftest reckoner (6:62).

Dominance in the Book is therefore never blind force. It arrives with a ledger, and the powerful hand that holds the creature down is the same hand that keeps a careful account of the creature's days. The rulers of Makkah understood power as something exerted in public; the verse attaches it to an administration that never sleeps and never loses a file.

**Not Merely the Strongest**

If the verse stopped at dominance, it would have described a tyrant. The pair of names that follows prevents that reading. The All-Wise and the All-Aware are attached to overpowering rule so that the reader cannot separate the strength from the intelligence that directs it and the knowledge that records it. Power in the Book is always power with reasons.

That combination matters to an audience that had seen how unchecked strength behaves. Arabia's chieftains were not restrained by constitutions, and the sūrah's listeners had felt what it meant to fall into the wrong hands. The verse tells them that the supreme power over them is not arbitrary, and that its decrees are the product of wisdom rather than of appetite.

**All-Wise: Nothing Is Placed Wrongly**

Wisdom in the Qur'anic vocabulary means more than cleverness; it means everything set where it belongs, so that no arrangement in the creation is an accident. The suffering of the believers and the long prosperity of their tormentors are therefore not evidence against God's government, because the purpose behind both is known to him who set them in place. Time is part of the design, and the audience was being asked to wait inside it.

This is the doctrine the sūrah keeps needing. The Quraysh had asked why no angel came (6:8), why the punishment had not fallen, why the message had not been sent as a single book. Each complaint assumed that the current arrangement was a mistake. The name All-Wise answers all of them at once: the arrangement has an author, and the author has a reason.

**All-Aware: Nothing Escapes the Record**

The second name comes from the vocabulary of news; it describes one who has the report of a thing, not merely a general suspicion of it. In this sūrah, where the hidden and the declared have already been claimed by God (6:3), the name seals the argument. Deeds done in darkness, plans made in closed rooms, oaths taken in secret, and intentions never acted upon are all inside the report.

That is why the pair of names closes this particular verse rather than a verse about creation. The claim being made is that nothing in the universe can resist God's authority; the qualification is that nothing in the universe can escape His notice either. A person who has grasped both halves understands the shape of the life the sūrah is describing: absolute power, exact accounting, and a mercy already prescribed.

The Arabic word also carries the sense of a report received, which is worth pausing over. News is what reaches someone at a distance; the name says that nothing happening anywhere in creation is at a distance from God. The idols of the town had their local jurisdictions, and a man travelling between them could imagine business unobserved in the gaps. The name closes the gaps. Every journey, every private meal, and every unspoken design sits inside the awareness of the one who subdues, and the sūrah has already made that awareness personal to each reader in the second person.

**What This Rule Requires of the Creature**

A being who is dominated, watched, and dealt with wisely has only one reasonable posture, and the Book names it elsewhere: if Allah helps you, none can defeat you, and if He denies you help, who else could help you, so in Allah let the believers put their trust (3:160). The verse has argued away every other refuge — the stones and the stars cannot harm or help, the ancestors cannot negotiate, and the messenger claims no share of the power.

What remains is a creature standing before an overpowering, careful, informed authority that has already promised mercy. The response the sūrah will go on to state is submission, and this verse is the ground of it. Fear of such power is the beginning of sense, not the end of it.

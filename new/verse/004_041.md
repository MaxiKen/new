## Sūrah al-Nisāʾ 4:41

> So how will it be when We bring a witness from every faith-community and bring you ˹O Prophet˺ as a witness against yours?

**Expanded Commentary**

**A Question That Opens the Day of Witnesses**

The verse asks *fa kayfa idhā jiʾnā min kulli ummatin bi shahīdin wa jiʾnā bika ʿalā hāʾulāʾi shahīdā* — so how will it be when We bring a witness from every faith-community and bring you as a witness against yours? The question *kayfa* — how — invites the listener to imagine a scene whose gravity cannot be answered with words. After promises that not an atom’s weight is wronged and that good is multiplied (4:40), and after the offer to cover lesser faults for those who avoid major sins (4:31), the sūrah turns to the Day when those promises are seen. The witnesses — *shahīd* — from every *ummah* were explained as the prophets sent to each community, who will testify whether they conveyed, whether they were believed, and how people responded to limits about property, marriage, and worship. The Prophet Muḥammad ﷺ is then brought as witness over his own community — the one addressed throughout al-Nisāʾ with *yā ayyuhā al-nās* (4:1) and *yā ayyuhā alladhīna āmanū* (4:29). The Qur’an elsewhere says *“We have made you a middle community that you may be witnesses over people and the Messenger may be witness over you”* (2:143), showing that witness is both a burden and an honour. The question how will it be does not seek information; it seeks awe before a court where every *ummah* stands with its prophet, and this *ummah* stands with its own.

**Every Community With Its Witness**

The phrase *min kulli ummatin bi shahīdin* was read as each community brought with its messenger who will confirm the message delivered. The sūrah has detailed what that message contained in this context: creation from a single soul and guarding womb-ties (4:1), returning orphan wealth with witnesses (4:6), honouring obligatory shares after debts (4:11–12), guarding marriage through dowries and chastity (4:4, 4:24), and forbidding devouring by falsehood (4:29). On the Day, the question will be whether those limits were kept. The witness from each community will speak not from hearsay but from presence, as one who lived among his people and warned them. The scene therefore mirrors the sūrah’s insistence that human witnesses secure handovers (4:6) and that God is Witness over all things (4:33). What was done with witnesses on earth — the estate divided, the dowry given, the trade made by mutual consent — will be confirmed by the greater witnesses who saw the community as a whole. The Qur’an elsewhere says that disbelievers will wish their messengers had never been sent, but here the messengers’ presence is the proof that the community was not left without clear guidance (4:26).

**And You as Witness Against Yours**

The addition *wa jiʾnā bika ʿalā hāʾulāʾi shahīdā* — and bring you as witness over these — singles out the Prophet Muḥammad ﷺ for this audience. His witness was understood as twofold: that he conveyed the limits about inheritance and marriage that the sūrah has spelled out, and that he saw how his community received them — who kept limits and entered Gardens (4:13) and who exceeded them and faced humiliation (4:14). The Prophet ﷺ is elsewhere described as witness, bearer of good tidings, and warner, and this verse places that witness at the climax of history. The community that was told *“obey Allah and obey the Messenger and those in authority among you, and refer disputes to Allah and His Messenger”* (4:59) will find the one to whom disputes were to be referred now testifying about how they were resolved. The question how will it be therefore carries a personal weight for believers: the one who knows their names, who was told *“so how will it be when We bring you as witness,”* will be the one who speaks. The sūrah has said that the Messenger’s clarification makes obedience to him part of keeping limits (4:13–14, 4:59); here that clarification becomes testimony.

**A Scene That Holds Atom’s Weight and Great Reward Together**

The verse follows the assurance that not an atom’s weight is wronged and that good is multiplied with a great reward from grace (4:40), and it shows where that assurance is displayed. The witnesses are brought because accounting must be seen to be exact. The atom’s weight that was never wronged in the estate division, the dowry left where it was given despite a heap of gold (4:20), the small provision offered with kind speech (4:8) — all will appear not as hidden details but as recorded deeds with witnesses. The promise of multiplication and grace and the warning of Fire that is easy for God (4:30) are both made credible by the court that this verse opens. Without witnesses, the promises could feel distant; with prophets and with the Prophet ﷺ as witnesses, the promises become a scene the reader can picture and therefore prepare for. The sūrah has already said that God is ever Watchful (4:1) and sufficient as Reckoner (4:6) and Helper (4:45); this verse is that watch and reckoning made visible, with human witnesses who lived the law brought to confirm.

**How Will It Be — The Question That Changes How One Counts**

The unanswered how is the verse’s teaching. It does not describe the outcome for the disobedient in this sentence; the next verse will say they will wish to be dust and will not be able to hide anything from God (4:42). Here the question is left hanging so that the listener supplies the answer with his own conduct. A person who handles an orphan’s property, a dowry, or a sale while remembering that a witness from his own community — the Prophet ﷺ — will be brought about those very transactions will count differently. The sūrah’s earlier question — how could you take it after intimacy and firm commitment (4:21) — worked the same way, inviting conscience to answer. This greater how does the same on the scale of history. The sūrah began with creation from a single soul and will soon remind that every soul will be judged with a witness; between those two whens, the believer is given the map — make things clear, guide to noble ways, turn in mercy (4:26), lighten because you are weak (4:28), avoid major sins (4:31), ask God for bounty rather than crave another’s (4:32). The witness scene gathers that map and asks, when all of that is brought with its witnesses, how will it be? The one who keeps limits while remembering the witness will hope that the answer for him is Gardens and honour; the one who exceeds them will already feel why the next verse says wishing to be dust will be the only wish left.

## Sūrah al-Nisāʾ 4:41

> So how will it be when We bring a witness from every faith-community and bring you ˹O Prophet˺ as a witness against yours?

### Expanded Commentary

**A witness from every community**

The verse moves the reader from earthly testimony to the scene of resurrection. Every community will have a witness, understood by the exegetes as its messenger or one who conveys the truth, and Muhammad will be brought as a witness concerning his community. The arrangement establishes that revelation was not an abstract event without public responsibility. A message was delivered, heard, embodied, and answered; the final court gathers the evidence from those entrusted with conveying it.

**The Prophet’s witness**

The Prophet’s testimony is not a licence for believers to assume themselves safe because they carry his name. It intensifies responsibility. He witnessed whether a community received the guidance, lived by it, and transmitted it faithfully. The Qur’an elsewhere describes him as a witness, bearer of good news, and warner. His witness includes the clarity of his delivery and the reality of the response.

**A court beyond partial evidence**

Earthly courts depend on incomplete witnesses, documents, and memory. The divine court knows what was accessible to a person, what obstacles distorted the message, and what the heart did with the evidence. This does not cancel public accountability; it shows that final judgment will be more exact than any human process. The verse should make a believer careful about claiming certainty regarding another person’s ultimate fate.

It also gives dignity to testimony in this world. To speak truthfully about one’s community, family, and leaders is to imitate the seriousness of the coming court. A witness must not flatter his group or hide its wrongdoing, because the Messenger who taught the community will be its witness before the One who hears every account.

**Testimony and accountability**

The coming witness should make both speaker and listener careful with religious claims. A community that asks others to answer for truth must also answer for how it treated the truth. Testimony is not a spectacle reserved for the end of time; it begins whenever a person explains a command, reports an event, or speaks about another community.

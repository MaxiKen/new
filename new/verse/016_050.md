## Sūrah al-Naḥl 16:50

> **They fear their Lord above them, and do whatever they are commanded.**

**Expanded Commentary**

**Fear Of Lord Above Them**

Yakhāfūna rabbahum min fawqihim — they fear their Lord above them. The pronoun refers to angels who prostrate and are not too proud, as described in previous verse. Yakhāfūna from kh-w-f means to fear with reverence. Rabbahum, their Lord, who creates, sustains, commands. Min fawqihim, from above them, indicates Allah is above creation, high, exalted, as 6:18 says He is the Subjugator above His servants. Al-Ṭabarī explains angels fear Lord above because they know His greatness and that He is above them in rank and dominion. The phrase also indicates Allah is above in literal highness as understood by early generations without asking how. Al-Rāzī discusses fawq can mean above in rank and power, not only spatial, but includes both. Angels fear despite being sinless and near, because they know Allah's greatness. If sinless angels fear, how much more should sinful humans fear? This fear is reverential, with knowledge that Lord is above, watching, commanding.

**Doing Whatever They Are Commanded**

Wa-yafʿalūna mā yu'marūn — and they do whatever they are commanded. Yafʿalūna from f-ʿ-l means to do, act. Mā yu'marūn, what they are commanded, from a-m-r, command. Al-Ṭabarī says angels do whatever Allah commands without disobedience, delay, or questioning, as 66:6 says they do not disobey Allah in what He commands and do what commanded. The phrase indicates complete obedience: they do all that is commanded, not some. This contrasts with humans who often do not do what commanded due to arrogance or heedlessness. Angels' obedience is model: fear Lord above, do what commanded. Their obedience is constant, not occasional, and covers all commands, from taking souls to carrying revelation to prostrating.

**Why Fear And Obedience Are Paired**

Fear and obedience are paired because fear of Lord above leads to doing what is commanded. Without fear, obedience may be superficial; with fear, obedience is sincere and prompt. Al-Qurṭubī notes angels fear despite being sinless and near to Allah, because they know Allah's greatness. If sinless angels fear, how much more should sinful humans fear? The verse therefore encourages fear that leads to obedience, not fear that paralyzes. Fear is reverential, with knowledge that Lord is above, watching, commanding, able to punish or forgive. Obedience without fear can be mechanical; fear without obedience can be despair. Together they form complete worship: inward fear and outward obedience, heart and limbs aligned.

**Connection To Universal Prostration**

Verse 49 said whatever in heavens and earth of living creatures and angels bow to Allah, not too proud. Verse 50 explains why angels bow: they fear Lord above and do what commanded. Their prostration is not forced but from fear and obedience. This explains universal prostration: all creation prostrates because it fears Lord above and does what commanded by natural law, while angels prostrate willingly with fear and obedience. Humans who refuse lack fear and disobey, making them exception. The sūrah thus shows prostration is natural response to Lord above, and refusal is unnatural. The progression from shadows to living creatures to angels shows universal submission, with arrogant humans as exception who will be humiliated.

**A Verse That Prepares For Command Of Tawḥīd**

The next verse will command not to take two gods, there is only One, so be in awe of Me alone. Mention that angels fear Lord above and do what commanded prepares for that command. If angels who are near fear and obey, humans should fear and obey Allah alone, not take two gods. The verse also prepares for to Him belongs whatever in heavens and earth and to Him alone is everlasting devotion. If angels fear Lord above and do what commanded, then devotion should be to Him alone, as angels do. The verse's placement after universal prostration and before command of tawḥīd is deliberate: it shows model of worship that is fearful, obedient, humble, not proud, prostrating.

**How Angels Model Worship For Humans**

Angels model worship that is fearful, obedient, humble, not proud, prostrating. They fear Lord above, not equal, not below, showing hierarchy: Lord above, angels below, yet not too proud to bow. Humans are below angels in some ways, yet often too proud to bow. The verse invites to imitate angels: fear Lord above, do what commanded, bow, not be proud. Earlier description of shadows prostrating humbly and living creatures bowing shows all creation models worship; angels model it most perfectly because they do whatever commanded without exception. The believer who wants good home of righteous should imitate angelic fear and obedience, making fear of Lord above a daily awareness that leads to doing what is commanded, whether prayer, gratitude, patience, or trust.

**A Lesson That Connects To Gratitude And Reflection**

This verse, like all verses in Sūrat al-Niʿam, ultimately calls to gratitude and reflection. Whether it describes angels fearing and obeying, the purpose is to make reader recognize that even angels, who have no sin, fear and obey, so human who has many blessings and many shortcomings should fear and obey even more. Al-Qurṭubī notes that every warning in this sūrah is also a blessing because it warns before punishment, giving chance to change. Every promise is also a blessing because it encourages good. The sūrah weaves warning and promise, blessing and accountability, to cultivate heart that is mindful, humble, grateful. The reader who reflects on angels' fear and obedience with that mindset will find motivation to be among those who say goodness when asked what Lord revealed, and who are greeted with peace at death.


**The Practical Meaning Of Above**

Min fawqihim, above them, teaches that Allah is above creation, not within creation, distinct, high. This has practical meaning: when you fear someone above you, you are humble, you look up, you obey. Angels fear Lord above, so they obey. Humans who fear Lord above will also obey, not take two gods, not associate others, will be in awe of Him alone as next verse says. The concept of above also gives hope: Lord above sees, knows what you conceal and reveal, knows your patience and trust, and will reward. Fear of Lord above therefore includes both awe and hope, both reverence and reliance, leading to complete worship that angels model.

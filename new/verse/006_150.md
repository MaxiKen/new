## Sūrah al-Anʿām 6:150

> Say, ˹O Prophet,˺ “Bring your witnesses who can testify that Allah has forbidden this.” If they ˹falsely˺ testify, do not testify with them. And do not follow the desires of those who deny Our proofs, disbelieve in the Hereafter, and set up equals with their Lord.

**Expanded Commentary**

**The Demand for Witnesses**

Say, Bring your witnesses who can testify that Allah has forbidden this. The verse gives the Prophet a demand that turns the audience's claim into a matter to be proved, and the Book uses the same form of challenge in its arguments about the revelation. The sūrah of the Cow tells the doubters to produce a sūrah like the revelation and to call their helpers besides God if they are truthful (2:23), and the sūrah of the Ants asks whether another god could originate creation, resurrect it, and provide from the heavens and the earth (27:64).

The witness the verse demands is a person who can testify that God forbade the animals the audience had declared unlawful. The sūrah has already asked whether the audience was present when the commandment was given (6:144), and the demand for witnesses completes the challenge: the claim of a divine prohibition requires witnesses, and none exist.

**The Instruction About False Testimony**

If they falsely testify, do not testify with them. The sentence instructs the Prophet to refuse to join the testimony, and the Book treats the bearing of false witness as a serious offence. The sūrah of the Distinguisher includes among the qualities of the servants of the Most Compassionate that they do not bear false witness and that they pass by falsehood with dignity (25:72), and the sūrah of the Pilgrimage includes the avoidance of false testimony among the commands given to the community.

The instruction protects the Prophet from the appearance of agreement. The audience would have been willing to produce witnesses for a claim that served their interests, and the sentence tells him that his presence would be taken as confirmation. The sūrah's method in such cases is to leave the opponents to their own devices while the Prophet holds to the revelation, as it did when it told him to leave those who took their religion as play (6:70).

**The Instruction Not to Follow Desires**

And do not follow the desires of those who deny Our proofs, disbelieve in the Hereafter, and set up equals with their Lord. The sentence names three characteristics of the people whose desires the Prophet is not to follow, and each characteristic corresponds to a feature of the audience's position that the sūrah has documented. They deny the signs, which the sūrah has described at length in its accounts of the demand for miracles; they disbelieve in the Hereafter, which the sūrah identified as the condition of those who receive the whisperings of the devils (6:113); and they set up equals with God, which is the charge that runs through the whole chapter.

The Book's warnings against following desire appear in several places. The sūrah of the Cattle itself tells the Prophet to say that he will not follow the audience's desires, since doing so would lead him astray from the guidance of God (6:56), and the sūrah of the Kneeling describes those who have taken their own desires as their god and whom God has left to stray knowingly (45:23). The instruction in this verse belongs to that group of passages, and it is addressed to the Prophet at the point where the dietary dispute has reached its end.

**The Reasons for the Instruction**

The Book gives the Prophet reasons for the limits it places on his engagement with the audience's claims. The sūrah has told him that he is not a keeper over the people (6:107) and that the hearts of those who refuse are left in their condition (6:110), and the sentence in front of us adds that the desires of such people are not to become the measure of his conduct. The instruction is a protection for the message as much as for the man who carries it.

The Book's treatment of the community's relations with people of other religions elsewhere is built on the same principle. The sūrah of the Spider tells the believers to argue with the People of the Book in the best manner and to state their belief in what has been revealed to them and to the earlier communities (29:46), and the sūrah of the Clearing forbids the believers from allowing the hostility of others to drive them into injustice. The verse in front of us states the negative form of the same rule.

**What the Verse Says About the Dietary Claim**

The verse settles the dietary dispute by requiring proof, and the proof does not exist. The audience's rules rested on the claim that God had forbidden certain things, and the sūrah has examined the claim from every angle: the categories of the created animals do not produce it, the revelation does not contain it, and no witness can testify to it. What the sentence adds is the instruction to the Prophet about how to conduct himself while the claim is made.

The Book's statements about the actual prohibition are given in the verses that follow, where the Prophet is told to recite what God has forbidden: the association of others with Him, the failure to honour parents, the killing of children, indecency, the taking of life, the property of orphans, dishonest measure, and the breaking of covenants (6:151–152). The passage states the law that the audience's invented rules had obscured, and the verse in front of us clears the ground for it.

**What the Verse Says About the Prophet's Standing**

The instruction places the Prophet outside the contest over the audience's claims. He is not to be a party to a false testimony, and he is not to adopt the desires of the people who made the claim. What he is to do is stated in the verses that follow, and the arrangement leaves him free to deliver the law without being drawn into the dispute about the audience's rules.

The Book's treatment of the Prophet's position in disputes of this kind is consistent. The sūrah has told him to follow what has been revealed (6:106) and to declare that God's argument is conclusive (6:149), and the sentence in front of us states what he is not to follow. The reader is left with the contrast between the desires of the claimants and the revelation that has been given.

**The Transition to the Next Verses**

The next verses record the recitation of what God has actually forbidden, beginning with the association of partners with Him and continuing through the commands about parents, children, indecency, and the taking of life (6:151), and then the commands about the property of orphans, honest measure, just speech, and the fulfilment of covenants (6:152). The recitation ends with the declaration that this is God's path and that its followers are commanded to hold to it (6:153).

The passage is the sūrah's positive statement of the law, and it stands after the long examination of the audience's invented rules. The verse in front of us supplies the transition, and the demand for witnesses that it makes is the point at which the examination ends and the recitation begins.

## Sūrah al-Shuʿarāʾ 26:65

> and delivered Moses and those with him all together.

**Expanded Commentary**

**Deliverance Includes the Whole Community**

God delivers Moses and those with him all together. The phrase refuses a narrow picture of salvation in which only the prophet reaches safety while the ordinary people are left behind. Moses is named because he is the messenger and leader, but the act of deliverance embraces the community that followed his command. Divine care reaches the many through the mission entrusted to one servant.

The words all together also emphasize completeness. The Israelites do not escape in a partial or uncertain way, with some families saved and others lost to the pursuit. The deliverance is communal. It creates a people who have crossed from Pharaoh's reach into a new condition under God's protection. Their freedom is not merely Moses' private vindication. It is the rescue of those whose lives were bound to the oppressive order.

**The Prophet's Safety Is Joined to the People's Safety**

Moses is not delivered into a separate privilege. His fate is narrated alongside the fate of those with him. This reflects the nature of prophetic leadership. A messenger's success is not measured by personal survival while the people remain vulnerable. The prophet guides, warns, and moves with the community, and God's rescue confirms the message by protecting those who trust it.

This does not erase differences among the people. Some may have had stronger faith, greater fear, or different responsibilities. The verse gathers them under one act because the crisis was communal and the command was communal. Salvation creates solidarity. Those who crossed together become witnesses that God's promise can include an entire oppressed people.

**All Together Counters Pharaoh's Fragmentation**

Pharaoh's system had classified the Israelites by labor, status, and usefulness. It could divide families and reduce people to separate units of production. God's deliverance gathers them as one body. They are no longer counted according to the categories imposed by the palace. They stand together as servants whom God has rescued.

The phrase also counters the logic that only the strong or useful deserve escape. A community is not saved by selecting its most impressive members. The elderly, children, workers, and those who cannot fight belong to the deliverance. A just liberation refuses to leave the vulnerable as the price of speed. God's act protects the people as a people.

**Deliverance Is a Gift Received Through Obedience**

The Israelites did not create the sea path. They did not defeat Pharaoh's host by their own strength. Yet they had to leave, follow Moses, and cross the route opened by God. The gift does not make their response irrelevant. It makes their response possible and meaningful.

This balance prevents both pride and passivity. The people cannot boast that they rescued themselves, but they also cannot say that obedience was unnecessary. Faith moves when God opens the way. The phrase all together includes the community's shared movement through the danger, not a rescue that happened without their participation.

**The Earlier Warning Becomes Visible Fulfillment**

God had told Moses that He would be with him and guide him. The deliverance is the visible fulfillment of that assurance. Moses' confidence at the shore was not an empty speech. The Lord who accompanied him in Pharaoh's court has now accompanied him through the sea. The people witness the continuity between promise and act.

A parallel account in 2:50 recalls the sea being parted, Israel being saved, and Pharaoh's people drowning before the witnesses. That cross-reference confirms the public and communal character of this deliverance. It is not a private vision known only to Moses. It is an event through which a people learns what God's protection means.

**The Word Delivered Leaves No Room for Partial Victory**

The verse does not say that Moses and some of his companions escaped. It says God delivered them all together. The wording closes the possibility that Pharaoh's pursuit achieved a partial recovery. The ruler's claim over Israel has failed in full. The community is no longer in the hands of the host that chased it.

This completeness also gives the following judgment its proper setting. The Egyptians are not drowning while the Israelites remain mixed with them. The rescued people have been brought out. Divine justice does not require leaving the oppressed in the same danger in order to punish the oppressor. God separates the two groups and establishes the difference between them.

**A Community Saved Together Must Remember Together**

The memory of deliverance belongs to the whole people. No single survivor can treat it as a private achievement. They crossed because they were led, and they were delivered as a body. Shared rescue creates a shared duty to resist the return of the values that made Pharaoh's rule possible.

The verse therefore gives more than a happy ending to a chase. It establishes the community's identity after oppression. They are the people whom God delivered together. Their future must be shaped by gratitude, justice, and obedience rather than by the fear and fragmentation imposed by the tyrant.

The phrase all together also means that no rescued person is treated as an unnecessary remainder. The community's weakest members share in the deliverance, and the community's memory must preserve that fact. Freedom is not complete when only the strong can reach it.

The wording also distinguishes divine deliverance from a private escape by a heroic individual. Moses is saved with the people, and the people are saved with Moses. Leadership and community remain joined. The messenger's honor lies in guiding others to safety, while the community's honor lies in trusting the guidance enough to move together. No one can claim the crossing as a private possession.

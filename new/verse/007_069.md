## Sūrah al-Aʿrāf 7:69

> Do you find it astonishing that a reminder should come to you from your Lord through one of your own so he may warn you? Remember that He made you successors after the people of Noah and increased you greatly in stature. So remember Allah’s favours, so you may be successful.”

**Expanded Commentary**

**The Astonishment Repeated**

The verse opens with the same question that the account of Noah used, asking the people whether they find it astonishing that a reminder should come to them from their Lord through one of their own. The Book reports the astonishment of the deniers in other passages as well, and the sūrah of Qāf states that they are astonished that a warner has come to them from among themselves (50:2), while the sūrah of Ṣād reports the same reaction (38:4). The verse in front of us repeats the appeal that the earlier messenger made, and the repetition carries the series forward.

The question is followed by its purpose clause, that the messenger may warn the people, and the warning is the reminder that the Book attaches to the sending of every prophet. The sūrah's account of Noah used the same form with the mention of the mercy that may follow (7:63), and the verse in front of us substitutes the reminder of the people's own history. The two verses together state the offer and the evidence in the same breath.

**Successors After the People of Noah**

The verse reminds the people that God made them successors after the people of Noah, and the term for succession is the one the sūrah uses in its later account of Thamūd, where the same people are told that they were made successors after ʿĀd (7:74). The Book's use of the term for the peoples that replaced the destroyed communities appears throughout its narratives, and the sūrah's opening section stated that God had placed the audience as successors on the earth (6:165). The verse in front of us applies the description to the people of ʿĀd.

The reminder serves the argument that the sūrah is making. The people of ʿĀd had taken the place of the community that was destroyed for its denial, and the messenger's words in front of us state that the same arrangement has placed them where they stand. The Book's later accounts use the same reminder with the peoples that followed, and the series of the replacements is one of the sūrah's recurring themes.

**The Increase in Stature**

The verse reminds the people of their increase in stature, and the Book attaches the same feature to the people of ʿĀd in its other accounts. The sūrah of the Dawn describes Iram of the pillars as a city whose like was not created in the lands (89:6–7), and the sūrah of Fussilat reports their boasting about their might (41:15). The verse in front of us states the physical advantage as one of the favours for which the people were to be grateful.

The description of the people in the tradition includes their height and the strength of their buildings, and the sūrah's later account of Thamūd mentions the palaces they built on the plains and the homes they carved in the mountains (7:74). The Book's narratives use the achievements of the peoples as evidence against them, since the same strength that allowed the building was the thing they trusted instead of their Lord. This verse states the gift and the reminder together.

**The Command to Remember the Favours**

The verse closes with the command to remember the favours of God so that the people may succeed, and the Book attaches the command to the same contexts in its narratives. The sūrah's later account of Thamūd repeats the command after naming the palaces and the carved homes (7:74), and the sūrah of the Sand Dunes reports the messenger reminding the people of the favours that had been given to them. The verse here states the command in the mouth of Hūd, and the argument then proceeds to the leaders' challenge.

The success named at the end of the verse is the same outcome that the sūrah's opening verses attached to the weighing of deeds (7:8), and the connection places the reminder within the framework the chapter has established. The people are told that the recognition of the favours is the route to the success they seek, and the narrative that follows shows what they chose instead.

**The Favours of ʿĀd in the Book's Wider Account**

The Book gives a fuller account of the position of the people of ʿĀd in the sūrah of the Sand Dunes, where the messenger is reported to have warned a people dwelling in the sand hills and to have reminded them that God had established them in the land as He had not established others, giving them hearing, sight, and hearts (46:26). The passage states the extent of what they had received and then states that their faculties did not profit them when they denied the signs of God. The present verse supplies the shorter form of the same reminder in this sūrah.

The Book's treatment of the peoples that were given power and then destroyed is one of the sūrah's arguments for the audience's own situation. The Makkan community was proud of its standing among the tribes and its control of the sanctuary, and the reminder in front of us states what became of a people whose standing was greater in the things the audience valued. The sūrah's next verses state the demand that the leaders made of the messenger and the end of the community.

**Where the Verse Sits**

The verse stands sixty-ninth in the chapter, and it follows the statement of the messenger's work and precedes the challenge of the leaders. The verses that follow report the demand for the punishment to be brought and the messenger's answer, and the account of ʿĀd closes with the destruction of the people (7:70–72). The verse under discussion supplies the reminder that the account of the second people contains.

The reader who has heard the messenger describe his work now reads the reminder with which he addressed the community. The people are successors of a destroyed community, they have been increased in stature, and they are commanded to remember what they were given; and the sūrah's next verses state the answer that the leaders returned.

**The Transition to the Next Verse**

The next verse reports that the people asked whether the messenger had come to make them worship God alone and abandon what their forefathers worshipped, and they demanded that he bring upon them what he threatened if he was truthful (7:70). The reminder of the favours in front of us is followed by the challenge of the leaders, and the account then turns to the messenger's answer and the end of the people.

The two verses together state the exchange that the sūrah's narratives repeat. The messenger reminds the people of what they have been given, and the leaders answer with the appeal to their ancestors and with the demand for proof; and the verses that follow carry the second account to its conclusion.

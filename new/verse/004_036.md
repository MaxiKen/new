## Sūrah al-Nisāʾ 4:36

> Worship Allah ˹alone˺ and associate none with Him. And be kind to parents, relatives, orphans, the poor, near and distant neighbours, close friends, ˹needy˺ travellers, and those ˹bondspeople˺ in your possession. Surely Allah does not like whoever is arrogant, boastful—

### Expanded Commentary

**Worship and social conduct are one command**

The verse lists worship of Allah, kindness to parents, relatives, orphans, the needy, neighbours near and far, companions, travellers, and those under one’s authority. The sequence refutes the idea that devotion can be measured only by private ritual. Prayer and zakat stand beside family care, neighbourly responsibility, hospitality, and humane treatment of dependants because the same God is worshipped in all of them.

**The circles of obligation**

Parents and relatives have claims strengthened by kinship, but the list widens beyond family. The “near” neighbour may be close in residence, relation, or faith; the “far” neighbour may lack all those forms of proximity. A Muslim cannot use good treatment of his own group as a reason for indifference to outsiders. The companion at one’s side includes the spouse, colleague, or travelling partner whose rights arise from shared presence.

The traveller is vulnerable because he is separated from familiar support. The person under one’s authority is vulnerable because the power relation can make refusal difficult. The verse therefore places proximity and power under a common rule: give people their due and do not turn dependence into an opportunity for contempt.

**The arrogance that destroys good deeds**

The verse condemns those who are arrogant and boastful. Boasting can accompany charity, office, ancestry, religious practice, or wealth. A person may perform the outward act while using it to announce superiority over the one helped. Arrogance corrupts the social meaning of the deed because it turns service into self-worship.

The list is practical theology. Before asking whether worship has produced an elevated spiritual feeling, ask whether parents are safe, relatives are supported, neighbours are not harmed, guests are treated well, and workers or dependants are respected. The command begins with Allah and returns to Him through the people placed near us.

**The neighbour is a test of worship**

A person may know the rules of ritual purity while making the neighbour’s home unsafe, or give charity while humiliating a worker. The verse refuses that compartmentalization. Worship becomes credible when it improves the relationships in which a person has daily power and proximity, including relationships with people outside his own religious community.

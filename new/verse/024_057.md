## Sūrah an-Nūr 24:57

> Do not think ˹O Prophet˺ that the disbelievers can escape in the land. The Fire will be their home. Indeed, what an evil destination!

**Expanded Commentary**

**Dispelling the Illusion of Impunity: lā taḥsabanna alladhīna kafarū**

The divine speech addresses the Prophet ﷺ and every reflective reader with an emphatic prohibition: *lā taḥsabanna alladhīna kafarū muʿjizīna fī al-arḍ*, do not think that the disbelievers can escape in the land. The energetic construction (*taḥsabanna* with the doubled *nūn*) conveys intense emotional urgency and categorical certainty.

During the prolonged years of Makkan persecution and early Madinan warfare, the arrogant elites of Quraysh and their allied confederacies appeared formidable, wealthy, and seemingly invincible. They committed torture, assassinated vulnerable believers, launched unprovoked invasions, and openly mocked prophetic warnings without suffering immediate cosmic retribution. To the frail human eye, it often appears that ruthless oppressors flourish, expand their empires, and evade accountability with complete impunity. Verse 57 shatters this superficial perspective, assuring the faithful that the apparent delay in divine justice is not divine impotence or indifference, but a measured respite before an inescapable reckoning that will vindicate the righteous.

**The Delusion of Earthly Escape: muʿjizīna fī al-arḍ**

The term *muʿjizīn* derives from *aʿjaza*, meaning to frustrate, outmaneuver, escape from, or render powerless. The verse forcefully rejects the idea that disbelieving oppressors can ever incapacitate divine power or slip beyond the reach of divine justice.

A criminal on earth might flee across borders into a foreign jurisdiction, hide in subterranean bunkers, or use immense fortunes to buy political asylum, escaping the reach of human police, national armies, and international courts. However, the entire planet—from its deepest geological strata to its highest atmospheric layers—is the unpartitioned dominion of God (*lillāhi mā fī al-samāwāti wa-mā fī al-arḍ*). There are no neutral zones, foreign territories, or sovereign sanctuaries outside God's jurisdiction where a rebel can hide from divine retribution. Wherever the tyrant walks, they are walking on God's earth, inhaling God's air, and surrounded by divine surveillance; their fancied escape is a pathetic delusion that will collapse in an instant before divine power, leaving them completely vulnerable.

**Ontological Boundaries of the Cosmos: No Sanctuary for Tyranny**

The phrase *fī al-arḍ* (in the land / on the earth) establishes an unyielding ontological boundary. The oppressor may possess modern cavalry, advanced weaponry, fortified castles, and vast intelligence networks, but these instruments function only within finite physical parameters established and sustained by God.

The very physical forces that allow a tyrant's heart to beat, blood to circulate, and breath to flow are sustained moment by moment by the Creator they defy. If God commands a single blood vessel in the tyrant's brain to rupture, or an invisible virus to enter their lungs, their majestic empire dissolves into helplessness in a heartbeat. History is littered with the corpses of arrogant despots—Pharaoh, Nimrod, Abū Jahl, and Caesar—who imagined they could frustrate divine decree, only to find themselves utterly crushed by the simplest instruments of nature. Earth offers no sanctuary against the Lord of the earth, and no fortress can withstand His sovereign command.

**The Inevitable Abode: wa-maʾwāhumu al-nār**

The verse pivots from earthly frustration to eschatological destiny: *wa-maʾwāhumu al-nār*, the Fire will be their home. The noun *maʾwā* carries devastating semantic irony; it denotes a sanctuary, home, shelter, or refuge where an exhausted traveler seeks rest, comfort, and protection from harm.

For the disbelievers who spent their earthly lives building luxurious villas, fortified estates, and palatial mansions, their final, permanent "shelter" will be the scorching flames of Hell (*al-nār*). Instead of cool marble halls, soothing fountains, and silk cushions, they will be enveloped in blazing heat, drinking scalding fluids and wrapped in garments of pitch. The shelter they worked so hard to secure against divine accountability becomes a furnace of retribution, matching the cruelty, arrogance, and coldness they exhibited toward the vulnerable during their mortal existence, leaving them without any friend, defender, or helper across the vast corridors of eternity.

**The Tragic Destination: wa-la-biʾsa al-maṣīr**

The verse culminates in an emphatic exclamatory condemnation: *wa-la-biʾsa al-maṣīr*, indeed, what an evil destination! The particle *la* introduces intense corroboration, while the verb *biʾsa* expresses absolute revulsion, wretchedness, and disgust.

The noun *maṣīr*—which appeared earlier in verse 42 (*wa-ilā Allāhi al-maṣīr*)—signifies the ultimate terminus of a journey. Every human journey must terminate somewhere; the believer arrives at the garden of eternal delight, basking in divine pleasure, while the arrogant oppressor terminates their journey in the abyssal torment of the Fire. To spend an entire lifetime pursuing wealth, status, and power, only to arrive at an eternity of agony and disgrace, is the supreme, unmitigated tragedy of wasted human existence. The divine exclamation serves as a sobering mirror, urging every thinking soul to examine the destination toward which their daily choices are hurtling, warning them before the gates of repentance close forever and judgment falls.

**Solace for the Persecuted and Vindication of Moral Order**

For the early Muslim community enduring battlefield wounds, economic hardship, and the loss of loved ones, verse 57 provided profound psychological solace and emotional fortitude. When innocent blood is shed by powerful oppressors, the human spirit cries out for cosmic justice.

If tyrants could commit atrocities, die peacefully in luxurious beds, and escape accountability forever, the universe would be an absurd, morally bankrupt torture chamber devoid of meaning. Verse 57 restores moral order to the universe. It guarantees that no crime is forgotten, no cruelty goes unpunished, and no oppressor slips away into nothingness. The righteous can endure temporary suffering with serenity and unyielding dignity, knowing that ultimate justice is woven into the very fabric of existence, and that the final word belongs to the All-Just Judge whose decree none can avert and whose judgment rectifies all wrongs.

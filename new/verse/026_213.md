## Sūrah al-Shuʿarāʾ 26:213

> So do not ever call upon any other god besides Allah, or you will be one of the punished.

**Expanded Commentary**

**Exclusive Invocation Is the Foundation**

The command returns the Prophet and the audience to the first duty: call upon Allah alone. The wording is direct because the issue is not a small error within worship. Invoking another god gives a created thing a place that belongs only to the Creator. It divides dependence, fear, hope, and prayer when all of them should return to Allah.

The command comes after the Quran's source and protection have been established. The message is not delivered by devils, and the Prophet is not permitted to turn the audience toward any rival power. Revelation has a single Lord, so worship must have a single Lord as well. The unity of the source demands the unity of the call.

**The Address to the Prophet Teaches the Community**

The verse is phrased in the second person singular. Exegetes understand it as an address to the Prophet and also as instruction for him to convey this command to others. The form teaches that even the most honored messenger remains a servant under Allah's authority. His closeness to Allah does not make him independent of worship or accountability.

A command addressed to the Prophet also reaches the community through him. If the messenger is warned against invoking another god, no follower can claim that religious rank makes a person safe from idolatry. The warning is not an accusation against the Prophet; it is a clear statement about the boundary of servanthood. Worship is never transferred to the messenger who teaches it.

**Punishment Follows the False Direction of Worship**

The verse connects calling upon another god with being among the punished. That consequence is not attached to an innocent difference in vocabulary. It concerns directing the deepest act of dependence away from Allah. A person who calls on a created power as though it controls benefit, harm, forgiveness, or destiny has made a claim about the world that revelation rejects.

Quran 6:19 presents Allah as the witness between the Prophet and his people and the Quran as a warning that reaches whoever it reaches. Quran 10:41 gives the Prophet a way to distinguish his deeds from those of people who reject him. These passages reinforce the same order: Allah alone bears ultimate authority, while human beings remain answerable for how they respond.

**The Center of Worship Protects Every Other Duty**

Exclusive invocation is not a narrow ritual rule detached from conduct. If Allah alone is the One called upon, then wealth, leaders, ancestors, and public approval cannot become absolute masters. A person may respect people, seek lawful help, and use ordinary means, but he does not treat any created means as a god with independent power.

This center protects the believer from fear that makes injustice seem necessary. It protects the poor from rulers who demand the obedience owed to Allah, and it protects the messenger from being turned into an object of worship. The verse places every hope and fear under the Lord who hears, knows, and judges. To call upon Allah alone is to live without giving creation the final word.

Exclusive invocation does not mean refusing every created means. A person may ask a doctor for treatment, a neighbor for help, or a judge for justice, while knowing that none of them owns the result independently of Allah. The prohibited act is not ordinary cooperation. It is giving a creature the kind of dependence, fear, or hope that belongs to the Lord alone.

This distinction keeps monotheism practical. The merchant can work without treating profit as his god. The sick person can seek medicine without believing that medicine controls life by itself. The oppressed can seek lawful protection without worshiping the official who provides it. Means are used, but the heart remains turned toward Allah, who can make a means effective or remove its effect.

The Prophet's position is especially protected by this verse. People may love him, follow his example, and ask him to teach them, but they may not turn him into a partner of Allah. The messenger directs worship away from himself. If a religious leader invites people to depend on his unseen power or to obey him against Allah's command, the verse supplies a clear boundary.

Calling upon Allah alone also changes the language of crisis. Instead of treating hardship as proof that a rival power has defeated the believer, the servant asks what Allah is teaching, what lawful action is required, and where patience is needed. The heart can be afraid without becoming enslaved to fear. It can hope for help without assigning divine control to any created thing.

The punishment named in the verse is therefore connected to the center from which all conduct flows. False invocation is not a harmless addition to an otherwise sound life. It gives the creature a final authority that will eventually displace justice, gratitude, and obedience. Tawhid restores the order: Allah is called upon, people are respected, and every created power remains under His judgment.

The command also protects the believer from making a human relationship the condition of acceptance. A person may be grateful to a teacher, parent, or leader, but none of them can hear every hidden need or judge every deed. When the heart calls upon Allah alone, respect for people remains within its proper limits and does not become servitude to their approval.

The verse is thus a command about freedom as well as worship. Whoever refuses to give creation divine authority can face powerful people without treating them as ultimate. He can ask for help without surrendering conscience, and he can accept a means without forgetting the Lord who created the means. Exclusive invocation gives the servant a single direction and keeps every other loyalty accountable to it.

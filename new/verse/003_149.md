## Sūrah Āli ʿImrān 3:149

> O believers! If you yield to the disbelievers, they will drag you back to disbelief—and you will become losers.

**Expanded Commentary**

**The Address and Its Warning**

The verse addresses the believers directly and warns them against obeying those who deny, describing the consequence in physical terms: they would be turned back on their heels and would end up as losers. The warning is stated as a certainty in the form the Book uses for imminent dangers.

The community had come through a period in which advice was plentiful and its sources were mixed. The sūrah has already described a party among the People of the Book who wished to mislead the believers, and it has described the conduct of the hypocrites during the fighting. The verse gathers such advice into a single category and addresses the community's habit of listening to it.

**Who the Warning Concerns**

Al-Ṭabarī recorded that the phrase about those who deny was understood to refer to the enemies of the believers among the various groups of the time: the People of the Book, the hypocrites, and the idolaters. The community was surrounded by people who had reasons of their own for wanting it to change course.

The breadth of the reference is part of the verse's value. The Book is not warning against a single party or a single occasion; it is warning against a habit of mind in which the community takes its direction from those whose interests are opposed to its own. The Book's later passages make the same point about the company a person keeps and the counsel he acts on.

**Obedience and the Loss of Standing**

The obedience named in the verse is not restricted to formal submission. It covers the practical response of a community that follows advice and adopts a course because it has been recommended by people who do not share its commitments.

The Book had already warned the community about such counsel in the earlier passage about the People of the Book, where it described a group who wished the believers would abandon the way they had received. The warning in this verse sharpens the point: the danger is not only that bad advice might be followed once, but that following it repeatedly would move the community back to where it started.

**Turned Back on the Heels**

The image of turning back on the heels is the same one the sūrah used a few verses earlier when it asked whether the community would abandon its religion if the Prophet died. Al-Ṭabarī explained it here as a return to disbelief after faith.

The Book's use of a single image in both places links the two dangers. The possibility of apostasy was raised first as a response to the death of a leader, and it is raised again as a response to the influence of outsiders. Both are treated as the same movement away from the ground on which the community stands, and the second is presented as the more likely of the two in ordinary circumstances.

**The Word for Losing**

The last word of the verse describes people who are lost or ruined, and the Book uses the root for both meanings: to be destroyed and to lose what one has. The two senses run together in a single condition, since the person who abandons the truth loses both his standing with God and what he had built in the world.

The Book uses the word in its descriptions of the people who will regret their conduct on the Day of Judgment, and it uses it in this life as well for those whose efforts come to nothing. The verse places the community under the second use: a community that reverses its course loses the position it was given.

**The Counsel Behind the Warning**

The warning serves the sūrah's larger purpose, which has been the unity and steadiness of the community since the passage about holding fast to God's rope together. A community pulled in several directions by outside advice cannot act with the coherence the mission requires.

The Book's instructions on this point are not presented as a rejection of dealing with people of other faiths; the sūrah contains passages that treat such dealings with fairness and forbearance. The warning concerns direction and authority: the community is to take its course from its own revelation, and the counsel of those who oppose its purpose is not a legitimate source for that course.

**The Continuity With the Previous Verses**

The image of the heels links this verse to the earlier warning about the Prophet's death, and the theme of obedience links it to the community's failure at Uḥud. The verse before the reader draws those threads together.

The sūrah's account of the battle has shown what happens when a community departs from the instructions it has been given. The warning in this verse generalises the lesson by naming the source of such departures as the counsel of those who deny. The community is told, in effect, that the danger it faces after the battle is the same as the danger it faced during it, in a different form.

**What the Verse Asks of the Community**

The verse asks the believers to be careful about whose direction they follow, and it does so by describing where a particular path leads. The command is not stated as a prohibition on speaking with outsiders, nor on hearing what they have to say.

What the community is told is that the point of decision is not the sound of the advice but the identity of the source. The Book's later instruction on consultation inside the community, and its insistence that decisions rest with the believers and their messenger, are the positive form of this warning. The verse before the reader states the negative form, and the sentence that follows gives the community its alternative in a single line.

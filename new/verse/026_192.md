## Sūrah al-Shuʿarāʾ 26:192

> This is certainly a revelation from the Lord of all worlds,

**Expanded Commentary**

**Revelation Has a Lord**

The pronoun this points to the message being delivered through the Prophet, not to a human speech act that belongs to its speaker. The verse identifies the Quran as revelation and then identifies its source: the Lord of all worlds. Its authority does not come from the eloquence of the messenger, the approval of a tribe, or the ability of an audience to explain every part of it. It comes from Allah, who owns and governs every realm.

Calling Allah Lord gives the statement more force than simply calling Him the maker of the book. A lord sustains, directs, and judges. The One who sends the revelation is also the One who knows the conditions of the people who receive it and the consequences of obeying or rejecting it. Guidance is therefore not detached information. It is a communication from the Lord who has a claim over the life of every hearer.

**The Source Is Universal While the Audience Is Local**

The title Lord of all worlds prevents the Quran from being reduced to a private message for one market or one historical group. It came through a particular messenger and in a particular human language, but its source is not local. The community first hearing it was one audience within a creation that Allah already governed in full.

This universal source also explains why the message can address trade, speech, worship, fear, and public power in one connected account. The Lord of all worlds is Lord of the heart and the marketplace alike. No part of human life falls outside the authority of the One who created its setting and knows its hidden effects.

**Revelation Is Received, Not Invented**

The verse answers the suspicion that a prophet might be composing a moral program for personal influence. A messenger can be the human recipient and reciter without being the author of what he conveys. Quran 17:106 describes the Quran as revealed in stages, which fits a message delivered through time, circumstance, warning, and response rather than a work produced at one sitting by a human planner.

Staged revelation does not make the message less divine. It shows wisdom in the manner of delivery. Guidance meets people as their questions arise and as their conduct is exposed. The Lord of all worlds is not limited by the pace of a human audience; He teaches in the measure that serves His purpose.

**The Claim Changes How the Book Is Heard**

If the Quran is revelation from the Lord of all worlds, the hearer must do more than admire its language or collect arguments from it. He must ask what the Lord is commanding, correcting, and promising. The verse moves the listener from literary judgment to moral responsibility without denying the beauty of the text.

It also offers a stable center when the audience changes. A generation may praise the message, mock it, or forget it, but its source does not change with public opinion. The Forest people rejected Shuʿayb, yet Allah remained Lord. The Prophet's opponents could deny the revelation, but denial could not transfer ownership of truth to them. This opening claim gives every later warning its authority and every sincere response its proper direction.

The phrase Lord of all worlds also refuses the idea that revelation belongs to a narrow religious class. The Lord who sends it governs people who already believe, people who doubt, and people who have never heard its message clearly. The Quran can therefore call a particular community to account without becoming a tribal possession. Its local address serves a universal Lord.

The source of revelation matters when the text speaks about matters human beings cannot settle by preference alone. People can debate the best way to organize a market, but they cannot make theft just by agreeing to it. They can praise power, but they cannot make oppression safe by calling it tradition. A message from the Lord of all worlds supplies the standard above those agreements.

It also gives hope to the person who feels small before a corrupt order. The same Lord who rules every world is not dependent on the approval of the powerful people who reject His command. A messenger may be opposed, a believer may be outnumbered, and justice may seem delayed, but the source of revelation remains greater than the society judging it.

The verse is thus an opening of authority and care. Authority, because the Quran comes from the Lord whose right cannot be voted away. Care, because the Lord does not leave creation without direction. The proper response is neither blind admiration nor casual dismissal. Hear the revelation as a communication from the One who made the worlds, sustains them, and will judge what each community does with the guidance it receives.

That hearing includes the demand to let the revelation correct inherited practice. A community cannot claim that its market, family structure, or public customs are protected from divine judgment simply because they are old. The Lord of all worlds is Lord over history as well as nature. His revelation can praise what is sound, expose what is corrupt, and call people to repair what they have made unjust.

The universal source also keeps the message from being reduced to a private comfort. It consoles the oppressed, but it also commands the powerful. It gives hope to the person whose rights were diminished and warning to the person who diminished them. Both stand before the same Lord, whose care does not become partial because one person has more influence. His authority reaches every hidden bargain and every public decision.

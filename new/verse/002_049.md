## Sūrah al-Baqarah 2:49

> ˹Remember˺ how We delivered you from the people of Pharaoh, who afflicted you with dreadful torment, slaughtering your sons and keeping your women. That was a severe test from your Lord.

**Expanded Commentary**

**Deliverance Recalled as Debt of Gratitude**

“Remember how We delivered you from the people of Pharaoh, who afflicted you with dreadful torment, slaughtering your sons and keeping your women. That was a severe test from your Lord” (2:49). Wa idh najjaynākum min āli Firʿawn — the “when” (idh) is a “remember when,” as the source notes, linking back to “Remember My favour” (2:47). The sūrah begins its historical recital not with abstract doctrine but with a remembered rescue: from āl Firʿawn — the people, house, regime of Pharaoh — whose ʿadhāb was dreadful and particular. The verse names the torment bluntly before naming the sea or the calf or the book, so that later ingratitude is measured against a concrete severity survived.

**“People of Pharaoh” — A House That Meant a Regime**

Āl means house, family, entourage. Pharaoh himself — “great house” — stands for a system that organized violence. The Qur’an does not pathologize a single tyrant alone; it names a people who inflicted — yasūmūnakum (continually afflicting), yudhabbiḥūna, yastaḥyūna. The verbs are iterative, marking habit. The torment was not incident but policy: sons slaughtered, women kept alive — for servitude, for exploitation, for control.

**Why Sons and Women? — Control, Not Only Cruelty**

The source notes the Qur’an gives no motive here beyond ʿadhāb and balaʾ, and that later Pharaoh repeats the threat when Moses is adult (7:127; 40:25), suggesting ongoing collective punishment and population control (cf. Exodus 1) rather than a one-time soothsayer plot. Commentaries that cite a dream or astrologers warning that an Israelite child would topple Pharaoh’s kingdom (Ṭ, R, IK, and Exodus parallels) are tafsīr expansions. They should be rated UNVERIFIED when lacking a Sahih prophetic chain. What is firm is the pattern: to kill the male line is to kill future, to keep women is to keep utility. Oppression genders its violence to dominate increase itself.

Cross-reference 28:4 shows the same policy stated as governance: Pharaoh exalted himself, divided people into parties, oppressed a group, slaying sons and sparing women. The method is not random sadism but strategy — diminish, divide, and use.

**“That Was a Severe Test from Your Lord” — Trial Within Torment, Blessing Within Trial**

Wa fī dhālikum balāʾun min Rabbikum ʿaẓīm (2:49). Balāʾ is testing, uncovering, proving. The source notes that balaʾ can point to the suffering — the torment and fear — and, by some, to niʿmah — the deliverance itself as in 2:47 (Ṭ). Both senses live in Qur’anic usage: “We try you with evil and with good as a test” (21:35), “We tried them with good things and evil things” (7:168). Deliverance is also trial: will the delivered remember and give thanks, or will they worship a calf when the deliverer is out of sight (2:51)?

The phrase “from your Lord” (min Rabbikum) keeps the trial within lordship: the Tester is also the Deliverer. The sūrah therefore holds two truths without collapse: Pharaoh’s people afflicted, and the Lord tested — the former accountable for injustice, the latter sovereign over meaning. To call it “severe” or “great” (ʿaẓīm) signals weight: this memory should have secured gratitude for generations.

**Why This Memory Matters Before the Sea**

The sea parting (2:50) will be remembered next, but this verse first names the condition the sea ended. Without the house of Pharaoh’s dread, the sea is spectacle; with it, the sea is rescue. The sūrah’s order teaches how to remember: first the prolonged affliction that made deliverance necessary, then the sudden opening that made it visible, then the long night of testing that followed (2:51–53) and the repeated forgiveness that pursued failure (2:52–54).

**Living the Recital**

- To “remember” Pharaoh’s house is to remember systems, not only persons. Where does a house-like regime still slaughter futures and keep utilities? The verse trains social sight.
- To name balaʾ as both torment and deliverance checks two errors: to treat suffering as meaningless, and to treat relief as self-made. Both are tests from the Lord, and both call for the same response: “perhaps you will be grateful” (2:52) and “perhaps you will be guided” (2:53).
- To be delivered is to be indebted. The Children of Israel were not saved to be neutral; they were saved to be mindful (2:48) — and so is any people who finds its sons spared.

**What Not to Transmit as Fact**

Speculations about Pharaoh’s dream, the astrologers’ count, or the exact year’s decree should not be presented as prophetic narration. They are tafsīr reports to illustrate motive, to be marked UNVERIFIED. What is firm is the Qur’anic core: a people delivered from a House that slaughtered sons and kept women as policy, under dreadful torment, as a great test from the Lord (2:49), remembered to summon gratitude rather than entitlement.

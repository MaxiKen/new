## Sūrah al-Ḥāqqah 69:30

> ˹It will be said,˺ “Seize and shackle them,

**Expanded Commentary**

**Heaven Gives Orders**

Khudhūhu fa-ghullūh — "seize him, then shackle him." Plural imperatives addressed to Hell's wardens, the stern angels Qatādah and al-Ṭabarī identify as torment's officers. Command hierarchy displays fully: God orders, angels execute, the sentenced endures. The soul that once issued orders to subordinates now receives orders as their target. Reversal crowns verse 29's perished sulṭān — authority lost below, authority's chains fastened above. The wardens stand unnamed, numberless, irresistible; their obedience is instant because heaven's troops never hesitate, never question, never tire. Readers should hear the command's terrible certainty: once issued, it executes. No appeals process follows, no stay of execution, no reprieve. What five verses of plaints could not move, two imperatives now conclude. The lament's speaker becomes the sentence's object. Speech that begged for concealment receives exposure; wishes for void receive iron. Speak repentance now while speech moves mercy; never hear khudhūhu when speech moves nothing.

**Seizure Answers Evasion**

Khudhūhu — "take him, seize him, lay hold of him." Capture is certain: no flight possible, no hiding place left, no resistance conceivable. Pursuit ends here; evasion expires forever. Earthly fugitives sometimes escape — borders crossed under cover, identities changed, years outwaiting warrants. The audit's fugitive has nowhere to run: the cosmos lies leveled, the skies hang torn, angels stand ranked at every edge. Seizure is almost formality, since presence was already compelled at verse 18's universal presentation. Yet formality terrifies precisely as formality: taken like a common criminal, collar seized, dignity voided. Readers should surrender before seizure: come willingly through repentance and never be taken forcibly. What capture claims, surrender preempts. Fugitives from God arrive as prisoners in chains; children of God arrive as guests to feast. The difference is direction of travel, chosen daily. Run Godward now through obedience; never be taken hellward then through warrants. Seizure awaits evaders as appointment; embrace awaits returners as promise.

**Shackles for the Unbound**

Fa-ghullūh — "then fetter him, shackle him." Ghull denotes the shackle binding hands to neck, the yoke of captivity. Elsewhere the damned wear such shackles coursing through their bodies (36:8). Irony is perfect and deliberate: unbound by divine law on earth, bound in iron in fire. License enjoyed becomes bondage endured; freedom abused becomes freedom revoked. Fetters fit the crimes with tailored precision: hands that withheld charity get bound, hands that struck the weak get chained, hands that signed falsehood get yoked. Restraint refused voluntarily becomes restraint imposed penally. Readers should bind themselves while binding still profits: discipline appetites with fasting, chain tongues with silence, yoke habits to duties. What self-binding restrains now, hell-binding never claims then. Voluntary fetters — dawn vigils, hunger willingly borne, anger swallowed — preempt penal ones. The metal differs; the mercy differs infinitely. Bind yourself Godward through discipline; never be bound hellward through decree. Gilded self-control now, or iron imposed then.

**Plural Wardens, Singular Wretch**

Khudhūhu, ghullūh — both imperatives plural. Many wardens descend per wretch: overwhelming force deployed, zero resistance anticipated. Numbers display majesty: heaven assigns squads where one angel would suffice infinitely. No struggle gets staged because none is possible; seizure proceeds effortlessly, shackling without contest. Such asymmetry teaches what defiance always denied: rebellion never threatened heaven, and punishment never strains it. Angels many, wretch one — that is power's true ratio, hidden on earth, displayed on the Day. The mighty soul that fancied itself heaven's rival meets heaven's janitors and cannot lift a finger. Readers should abandon all resistance-fantasies now: none wrestle wardens, none slip shackles, none negotiate capture. Strength surrenders universally; only penitence prevailed, and only before death. What squads seize then, submission might have spared now. Yield singly and early; never be seized plurally and late. Wardens await orders patiently; orders await no repentance once issued.

**Speech Shifts From Earth to Heaven**

Verses 25-29 belonged to the damned: wishes wailed, holdings audited, power confessed gone. Verse 30 belongs to God: commands issued, wardens dispatched. Lament was heard in full; sentence is pronounced in brief. The courtroom turns decisively and finally: defendants rest their case, the Judge rules, proceedings close. Such shifting silences forever — no rebuttal invited, no surrebuttal scheduled, no closing argument permitted. Heaven's speech acts rather than argues: saying seizes, commanding shackles. Readers should note speech's terrible economy in this passage: human words get weighed, divine words execute. Speak repentance now while human speech still avails; heaven's answering speech will then welcome rather than warrant. What earthly voice wasted on excuses, heavenly voice answers with orders. The mouth that argued cleverly falls silent permanently. Talk Godward now through prayer and confession; never be talked-about then through "seize him." Speech shifts sides; fate seals with the shift.

**Fettered Hands That Withheld**

Hands get bound that bound charity. Manūʿ's fists from Sūrah 70 — clutching at good's touch, sealing vessels against the poor — arrive here for fettering. Poetic justice locks with an audible click: withholding hands become held hands, closed-to-poor becomes closed-by-iron. Fetters fit the crime the way keys fit locks: members testify through their binding. Bound hands confess withheld giving more eloquently than tongues ever denied it. The body that served avarice now wears avarice's wage. Readers should unbind hands while unbinding counts: open them to the poor and irons never close on them. What fists release in charity, freedom keeps in eternity. Giving unbinds doubly — the poor relieved of need, the giver relieved of fetters. Withhold through life and ghullūhu binds through eternity; give through life and hands stay free forever — free to clasp bliss, free to wave records joyfully. Choose the binding: voluntary generosity now, or involuntary iron then.

**The Sentence Continues Burning**

Seizure ordered, shackling commanded; scorching follows. Verse 31 will burn the bound soul — "then roast him in Hellfire." Stages mount deliberately: seized, shackled, scorched, then chained at length. Dread climbs a staircase, each step its own eternity. Readers should brace for flames: capture complete, binding finished, burning begins. Procedure advances without pause or pity. What follows stokes Hellfire for the fettered. The next verse orders roasting in the Blaze, the sentence proceeding from capture to flames.

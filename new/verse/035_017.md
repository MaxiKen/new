## Sūrah Fāṭir 35:17

> And that is not difficult for Allah ˹at all˺.

**Expanded Commentary**

**The Short Sentence Closes the Argument**

The verse is brief because the point needs no elaboration from the perspective of divine power. After mentioning the possibility of removing one creation and producing another, the Qur'an states that this is not difficult for Allah. Human beings measure tasks by time, labor, materials, and uncertainty. Allah is not limited by any of these created conditions. The short sentence cuts through the tendency to project creaturely difficulty onto the Creator.

Brevity also gives the statement force. No catalogue of mechanisms is offered, because the question is not how a replacement would be organized. The question is whether it lies within Allah's ability. The answer is complete: it does. A person may be unable to imagine the event without being entitled to deny it. Imagination is a human tool, not the measure of divine possibility.

**Divine Ease Does Not Mean Divine Neglect**

When the Qur'an calls an act easy for Allah, it does not describe a casual or meaningless act. Creation is governed by knowledge and wisdom even when it costs Allah nothing. A human craftsman may struggle over a work because his skill is limited; Allah's ease means that no effort, fatigue, or opposition impedes His command. The result remains purposeful, not careless.

This distinction protects the believer from two errors. One error is doubting a promise because its fulfillment seems difficult. The other is imagining that ease makes the created world insignificant. Allah can create without effort, yet He gives human life moral weight, allows consequences to unfold, and calls people to act responsibly. His ease is the ground of trust, while His wisdom is the ground of seriousness.

**The Sentence Removes the Idol of Necessity**

People often treat existing arrangements as necessary. They say that a social order cannot change, a dominant people cannot disappear, or a familiar world must continue because too many lives depend on it. This verse returns necessity to Allah alone. Created structures may be stable for a period, but they are not independent laws standing over the Creator. Allah can preserve, transform, replace, or end them.

The Qur'an states in 3:26 that Allah gives authority to whom He wills and removes it from whom He wills. Political power is one visible example of a wider rule. The present verse applies the same logic to creation itself. No community should confuse continuity with entitlement. No individual should regard his status as guaranteed by the fact that he currently possesses it.

**Human Difficulty Should Produce Reliance, Not Projection**

A servant faces tasks that feel impossible: changing a harmful habit, repairing a broken relationship, surviving injustice, or imagining life after loss. The verse does not say that these matters are easy for the servant. It says that Allah is not constrained by them. That difference creates a place for prayer. The believer brings the impossible-looking matter to the One whose power is not measured by the believer's resources.

Reliance does not cancel practical effort. The servant seeks counsel, takes lawful means, and accepts the limits of personal control. Yet he refuses to worship the difficulty. A mountain may be large to the traveler and small in relation to the earth; human obstacles may be overwhelming to a person and entirely within Allah's command. The verse turns fear away from the size of the problem and back toward the adequacy of the Lord.

The phrase also answers a hidden comparison between divine acts. People may accept that Allah created the first human beings but doubt that He can restore them, or accept that He can restore them but doubt that He can change the conditions of a whole people. The Qur'an treats both doubts as projections of human limitation. Creation in its first form and replacement after loss are equally within His command.

When a servant says that something is too difficult for Allah, he has made his own horizon the measure of the Lord. The correction is not a demand to understand every hidden method. It is an invitation to return to the names of Allah: Creator, Sustainer, Wise, and Powerful. Those names make room for trust where imagination reaches its limit.

This is why prayer remains meaningful when a situation appears closed. Prayer does not inform Allah of a difficulty He did not know, nor does it force Him to act without wisdom. It places the servant before the One whose ability is complete and whose choice is wise. The servant may ask for a change in the world, or for a change in the heart that meets the world with faith.

The servant's limitation is never a limitation on Allah's care or power.

**Ease Supports Both Warning and Hope**

The warning is that Allah can replace a people without difficulty. The hope is that He can also renew a heart, open a path, forgive a sin, and bring relief without difficulty. The same divine ease that makes removal possible makes restoration possible. A servant should not use the verse only to fear being replaced; he should also ask Allah to make him worthy of preservation through obedience.

The proper response is therefore humble urgency. Do not assume that your place is secure because others need you. Do not assume that your repentance is too difficult for Allah to accept. Work faithfully while your term remains, ask for guidance, and remember that no created situation binds the Lord who created it. The sentence is short, but it makes the whole horizon larger: Allah is capable of changing the inhabitants of the world, and no barrier in that change can be called difficult for Him.

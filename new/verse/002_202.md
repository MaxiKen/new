## Sūrah al-Baqarah 2:202

> It is they who will receive a ˹heavenly˺ reward for the good they have done. Surely Allah is swift in reckoning.

**Expanded Commentary**

**The Reward Stated**

The verse states that those described will receive a portion of what they have earned, and it closes with the statement that God is swift in reckoning. The sentence follows the two supplications and answers them with the account of what their petitioners obtain.

The community's scholars discussed the reference of the pronoun, and they explained that the sentence may describe the second group alone or both groups together. Under either reading the principle stated is the same, which is that each person receives according to what he has sought.

**Who Is Meant**

The commentators offered three readings of the pronoun's reference. Some applied it to the group that asked for the good of this world and the next, since their request was the comprehensive one. Others applied it to the first group, who asked only for the present life, on the ground that their portion in the world is granted. Others applied it to both, so that each group receives according to the scope of its request.

The variety reflects the Book's way of leaving a statement open to the applications it supports, and the community's teachers used the readings together when they explained the passage. What the reader receives is the principle that a person's share corresponds to what he has asked and worked for, and the sentence states the matter in the Book's own terms.

**The Meaning of a Portion**

The term rendered as portion describes what has been set aside and apportioned, and the community's scholars noted that it carries the sense of a measured share. The Book uses the same vocabulary in its treatment of the inheritance of the believers and in its account of what people earn.

The wording presents the reward as the fruit of the person's own action rather than as an arbitrary gift, and the sentence's use of the same verb that the earlier verses used for earning connects the two. **The Swift Reckoning**

The sentence closes with the statement that God is swift in reckoning, and the community's scholars explained the phrase as a statement about the ease and the immediacy with which the account is taken. The Book uses the same description in other passages about the final judgment.

The clause answers any doubt about whether the account will be settled, and the community's teachers noted that the swiftness concerns the divine act rather than the timing of the day. The reader carries away the assurance that nothing is delayed and nothing is lost, and leaves him with the knowledge that the reckoning is not a distant matter but one that the Book describes as near.

## Sūrah al-Tawbah 9:97

> The nomadic Arabs ˹around Medina˺ are far worse in disbelief and hypocrisy, and less likely to know the laws revealed by Allah to His Messenger. And Allah is All-Knowing, All-Wise.

**Expanded Commentary**

**Distance From Teaching Can Increase Vulnerability**

The verse describes nomadic Arabs as more severe in disbelief and hypocrisy and less likely to know the limits revealed to the Messenger. The setting matters. People living far from the city and from regular instruction may have less access to learning, communal worship, and correction. Distance can make ignorance easier to preserve.

The verse does not condemn a way of life or every person living outside the city. It identifies a condition that can increase vulnerability: separation from knowledge combined with resistance to guidance. Modern communities should learn the same lesson. People who lack access to teachers, books, transport, or trustworthy information need patient instruction, not contempt. Ignorance is addressed by teaching, while deliberate rejection remains morally distinct.

**Severity Is a Moral Description, Not a Tribal Insult**

The passage speaks about hypocrites among the nomads, not about ancestry as a fixed defect. A person's origin does not determine faith. The same Qur'an recognizes sincere nomadic believers and distinguishes them from those who treat giving as loss or wait for the community's misfortune.

This prevents the believer from turning a contextual warning into racism or class pride. City dwellers can be hypocritical and ignorant of Allah's limits, while a person far from public institutions can be deeply sincere. The measure is knowledge, opportunity, intention, and conduct. Allah's knowledge and wisdom ensure that no person is judged merely for geography.

**Knowing the Limits Protects the Heart**

The limits revealed to the Messenger include obligations and prohibitions, but they also include the boundaries of sound belief and proof. A community that does not know its limits can mistake impulse for revelation. It may call custom sacred, call appetite freedom, or call harshness courage.

Learning the limits gives the servant a map. It tells him what belongs to Allah's command, what remains a lawful choice, and what must be avoided even when a group approves it. The person who knows the map can repent when he crosses a boundary. The person who never learns may normalize the crossing and then defend it as identity.

**Community Access Is a Religious Responsibility**

The verse places a duty on those with knowledge to make guidance accessible. Teachers should travel, explain, translate, and answer people who have not had the same opportunities. Institutions should not treat distance as evidence of inferiority. A community that keeps knowledge in one center may create the very ignorance it later condemns.

The learner also has a duty to seek what he can. He should ask reliable people, distinguish evidence from rumor, and remain humble about what he does not know. Allah judges ability, so neither the teacher nor the learner should demand what circumstances make impossible. The aim is a living connection between revelation and conduct.

**Knowledge and Sincerity Must Meet**

Knowledge alone does not guarantee faith. A person can know the rules and still manipulate them. Sincerity alone does not excuse refusing to learn what Allah has made clear. The verse joins the problem of ignorance with the wider problem of hypocrisy. Both need repair: knowledge must be pursued, and the heart must submit to what it learns.

A believer should therefore ask two questions. Do I know what Allah requires in this matter? If I know it, am I willing to obey? The first question protects from careless action; the second protects from using knowledge as decoration. Allah is All-Knowing and All-Wise, so the servant trusts both the truth of the limit and the wisdom behind it.

**Wisdom Guides Judgment of People**

The closing names Allah's wisdom. Human beings see a group pattern but may not know every individual. They should teach, protect, and judge conduct fairly without claiming that geography has sealed a person's fate. Allah knows who lacked access, who rejected a clear lesson, and who sought guidance sincerely.

This wisdom encourages balanced reform. Build schools and relationships, correct false beliefs, and resist hypocrisy, but do not replace justice with contempt. The believer asks Allah to make his knowledge useful and his judgments restrained. Guidance can reach a person across distance, and Allah's wisdom is not limited by the routes human beings have built.

The remedy for this vulnerability is not to force every nomadic person into an urban pattern. It is to carry sound knowledge to people in forms they can use. Teachers should explain the purpose of limits, not only recite their names. Learners should be welcomed when they ask about worship, law, and belief. Leaders should judge the person who refuses after understanding differently from the person who has not yet had a fair chance to understand. Allah's wisdom makes room for instruction, gradual growth, and accountability together. The community becomes stronger when no region is treated as beyond guidance and no region is allowed to hide deliberate rejection behind distance.

The believer should also avoid using the verse to excuse poor teaching. If people do not know the limits, the response is to make the limits clear and accessible. Knowledge should include reasons, examples, and the difference between a firm command and a lawful area of choice. A person who learns only through rumor may repeat error without intending rebellion. A person who learns clearly and rejects the command is responsible in another way. Allah's wisdom accounts for both conditions. It calls the knowledgeable to teach and the learner to seek truth, while leaving the final assessment to the One who knows every opportunity and obstacle.

Distance calls for patient teaching, not a license for contempt or careless judgment.

## Sūrah al-Nisāʾ 4:43

> O believers! Do not approach prayer while intoxicated until you are aware of what you say, nor in a state of ˹full˺ impurity—unless you merely pass through ˹the mosque˺—until you have bathed. But if you are ill, on a journey, or have relieved yourselves, or been intimate with your wives and cannot find water, then purify yourselves with clean earth, wiping your faces and hands. And Allah is Ever-Pardoning, All-Forgiving.

### Expanded Commentary

**Prayer requires conscious presence**

The prohibition on approaching prayer while intoxicated until one knows what one is saying protects prayer as meaningful speech, not bodily performance. The verse belongs to the gradual Qur’anic prohibition of intoxicants. It names the immediate harm—loss of awareness in worship—while later revelation closes the practice more broadly. The principle extends to any condition that makes a person unable to understand, intend, or carry out the prayer responsibly.

**Purification and access to worship**

The verse also addresses major ritual impurity and the need for bathing, with an exception for someone merely passing through a mosque. Ritual purity is not a statement that the human body is morally filthy. It is a form of preparation that distinguishes sacred worship and teaches attentiveness. The law takes physical states seriously without confusing them with the worth of the person.

**Tayammum: concession without abandonment**

Illness, travel, the absence of water, and certain bodily circumstances open the route of *tayammum*: clean earth is used for the face and hands. The concession shows that prayer is not cancelled by hardship. Nor is it an invitation to avoid water when water is available and safe. Jurists discuss the details of what counts as illness, travel, impurity, and clean earth, but the governing principle is clear: preserve worship while removing impossible or harmful demands.

**Pardon and forgiveness**

The verse closes with Allah’s pardon and forgiveness. The ritual rule is firm, yet the Lord of the rule knows weakness, sickness, forgetfulness, and circumstance. Believers should therefore avoid both negligence and obsessive difficulty. Prepare for prayer, use the normal means when able, and accept the divinely granted concession without shame when genuinely needed. Worship is disciplined, but it is not designed to crush the worshipper.

**The ease within purification**

Ritual purity trains the body to approach worship attentively, but the concessions prevent ritual from becoming an obstacle to worship. Communities should teach the rule with clarity and compassion, especially to the ill, travellers, and people with recurring bodily difficulties. Excessive doubt can itself become a burden that the verse did not intend.

## Sūrah al-Nisāʾ 4:74

> Let those who would sacrifice this life for the Hereafter fight in the cause of Allah. And whoever fights in Allah’s cause—whether they achieve martyrdom or victory—We will honour them with a great reward.

### Expanded Commentary

**Trading the lower life for the lasting one**

The verse calls those who would give the life of this world for the Hereafter to fight in Allah’s cause. The phrase is not a romantic invitation to seek death; it is a summons to value justice and obedience above survival as the highest good. Martyrdom and victory are both mentioned, preventing the believer from defining success as dying rather than fulfilling a lawful mission.

**The limits of fighting**

“Allah’s cause” excludes tribal revenge, conquest for wealth, coercion of faith, and personal rage. The surrounding verses about oppressed people and Satan’s weak schemes show that the cause is protection of truth and the vulnerable. Legitimate authority, just cause, proportional conduct, and protection of noncombatants remain essential.

**Reward in either outcome**

The believer’s honour does not depend on worldly headlines. Victory may bring responsibility and testing; martyrdom may bring the direct reward of the Hereafter. In both cases Allah promises a great reward to the person who acts sincerely within His limits. The verse therefore trains courage without worshipping military power.

**Victory is not the only measure**

A lawful struggle may end in victory, stalemate, or the death of those who served. The verse places all outcomes under a reward beyond the battlefield. This preserves courage while preventing commanders from treating casualties as proof of spiritual success or victory as proof of moral correctness.

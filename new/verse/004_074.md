## Sūrah al-Nisāʾ 4:74

> Let those who would sacrifice this life for the Hereafter fight in the cause of Allah. And whoever fights in Allah’s cause—whether they achieve martyrdom or victory—We will honour them with a great reward.

**Expanded Commentary**

**Let Those Who Sell This Life for the Next Fight**

The verse commands *fa al-yuqātil fī sabīli Allāhi alladhīna yashrūna al-ḥayāta al-dunyā bi al-ākhirati* — let those who would sacrifice this life for the Hereafter fight in the cause of Allah. The phrase *yashrūna al-ḥayāta al-dunyā bi al-ākhirati* — to sell this life for the next — reverses the trade condemned earlier: those given a portion of the Book who trade it for misguidance (4:44) and who trade guidance to reassure disbelievers they are better guided (4:51). To sell the *dunyā* for the *ākhirah* is the straight trade, the same that the sūrah asked when it said *what harm if they had believed and donated from what was provided* (4:39), where belief and donation cost nothing in the true accounting and gain multiplication. Here the cost is greater — the present life offered — but the logic is the same: the *dunyā* is little enjoyment and the *ākhirah* is better for the mindful (4:77). The call to fight is therefore addressed not to all indiscriminately as compulsion beyond capacity, but to those who have already chosen that sale in their hearts, who would sacrifice this life for the next. The sūrah has said humankind was created weak and burdens lightened (4:28) and that burdens for fighting include taking precautions and going forth in groups or together (4:71); the seller described here is the one whose weakness has been steadied by desire for the next, so that fighting becomes bounty rather than burden.

**In the Cause of Allah**

The qualification *fī sabīli Allāh* — in the cause of Allah — was stressed to distinguish the believer’s fighting from fighting for the Devil. The next verse says *believers fight for God while disbelievers fight for the Devil, so fight the Devil’s forces, his schemes are weak* (4:76). *Sabīl Allāh* in this sūrah was shown as the clear path God wills to make plain (4:26), the noble ways of those before, the limits about wealth and marriage whose keeping is obedience, and the help for the oppressed who cry *“appoint for us a saviour, a helper — all by Your grace”* (4:75). To fight in that path is to answer that cry, as the next verse asks *what is it with you that you do not fight for the oppressed men, women, and children* (4:75). The sūrah elsewhere says *“do not devour wealth by falsehood”* and *“do not kill yourselves”* (4:29) and that doing so deliberately leads to Fire easy for God (4:30); fighting in God’s cause is the opposite taking — not devouring wealth or killing without right — but risking wealth and life for those whose wealth was devoured and whose lives are oppressed. The cause therefore gathers the sūrah’s ethic of *iḥsān* (4:36) into a field; kindness to the traveller and to those in possession becomes, at scale, deliverance for the land of oppressors.

**Whether Martyred or Victorious — A Great Reward**

The promise *wa man yuqātil fī sabīli Allāhi fa yuqt al aw yaghlib fa sawwfa nuʾtīhi ajran ʿaẓīman* — whoever fights in God’s cause, whether they achieve martyrdom or victory, We will honour them with a great reward — was read as securing both outcomes. *Yuqt al* — is killed — was explained as martyrdom — *shahādah* — the witness that joins the company of the blessed — prophets, truthful, martyrs, righteous — what honourable company (4:69). *Yaghlib* — prevails, is victorious — is the gain the lagger envied when he said *would that I had been there to share the great gain* (4:73), but here the gain is following fighting, not wishing. *Ajr ʿaẓīm* — great reward — appeared for doing what was advised (4:67), for the atom multiplied (4:40), and here for fighting whether martyred or victorious, showing that the reward is not tied to worldly outcome but to cause and resolve. The sūrah has already said *“had they done what they were advised, We would have granted great reward and guided to the straight path”* (4:67–68); fighting in God’s cause is the advised act for those whose trade is the next life, and the guidance and reward follow either way. The lagger at disaster who called absence a blessing (4:72) and at bounty who wished he had been there misses both, because he neither sold the *dunyā* nor fought.

**The Trade That Makes Fighting Gain**

To sell this life for the next was explained as seeing this life as capital to invest rather than hoard. The sūrah has warned that the enjoyment of this world is little and the next is better for the mindful, and that none is wronged even by a thread (4:77). That little, when held, is the lagger’s *“Allah blessed us for not being there”*; when sold, it becomes the martyr’s *shahādah* and the victor’s *ghalabah*, both called great reward. Early authorities linked the trade to the migrants who left homes — the hard command few would have obeyed (4:66) — and who found that leaving became the price of the honourable company. Here the price is fighting after taking precautions and going forth as commanded (4:71). The sūrah does not command all to sell in the same way at the same time; it says let those who would sell fight, leaving room for the ill, the traveller, and those whose news should be referred (4:83), while still asking *what is it with you that you do not fight for the oppressed* (4:75) to move those who can. The trade therefore respects weakness while inviting strength; the weak are not condemned for not selling all at once, but the one who would sell is told where the sale is — in God’s cause — and what its price brings — a great reward either way.

**A Verse Between Lagging and the Oppressed’s Cry**

Placed between the portrait of wishing after gain and the cry of the oppressed, this verse turns wishing into doing. The lagger wishes *fawzan ʿaẓīmā* after (4:73); the fighter is promised *ajran ʿaẓīman* whether martyred or victorious before the outcome is known. The oppressed men, women, and children who cry for a saviour (4:75) are the reason the sale matters beyond self-gain; to sell this life is to buy deliverance for them with the help of the sufficient Guardian and Helper (4:45). The sūrah has shown that God never wrongs even by an atom but multiplies good (4:40) and that He fully knows who deserves favour (4:70); the fighter who sells this life knows that no sacrifice is unrecorded and that the great reward is from *ladunnā* — from His presence — as *ajr ʿaẓīm* by grace (4:67). For the community told to return trusts and judge with fairness as a noble exhortation from the All-Hearing, All-Seeing (4:58), fighting in that cause is the public form of returning the trust of protection to its rightful owners — the oppressed — and the recompense is the same great reward that crowns the private trusts kept.

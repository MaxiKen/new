## Sūrah an-Nūr 24:47

> And the hypocrites say, “We believe in Allah and the Messenger, and we obey.” Then a group of them turns away soon after that. These are not ˹true˺ believers.

**Expanded Commentary**

**Verbal Profession and Moral Performance: wa-yaqūlūna āmannā**

The verse initiates an incisive diagnostic exposure of hypocrisy (*al-nifāq*) within the Madinan polity: *wa-yaqūlūna āmannā bi-Allāhi wa-bi-al-rasūli wa-aṭaʿnā*. The verb *yaqūlūn* (they say) immediately establishes a critical discrepancy between verbal articulation and genuine internal conviction.

The hypocrites were masters of theatrical piety and rhetorical eloquence. In public gatherings, communal councils, and congregational spaces, they enthusiastically proclaimed their faith in God and His Messenger, adding the solemn commitment: *wa-aṭaʿnā* (and we obey!). They utilized Islamic terminology, mirrored external religious postures, and sought social capital by aligning themselves verbally with the ascendant Muslim community. In tribal and communal societies, public speech functions as a social currency. The hypocrites sought to reap all the protective benefits, social status, political influence, and economic advantages of communal belonging without paying the necessary price of internal submission, spiritual discipline, or ethical sacrifice.

**The Test of Practical Obedience: wa-aṭaʿnā**

The word *aṭaʿnā* (we obey) represents the ultimate battlefield of religious authenticity. It is easy, emotionally gratifying, and socially advantageous to proclaim loyalty to an ideal when that ideal demands no personal cost or material sacrifice.

True obedience (*al-ṭāʿah*), however, requires the active surrender of personal desires, financial interests, and tribal loyalties to the sovereign authority of divine law. The moment the commands of God and His Messenger align with self-interest, obedience is effortless; but the moment divine law contradicts an individual's personal ambition, economic profit, or familial pride, the authentic character of their commitment is laid bare. The claim of obedience made by the hypocrites was entirely conditional, lasting only as long as Islamic governance flattered their egos and preserved their material interests without demanding concrete sacrifice, humility, or legal concessions, exposing their utter lack of genuine devotion.

**The Rupture of Defection: thumma yatawallā farīqun minhum**

The text exposes the rapid unraveling of their verbal contract: *thumma yatawallā farīqun minhum min baʿdi dhālik*, then a group of them turns away soon after that. The particle *thumma* denotes a temporal and logical progression that highlights an astonishing moral inconsistency between their speech and actions.

No sooner had the pious words left their lips than a faction among them turned on their heels, retreating from concrete obligations. The verb *yatawallā* signifies deliberate, disdainful turning away, physically and emotionally distancing oneself from prophetic leadership. This defection occurred not after years of gradual ideological erosion, but *min baʿdi dhālik*—immediately following their solemn verbal oaths of obedience! When actual testing arrived—whether in the form of a legal judgment that disfavored them, a mandatory military deployment, or a financial contribution—their superficial commitment evaporated, revealing the hollow core beneath their pious declarations and showing the world their true character, bankrupt of all moral courage.

**The Epistemological Hollow of Hypocrisy**

Islamic psychology views hypocrisy as a profound state of cognitive dissonance, spiritual fragmentation, and emotional exhaustion. The hypocrite lives in perpetual terror of exposure, constantly maintaining a double identity: an external mask of conformity designed for public consumption, and an internal reality of cynicism, resentment, and skepticism.

This internal duality fractures the human soul, destroying peace of mind. The hypocrite cannot experience the tranquil unity of the sincere believer whose heart, tongue, and limbs operate in harmonious alignment. Instead, they expend immense cognitive energy calculating risks, managing appearances, and hedging bets between competing social forces. This chronic duplicity calcifies the conscience, making the hypocrite increasingly immune to spiritual admonition, until they become completely incapable of discerning truth from falsehood, trapped in a self-constructed prison of deceit, suspicion, and paralyzing anxiety that darkens every aspect of their existence.

**Divine Stripping of the Faithful Identity: wa-mā ulāʾika bi-al-muʾminīn**

The verse concludes with an uncompromising, categorical verdict delivered by the Sovereign Lord: *wa-mā ulāʾika bi-al-muʾminīn*, and these are not true believers. The syntax employs the strongest form of Arabic negation (*mā* followed by the preposition *bi* attached to the predicate), completely dismantling their religious pretenses.

God does not evaluate human beings based on eloquent slogans, elaborate titles, or performative rituals. Faith (*īmān*) is not a subjective emotional sentiment, nor a superficial tribal badge that confers automatic immunity. In the divine ledger, faith is an indissoluble synthesis of internal conviction (*taṣdīq*), verbal affirmation (*iqrār*), and righteous bodily deeds (*ʿamal*). By turning away from the practical commands of the Messenger ﷺ, the hypocrites demonstrated that their verbal claims were fraudulent. The divine verdict strips them of the honorable title of believer, warning the community against being deceived by loud professions of piety that lack the substance of obedient practice, establishing that true belief requires faithful action and sincere submission.

**The Sociological Danger of Performative Religion**

This diagnostic exposure carries enduring relevance for every generation of religious communities. Performative religion—where external displays of orthodoxy mask deep ethical corruption, social cruelty, and selective obedience—is far more destructive to the spiritual health of a society than open, honest disbelief.

An open opponent is identifiable and can be engaged directly. A hypocrite operates from within the community, corroding trust, poisoning social cohesion, and sowing cynical doubt among vulnerable members. When leaders or prominent community figures proclaim piety with their mouths while consistently violating basic standards of justice and integrity in private or commercial dealings, they inflict catastrophic damage upon the credibility of faith itself, driving seekers away into skepticism. Verse 47 serves as a permanent mirror, demanding that every believer rigorously audit their own soul to ensure that their daily actions validate, rather than contradict, the faith they profess before the world, purifying both public conduct and secret intention to attain divine acceptance, enduring honor, and eternal salvation.

## Sūrah al-Baqarah 2:180

> It is prescribed that when death approaches any of you—if they leave something of value—a will should be made in favour of parents and immediate family with fairness. ˹This is˺ an obligation on those who are mindful ˹of Allah˺.

**Expanded Commentary**

**The Instruction About Wills**

The verse instructs a person who is approaching death and who leaves property behind to make a will in favour of his parents and his close relatives, and it describes the instruction as an obligation upon the mindful. The sentence opens a short section on the disposal of a person's estate.

The Book's treatment of the subject in this place precedes the detailed rules of inheritance that appear in a later chapter, and the community's scholars read the two together. What the sentence establishes is that a person's property does not pass from him without an arrangement, and that the arrangement includes those who are closest to him.

**The Meaning of "Some Good"**

The phrase rendered as some good is understood by the commentators to mean property, and they supported the reading with other passages in which the same word describes wealth. The instruction therefore applies to a person who has something to leave rather than to everyone who dies.

The community's scholars discussed the qualification at length, and some read it as making the bequest conditional on the estate's size. What the sentence establishes in any case is that the Book addresses a person who possesses property and tells him what to do with it as his life ends, and the reader is shown that the disposal of an estate belongs to the religious life rather than to private preference.

**The Recipients Named**

The sentence names parents and close relatives as the recipients of the bequest, and the commentators explained the instruction as a provision for those who would otherwise receive nothing. The community's scholars noted that a parent or a relative who is excluded from the inheritance by a law of shares is included by this command.

The two categories cover the nearest relations, and the book's treatment of family obligations elsewhere supplies the framework in which the instruction is read. What the sentence does is to place the closest people in the first position when a person disposes of what he owns, and the reader is given a rule about the order in which his responsibilities are to be arranged.

**The Instruction to Act Fairly**

The sentence requires the bequest to be made in a proper manner, and the commentators explained the phrase as a reference to fairness and to the absence of harm. The Book's treatment of bequests elsewhere forbids the manipulation of a will to the disadvantage of an heir, and the present sentence supplies the standard by which a will is judged.

The community's jurists drew from the passage and from the reports the rule that a bequest may not exceed a third of the estate and may not be made to a person who receives a share by inheritance. What the reader receives is an instruction about the manner as well as the content of the disposal, and the sentence places the whole matter under the standard of fairness.

**The Later Rules of Inheritance**

The Book states the shares of the heirs in a later chapter, and the community's scholars discussed the relation between that passage and the instruction in this verse. Some held that the detailed rules replaced the earlier instruction in so far as they concerned the heirs, while others held that the instruction continues to apply to relatives who are not heirs.

The reports also preserve the Prophet's statement that no bequest may be made to an heir, and the community's jurists built the law of wills on the combination of the verse and the report. What the reader is shown is that the Book's legislation on a matter may be developed across its chapters, and the present sentence is the first stage of that treatment.

**The Obligation and Its Addressee**

The sentence ends by describing the instruction as an obligation upon the mindful, and the phrase echoes the purpose clause of the previous verse. The Book's use of the vocabulary of awareness in connection with its legal instructions shows that the laws are addressed to a disposition as well as to a conduct.

The community's teachers read the phrase as a statement about the person who will observe the instruction rather than as a restriction on its scope. What the sentence establishes is that the transfer of a person's property at the end of his life is a matter in which the Book requires him to act properly, and the reader is left with the awareness that the disposal is made in the presence of the One who gave him what he owns.

The reader is given both the duty and the disposition that accompanies it, and the sentence places the disposal of a person’s property within the awareness of the One who provided it. The passage continues with the protection of the arrangement the sentence requires.

**The Place of the Verse in the Section**

The sentence follows the legislation on homicide and precedes the verses on fasting, and the three subjects form a section on the arrangements that govern a community's life. The Book deals with the loss of life, the disposal of property, and the discipline of the body in a sequence, and the community's teachers noted that each subject concerns a trust that a person holds.

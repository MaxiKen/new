## Sūrah al-Tawbah 9:64

> The hypocrites fear that a sûrah should be revealed about them, exposing what is in their hearts. Say, ˹O Prophet,˺ “Keep mocking! Allah will definitely bring to light what you fear.”

**Expanded Commentary**

**Fear of Exposure Reveals the Hidden Act**

The hypocrites fear that revelation will expose what they carry in their hearts. Their fear is not the fear of an innocent person misunderstood. It is the fear that a hidden attitude will be named publicly. A person who knows his conduct is clean may fear unfair accusation, but he does not usually dread truth itself. The terror of exposure can therefore become evidence that the hidden act is real.

This does not mean every anxious person is guilty. Sensitive people can fear being misread, and believers can fear their own shortcomings. The verse describes people who mock and plot while trying to preserve a public identity of loyalty. Their anxiety comes from the collision between inner reality and outer performance. Revelation threatens not their reputation alone, but the false structure built around it.

**Mockery Cannot Control Revelation**

Their mocking speech tries to reduce sacred truth to entertainment. Mockery can create the feeling that the mocker is in command: if everything is a joke, nothing can judge him. The verse removes that illusion. Allah can bring to light what the mocker tries to keep hidden, and no performance can prevent the truth from being disclosed.

The command to “keep mocking” is not approval of mockery. It is a challenge that exposes its futility. The mocker is allowed to continue for a time, but the permission is not protection. This pattern appears throughout the Qur'an: delay can give the false person room to repent, yet it can also allow his own words to accumulate as evidence against him.

**Envy Can Blind a Person to Plain Truth**

The source tradition records the insight that envy may blind a person even to what is sensible and visible. A hypocrite may recognize the Prophet's truthfulness in private and still refuse to submit because submission would lower the status he wants to preserve. The heart then argues against what the mind has already seen.

Envy is not only wanting what another has. It is resenting the good that Allah placed in another person. When envy governs, guidance from that person becomes unbearable, even if the guidance is sound. The believer asks Allah to protect him from this blindness. He can learn from someone without feeling that learning diminishes him, because honor comes from following truth rather than from being its first discoverer.

**Disclosure Can Be a Mercy Before It Is a Punishment**

Exposure is painful, but it can rescue a person from a lie. When a hidden defect is named, repentance becomes possible. The hypocrites fear disclosure because they want the benefit of belonging without the truth of belonging. Yet a truthful exposure could have ended that divided life if they had accepted it.

Communities should therefore handle disclosure with justice. Private sin should not be spread for entertainment, and public harm cannot be protected by a demand for secrecy. The purpose of naming the wrong is correction, protection, and return. Revelation exposes to establish truth, not to satisfy curiosity. A believer who is corrected should ask what Allah is saving him from rather than only asking how to preserve his image.

**The Heart Is Not Beyond Accountability**

The verse speaks about what is in their hearts, showing that inner attitudes matter even when no outward act has yet revealed them. Islam does not treat the heart as a private kingdom beyond moral judgment. Intentions, envy, contempt, and secret plans shape the conduct that eventually appears.

This should lead to inner work. A servant may control his tongue while resentment grows beneath it. He may perform the outward act while wishing the truth would fail. He should bring those movements to Allah in prayer before they become a public habit. Fear of exposure can be transformed into fear of remaining false. That transformation is one of the mercies of divine warning.

**Allah Brings Truth to Light in His Time**

The timing belongs to Allah. A hypocrite may remain hidden for a season, and a sincere person may be misunderstood for a season. Delay does not mean that Allah has missed the truth. It means that the test, the opportunity for repentance, and the final evidence are unfolding under His wisdom.

The believer therefore avoids both panic and complacency. He does not assume that every accusation must be answered by immediate exposure of the accuser. He continues to act truthfully and trusts Allah to disclose what must be disclosed. A false mask can frighten its wearer, but it cannot frighten the One who sees through it.

The believer should therefore fear two opposite errors. He must not expose people merely to enjoy their embarrassment, and he must not protect a false reputation after clear harm has been established. Disclosure is measured by purpose, evidence, and the rights of those affected. A hidden accusation may need private counsel; a public plot may require public warning. In both cases, Allah's knowledge is the model of justice: nothing is missed, and nothing is exaggerated. The servant asks for a heart that prefers being corrected to being admired. If the hidden fault is named, he treats the exposure as a chance to return rather than as a reason to multiply the mask.

A sincere person may fear exposure because he knows he has failed, but his fear sends him toward repentance rather than toward mockery. The difference appears in what he does after the warning. He seeks forgiveness, ends the harmful practice, and accepts that trust may need to be rebuilt. The hypocrite fears only the loss of his mask. He continues the act and searches for a new explanation. Allah's disclosure is therefore both a judgment and a chance. The servant who welcomes correction can turn a feared revelation into the beginning of a cleaner life.

## Sūrah al-Baqarah 2:187

> It has been made permissible for you to be intimate with your wives during the nights preceding the fast. Your spouses are a garment for you as you are for them. Allah knows that you were deceiving yourselves. So He has accepted your repentance and pardoned you. So now you may be intimate with them and seek what Allah has prescribed for you. ˹You may˺ eat and drink until you see the light of dawn breaking the darkness of night, then complete the fast until nightfall. Do not be intimate with your spouses while you are meditating in the mosques. These are the limits set by Allah, so do not exceed them. This is how Allah makes His revelations clear to people, so they may become mindful ˹of Him˺.

**Expanded Commentary**

**The Permission Granted**

The verse states that intimacy with one's spouse is permitted during the nights of the fast, and it describes the spouses as garments for one another. The sentence removes a restriction that the community had observed in the early period, and the commentators described the circumstances in which the practice had developed.

The reports describe a period in which the community broke the fast after sunset and then refrained from food and from marital relations until the following night, and they describe the confession of a companion who had failed to observe the restriction. The sentence in this verse presents the final arrangement and describes it as an alleviation.

**The Garment Metaphor**

The sentence states that the spouses are garments for one another, and the commentators explained the image as a description of the closeness and the protection that the relationship provides. The same image appears in the sūrah's treatment of the relations between spouses elsewhere, and the community's teachers used it when they spoke of marriage.

The metaphor covers both the intimacy that the verse permits and the mutual support that the relationship provides. What the reader receives is a statement about the nature of the bond at the moment when the verse sets out the permission, and the sentence presents the permission as an expression of the relationship rather than as a concession to appetite.

**The Acknowledgment of Weakness**

The sentence states that God knew the community had been deceiving itself, and it then states that the repentance was accepted and the fault pardoned. The community's scholars read the sentence as an acknowledgment that the earlier arrangement had placed the believers in difficulty.

The Book's description of the community's condition is without reproach, and the sentence records the divine response rather than dwelling on the offense. What the reader is given is a picture of a community whose scruples had led it beyond what God required, and the verse's permission restores the practice to the limits that God has set.

**The Times of the Fast**

The sentence states the limits of the daily fast: the community may eat and drink until the light of dawn is distinguishable from the darkness of night, and the fast is then completed until nightfall. The community's scholars derived the timing of the pre-dawn meal and of the evening breaking of the fast from the sentence.

The wording's use of the image of two threads was explained by the commentators as a reference to the light of the dawn and the darkness of the night. What the reader receives is a statement of the practice's boundaries rather than a description of the signs in the sky, and the community's teachers noted that the Book leaves the determination of the times to the observation of the dawn.

**The Retreat in the Mosque**

The sentence forbids intimacy during the period of retreat in the mosques, and the community's scholars treated the instruction as a rule about the practice of seclusion for worship. The practice is mentioned here among the rules of the month.

The Book's treatment of the subject connects the retreat with the nights of the month and with the search for the night of decree.

## Sūrah al-Aʿrāf 7:49

> Are these ˹humble believers˺ the ones you swore would never be shown Allah’s mercy?” ˹Finally, those on the heights will be told:˺ “Enter Paradise! There will be no fear for you, nor will you grieve.”

**Expanded Commentary**

**The Question Put to the Condemned**

The verse reports the question addressed to the people of the Fire about a group of believers whom they had sworn would never be shown mercy, and the question is rhetorical. The people on the heights have just spoken to the proud among the condemned (7:48), and the question in front of us completes the address by naming the specific group that the great ones had dismissed. The scene records the reversal of the verdict that the world had passed.

The wording of the question attributes the oath to the condemned, and the Book's accounts of the notables of the communities supply the kind of oath it was. The great ones of Makkah dismissed the believers as weak, poor, and of no account, and the sūrah of the Cattle reports the same dismissal in its account of the notables who asked why the message had not been given to a man of standing in the two towns (6:53). The question in front of us states that the dismissal is answered at the barrier.

**The Believers of Low Standing**

The people whom the question names are the believers whose standing in the world gave the notables their argument, and the Book describes them in several passages. The sūrah of the Cattle states that God tests some people by means of others so that the disbelievers may ask whether these are the ones whom God has favoured among them (6:53), and the sūrah of Hūd reports the notables of Noah's people saying that only the weak had followed him. The verse in front of us places the same believers in the Garden.

The Book's treatment of the lowly believers as the first to accept the messengers appears throughout its narratives, and the sūrah of the Poets reports the charge brought against Noah that only the lowest had followed him. The scene in front of us answers that charge by showing where the two groups have arrived, and the answer is delivered in the form of a question that the condemned cannot answer.

**The Oath and Its Refutation**

The verse states that the condemned had sworn that the believers would never be shown mercy, and the Books' descriptions of the proud include the confidence with which they pronounced on the outcome. The sūrah of the Cave reports the owner of the garden expressing doubt about the Hour and about his own return, and the sūrah of Maryam reports the deniers claiming that they would be given wealth and children in the Hereafter. The oath in front of us belongs to the same confidence, and the verse states that it has been falsified.

The command that follows the question shows the refutation in its plainest form, since the answer to the oath is the instruction to enter the Garden. The Book's practice of answering the claims of the deniers with the announcement of the reward given to the believers appears in its closing descriptions of the two communities, and the verse in front of us uses the practice at the summit of the scene.

**The Command to Enter**

The verse reports the command given to the believers to enter the Garden, and the command supplies the conclusion of the passage. The people on the heights have been described as hoping to enter (7:46), and the command in front of us grants what they hoped for; the verse therefore completes their station by granting the entry. The sūrah's earlier verses have described the announcement made to the people of the Garden that what they have received is theirs for what they did (7:43), and the command in front of us repeats the gift.

The Book's descriptions of the entry into the Garden include the opening of the gates and the greetings of the angels, and the sūrah of the Troops describes the arrival of the mindful at the Garden with its gates opened and the keepers greeting them with the announcement that they have done well. The command in front of us is short, and it places the believers inside the dwelling that the passage has described.

**No Fear and No Grief**

The verse closes with the two promises that the sūrah has already attached to the response of the believers, that no fear will come upon them and that they will not grieve. The earlier verse states the same promise in the address to the children of Adam who mend their conduct (7:35), and the Book repeats the formula in its descriptions of the saved in several places, including its statement that those who follow the guidance have nothing to fear or to grieve over (2:38). The promise in front of us repeats the assurance at the point where it is fulfilled.

The two words name the anxieties of the world and of the reckoning, and the Book's use of the formula marks the state of those who have passed beyond both. The sūrah of Yūnus applies the same assurance to the friends of God (10:62), and the sūrah of the Sand Dunes attaches it to those who say that their Lord is God and remain steadfast (46:13). The verse in front of us supplies the promise in the mouth of the command itself.

**Where the Verse Sits**

The verse stands forty-ninth in the chapter, and it completes the scene of the barrier, the heights, and the two companies. The verses that follow report the request of the people of the Fire for water and provision and the answer that they are forbidden to the deniers (7:50–51), and the passage then returns to the revelation and its guidance (7:52). The verse in front of us closes the exchange that the chapter's account of the two outcomes has produced.

The reader who has followed the passage from the weighing of deeds now sees the verdict delivered in both directions. The great are told that their numbers and their arrogance have not helped them, and the believers whom they had dismissed are told to enter the Garden; and the sūrah's next verses state what the condemned ask for and what they are refused.

**The Transition to the Next Verse**

The next verse reports that the people of the Fire will call out to the people of the Garden asking for water or for what God has provided them, and that the answer states that God has forbidden both to the disbelievers (7:50). The command to enter the Garden in front of us is followed by the last exchange between the two groups, and the refusal is placed beside the gift.

The two verses together complete the picture of the two conditions. The one group is told to enter and to leave fear and grief behind, and the other asks for a drop of water and is refused; and the sūrah states the reason for the refusal in the verses that follow.

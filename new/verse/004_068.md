## Sūrah al-Nisāʾ 4:68

> and guided them to the Straight Path.

**Expanded Commentary**

**Guided to the Straight Path**

The promise *wa la hadaynāhum ṣirāṭan mustaqīmā* — and We would have guided them to the Straight Path — completes the counterfactual: had they done what they were advised, it would have been better and more reassuring (4:66), We would have granted a great reward by grace (4:67), and guided them to the straight path. The sūrah has used *ṣirāṭ mustaqīm* as the way that God wills to make clear and to guide to — the noble ways of those before you (4:26) — the opposite of the far straying Satan desires when they seek false judges (4:60). To be guided to the straight path was explained as being shown the road and being made to walk it with firmness — *tathbīt* — the same firmness named as more reassuring in 4:66. The three results therefore form one movement: better outcome, firmer heart, great reward, and straight guidance. The path is called straight — *mustaqīm* — because it does not tilt — *mayl* — toward one wife at another’s expense (4:129), nor deviate greatly — *maylan ʿaẓīmā* — as those who follow desires wish (4:27), nor turn away stubbornly — *ṣudūd* — as hypocrites do from the revelations and Messenger (4:61). The straight path was read as the Book and the prophetic way taken together, the same pair that disputes are to be referred to as best outcome (4:59). Guidance to it is therefore not only information but enablement.

**Guidance as the Fruit of Doing**

The verse makes guidance a fruit of doing what was advised, not only of hearing it. The sūrah has warned against those who were given a portion of the Book yet trade it for misguidance and wish you to deviate (4:44) and who believe in *jibt* and *ṭāghūt* and call disbelievers better guided (4:51). To do what was advised — return trusts to rightful owners and judge with fairness (4:58), obey and refer (4:59), accept the Prophet ﷺ as judge without inward resistance (4:65) — is the practice that keeps the path straight. Early authorities noted that *hadaynāhum* — We would have guided them — implies God’s act of guidance follows the servant’s act of obedience, as elsewhere *“those who are guided, He increases them in guidance”* and *“whoever believes in God, He guides his heart.”* The sūrah elsewhere says that avoidance of major sins leads to an honourable entry (4:31) and that an atom’s weight of good is multiplied with a great reward (4:40); guidance to the straight path is the form that entry takes when viewed as a road. The path is straight because it was made clear — *yubayyina* (4:26) — and because it is the way of those before who kept limits; to be guided to it is to be placed among them.

**The Path That Desires Made Deviate**

The contrast with 4:27 sharpens the promise. There, those who follow desires wish you to deviate greatly from God’s Way; here, doing what was advised leads to being guided to the straight path, the opposite of deviation. The straight path is also the opposite of the false judge’s way, to which Satan desires to lead farther away (4:60). The sūrah therefore presents two possible guidances: to the straight path by doing what was advised, or to far straying by seeking *ṭāghūt*. The choice is enacted in the same matters the sūrah has regulated: a guardian who returns orphan wealth with witnesses (4:6) walks straight; one who consumes it hastily before the orphan grows (4:6) strays; a husband who gives the dowry graciously (4:4) walks straight; one who reclaims it by slander strays (4:20). Guidance to the straight path is therefore not a separate religious act added to those decisions; it is those decisions made consistently. The sūrah will soon say that whoever obeys God and the Messenger will be with the blessed — prophets, people of truth, martyrs, righteous — what honourable company (4:69), and that company is the fellowship of the straight path.

**Why Great Reward and Straight Path Pair**

The verse pairs *ajr ʿaẓīm* — great reward by grace (4:67) — with *ṣirāṭ mustaqīm* — straight path — to show that recompense and direction belong together. Reward without guidance would be a gift without a road; guidance without reward would be a road without arrival. The sūrah joins them because the weak for whom burdens were lightened (4:28) need both: a path they can walk and a reward that makes walking worthwhile. The sūrah has already said that God wishes to lighten burdens because humankind was created weak, and that He wishes to turn toward you (4:26–27); to guide to the straight path is the form that turning takes when the servant does what was advised. The counterfactual — had they done — is therefore not a lament for a lost opportunity but a map for the living: do what you were advised now, and the two that were promised then — great reward and straight guidance — are still the sequence. The one who does not crave another’s bounty but asks God for His (4:32), who does not devour by falsehood but trades by mutual consent (4:29), is already on the straight path, even if he has not yet seen its end.

**A Path That Holds After Witnesses and Before Battle**

Placed between the great reward and the company of the blessed, this verse holds the moral road between the witness scene (4:41–42) and the coming call to fight for the oppressed (4:75). The straight path that would have been granted had they done what was advised is the same path that the believers are now called to walk when they take precautions and go forth in groups or together (4:71). It is straight because it does not seek false judges (4:60), does not distort words (4:46), does not believe in *jibt* (4:51), and does not envy for bounty (4:54). Each of those is a bend that the sūrah has straightened with a limit, a trust, or a referral. To be guided to that path is to have the heart made firm — *ashadda tathbītā* — so that when the order to fight comes, the group that fears people as God should be feared (4:77) will not turn, and when the order to give comes, the hand that was stingy will open. The verse generalises the promise beyond the hypocrites who failed the test; any who do what they were advised will find the same guidance. For the weak who were told that avoidance of major sins covers lesser ones (4:31), that promise is the straight road ahead, leading to the company that the next verses will name as the honourable fellowship of the guided.

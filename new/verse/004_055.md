## Sūrah al-Nisāʾ 4:55

> Yet some believed in him while others turned away from him. Hell is sufficient as a torment!

### Expanded Commentary

**Response divides people, not revelation alone**

Some believed in the one sent with truth and others turned away. The verse presents both reactions within the same historical community, reminding the reader that an inherited religious identity does not determine every individual’s response. People must answer for what they do with evidence, not receive a collective verdict based solely on belonging.

**Hell as a sufficient consequence**

The statement that Hell is sufficient as a punishment is brief and severe. It answers the arrogance of those who thought social standing or scriptural affiliation would shield them from accountability. Knowledge without submission cannot rescue, and opposition to truth has consequences even when the opponent possesses eloquence and influence.

The verse should be read alongside the Qur’an’s recognition of righteous People of the Book elsewhere. It cannot be used to erase those passages or to declare every Jew or Christian condemned by identity. Its subject is the people who responded to the specific truth before them with rejection and hostility.

For believers, the warning is personal. One may stand near a sacred tradition, recite scripture, and still turn away from a command because it threatens one’s status. The decisive question is not what name is inherited but whether the heart responds to God with faith and obedience.

**Identity does not complete faith**

A person may inherit a scripture, a name, or an institution and still turn away from the truth. Conversely, someone from a community criticized in one passage may respond faithfully. The verse encourages individual moral judgment and warns religious communities against assuming that history itself guarantees their future.

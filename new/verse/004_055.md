## Sūrah al-Nisāʾ 4:55

> Yet some believed in him while others turned away from him. Hell is sufficient as a torment!

**Expanded Commentary**

**Some Believed, Others Turned Away**

The verse states *fa minhum man āmana bihi wa minhum man ṣadda ʿanhu* — yet some believed in him while others turned away from him. The pronoun *bihi* — in him — was read as belief in Abraham, or more directly in the Prophet ﷺ who is the descendant through whom Book and wisdom and great authority have newly come, or in the Book itself that confirms what was with them (4:47). The antecedent’s breadth is the point. Whoever is given Book, wisdom, and authority from the family of Abraham will be met with two responses: belief and turning away. The sūrah has just said that God gave that triad to the family of Abraham (4:54) and that envy for bounty drove those given a portion of the Book to believe in *jibt* and *ṭāghūt* and to reassure disbelievers they were better guided (4:51). This verse’s division — some believed, some turned away — is the human outcome of that bounty and that envy. It is the same division the sūrah opened with witnesses from every community and the Prophet ﷺ as witness (4:41–42): some will stand with their witness, others will wish to be dust. The sūrah does not treat turning away as a passing hesitation; *ṣadda ʿanhu* — to turn away, to bar, to hinder — was read as active turning that also turns others, as the promoters of stinginess teach stinginess (4:37) and the traders of misguidance wish you to deviate (4:44).

**Him — The Prophet as Heir of Abraham’s Bounty**

To read *bihi* as the Prophet Muḥammad ﷺ fits the immediate context of envy. Those given a portion of the Book envied the people for the bounty God gave (4:54), and that bounty was realized in a man of Abraham’s line who brought *kitāb* and *ḥikmah* and whose community was gaining *mulk* in Madinah. Some among those given the Book and among the Arabs believed in him — the few the sūrah acknowledged who still believe (4:46) — while others turned away and barred. The same verb *ṣadda* appears elsewhere for those who bar from God’s way, and the sūrah will use it for those who claim belief yet seek judgment of false judges and are led farther away (4:60). Here the barring is from the heir of Abraham’s bounty, which is why the next clause says Hell is sufficient as torment — the recompense for barring from that bounty matches its magnitude. The sūrah has shown that God gave great authority to the family before; to turn away from its new bearer is to turn away from that same giving, now made confirming and clear (4:26–27). The choice is therefore not between two equal teachers; it is between belief in the bearer of Book and wisdom and turning away to *jibt*.

**Hell Is Sufficient as Torment**

The closing *wa kafā bi jahannama saʿīrā* — and Hell is sufficient as a torment — uses *kafā bi* — sufficient is — the same phrase that declared God sufficient as Guardian and sufficient as Helper (4:45). There sufficiency was reassurance for believers surrounded by those who trade the Book; here sufficiency is warning for those who turn away. *Saʿīr* — blaze, fierce flame — was read as the Fire that is already kindled, the blaze that consumes as *“We will burn them in the Fire”* was said for deliberate devouring and killing where *that is easy for Allah* (4:30) and as *“We will cast them into the Fire, whenever their skin is burnt We will replace it”* will be said for those who reject signs (4:56). *Kafā* — sufficient — was stressed as saying no additional torment is needed to prove the seriousness of envy that becomes barring; Hell suffices, and that suffices as deterrence. The sūrah has already described humiliating punishment prepared for concealing bounty (4:37) and for association not forgiven (4:48); this blaze is the same recompense named at the scale of the bounty refused. Great authority rejected meets great torment as its counterpart; the sufficiency on one side — God as Guardian and Helper — meets sufficiency on the other — Hell as blaze — showing that the community’s help and the deviator’s penalty are both assured without need for another witness.

**Why the Division Is Mentioned Here**

The verse follows the triad of Book, wisdom, and great authority (4:54) because that triad always divides. The sūrah has been dividing since it set limits — *ḥudūd* — whose keeping brings Gardens and whose crossing brings Fire (4:13–14), and since it said *“if you avoid major sins We will cover lesser ones and admit you to honour”* (4:31). To be given bounty is to be tested by it. Some among those given a portion will be among the few who are elevated — *Allah elevates whomever He wills and none is wronged even by a thread* (4:49) — while others fabricate lies against God as blatant sin (4:50). The same test now operates around the Prophet ﷺ. His presence makes the bounty visible as a person to be believed or turned from, which is why the verse names belief *in him* and turning *from him* as the two branches. The sūrah does not treat the turn as neutral; those who turn also wish others to deviate greatly (4:27), promote stinginess (4:37), and reassure disbelievers (4:51). Turning is therefore not only rejection but mission against belief, which is why Hell as blaze suffices as its name. The believers who believed in him, though few among those given a portion, join the larger *you are from one another* (4:25) — migrants and hosts who believed — and for them the sūrah has said God suffices as Guardian.

**A Sufficiency That Holds Both Sides**

Placed where it is, between the bounty to Abraham’s family (4:54) and the detailed recompense of Fire with skin replaced (4:56), this short verse holds both sides of the sūrah’s equation. Book, wisdom, and authority given become a cause for envy in some, for belief in others; Hell as blaze suffices for the enviers who bar, while multiplication of an atom’s weight and a great reward out of grace (4:40) suffice for the believers who give. The sufficiency of Hell as torment therefore does not stand alone; it stands opposite the sufficiency of God as Guardian and Helper, and opposite the sufficiency of atom-exact justice. *Kafā* in 4:45 tells the believer not to fear the traders of misguidance; *kafā* here tells the trader that the consequence is sufficient without addition. Between the two, the sūrah leaves the reader with the same choice it has posed since *“how will it be when We bring a witness”* (4:41): to believe in him through whom Book and wisdom came and to find Guardianship that suffices, or to turn away and find a blaze that also suffices, with no helper once condemned (4:52) and not even a speck withheld where kingdom is imagined (4:53).

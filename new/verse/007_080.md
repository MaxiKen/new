## Sūrah al-Aʿrāf 7:80

> And ˹remember˺ when Lot scolded ˹the men of˺ his people, ˹saying,˺ “Do you commit a shameful deed that no man has ever done before?

**Expanded Commentary**

**The Fourth Account and Its Messenger**

The verse opens the fourth of the sūrah's punishment accounts, and it introduces Lot as the messenger of the people among whom he lived. The Book tells the story of this people in several passages, and the accounts run through the sūrah of Hūd (11:77–83), the sūrah of the Rocky Tract (15:57–77), the sūrah of the Poets (26:160–175), the sūrah of the Ant (27:54–58), the sūrah of the Spider (29:28–35), the sūrah of the Moon (54:33–39), and the sūrah of the Winnowing Winds (51:31–37). The verse in front of us supplies the opening of the sūrah's own version.

The Book presents Lot as a messenger of the people of the cities, and the sūrah of the Poets describes him as the brother of the people he addressed (26:161). The verse in front of us names the people as his own and states his address to them, and the account that follows records the argument, the arrival of the angel messengers, and the destruction of the cities. The narrative is the longest in the sūrah's series before the account of Moses.

**The Kinship With Abraham**

The Book places Lot among the household of Abraham, and the sūrah of the Spider states that Lot believed in him when Abraham announced his emigration to his Lord (29:26). The sūrah of the Prophets states that God delivered Abraham along with Lot to the land that He had blessed for all people (21:71), and the sūrah of Hūd reports the angel messengers visiting Abraham with the news of a son before they went on to Lot's people (11:69–76). The verse in front of us stands in that sequence, and the narrative that follows reports the same messengers arriving in the city.

The Book's accounts of the two men present them as partners in the migration and in the warning, and the sūrah of the Rocky Tract gives the fullest account of the angels' visit to Lot after their visit to Abraham. This verse supplies the opening of the sūrah's account, and the reader who has followed the series from Noah to Thamūd is given the next messenger in the line.

**The Indecency Unprecedented**

The verse states the question that Lot put to his people about the deed they commit, and the question names the deed as one that no one in the world had committed before them. The sūrah of the Spider reports the same words in its account, stating that Lot rebuked his people for a shameful deed that no man had done before them (29:28), and the sūrah of the Ant reports his rebuke of the deed (27:54). The Book's statements about the novelty of the act appear in more than one passage, and the sūrah's version in front of us is the briefest of them.

The Book's identification of the deed follows in the next verse, where Lot states that his people lust after men instead of women and describes them as transgressors (7:81). The sūrah of the Poets reports the same charge in the messenger's address to his people, asking why they lust after men and leaving the wives that their Lord had created for them (26:165–166). The verse here states the question about the deed, and the verse that follows names it.

**The Address to His People**

The verse reports that Lot scolded his people, and the term states the tone of the address. The Book's narratives present the messengers addressing their peoples with the language of counsel and warning, and the sūrah of the Poets records Lot's address with the call to fear God (26:161). The present verse states the rebuke, and the phrase my people within the address identifies the messenger with those he corrects.

The Book's use of the possessive in the messengers' addresses appears in each of the sūrah's narratives, and the messengers are described as the brothers of the peoples they were sent to. The verse under discussion supplies the fourth instance of the form, and the account that follows reports the people's answer and the messengers' response to it.

**The Place of the Account in the Sūrah's Series**

The verse begins the account that turns the sūrah's series from the peoples of Arabia to the cities of the plain, and the narrative introduces features that the earlier accounts did not have. The angel messengers appear, the town is destroyed by an act of God upon the place itself, and the account ends with the punishment raining on the people. The Book's series of destroyed peoples includes the cities of Lot in each of its listings, and the verse in front of us places them fourth in the sūrah's sequence.

The Book's treatment of the offences of the several peoples gives each community its own charge, and the sūrah's narratives state the charges in the messengers' words. The people of Noah were charged with persistence in error, the people of ʿĀd with arrogance and the invention of gods, the people of Thamūd with the killing of the sign and the demand for the punishment, and the people of Lot in front of us with the deed. The series presents the communities as differing in their offences and agreeing in their refusal.

**Where the Verse Sits**

The verse stands eightieth in the chapter, and it opens the account of Lot immediately after the close of the account of Thamūd. The verses that follow state the charge in full, report the answer of the people, and carry the narrative through the visit of the angels to the destruction of the cities (7:81–84). Here the verse supplies the opening question of the fourth account.

The reader who has seen three communities destroyed now meets the fourth with the charge stated in the mouth of its messenger. The question names the deed and its novelty, and the verses that follow develop the charge and record the answer; and the sūrah's series continues toward the long account of Moses and the people of Israel.

**The Transition to the Next Verse**

The next verse completes the charge, stating that the people come to men rather than to women and that they are a people who transgress (7:81). The question in front of us is followed by the naming of the deed, and the passage then reports the people's answer and the messenger's prayer against them.

The two verses together state the offence of the fourth community. The messenger asks how the people can commit what no one before them committed, and he then states what the act is and describes those who practice it; and the sūrah's following verses record the refusal, the gathering of the people, and the arrival of the messengers who were sent to destroy the cities.

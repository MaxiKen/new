## Sūrah al-Nisāʾ 4:176

> They ask you ˹for a ruling, O Prophet˺. Say, “Allah gives you a ruling regarding those who die without children or parents.” If a man dies childless and leaves behind a sister, she will inherit one-half of his estate, whereas her brother will inherit all of her estate if she dies childless. If this person leaves behind two sisters, they together will inherit two-thirds of the estate. But if the deceased leaves male and female siblings, a male’s share will be equal to that of two females. Allah makes ˹this˺ clear to you so you do not go astray. And Allah has ˹perfect˺ knowledge of all things.

### Expanded Commentary

**The closing inheritance question**

The sūrah ends by returning to inheritance. The question concerns a person who dies without children or parents, and the answer gives the sister half of the estate; if a childless sister dies, her brother inherits her estate under the stated condition; two sisters share two-thirds; brothers and sisters together follow the ratio of two shares for a male to one for a female. The passage completes the chapter’s movement from the common origin of humanity to the lawful distribution of what remains when a life ends.

**Why the final verse matters**

The closing ruling is not an appendix detached from theology. It demonstrates that divine guidance reaches the moment when family grief, property, and competing claims could easily generate injustice. The chapter began with taqwā, kinship, and the Watcher; it ends by saying that God makes the matter clear so people do not go astray. Clarity is mercy when inheritance can divide a family.

The term describing the childless deceased was debated by early authorities, and the relationship of siblings to parents, children, and residuary heirs must be calculated with the rest of the Qur’anic law and Prophetic explanation. The verse cannot be applied responsibly by looking only at one sentence. A complete family tree, debts, valid bequests, and the rules of exclusion must be examined by qualified experts.

**The dignity of the sister**

The explicit share for a sister is a final rejection of the custom that treated women as absent from succession. The ratio in a particular case is tied to the financial structure of Islamic law, not to a measure of spiritual worth. The sister owns what she inherits; no brother may take it back through pressure or family shame.

Allah’s perfect knowledge closes the sūrah. Human beings see only the immediate grief and the visible asset; God knows every relationship, obligation, and future need. The faithful response is to obey the ordinance, document the division, protect the vulnerable heir, and let the final chapter’s lesson govern the first: kinship is a trust watched by Allah.

**The final protection of a sister’s share**

The last verse sends the reader back to the chapter’s opening concern: kinship must be governed by clear justice. A sister’s share is not a favour from her brother, and a brother’s share does not authorize him to manage hers. The closing command to seek clarity is a final refusal to let family power become a substitute for divine law.

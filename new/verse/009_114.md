## Sūrah al-Tawbah 9:114

> As for Abraham’s prayer for his father’s forgiveness, it was only in fulfilment of a promise he had made to him. But when it became clear to Abraham that his father was an enemy of Allah, he broke ties with him. Abraham was truly tender-hearted, forbearing.

**Expanded Commentary**

**Compassion Can Exist Before the Final Clarity**

Abraham prayed for his father's forgiveness because of a promise he had made. The prayer arose from compassion and from hope that the relationship might change. It was not a claim that idolatry was acceptable. While a person is alive, the possibility of guidance remains, and a loving relative may pray for that guidance and forgiveness.

The verse therefore protects tenderness from being treated as weakness. Abraham did not begin with cruelty toward his father. He spoke with care and gave the relationship room while the final hostility was not yet clear. A believer can love a relative who rejects faith and seek good for him without surrendering the truth of monotheism.

**Clarity Can Require a Change in Relationship**

When it became clear that the father was an enemy of Allah, Abraham broke ties with him in the religious sense. The change was not caused by a passing disagreement or personal irritation. It followed the recognition of a settled opposition to Allah. The verse teaches that affection has a boundary when a relationship is being used to fight truth.

Breaking ties does not require cruelty or revenge. It means refusing to treat the person's hostility as a harmless family difference. The believer may continue to act justly, fulfill ordinary rights, and avoid oppression while withdrawing religious solidarity and the forms of loyalty that strengthen hostility. Truth can alter a relationship without erasing the duty of justice.

**Tenderness and Forbearance Are Praised**

The verse calls Abraham tender-hearted and forbearing. These qualities remain after the relationship boundary is drawn. He is not praised for enjoying the break or for humiliating his father. He is praised as a servant whose heart feels deeply and who restrains himself from rash anger.

This is a model for difficult family situations. A person can refuse a parent's false teaching without insulting the parent. He can limit contact when harm requires it while avoiding a spirit of vengeance. Forbearance means carrying the pain without allowing pain to decide every word. Tenderness keeps firmness from becoming cruelty.

**A Promise Does Not Bind a Person to Falsehood**

Abraham's promise did not require him to continue praying after the truth about his father's enmity became clear. A promise made under one understanding can be limited by a later revelation of reality. The servant should keep his commitments, but he cannot use a promise to justify disobedience after its moral basis has changed.

This principle applies to loyalty, business, and family. Agreements are honored when they remain within justice. If a promise supports abuse or rebellion, the person seeks a lawful way to end it and accepts the cost. Keeping one's word is a virtue; keeping a word that requires betrayal is not faithfulness.

**The Verse Clarifies the Previous Boundary**

The verse explains why the believers cannot simply imitate Abraham's earlier prayer without considering the later outcome. His prayer was connected to a living relationship and a promise, while the final break came after clear enmity. The context matters. Scripture gives moral guidance through the whole story, not through one sentence removed from its development.

The believer reads the passage with both hope and discernment. Hope for guidance remains open while a person lives. Discernment recognizes when a relationship has become a channel for attacking faith. The two virtues support each other rather than compete.

**Love for Allah Gives Family Love Its Place**

Abraham's example shows that family love is real but not ultimate. The servant loves relatives for Allah's sake and refuses to let family loyalty become worship. If a relative supports truth, love becomes cooperation. If a relative opposes Allah, love may include prayer for guidance and just treatment, but it cannot include approval of the opposition.

The balanced heart is tender without being confused and firm without being hateful. It asks Allah for wisdom to know when to speak, when to step back, and how to preserve justice. Abraham's forbearance teaches the manner; his break with enmity teaches the limit.

The example also teaches a parent or child how to step back without becoming unjust. A person may stop participating in a relative's religious hostility while still returning a loan, caring for a genuine need, and refusing to spread private shame. The break is from allegiance to the opposition, not from every duty of human decency. Abraham's tenderness keeps the separation from becoming revenge, and his decision keeps tenderness from becoming agreement. The believer asks Allah for both qualities because either one without the other can produce harm.

The passage also protects a believer from two opposite errors in family conflict. One error is to call every disagreement enmity and cut people off in anger. The other is to call settled hostility a private family matter and keep giving it religious support. Abraham's example requires evidence and proportion. He remained tender and forbearing, but when opposition to Allah became clear he no longer treated the relationship as a basis for prayerful allegiance. The servant asks Allah to make his judgment calm, truthful, and free from the desire either to punish or to please people.

A tender heart may therefore need firm practices. It can limit conversations that repeatedly mock faith, refuse to fund hostile activity, and seek counsel before making a major family decision. These steps are not proof that love has died. They are ways to prevent love from being used against obedience. Abraham's forbearance shows that the heart can remain gentle while the relationship's religious boundary becomes clear. The believer leaves the final judgment to Allah and continues to ask for guidance for everyone who is still alive.

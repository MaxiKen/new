## Sūrah al-Ḥāqqah 69:32

> then tie them up with chains seventy arms long.

**Expanded Commentary**

**Seventy Cubits of Iron**

Thumma fī silsilatin dharʿuhā sabʿūna dhirāʿan — "then in a chain whose measure is seventy cubits." Silsilah denotes linked iron, chain proper. Sabʿūna, seventy, functions in classical idiom as "multitudes" — a large number known precisely to God alone, as al-Rāzī and al-Ṭabarī explain. Length runs to deliberate excess: seventy arms of binding per felon, fathoms of iron. Whether read as literal cubits or idiomatic many, the excess teaches identically: binding beyond escape, restraint beyond resistance. Readers should picture the coiled lengths honestly: iron enough for eternities. What seventy cubits bind, no strength breaks; what heaven measures, none unmeasures. Chains stand measured in advance; escape stands voided in advance. Length proclaims permanence more loudly than locks: bound long means bound lasting. The chain's measure is mercy's opposite — as gardens are boundless in bliss, fetters are boundless in bondage.

**Bound Together in Fetters**

Chains link souls plurally: the guilty bound together in fetters (paraphrased from 14:49). Collective punishment follows collective defiance — company kept in torment matching company kept in rebellion. Linked lines shuffle through fire: shared shame, mutual blame, conspirators' reunion in iron. Such joining inverts earthly fellowship precisely: gangs that schemed together get chained together, accomplices' pact honored in fetters. Mockers who laughed in companies wail in columns. Readers should choose company with eternity's chains in view: earthly fellowships escort their members past death. What gangs share in plotting, chains share in shaming. Befriend the praying and chains never link you; carouse with mockers and links await. Company kept predicts company chained with mathematical certainty. Walk with the righteous through life's streets; never shuffle with the chained through fire's. Fellowship is destiny's advance party — inspect your companions.

**Through the Body**

Early reports chill the blood: chains threaded through bodies — entering mouths, exiting sides, coursing through torsos. Al-Baghawī, Ibn Kathīr, and al-Rāzī preserve this threading: not merely wrists bound but bodies pierced, flesh strung on iron like beads. Such threading exceeds restraint and enters display: pain as passage, torment as thoroughfare. Bodies become chain-links themselves, anatomy converted to hardware. Horror defies picturing, yet the verse states it flatly, without flinching. Readers should flinch rightly and usefully: flinching now, through repentance, prevents threading then. What bodies did in defiance — strutting through markets, hoarding with clenched fists, reclining through prayers — bodies suffer in piercing. Flesh disciplined voluntarily now through fasting and prostration escapes threading penally then. Mortify appetites by choice; never be mortified by chains. Bodies face two threadings: veins of grace through worship, or iron through rebellion. Choose veins.

**Length Matches Long Defiance**

Seventy cubits answer decades of denial: long rebellion earns lengthy chain. Proportion governs requital — each year mocked adds links, each warning spurned adds length. Measure-for-measure, the Book's fixed arithmetic: punishment fitted to crime the way sentences fit felonies. Brief lives earn lengthy chains because eternity does the math — seventy years of defiance coil into seventy cubits, then uncoil forever. Readers should shorten chains while shortening remains possible: repentance unlinks what defiance linked, charity melts what miserliness forged. What rebellion lengthened link by link, obedience may shorten before forging ends. Chains stay cool and unfinished until death; then they temper eternally, unbreakable. Break links now through alms and tears; wear none then through pride and delay. Lengthen devotion daily; shorten chains correspondingly. The forge cools only for the living.

**The Sentence's Third Stage**

Seize (verse 30), scorch (verse 31), chain (verse 32) — three stages, sentence complete. Thumma...thumma paces the trilogy: then...then, procedure deliberate. No fourth stage is needed: bound, burned, and linked suffices for eternity. Such triads satisfy justice's display: capture (power), pain (requital), permanence (finality). Sentence stands structured; righteousness stands exhibited. Readers should hear completeness as finality: nothing omitted, nothing appealable. What three stages stage in sequence, one repentance could have voided entirely. Interrupt trilogies at their first line: refuse the opening scene and no sequel follows. Stages scheduled remain stages escapable — but only livingly, only now. Break sequence through return; skip every stage through surrender. The trilogy awaits all who persist; the cancellation awaits all who repent. Choose cancellation.

**Chains They Forged Themselves**

Deeds are links: habits harden into fetters link by link. Each withheld charity forged a link; each spurned warning added length; each persisted sin tempered the metal. Self-forged bondage accuses most: wardens merely deliver iron the sentenced smithied themselves. Habits unrestrained in life become fetters unrestrained in fire — freedom abused converts to freedom revoked. The forge operated daily: small refusals, repeated, became chain. Readers should audit their forges honestly: what links are forming this week? Break patterns before patterns harden; melt chains in repentance's furnace while metal still yields. What selves forge, selves wear — smiths cannot sue their anvils. Forge worship instead: prayer-links become garlands, charity-links crowns. Smiths choose their products daily. Hammer out prayers; never hammer chains. The anvil years are these.

**Iron Unbreakable, Mercy Unreachable**

Two irreversibilities face each other across death. In life, chains stayed breakable — habits unformed, repentance unworn, mercy within reach of every extended hand. In fire, iron stands unbreakable and mercy unreachable: the window that stood open for decades seals in a moment. Such reversal defines the verse's terror: permanence arrives exactly where flexibility reigned. Seventy cubits admit no file, no accomplice, no appeal. Eternity tempers what time left soft. Readers should convert timelines urgently: breakable now means broken never, if wills act. Every chain-link of habit can still melt in tears; every fetter of appetite can still snap in fasting. What iron threatens, mercy preempts — but only pre-mortem. Post-mortem iron answers pre-mortem delay. The chain's unbreakability is mercy's deadline made metal. Break habits; never wear chains. Deadlines pass; iron lasts.

**Reasons Follow Next**

Sentence pronounced across verses 30-32; grounds follow in verses 33-34: no faith in God, no feeding of the poor. Justice states its reasons plainly: disbelief (intellect's crime), uncharity (action's crime). Sentence first, then grounds — verdict explained, righteousness exhibited. Readers should learn causes to avoid effects: believe firmly, feed generously. What follows convicts doubly, mind and hand. The next verses state the sentence's twin grounds, disbelief and uncharity.

## Sūrah al-Nisāʾ 4:164

> There are messengers whose stories We have told you already and others We have not. And to Moses Allah spoke directly.

**Expanded Commentary**

**Messengers Whose Stories We Told You and Others We Did Not**

The verse states *wa rusulan qad qaṣaṣnāhum ʿalayka min qablu wa rusulan lam naqṣuṣhum ʿalayka* — and messengers, apostles, whose stories We have already narrated, cut, to you before, and messengers We did not narrate to you. *Rusulan* — messengers — was explained as the continuation of *“We have sent revelation to you as We sent to Noah and prophets after him… Jesus, Job, Jonah, Aaron, Solomon, David We gave Psalms”* (4:163) — now as *recounted and not recounted*. *Qad qaṣaṣnāhum ʿalayka min qablu* — whose stories We have told you before — was read as those named in Qur’an — the chain from Adam to Jesus — told as proof that God bears witness with His knowledge (4:166). *Wa rusulan lam naqṣuṣhum ʿalayka* — and messengers We did not narrate — was explained as that knowledge is with Him who is Fully Aware encompassing everything (4:126) — He sent many more than told, because to Him belongs whatever is in heavens and earth (4:126) — among all peoples He sent. The sūrah has shown that those well-grounded in knowledge believe in what was revealed to you and before (4:162) — even those messengers not recounted are among *mā unzila min qablika* — what was revealed before you (4:162) — and to believe accepting all, rejecting none (4:152) includes those not narrated, for their existence is asserted here. To know that some were not told was explained as humility: the Book does not claim to list all His sent, because to Him belongs whatever is in heavens and earth (4:126) — among all, He sent, and He knows. The command to be mindful (4:131) therefore rests not on counting messengers but on trusting the Sender who sent many whose names only He encompasses as *Muḥīṭ* (4:126).

**And to Moses God Spoke Directly**

The addition *wa kallama Allāhu Mūsā taklīman* — and God spoke to Moses — *kallama* — speaking — *taklīman* — directly, with speech — was explained with *taklīm* — speaking mouth to ear without intermediary, the honour that Moses is *Kalīmullāh* — Speaker with God. *Taklīman* — emphatic verbal noun — was read as absolute direct speech, not inspiration as to Noah (4:163) — *awḥaynā* — but *kallama*. Early reports linked this to Sinai where Mount was raised over them for covenant (4:154) and where thunderbolt struck them for demand greater than a book — *show God visible* (4:153) — yet to Moses He spoke directly without seeing. The sūrah has shown that *“there is no knowledge except following assumption”* where boasters claim killing Messiah (4:157) — true knowledge is where He speaks, as He spoke to Moses. The verse was also linked to *“Gardens for those who believe and do good”* (4:122) — Moses’ direct speech is the witness that revelation is real, against the People of the Book demand *bring down a book from heaven* (4:153) — revelation already spoken.

**A Verse That Follows Revelation Lineage With Limit of Narration**

Placed after *We have sent revelation to you as We sent to Noah and prophets after him… Psalms to David* (4:163), this verse follows lineage with limit of narration: some narrated, some not narrated, and Moses spoken to directly. The sūrah has shown that *“We have commanded those before you and you to be mindful”* (4:131) — revelation before you is many; to not narrate some is not to deny them, as the sūrah says *with Him are both rewards He hears and sees* (4:134) — He knows those sent. The sūrah that warns not to make distinction between messengers (4:150–152) says even those not narrated are included in not distinguishing: to accept all messengers includes the unnamed. The sūrah that warns not to take disbelievers as allies instead of believers (4:144) and describes lowest depth without helper (4:145) makes direct speech to Moses the helper for the weak: where you were told to refer disputes to God and Messenger (4:59), here is the Speaker who can be referred to.

**Direct Speech as Proof After Demand for Visible**

The phrase *kallama Allāhu Mūsā taklīman* was linked to the initial demand — *bring down a book from heaven* (4:153) and *show God visible* — demand for visible proof: Moses heard Him directly, yet his people still demanded visible, took calf after clear signs, broke covenant, so the Mount was raised (4:154). Direct speech thus did not suffice for those who demanded visible; to demand visible after speech is the *ẓulm* for which *ṭayyibāt* were forbidden (4:160). For the Community, direct speech is the proof that the Book was sent with truth — *to judge between people by what He shows* (4:105) — He shows by speaking directly and clearly.

**The Unrecounted Messengers and Comprehensive Faith**

The mention *rusulan lam naqṣuṣhum ʿalayka* — messengers We did not narrate — was explained to include future and distant peoples: revelation not told with story is still revelation believed in, as *“do not let desires deviate from justice”* (4:135) and *“have faith in God, Messenger, Books, angels, Last Day”* (4:136) make faith a guard. The sūrah has shown that even religious error before prayer is covered by pardon (4:43) where seeking is sincere; to not know a messenger’s name does not remove the command to accept all. The next verse will note the purpose of all messengers narrated and not — delivering good news and warnings so excuse is cut — and He remains Almighty, All-Wise (4:165). Elsewhere the Book ties that purpose to mercy, for He wishes to turn toward you in mercy (4:27).

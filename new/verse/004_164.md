## Sūrah al-Nisāʾ 4:164

> There are messengers whose stories We have told you already and others We have not. And to Moses Allah spoke directly.

### Expanded Commentary

**Known and unknown messengers**

Some messengers’ stories have been told and others have not. The Qur’an’s silence about a prophet’s name does not imply that no message reached a people. Divine guidance is wider than the narrative selected for this Book. This should make believers cautious about assuming that their historical map contains every person God sent.

Moses is singled out as one to whom Allah spoke directly, an honour of intimate communication that does not make him divine. Prophetic rank remains servanthood before God. The verse honours particular forms of revelation without turning them into rival sources of worship.

The existence of untold messengers also supports humility in interfaith conversation. Muslims can affirm God’s universal justice without inventing names or stories that revelation has not established.

**Humility about what is not recorded**

The existence of unnamed messengers teaches that absence from our scripture’s narrative is not evidence of absence from divine care. Muslims should avoid inventing details merely to fill gaps. It is enough to affirm God’s justice and honour the messengers He has named.

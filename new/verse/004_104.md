## Sūrah al-Nisāʾ 4:104

> Do not falter in pursuit of the enemy—if you are suffering, they too are suffering. But you can hope to receive from Allah what they can never hope for. And Allah is All-Knowing, All-Wise.

### Expanded Commentary

**Do not surrender to fatigue**

Believers are told not to falter in pursuing the enemy, especially after suffering. The reason is realistic: the enemy also suffers. The verse does not deny exhaustion; it prevents exhaustion from becoming a reason to give up a just defence while the threat remains.

**The different hope**

Believers hope for what their opponents cannot hope for—Allah’s reward, forgiveness, and the ultimate success of truth. This does not make pain pleasant or guarantee worldly victory. It gives suffering a horizon beyond the immediate contest and helps prevent despair.

The verse must be constrained by the surrounding law. Persistence is not blind pursuit after a conflict has ended, nor is it permission to harm civilians or ignore a peace offer. It is steadfastness under legitimate duty. Allah’s knowledge and wisdom ensure that courage must remain intelligent, measured, and morally directed.

**Endurance without recklessness**

The suffering of both sides does not mean that both causes are morally equal. It means pain alone cannot decide who is right. Seek relief, rotate exhausted people, and pursue peace when justice allows; steadfastness is not the same as refusing every prudent pause.

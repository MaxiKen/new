## Sūrah al-Nisāʾ 4:97

> When the angels seize the souls of those who have wronged themselves—scolding them, “What do you think you were doing?” they will reply, “We were oppressed in the land.” The angels will respond, “Was Allah’s earth not spacious enough for you to emigrate?” It is they who will have Hell as their home—what an evil destination!

### Expanded Commentary

**When oppression becomes a test of movement**

The angels question those who wronged themselves by remaining in a place where they could not live their faith or escape oppression. The response—“we were oppressed”—is not accepted when the people had a genuine ability to leave. The issue is not that victims are to blame for being oppressed; it is that a person with a viable route to safety and obedience may not choose permanent submission to a tyrannical environment merely for comfort.

**Migration as moral action**

The angels ask whether Allah’s earth was not spacious. Migration in the early Muslim context meant leaving persecution and joining a community where worship and safety were possible. The principle extends to seeking a lawful environment, changing an abusive situation, or moving away from systems that make obedience impossible, when one has capacity.

The verse requires careful application. Not every oppressed person can leave, and not every hardship is solved by travel. The judgment concerns those who had means and chose not to use them. Communities must not weaponize the verse against refugees; they should help the unable and address the oppressor.

**Escape from oppression requires means**

Migration may involve legal planning, family coordination, money, and the help of those who already live in safety. The verse does not romanticize displacement. It asks the person with a real path to use it and asks the secure community to make such paths possible for those who cannot create them alone.

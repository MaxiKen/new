## Sūrah al-Nisāʾ 4:130

> But if they choose to separate, Allah will enrich both of them from His bounties. And Allah is Ever-Bountiful, All-Wise.

**Expanded Commentary**

**But If They Separate — He Will Enrich Both**

The verse states *wa in yatafarraqā yughni Allāhu kullan min saʿatihī* — but if they two separate, become separate, part, God will enrich, make free of need, each from His bounty, His spaciousness, His abundance. *Yatafarraqā* — to separate, to split, to become distinct — was explained as divorce or separation after settlement was best (4:128) yet total inclining was prohibited (4:129) — where remaining together in hanging was not chosen and settlement could not hold. *Yughni* — to enrich, to suffice, to make independent — was read as God making each free of need where the other was expected to suffice, as the emigrant finds many havens and abundance where earth is spacious (4:100) and as the prayer shortened on travel is still writ at fixed time (4:103). *Kullan* — each, both, every one — was explained as each — the man and the woman — not only the husband upon whom maintenance lay nor only the wife who feared *nushūz* or *iʿrāḍ* (4:128); both are enriched. *Min saʿatihī* — from His spaciousness, His bounty, His amplitude — was explained as from His wide provision, the same *saʿah* as *murāgham kathīr wa saʿah* — many havens and spaciousness — promised to the emigrant (4:100), and as *to Him belongs whatever is in heavens and earth* (4:126, 4:131) whose spaciousness is the source. Early reports linked the verse to the household where settlement with waiver and reconciliation was offered yet separation was chosen: no shame in separation where enrichment is promised to both from Him.

**From His Bounties — Min Saʿatihī**

The phrase *min saʿatihī* was explained as from His amplitude that does not narrow when given, as the sūrah says *“ask God for His bounties; God has perfect knowledge of all things”* (4:32) where craving what others have is forbidden — here bounty is given to both from His bounty, not from taking between them. *Saʿah* — spaciousness — was read as breadth of provision, of dwelling, of tranquillity, of replacement — spouse, household, sustenance — as the sūrah says the single soul spread into countless men and women (4:1) showing that earth’s spaciousness holds replacements. The contrast with *shuḥḥ* — selfishness ever present with souls (4:128) — was noted: where settlement failed because *shuḥḥ* kept each grasping, separation enriched from *saʿah* removes grasping by giving each from Him, not by stripping one. Where the sūrah warns there is no good in most secret talks except charity, kindness, reconciliation (4:114), separation where graciousness and mindfulness remain is itself counted as good where it removes hanging — *muʿallaqah* (4:129) — and enrichment from *saʿah* is the broad good that follows.

**And God Is Ever-Bountiful, All-Wise**

The seal *wa kāna Allāhu Wāsiʿan Ḥakīmā* — And God is Ever-Bountiful, Ever-Encompassing, All-Wise — was explained as suitability. *Wāsiʿ* — Wide, Bountiful, Encompassing, Spacious — was read as whose bounty is wide enough to enrich both without diminishing, as He is Fully Aware encompassing everything (4:126), and as earth is spacious for emigration (4:97). *Ḥakīm* — All-Wise — was explained as wisdom that legislates both staying — settlement best (4:128) with prohibition of total inclining (4:129) — and leaving — enrichment from bounty where they separate — each in its wisdom, as He is All-Knowing, All-Wise where sin is earned (4:111) and where pursuit is without faltering (4:104). The sūrah has shown that God wills to make clear, to guide to noble ways, to turn in mercy, and is All-Knowing, All-Wise (4:26–28); to be *Wāsiʿ Ḥakīm* here is to be the One whose spaciousness is guided by wisdom: not enrichment that encourages frivolous divorce, but enrichment where settlement was tried and hanging remained. The two names together show that separation is not failure of law but a provision within law made by the Wide in wisdom.

**A Verse That Follows Limit With Provision**

Placed after *you will never be able to be just between wives even if keen, so do not incline entirely leaving the other suspended, yet if you set right and are mindful He is All-Forgiving* (4:129), this verse follows limit with provision. The sūrah’s ethic for the household mirrors its ethic for the battlefield: many havens where emigration is chosen (4:100), shortening where travel and fear constrict (4:101), guarding where the enemy wishes neglect for a sweep (4:102), remembrance in all postures (4:103) — here many enrichments where separation is chosen after trying settlement. The sūrah that warns not to deviate by following desires (4:27) and not to follow other than believers’ path (4:115) does not bind spouses to hanging as piety; it says separation enriched from His bounty is better than hanging, as settlement is better than *nushūz*. For the weak this is the lightest of provisions: where you feared rising or turning away and settlement with waiving was offered, if the tie still cannot be lived without total inclining, He will suffice both from His wide bounty.

**The Wide Who Can Replace Without Diminishing**

The verb *yughni* — to enrich, to suffice — was linked to *“ask God for His bounties”* (4:32) and to *“with Him are rewards of this world and the Hereafter”* (4:134): the enrichment promised here is not a vague hope but a writ from the Wide. Classical notes added that *kullan min saʿatihī* — each from His bounty — proves that provision is not zero-sum between the two who part: His *saʿah* gives to each separately, as *“to Him belongs whatever is in heavens and earth”* (4:126) — so that the woman enriched is not enriched at man’s expense before God, though inter-human rights remain judged fairly as the Book judges (4:105, 4:135). The next passage will anchor that bounty in universal ownership: to Him belongs whatever is in heavens and earth, We have commanded those given Scripture before you and you to be mindful, if you disobey to Him still belongs all, and He is Self-Sufficient, Praiseworthy (4:131).

## Sūrah al-Munāfiqūn 63:11

> But Allah never delays a soul when its appointed time comes. And Allah is AllAware of what you do.

**Expanded Commentary**

**The Answer to the Plea in the Verse Before**

The request quoted in 63:10 asked for a short extension. This sentence is its reply, and the reply is not a refusal delivered to one man. It is a law stated in general terms so that no one else will ask: Allah never delays a soul when its appointed time comes. The plea for more time is not met with a lesser amount of time. It is met with the announcement that the quantity was never negotiable.

The Arabic construction carries its own emphasis, an emphatic negation fastened to the noun for a living soul, so the sentence excludes every exception. Rich and poor, tyrant and orphan, prophet and hypocrite: the rule covers all of them, and it is spoken in the same Book that commands men to prepare for it.

The verse deals in the same breath with both halves of the human fear. Nobody dreads dying; people dread arriving without having done what they intended. The answer given here is that the arrival is fixed, which leaves the intention as the only variable a person still controls.

**A Term Set for Every Community, and Every Person**

The Qur'an states the doctrine of the appointed term repeatedly, and the repetition is itself part of the teaching. Sūrah al-Aʿrāf puts it in a form no one can miss: *“For each community there is an appointed term. When their time arrives, they can neither delay it for a moment, nor could they advance it.”* (7:34). Sūrah Yūnus speaks the same words to a Prophet being asked for a sign: *“Say, ˹O Prophet,˺ “I have no power to benefit or protect myself, except by the Will of Allah.” For each community there is an appointed term. When their time arrives, they cannot delay it for a moment, nor could they advance it.”* (10:49).

Sūrah al-Naḥl gives the reason the delay exists at all: *“If Allah were to punish people ˹immediately˺ for their wrongdoing, He would not have left a single living being on earth. But He delays them for an appointed term. And when their time arrives, they cannot delay it for a moment, nor could they advance it.”* (16:61). Sūrah Fāṭir repeats it almost word for word.

The term is thus a mercy before it becomes a boundary. It is the time granted for turning, held open by God for as long as He knows it should be held.

**No Soul Dies Except by the Will of God**

One further statement closes any remaining gap. Death does not happen to a person; it arrives at a time set by God, and it cannot be hurried by a sword or postponed by medicine: *“No soul can ever die without Allah's Will at the destined time. Those who desire worldly gain, We will let them have it, and those who desire heavenly reward, We will grant it to them. And We will reward those who are grateful.”* (3:145).

That verse makes the practical consequence explicit, and it is not fatalism. If the term cannot be shortened by prudence or extended by wealth, then the only thing left to decide is what a person wants, and the three options it names are desire for the world, desire for the reward, and gratitude. A man who believes the term is fixed is freed from the two fears that rule the rest of humanity: that someone else's decision will end his life, and that he can buy more of it.

The threat uttered by the hypocrites in 63:8 about expelling the weaker is answered by this same doctrine, quietly, at the end of the sūrah.

**Why the Sūrah Ends on This Sentence**

The sūrah opened with a witness that was false: men saying what they did not hold. It closes with an awareness that cannot be escaped: Allah is All-Aware of what you do. Between those two points it has shown what the difference produces in a community — oaths used as armour, provision mistaken for a weapon, honour treated as property, and a man begging for time.

The final word in Arabic is the divine name that denotes complete, informed awareness, one who knows a thing through and through and is never given news. Coming after a sūrah devoted to the gap between what people show and what they conceal, the name is the verdict on the gap itself. The pretence was never successful, because it was never unseen.

The previous sūrah, al-Jumuʿah, had closed on the same note of things being with God, and the two sūrahs are recited together in the Friday prayer, so the community hears both endings weekly.

**Nothing Was Hidden From the Record**

A person's doings are the subject of the final clause, and the word covers the small and the large: the alms given quietly, the prayer shortened when nobody was watching, the sentence spoken in a moment of anger. God's awareness of them is total, and the verse says so without offering a threat. It does not need one.

That is the soberest ending available to the sūrah, and the most useful. Men who feared every cry because they thought someone was closing in on them are contrasted with an awareness that was never a rumour. The believer reads the same clause and finds something else in it: the companion of the unobserved good deed, the one who saw the charity given when no one else could have known it was given.

The verse closes the chapter without promising the hypocrites anything or threatening the believers with anything. It states what is true, and lets each hearer locate himself in it.

**The Door That Is Still Open**

Everything the man in 63:10 asked for is unavailable after his term comes, and the sūrah has been careful to keep the reader on the near side of that boundary while he reads. That is why the same Book that closes the door so firmly holds it open so widely beforehand: *“Say, ˹O Prophet, that Allah says,˺ “O My servants who have exceeded the limits against their souls! Do not lose hope in Allah's mercy, for Allah certainly forgives all sins. He is indeed the All-Forgiving, Most Merciful.”* (39:53).

Before the term comes, no record is final and no distance is too great. After it comes, no extension is granted and no promise is accepted. The sūrah of the hypocrites ends with that boundary drawn and the ground on the reader's side of it still free.

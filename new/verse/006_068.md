## Sūrah al-Anʿām 6:68

> And when you come across those who ridicule Our revelations, do not sit with them unless they engage in a different topic. Should Satan make you forget, then once you remember, do not ˹continue to˺ sit with the wrongdoing people.

**Expanded Commentary**

**An Instruction About Where a Person Sits**

The verse gives a rule about physical presence, which is the kind of instruction the Book issues when a principle needs a form a community can actually follow. The command is not to argue with the mockers, not to retaliate against them, and not to leave the city on their account. It is to get up and go if the mockery continues, and the Book treats that act of withdrawal as the answer.

The rule is stated in the second person singular, and its addressee is the Prophet, but the sūrah of the Women makes the same statement to the whole community in the same words, saying that when they hear God's revelations being denied or ridiculed they should not sit in that company unless the conversation changes, or else they will be like those who mock (4:140). The two verses together give the instruction a general application without removing it from the particular scene that produced it.

**The Reason for the Rule**

The reason given in the sūrah of the Women is that sitting with the mockers makes the listener like them, and that reasoning explains the shape of the rule in this verse. Presence at a gathering is a form of participation in it, and the Book does not treat a person's silence as neutrality when the revelation of God is being treated as a joke.

That principle is worth stating in full because it is the hinge of the passage. The Book is not asking the believers to avoid people who disagree with them, since the same Book tells the Prophet to argue gracefully with the People of the Book (29:46), and it tells him that the message has been sent as a mercy to all people. What is forbidden is attendance at an assembly whose purpose is to degrade the revelation.

**The Exception and What It Reveals**

The command allows for the conversation to change: unless they engage in a different topic. That exception shows the point of the rule is not social separation. If the same people begin talking about ordinary matters, the believer may stay, which means the objection is to the activity and not to the persons.

That distinction has consequences for the way the community was to live in a city full of people who did not accept the message. The believers were to trade, marry, and negotiate with the same Makkans who contested the Prophet, and the Book's rule only removes them from the one setting where the revelation is turned into entertainment. The verse draws a line around an activity, and the line leaves everything else intact.

**"Should Satan Make You Forget"**

The verse then allows for the failure to observe the rule. If forgetfulness happens and the believer later remembers, he is told that the obligation returns immediately: do not continue to sit with the wrongdoing people. The mention of Satan as the cause of the lapse is the Book's way of naming the ordinary source of distraction without turning the lapse into a mortal fault.

The Book uses the same device in the story of Moses and his companion, who explains that only Satan made him forget the fish (18:63). The pattern is consistent: the Book acknowledges that human beings lose track of their obligations, and its remedy for the everyday kind of failure is to resume the duty as soon as it comes back to mind, without treating the interim as a catastrophe.

**Where the Believers Were Not to Sit**

The phrase about the wrongdoing people at the end of the verse connects the instruction to the sūrah's larger argument. The sūrah has described the wrongdoers across the previous sixty verses: those who turn others away from the message (6:26), who deny the signs (6:49), who respond to God's rescue by associating partners with Him (6:64), and who were warned about the punishments that could reach them from above, from below, and through their own divisions (6:65).

The command to leave that gathering is therefore a command to leave the company of a group the Book has just finished describing, and the leaving has a doctrinal weight. The person who walks away from the mockery is performing the same refusal the Prophet was told to make when he was instructed to declare his rejection of the gods of his people (6:56).

**What the Instruction Protects**

The rule protects two things, and the second is easily missed. It protects the revelation from being treated as material for idle entertainment, which is the concern the verse states directly. It also protects the person who stays, since the Book's reasoning in the parallel passage is that the listener becomes like the mockers, and the Book knows how quickly a person's sense of the sacred can be eroded by watching other people treat it lightly.

The next verse addresses a question the rule raises, which is what responsibility the believers carry for the mockers themselves. The answer given there releases them from liability while leaving the duty of advice in place, and the two verses together form the Book's complete instruction about this particular social situation.

**What Kind of Discussion the Verse Is About**

Commentators drew a line between the discussion that this verse forbids and the deliberation that the Book encourages. The Arabic verb used for the mockers' activity describes argument that is disparaging and disrespectful rather than inquiry, and al-Rāzī rejected the reading that would ban religious discussion in general, since the Book elsewhere commands people to reflect and to reason about the signs. The distinction is between a conversation that treats the revelation as a target and one that treats it as a question.

The Book's own usage supports that reading. The believers are described among the successful as those who avoid idle talk (23:3), and the sūrah of the Cattle states that God has made enemies for every prophet from among the devilish humans and jinn, whispering elegant words of deception, and then tells the Prophet to leave them and their deceit (6:112). What is being excluded is a specific kind of speech, not the exchange of views.

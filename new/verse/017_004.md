## Sūrah al-Isrāʾ 17:4

> **And We warned the Children of Israel in the Scripture, “You will certainly cause corruption in the land twice, and you will become extremely arrogant.**

**Expanded Commentary**

**Warning In Scripture About Two Corruptions**

Wa-qaḍaynā ilā banī isrā'īla fī al-kitābi la-tufsidunna fī al-arḍi marratayni — and We decreed, warned, to Children of Israel in Scripture, you will certainly cause corruption in land twice. Qaḍaynā from q-ḍ-y means to decree, decide, inform, warn, from same root as qaḍā', decree. Ilā banī isrā'īl, to Children of Israel. Fī al-kitāb, in Scripture, meaning Torah, or Preserved Tablet, or Scripture generally. La-tufsidunna, you will certainly cause corruption, from f-s-d, to corrupt, with la- and nun for emphasis, surely. Fī al-arḍi, in land, meaning Holy Land, Jerusalem and surroundings, or land generally. Marratayni, twice, two times. Al-Ṭabarī explains that Allah decreed in Torah that Children of Israel would cause corruption twice, meaning they would disobey, commit sins, kill prophets, and cause mischief. The warning was in Scripture to make them aware and to establish proof.

**Becoming Extremely Arrogant**

Wa-la-taʿlunna ʿuluwwan kabīran — and you will become extremely arrogant, exalt yourselves with great arrogance. Taʿlunna from ʿ-l-w, to be high, exalt, with la- and nun for emphasis, you will surely become high, arrogant. ʿUluwwan kabīran, great highness, great arrogance, from ʿ-l-w and k-b-r, great. Al-Ṭabarī says ʿuluwwan kabīran means great arrogance, tyranny, exceeding bounds, claiming greatness, oppressing others. Al-Rāzī explains that corruption and arrogance are linked: corruption in land is outward mischief, arrogance is inward attitude that leads to corruption. The Israelites' arrogance included claiming they are chosen and can do whatever, killing prophets, and attributing to Allah what does not befit.

**Why Two Corruptions Are Mentioned**

Two corruptions indicate that Israelite history has pattern: corruption, punishment, repentance, restoration, then corruption again, punishment again, then possibility of mercy. Al-Ṭabarī reports various opinions on what two corruptions were: some say first was killing of prophet like Shuʿayb or Zakariyyā, and second was killing of Yaḥyā and ʿĪsā's attempted killing; others say first was corruption that led to Nebuchadnezzar's destruction of Jerusalem, second was corruption that led to Roman destruction. Al-Qurṭubī says first corruption was when they killed prophets and were punished by Nebuchadnezzar who destroyed Temple and enslaved them, second was when they killed again and were punished by Romans under Titus. The exact identification differs, but principle is that they corrupted twice and were punished twice, showing divine law that corruption leads to punishment.

**The Purpose Of Warning**

Wa-qaḍaynā, We decreed, warned, indicates that warning was given in advance, in Scripture, so that they would avoid corruption, but they did not. The warning serves as proof that Allah knows future and warns, and that punishment is not arbitrary but after warning. The same pattern applies to Makkans: they are warned via Qur'an that if they cause corruption and become arrogant, they will be punished as Israelites were. The sūrah's mention of two corruptions therefore is both history and warning for current community. Al-Zamakhsharī notes that la-tufsidunna and la-taʿlunna with emphatic la- and nun indicate certainty: you will certainly corrupt and become arrogant, because Allah knows their nature, but warning still given to establish proof and to invite repentance.

**Corruption And Arrogance As Linked Sins**

Fasād, corruption, and ʿuluww, arrogance, are often paired in Qur'an as root sins that lead to destruction, as in 28:83: *That home of Hereafter We assign to those who do not desire exaltation in land nor corruption* (28:83). Corruption is outward harm to land and people, arrogance is inward claim of superiority that justifies corruption. For Israelites, corruption included killing prophets, shedding blood, and oppression; arrogance included claiming they are above law. For Makkans, corruption included persecuting believers and plotting against Prophet, arrogance included saying we are too great to follow human messenger. The verse therefore diagnoses that corruption and arrogance go together and lead to punishment.

**Linguistic Note On Qaḍā' And Fasād**

Qaḍaynā from q-ḍ-y means to decree, decide, judge, inform, from same root as qaḍā', decree, judgment. Fī al-kitāb, in Scripture, from k-t-b. La-tufsidunna from f-s-d, to corrupt, spoil, with la- for oath and nun for emphasis. Fī al-arḍi, in land, from a-r-ḍ. Marratayni, twice, dual of marrah, time, occurrence. Wa-la-taʿlunna from ʿ-l-w, to be high, exalt, with la- and nun. ʿUluwwan from ʿ-l-w, highness, exaltation. Kabīran from k-b-r, great. The verse's structure — We decreed to Children of Israel in Scripture you will corrupt twice and become extremely arrogant — with decree, address, location, two sins, creates warning that is both specific, twice, and general, corruption and arrogance, showing that divine knowledge includes future and that warning is given to prevent.

**How This Verse Prepares For Punishment And Mercy**

Verses 5-8 will detail punishment for first corruption, restoration, second corruption punishment, and possibility of mercy if they repent. This verse is introduction: you will corrupt twice and become extremely arrogant. It sets up history that will be detailed next, showing that divine law is consistent: corruption and arrogance lead to punishment via servants of great might who ravage homes, then restoration with wealth and offspring, then if you do good it is for yourselves, if you do wrong it is against yourselves, then second punishment with disgrace and Temple entry and destruction, then perhaps mercy if you repent, but if you return to sin, punishment returns, and Hell is confinement for disbelievers. The verse therefore is not only about Israelites but about universal law that applies to all communities, including Makkans.


**Why The Warning Is In The Scripture**

Fī al-kitāb, in the Scripture, indicates that warning was written, recorded, not only oral, to be preserved and to establish proof. Al-Qurṭubī says al-kitāb here can mean Torah, which contained warning about two corruptions, or Preserved Tablet, al-lawḥ al-maḥfūẓ, where all decrees written. In either case, warning in Scripture shows that Allah's knowledge and decree precede events, and that humans are warned via revelation. The Israelites had warning in their own Scripture, yet they corrupted, showing that having Scripture alone does not prevent corruption; following its commands does. The same applies to Makkans who have Qur'an as guide to most upright as verse 9 will say: having Qur'an does not prevent corruption unless followed.

**A Lesson On Arrogance As Root**

ʿUluwwan kabīran, great arrogance, is described as root of corruption. Arrogance makes person think he is above law, above accountability, so he corrupts. For Israelites, arrogance was thinking they are chosen people who will not be punished, or that they can kill prophets without consequence. For Makkans, arrogance was thinking they are too great to follow human messenger, or that their idols will protect them. The sūrah's repeated condemnation of arrogance, mustakbirīn, in previous chapter and in this chapter's verses about those who do not believe in Hereafter having denying hearts and being arrogant, shows that arrogance is central sin that prevents acceptance of truth. The remedy is humility, being humble with parents out of mercy as verse 24 will say, and not walking exultantly in land as verse 37 will say.

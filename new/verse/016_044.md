## Sūrah al-Naḥl 16:44

> **˹We sent them˺ with clear proofs and divine Books. And We have sent down to you ˹O Prophet˺ the Reminder, so that you may explain to people what has been revealed for them, and perhaps they will reflect.**

**Expanded Commentary**

**Clear Proofs And Divine Books**

Bi-l-bayyināti wa-l-zubur — with clear proofs and divine Books. Bayyināt from b-y-n means clear proofs, evidences, signs that make truth manifest. Zubur from z-b-r means Books, writings, scriptures, from same root as zabūr, Psalms. Al-Ṭabarī explains that previous messengers were sent with bayyināt, clear proofs like miracles, and zubur, Books containing guidance. The phrase bi- indicates they were sent with these as support, so that people cannot claim message was unclear. The previous verse said messengers were men inspired; this verse adds they were not without evidence: they had clear proofs and Books. This answers objection that human messengers have no proof. They did have proof: miracles like staff of Mūsā, and Books like Torah, Gospel, Psalms. The Makkans who demanded proof were told previous messengers had proof, and still many denied, so proof alone does not compel belief; willingness is needed.

**We Sent Down To You The Reminder**

Wa-anzalnā ilayka al-dhikra — and We sent down to you the Reminder, the dhikr. Dhikr here means Qur'an, Reminder, that reminds of Allah, of previous messages, of blessings, of accountability. Al-Ṭabarī says dhikr is Qur'an, called Reminder because it reminds of what was forgotten, of tawḥīd, of previous scriptures. The verb anzalnā, We sent down, indicates descent from above, as with rain and revelation earlier. Ilayka, to you, O Prophet, indicates that Qur'an was sent to Prophet ﷺ specifically to deliver. The phrase wa-anzalnā ilayka al-dhikra after mentioning previous messengers with bayyināt and zubur shows continuity: previous messengers had Books, you have Reminder that confirms and completes.

**So That You May Explain To People**

Li-tubayyina li-l-nāsi mā nuzzila ilayhim — so that you may explain, make clear to people what has been revealed for them. Tubayyina from b-y-n means to explain, clarify, make manifest. Li-l-nāsi, to people, generally, all people to whom message reaches. Mā nuzzila ilayhim, what has been revealed for them, what was sent down to them, meaning Qur'an and previous Books. Al-Ṭabarī explains that Prophet's role is to explain Qur'an to people, to clarify its meanings, rulings, stories, via his words, actions, and example. Al-Rāzī notes that tubayyina indicates that Qur'an needs explanation by Prophet, not only recitation, because it contains general principles that need detailing, as in prayer, zakat, etc. This is basis for Sunnah as explanation of Qur'an. The phrase li-tubayyina also shows that previous Books needed explanation, and Qur'an is explanation, and Prophet explains Qur'an, creating chain of clarification.

**Perhaps They Will Reflect**

Wa-laʿallahum yatafakkarūn — and perhaps they will reflect, think deeply. Yatafakkarūn from f-k-r, to reflect, think, as in verse 11 seal for those who reflect. Laʿallahum indicates hope, purpose: so that perhaps they will reflect. Al-Qurṭubī says reflection is needed to understand explanation and to see that what was revealed is for their benefit. The sūrah has repeatedly called to reflection, understanding, mindfulness, and now adds that Prophet's explanation is to facilitate reflection. The sequence is: Allah sends Reminder, Prophet explains, people reflect. Reflection is not automatic; it requires explanation and willingness. The verse therefore places Prophet as mediator who makes reflection possible by clarifying what was revealed.

**Why Explanation Is Needed**

Qur'an contains concise verses that need elaboration, as well as stories that need context, and rulings that need detail. Without explanation, people might differ on meaning, as they differed before, as verse 39 said resurrection will make clear what they differed on. In world, Prophet's explanation makes clear what they differed on, as verse 64 will say *We revealed Book only to clarify what they differed about* (16:64). Explanation is therefore mercy that prevents dispute. Al-Zamakhsharī notes that li-tubayyina li-l-nāsi indicates that explanation should be in language people understand, with wisdom and good exhortation as verse 125 will say. The Prophet ﷺ explained via speech, action, and character, making abstract principles concrete.

**Connection To Asking Knowledgeable**

Verse 43 said if you do not know, ask people of remembrance. Verse 44 says We sent messengers with clear proofs and Books and sent down to you Reminder so you may explain to people what was revealed and perhaps they will reflect. The connection is that asking knowledgeable is part of reflection, and Prophet is most knowledgeable to explain. Those who do not know should ask, and Prophet explains, so that they may reflect. The two verses together establish chain of knowledge: Allah sends, messengers deliver with proofs and Books, knowledgeable preserve, people ask, Prophet explains, people reflect. This chain ensures guidance is accessible.

**Linguistic Note On Bayyināt, Zubur, Dhikr**

Bayyināt from b-y-n means clear, manifest, from same root as bayān, clarification. Zubur from z-b-r means Books, writings, from zabara, to write. Dhikr from dh-k-r means remembrance, reminder, mention, from same root as dhikr, remembrance of Allah. Anzala from n-z-l means to send down. Tubayyina from b-y-n means to make clear, explain. Nuzzila from n-z-l means to be sent down, revealed. Yatafakkarūn from f-k-r means to reflect, think deeply. The verse's structure — with clear proofs and Books, and We sent down to you Reminder so you may explain what was revealed and perhaps they will reflect — moves from previous messengers' equipment to current messenger's Book to his role to purpose, showing continuity and progression in guidance, with reflection as intended outcome.


**A Lesson That Connects To Gratitude And Reflection**

This verse, like all verses in Sūrat al-Niʿam, ultimately calls to gratitude and reflection. Whether it describes emigration, patience, trust, human messengers, clear proofs, earth swallowing, or angels fearing, the purpose is to make reader recognize blessings and respond with thanks and reflection. Al-Qurṭubī notes that every warning in this sūrah is also a blessing because it warns before punishment, giving chance to change. Every promise is also a blessing because it encourages good. The sūrah weaves warning and promise, blessing and accountability, to cultivate heart that is mindful, humble, grateful. The reader who reflects on this verse with that mindset will find both knowledge and motivation to be among those who say goodness when asked what Lord revealed, and who are greeted with peace at death and enter Gardens.

**How This Verse Fits The Sūrah's Argument**

The sūrah moves from blessings to argument against shirk to warnings to examples to guidance. This verse fits in that flow by showing either reward for patience and trust, or principle of human messengers, or modes of punishment, or universal submission. Each verse adds layer to argument that Allah alone creates, sustains, knows, and deserves worship. The verse's placement after previous warnings and before next commands is deliberate: it shows consequence of previous attitude and prepares for next command. The reader is invited to see connection and to respond with gratitude, patience, trust, and asking knowledgeable, rather than with denial, arrogance, and plotting.

## Sūrah al-Naḥl 16:27

> **Then on the Day of Judgment He will humiliate them and say, “Where are My ˹so-called˺ associate-gods for whose sake you used to oppose ˹the believers˺?” Those gifted with knowledge will say, “Surely disgrace and misery today are upon the disbelievers.”**

**Expanded Commentary**

**Humiliation After Plotting**

Thumma yawma al-qiyāmati yukhzīhim — then on Day of Resurrection He will humiliate them, disgrace them. Yukhzīhim from kh-z-y means to humiliate, disgrace, expose shame. After describing worldly collapse of their building in verse 26, this verse moves to otherworldly humiliation. Thumma indicates sequence: first worldly punishment from unexpected direction, then Resurrection Day humiliation. Al-Ṭabarī explains yukhzīhim as exposing their falsehood and making their disgrace visible to all. In world, plotters may appear powerful, respected; on Day, their plotting is exposed and they are humiliated. The verse shows that divine justice has two stages: immediate and ultimate. Worldly collapse may be seen by some, but Resurrection humiliation will be seen by all. This is both warning and consolation: oppressors may seem to escape in world, but they will not escape humiliation on Day.

**Where Are My Associate-Gods?**

Wa-yaqūlu ayna shurakā'iya al-ladhīna kuntum tushāqqūna fīhim — and He will say, where are My partners for whose sake you used to oppose believers? Shurakā'ī, My partners, is said by Allah with irony: those you claimed as partners to Me, where are they? The phrase al-ladhīna kuntum tushāqqūna fīhim means those for whose sake you used to oppose, show enmity, split from believers. Tushāqqūna from sh-q-q means to oppose, to be on opposite side, to cause split. Al-Ṭabarī explains that idolaters opposed believers, argued, fought, and broke relations for sake of idols. They defended idols, sacrificed for idols, and made enmity for idols. On Day, they will be asked, where are those idols now? Can they help you against humiliation? The question is rhetorical to expose impotence. The idols are absent, dead, not knowing when they will be raised, as verse 21 said. The question will be asked to increase humiliation: you opposed truth for sake of what cannot even appear to help you.

**Those Gifted With Knowledge Speak**

Qāla al-ladhīna ūtū al-ʿilma — those given knowledge will say. Who are they? Al-Ṭabarī says they are angels, prophets, scholars, believers who were given knowledge in world and now witness outcome. Al-Rāzī says they are those who knew in world that shirk leads to disgrace, and now they declare what they knew. Their statement: inna al-khizya al-yawma wa-l-sū'a ʿalā al-kāfirīn — surely disgrace and misery today are upon disbelievers. Khizy means disgrace, humiliation, shame. Sū' means evil, misery, suffering. The phrase indicates that today, Day of Resurrection, disgrace and evil are specifically upon disbelievers, not upon believers. Those given knowledge announce this as witness, confirming divine justice. Their speech also contrasts with earlier speech of deniers who said ancient fables. Now those with knowledge speak truth that is visible: disgrace is upon disbelievers.

**Why Knowledgeable Speak On That Day**

The fact that those given knowledge speak on Day of Judgment shows that knowledge in world has value in Hereafter. Those who sought knowledge of tawḥīd, who reflected on blessings, who understood signs, will be given role as witnesses. Al-Qurṭubī notes that ūtū al-ʿilma includes both revealed knowledge and sound reasoning that leads to truth. Their statement is not new judgment but declaration of what is already happening: disgrace is upon disbelievers. They say it to affirm and to make it known. This also shows that knowledge is not only for personal salvation but for bearing witness. In world, scholars warned disbelievers; in Hereafter, they witness that warning was true. The verse thus honors knowledge and encourages seeking it.

**The Connection To Arrogance**

Verse 27 mentions humiliation, khizy, which is opposite of arrogance, istikbār, mentioned in verses 22-23, 29. Arrogant sought greatness in world by opposing believers for sake of idols; on Day they are humiliated. The Qur'an often pairs arrogance in world with humiliation in Hereafter, as in 40:76: *Enter gates of Hell to abide therein; evil indeed is abode of arrogant* (40:76). The question *Where are My partners?* also humiliates because it exposes that what they were arrogant for is nothing. They were proud of idols, proud of opposing believers, proud of their plotting. Now idols absent, opposition failed, plotting collapsed. Humiliation is therefore fitting recompense: they sought honor through false gods, they receive disgrace because false gods cannot honor.

**Translation Note On So-Called And Believers**

Translators add ˹so-called˺ before associate-gods to indicate that shurakā' are not true partners but claimed partners. The Arabic shurakā'ī, My partners, is in Allah's speech with irony, as if Allah says you called them My partners, where are they? The bracket clarifies irony. Similarly, ˹the believers˺ is added to explain fīhim, for whose sake you opposed, meaning you opposed believers for sake of idols. The Arabic tushāqqūna fīhim could mean you opposed each other concerning them, or you opposed believers concerning them. Tafsīr explains it as you showed enmity toward believers for sake of idols, you split from believers because of idols. The brackets help English reader understand that opposition was directed at believers, not just abstract. The verse's two parts — divine question and knowledgeable answer — create a courtroom scene: Judge asks where are partners, witnesses declare disgrace upon disbelievers, leaving no doubt about verdict.

**The Courtroom Imagery And Its Effect**

The scene in this verse is judicial. Allah questions, those given knowledge testify, disgrace is declared upon disbelievers. This mirrors worldly courts where accused is asked about accomplices and witnesses testify. Here accomplices are idols who cannot appear, and witnesses are those who knew truth in world. Al-Rāzī notes that this courtroom imagery is meant to make listeners imagine themselves in that position before it happens, so they may change course. The question *Where are My partners?* is asked not because Allah does not know, but to make the accused admit their absence. The testimony *disgrace today is upon disbelievers* is not new information to Allah but declaration for all to hear. The combined effect is to show that Day of Judgment is public, not private, and humiliation is part of punishment. Those who sought status through idols will lose status publicly.

**How Knowledge Becomes Witness**

Those given knowledge in world become witnesses in Hereafter because knowledge entails responsibility to convey. The Qur'an says *Thus We have made you a middle community that you may be witnesses over people* (2:143). In this verse, those given knowledge witness that disgrace is upon disbelievers, confirming that warnings given in world were true. This encourages seeking knowledge not only for personal benefit but for role as witness for truth. Al-Qurṭubī says ūtū al-ʿilma includes prophets, angels, and scholars who taught tawḥīd. Their testimony on that Day will be against those who called revelation fables and plotted. The verse therefore honors knowledge and warns that ignoring knowledgeable is ignoring future witnesses against you.

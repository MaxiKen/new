## Sūrah al-Baqarah 2:218

> Surely those who have believed, emigrated, and struggled in the Way of Allah—they can hope for Allah’s mercy. And Allah is All-Forgiving, Most Merciful.

**Expanded Commentary**

**The Promise Stated**

The verse states that those who believed, emigrated, and struggled in the way of God may hope for His mercy, and it closes with the names that describe God as forgiving and merciful. The sentence follows the warning of the previous verse about renunciation, and it supplies the promise that answers it.

The Book's method of following a warning with a promise appears throughout the sūrah, and the reader is given the two outcomes together. What the sentence establishes is that the three actions it names belong together, and the community's teachers read the list as a description of the life the passage has been calling for.

**The Three Descriptions**

The sentence names belief, emigration, and struggle, and the order moves from the inward acceptance to the departure from home and then to the effort the community undertakes. The community's scholars noted that the three were the marks of the community in its earliest period, when the believers left Makkah and defended themselves in Madinah.

The list is not a set of ranks but a single description of the community's response to the message, and the reader is shown what the faith required of those who accepted it. The community's teachers used the verse when they spoke about the cost of the migration, and they noted that the three terms describe the conduct of the people the passage has been addressing.

**The Comfort of the Companions**

The commentaries report that the verse was revealed to reassure the members of the scouting party whose expedition had raised questions, and they describe the companions' fear that they would receive no reward for what they had done. The sentence answers that fear with the promise of mercy.

The report shows how the Book's assurance reached the people whose situation had prompted it, and the community's teachers read the passage as a whole in the light of the incident. Readers are given the account of a community whose members worried about the value of their actions, and the sentence's promise supplies the answer they needed.

**The Meaning of Hope**

The commentators discussed the word rendered as hope, and they explained it as the attitude with which the believers approach their Lord. Some read the sentence as a statement that the reward is not a matter of certainty but of the divine will, and others read it as a description of what the faithful are promised.

The community's teachers drew from the discussion the balance between hope and fear that the Book commends elsewhere, and they noted that the verse's phrasing expresses a confidence that is not presumption. The reader is shown that the Book presents the reward as something sought from God rather than as a debt owed to the person who performs the deeds.

**The Names at the End**

The sentence closes with the description of God as forgiving and merciful, and the community's scholars read the pair as the ground of the promise that preceded it. The attributes answer the doubts that the companions had expressed, and the reader is shown that the verse's assurance rests on the character of the one who gives it.

The Book's practice of ending a passage about the community's conduct with a description of God appears throughout the sūrah, and the sentence here follows the pattern. The community's teachers read the ending as the completion of the promise, and the reader is left with the names that the sentence supplies.

**The Passage's Movement**

The sentence closes the short section that began with the question about fighting in the sacred months, and it follows the warning about renunciation with the promise made to those who held firm. The Book has answered the community's question, described the offences of its opponents, and warned it against abandoning its faith; the present sentence names the conduct that secures the mercy of God.

The passage that follows turns to the questions about wine and gambling and about the treatment of orphans, and the reader is carried from the account of the community's struggle to the regulation of its ordinary affairs. The transition shows that the Book's instructions address the whole of the community's life, and the sentence in this place supplies the promise that sustains it in the harder part.

**Emigration and Its Rank**

The sentence names three acts and joins them in a single description, and the community's teachers read the sequence as the account of a life given to the cause. Faith is named first, because the acts that follow rest on it, then the departure from one's home, then the effort in the field.

The reports describe the families who left Makkah with nothing and the welcome they received in Madinah, and the community preserved the names of those who emigrated as a rank of its own. The jurists later discussed the standing of emigration when the community had established its rule, and many held that the duty remains for the believer who cannot practise the faith openly in his own land.

The sentence closes with the language of hope rather than certainty, since it states that they hope for the mercy of God. The exegetes noted the choice of the word, because the greatest deeds of the community are presented in the Book together with the trust that the outcome belongs to God.

**What the Verse Establishes**

The sentence establishes the three marks of the community's response, the hope that belongs to those who bear them, and the attributes of God on which the promise rests. The community's teachers used the verse when they encouraged steadfastness in difficult periods, and the reader is left with the account of a community whose effort is met by the mercy it seeks.

The community remembered those who emigrated as the first of its people, and the sentence supplied the description by which their standing was recorded. The hope the verse names has been read as the disposition of those who act without claiming a right to the reward, and the closing attributes complete the assurance the sentence gives.

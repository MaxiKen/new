## Sūrah al-Tawbah 9:36

> Indeed, the number of months ordained by Allah is twelve—in Allah’s Record since the day He created the heavens and the earth—of which four are sacred. That is the Right Way. So do not wrong one another during these months. And together fight the polytheists as they fight against you together. And know that Allah is with those mindful ˹of Him˺.

**Expanded Commentary**

**Time Belongs to Allah Before It Belongs to People**

Verse 36 establishes the calendar as part of Allah's created order. There are twelve months in Allah's Record from the day He created the heavens and the earth. The statement is not merely a calculation for scheduling. It says that time has an order given by the Creator, and human communities must not rearrange sacred distinctions to serve their desires.

The four sacred months are traditionally identified as Dhū al-Qaʿdah, Dhū al-Ḥijjah, Muḥarram, and Rajab. Three are consecutive and one is separated. The arrangement was known in Arabian practice, though its meaning had been manipulated. The verse restores the months to the order Allah appointed. Sacred time creates a pause in which violence, injustice, and reckless desire are restrained. It is a public reminder that even conflict must not consume every season.

**Sacred Time Protects Human Beings**

The command not to wrong yourselves during these months shows the purpose of the designation. Sacred time is not a superstition attached to numbers. It protects life and gives people a period in which travel, pilgrimage, trade, and reconciliation can occur with reduced fear. To violate the months is to harm the social trust that the calendar was designed to support.

The phrase do not wrong yourselves can also be read broadly, since wrongdoing damages the soul of the wrongdoer as well as the victim. Sin during any month is serious, but transgression in a time set apart for restraint carries a special contradiction. A person has been given a public aid toward self-control and chooses to break it. The verse trains the community to treat time as a moral environment rather than as empty space.

**Manipulating the Calendar Manipulates Obligation**

The following verse describes nasīʾ, the postponement or rearrangement of sacred months. The practice allowed people to preserve the number four while moving the prohibition from one month to another. That kind of manipulation protects the appearance of obedience while removing its cost. A group can claim to honor the sacred order and still choose which months will be inconvenient for its interests.

Verse 36 calls the fixed order the upright religion. The point is not that calendars are inherently holy in every detail. It is that divine law cannot be edited by a community that wants the benefit of a command without its restraint. The same danger appears whenever people reinterpret a clear obligation only when it blocks profit, politics, or pleasure. The calendar is a visible example of a general moral disease.

**Together Against Aggression Does Not Erase Taqwā**

The verse tells the believers to fight the polytheists together as they fight the believers together. This language concerns a collective hostile force and the need for communal unity. It does not turn every non-Muslim person into an enemy. The surrounding treaty laws remain relevant, and the command to fight is directed at an organized group that is fighting as a group.

The instruction to unite also prevents fragmented defense. A community cannot respond to a coordinated threat through private rivalries. Yet the final clause says Allah is with those mindful of Him, not simply with those who are numerous or aggressive. Unity is valuable only when it remains under taqwā. A united community that abandons justice has not fulfilled the verse. The believers must be together in purpose and together under limits.

**The Sacred Months and the Ethics of War**

The verse does not create a world in which war is impossible. It establishes periods in which aggression is specially restrained, while allowing response to an enemy that violates the restraint. Jurists debated how the sacred-month rule interacts with fighting initiated by the other side. The underlying principle is consistent: sacred time is a serious norm, but it cannot be used to hand a hostile aggressor a permanent shield for attack.

This balance resembles the treaty rules earlier in the sūrah. Peace is the norm when the other side honors it. A party that attacks within a protected arrangement changes the legal situation. Even then, the believer's response is governed by proportion and Allah-consciousness. The sacred order is not discarded because one group abuses it; it is restored by refusing to let abuse determine the believer's character.

**A Calendar for the Inner Life**

For modern believers, verse 36 invites attention to the moral use of time. Sacred seasons can interrupt habits and create space for worship, reconciliation, and restraint. The believer should not treat Ramadan, the pilgrimage months, Fridays, or other appointed times as cultural decorations. They train the soul by giving it rhythms that do not come from the market.

The verse also asks whether people manipulate time to avoid obedience. A person may postpone charity, prayer, repentance, or a difficult duty until the deadline loses emotional force. The act resembles nasīʾ: the appearance of compliance remains while the obligation is moved out of reach. Allah's companionship belongs to the mindful, those who let His order govern their calendar rather than making their calendar govern Him. Sacred time is received as support for obedience, never as a permission to postpone it. Its rhythm teaches the community to stop, remember, and repair before ordinary activity begins again. A sacred season is successful when it changes conduct after it ends. The calendar has done its work when restraint becomes a habit rather than a temporary performance.

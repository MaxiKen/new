## Sūrah al-Baqarah 2:221

> Do not marry polytheistic women until they believe; for a believing slave-woman is better than a free polytheist, even though she may look pleasant to you. And do not marry your women to polytheistic men until they believe, for a believing slave-man is better than a free polytheist, even though he may look pleasant to you. They invite ˹you˺ to the Fire while Allah invites ˹you˺ to Paradise and forgiveness by His grace. He makes His revelations clear to the people so perhaps they will be mindful.

**Expanded Commentary**

**The Prohibition Stated**

The verse forbids marriage to those who associate partners with God until they believe, and it applies the prohibition in both directions. The sentence follows the rules about orphans and begins the section on marriage, and it states the standard that governs the community's unions.

The instruction concerns the religion of the parties rather than their social standing, and the community's teachers read the sentence as a statement about the household that a marriage creates. Readers are shown that the Book treats the union of two people as a matter that touches the faith of the community, and the sentence supplies the rule before it supplies the comparison that supports it.

**The Comparison Drawn**

The sentence states that a believing servant is better than a free person who associates others with God, even though the latter may appear attractive, and the comparison is repeated for the two directions. The Book's use of the comparison places the community's values against the standards of status that the society observed.

The community's scholars noted that the sentence reverses the ranking that the people of the time took for granted, since a servant had no standing and a free person of the leading families had much. Readers are shown that the Book measures a person by his faith, and the sentence's repetition in both forms makes the standard apply to men and women alike.

**The Reason Given**

The sentence states that those who associate others with God invite people to the Fire while God invites them to the Garden and to forgiveness, and the statement supplies the reason for the prohibition. The community's teachers read the clause as a description of the direction that each party's influence takes.

The Book's treatment of the household elsewhere describes the influence that family members have on one another, and the community's scholars used the sentence when they discussed the importance of the religion in the choice of a spouse. The account describes what the marriage is understood to bring, and the sentence places the matter within the invitation that the passage has described.

**The Reports and the Law**

The commentators discussed the relation between the prohibition and the permission given in a later chapter for marriage to the women of the People of the Book, and some held that the later passage restricted the earlier ruling while others maintained that the prohibition had never applied to those communities. The community's jurists built the law on the combination of the two passages.

The reports show the community's scholars working through the question, and the differences among them reflect the different ways of reading the two texts. The account describes a ruling that occupies a settled place in the community's law, and the sentence in this place supplies the general standard from which the exceptions were drawn.

**The Instruction to Remember**

The sentence closes with the statement that God makes His signs clear to people so that they may remember, and the phrase echoes the closings of the surrounding verses. The community's teachers read the ending as the invitation that accompanies the rulings of the passage.

The Book's practice of attaching a reminder to its commands gives the reader the disposition in which they are received, and the sentence here follows the pattern. The passage shows that the instruction about marriage belongs to the body of guidance the sūrah has been setting out, and the passage continues with the rules about the relations between spouses.

**The Household and the Faith**

The standard the sentence establishes concerns the faith of the person who is to be joined in marriage, and the community's jurists worked out its application to the households of the people among whom they lived. The passage forbids the union with those who associate partners with God, and a later chapter permits marriage with the chaste women of the People of the Book, so that the teachers read the two texts together.

The earlier community in Makkah had met the difficulty directly. Some of the believers had spouses who remained with the idols, and the separation that followed the emigration divided families whose children were caught between them. The ruling answered that situation by naming the faith as the ground on which the household is built.

The sentence also supplies the reasoning of the Book's instruction, since it describes the direction in which each party invites the other. The persuasion of the household works on a person through the ordinary affections of the family, and the verse supplies the measure by which the believer weighs the alliance before it is made.

**What the Verse Establishes**

The sentence establishes the prohibition, the standard by which a spouse is to be chosen, the reason for the rule, and the invitation to remember that closes it. The community's teachers used the verse when they spoke about the foundations of the household, and the reader is shown that the Book's legislation on marriage begins with the faith that the two parties share. The passage that follows treats the physical relations of the married couple, and the reader is carried from the choice of a spouse to the conduct of the life that follows it.

The community's practice followed the sentence in the unions it contracted, and the teachers used it when they answered those who asked about the marriages of their relatives. The ruling concerns the bond of marriage rather than the treatment owed to the neighbor or the guest, and the teachers kept the two apart in their explanations of the passage. The closing clause leaves the community with the invitation to remember what the sentence has established.

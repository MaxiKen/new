## Sūrah al-Aʿrāf 7:58

> The fertile land produces abundantly by the Will of its Lord, whereas the infertile land hardly produces anything. This is how We vary ˹Our˺ lessons to those who are thankful.

**Expanded Commentary**

**Two Kinds of Ground**

The verse divides the land into two kinds and states the different results of the same rain. The good land brings forth its produce by the permission of its Lord, and the poor land brings forth what it brings forth with difficulty and little of it. The verse follows the account of the rain that revives a dead land (7:57), and it supplies the qualification that the rain alone does not determine the harvest.

The image in front of us turns the natural observation into a statement about reception. The rain in the previous verse was presented as the mercy of God that descends on the earth, and the verse in front of us states that the effect of the descent depends on the condition of what receives it. The sūrah's argument has been built on the signs of the created order, and the two verses together complete the picture of the sign.

**The Parable of the Soil and the Heart**

The commentary tradition reads the two kinds of land as the two responses to the revelation, and the sūrah's own arrangement supports the reading, since the accounts of the prophets and their peoples follow immediately. The rain is the message that descends on every community, and the harvest is the response that each community produces. The difference between the good land and the poor land is therefore the difference between the heart that receives the warning and the heart that does not.

The Book uses the same image of the soil in its parables of the word, and the sūrah of Abraham states that a good word is like a good tree whose root is firm and whose branches reach the sky, while an evil word is like an evil tree uprooted from the surface of the earth (14:24–26). The sūrah of the Thunder states that the water flows in the valleys according to their measure and carries the foam on its surface (13:17). The verse in front of us belongs to the same family of comparisons.

**The Signs Varied for the Thankful**

The verse closes with the statement that God varies the signs for people who give thanks, and the Book uses the phrase in its accounts of the revelation. The sūrah's own earlier verses have stated that the Book is explained with knowledge (7:52), and the sūrah of the Cattle uses the language of varying the signs in its descriptions of the created order. The verse in front of us states that the signs are presented in more than one form so that the reader has more than one route to the same conclusion.

The audience of the sūrah was addressed through the sky, the rain, the soil, its own history, and the recitation of the messenger, and the phrase in front of us accounts for the variety. The commentary tradition notes that the signs are varied for the people who give thanks rather than for all people, since the recognition of the variety requires a disposition toward gratitude. The sūrah's readers are thereby told what the signs are for.

**The Comparison With the Gospel Parable**

The comparison between the two kinds of soil and the two responses to the word appears in the Gospels as well, where the parable of the sower describes seed falling on the path, on rocky ground, among thorns, and on good soil that produces a harvest. The sūrah's version of the comparison is brief and does not describe the intermediate cases, and the difference between the two texts is instructive: the Book's account in front of us divides the world into the land that responds and the land that does not.

The Book's version keeps the attention on the giver of the rain as well as the receiver of it, since the good land is described as producing by the permission of its Lord. The Book's practice of attributing the outcome to God while holding the person responsible appears throughout its parables, and the verse in front of us states both halves in one sentence.

**The Section That Follows**

The verse closes the passage on the created signs and opens the long sequence of the prophets and their peoples that runs through the middle of the chapter. The sūrah's introduction states that the accounts describe the destruction of communities that rejected their messengers, and the comparison in front of us supplies the frame for reading them. Each community received the rain of the warning, and each produced the harvest that its condition allowed.

The Book's arrangement of the two themes side by side is a feature of the sūrah's method. The signs of the created order demonstrate the power of God, and the accounts of the prophets demonstrate the response of the communities to His message; and the verse in front of us supplies the transition between the two sections.

**Where the Verse Sits**

The verse stands fifty-eighth in the chapter, and it completes the passage on the rain and the land that began with the command to call upon God with hope and fear (7:56). The verse that follows begins the account of Noah and his people (7:59), and the sequence of the prophets continues from there to the account of Moses and the people of Israel. The verse in front of us supplies the image that governs the reading of those accounts.

The reader who has been shown the rain, the dead land, and the two harvests is now prepared for the history that follows. The messengers are sent to communities that have their own soil, and the sūrah's next verses state what the first of those communities did with what it received.

**The Transition to the Next Verse**

The next verse begins the account of Noah, whom God sent to his people with the call to worship God alone and with the warning of the punishment of a tremendous day (7:59). The parable of the two kinds of land in front of us gives way to the first of the peoples, and the account of the messengers and their communities begins.

The two verses together start the sūrah's history. The signs have been stated, the difference between the two kinds of ground has been drawn, and the first messenger is sent to the first people; and the verses that follow record the answer of the notables and the outcome of the community that refused.

## Sūrah al-Nisāʾ 4:30

> And whoever does this sinfully and unjustly, We will burn them in the Fire. That is easy for Allah.

### Expanded Commentary

**A short legal warning with a long reach**

Allah warns that whoever commits the prohibited acts unjustly will be entered into the Fire. The word “that” gathers the preceding offences: taking wealth through falsehood, consuming another’s property, and participating in self-destruction. The warning does not make every financial mistake an eternal sentence; it emphasizes deliberate injustice and the seriousness of treating another person’s right as an opportunity.

**Why material wrongdoing is spiritual wrongdoing**

The verse prevents a modern misconception that business is morally neutral while worship is sacred. A fraudulent contract can be an act of disobedience just as real as neglecting prayer. Wealth shapes food, housing, education, health, and family security; corrupting its circulation harms bodies and futures. The Fire image makes visible the hidden human cost behind a transaction that may look profitable on a ledger.

**Mercy does not erase restitution**

Allah’s mercy appears in the surrounding chapter through repentance and repair. The person who has taken another’s wealth must stop, regret, return, and seek reconciliation. A private prayer for forgiveness cannot substitute for restoring a stolen right when restoration is possible. At the same time, victims are not required to deny their injury in order to appear forgiving; justice and mercy can coexist.

The final description of Allah as Merciful keeps the threat from becoming despair. It calls the wrongdoer to act before the consequence becomes final and calls the community to create markets in which consent is real, records are reliable, and the vulnerable can obtain redress.

**The social cost of private greed**

Financial injustice can appear to concern only two parties, but it spreads through families and institutions. Debt, fraud, and self-destruction consume trust and make ordinary cooperation expensive. The verse’s warning calls communities to create channels for advice, relief, and redress before a private wrong becomes a public collapse.

## Sūrah al-Nisāʾ 4:116

> Surely Allah does not forgive associating ˹others˺ with Him ˹in worship˺, but forgives anything else of whoever He wills. Indeed, whoever associates ˹others˺ with Allah has clearly gone far astray.

**Expanded Commentary**

**God Does Not Forgive Association — But Forgives What Is Less**

The verse states *inna Allāha lā yaghfiru an yushraka bihī wa yaghfiru mā dūna dhālika li man yashāʾ* — God does not forgive that others be associated with Him, but forgives what is below that — anything less — for whomever He wills. The same sentence was given at 4:48, and its return here was explained as deliberate closure after defiance after guidance and following other than believers’ path whose end is burning (4:115). *An yushraka bihī* — that association be made with Him — was read as setting a partner beside God in worship, the *shirk* repeatedly defined where limits were set and the caller was warned not to go to extremes regarding faith (4:171) and where *“He does not forgive associating”* anchors the whole sūrah’s monotheism. *Mā dūna dhālika* — what is below that, less than that — was explained as every sin besides association: error, sin, throwing sin on the innocent (4:111–112), deceit, advocacy for deceit, even intentional killing where repentance might yet be sought. The sūrah has shown that whoever earns sin earns only upon himself (4:111) and whoever commits evil then seeks finds Him All-Forgiving (4:110); the limit is that association, persisted in without repentance, is not covered. The seal *li man yashāʾ* — for whom He wills — was read as divine will in forgiveness, not human claim: *“Divine grace is neither by your wishes nor those of the People of the Book”* (4:123), but by His will after justice to His measure.

**Indeed Whoever Associates Has Gone Far Astray**

The reason *wa man yushrik bi Allāhi fa qad ḍalla ḍalālan baʿīdā* — indeed whoever associates with God has clearly, far, gone astray, wandered far astray — was explained as distance in path. *Ḍalla ḍalālan baʿīdā* — strayed a far straying — was read as the opposite of the straight path the Book guides to (4:68) and of the noble ways He wills to make clear (4:26). The sūrah has said that the wishers who deviate wish you deviate entirely (4:27), that false judges wish the deviant far path (4:60), and that to defy the Messenger and follow other than believers’ path is to be left to stray (4:115); association is the furthest of those distances because it makes an equal beside the Single Source who created from a single soul (4:1) and to whom belongs whatever is in heavens and earth (4:126). Early notes linked *baʿīd* — far — to remoteness of return: the one who thinks God’s forgiveness is bound by wish — *“neither by your wishes nor those of the People of the Book”* (4:123) — and yet associates, has gone so far that return requires abandoning the partner, not merely seeking pardon while holding it.

**A Verse That Follows Threat of Burning With the Unforgivable Root**

Placed after *“We will let him pursue what he chose and burn, evil destination”* (4:115), this verse names the root that makes burning not arbitrary: association is the one sin not forgiven without repentance because it denies the very One to whom forgiveness is sought. The sūrah has traced *shirk*’s allies — devouring wealth unjustly (4:29), envy of God’s bounties (4:54), believing in *jibt* and *ṭāghūt* (4:51) — and here gathers them: to associate is to have gone far, while the helpless hoped to be pardoned (4:99) and the emigrant finds many havens (4:100) remain within forgiven range. The repetition from 4:48 to 4:116 was explained as bracketing the judgment passage: the first came before the limits of inheritance and the second after the law of deceit and advocacy, both saying: the framework — trusts returned, disputes referred, prayer guarded, judgment by Book — assumes the One to whom referral is made. To associate while referring to Him is the contradiction the Book in truth came to remove (4:105, 4:105).

**Forgives What Is Less — But Only for Whom He Wills**

The clause *wa yaghfiru mā dūna dhālika li man yashāʾ* was explained as opening hope without opening presumption. The sūrah has shown that intentional killing earns Hell with anger and curse yet the door of seeking remains (4:93, 4:110), that throwing sin on the innocent earns double burden yet seeking remains, that defying the Messenger earns burning yet seeking before that end remains. *Li man yashāʾ* — for whom He wills — was read to prevent the wishful — *“neither by your wishes”* (4:123) — from counting forgiveness as debt. The sūrah pairs this will with knowledge and wisdom: *All-Knowing, All-Wise* where earning stays on self (4:111), *All-Hearing, All-Seeing* where trusts are judged (4:58). Forgiveness below association is therefore not automatic, nor random, but will governed by knowledge of repentance, of plotting by night, of advocacy, of throwing. For the weak for whom burdens were lightened, the verse is steadying: what is below association remains within the spaciousness that Earth’s havens image (4:100) — many exits — yet association removes the earth itself, because the earth’s spaciousness is *His* earth (4:97).

**The Far Straying After Clear Guidance**

The description *ḍalālan baʿīdā* — far astray — was linked to *“after guidance became clear to them”* (4:115): going far after being shown nearness is the furthest. The sūrah has said that reflecting on the Qur’an would show no inconsistency had it been from other than God (4:82); to associate after that reflection is to impose the inconsistency upon the Source. The verse generalises the particular defiance of the theft case into theology: to follow other than believers’ path after clarity is often a step before setting a partner beside God, for Satan’s next claim is to be taken as guardian instead of God and to command altering creation (4:119). Association is therefore not only an altar act but a path act — choosing the other side after guidance was clear — and its distance is measured as *baʿīd*. The next verses will anatomise that far straying: calling female gods and none but rebellious Satan (4:117), cursed who takes a share of servants (4:118).

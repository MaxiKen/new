## Sūrah al-Nisāʾ 4:116

> Surely Allah does not forgive associating ˹others˺ with Him ˹in worship˺, but forgives anything else of whoever He wills. Indeed, whoever associates ˹others˺ with Allah has clearly gone far astray.

### Expanded Commentary

**The repeated warning about shirk**

This verse repeats that Allah does not forgive associating partners with Him for one who dies persisting in it, while other sins remain under His will. Repetition signals centrality. The chapter has moved through law, politics, family, and judgment, and now returns to the foundation: no social order is sound if worship is divided between God and rivals.

The phrase “far astray” describes more than a wrong turn. Shirk misdirects the entire journey because the person’s ultimate loyalty is attached to a created object. Yet the repetition also preserves hope for the living: the call to exclusive worship remains available as long as the person can repent.

**Repentance remains the dividing line**

The warning about shirk is a warning about the end-state of a person who dies without turning back. It should lead to inviting people toward pure worship rather than declaring living neighbours beyond hope. The believer fears hidden forms of idolatry in status and desire while trusting Allah’s promise to the repentant.

## Sūrah al-Baqarah 2:165

> Still there are some who take others as Allah’s equal—they love them as they should love Allah—but the ˹true˺ believers love Allah even more. If only the wrongdoers could see the ˹horrible˺ punishment ˹awaiting them˺, they would certainly realize that all power belongs to Allah and that Allah is indeed severe in punishment.

**Expanded Commentary**

**The Rivals Taken Beside God**

The verse states that some people take others as equals to God, and the commentators explained the term as describing competitors that are set alongside Him. The Book has already used the same word in the description of those who claim rivals in the creation, and the sentence here applies it to the objects of devotion.

The item that follows identifies the conduct that accompanies the setting up of rivals, which is love, and the verse says that they love them as God should be loved. The charge therefore concerns the direction of a person's love rather than only the correctness of his beliefs, and the reader is shown that the Book treats devotion and affection as belonging to the same subject.

**What It Means to Love Them as God**

The commentators offered several explanations of the comparison. Some read it as describing people who love their idols in the way the believers love God, others as describing obedience offered to leaders as though it were owed to God, and others as describing a love that is divided between God and the rivals.

The variety follows from the wording, which can be understood in more than one way, and the readings converge on the same point. What is being described is a person who gives to something created the attachment that belongs to the creator, whether the object is an idol, a leader, or a habit of the heart. The sentence leaves the reader to examine the direction of his own love, and the Book's treatment of the subject elsewhere supplies the standard.

**The Believers' Love as Greater**

The sentence then states that the believers are stronger in their love of God, and the comparison places the two groups side by side. The community's scholars explained the strength of the believers' love as its being directed undividedly at one object, whereas the love of the idolaters is distributed and is at its strongest only when they are in danger.

The Book's account elsewhere supports the description, since it reports that people call on God with sincerity when they are in a boat on troubled water and then return to their rivals once they reach the shore. The verse's comparison therefore concerns more than intensity; it concerns the constancy with which the love is held, and the community's teachers read the sentence as a description of who the believers are.

**The Love of God in the Book**

The Book uses the language of love in several places, and the community's scholars gathered the passages in which God commands the Prophet to tell people that they should follow him if they love God, in which God describes a people whom He loves and who love Him, and in which the believers are warned that family and property must not be dearer to them than God and His Messenger.

The reports also preserve the words of a man who told the Prophet ﷺ that he had not prepared for the Hour with much prayer or fasting but that he loved God and His Messenger, and the Prophet's answer was that he would be with those he loved. The verse's statement about the believers' love belongs to this body of material, and the community's teachers used it to explain what the love of God consists of in practice.

**What the Wrongdoers Will See**

The verse states that if the wrongdoers could see the punishment they would realise that all power belongs to God and that He is severe in punishment, and the conditional form continues in the next verse, where the scene of the hereafter is described. The sentence tells the reader what the offenders will understand when the matter is no longer hidden from them.

The Book's use of the conditional here gives the statement an unfinished quality that the following verses complete. What the wrongdoers will see is the demonstration of what the previous verse had already declared through the signs, and the reader who has followed the passage from the declaration of God's oneness through the list of signs arrives at a description of the moment when understanding arrives too late.

**The Love That Fear Replaces**

The verse presents the moment of realization as one in which power is seen to belong entirely to God, and the community's scholars noted that this is the knowledge the idolaters lacked in this life. Their failure was not a shortage of information but a refusal to let the information shape their attachment.

What the passage sets before the reader is the difference between knowing that God is one and loving Him as the one, and the verse pairs the two so that the reader can measure his own condition. The believers are described as those whose love is undivided, and the wrongdoers are described as those who will see the truth at the point when it can no longer change them.

**The Verse in the Passage**

The sentence completes the movement that began with the declaration of God's oneness and the list of signs, and it prepares the reader for the account of the hereafter that follows. The Book states the claim, sets out the evidence, and then describes the two responses to it. The reader receives the whole sequence, and the passage leaves him with the question of which of the two loves describes his own.

## Sūrah al-Tawbah 9:71

> The believers, both men and women, are guardians of one another. They encourage good and forbid evil, establish prayer and pay alms-tax, and obey Allah and His Messenger. It is they who will be shown Allah’s mercy. Surely Allah is Almighty, All-Wise.

**Expanded Commentary**

**Believers Protect One Another**

The verse describes believing men and women as guardians, allies, and responsible friends to one another. Faith is not presented as a private arrangement between an individual and Allah. It creates bonds of care, counsel, protection, and shared duty. A believer watches over another person's dignity, safety, and faith without claiming ownership of the other person.

The pairing of men and women is important. Both belong to the moral community, both carry obligations, and both can help preserve what is good. Guardianship does not mean that every person has the same role in every setting. It means that no sex is excluded from spiritual agency. A woman can advise, protect, teach, and resist wrong; a man can do the same. The community's health depends on the virtue of both.

**Goodness Needs Encouragement**

Believers encourage good rather than waiting for goodness to appear without support. Encouragement includes teaching, making lawful obedience easier, praising honest effort, and arranging institutions that help people keep their duties. A person may know what is right and still need companionship, resources, or a clear example before he can act on it.

This duty should not become a search for control. Encouraging good is different from forcing everyone into one personality or treating a personal preference as divine law. The believer explains what Allah has made clear and leaves room for lawful differences. He uses wisdom because the goal is obedience, not the satisfaction of correcting people.

**Wrong Must Be Resisted With Wisdom**

Forbidding evil requires knowledge of what is wrong, the ability to speak, and attention to the likely result. A person who condemns an act without understanding it may create confusion. A person who confronts a wrong in a way that causes greater harm may have served anger rather than reform.

The duty still requires courage. Silence can become cooperation when evil is praised and the vulnerable have no protection. The believer chooses a form suited to capacity: counsel privately, report abuse through a safe process, withdraw support from corruption, or speak publicly when public harm demands it. Guardianship means refusing to abandon people to those who exploit them.

**Prayer and Alms Give the Bond a Structure**

The verse joins social responsibility to prayer and alms-tax. The community cannot be sustained by activism without worship, and worship cannot be defended while the needy are ignored. Prayer turns the believers toward Allah repeatedly. Alms-tax turns their resources toward lawful social care. The two practices prevent guardianship from becoming either empty idealism or mere administration.

Establishing prayer means more than performing isolated movements. It protects the times, conditions, and meaning of the prayer so that remembrance shapes conduct. Paying alms-tax means more than occasional generosity. It accepts an obligation in wealth and recognizes that the poor have a claim. A believer who guards both inner worship and outward justice becomes a more reliable guardian of others.

**Obedience Joins the Whole Program**

The final practical quality is obedience to Allah and His Messenger. Without this center, people may encourage what they personally call good and forbid what threatens their power. Obedience supplies the measure by which communal authority is judged. Leaders are not allowed to redefine good as whatever preserves their position, and followers are not allowed to call rebellion virtue merely because it feels independent.

The Messenger's guidance also keeps social concern connected to mercy. Correction must serve truth, and truth must be carried with justice. A community that quotes duty while practicing humiliation has lost the balance of the verse. Obedience gives its guardianship a purpose beyond faction and reputation.

**Mercy Is Promised to Those Who Practice Care**

Allah's mercy is promised to the people who join mutual guardianship, moral action, prayer, alms, and obedience. Mercy is not earned as a payment that humans can demand. It is a promised grace reached through the path Allah has described. The believer acts because Allah commands, then hopes because Allah is merciful.

The closing names Allah as Almighty and Wise. His power makes mercy effective, and His wisdom makes its distribution just. Believers can therefore work without imagining that their care alone saves the community. They protect one another, but Allah is the true Protector. Their task is to become instruments of His mercy through knowledge, courage, worship, and fair treatment.

Mutual guardianship also means that believers do not leave moral work to famous people. A small act of protection, a private correction, or a fair refusal to repeat gossip can preserve a community. The duty is shared according to knowledge and ability. Someone who lacks authority may still withhold support from wrong and direct a vulnerable person to help. Someone with authority bears a greater burden to create safe ways for truth to be spoken. In every case, prayer keeps action from becoming self-righteousness, and obedience keeps compassion from becoming mere sentiment. Allah's mercy reaches through the people who make goodness possible for one another.

The promise of mercy also gives moral work its right spirit. Believers do not correct others because they enjoy superiority. They correct because they want good to prevail and harm to stop, while knowing that Allah can correct them through the person they advise. Men and women should cooperate without competing over who owns religious authority. Knowledge creates responsibility, and authority creates accountability. Prayer keeps the reformer dependent on Allah; alms-tax keeps him attentive to material need; obedience keeps him within the limits of the message. Mutual guardianship becomes mercy in practice when it is carried with humility.

## Sūrah al-Naḥl 16:105

> **No one fabricates lies except those who disbelieve in Allah’s revelations, and it is they who are the ˹true˺ liars.**

**Expanded Commentary**

**Why Deceiving Oaths Leads To Slipping Feet**

This verse states: No one fabricates lies except those who disbelieve in Allah's revelations, and it is they who are the ˹true˺ liars.... The context in Sūrat al-Naḥl places it after discussion of blessings, warnings, and examples. Al-Ṭabarī explains that this verse addresses either those who are given shade, shelter, and garments as perfected favours, or those who turn away from clear delivery, or those aware of favours yet ungrateful, or the Day when witnesses are called. The sūrah's method is to alternate blessings with accountability, and this verse is part of that alternation. The translation includes brackets like ˹fully˺ or ˹O Prophet˺ to clarify implied meaning. The Arabic uses concise phrasing that carries layers: command, prohibition, promise, or parable. The reader who has followed the sūrah from cattle to bees to mountains will see this verse as continuation of argument that Allah alone creates and deserves gratitude. The verse invites reflection on how favours are perfected and how response should be submission, gratitude, and mindfulness.

**Not Trading Allah's Covenant For Fleeting Gain**

Lexically, the verse uses roots that appear elsewhere in the sūrah to create echo. For example, words for shade, shelter, clothes, armour, covenant, oaths, justice, grace, and fabrication all come from roots that indicate protection, binding, beauty, and truth. Classical lexicons define these roots with examples from poetry and Qur'anic usage. Al-Qurṭubī notes that understanding root helps see why this wording chosen: it indicates not only action but quality behind it, whether gratitude or ingratitude. The translators add clarifications in brackets to make English flow, but Arabic is concise. The verse's rhythm, with its balance of clauses, makes it memorable. The choice of words also links to other verses that use same root, reinforcing theme that blessings are from Allah and should lead to submission. The verse's placement after previous section about homes, tents, and furnishings shows that even shade and garments are perfected favours that should lead to submission.

**What Ends And What Is Everlasting With Allah**

Theologically, this verse emphasizes either divine ownership, exclusive devotion, or justice. For instance, if Allah provides shade from what He created and shelter in mountains, then He owns shade and mountains, and devotion should be to Him alone. If He commands justice, grace, and courtesy to relatives and forbids indecency, wickedness, and aggression, then His commands are just and wise. If He says those who disbelieve and hinder others will have added punishment, then justice requires that those who cause others to stray bear extra burden, as verse 25 said. Al-Rāzī explains that divine commands in this sūrah are always tied to blessings: because He gave you hearing, sight, intellect, you should be thankful; because He gave you homes, you should rest and remember; because He gave you Book as explanation of all things, you should follow. The verse therefore shows that theology is not abstract but connected to everyday favours and to accountability.

**Good Life For Believing Men And Women Who Do Good**

Practically, the verse teaches lessons for daily conduct. If Allah made your homes a place to rest and gave you tents from animal hide light to handle when traveling and camping, then be grateful for homes and use them to rest and worship, not to plot evil. If He commands honouring covenant and not breaking oaths after confirmation, then keep promises and do not use oaths to deceive. If He sets forth parable of slave who lacks means versus free man granted good provision who donates openly and secretly, then recognize that you are like slave who lacks means compared to Allah who grants provision, so donate and be grateful. If He says whatever you have will end but whatever with Allah is everlasting, then invest in everlasting. Al-Qurṭubī says practical lessons include keeping oaths, not trading covenant for fleeting gain, doing good whether male or female, seeking refuge from Satan before reciting Qur'an, and inviting to way of Lord with wisdom.

**Seeking Refuge From Satan Before Reciting Qur'an**

In terms of the sūrah's overall argument, this verse fits as either conclusion of blessings section or transition to commands section or preparation for final counsel on patience. Sūrat al-Naḥl moves from creation to gratitude to argument against shirk to warnings to examples to commands to final counsel on calling to Allah and patience. This verse, with its mention of shade, mountains, clothes, armour, or covenant, oaths, justice, or witnesses, or Book as explanation, is part of that movement. For example, after describing homes, tents, wool, fur, hair as furnishings for a while, the sūrah mentions shade, mountains, clothes protecting from heat and cold, armour shielding in battle, perfecting favour so perhaps you will submit. The perfecting of favour is key: blessings started with cattle warmth and ended with armour shielding, showing completeness. The reader is invited to see that favour is perfected and response should be full submission.

**Why Satan Has No Authority Over Believers Who Trust**

Finally, reflection is intended outcome. The sūrah repeatedly ends sections with so perhaps you will submit, be mindful, be thankful, reflect, understand, listen, believe. This verse ends with similar purpose: so perhaps you will fully submit, or so perhaps you will be mindful, or so perhaps you will be thankful, or for those who believe, or for those who submit. Al-Zamakhsharī notes that reflection in Qur'an is not idle but leads to action: gratitude, patience, trust, asking knowledgeable, explaining to people, inviting with wisdom, being patient. The verse therefore calls not only to know but to do: to submit fully, to honour covenant, to do justice and grace, to not fabricate lies, to eat good lawful things gratefully, to follow faith of Abraham, to invite with wisdom, to be patient with Allah's help, and to know that Allah is with those who shun evil and do good. The wise person reflects on this verse and acts, before appointed term arrives that cannot be delayed or advanced, and before Day when every soul pleads for itself and is paid in full.

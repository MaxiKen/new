## Sūrah al-Baqarah 2:194

> ˹There will be retaliation in˺ a sacred month for ˹an offence in˺ a sacred month, and all violations will bring about retaliation. So, if anyone attacks you, retaliate in the same manner. ˹But˺ be mindful of Allah, and know that Allah is with those mindful ˹of Him˺.

**Expanded Commentary**

**A Rule About Sacred Months**

The verse states that an offence committed in a sacred month may be answered in a sacred month, and it states that all violations bring about retaliation in proportion. The sentence belongs to the passage on conflict, and it addresses the particular anxiety of a community that feared fighting during the months the Arabs held inviolable.

The Book's answer is that the sacredness of a time does not shield an aggressor, and the ruling is stated in the form of a correspondence rather than a permission. What the reader receives is a rule about the limits of a truce, and the sentence connects it with the general prohibition of excess that the previous verses have established.

**The Occasion Reported**

The commentators connect the passage with the treaty the community concluded with the Quraysh at Ḥudaybiyah, under which the believers turned back and were to return for the lesser pilgrimage the following year. When the time came, some of the companions were uneasy about entering the precinct and the sacred months, and they feared both an attack and the blame for fighting in a time that was held inviolable.

The verse answers that anxiety, and the reports show how the community's scholars applied it to the situation. What the reader receives is a ruling whose circumstances are documented, and the text's terms become clear when it is read against the events that produced the questions.

**The Principle of Correspondence**

The sentence repeats the principle stated in different words a few verses earlier: the response is measured by the offence. The Book's formulation of that principle in three places within one passage indicates the importance the legislation attaches to proportion.

The community's scholars read the correspondence as a restriction as well as an authorisation, since a response that exceeds the offence is itself a violation. What the sentence establishes is that the community's conduct in conflict is governed by the same standard that governs its other dealings, and the reader is shown that the permission to fight does not release the community from the requirement of justice.

**What the Commentators Derive**

The commentaries use the passage to set out the rules that govern the conduct of war, and they preserve the instruction not to kill women, children, the elderly, monks, hermits, the chronically ill, and those who take no part in the fighting. The reports attribute statements of this kind to the companions and to the early teachers, and the community's jurists built their rulings on the combination of the verse and the reports.

The material shows what the Book's prohibition of transgression was understood to include. The reader carries away the account of a body of law that limits the conduct of war in detail, and the sentence in this place supplies the principle from which the rulings were drawn.

**The Address to Be Mindful**

The sentence closes with the instruction to be mindful of God and the statement that God is with those who are mindful of Him, and the ending resembles the close of the verse that opened the passage. The Book's practice of returning to the vocabulary of awareness at the close of a legal instruction gives the reader the disposition in which the rule is to be observed.

The community's teachers noted that the sentence's command concerns the conduct of a conflict, which is the setting in which the awareness of God is most easily forgotten. The Book's legislation reaches the moments of anger as well as the ordinary affairs of life, and it places the conduct of war in the presence of God.

**The Limits Restated**

The verse's two clauses together state the permission and the restriction, and the community's scholars read them as a single rule. The Book has established in the preceding verses that the community is not to exceed what its defence requires, and the sentence here applies that principle to a particular question that the community raised.

What the reader is given is the account of a law that answers a specific anxiety without relaxing its general standard. The passage continues with the command to spend in the way of God, and the reader is carried from the conduct of the community's conflicts to the disposition of its property.

The community's teachers noted that the sentence answers a question raised in a particular season, and that its principle applies wherever the community's conflicts recur. What the Book establishes is that the sacredness of a time or a place belongs to God and does not shield an aggressor from the response his conduct has invited, while the response itself remains measured by the offence. The reports show the companions applying the same reasoning when they discussed the conduct of the community's campaigns, and the reader is given the rule and its reason together.

**The Verse in the Passage**

The sentence stands at the end of the short section on fighting and at the beginning of the verses on pilgrimage, and it belongs to the sequence of instructions that organise the community's life. The Book has dealt with the defence of the community and now turns to the worship that gathers it from every region, and the reader is shown that the legislation addresses the whole of the community's affairs in a single flow.

The position of the sentence at the end of the section on fighting gives the reader a summary of what the Book has said about the community's conflicts, and its reference to the sacred months connects the rules of war with the discipline of worship. The verses that follow turn to the pilgrimage, and the reader is carried from the defence of the community to the journey that gathers it from every region. The community's teachers read the two subjects together and noted that the Book's legislation addresses the whole of the community's life in one sequence.

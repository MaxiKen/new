## Sūrah al-Nisāʾ 4:50

> See how they fabricate lies against Allah—this alone is a blatant sin.

**Expanded Commentary**

**See How They Fabricate**

The command *unẓur kayfa yaftarūna ʿalā Allāhi al-kadhib* — see how they fabricate lies against Allah — directs the gaze to a practice just described: declaring themselves pure (4:49) while Allah alone purifies whomever He wills without wronging by a thread. *Iftarā* — to fabricate, to cut out a lie and present it as truth — was read as inventing a claim and attaching God’s Name to it, saying God has approved what He has not. In the Madinan context that fabrication took forms the sūrah has traced: that only one group will enter the Garden, that God loves them because of lineage, that distorted words — *we hear and disobey* veiled as obedience (4:46) — represent His intent, or that trading a portion of the Book for misguidance (4:44) is guidance. The verb *yaftarūna* suggests ongoing work; the lie is not a slip but a craft, repeated until it feels like doctrine. The sūrah elsewhere says *“whoever associates with Allah has fabricated a grave sin”* (4:48), showing that *shirk* is the root fabrication; this verse shows the branch that grows from self-purification — the claim that God has already endorsed the claimant, which is a lie against Him.

**This Alone Is a Blatant Sin**

The clause *wa kafā bihi ithman mubīnā* — this alone is a blatant sin, sufficient as a clear sin — measures the fabrication without needing to add other faults. *Kafā bihi* — sufficient is it — was read as this single lie suffices to be counted as manifest sin, even if no other deed were considered. *Ithman mubīnan* — clear, open, evident sin — was used earlier for reclaiming a heap of gold by slander (4:20) — *buhtānan wa ithman mubīnan* — showing that fabrication against God and fabrication against a wife share the same structure: a lie told to justify taking or keeping what is not one’s. The sūrah has shown both forms — the husband who invents fault to reclaim a dowry, and the scholar who invents approval to keep influence. Both are called *buhtān* and *iftirā*, both are *mubīn* — clear — because the lie is open once the Book confirms what was hidden. The verse therefore does not say this is one sin among many; it says this alone is enough to be called blatant, which is why the previous verse corrected self-elevation with *Allah purifies whomever He wills* (4:49) and the verse before that placed association outside pardon (4:48). To fabricate against Him is to misplace His will and His knowledge.

**Fabrication After Self-Elevation**

The link to the previous verse is direct. Those who elevate themselves — *yuzakkūna anfusahum* (4:49) — must supply a story for why they are elevated; the story becomes a lie against God — He told us we are pure, He will not punish us, He favours our lineage. The sūrah elsewhere records such stories: *“we are God’s children and beloved”* and *“none will enter the Garden except Jews or Christians.”* Each attributes to God a promise He did not make. The verse says look at how the fabrication is done: by taking a thread’s width of status and spinning it into a garment of purity, then presenting it as revelation. The earlier warning that no one will be wronged by even a thread — *fatīl* — (4:49) and by even a speck on a date stone — *naqīr* — (4:53) is the measure that exposes the fabrication. A community where an atom’s weight is not wronged (4:40) cannot also have a community where purity is assigned by birth without deed, because the second claim would wrong every doer whose atom was counted. The fabrication therefore contradicts the sūrah’s ethic of deed-based shares — *men according to what they earned and women according to what they earned* (4:32) — and the ethic of asking God for bounty (4:32) rather than claiming it.

**A Sin That Is Clear Without Further Evidence**

To call the fabrication *ithman mubīnan* — a blatant sin — is to say it is evident without needing a hidden motive to be uncovered. The lie is blatant because the Book that confirms (4:47) is present to compare, because the Witness over all things (4:33) is invoked, and because the atom’s weight that is never wronged is the standard that shows the lie’s excess. Early authorities noted that the sūrah makes this sin visible by its own method: it quotes the distorted phrases — *we hear and disobey, herd us* — (4:46) and then quotes the straight phrases — *we hear and obey, tend to us* — so the fabrication can be heard. Here it quotes the act of self-elevation and then quotes the correction — *Allah purifies whomever He wills* — so the sin’s clarity is audible as well. The verse does not detail the punishment here, as it did for devouring orphan wealth (4:10) or exceeding limits (4:14), because *kafā bihi* — sufficient is it — already tells the reader that this fabrication alone suffices to be counted as grave. The next verses will add the broader idolatry where those given a portion of the Book reassure disbelievers they are better guided than believers (4:51), showing how one fabrication leads to another, but this verse holds the focus on the lie against God as enough on its own.

**Looking So That You Do Not Imitate**

The opening *unẓur kayfa* — see how — was read as both observation and caution. See how they fabricate, so that you recognize the pattern; see how, so that you do not reproduce it. The sūrah has taught a community that was learning to divide estates, guard dowries, and trade by consent to look at how others handle trusts. Here it teaches them to look at how others handle the greatest trust — speaking about God. The community that has been told to be kind to parents, relatives, orphans, poor, neighbours, companions, travellers, and those in possession (4:36) might still be tempted to claim purity because it has done some of that; the verse warns that to turn that deed into a claim — God has made us pure — is to fabricate. The purer way is the one the sūrah has shown: do the deed, ask for bounty, avoid major sins so lesser faults are covered (4:31), and trust the One who elevates whomever He wills without wronging by a thread. That way leaves no room for a lie against Him, because it does not need one. The blatant sin is sufficient warning that the path of self-elevation ends not in purity but in a fabrication whose clarity no tongue can hide.

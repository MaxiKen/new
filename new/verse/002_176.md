## Sūrah al-Baqarah 2:176

> That is because Allah has revealed the Book in truth. And surely those who differ regarding it are totally engrossed in opposition.

**Expanded Commentary**

**The Reason Given**

The verse states that the punishment described in the preceding sentences follows from the fact that God sent down the Book in truth, and it then describes those who differ concerning it as being in deep opposition. The sentence supplies the reason for the judgment, and the reason concerns the treatment of the revelation.

The Book's method of attaching a cause to a punishment appears throughout the sūrah, and the reader is shown that the outcomes described are not arbitrary. What the offenders did was measured against what they had been given, and the sentence names the gift before it names the offence against it.

**The Book Sent Down in Truth**

The phrase states that the revelation came from God with truth, and the sūrah has used the same form of words at other points in its argument. The statement establishes both the origin and the character of the Book, and the community's teachers read it as the ground of the accountability that the passage describes.

What follows from the phrase is that the concealment of the Book is not a matter of preference among opinions. A person who hides a truth that has come from God is opposing something whose authority is not in question, and the sentence presents the offence in that light before it describes the punishment that belongs to it.

**Those Who Differ Concerning It**

The second half of the sentence describes those who differ concerning the Book as being in deep opposition, and the commentators explained the term as describing a division that has become a standing disagreement. The Book uses the same word in other passages for the conduct of communities that set themselves against the truth.

Some commentators read the reference to the Book as covering the earlier scriptures as well as the final revelation, and others took it as a statement about the disputes among the People of the Book over the meaning of what they had received. Under either reading the sentence concerns disagreement that has become an end in itself, and the community's teachers noted that the term describes a condition of the disputants rather than the subject of their dispute.

**Schism as a Condition**

The Book's word for the condition is connected with the idea of splitting, and the sūrah has described communities that divided their religion into factions. The sentence states that the offenders have arrived at a position in which the truth of the revelation is settled and their opposition nevertheless continues.

The community's scholars read the verse as a warning about the habit of disputing for its own sake, since a person who disagrees after the matter has been established is no longer seeking an answer. What the sentence establishes is that the disagreement itself can be the offence, and the reader is shown that the Book treats a certain kind of controversy as a condition rather than as an activity.

**The Charge Against the Concealers**

The sentence follows the account of those who concealed the Book and sold it for a small price, and it explains what their conduct amounts to in the Book's terms. What began as a transaction ends as a division, and the community's teachers noted the progression in the passage.

The reader is shown that the mishandling of knowledge produces conflict, and the sūrah has already described communities that concealed what they knew and then disputed about the figures of their own history. The verse in this place states the general form of the matter, and it leaves the reader with the connection between the concealment of the truth and the disputes that follow from it.

**The Truth as the Standard**

The phrase about the Book being sent down in truth also supplies the standard by which the disputes are to be judged. The community's teachers used the sentence when they discussed how disagreements among those who possess the revelation are to be resolved, and they pointed to the Book itself as the criterion.

The sūrah has described the revelation as the criterion in an earlier verse, and the present sentence repeats the idea in the context of a dispute. What the reader receives is the reminder that the truth has been given, and the sentence places the responsibility for division on those who have received it and turned aside.

**What the Verse Adds to the Passage**

The sentence completes the account of the offenders by naming the ground of their punishment, and it then describes their condition in a single phrase. The Book has stated what they concealed, what they received in exchange, and what they have lost; the present verse names the truth that stood behind all of it. The passage continues with the description of the righteous, and the reader is carried from the account of a failed transaction to a statement of what a life devoted to the truth consists of.

The passage's arrangement is deliberate, since the description of the righteous that follows is meant to be read against the account of those who disputed the Book. What the one group did with the revelation and what the other did with their lives are placed side by side, and the reader is left to weigh the two. The community's teachers noted that the sūrah frequently closes a passage of rebuke with a portrait of the conduct it commends.

## Sūrah al-Aʿrāf 7:61

> He replied, “O my people! I am not misguided! But I am a messenger from the Lord of all worlds,

**Expanded Commentary**

**The Denial of the Charge**

The verse reports the reply of Noah, and the reply begins by rejecting the charge that the notables brought against him. He states that he is not misguided, and the phrase answers the accusation in the terms in which it was made, so that the denial and the charge use the same word. The Book reports the same exchange in its other accounts of the prophets, and the sūrah's later narratives repeat the pattern with Hūd (7:67) and with Shuʿayb.

The reply's first sentence is a denial of a personal fault, and the second states the office that explains the messenger's conduct. The two halves belong together, since the message he brings is the reason his people consider him astray, and his identification of himself as a messenger is the answer to their judgment. The verse in front of us states the answer in the form of an address to the people, and the messenger's care for them is visible in the address itself.

**A Messenger From the Lord of All Worlds**

The verse states the messenger's office in the same words that the Book uses for the Prophet Muhammad and for the other messengers. The sūrah of the Poets reports Noah's words in almost the same terms (26:106–107), the sūrah of Hūd reports them from the same messenger, and the sūrah's own account of the prophet's title appears in its descriptions of the messengers of the peoples. The phrase Lord of all worlds identifies the sender as the Lord of everything rather than as a tribal deity.

The title in front of us answers the audience's understanding of religion as the property of a community. The people of Noah had their own traditions and their own claims about the divine, and the messenger's words place him in the service of the One who is the Lord of all worlds rather than of a particular people. The Book's use of the same title throughout its accounts of the messengers shows that the office was the same in each case.

**The Address to the People**

The reply begins with an address, and the messenger calls the people his own. The Book uses the same opening in the mouths of Hūd, Ṣāliḥ, and Shuʿayb, each of whom addresses his people as my people, and the sūrah's narratives present the messengers as men of the communities they were sent to. The address in front of us is therefore a statement of kinship as much as a form of speech, and the refusal of the people is a refusal of one of their own.

The Book's treatment of the messengers' humanity appears in the objections of the communities and in the answers that the messengers give. The sūrah of the Sand Dunes states that the Prophet is told to say that he is not the first messenger ever sent and that he follows only what is revealed to him (46:9), and the sūrah of the Prophets states that every messenger before him received the same revelation of the unity of God (21:25). The verse in front of us supplies the first of the sūrah's replies to the objection.

**The Structure of the Prophet Narratives**

The verse belongs to a structure that the sūrah's accounts repeat with each people, and the structure consists of the call, the refusal of the notables, the messenger's denial of the charge, the statement of his office, the description of his work, and the warning of the outcome. The fixed order is what makes the accounts a series rather than a collection of stories, and the verse in front of us supplies the third and fourth elements of the first account.

The Book's use of the same sequence in the sūrah of the Poets and the sūrah of Hūd shows that the arrangement is deliberate rather than accidental, and the repetition serves the sūrah's argument. Each community heard the same claim, made the same objection, and received the same answer, and the difference between the peoples lies in the outcome of their refusal rather than in the message they were offered.

**What the Verse Says About the Accusation**

The charge of the notables in the previous verse was that the messenger stood in clear error, and the reply in front of us separates the messenger's person from the message he carries. The people had judged the message by the standing of the man who brought it, and the answer states that the man is a messenger and that the message comes from the Lord of all worlds. The sūrah's later narratives show the same judgment being made by the notables of ʿĀd and Thamūd, and each of them receives the same answer.

The Book's treatment of the messengers' lack of worldly standing appears in several passages, and the sūrah of the Cattle reports the objection that the message should have come to a man of importance in one of the two towns. The reply in front of us points away from the messenger's standing and toward the sender, and the sūrah's reader is given the criterion by which the messengers are to be judged.

**Where the Verse Sits**

The verse stands sixty-first in the chapter, and it answers the charge of the notables in the previous verse. The two verses that follow describe the work of the messenger and the astonishment of the people at the reminder that came to them through one of their own (7:62–63), and the account then proceeds to the denial and the flood. The verse in front of us supplies the messenger's own statement of his office.

The reader who heard the leaders dismiss the messenger now hears the messenger's answer. The denial of error is followed by the claim of the office, and the claim is the same one that the sūrah's other messengers make; and the verses that follow state what the office requires him to do.

**The Transition to the Next Verse**

The next verse states that the messenger conveys the messages of his Lord and gives sincere advice to his people and that he knows from God what they do not know (7:62). The statement of the office in front of us is followed by the description of its work, and the passage then records the messenger's appeal to the people's sense of wonder.

The two verses together state the messenger's position in full. He is not astray, he is sent by the Lord of all worlds, and his work among his people is the delivery of the message and the counsel that accompanies it; and the verse that follows states the knowledge that the delivery carries.

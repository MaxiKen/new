## Sūrah al-Nisāʾ 4:89

> They wish you would disbelieve as they have disbelieved, so you may all be alike. So do not take them as allies unless they emigrate in the cause of Allah. But if they turn away, then seize them and kill them wherever you find them, and do not take any of them as allies or helpers,

**Expanded Commentary**

**They Wish You Would Disbelieve as They Have**

The verse states *waddū law takfurūna kamā kafarū fa takūnūna sawāʾ* — they wish you would disbelieve as they have disbelieved, so you may all be alike. *Waddū* — they wish, they love — was explained as the desire of the regressed hypocrites and those who turned away (4:88) that the believers join their regression. The sūrah has shown that wish before: those who follow desires wish you to deviate greatly (4:27), those given a portion trade it for misguidance and wish you to deviate from the path (4:44), and the lagger wishes after gain to be alike in share (4:73). Here the wish is more radical: that you disbelieve as they have, so that sameness — *sawāʾ* — replaces distinction. To disbelieve as they have was read as leaving the limits about wealth and marriage, the referral to the Messenger (4:59), and the obedience that places with the blessed (4:69), and returning to covering signs (4:56) and belief in *jibt* (4:51). Their wish is not for your opinion but for your sameness; if all are alike in disbelief, their regression is no longer visible. The Qur’an elsewhere says that disbelievers would love you to be as they are, because likeness removes the witness that they were left to stray.

**So Do Not Take Them as Allies Unless They Emigrate**

The command *fa lā tattakhidhū minhum awliyāʾa ḥattā yuhājirū fī sabīli Allāhi* — so do not take them as allies unless they emigrate in the cause of Allah — was read as the sūrah’s test of that wish. *Awliyāʾ* — allies, close protectors — was explained as the bond that the sūrah opened as *mawaddah* — affection (4:73) — and as *you are from one another* (4:25). To take them as *awliyāʾ* while they remain regressed would be to accept sameness on their terms. Emigration — *hijrah* — *in the cause of Allah* was the sūrah’s concrete sign of reversal, as the migrants endured the hard command to leave homes that few would have obeyed (4:66) and as fighting for the oppressed required going forth (4:71). Early reports placed the verse after Uḥud where hypocrites lagged and after groups in the desert sought safety with both sides; to emigrate in God’s cause meant to join the community that returns trusts and refers disputes (4:58–59) and that fights where the oppressed cry (4:75). The condition *ḥattā yuhājirū* — until they emigrate — was read as the threshold where sameness could be on belief, not on disbelief. Until then, the believers’ *mawaddah* is not extended as alliance, because the regressed wish the believers to be alike in the wrong direction.

**But If They Turn Away**

The clause *fa in tawallaw* — but if they turn away — was explained as turning away from that emigration, persisting in regression after the test. *Tawallā* — to turn the back — matches the hypocrites’ turning away stubbornly — *yaṣuddūna ṣudūdā* (4:61) — and seeking *ṭāghūt* though commanded to reject it (4:60). To turn away here is to refuse the route that would have shown that doing what was advised is better and more reassuring (4:66). The verse does not command hostility for mere hesitation; it commands it after the wish to make you alike has been displayed and after the path to join has been offered and refused. The sūrah has said that whoever is left to stray you will never find a way for them (4:88); *tawallaw* is the human side of that leaving — they turn, and He allows regression. The community divided into two groups about them (4:88) is here given a single rule: no alliance until emigration, and if they turn from that, then the next measure.

**Seize and Kill Wherever You Find Them**

The measure *fa khudhūhum wa aqtulūhum ḥaythu wajadtumūhum* — then seize them and kill them wherever you find them — was read as the wartime ruling for the regressed who are combatants or who align with combatants against the believers, not as a rule for every individual who errs. Context shows the sūrah immediately adds exceptions: those allied to a treaty people or those opposed to fighting either side (4:90), and those who keep away, offer peace, and refrain from attacking (4:90), for whom God does not permit harm. Early authorities noted that the *khudh* and *uqtilū* here address the hostile group that wishes you disbelieve and that, if it turns away from emigration, remains a threat to the oppressed who cry for a helper (4:75). The sūrah elsewhere forbids killing a believer except by mistake with expiation and blood-money (4:92) and promises Hell for killing intentionally (4:93), showing that the permission here is delimited by treaty, by peace offered, and by opposition to fighting. To seize and kill the hostile turner is therefore not a lapse of the ethic *“do not kill yourselves”* (4:29) but its protection at community scale, where the Devil’s schemes are weak (4:76) yet must still be curbed (4:84).

**No Allies or Helpers Among the Hostile Turners**

The closing *wa lā tattakhidhū minhum waliyyan wa lā naṣīrā* — and do not take any of them as allies or helpers — repeats the opening prohibition with the two sufficiencies the sūrah said God alone provides — *sufficient as Guardian and sufficient as Helper* (4:45). *Walī* and *naṣīr* — guardian and helper — were explained as the two roles the oppressed ask for from grace (4:75). To take the hostile turner as *walī* or *naṣīr* would be to seek guardianship from those God allowed to regress because of misdeeds, and who wish you alike in disbelief. The sūrah has said that whoever is condemned will have no helper (4:52) and that if those given no share of kingdom had it they would not give a speck (4:53); the hostile turners are therefore not helpers in any case. The verse generalises what earlier verses narrowly said for marriage and inheritance: do not seek the bar — the Devil’s cause — where God’s cause is available. For the weak for whom burdens were lightened, the rule is light: do not take as allies those who wish you to disbelieve as they have; wait for emigration in God’s cause as sign of reversal, and if they turn away as hostile, meet hostility with the measure the sūrah bounds, and otherwise take no ally among them, because the Guardian and Helper who suffices for the oppressed is sufficient for you.

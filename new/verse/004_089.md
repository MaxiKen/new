## Sūrah al-Nisāʾ 4:89

> They wish you would disbelieve as they have disbelieved, so you may all be alike. So do not take them as allies unless they emigrate in the cause of Allah. But if they turn away, then seize them and kill them wherever you find them, and do not take any of them as allies or helpers,

### Expanded Commentary

**A wartime command, not a license for indiscriminate violence**

The verse addresses people who wanted believers to abandon faith so that all would be alike, and it discusses hostile opponents in the context of the early Muslim polity. Its severe command must be read with verses 90–91, the treaty rules, the distinction between combatants and those who offer peace, and the Qur’an’s prohibition of transgression. It is not a general order to kill people who disagree with Islam.

**Alliance and active hostility**

The concern is not mere difference of belief but organized hostility that seeks to make the community abandon its faith and supports attack. The command to withhold alliance refers to military and political loyalty in an existential conflict, not to ordinary kindness, trade, neighbourliness, or justice toward non-Muslims.

The condition concerning emigration in Allah’s cause belongs to the historical obligation of joining and strengthening the persecuted community. Later legal application requires attention to context, treaty, citizenship, and authority. Individuals cannot take this verse as permission to commit violence; war is governed by legitimate command and stringent limits.

The verse’s purpose is protection of a community under attack, and its boundaries in the next passages prove that purpose. Removing those boundaries turns a regulated wartime judgment into the very transgression the Qur’an condemns.

**Context is a moral safeguard**

The severe wartime language must be interpreted with its treaty exceptions and with the general prohibition of transgression. Removing context is not strictness; it is distortion. A faithful reader asks who is addressed, what conflict exists, what peace terms were offered, and which limits the chapter itself places on force.

## Sūrah al-Nisāʾ 4:117

> Instead of Allah, they only invoke female gods and they ˹actually˺ invoke none but a rebellious Satan—

**Expanded Commentary**

**They Invoke Nothing Besides Him Except Females**

The verse states *in yadʿūna min dūnihī illā ināthan* — they call, they invoke, besides Him — instead of God — except females, female ones. *Yadʿūna* — to call, to invoke, to pray to — was explained as worship by invocation, the same calling that the sūrah condemns where people call upon another god with God (25:68 cited) and where association is not forgiven (4:116). *Min dūnihī* — besides Him, instead of Him, short of Him — was read as departure from the Single Source to whom belongs whatever is in heavens and earth (4:126). *Illā ināthan* — except females, female gods — was explained as the Meccan invocation of female-named deities — al-Lāt, al-ʿUzzā, Manāt — called daughters of God, and more broadly as weak, powerless beings chosen as partners, the female being taken as symbol of weakness in that culture’s idolatry though in reality both male and female beyond God are powerless. The sūrah has shown that those given a portion believe in *jibt* and *ṭāghūt* (4:51) and wish you deviate, and that devouring wealth unjustly is condemned (4:29); invoking females besides God was read as the religious form of that deviation: to give what belongs to the Creator of the single soul (4:1) to imagined females. The phrase was linked to *“they only invoke female gods”* as sign of the far straying — *ḍalālan baʿīdā* (4:116) — where association takes the form of venerating the weakest claimants.

**They Actually Invoke None But Rebellious Satan**

The correction *wa in yadʿūna illā shayṭānan marīdā* — and they actually invoke none but a rebellious, obstinate, defiant Satan — was explained as the reality behind the image. *Shayṭān* — Satan, the adversary — was read as Iblīs who was granted respite until the appointed Day and who vowed to sit in ambush (7:15–17), whose schemes are weak (4:76) yet whose whisper deludes. *Marīd* — rebellious, defiant, smooth, obstinate — was explained as one who has rebelled, broken away, become excessively disobedient, as the sūrah will say *cursed by God who said, I will take a share of servants* (4:118). To invoke females besides God was explained as invocation of Satan in effect, because Satan commands the veneration of the female idols and the altering of creation (4:119). The sūrah has said that God does not like the deceitful sinner (4:107) and that night plotting displeases Him (4:108); here the invocation displeasing is shown to have a commander — the rebellious Satan — who orders it. Early reports noted that idol worshippers thought they called Lāt and ʿUzzā; the Qur’an says they call Satan, because obedience to Satan is worship of him even where his name is not spoken.

**A Verse That Unmasks Idolatry as Satanism**

Placed after *whoever associates has gone far astray* (4:116), this verse unmasks idolatry as Satanism. The arc from defying the Messenger and following other than believers’ path (4:115) to association’s far straying (4:116) to invoking females besides Him is the path of deviation: guidance was clear, yet *ṭāghūt* was sought (4:60), trust betrayed (4:107), night plot hidden from people (4:108); the religious expression is to call upon what cannot hear. The sūrah elsewhere says *“have you not seen those given a portion who believe in *jibt* and *ṭāghūt*”* (4:51); here *ināth* and *shayṭān marīd* are that *jibt* and *ṭāghūt* named in Meccan form. The identification — females but really Satan — was read as the Book’s demystification: the many gods dissolve into one enemy, the rebellious Satan, whose promise is only delusion (4:120) and whose portion is Hell without escape (4:121). Where the sūrah promised great reward for commanding charity, kindness, reconciliation seeking God’s pleasure (4:114), this verse shows the opposite command — Satan orders slitting ears of cattle and altering creation (4:119) — and promises nothing but loss.

**Female Naming as Sign of Weakness Chosen**

The phrase *illā ināthan* — except females — was explained linguistically as showing that the worshippers chose the weakest form of partner, for the female among their idols was not chosen for strength but for association as offspring, as daughters, implying God has children — a claim the sūrah later refutes with *“God is One, far above having a son; the Messiah was but messenger, Word through Mary and spirit from Him, do not say Trinity”* (4:171). To call females besides the Single who created from a single soul and spread men and women (4:1) was read as inversion: from Him who creates both, they make the female a separate deity. The Qur’an elsewhere says that when a daughter is announced, faces darken (16:58); yet they assign daughters to God, showing desire not for truth but for a partner that cannot challenge. The sūrah pairs this with *shayṭān marīd* to show that weakness worshipped is not harmless — the rebellious Satan uses that weakness as doorway, ordering the slitting of cattle ears and altering of creation that imposes human will upon what God formed intact.

**A Short Verse That Bridges Association and Satan’s Vow**

Though only *“they invoke beside Him only females, and they invoke only rebellious Satan,”* the verse bridges association’s doctrine (4:116) and Satan’s vow (4:118–119). The sūrah has shown that the Devil’s cause is fought for by disbelievers (4:76), that his followers wish you deviate greatly (4:27), and that whoever takes Satan as guardian instead of God suffers tremendous loss (4:119). To invoke females besides Him is the act; to invoke rebellious Satan is the agency. The verse was read as the sūrah’s answer to those who claim *“we only worship them to bring us near to God”* (39:3): you claim nearness by females, but you arrive at Satan, for his command is behind the rite. The next verses will let Satan speak: cursed, he says *I will take a share, mislead, delude with hopes, order slitting and altering* (4:118–119), and *he promises only delusion* (4:120), whose end is Hell without escape (4:121) versus the Gardens promised truly to those who believe and do good (4:122).

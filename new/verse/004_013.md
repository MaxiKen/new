## Sūrah al-Nisāʾ 4:13

> These ˹entitlements˺ are the limits set by Allah. Whoever obeys Allah and His Messenger will be admitted into Gardens under which rivers flow, to stay there forever. That is the ultimate triumph!

### Expanded Commentary

**Limits are protective boundaries**

After detailing shares, the Qur’an calls them the limits set by Allah. A limit is not merely a restriction on desire; it marks a protected boundary within which vulnerable rights are safe. The inheritance fractions prevent the powerful heir from absorbing the estate, the grieving testator from favouring one child, and custom from excluding women. To treat the shares as optional advice is therefore to remove the fence that protects the weak.

**Obedience includes practical distribution**

The verse connects obedience to Allah and His Messenger with admission to gardens. This connection corrects a false division between spiritual devotion and legal conduct. Prayer, fasting, and charitable feeling cannot compensate for deliberately withholding a widow’s share. At the same time, accurate distribution is not mere bureaucracy: it becomes an act of obedience when the heirs submit their preferences to revealed justice.

Prophetic practice is necessary for application. The Qur’an gives the central portions, while the Messenger explained the order of debts and bequests, the treatment of residuary heirs, and the details of competing claims. The believer follows both the principle and the authoritative explanation rather than choosing whichever calculation produces the desired result.

**The great success**

The word “triumph” redirects the family’s measure of success. A person may think he has triumphed by obtaining a larger portion, but if the gain rests on disobedience it is a loss that extends beyond the grave. Conversely, an heir who relinquishes an unlawful advantage or ensures that a weaker relative receives her due may appear to lose materially while attaining the success the verse names. Paradise is promised not to the best negotiator but to those who honour the boundary.

**Limits preserve peace**

The divine limits also protect the heirs from endless negotiation. Once the shares are known, a family can choose voluntary gifts from its own property without forcing a weaker person to bargain for recognition. Obedience thus makes reconciliation easier: people can disagree about feelings while submitting to a rule that is not controlled by the loudest relative.

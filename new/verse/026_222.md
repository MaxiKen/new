## Sūrah al-Shuʿarāʾ 26:222

> They descend upon every sinful liar,

**Expanded Commentary**

**Sin and Lying Make a Compatible Receiver**

The verse names two qualities together: persistent sin and lying. Sin damages the relationship with Allah, while lying damages the relationship with people and with reality itself. Joined together, they describe a person who is willing to violate the moral order and then use speech to conceal, defend, or profit from the violation.

The Arabic wording carries force beyond a single mistake. The liar is not merely someone who once spoke inaccurately. The term points to a person marked by fabrication, someone who repeatedly bends truth for a purpose. The sinful companion term identifies conduct that is not a harmless verbal defect. The receiver has made wrongdoing part of his chosen posture.

**A Habit of Falsehood Opens the Door to More Falsehood**

A person who lies to protect a sin must often lie again to protect the first lie. The story becomes larger, the conscience becomes quieter, and the false version begins to feel safer than the truth. Satanic influence finds opportunity in that cycle because the person already prefers a useful falsehood to an honest submission.

This is why the verse does not say merely that devils descend upon clever speakers. Intelligence does not make a person satanic. The issue is the union of intelligence with moral corruption and deliberate falsehood. A truthful person can make an error and correct it; a sinful liar protects the error because it serves desire.

**The Receiver Is Responsible for What He Accepts**

The description does not absolve the human recipient by blaming every lie on an unseen force. The person is named as sinful and lying before the influence is mentioned. He has a moral condition that makes him receptive. The devil may decorate the falsehood, but the person has chosen the appetite that welcomes the decoration.

This protects a necessary distinction between temptation and excuse. A whisper can suggest pride, revenge, or fraud, but a person cannot say that the whisper performed the act. The verse explains the partnership without erasing the human share. A person who wants protection must break with the sin and return to truth rather than merely complain about temptation.

**Truthful Speech Is a Protection**

The Quran gives a practical contrast to this type of receiver. A servant who values truth admits ignorance, corrects an error, and refuses to turn revelation into a tool for personal gain. Such a servant may still be tempted, but he does not build a home for falsehood in the heart.

Quran 49:6 commands verification when news comes from an evildoer, because unexamined information can lead to harm and regret. The instruction assumes that character affects how reports should be received. It does not license suspicion of everyone; it teaches careful truth-seeking rather than surrender to the sinful liar's version of events.

**The Description Separates Prophecy From Soothsaying**

A prophet conveys revelation under Allah's protection. A sinful liar uses unseen claims, invented reports, or persuasive speech to gain influence. The two should not be confused because both may speak about matters people cannot immediately see. The Quran's trustworthy chain and moral call distinguish it from the claims of those upon whom devils descend.

The verse therefore answers the accusation at its root. The devil's receiver is a habitual sinner who fabricates, while the revelation calls people to worship, justice, repentance, and accountable speech. The source of a message is tested by its truth and direction, not by the excitement created when it enters a crowd.

The description every sinful liar also exposes a false kind of charisma. A speaker may be quick, confident, and entertaining while carrying no concern for truth. The crowd may mistake certainty of tone for certainty of knowledge. The Quran refuses that measure. Character and conduct matter before the drama of delivery.

The sinful liar can also make other people sinful by normalizing falsehood. If a leader invents a claim to protect wealth, followers may repeat it to protect belonging. If a performer turns slander into entertainment, listeners may learn to treat another person's honor as a cheap source of amusement. The influence spreads through willing repetition, but the first responsibility remains with the person who chooses to fabricate.

A truthful person may possess a strong imagination or an unusual way of speaking. Those qualities do not make him a receiver of devils. The dividing line is not plainness versus creativity. It is whether speech is submitted to Allah and whether the speaker is willing to correct himself when the truth opposes his desire. Sincerity can coexist with eloquence; it cannot coexist comfortably with deliberate lying.

The verse also teaches how to respond when a report comes from a morally compromised person. Do not spread it because it contains one detail that fits what you already believe. Verify it, seek another account, and consider who would be harmed if it were false. Quran 49:6 makes this discipline explicit. Careful verification is an act of justice, not a failure of trust.

The distinction between prophet and soothsayer becomes clear at this point. The prophet is bound by revelation and accountable to the Lord who sends him. The soothsayer or sinful liar uses claims of hidden access to gain authority while remaining willing to deceive. The Quran's protection from satanic descent preserves the first role from being confused with the second.

The verse therefore calls for a culture in which truth is more valuable than a thrilling report. A community that rewards the liar will receive more lies. A community that honors repentance, evidence, and honest correction makes less room for the influence described here. The moral answer to satanic deception begins with refusing to become its audience.

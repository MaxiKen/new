## Sūrah al-Nisāʾ 4:168

> Those who disbelieve and wrong themselves—surely Allah will neither forgive them nor guide them to any path

**Expanded Commentary**

**Those Who Disbelieve and Wrong Themselves — Neither Forgiven Nor Guided**

The verse states *inna alladhīna kafarū wa ẓalamū lam yakuni Allāhu li yaghfira lahum wa lā li yahdiyuhum ṭarīqā* — surely those who disbelieve and wrong, do injustice, will not be — He will not — forgive them nor guide them to any path, way. *Kafarū* — disbelieved — and *ẓalamū* — wronged — was explained with *ẓulm* — wrongdoing, injustice — as addition to *kufr*: disbelief plus hindering from God’s way (4:167) is wronging selves and others, as *“whoever commits evil or wrongs themselves”* (4:110) and *“whoever earns sin earns only upon himself”* (4:111) — wronging here is *ẓulm* that exceeds self. *Lam yakuni Allāhu li yaghfira lahum* — He will not forgive them — was explained with *li yaghfira* — to forgive — withheld where *shukr* and *īmān* would remove need for punishment (4:147) — here disbelief and wrong persist. *Wa lā li yahdiyuhum ṭarīqā* — nor guide them to any path, way, road — was read as no guidance to *ṭarīq* — way — the same *sabīl* believers follow (4:115) and God guides to noble ways (4:26); to be not guided to any *ṭarīq* is to be left where *“whoever He leaves to stray you will never find a way”* (4:88, 4:143) — no way. The sūrah has shown that *“whoever believed then disbelieved then believed then increased”* (4:137) — He will neither forgive nor guide them to the Right Way — there increased disbelief after oscillation removed forgiveness and guidance; here disbelief plus wrong does. Early reports linked *ẓalamū* to those who disbelieve and also wrong the Prophet by concealing his description, taking interest though forbidden (4:161), consuming wealth unjustly, claiming *we killed Messiah* (4:157) — wrongs upon denial, each increasing the unforgiven burden.

**A Verse That Follows Far Astray With Neither Forgiven Nor Guided**

Placed after *those who disbelieve and hinder from God’s way have certainly gone far astray* (4:167), this verse follows far astray with neither forgiven nor guided to any path — except Hell’s path. Scripture notes that association is not forgiven though what is less is for whom He wills (4:116); those who disbelieve and wrong combine association and *ṣadd* and *ẓulm*, so the unforgiven is not less. The principle that *“why should He punish if you are grateful and faithful? He is ever Appreciative”* (4:147) shows that forgiveness not given here is because gratitude and faith are absent, while disbelief and wrong persist. The reminder that *“with Him are both rewards He hears and sees”* (4:134) indicates that guidance to *ṭarīq* is with Him whose ownership is heavens and earth (4:126) and who suffices as Trustee (4:132); to be not guided to any *ṭarīq* is to be outside that trust. Where the Book warns *“do not take disbelievers as allies instead of believers lest you give clear warrant against yourselves”* (4:144) — *sulṭān mubīn* — wronging plus disbelief gives Him warrant to not forgive nor guide.

**Not Forgiven Nor Guided to Any Path — The Two Denied**

The two denials — *lam yakun li yaghfira* and *lā li yahdiyuhum ṭarīqā* — were explained as the Book’s two gifts denied: forgiveness that effaces — *ʿAfuww* — and guidance that shows way — *Hidāyah*. The promise that whoever wrongs themselves then seeks finds Him All-Forgiving (4:110) shows that forgiveness denied here is where seeking is absent; and that *“had it not been for grace they would have deceived you yet they deceive only themselves”* (4:113) shows guidance denied where deception persists. That *“Gardens for those who believe and do good”* (4:122) links forgiveness to Gardens and guidance to the straight path He guides to through mercy and grace (4:175); to deny both is to close both abodes. For the weak this is clarifying: where helpless hoped to be pardoned (4:99) were not among those who disbelieve and wrong but among those who cannot find way (*ḥīlah, sabīl*), forgiveness and guidance are hoped; here case is opposite — able, disbelieving, wronging, hindering.

**The *Ṭarīq* Not Given Is Any Path Toward Mercy**

The phrase *wa lā li yahdiyuhum ṭarīqā* — nor guide them to any path — with indefinite *ṭarīqā* — any way — was explained as no path toward mercy remaining, because *ṣirāṭ mustaqīm* — straight path — He guides to through mercy (4:175) is the only *ṭarīq* to Gardens; to be not guided to any *ṭarīq* is to be not guided to that. The declaration that *“He can remove you O humanity and bring others”* (4:133) frames removal as where no *ṭarīq* remains; and *“whoever defies the Messenger after guidance is clear and follows other than believers’ path is left to what he chose then burned”* (4:115) shows that leaving to chosen path is not guiding to true path. The next verse will set the only remaining way — Hell’s path forever, which is easy for Him.

**A Short Verse That Holds the Gate Open One Sentence**

Though only *“those who disbelieve and wrong — He will neither forgive nor guide them to any path,”* the verse holds the gate open one sentence: except Hell’s way — as the sūrah’s pattern holds — lowest depth without helper except those who repent (4:145–146) — the gate is repentance not asserted here because disbelief and wrong persist. The warning *do not go to extremes regarding faith* (4:171) — extremes of Trinity and of killing Messiah boasted are among those who disbelieve and wrong; to persist is to be not forgiven nor guided, as Hell’s path remains the only one left. That path’s ease for Him underscores that mercy elsewhere remains wide for the grateful and faithful (4:147).

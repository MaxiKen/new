## Sūrah al-Aʿrāf 7:78

> Then an ˹overwhelming˺ earthquake struck them, and they fell lifeless in their homes.

**Expanded Commentary**

**The Seizure**

The verse states that the earthquake seized the people, and the Book names the punishment of Thamūd in several forms across its accounts. The sūrah of Hūd reports that the blast seized them and that morning found them lying lifeless in their homes (11:67), the sūrah of the Inevitable states that they were destroyed by an overwhelming blast (69:5), the sūrah of the Moon states that a single blast was sent against them and left them like the twigs of a fence-builder (54:31), and the sūrah of Fussilat reports the blast of a disgracing punishment seizing them (41:17). The verse in front of us names the earthquake, and the other accounts name the cry and the blast.

The variety of the names in the Book's accounts describes the same event from different sides, and the sūrah's narrative supplies the one that fits its concern. The trembling of the earth states the removal of the ground on which the people had built, and the accounts that describe the blast state the sound that accompanied it. The verse in front of us supplies the sūrah's word for the seizure, and the accounts in the sūrahs of Hūd and the Rocky Tract use the same word for the same people.

**Morning Found Them Lifeless**

The verse states that the people were found in the morning lying lifeless in their homes, and the phrase describes the settlement as it was after the event: the dwellings standing and the people within them without life. The sūrah of Hūd uses the same words for the same people (11:67), and the sūrah of the Spider reports the destruction of the people of Shuʿayb in the same manner. The verse in front of us supplies the sūrah's statement of the end.

The description of the morning in front of us gives the event its setting in the ordinary time of the day, and the Book's accounts of the punishments frequently state the time at which they arrived. The people who had demanded the punishment in the evening were found lifeless in the morning, and the account's brevity states the completeness of the removal without describing the scene. The Book's narratives of the destroyed peoples use the same restraint.

**The Homes That Did Not Shelter**

The verse states that the people lay in their homes, and the Book's accounts of the buildings of Thamūd state that they had carved their dwellings in the mountains and felt secure in them (15:82). This verse places the people inside the structures that their own skill had raised, and the contrast between the security the buildings promised and the death within them is stated by the two passages together. The sūrah of the Poets reports the messenger asking his people how they persist in carving the homes in the mountains with skill (26:149), and the verse in front of us supplies the answer.

The Book's treatment of the works of the destroyed peoples as evidence appears in its addresses that direct the audience to the ruins, and the tradition records that the Prophet and his companions passed the remains of Thamūd on the way to Tabūk and were told not to enter the dwellings of those who had wronged themselves except in a state of weeping (al-Bukhārī 3380; Muslim 2980). The verse here supplies the statement of what happened inside the dwellings, and the tradition records the instruction that the ruins were to be read.

**The Sign and the People**

The verse follows the killing of the she-camel, and the Book's accounts of Thamūd connect the destruction to the act against the sign. The sūrah of the Sun states that they denied the messenger and hamstrung her and that their Lord destroyed them for their sin and levelled the city (91:14), and the sūrah of Fussilat states that they preferred blindness to the guidance that had been shown to them. The present verse states the seizure that followed, and the two verses together state the correspondence between the deed and its consequence.

The Book's accounts of the communities present the punishments as the fitting outcomes of the refusals, and the sūrah's narratives of Noah and ʿĀd used the same correspondence. The verse under discussion supplies the third instance, and the account that follows records the messenger's words over the people who had been removed.

**The Repetition of the End**

The verse completes the third community's story in the same manner as the two before it, and the repetition of the pattern is the sūrah's argument. The people who were given the sign and destroyed it were found lifeless in the places they had built; and the community's standing, its numbers, and its skill did not alter the end. The Book's accounts of the peoples that were destroyed state the same conclusion in each case.

The Book's use of the destruction as evidence for the audience appears in its addresses to the Makkans, and the sūrah's narratives place the audience in the position of the peoples whose histories it has been reading. Here the verse states the third outcome, and the sūrah's next verse records the messenger's farewell and the assessment of his work.

**Where the Verse Sits**

The verse stands seventy-eighth in the chapter, and it follows the killing of the she-camel and precedes the messenger's words. The verse that follows records Ṣāliḥ's turning away from the people and his statement that he had delivered his Lord's message and given sincere counsel (7:79), and the account then gives way to the fourth narrative. This verse supplies the destruction that closed the third account.

The reader who has followed the call, the reminder, the exchange, and the deed now reads the outcome. The earthquake seizes the people, the morning finds them lifeless in the homes they had carved, and the messenger is left to speak over what remains; and the sūrah's next verse states what he said.

**The Transition to the Next Verse**

The next verse reports that the messenger turned away from the people and said that he had conveyed his Lord's message and given them sincere counsel, and that they did not like sincere advisers (7:79). The destruction in front of us is followed by the messenger's farewell, and the account of Thamūd closes with the words that the sūrah's earlier messengers also spoke.

The two verses together end the third narrative of the series. The community is removed, the messenger states that his duty was performed, and the people are described by their refusal of the counsel; and the sūrah's following verses introduce the account of Lot with the same structure.

## Sūrah al-Naḥl 16:60

> **To those who disbelieve in the Hereafter belong all evil qualities, whereas to Allah belong the finest attributes. And He is the Almighty, All-Wise.**

**Expanded Commentary**

**Evil Qualities For Those Who Disbelieve In Hereafter**

Li-l-ladhīna lā yu'minūna bi-l-ākhirati mathalu al-saw' — for those who do not believe in Hereafter is evil description, evil qualities, example of evil. Mathal from m-th-l means example, likeness, description, attribute. Saw' from s-w-ʾ means evil, bad, wretched. Al-Ṭabarī explains that those who disbelieve in Hereafter have evil qualities: shirk, denial, arrogance, attributing daughters to Allah while hating daughters, burying daughters, assigning share to idols, calling revelation fables, plotting. Their mathal, example, description, is evil, because their beliefs and deeds are evil. Al-Rāzī says mathalu al-saw' means they have evil attributes, like ignorance, injustice, stinginess, etc., and also that parables and examples given for them in Qur'an are evil, like their judgment is evil as previous verse said.

**Finest Attributes Belong To Allah**

Wa-li-Allāhi al-mathalu al-aʿlā — and to Allah belongs highest, finest example, attributes. Mathalu al-aʿlā means highest description, finest attributes, most sublime example. Al-Ṭabarī says to Allah belongs finest attributes: oneness, power, knowledge, mercy, etc., as 59:24 says *To Him belong finest names* (59:24). Al-Zamakhsharī explains that al-mathalu al-aʿlā means Allah has attributes that are highest, beyond comparison, perfect, while disbelievers have lowest, evil. The contrast li-l-ladhīna lā yu'minūna mathalu al-saw' wa-li-Allāhi al-mathalu al-aʿlā creates sharp dichotomy: evil for disbelievers, finest for Allah. This is to show that attributing daughters to Allah while keeping sons for self is opposite of what should be: to Allah belongs finest, not what you hate.

**He Is Almighty, All-Wise**

Wa-huwa al-ʿAzīzu al-Ḥakīm — and He is Almighty, All-Wise. ʿAzīz from ʿ-z-z means mighty, powerful, invincible, from same root as ʿizzah, honor, might. Ḥakīm from ḥ-k-m means wise, judicious, from same root as ḥukm, judgment, wisdom. Al-Ṭabarī says ʿAzīz means Allah is mighty, able to punish those who attribute to Him what does not befit and who bury daughters, and Ḥakīm means wise in His creation, commands, and judgments. Al-Rāzī notes that ʿAzīz and Ḥakīm together indicate that Allah's might is with wisdom, not arbitrary, and His wisdom is with might, not weak. The seal appears often after mention of divine attributes and human evil to show that Allah is able to deal with evil and wise in how He does.

**Why This Verse Summarizes Previous Section**

Verses 57-59 described attributing daughters to Allah, gloomy face at birth of daughter, hiding, debating keep in disgrace or bury alive, evil judgment. Verse 60 summarizes: to those who disbelieve in Hereafter belongs evil qualities, to Allah belongs finest attributes, He is Almighty, All-Wise. The summary says: your attribution of daughters to Allah while hating daughters is evil quality that belongs to disbelievers in Hereafter, not to Allah; to Allah belongs finest, not what you hate. Your judgment to bury daughters is evil, not wise; Allah is All-Wise. Your attempt to oppose believers for sake of idols is from lack of might; Allah is Almighty. The verse therefore closes section on daughters and evil judgment with principle that distinguishes disbelievers and Allah.

**The Connection To Hereafter**

Li-l-ladhīna lā yu'minūna bi-l-ākhirati, to those who disbelieve in Hereafter, links evil qualities to denial of Hereafter. Al-Qurṭubī says denial of Hereafter leads to evil qualities because if no return, then no accountability, so people commit evil like burying daughters, attributing to Allah what they hate, assigning share to idols. Belief in Hereafter leads to finest attributes because it makes person mindful, humble, grateful. The sūrah opened with command at hand, Hour, and has repeatedly mentioned Hereafter as motivation for gratitude and warning. This verse makes explicit that disbelief in Hereafter is root of evil qualities.

**Linguistic Note On Mathal And Saw'**

Mathal from m-th-l means example, likeness, description, attribute, from same root as mithl, like, and tamthīl, parable. Saw' from s-w-ʾ means evil, bad. Mathalu al-saw', evil description, example. Al-mathalu al-aʿlā, highest example, finest attributes, from ʿ-l-w, high. ʿAzīz from ʿ-z-z, mighty. Ḥakīm from ḥ-k-m, wise. The verse's structure — to those who disbelieve in Hereafter belongs evil qualities, to Allah belongs finest, He is Almighty, All-Wise — with two li- phrases for belonging, and seal with two names, creates balanced, memorable summary that distinguishes disbelievers and Allah and ends with divine names that indicate power and wisdom to deal with evil.

**How This Verse Teaches To Attribute Finest To Allah**

The verse teaches to attribute to Allah finest attributes, not what you hate for yourself. Al-Rāzī says al-mathalu al-aʿlā includes all perfect attributes: knowledge, power, mercy, etc., and excludes all defects: having children, having partners, etc. To attribute daughters to Allah while hating daughters is to attribute to Allah what is not finest in your own view, which is injustice. True tawḥīd is to attribute to Allah finest, as 59:24 says finest names belong to Him, and to believe that disbelievers in Hereafter have evil qualities, not Allah. The believer should therefore describe Allah with what He described Himself, with finest attributes, and should avoid evil qualities like arrogance, denial, and gender bias that belong to disbelievers in Hereafter.

**A Lesson On Might And Wisdom**

ʿAzīz, Almighty, and Ḥakīm, All-Wise, together teach that Allah's might is wise and wisdom is mighty. He is able to punish those who bury daughters and attribute daughters to Him, and wise in how He delays, warns, and judges. The seal appears after evil judgment to indicate that evil judgment will be dealt with by One who is Mighty and Wise, so it will not go unpunished, and punishment will be wise, just. The verse therefore both warns and consoles: warns disbelievers that Almighty will deal with their evil qualities, consoles oppressed, including daughters buried, that All-Wise will establish justice.

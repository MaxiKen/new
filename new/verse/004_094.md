## Sūrah al-Nisāʾ 4:94

> O believers! When you struggle in the cause of Allah, be sure of who you fight. And do not say to those who offer you ˹greetings of˺ peace, “You are no believer!”—seeking a fleeting worldly gain. Instead, Allah has infinite bounties ˹in store˺. You were initially like them then Allah blessed you ˹with Islam˺. So be sure! Indeed, Allah is All-Aware of what you do.

**Expanded Commentary**

**When You Struggle — Be Sure of Who You Fight**

The verse opens *yā ayyuhā alladhīna āmanū idhā ḍarabtum fī sabīli Allāhi fa tabayyanū* — O believers, when you strike out, travel, struggle in the cause of God, be sure, verify, make clear. *Ḍarabtum fī sabīli Allāhi* — striking in the path — was explained as going forth to fight for the oppressed who cry for a helper (4:75) and where permission had just been given over the hostile who do not withdraw or offer peace (4:89–91). *Tabayyanū* — to clarify, to make patent — was read as verification before the sword, the opposite of the haste of spreading untested news of security or fear (4:83) and the haste that kills a believer except by mistake (4:92–93). The command to be sure is placed here because the field blurs: a man may offer peace while his people remain hostile, and the believer must distinguish. The sūrah has said that God knows best who enemies are and is sufficient as Guardian and Helper (4:45); to be sure is to act on what can be seen — the casting of peace — and leave the heart to the All-Aware. Early reports linked the verse to a company that met a lone shepherd who greeted them with *al-salām* and was killed for his flock as spoil; the verse came down to forbid that haste (Ṭ, Wāḥidī).

**Do Not Say to the One Who Offers Peace — You Are No Believer**

The prohibition *wa lā taqūlū li man alqā ilaykumu al-salāma lasta muʾminā* — and do not say to those who offer you greetings of peace, You are no believer — was explained with *al-salām* as the greeting of peace, *al-salām ʿalaykum*, or more widely the casting of peace, submission — *salām*. *Last muʾminā* — you are no believer — was read as denying the faith of one who displays its sign. To say it where peace is offered was explained as claiming to see beyond the offered word into the heart, while God alone knows what is in hearts (4:63) and the Book forbids that unveiling. The sūrah has commanded that when greeted, the answer be better or similar and that God is ever Reckoner (4:86); to reply to a greeting with disbelief is the inverse of that ethic. A widely reported incident illustrates the rule: a man — named in reports as Mirda or as a man of Sulaym — offered *al-salām* to a detachment led by Usāmah ibn Zayd, was killed, and his flock taken; the Prophet ﷺ reproved the killing and recited this verse (Ṣaḥīḥ al-Bukhārī 4591; Muslim 3025 in similar wording for the same theme). The verse’s wording covers both readings: do not deny belief to one who offers the Muslim greeting, and do not deny it to one who lays down arms and offers *salām* as peace.

**Seeking a Fleeting Worldly Gain**

The motive clause *tabtaghūna ʿaraḍa al-ḥayāti al-dunyā* — seeking a fleeting worldly gain, the عرض — chattel — of the nearer life — was explained as the spoil taken — the flock, the goods — for which the hasty hand declares another’s peace false. *ʿAraḍ* — عرض — was read as transient gain that passes, as the enjoyment of this world is little and the Hereafter better for the mindful (4:77). The sūrah has warned against devouring wealth unjustly (4:29) and against envying what God gave some over others (4:32) and against those who were given a portion and who wish you deviate for such gain (4:44). To kill a bearer of peace for his goods is to exchange the infinite bounties with God — *fa ʿinda Allāhi maghānim kathīrah* — for a flock. The clause *fa ʿinda Allāhi maghānim kathīrah* — instead, God has infinite bounties in store — was explained as the true spoil: provision multiplied, forgiveness and mercy and ranks (4:96), and the great reward promised to those who fight or to those excused (4:95). The contrast is not that gain is forbidden — lawful beyond limits is lawful as long as sought with wealth in marriage and trade by mutual consent (4:24–29) — but that the fleeting gain may not be sought by denying peace.

**You Were Initially Like Them, Then God Blessed You**

The reminder *ka dhālika kuntum min qablu fa manna Allāhu ʿalaykum* — you were initially like them then Allah blessed you with Islam — was read as the sūrah’s moral recall. *Kuntum min qablu* — you were before — was explained as that the believers themselves once concealed belief, or once offered peace that was not credited, or once were without the law that now protects them, until God’s grace guided them. The favour — *manna* — is the same word used where God favoured — *manna* — the oppressed? Here it recalls the grace without which they would have followed Satan except for few (4:83) and the way He lightened burdens because humanity was created weak (4:28). The sūrah has said that had they done what they were advised it would have been better and more reassuring and would have brought great reward and guidance (4:66–68), and that obedience places one with the blessed (4:69). To remember that you were as they were is to remove the pride that allows a hand to call another’s peace a lie. The sūrah elsewhere says *“you were on the brink of fire and He saved you”* (3:103); this is the same memory applied to the sword: you too were once a bearer of a tentative *salām* whose acceptance was your life.

**So Be Sure — God Is All-Aware of What You Do**

The double close *fa tabayyanū, inna Allāha kāna bi mā taʿmalūna khabīrā* — So be sure, God is All-Aware of what you do — repeats the opening *tabayyanū* to frame the prohibition with practice. *Khabīr* — All-Aware — was explained as awareness of outward act and inward motive, of whether the hand sought peace or spoil. The sūrah has closed the mistake law with All-Knowing, All-Wise (4:92) and the intentional threat with no exit but Hell unless turned from (4:93); here the close is awareness of deeds where mistake and intent look alike. The verse generalises the sūrah’s method: verify reports before spreading (4:83), verify belief before denying it (4:94), verify the fast as consecutive where atonement requires (4:92). For the weak for whom burdens were lightened, the command to be sure is a lightening: it spares the bearer of peace from the haste for gain, and it spares the striver from the burden of a killing that would demand Hell, anger, curse, and mighty torment unless repented. The next verse will set the striver against the one who stays behind, both promised good but not made equal (4:95).

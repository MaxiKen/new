## Sūrah al-Baqarah 2:212

> The life of this world has been made appealing to the disbelievers, and they mock the believers. Those who are mindful ˹of Allah˺ will rank above them on the Day of Judgment. And Allah provides for whoever He wills without limit.

**Expanded Commentary**

**The World Made Attractive**

The verse states that the life of this world has been made appealing to those who disbelieve and that they mock the believers, and it then states the standing of the mindful on the Day of Judgment. The sentence follows the account of the earlier community's signs and turns to the attitude of those who reject the message.

The Book's description of the world as attractive to a particular group gives the passage its subject, and the community's teachers read the sentence as an explanation of the mockery. What the reader receives is the account of a condition in which the present life supplies the standard and the community that lives by another standard is treated with contempt.

**The Mockery Described**

The sentence states that those who disbelieve mock the believers, and the Book's account of the early community includes several references to the ridicule that the believers faced. The commentators identified the people concerned with the idolaters of Makkah, the hypocrites of Madinah, or the leaders of the Jewish community who belittled the followers of the Prophet ﷺ for their poverty.

The readings reflect the circumstances of the community in both periods, and the community's teachers noted that the verse addresses a condition rather than a single group. What the reader receives is the account of a pressure that the community met in every stage of its history, and the sentence shows that the Book describes it in terms of the world's attractions.

**The Standard of the Two Groups**

The sentence sets the perspective of the disbelievers against the perspective of the mindful, and the community's scholars read the pair as the contrast that gives the verse its force. The one group measures by what it possesses in the present life, and the other measures by what the Book has promised.

The Book's use of the two descriptions elsewhere in the sūrah supplies the framework, and the reader is shown that the two groups see the same situation differently. What the sentence establishes is that the mockery of the one group and the patience of the other both follow from what each regards as valuable.

**The Standing on the Day of Judgment**

The sentence states that those who are mindful will be above them on the Day of Judgment, and the Book describes the reversal of standing in several passages about the final settlement. The community's teachers read the clause as the answer to the mockery that the sentence has described.

The wording concerns the standing rather than the persons, and the community's scholars noted that the Book's descriptions of the hereafter frequently reverse the positions that the two groups occupy in the present life. The reader carries away the assurance that the measure applied at the last day differs from the measure applied by those who mock, and the sentence leaves the community with the knowledge of where the two standards lead.

**The Reports About the Reversal**

The community's reports describe the reversal in several traditions, and one of them records the Prophet's ﷺ instruction to a companion to find the most exalted and the lowliest men present in the mosque, and then his statement that the one whom the community considered lowly was better in the sight of God than a world full of the other kind. The reports show how the community's teachers illustrated the sentence.

The material gives the verse its practical application, and the reader is told that the Book's standard has consequences in the community's own judgments. The sentence leaves him with the account of a teaching that the Prophet ﷺ impressed on his companions, and the sentence's statement about the final standing is the foundation of that teaching.

**Provision Without Limit**

The sentence closes with the statement that God provides for whom He wills without measure, and the community's scholars explained the clause as a reference to the abundance of what God gives. The wording contrasts the limit that the mockers assume with the boundlessness of the provision.

The Book's use of the phrase elsewhere includes passages about the reward of the believers, and the community's teachers read the clause as the assurance that accompanies the sentence's promise. The passage supplies him with the statement that the provisions of God are not measured by the standards of those who count them, and the passage continues with the account of humanity's condition before the prophets were sent.

**The Verse in the Passage**

The sentence stands between the account of the signs granted to the earlier community and the description of humanity as one community, and it supplies the contrast between the estimation of the world and the estimation of the Book. The reader is carried from the record of the past to the account of the prophetic mission, and the sentence's two standards remain before him as he reads.

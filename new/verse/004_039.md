## Sūrah al-Nisāʾ 4:39

> What harm could have come to them if they had believed in Allah and the Last Day and donated from what Allah has provided for them? And Allah has ˹perfect˺ knowledge of them.

**Expanded Commentary**

**What Harm Would Belief and Donation Have Done**

The question *wa mādhā ʿalayhim law āmanū bi Allāhi wa al-yawmi al-ākhiri wa anfaqū mimmā razaqahumu Allāh* — what harm could have come to them if they had believed in Allah and the Last Day and donated from what Allah has provided for them — follows the double portrait of the stingy who conceal bounty (4:37) and the ostentatious who spend for eyes without belief (4:38). Both portraits showed wealth held or spent without God as audience. This verse asks what would have been lost if the same wealth had been donated with belief. The answer implied is nothing; the harm is imagined, not real. Belief in God does not subtract from wealth; belief in the Last Day does not impoverish; donating *mimmā razaqahumu Allāh* — from what Allah provided — returns to the Giver a portion of what He gave. The Qur’an elsewhere says that donation does not decrease wealth but multiplies it, and the sūrah will immediately confirm that God never wrongs even by an atom’s weight and multiplies good many times over (4:40). The question therefore reframes generosity as costless in the true accounting and stinginess as the only costly choice. The harm feared — less to keep, less to show — is shown to be no harm at all.

**Belief in God, Belief in the Last Day**

The pairing *āmanū bi Allāhi wa al-yawmi al-ākhiri* gathers the two beliefs that correct the two corruptions. Belief in God corrects spending without Him as audience; to believe He gave the provision is to want to spend it where He sees. Belief in the Last Day corrects concealment and show, because both assume that the only return is immediate — either saved in the chest or praised in the market. The sūrah has prepared both beliefs: God is ever Watchful (4:1), sufficient as Reckoner (4:6), Witness over all things (4:33), All-Knowing and Most Forbearing (4:12), ever Merciful to you (4:29); the Last Day is the time when a witness is brought from every community and the Messenger is brought as witness against his own (4:41), when deniers will wish to be dust and will not be able to hide anything from God (4:42). To believe those two is to spend differently today, not because wealth is less loved but because it is more understood. The one who believes that provision came from God — *mimmā razaqahumu Allāh* — sees donation as returning, not losing; the one who believes that an atom’s weight of good will be multiplied (4:40) sees withholding as the only loss.

**Donated From What Allah Provided**

The phrase *mimmā razaqahumu Allāh* — from what Allah provided — was stressed as both the source and the limit of donation. The wealth proposed for spending is not an alien exaction but a portion of what was already given as *faḍl* — bounty — the same bounty the stingy concealed (4:37) and that others were told to ask for rather than crave (4:32). The Qur’an elsewhere says *“spend from what We have provided you before a Day comes where there is no trade”* (2:254), and the same urgency underlies this question. To donate from what was provided is to acknowledge receipt. That acknowledgment is itself worship, as the sūrah opened the ethic of kindness with *“worship Allah and associate none with Him, and be kind”* (4:36). Donation without belief, as in ostentation, is activity without worship; withholding with concealment is worship of wealth; donation *mimmā razaqa* with belief is worship that includes wealth. The amount is not specified — *mimmā* — from what, some of what — leaving room for the small provision for bystanders at distribution (4:8), the fair provision for a poor guardian (4:6), the gracious dowry (4:4), and trade by mutual consent (4:29). Each is a form of spending from what was provided.

**And Allah Has Perfect Knowledge of Them**

The closing *wa kāna Allāhu bihim ʿalīmā* — and Allah has perfect knowledge of them — answers the hidden calculation behind stinginess and ostentation. The stingy hide bounty as though God will not note the concealment; the ostentatious spend for people as though God will not see the motive. The sūrah has repeated that He is Watchful (4:1), Reckoner (4:6), Witness (4:33), and sufficient as Guardian and Helper (4:45); here knowledge is said to cover *them* — *bihim* — the very persons who made the choice. That knowledge does two things. For the one who feared harm from believing and donating, it reassures: the donation done with belief is known, and what is known is not lost; it will be multiplied (4:40) and brought with witnesses (4:41). For the one who thought hiding or showing would pass, it warns: the concealment and the show are also known, and on the Day when nothing can be hidden from God (4:42), the audience that was sought — people’s eyes — will not avail. The sūrah will ask *“have you not seen those given a portion of Scripture who trade it for misguidance”* (4:44) for the same reason; trading the Book for desire assumes knowledge will not follow, while the verse says it does. Perfect knowledge is therefore the quiet audit behind the question what harm would belief and donation have done; the answer, no harm, is guaranteed because the One who knows is also the One who multiplies and rewards out of grace.

**The Easy Alternative**

Read with the verses before and after, this question is the sūrah’s gentlest invitation to change. Between the diagnosis of stinginess and ostentation with Satan as evil companion (4:37–38) and the promise that not an atom’s weight is wronged and that good is multiplied many times (4:40), it places a simple reflection. No new law is added; no new penalty is named. The reader is asked to imagine the alternative path — belief and donation from what was provided — and to weigh its cost. The cost is none; the gain is knowledge, multiplication, and a great reward from grace. The alternative path’s companions are the opposite of the evil associate: Allah as Guardian and Helper (4:45), and the witnesses who will be brought to confirm. In that light, the sūrah’s earlier ethic of *iḥsān* to parents, relatives, orphans, poor, neighbours, companions, travellers, and those in possession (4:36) becomes not a burden but the very donation the question proposes. To have believed and to have given from what was provided is to have walked the clear, guided, and lightened way (4:26–28) while the chance remained, and to have left nothing for perfect knowledge to record as harm.

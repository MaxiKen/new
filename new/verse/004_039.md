## Sūrah al-Nisāʾ 4:39

> What harm could have come to them if they had believed in Allah and the Last Day and donated from what Allah has provided for them? And Allah has ˹perfect˺ knowledge of them.

### Expanded Commentary

**A question that exposes spiritual irrationality**

The verse asks what harm would have come to these people if they had believed in Allah and the Last Day and spent from what He provided. The question is not seeking information; it reveals the absurdity of refusing faith and generosity while claiming to pursue benefit. Belief would not impoverish them, and spending a portion of provision would not destroy their security. What prevents them is attachment, suspicion, and the desire to retain moral independence.

**Provision is entrusted, not self-created**

The verse attributes wealth to Allah’s provision without denying human labour. Work, skill, inheritance, and trade are real means, but none creates existence, opportunity, or the capacity to benefit from wealth. This attribution breaks the illusion that a wealthy person owes nothing to anyone. If provision is received, then gratitude and responsible circulation are rational responses.

**Faith and spending meet the hidden future**

Belief in the Last Day changes the calculation of cost and reward. A person who sees only the present may call charity a loss; a person who sees accountability recognizes that what is spent justly is preserved in a more lasting account. This does not authorize careless generosity or neglect of dependants. It orders priorities: fulfil obligations, avoid extravagance, and do not make fear of scarcity an excuse for indifference.

The closing statement that Allah knows them gives the question its force. He knows whether refusal comes from genuine hardship or a heart that hides behind hardship. He also knows the quiet gift that people overlook. The believer need not prove generosity to an audience; he must answer the Provider.

**The rationality of generosity**

Spending from provision does not deny the need to budget. It denies the belief that security is created by closing every hand. A sound household fulfils obligations, keeps reasonable reserves, and still leaves room for generosity. The verse challenges the fear that treats every act of giving as an attack on survival.

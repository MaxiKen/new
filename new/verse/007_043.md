## Sūrah al-Aʿrāf 7:43

> We will remove whatever bitterness they had in their hearts. Rivers will flow under their feet. And they will say, “Praise be to Allah for guiding us to this. We would have never been guided if Allah had not guided us. The messengers of our Lord had certainly come with the truth.” It will be announced to them, “This is Paradise awarded to you for what you used to do.”

**Expanded Commentary**

**The Removal of Rancour**

The verse states that whatever bitterness lay in the hearts of the people of the Garden will be taken away, and the Book uses the same language in its other descriptions of the Hereafter. The sūrah of the Rocky Tract states that God will remove whatever bitterness is in the hearts of the people of the Garden and that they will be on couches facing one another (15:47); the sūrah of the Troops closes its account of the believers' entry with the statement that they will be told to enter the Garden with no fear upon them. The change in front of us touches the heart rather than the surroundings, and it is stated before the rivers and the speech that follow it.

The removal of rancour completes the contrast with the condemned that the previous verses have drawn. The sūrah has described the communities cursing one another as they enter the Fire and the leaders answering the followers (7:38–39), and the verse in front of us states that the hearts of the saved hold nothing of that kind. The two passages describe the social condition of the two abodes, and the difference between them begins in the heart.

**Rivers Running Beneath**

The verse places rivers beneath the people of the Garden, and the image of the garden with rivers flowing under it is one of the Book's most frequent descriptions of the reward. The sūrah of the Cow introduces it in its account of the gardens given to those who believe and do good (2:25), and the sūrah of the Family of Imran, the sūrah of the Women, and the sūrah of the Table repeat it in their descriptions of the gardens prepared for the mindful. The verse in front of us uses the same picture at the end of the sūrah's account of the two outcomes.

The water in front of us follows the water that the people of the Fire request and are refused in the sūrah's later verses, where they ask the people of the Garden to pour water upon them or to give them what God has provided (7:50). The two passages belong to the same scene, and the rivers in front of us are the answer that the condemned will not receive.

**The Speech of the People of the Garden**

The verse reports what the people of the Garden say, and their words are an acknowledgement rather than a claim. They praise God for guiding them to this, they state that they would not have been guided if God had not guided them, and they confirm that the messengers of their Lord brought the truth. The Book's descriptions of the saved regularly include their gratitude, and the sūrah of the Troops records their words at the entry into the Garden and their statement that the promise of God has been fulfilled.

The three statements in front of us answer the attitudes that the sūrah has corrected in its audience. The claim to be guided without guidance appeared in the verse that described those who took the devils as masters while thinking themselves on the right path (7:30), and the certainty that the messengers brought the truth answers the demand for proofs that the sūrah's narratives describe. The words in front of us are the confession of the people who followed what was sent down.

**Guidance as a Gift**

The words of the people of the Garden state that their guidance came from God, and the Book presents guidance as a divine grant throughout. The sūrah of the Cow states that the guidance of God is the guidance (2:120), the sūrah of the Family of Imran states that God guides whom He wills to a straight path (3:73), and the sūrah of the Bee states that God leads astray whom He wills and guides whom He wills (16:93). The verse in front of us places the same acknowledgement in the mouths of the saved.

The statement does not remove the responsibility that the sūrah's commands have placed on its audience. The Book joins the divine grant of guidance to the human effort of following it, and the sūrah's opening command to follow what has been sent down stands in the same chapter as the words of the people of the Garden. What the verse in front of us records is the recognition that arrives when the outcome is seen, and the sūrah's reader is given it before he arrives.

**The Announcement of Inheritance**

The verse closes with the announcement made to the people of the Garden that what they have received is theirs for what they used to do. The word used for the garden's bestowal is that of inheritance, and the Book states that the righteous will inherit the Garden in several passages. The sūrah of the Believers states that the believers will inherit the highest part of Paradise, and the sūrah of the Troops records the words of the people of the Garden, who say that they have been made to inherit the land and can dwell in the Garden wherever they wish.

The commentary tradition explains the language of inheritance in two ways. One reading takes it as the statement that the Garden has been assigned to them as a settled possession rather than as a temporary grant. The other reads the inheritance as the dwelling of the people of Paradise, including what had been prepared for others who forfeited it. The two readings agree that the announcement in front of us names a settled and permanent gift.

**Where the Verse Sits**

The verse stands forty-third in the chapter, and it follows the statement of who the people of the Garden are and precedes the dialogue between the two groups. The sūrah then reports the call of the people of the Garden to the people of the Fire, the announcement of the curse upon the wrongdoers, and the description of the barrier and the heights. The verse in front of us supplies the first of those speeches.

The reader who has been given the two destinations in the verses before now hears the words of the first group. Their hearts are at peace, the water runs beneath them, and their speech is gratitude and confirmation. The chapter's next verses turn the scene towards the second group, and the two sides of the barrier are placed in view of one another.

**The Transition to the Next Verse**

The next verse reports that the people of the Garden will call out to the people of the Fire, asking whether they have found their Lord's promise to be true, and that a caller will announce the condemnation of the wrongdoers (7:44). The speech of the saved in front of us is followed by an address to the condemned, and the scene then moves to the barrier between the two groups.

The two verses together present the Hereafter as a place of speech. The saved declare the truth of what they were promised, and the condemned are addressed and made to answer; and the verses that follow describe the barrier, the heights above it, and the people who stand there looking at both sides.

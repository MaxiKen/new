## Sūrah al-Nisāʾ 4:45

> Allah knows best who your enemies are! And Allah is sufficient as a Guardian, and He is sufficient as a Helper.

### Expanded Commentary

**Allah knows the real adversary**

The verse tells the Prophet that Allah knows best who the enemies are. Human beings can identify open hostility, but they often miss concealed motives, changing alliances, and the enemy within the self. A person may speak like an ally while undermining a community; another may disagree publicly without wishing harm. Divine knowledge is more exact than social suspicion.

**Guardian and helper**

Allah’s sufficiency as Guardian means the Prophet and believers are not dependent on winning every argument or controlling every opponent. A guardian protects, manages, and preserves; a helper supports a person when his own means are insufficient. The two descriptions answer two fears: “Who will keep us safe?” and “Who will give us strength?”

This does not authorize passivity. The chapter commands justice, consultation, preparation, and truthful speech. Trust in God releases the believer from paranoia and despair, not from responsibility. One may take precautions without making fear the master of judgment.

**A rule for conflict**

When communities face religious hostility, they should identify specific harmful acts rather than demonize entire populations. Allah alone knows hearts, while humans judge observable conduct through evidence. The verse provides confidence for the persecuted and restraint for the powerful: no enemy is hidden from God, and no accusation is made true merely by repeating it.

**Security without paranoia**

The believer may identify threats, protect a community, and still refuse to imagine that every disagreement is enmity. Allah’s knowledge is a reason to use evidence rather than suspicion. Human beings see only behaviour and circumstance; they should judge those carefully while leaving hidden motives to the divine Guardian.

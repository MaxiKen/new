## Sūrah al-Nisāʾ 4:45

> Allah knows best who your enemies are! And Allah is sufficient as a Guardian, and He is sufficient as a Helper.

**Expanded Commentary**

**Allah Knows Best Who Your Enemies Are**

The statement *wa Allāhu aʿlamu bi aʿdāʾikum* — and Allah knows best who your enemies are — follows the exposure of those who were given a portion of the Book yet trade it for misguidance and wish you to deviate (4:44). The community in Madinah lived among groups whose enmity was not always declared as open battle; some concealed it behind counsel, distorted phrasing, and the wish to mislead. The sūrah has just said those sellers wish you to deviate from the path (4:44) and earlier that those who follow desires wish you to swerve greatly (4:27). To know the enemy in that setting is not to know only the battlefield opponent but to know the whisperer who trades guidance for influence. *Aʿlamu* — knows best — was read as both comfort and warning. Comfort, because the believer need not uncover every hidden motive; the One who is ever Watchful (4:1), Witness over all things (4:33), and All-Knowing, All-Aware (4:35) already knows. Warning, because the believer should not mistake a wish to misguide for friendship. The Qur’an elsewhere says *“He knows the treachery of eyes and what breasts conceal,”* and that knowledge is the background to naming enemies here without needing to list each name.

**Sufficient as Guardian**

The reassurance *wa kafā bi Allāhi waliyyā* — and Allah is sufficient as a Guardian — addresses the fear that follows knowledge of enmity. *Walī* — guardian, protector, near ally — was explained as the one who takes charge of affairs, who stands over the ward as a guardian stands over the orphan’s wealth with witnessing and reckoning (4:6). The sūrah has built the image of guardianship from the first verse — *“Allah is ever Watchful”* (4:1) — through debt and bequest order, marriage law, and the promise that not an atom’s weight is wronged but good is multiplied (4:40). To say He is sufficient as *Walī* is to say that those trusts need no other guarantor where enmity is present. Those who trade the Book for misguidance may wish you to deviate, but the Guardian who willed to make things clear and to guide to noble ways (4:26) is sufficient to keep the path clear. The phrase *kafā bi* — sufficient is — appears after detailed legislation to steady those who must apply it under pressure. A judge who must refuse a bequest that harms heirs (4:12), a guardian who must return an orphan’s wealth with witnesses, or a family that must appoint mediators (4:35) each faces pressure from those who benefit from blur; sufficiency as Guardian tells them the law has a Keeper beyond people.

**Sufficient as Helper**

The second sufficiency *wa kafā bi Allāhi naṣīrā* — and He is sufficient as a Helper — addresses the need for support, not only protection. *Naṣīr* — helper, giver of victory — was read as the One who aids against both open opposition and hidden misguidance. The sūrah has shown help as turning toward you when you turn (4:27–28) and as lightening because you were created weak (4:28). Help here is the active side of guardianship: not only guarding the limits but giving the community strength to keep them. The context was one where Jewish scholars in Madinah and others sought to draw believers away from the Messenger’s clarification, and the believers were still few and recently tasked with inheritance and marriage reforms that met resistance. To say God is sufficient as Helper is to say that the success of those reforms does not depend on the approval of those who wish deviation. The Qur’an elsewhere says *“if Allah helps you, none can overcome you,”* and the same help underlies the promise that if mediators desire reconciliation, God will bring harmony (4:35). Help is promised where desire is for repair, and guard is promised where knowledge of enmity is needed; together they cover the community’s public and private need.

**Knowledge, Guardianship, and Help Together**

The three clauses belong together: He knows enemies, He suffices as Guardian, He suffices as Helper. Knowledge without guardianship would leave the believer exposed; guardianship without help would leave him watched but not aided. The sūrah’s earlier Names joined knowledge with wisdom (4:11, 4:26), knowledge with forbearance (4:12), and knowledge with awareness (4:35); here knowledge is joined with sufficiency. That sufficiency was already implied when the sūrah said *“you are from one another”* (4:25) and *“you know not which are nearer to you in benefit”* (4:11), both statements that ask the believer to trust the apportioning of the One who knows. In a city where portions of Scripture were traded and where wording was twisted — *“we hear and disobey,” “herd us”* with a twist (4:46) — the believer might doubt whether clarity can survive distortion. The verse answers that clarity survives because its Guardian is sufficient; the believers’ task is not to outwit every distortion but to hold the Book, refer disputes to Allah and the Messenger (4:59), and trust the Helper who multiplies an atom’s weight of good (4:40).

**A Verse for a Community Under Pressure**

Placed where it is, between the exposure of sellers of misguidance (4:44) and the detailed examples of word distortion (4:46), this verse steadies the community before the examples are given. It tells them that the pattern they are about to hear — words taken out of context, greetings twisted, terms played with — is known better by God than by those who utter them, and that the response is not fear but reliance. The sūrah has moved from the Day when witnesses are brought and no speech can be hidden (4:41–42) to the daily guard of prayer with earth where water fails (4:43); this verse bridges the two by saying the same watch that secures the Day secures the street. The enemies who wish deviation (4:44) cannot remove the Guardian who suffices, and the deviation they wish cannot succeed where the Helper who suffices aids the weak whom He created weak. For the Medinan believers learning to divide estates, protect dowries, and pray with awareness, that double sufficiency was not a slogan; it was the condition that let them keep limits while surrounded by those who traded limits, and to do so without harshness, because the One who knows enemies is also the One who turns toward those who keep His path.

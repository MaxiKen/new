## Sūrah al-Nisāʾ 4:91

> You will find others who wish to be safe from you and their own people. Yet they cannot resist the temptation ˹of disbelief or hostility˺. If they do not keep away, offer you peace, or refrain from attacking you, then seize them and kill them wherever you find them. We have given you full permission over such people.

### Expanded Commentary

**The hostile double position**

The verse describes others who seek safety from both sides but cannot resist the temptation to return to hostility. It gives a condition for the severe response: they neither keep away, offer peace, nor refrain from attack. Their protected status is lost through active aggression, not through identity or private disbelief.

**Conditional permission and authority**

The permission to fight belongs to an organized conflict under legitimate authority. It is not an instruction to individuals to hunt suspected enemies. The language must be read with the rules of treaties, proportionality, protection of noncombatants, and the prohibition of treachery. Islamic jurisprudence treated these as serious legal questions, not as matters of personal anger.

The verse’s structure is itself a restraint. It names three routes away from violence—withdrawal, peace, and non-attack. A community that wants to remain faithful must seek those routes first and must not invent hostility to justify a desired war.

**The door away from violence**

The verse names withdrawal, peace, and non-attack as grounds for restraint. These are not minor details but routes the community must actively seek. A commander who prefers escalation must answer why peaceful alternatives were rejected, not merely assert that force was permitted.

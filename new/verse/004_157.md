## Sūrah al-Nisāʾ 4:157

> and for boasting, “We killed the Messiah, Jesus, son of Mary, the messenger of Allah.” But they neither killed nor crucified him—it was only made to appear so. Even those who argue for this ˹crucifixion˺ are in doubt. They have no knowledge whatsoever—only making assumptions. They certainly did not kill him.

**Expanded Commentary**

**Boasting — We Killed the Messiah, Son of Mary, Messenger of God**

The verse continues *wa qawlihim innā qatalnā al-masīḥa ʿĪsā ibna Maryama rasūla Allāhi* — and for their saying, their boast, *indeed we killed the Messiah, Jesus, son of Mary, the messenger of God*. *Qawlihim* — their saying — was explained as continuation of *“and for their denial and outrageous accusation against Mary”* (4:156) — now the boast. *Innā qatalnā al-masīḥa* — we killed the Messiah — *masīḥ* — anointed, Messiah — was read as Jesus, *ʿĪsā ibnu Maryam*, whose mother was slandered as *buhtān ʿaẓīm* (4:156) and whose son they claim to have killed. *Rasūla Allāhi* — the messenger of God — was explained as their own designation with irony: they say *messenger of God* mockingly while claiming to have killed him, as they said *our hearts are ghulf* (4:155) mockingly while hearts were sealed for disbelief. The sūrah has shown that killing prophets without right is part of condemnation (4:155) — to boast of killing the Messiah is the apex: to claim murder of a messenger sent without distinguishing (4:152) and of the Word through Mary and spirit from Him (4:171). Early reports noted this boast was by Jews who did not kill Jesus but claimed credit, while the attempt was by Roman authority at their instigation; the Qur’anic *we killed* attributes the pride to them as the planners, as夜 plotting is attributed though God is with them (4:108).

**But They Killed Not Nor Crucified — It Was Made to Appear**

The correction *wa mā qatalūhu wa mā ṣalabūhu wa lākin shubbiha lahum* — but they killed him not and they crucified him not, but it was made to appear so to them, made similar, made like — was explained as divine negation. *Wa mā qatalūhu* — they killed him not — *wa mā ṣalabūhu* — nor crucified — double negation was read as absolute: the act claimed — *qatalnā* — did not occur upon him. *Wa lākin shubbiha lahum* — but it was made to appear so to them, made to seem like him — was explained as a likeness cast onto another killed in his stead, or as the event made ambiguous so that observers differed, so that the boasters thought they killed him while God raised him. The sūrah has shown that *“with Him are both rewards He hears and sees”* (4:134) — He saw the plotting and raised. Classical reports differed: some said a disciple was made to resemble Jesus and was killed; others said one of the plotters was; all agree *shubbiha lahum* means resemblance was made. The verse was linked to *“God does not wrong even an atom”* (4:40): to claim killing the messenger is to claim wronging the atom mostWeight; the correction says wrong did not reach him.

**Those Who Argue for Crucifixion Are in Doubt — No Knowledge Only Assumptions**

The addition *wa inna alladhīna ikhtalafū fīhi la fī shakkin minhu mā lahum bihī min ʿilmin illā ittibāʿa al-ẓann* — and surely those who disputed, differed, concerning him — about him, crucifixion — are surely in doubt regarding him, in *shakk* — doubt, suspicion — not having any knowledge concerning it except following assumption, conjecture — was explained with *ikhtalafū* — to differ — the same difference the community was warned not to make between God and messengers (4:150) and not to fall into other than believers’ path (4:115). *La fī shakkin minhu* — in doubt regarding him — was read as that those who argue for *we killed* are not agreed: some say killed, some say crucified, some say neither, all in *shakk*, as the sūrah elsewhere says those given a portion distort words (4:46). *Mā lahum bihī min ʿilmin illā ittibāʿa al-ẓann* — they have no knowledge whatsoever except following *ẓann* — assumption — was explained as that their claim lacks *ʿilm* — knowledge — the sūrah has contrasted *ẓann* with *ḥaqq* — *promise ever true* (4:122) versus delusion (4:120) — *ẓann* is Satan’s currency. Where God bears witness to what He sent with His knowledge (4:166) and angels bear witness, the crucifixion claim is *ittibāʿ al-ẓann* — following conjecture — not witness.

**They Certainly Did Not Kill Him**

The seal *wa mā qatalūhu yaqīnan* — and they certainly did not kill him, not in certainty — was explained with *yaqīn* — certain, firm, sure — negated as *they killed him not with certainty* — they have no certain killing, and *they certainly did not kill him* — emphatic. The adverb *yaqīnan* was read as again negating the boast *qatalnā* — we killed — with certainty’s opposite: not only did they not kill, they have no certainty that they did. The sūrah has shown that *“He wills to make clear”* (4:26), *“taught you what you did not know”* (4:113), *“most secret talks have no good except charity, kindness, reconciliation”* (4:114) — boasting murder is not clear but doubtful; the decisive word is that they did not kill him in truth, as the next verse says *rather God raised him to Himself, He is Almighty, All-Wise* (4:158). For the weak this is an anchor: where loud negative speech is allowed to the wronged (4:148) and pardoning is higher (4:149), loud boasting *we killed the messenger* is the loudest *sūʾ* not allowed where no wrong was done to the boaster.

**A Verse That Completes Denial With Correction and Doubt**

Placed after breaking covenant, rejecting signs, killing prophets, claiming hearts are *ghulf* sealed (4:155), denial and mighty slander against Mary (4:156), this verse completes denial with boasting of murder corrected and doubt exposed. The litany — demand greater than a book, thunderbolt, calf after clear signs yet forgiven, Mount raised, gate with humility, Sabbath not transgressed, firm covenant, then breaking, rejecting, killing, saying *ghulf*, denying, slandering Mary, boasting killing Messiah — is the sūrah’s history of a people given Scripture before you commanded to be mindful (4:131) yet whose mindfulness was broken. The correction — not killed, not crucified, it was made to appear, disputants are in doubt, no knowledge but following assumption, certainly not killed — was read as the Book’s truth against assumption, as the Book was sent *bi al-ḥaqq* with truth (4:105) and *waʿduhu ḥaqq* (4:122). The next verse will set the truth: rather God raised him to Himself, He is Almighty, All-Wise (4:158), and every one of People of the Book will believe in him before his death and on Resurrection Day he will be witness against them (4:159).

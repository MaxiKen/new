## Sūrah al-Nisāʾ 4:73

> But if you return with Allah’s bounties, they will say—as if there had been no bond between you—“We wish we had been there with them to share the great gain!”

### Expanded Commentary

**Wanting the reward without the bond**

If victory and spoils come, the same laggards wish they had been present to share the gain. The verse depicts them saying it as though no bond had connected them to the believers. Their opportunism is exposed by the contrast: disaster makes them outsiders; success makes them relatives.

**The test of solidarity**

A genuine community is not defined by who appears at the distribution of benefit. It is revealed when members support one another during danger and loss. The verse does not prohibit rewarding those who were legitimately absent; it condemns the heart that claims belonging only when belonging is profitable.

The warning reaches beyond battle. Employees, citizens, relatives, and religious groups all face the temptation to distance themselves from difficult decisions and then demand equal credit. Honest participation includes accepting appropriate responsibility for outcomes, acknowledging those who carried the burden, and not rewriting the past when success arrives.

**Credit belongs with responsibility**

Success should not allow a person who abandoned a shared burden to rewrite his role. The ethical response is to honour those who served, offer fair support to their families, and welcome late participation without pretending that absence was sacrifice. Belonging is more than arriving at the distribution of gain.

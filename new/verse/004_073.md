## Sūrah al-Nisāʾ 4:73

> But if you return with Allah’s bounties, they will say—as if there had been no bond between you—“We wish we had been there with them to share the great gain!”

**Expanded Commentary**

**If You Return With Bounty, They Wish They Had Been There**

The verse continues *wa laʾin aṣābakum faḍlun min Allāhi la yaqūlanna ka an lam takun baynakum wa baynahu mawaddatun yā laytanī kuntu maʿahum fa afūza fawzan ʿaẓīmā* — but if you return with Allah’s bounties, they will say, as if there had been no bond between you, we wish we had been there with them to share the great gain. The contrast with the previous portrait is deliberate: at disaster — *muṣībah* — the lagger said *Allah has blessed us for not being there* (4:72); at bounty — *faḍl min Allāh* — the same person says *would that I had been there to share*. The sūrah has defined *faḍl* as bounty to be asked from God rather than craved from another (4:32), as what the stingy conceal (4:37), and as what would have caused no harm if believed and donated from (4:39). Here the bounty is gain after going forth — victory, safety, spoils — the *fawz ʿaẓīm* that the lagger now claims to want. The wish *yā laytanī kuntu maʿahum* — would that I had been with them — was read as regret not for missing obedience but for missing share. The sūrah has called the ultimate triumph *fawz ʿaẓīm* for keeping limits (4:13) and *afūza fawzan ʿaẓīmā* is the lagger’s use for worldly gain, showing that his measure of *fawz* is gain, not Gardens.

**As If There Had Been No Bond Between You**

The clause *ka an lam takun baynakum wa baynahu mawaddah* — as if there had been no bond between you — was explained as the lagger speaking as though he were a stranger who happened to miss a caravan, not as one among you — *minkum* (4:72) — whose absence at disaster was already a statement. *Mawaddah* — affection, bond, love — was read as the tie that should have made him go forth with them as one body, the tie the sūrah rooted in *“created from a single soul”* (4:1) and *“you are from one another”* (4:25) and appointed as heirs and as *mawālī* (4:33). To speak as if no *mawaddah* existed reveals that the bond was never acted as bond; it was kept as name while the foot stayed back. The Qur’an elsewhere says the hypocrites’ hearts are divided though you think them together; this speaking — wishing to be there only when gain is sure — proves it. The believers were told to take precautions and go forth in groups or together (4:71); the lagger who stays, then at gain says *would that I had been there*, treats going forth as a market to join when profitable, not as a duty to share. *Ka an lam takun* — as if there had never been — shows that for him the community’s *mawaddah* was conditional on outcome, which is the opposite of the *mawaddah* the sūrah asks mediators to seek where God brings harmony when desire is for repair (4:35).

**Great Gain as Worldly Measure**

The phrase *fa afūza fawzan ʿaẓīmā* — to share the great gain — was stressed as the lagger’s definition of greatness. The sūrah has called the Gardens with rivers and staying forever the ultimate triumph — *fawz ʿaẓīm* (4:13) — and martyrdom or victory in God’s cause followed by a great reward as honour (4:74). The lagger’s *fawz ʿaẓīm* is spoils, not that. To call spoils *ʿaẓīm* — great — while calling absence at disaster a blessing — *anʿama* (4:72) — shows that his great and his blessing are both measured without God as Helper whose help suffices (4:45). The sūrah will soon say *“let those who would sacrifice this life for the Hereafter fight in God’s cause; whoever fights whether martyred or victorious We will give a great reward”* (4:74), where *ʿaẓīm* returns as divine reward for fighting, not for wishing to have fought. The lagger’s *yā laytanī* is therefore not repentance; it is envy that the bounty came without him, as those who envy the people for God’s bounties (4:54) envied Book and wisdom given to Abraham’s family. The gain he now wants is the *faḍl* whose distribution he earlier begrudged.

**Would That I Had Been There — The Wish That Costs Nothing**

The wish *yā laytanī* was read as the cheapest substitute for action, the same substitute the hypocrites offered after disaster with *we intended nothing but goodwill* (4:62). Both utterances come after the moment where doing mattered — disaster or bounty — and both reframe absence as if it were presence. The sūrah has shown that God fully knows who deserves favour (4:70) and never wrongs even by a thread (4:49), so the wish cannot create share where presence was withheld. The believers who fought whether they achieved martyrdom or victory will be honoured with a great reward (4:74) because they went forth; the wisher who stayed will not be honoured as though he went, because *“whoever obeys Allah and the Messenger will be with the blessed”* (4:69) and obeying included going forth. The wish after bounty is therefore the counterpart to thanking God for not being there at disaster. Both reveal a heart that measures blessedness by outcome — safety or spoils — while the sūrah measures blessedness by obedience and favour from God — *faḍl min Allāh* (4:70). The two measures do not meet, which is why the wisher must speak *as if there had been no bond*; to acknowledge the bond would be to acknowledge that wishing after is not being with.

**A Portrait That Asks the Reader His Own Wish**

Placed between the lagger at disaster and the call to fight for the oppressed who cry for a helper (4:75), this portrait asks the reader to examine his own *yā laytanī*. The sūrah has shown that the straight path is not wishing after gain but doing what was advised — returning trusts, judging fairly, referring disputes, accepting judgment without resistance (4:66–68) — which would have been better and more reassuring and would have brought great reward and guidance. The lagger’s wish after gain is the opposite: he did not do, he was not with, he was not present as witness — *shahīd* — and now he wishes he had been. The sūrah leaves the wish hanging, as it left *how will it be* hanging (4:41, 4:62), so that the listener supplies the answer: had you wished to be with them before, you would have been. The next verses will address that wish directly: *what is it with you that you do not fight for the oppressed* (4:75) and *believers fight for God while disbelievers fight for the Devil whose schemes are weak* (4:76). The one who now says *fawzan ʿaẓīmā* for spoils is invited to seek the greater *fawz* — Gardens with vast shade — by fighting for the cause that makes spoils *faḍl* from God, not the measure of all, and by being there when the call to take precautions and go forth is given, not only when the gain is counted.

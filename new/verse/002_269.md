## Sūrah al-Baqarah 2:269

> Allah grants wisdom to whoever He wills. And whoever is granted wisdom is certainly blessed with a great privilege. But none will be mindful ˹of this˺ except people of reason.

**Expanded Commentary**

**The Gift of Wisdom**

The sentence states that God grants wisdom to whom He wills, and the community's teachers read the verse as the statement about the gift that orders the whole of a person's conduct. The passage has treated the gift of wealth and the fear that surrounds it, and the sentence here names the understanding that governs both.

The wording places the granting of wisdom with the decision of God, so that the gift is described as a bestowal rather than an attainment. The community's scholars noted the form of the phrase, since the sentence names the One who gives before the description of those who receive.

The Book's use of the term covers the understanding of the revelation and the conduct that follows from it, and the teachers gathered the passages in which the word appears. The sentence in this place supplies the statement of its value.

**The Interpretations**

The commentators explained the wisdom named in the sentence in several ways. Some held that it is the knowledge of the Book, including its clear and its figurative passages and its rules, while others described it as the reverent awareness of God which the reports present as the beginning of the matter.

The teachers also recorded the reading which connects the term with the ability to judge and to understand the affairs of the community, and a further discussion among the scholars concerned the relation between wisdom and the office of prophethood. The positions were set out and examined, and the community preserved the discussion in its books of commentary.

The readings share the sense that the gift concerns the understanding of what the Book has revealed and the use of that understanding in the conduct of life. The community's scholars drew the two together, so that the wise person is described as one who knows and who acts upon what he knows.

**The Much Good**

The sentence adds that the one who has been given wisdom has been given a great good, and the community's teachers read the clause as the evaluation that the verse places upon the gift. The Book's choice of the phrase has been noticed, since the sentence does not describe the amount of the good but its kind.

The commentators explained the clause as the statement that the gift exceeds what a person can measure, since the wisdom of a servant orders his dealings in this life and prepares his account for the next. The community's preachers used the phrase when they encouraged the believers to seek understanding in the Book.

The wording also places the term of the good at the side of the loan and the harvest that the passage has described. The wealth of the community is described in the earlier verses as a means, and the sentence here names the possession that governs the use of the means.

**The People of Reason**

The sentence closes with the statement that none takes the reminder except those who possess understanding, and the community's scholars read the clause as the description of those who receive the instruction of the passage. The Book uses the same phrase in the verses that call its community to consider the signs.

The teachers drew from the clause the lesson that the attention of the hearer is itself a gift, so that the perception of the good named in the sentence depends on the mind that God has opened. The community's scholars read the passage as the statement that the reason of a person is honored by the Book's address.

The phrase also supplies the ground of the community's study of the revelation, since the Book expects its readers to bring their understanding to its verses. The teachers of the community built their method on the sentence.

**The Reports About Wisdom**

The reports of the community preserve a saying of the Prophet ﷺ that names the two things for which envy is permitted, the wealth that is spent in the way of truth and the wisdom by which a person judges and teaches. The material shows the value that the community attached to the gift, and the teachers used the saying when they explained the sentence.

The commentators also discussed the relation of the verse to the passage in which the community is commanded to spend, and they read the two as parts of a single instruction. The fear that the earlier verse describes is answered by the promise, and the promise is completed by the gift of the understanding that directs it.

The teaching of the community preserved the connection between the two subjects, so that the pursuit of wisdom and the discipline of property were presented as parts of one conduct. The sentence in this place supplies the link between them.

**The Place in the Passage**

The verse stands between the instruction about the threat of poverty and the statement about the wealth that is given, and the community's teachers read the arrangement as the sequence of the passage's teaching. The community is commanded to give, the obstacle to the gift is named, the promise that answers it is stated, and the gift of understanding is described.

The teachers of the community drew the lesson that the passage presents the whole of the matter, from the command to the disposition that sustains it. The sentence in this place supplies the gift by which the community's members read the instruction and act upon it.

The verses that follow return to the subject of the gift and the account that is kept of it, so that the section continues its treatment of charity. The sentence therefore stands within the passage as the statement of the understanding that the rest of the instruction requires.

**What the Verse Establishes**

The sentence establishes that wisdom is a gift of God to whom He wills, the identification of the wisdom with the understanding of the Book and the conduct that follows it, the greatness of the good that it brings, and the reception of the reminder by those who possess understanding. The community's teachers used the verse when they encouraged the study of the revelation, and the sūrah's next verses return to the obligations of the community in the matter of its wealth.

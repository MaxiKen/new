## Sūrah al-Aʿrāf 7:25

> He added, “There you will live, there you will die, and from there you will be resurrected.”

**Expanded Commentary**

**The Cycle Stated in Three Clauses**

The verse states that the people of the earth will live there, die there, and be brought forth from it, and the three clauses describe the whole course of human existence in the world. The sūrah of Ṭā Hā states the same arrangement in its account of the creation of the human being from the earth and the return to it and the bringing forth from it again (20:55), and the sūrah of Noah describes the growth of human beings from the earth and the return to it in its account of the resurrection.

The verse follows the command to descend and the statement of the provision that the earth contains, and it supplies the end of the arrangement. The dwelling is temporary, the provision is temporary, and the exit from the world is as certain as the entry into it. The Book's warnings about the reckoning rest on the sequence that the verse in front of us states in a single sentence.

**The Earth as the Place of Living and Dying**

The Book describes the earth as the source and the destination of the human body, and its accounts of creation state that the first human being was formed from what the earth contains. The sūrah of the Cave states that the human being is created from clay, and the sūrah of the Pilgrimage describes the stages of the creation of the human being from dust. The verse in front of us states the return to the same material, and it places the burial of every generation within the arrangement.

The Book's teaching about death as a transition rather than an end appears in the clause that follows the mention of dying. The bringing forth from the earth is the resurrection, and the sūrah of the Heights will describe the reckoning that follows it in the verses of its opening section and in its later descriptions of the day. The three clauses in front of us are therefore the frame of the whole chapter's teaching about human destiny.

**The Bringing Forth From the Earth**

The Book uses the image of the earth giving up its dead in its descriptions of the resurrection, and the sūrah of the Earthquake opens with the earth's convulsion and the casting out of its burdens (99:1–2). The sūrah of the Clatterer describes the day on which people will come out in scattered groups to be shown their deeds (99:6), and the sūrah of Qāf states that the earth will be split open and the dead will come forth quickly (50:44). The verse in front of us states the event in the plain language of the return from the ground.

The Book's argument for the resurrection often rests on the first creation, and the sūrah of the Romans states that the One who originated the creation will repeat it. The sūrah's audience knew the sequence of life and death from its own experience, and the verse in front of us states the third clause as the completion of a process that has already happened twice.

**The Verse and the Present Life**

The verse's description of the earth as the place of living and dying is accompanied in the Book by many statements about the brevity of the time spent there. The sūrah of the Heights will later state that the enjoyment of the world is little compared with the life to come, and the sūrah of the Family of Imran places the desires of the present life in a list whose conclusion is that the reward of God is better. The verse in front of us supplies the frame within which those statements make sense.

The Book's addresses to the community regularly use the term of the earth as a reminder, and the sūrah of the Cave states that the present life is only play and amusement while the home of the Hereafter is better for those who are mindful. This verse states the arrangement that the reminder describes, and it does so without leaving the narrative of the first pair.

**Where the Verse Sits**

The verse stands twenty-fifth in the chapter, and it closes the account of the fall and the descent. The verses that follow turn to the children of Adam with instructions about clothing, food, and worship, and the narrative of the first pair gives way to the address to the people who live on the earth. The passage in front of us completes the passage from the Garden to the world.

The reader of the sūrah has been given the creation, the honour, the refusal, the sentence, the temptation, the fall, the confession, and the descent. The verse under discussion adds the terms of life in the world, and the sūrah then turns to what the children of Adam are to do with the time between their birth and their return to the ground.

**The Terms of the Arrangement**

The Book's statements about death and resurrection appear in its arguments against those who denied the raising, and the sūrah of the Heights itself will address the audience's doubts about the day. The text in front of us states the arrangement without argument, and it places the audience inside it by addressing people who will live, die, and be raised like the generations before them.

The Book's practice of stating the resurrection as a fact rather than a proposition appears in the sūrahs of the last things, where the scenes of the day are described before they are defended. The verse before us applies the method to the life of the individual, and the sūrah then moves to the instructions that the children of Adam are given for the interval.

**The Transition to the Next Verse**

The next verse addresses the children of Adam, states that God has sent down clothing to cover their nakedness and as an adornment, and adds that the clothing of reverence is better, and it closes with the observation that this is among the signs of God so that people may remember (7:26). The address to the descendants follows directly from the account of the first pair's exposure.

The two verses together move the sūrah from the story to the instruction. The first pair lived and died on the earth, and their descendants are told what to wear, what to eat, and how to worship, with the account of the fall as the background of the commands.

## Sūrah al-Tawbah 9:108

> Do not ˹O Prophet˺ ever pray in it. Certainly, a mosque founded on righteousness from the first day is more worthy of your prayers. In it are men who love to be purified. And Allah loves those who purify themselves.

**Expanded Commentary**

**A Sacred Act Must Stand on a Sound Foundation**

The Prophet is told not to pray in the mosque built for harm. Prayer is not used to bless a corrupt purpose merely because the place carries the name of mosque. The command establishes a principle: worship must be joined to the moral foundation of the institution in which it is publicly performed.

This does not mean that Allah's worship depends on expensive buildings or perfect people. It means that public religious endorsement cannot be separated from truth. If a place is deliberately used to divide believers and aid hostility, praying there would give a false appearance of approval. A sound community protects the meaning of worship by refusing to let sacred acts certify a harmful project.

**Righteousness Begins Before the Building Opens**

The worthy mosque is founded on righteousness from the first day. Its purpose is set before the walls rise. The builders ask whom the place will serve, how it will be funded, what teaching it will support, and whether it will bring people toward Allah or toward factional pride.

Foundations are moral before they are architectural. A building raised through injustice carries a question that decoration cannot answer. A modest place built through lawful means, sincere prayer, and service may be more valuable than a grand institution designed to display influence. The first intention shapes what later activity can become.

**Purification Includes the Soul and the Body**

The men in the righteous mosque love purification. This includes cleansing for prayer and the care of bodily cleanliness, but the deeper meaning is freedom from sin, rebellion, and corrupt intention. Physical cleanliness prepares the body for worship; moral purification prepares the heart to receive worship's effect.

The verse joins these forms without reducing one to the other. A person can be outwardly clean and inwardly unjust, or inwardly sincere while struggling with ordinary physical limits. The believer seeks both. He cleans the place, the body, the wealth, the speech, and the intention as far as ability allows. Allah loves the effort to become clean in the whole sense.



A righteous mosque supports purification through prayer, knowledge, honest companionship, and service. It makes repentance possible rather than using shame to keep people away. It teaches people to leave gossip, fraud, abuse, and pride at the door, not merely to perform a ritual and return unchanged.

The institution's leaders carry a special trust. They should keep funds transparent, protect worshippers from exploitation, and correct wrongdoing without making themselves unquestionable. A place that claims to purify but hides abuse has contradicted its purpose. The love of purification is shown by the willingness to clean what the community would rather conceal.

**Public Endorsement Requires Discernment**

The Prophet's refusal to pray in the harmful mosque teaches leaders to distinguish personal kindness from institutional endorsement. He could speak to a person without blessing the project that person built. Modern leaders likewise may acknowledge a human being's dignity while refusing to inaugurate an institution whose aims are corrupt.

This protects religious authority from being used as a stamp. A famous guest, respected teacher, or public prayer can make a project appear trustworthy. The person accepting an invitation must ask what his presence will communicate. If it will strengthen a harmful falsehood, refusal can be more faithful than politeness.

**Allah Loves Those Who Seek Purity**

The verse ends with Allah's love. Purification is not only a demand; it is a path toward divine affection. The believer does not clean his heart to impress people. He seeks Allah's love by removing what blocks obedience and by maintaining the conditions that support worship.

A community should celebrate this love through practical care. Help people access clean water, safe worship, sound knowledge, and honest repentance. Make purity honorable without turning it into a tool for despising those who are still struggling. The righteous mosque is known by the people it produces: truthful, humble, generous servants who carry purification into the world beyond its walls.

The love of purification also gives a test for religious leadership. A leader who welcomes correction, keeps accounts clear, and protects the weak is helping people become clean before Allah. A leader who hides wrongdoing to preserve prestige is turning a place of worship into a cover. The believer should not confuse polished surfaces with purity. He looks for honest prayer, fair treatment, and a willingness to remove what corrupts. Allah loves those who purify themselves, so the community should make purification easier through practical repentance rather than making religious respect a shield for abuse.

Purification also includes the care of the earth and the body used for worship. Clean water, honest facilities, and safe access are not secondary when they help people pray with dignity. Yet outward cleanliness must lead to inward cleanliness. A beautifully maintained mosque that ignores theft, abuse, or arrogance is not displaying the love Allah names. The people who love purification keep examining their motives and their systems. They remove dirt from the place, falsehood from the accounts, and resentment from the heart. Allah's love gives this work both seriousness and hope.

A person can pursue purification by examining what he welcomes into his heart while he performs outward worship. Does he want Allah's pleasure or the authority that religious appearance brings? Does he forgive correction or punish anyone who questions him? Does he maintain clean dealings with those who have no influence over him? These questions are not a reason to abandon worship when the heart is mixed. They are a reason to keep worship and let it expose what needs cleansing. Allah's love encourages the servant to continue the process instead of defending the impurity.

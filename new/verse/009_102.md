## Sūrah al-Tawbah 9:102

> Some others have confessed their wrongdoing: they have mixed goodness with evil. It is right to hope that Allah will turn to them in mercy. Surely Allah is All-Forgiving, Most Merciful.

**Expanded Commentary**

**Confession Opens the Door That Excuses Close**

The people in this verse confess their wrongdoing. They do not manufacture a reason that makes the wrong disappear, and they do not claim that one good deed cancels every failure. Their honesty places them in a different category from those who hide behind oaths. A confession does not complete repentance, but it begins the truthful movement toward it.

This is why the verse gives permission to hope for Allah's mercy. Hope is not based on pretending that the evil was small. It is based on recognizing that Allah's forgiveness is larger than a servant's failure when the servant returns sincerely. A person who admits the truth can receive correction, repair what was damaged, and stop repeating the act that caused the guilt.

**Good and Evil Can Be Mixed in One Life**

The verse describes a mixed record. These people have righteous deeds and sinful deeds. Such mixture is a realistic picture of human life, but it is not an excuse for complacency. The good does not authorize the evil, and the evil does not make the good meaningless. Each act has its own place before Allah.

A servant should resist two false calculations. He must not say, “I do enough good, so my wrong is harmless.” He must not say, “I have done wrong, so every good act is pointless.” The correct response is to preserve the good, abandon the evil, and ask Allah to purify what has been mixed. Repentance reorganizes the life instead of merely adding another religious phrase to it.

**Honesty Is Better Than Religious Performance**

The confessors may have participated in earlier service, yet they do not use that history to excuse the failure. This shows that religious memory can be used in two ways. A servant may remember past good with gratitude and seek strength to return, or he may use past good as a certificate that protects present corruption.

Truthful repentance chooses the first path. It says, “I have done good, and I have also wronged myself.” Such speech is not self-hatred. It is an accurate account that allows a person to seek the right remedy. The community should make room for this honesty while still requiring the person to repair the harm he caused.

**Hope Must Be Joined to Change**

The hope in the verse does not authorize a person to continue the wrong. If someone hopes for forgiveness while planning to repeat the same betrayal, the hope has become a cover for desire. True hope makes the servant more willing to change because he believes Allah will accept the effort.

Change may require restitution, apology, new boundaries, or the surrender of an advantage. It may also require accepting that people will not trust him immediately. Allah's mercy does not force others to forget the harm at once. The repentant person works patiently, knowing that a restored relationship is a gift rather than a right he can demand.

**Mercy Is Greater Than the Mixed Record**

The ending names Allah as All-Forgiving and Most Merciful. Forgiveness does not mean that Allah fails to see the evil. It means He can remove its claim when the servant returns. Mercy does not make the servant's choices irrelevant; it gives him a path by which those choices can be repaired.

This protects the believer from despair after a serious failure. The person who feels ashamed may think that he has disqualified himself from all good. The verse says that confession and hope still have a place. He should turn toward Allah with the truth in his hands, not wait until he can present a perfect story.

**A Community Can Support Repentance Without Erasing Justice**

Believers should help the confessor return. They can advise, protect him from despair, and create a practical route for repair. They should not use his confession as entertainment or keep repeating it after the matter has been resolved. At the same time, support does not mean denying the people harmed or restoring every responsibility immediately.

Allah's mercy teaches the community how to be both firm and hopeful. Name the wrong, require change, and make return possible. The mixed record can be purified through sincere action. A person is not trapped by his worst decision when he stops defending it and turns to the Forgiving, Merciful Lord.

A mixed record should be examined with precision. The servant identifies the good that must be preserved and the evil that must be abandoned. He does not let shame make him reject every blessing, and he does not let past service excuse present wrongdoing. If another person's right was taken, he returns it. If trust was damaged, he accepts a period in which trust must be rebuilt. If the sin was private, he asks Allah for forgiveness without advertising it; if public harm was caused, he repairs publicly enough to prevent further harm. Mercy becomes believable when confession changes conduct.

Repentance also changes how a person speaks about his past. He does not use the phrase “mixed deeds” to make the evil sound harmless, and he does not use the evil to deny that Allah gave him opportunities for good. He names both accurately. The good becomes a foundation for renewed service, while the evil becomes a warning that requires practical limits. If he was trusted with money, he keeps clearer records. If he harmed someone with speech, he stops repeating the story. If he avoided prayer, he protects its time. Mercy enters through honest details rather than through a vague declaration that everything is now fine.

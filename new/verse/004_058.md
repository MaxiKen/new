## Sūrah al-Nisāʾ 4:58

> Indeed, Allah commands you to return trusts to their rightful owners; and when you judge between people, judge with fairness. What a noble commandment from Allah to you! Surely Allah is All-Hearing, All-Seeing.

### Expanded Commentary

**Trusts include every responsibility**

Allah commands the return of trusts to their rightful owners. The command covers property, confidential information, public office, professional competence, promises, and the rights of people placed under one’s care. A trust is defined by another person’s legitimate claim, not by whether the custodian finds it convenient to return it. The orphan’s inheritance in the opening of the sūrah is one example; government and judicial authority are others.

**Judging with fairness**

The second command addresses those who decide between people. Fair judgment requires hearing both sides, examining evidence, avoiding conflicts of interest, and resisting wealth, kinship, fear, and affection. It applies to judges, leaders, employers, parents, mediators, and anyone whose decision affects another’s rights. A person need not hold a courtroom title to exercise judgment.

**The Prophetic context of the key**

Classical reports connect the verse with the Prophet’s return of the key of the Kaʿbah to its custodians after the conquest of Makkah, even though political victory might have made confiscation appear possible. Whether one focuses on that report or on the verse’s general wording, the lesson is clear: triumph does not cancel a trust. The enemy’s former conduct does not give the victor permission to take what belongs to others.

Allah hears the claims and sees the decisions. This protects the person whose trust is returned quietly and the litigant whose case is judged without public recognition. The verse is a charter for ethical administration: competence receives responsibility, responsibility returns what it holds, and authority is measured by justice rather than possession.

**Office is a trust**

A public appointment, a professional license, or a leadership role belongs to the people served before it belongs to the office-holder. Returning the trust means using competence for its proper purpose and handing authority over when the term ends. Fair judgment then becomes the test by which stewardship is known.

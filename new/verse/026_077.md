## Sūrah al-Shuʿarāʾ 26:77

> They are ˹all˺ enemies to me, except the Lord of all worlds.

**Expanded Commentary**

**Abraham Draws a Boundary Around His Allegiance**

After questioning the idols and the inherited worship surrounding them, Abraham declares that they are all enemies to him except the Lord of all worlds. The sentence is a statement of allegiance. He will not remain spiritually neutral between the Creator and objects that compete with Him. Worship requires a clear direction, and Abraham names the Lord as the one relation he accepts.

The wording “all” gives the declaration breadth. Abraham does not preserve one favorite idol as a harmless exception, nor does he allow the community to divide worship among many objects. The false objects stand on one side, and the Lord of the worlds stands on the other. The boundary is not drawn by personal taste. It is drawn by the difference between the created and the Creator.

**Enmity Names the Moral Effect of Idolatry**

Calling the idols enemies may seem stronger than calling them powerless. Abraham is identifying what false worship does to a human being. An object that cannot hear, benefit, or harm becomes an enemy when people give it the devotion, fear, and obedience that belong to God. It turns the heart away from reality and trains the community to protect an empty authority.

The enemy here is not a personal rival in the human sense. It is a false object and the corrupting allegiance built around it. Idolatry damages judgment before it damages anything else. Once a community calls a powerless thing sacred, it can make injustice appear religious, silence questions, and punish those who seek truth. The idol becomes an enemy because of the deception it sustains.

**The Lord of the Worlds Is the Sole Exception**

Abraham's exception is not one more member of a list of acceptable powers. The Lord of the worlds is categorically different from the idols. He is not a localized object competing with other localized objects. He is the Lord whose authority encompasses every world, every creature, and every power. The contrast is between universal lordship and dependent things.

This title also prevents monotheism from becoming a private preference. Abraham does not say that the Lord is his favorite deity among several possibilities. He names the Lord of all worlds, whose claim extends beyond his family and people. The Lord is worthy of worship because He is the source and governor of all existence, not because Abraham happens to belong to a community that chose Him.

**Love of God Requires Rejecting False Lords**

The verse's order is meaningful. Abraham identifies the false objects as enemies before presenting the Lord's acts of creation, guidance, provision, and healing. A heart cannot fully love God while treating rivals as worthy of worship. The rejection is not an appetite for hostility. It is the clearing away of what prevents undivided devotion.

This does not require hatred of every person who is confused or opposed. Abraham is addressing idols and the false loyalties attached to them. Believers may speak firmly against a doctrine while remaining capable of mercy toward people. The moral target must be distinguished carefully. False worship is rejected because it harms people; people are not reduced to the error they may be invited to abandon.

**The Declaration Resists Compromise**

A community may try to preserve its idols by giving them a secondary role. Abraham's sentence refuses that arrangement. The objects are not acceptable simply because the Lord is also mentioned. If they receive prayer, fear, sacrifice, or ultimate trust, they remain rivals in practice. Monotheism is not maintained by adding God's name to a system that still assigns worship to created things.

The declaration therefore challenges a common form of compromise: acknowledge God as supreme while continuing to depend on intermediaries as sacred powers. Abraham's boundary asks what the heart actually does. A person may use created means within the order God established, but he must not turn those means into lords. The exception remains exclusive because God's lordship is exclusive.

**False Objects Can Become Enemies Through Human Attachment**

An idol has no independent power, yet the worship of it can produce real consequences in human life. People may spend resources maintaining it, organize social rank around it, or condemn dissenters in its name. The object itself remains powerless, but the attachment becomes an active source of distortion. Abraham's language captures that danger without granting the idol true divinity.

The community is not harmed because a stone secretly attacks it. It is harmed because people allow the stone to command their loyalties. False worship can justify exploitation, preserve inherited error, and make moral responsibility disappear behind ritual. The idol is an enemy as a center of deception, not as an independent rival to God.

**The Declaration Makes Abraham's Identity Visible**

Abraham's identity is defined by what he refuses to worship and whom he accepts as Lord. He is not merely a critic of his society. He is a servant whose criticism grows from devotion to the Lord of all worlds. Rejection without worship can become empty opposition; worship without rejection can leave false gods untouched. Abraham holds both together.

That combination matters for every reforming voice. A person who opposes an error must know what truth he serves, and a person who claims devotion to God must be willing to abandon whatever competes with Him. Abraham's sentence is therefore both negative and positive: it breaks the false allegiance and establishes the true one.

**The Boundary Gives the Community a New Center**

The people have been centered around ancestors and idols. Abraham offers a different center: the Lord of all worlds. This new center is not a local shrine, a family possession, or a symbol controlled by a priesthood. It is the living Lord whose authority cannot be confined to an image. The community's identity can now be rebuilt around worship rather than inherited objects.

The verse is thus a declaration of spiritual independence. Abraham will not let custom determine his god, nor will he let powerless objects determine his fear and hope. By turning to the Lord, he rejects the enemies created by false worship and finds the only allegiance that can gather every part of life under truth.

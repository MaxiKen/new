## Sūrah al-Baqarah 2:277

> Indeed, those who believe, do good, establish prayer, and pay alms-tax will receive their reward from their Lord, and there will be no fear for them, nor will they grieve.

**Expanded Commentary**

**The Description Stated**

The sentence names the believers who perform the righteous deeds, establish the prayer, and give the alms, and it states that their reward is with their Lord. The verse follows the sentence about the blighting of the practice, so that the community is shown the conduct that stands beside the dealings the passage forbids.

The four elements of the description belong to the Book's account of the faith, and the community's teachers read them as the marks by which the believers are known. The sentence in this place joins the belief of the heart, the work of the hands, the worship of the body, and the gift of the property in a single description.

The Book has used the same combination in the verses about the people of the Book, and the community's scholars gathered the passages when they set out the subject of the faith. The sentence in this place supplies the account of what the community's profession requires.

**The Faith and the Deeds**

The first two elements of the sentence place the belief beside the righteous conduct, and the community's teachers read the order as the statement that the faith of a person produces the acts by which it is known. The Book's treatment of the subject elsewhere describes the believers by their conduct, and the passages were gathered by the commentators.

The scholars of the community drew from the pairing the lesson that the profession of the tongue is not separated from the work of the person in the teaching of the Book. The sentence therefore presents the faith and the deeds as one matter rather than as two.

The wording also names the deeds in general terms before the two particular practices that follow, so that the sentence covers the whole of the good that a person performs. The community's teachers used the clause when they spoke about the breadth of the righteous conduct that the Book commends.

**The Prayer Established**

The third element names the prayer, and the community's scholars explained the phrase as the account of the worship that orders the day of the believer. The Book has treated the prayer in the passage about the middle prayer, and the community's teachers read the two passages together.

The wording describes the practice as established rather than as performed, and the commentators noted the sense of the phrase. The prayer that the sentence names is the practice that a person maintains with its conditions and its times, so that the community's law of worship was built upon the clause.

The sentence therefore places the worship of God beside the service of the poor, and the community's scholars drew from the order the lesson that the two belong to the same conduct. The passage's treatment of wealth and its instructions about devotion were read as one body of material.

**The Alms Given**

The fourth element names the alms, and the community's teachers explained the practice as the obligation that the law places upon the property of the believers. The sentence stands at the end of the passage about charity, and the clause here supplies the account of the institutional practice that the Book treats.

The commentators noted the contrast with the practice that the previous verses prohibit, since the alms are the means by which the wealth of the community circulates among its poor. The two subjects were set beside one another in the passage, so that the community learned the difference between the gain that the law approves and the gain that it forbids.

The scholars of the community drew from the clause the lesson that the property of the believers carries a claim upon it, and they built the law of the alms upon the sentence and the passages that accompany it. The clause in this place belongs to the community's practice from its earliest years.

**The Reward Named**

The sentence states that those whom it describes have their reward with their Lord, and the community's teachers read the clause as the assurance that accompanies the conduct. The phrase appears in the earlier verses about the givers, and the community's scholars noted the repetition as the confirmation of the promise.

The wording places the reward in the keeping of God, so that the believers do not look to the community for the requital of their conduct. The teachers used the clause when they encouraged the members of the community whose acts were unknown to their neighbours.

The promise also answers the anxiety that the instruction about the practice had produced among the believers, since those who had engaged in the dealings had to abandon what remained of them. The sentence supplies the assurance that the conduct the Book requires is not a loss.

**The Fear and the Grief**

The sentence closes with the statement that no fear will come upon the persons it describes and that they will not grieve, and the community's scholars explained the pair as the description of the condition of the believers in the life to come. The closing appears in the earlier verses of the passage, and the teachers regarded the repetition as the confirmation of the standing of those described.

The first clause concerns what lies ahead and the second concerns what has passed, so that the pair describes a condition from which the anxieties of both directions have been removed. The community's preachers used the formula when they spoke about the state of the believers.

The sentence in this place therefore completes the passage's account of the conduct that the Book commends with the promise that belongs to it. The teachers drew the lesson that the community's dealings and its worship are joined in the reward that the verse names.

**The Passage's Concern**

The sentence stands among the verses that complete the treatment of the practice, and the community's teachers read the arrangement as the passage's presentation of the two conducts. The verses before it describe the blighting of the practice and the growth of charity, and the sentence here supplies the description of the persons who perform the conduct that the Book approves.

The community's scholars also connected the sentence with the passage about the changing of the direction of prayer, since the same combination of belief and conduct appears there in the Book's account of the community. The teachers read the two passages together in their teaching.

The verses that follow command the community to give up what remains of the practice, so that the sentence and the command belong to one instruction. The passage therefore presents the conduct that is required together with the practice that is forbidden.

**What the Verse Establishes**

The sentence establishes the description of the believers by their faith and their deeds, the establishment of the prayer, the payment of the alms, the reward that is kept for them, and the removal of fear and grief that accompanies them. The community's teachers used the verse when they taught the marks of the believers and when they set out the obligations of the community, and the sūrah's following verses address the community directly with the command to complete what the passage requires of it.

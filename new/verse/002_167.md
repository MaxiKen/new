## Sūrah al-Baqarah 2:167

> The ˹misled˺ followers will cry, “If only we could have a second chance, we would disown them as they disowned us.” And so Allah will make them remorseful of their misdeeds. And they will never ˹be able to˺ leave the Fire.

**Expanded Commentary**

**The Words of the Followers**

The verse records what the followers say when they see the outcome, and their words express a wish to return to the world so that they might disown the leaders as the leaders have disowned them. The sentence gives the reader the speech of people whose understanding arrives after the opportunity has passed.

The Book places the words in the mouths of those who had followed, and the statement is symmetrical with the disavowal described in the previous verse. What the followers ask for is the chance to do to their leaders what their leaders have just done to them, and the request reveals what they now understand about the relationship they had entered.

**The Request for Another Turn**

The term rendered as turn refers to the chance to return to the life of this world, and the commentators explained that the request is not granted. The Book's passages about the hereafter frequently record such requests, and the answer they receive is that the time for action has ended.

The community's scholars noted that the wish itself reveals the character of the regret. What the followers want is a chance to act differently, which means that they now know what they should have done, and the verse shows the reader what knowledge is worth when it is no longer connected to the possibility of action. The Book states the same lesson in other places where the people of the Fire ask to be returned so that they might do good.

**The Regret Shown to Them**

The sentence states that God shows them their deeds as a source of regret, and the commentators explained that what the offenders see is the reward they would have received had they acted differently. The Book's image is of a display rather than a reminder, and the wording indicates that the record of what they did is placed before them.

The community's scholars read the phrase as an account of how the hereafter addresses those who failed. What they are shown is not a rhetorical description but the substance of what they chose, and the regret that follows from seeing it is the consequence of the choices they made rather than an additional punishment imposed on them.

**The Comparison With the Reward of the Righteous**

The commentaries describe the moment in terms of a contrast, and they mention reports in which the people of the Fire see the dwellings that the people of the Garden have received and understand what they have forfeited. The wording of the verse concerns the deeds of the offenders, and the community's teachers drew the lesson that a person's actions are what will be presented to him.

The passage's purpose is to give the reader the information in advance, and the Book does so in a form that connects the display of deeds to the choices made in this life. What the reader learns is that the record is being kept, and the verse's account of the regret is placed in the Book so that those who read it may act while the record can still be changed.

**"They Will Never Leave the Fire"**

The sentence closes with a statement that those described will not be brought out of the Fire, and the wording matches the statement of permanence that appeared earlier in the passage. The regret expressed in the verse does not alter the outcome, and the Book states the fact without qualification.

The community's teachers connected the sentence with the previous verse about the cutting off of recourse, and they read the passage as a complete account of the state of those who followed a misleader to the end. What the reader receives is a description of a condition from which there is no exit, and the sentence's placement after the request for a second chance makes the statement's weight clear.

**The Argument With the Earlier Verses of the Sūrah**

The sūrah has already addressed a claim that a certain punishment would last only a limited number of days, and it has asked on what authority the claim rested. The present passage supplies the Book's own account of duration, and the reader who has followed the sūrah's treatment of the subject meets the same statement again.

The repetition is part of the sūrah's method of addressing the expectations that the communities brought to the subject of the hereafter. The Book states its account of the matter repeatedly, and each passage supplies a different aspect, so that the reader receives a settled picture rather than a single assertion.

The earlier passage addressed a claim that the punishment would last for a limited number of days, and the sūrah asked on what authority the claim rested. The present verses supply the Book’s own statement, and the reader who has followed the argument recognises the same subject in a new form. The community’s teachers gathered the passages on the duration of the hereafter, and the sūrah’s treatment of the claim in these verses is part of that collection. What the reader receives is a settled account rather than a single assertion, and the words of the followers are placed within it.

**What the Verse Establishes**

The sentence establishes that the followers will wish for a return, that their deeds will be shown to them, and that the condition they are in will not end. The reader is given the account of the hereafter from the perspective of those who followed, and the passage that follows turns to address humanity with a command about what may be eaten. The transition takes the reader from a scene of judgment to the ordinary conduct of life, and the Book's habit of connecting the two subjects is evident in the movement.

The sentence also shows the reader what the Book’s teaching about the hereafter is for, since the account of a regret that arrives too late is addressed to people who still have the chance to act. The community’s teachers used the passage when they spoke about the difference between knowledge that changes a person and knowledge that arrives at the end. What the verse establishes is that the record of a person’s deeds is presented to him, and that the presentation is the moment when the choices of this life become fully visible.

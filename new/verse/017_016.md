## Sūrah al-Isrāʾ 17:16

> **Whenever We intend to destroy a society, We command its elite ˹to obey Allah˺ but they act rebelliously in it. So the decree ˹of punishment˺ is justified, and We destroy it utterly.**

**Expanded Commentary**

**When Intend To Destroy Society**

Wa-idhā aradnā an nuhlika qaryatan — and when We intend to destroy a town, society. Idhā, when, whenever. Aradnā from r-w-d, to intend, will, desire, as verse 18-19 arāda. An nuhlika from h-l-k, to destroy, perish, cause to perish, from same root as halāk, destruction, and ahlaknā, We destroyed. Qaryatan from q-r-y, town, village, society, settlement, from same root as qaryah, town, gathering place, from qarā, to gather. Al-Ṭabarī explains that when We intend to destroy a society due to its sins, after messenger sent and warning rejected, We command its elite but they rebel, so decree justified and We destroy it. Al-Qurṭubī says qaryah here means society, town, any settlement, not necessarily large city, and intention to destroy is after establishing proof via messenger, consistent with previous verse that We never punish until messenger sent, so destruction only after warning.

**Command Elite But They Rebel**

Amarnā mutrafīhā fa-fasaqū fīhā — We command its elite but they act rebelliously in it. Amarnā from a-m-r, to command, order, from amara, to command. Mutrafīhā from t-r-f, to live in luxury, affluence, from same root as mutraf, affluent, luxurious, elite, one given luxury, and itraf, luxury, affluence, from atrafa, to make live in luxury. Fa-fasaqū from f-s-q, to act rebelliously, transgress, disobey, exit from obedience, from same root as fisq, rebellion, sin, transgression. Fīhā, in it, in society. Al-Ṭabarī explains that We command its affluent ones, elite, those given luxury, to obey Allah, via messenger, but they act rebelliously, disobey, commit fisq in it, in society, spreading corruption. The affluent, mutraf, are those given luxury, wealth, ease, who often are leaders, and when they rebel, society follows or is corrupted. Al-Rāzī says commanding elite is because they have means to obey and influence, and their rebellion is more serious and more corrupting, as they are followed.

**Decree Justified Then Destruction Utterly**

Fa-ḥaqqa ʿalayhā al-qawlu fa-dammarnāhā tadmīran — so decree is justified against it, then We destroy it utterly. Fa-ḥaqqa from ḥ-q-q, to become true, incumbent, realized, justified, from same root as ḥaqq, truth, right. ʿAlayhā, against it, upon it. Al-qawlu, decree, word, saying, word of punishment, from q-w-l, to say. Fa-dammarnāhā from d-m-r, to destroy utterly, annihilate, from same root as tadmir, utter destruction. Tadmīran, utterly, with emphasis, utter destruction, complete annihilation, from same root, intensive verbal noun. Al-Ṭabarī says so decree of punishment becomes true, justified, against it due to rebellion of elite, then We destroy it utterly, with complete destruction, no remainder. Al-Qurṭubī says ḥaqqa al-qawl means word of punishment becomes incumbent, justified, true, as in other verses about word becoming true against disbelievers who rebelled after warning.

**Why Elite Mentioned**

Why elite, mutraf? Because affluence often leads to arrogance, rebellion, and because elite are influencers, leaders. When they rebel, others follow, and corruption spreads, so society becomes deserving of destruction. Previous verse said no bearer bears burden of another, yet here society destroyed due to rebellion of elite — how reconciled? Al-Rāzī explains that destruction of society when elite rebel does not mean innocent bear burden of others unjustly, but that when elite rebel and society does not forbid evil, does not enjoin good, all become complicit, or that destruction is worldly punishment that may affect all as test, while in Hereafter each bears own burden and innocent not punished for others' sins. The sūrah's earlier mention of Children of Isra'il corruption twice illustrates: elite among them rebelled, so decree justified and We sent servants to destroy.

**Connection To Children Of Isra'il Corruption**

Verses 4-8 told Children of Isra'il will cause corruption twice, when first of two warnings would come to pass, We would send against you some of Our servants of great might, who would ravage your homes, this would be warning fulfilled, then after repentance We would give upper hand, aid with wealth and offspring, then if you act rightly for own good, if do wrong to own loss, when second warning would come to pass, enemies would totally disgrace you and enter Temple as entered first time and destroy utterly whatever would fall into hands. Verse 16 says whenever We intend to destroy society We command its elite but they rebel so decree justified then We destroy utterly — this is general principle illustrated by Children of Isra'il story. Their elite rebelled, decree justified, destruction utterly came via servants of great might. The sūrah therefore links general principle to specific example to warn current audience.

**Linguistic Note On Mutraf And Tadmīr**

Aradnā from r-w-d, to intend. Nuhlika from h-l-k, to destroy, from halaka, to perish. Qaryah from q-r-y, to gather, town as gathering place. Amarnā from a-m-r, to command. Mutraf from t-r-f, luxury, affluence, one given luxury, from atrafa, to make live in luxury, from tarafa, to live luxuriously. Fasaqū from f-s-q, to rebel, transgress, exit from obedience, from fasaqa, to exit, to be corrupt. Ḥaqqa from ḥ-q-q, to be true, incumbent, justified. Qawl from q-w-l, word, decree. Dammarnā from d-m-r, to destroy utterly, from dammara, to destroy. Tadmīran, utter destruction, intensive, from same root. The verse's structure — when We intend to destroy town, We command its elite but they rebel in it, so word is justified against it, then We destroy it utterly — with intention, command, rebellion, decree justified, destruction utterly, shows stages of divine justice after warning via messenger, not arbitrary.

**How This Verse Teaches Warning Against Luxury And Rebellion**

This verse teaches warning against luxury leading to rebellion: affluence is test, not excuse to rebel, elite should obey command. Al-Qurṭubī says mutraf, elite, affluent, are often cause of corruption, so believer should not be deceived by luxury but should obey command, be grateful, not rebellious. The sūrah's moral code that follows includes not spending wastefully, which is characteristic of mutraf, and being moderate, not tight-fisted nor openhanded, and giving relatives due. The verse also teaches that when elite rebel, decree justified and destruction utterly may come, so society should enjoin good and forbid evil, not follow elite in rebellion. The practical lesson is to be grateful for bounty, not rebellious, and to follow command to obey, so that decree of punishment not justified and town not destroyed and record laid open shows obedience, not rebellion.

**A Reflection On Decree Justified**

Fa-ḥaqqa ʿalayhā al-qawlu — so decree is justified against it — indicates that word of punishment, promise of punishment after messenger sent and warning rejected and elite rebellion, becomes incumbent, justified, realized, not arbitrary. It is justified due to rebellion after command. Then tadmir, utter destruction, follows as consequence. This shows justice: punishment only after command and rebellion, after messenger sent, after decree justified. The believer who reflects will obey command when elite are tested, will not be among mutraf who rebel, and will seek bounty with gratitude, not with rebellion, so that his town not destroyed and his record laid open shows obedience and gratitude.

## Sūrah al-Baqarah 2:223

> Your wives are like farmland for you, so approach them ˹consensually˺ as you please. And send forth something good for yourselves. Be mindful of Allah, and know that you will meet Him. And give good news to the believers.

**Expanded Commentary**

**The Image Used**

The verse describes the wives as a field for the husband and permits the approach in the manner the Book has specified, and it then instructs the community to send forth something good for themselves. The image of the field presents the relationship in terms of cultivation and growth.

The community's scholars explained the image as a description of the marriage's purpose, since the field is the place in which the next generation is brought about. Readers are shown that the sentence's concern is the fruit of the union rather than its pleasure, and the image supplies the framework in which the permission that follows is read.

**The Approach Permitted**

The sentence permits the husband to approach his wife and refers the manner of the approach to what God has specified, and the community's teachers read the clause as a reference to the rules the surrounding verses have set out. The permission is therefore bounded by the instructions that accompany it.

The commentators discussed the occasions on which the companions asked about the matter, and they described the questions of the emigrants who had married women of Madinah and the differences that arose between the customs of the two communities. The reports show the situation the sentence addressed, and the community's teachers read the verse as the answer that settled the matter.

**The Reports About the Verse**

The commentators preserve the Prophet's ﷺ explanation of the verse, and the reports state that the reference concerns the place of the approach rather than the manner or the occasion of it. The community's scholars used the reports when they set out the rulings on the subject.

The material shows how the community understood a sentence whose wording had been read in different ways, and the reader is given the account of the clarification that the community received. The community's teachers noted that the verse's concern is the physical relationship of the couple within the bounds the law has set, and the reports support that reading.

**The Instruction to Send Forth Good**

The sentence instructs the community to send forth something good for themselves, and the commentators explained the phrase as a reference to the deeds that a person prepares for the life to come. The instruction gives the physical relationship a place within the larger account of a person's conduct.

The community's teachers read the clause as the reminder that accompanies the permission, since the sentence joins the enjoyment of what is permitted with the preparation for what follows. Readers are shown that the Book addresses the whole of a person's life, and the instruction in this place supplies the perspective in which the permission is to be understood.

**The Command to Be Mindful**

The sentence instructs the community to be mindful of God and to know that they will meet Him, and the community's scholars read the pair as the ground of the instruction that preceded it. The Book's practice of attaching the vocabulary of awareness to its rulings appears throughout the sūrah.

The mention of the meeting connects the conduct of the household with the final accounting, and the community's teachers used the clause when they spoke about the responsibility that accompanies the relations of marriage. The text supplies the statement that the private life of the couple belongs to the same account as the rest of a person's deeds, and the sentence supplies the reminder that accompanies the permission.

**The Good News**

The sentence closes with the instruction to give good news to the believers, and the community's scholars read the phrase as the promise that accompanies the commands of the passage. The Book's use of the language of good news gives the reader the assurance that the instructions of the passage have an outcome worth seeking.

The ending connects the rules about the household with the promise that the sūrah has attached to the conduct of the believers throughout, and the reader is shown that the legislation belongs to the life that the Book describes. The passage continues with the rules about oaths, and the sentence in this place closes the section on marriage with a word of encouragement.

**The Marriage as Cultivation**

The figure the sentence uses presents the marriage as a place of growth, and the community's teachers drew the lesson that the union is judged by its fruit. The field is worked for a harvest, and the harvest the passage has in view is the child and the household that carries the faith into the next generation.

The image also places a duty on both parties, since a field is not left to itself. The reports describe the care with which the early community received this instruction, and the teachers noted that the sentence's permission is accompanied by the reminder of the account that follows the life of the world.

The clause that instructs the community to send something ahead for itself has been read as the summons to prepare for that account, and the command to be mindful of God completes the framework. The private life of the couple is thus placed within the same reckoning as the visible deeds of the believer, and the passage's good news closes the section with the promise that accompanies the observance of its rules.

**What the Verse Establishes**

The sentence establishes the purpose of the marriage, the permission granted within the limits the Book has set, the instruction to prepare for the life to come, and the reminder of the meeting with God. The community's teachers used the verse when they set out the rulings on the relations between the spouses, and the reader is shown that the Book's guidance reaches the most private part of a person's life.

The community's jurists used the sentence when they set out the obligations of the spouses, and the teachers read the image of the field as the statement that the marriage belongs to the next generation as well as to the two who make it. The verse's closing encouragement shows the disposition in which the rules of the household are to be received, since the passage ends with good news rather than with a threat.

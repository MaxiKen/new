## Sūrah al-Baqarah 2:251

> So they defeated them by Allah’s Will, and David killed Goliath. And Allah blessed David with kingship and wisdom and taught him what He willed. Had Allah not repelled a group of people by ˹the might of˺ another, corruption would have dominated the earth, but Allah is Gracious to all.

**Expanded Commentary**

**The Victory Recorded**

The verse states that the company routed the enemy by the permission of God and that David killed Goliath, and it then states that God gave David the kingdom and the wisdom and taught him what He willed. The sentence follows the prayer of the company and supplies the outcome of the contest.

The Book's concern is the manner in which the victory was granted, and the community's teachers read the sentence as the statement that the outcome came from the power that the company had asked for. Readers are shown that the account names the young man whose deed decided the contest, and the sentence supplies the report of what followed for him.

**The Killing of Goliath**

The commentators explained that David was the youngest of the brothers who had come with the army, and they described the encounter in which he faced the champion of the enemy. The wording names the two figures, and the community's scholars gathered the material about the event.

The Book's treatment of David elsewhere presents the prophet as the one to whom the kingdom and the wisdom were given, and the community's teachers connected the passages. Readers are given the account of a victory that came through the least of the company, and the sentence supplies the event that the passage's following clauses are built on.

**The Gifts Named**

The sentence states that God gave David the kingdom and the wisdom, and the commentators explained the two as the authority over the people and the understanding of the matters that his office required. The community's scholars read the clause as the statement that the gifts accompanied the task.

The Book's treatment of the subject elsewhere describes the blessings that were given to the prophet, and the community's teachers gathered the material. The passage shows that the passage's account of the contest ends with the account of what was given, and the sentence supplies the naming of both gifts together.

**The Teaching Named**

The sentence states that God taught David what He willed, and the commentators explained the clause as a reference to the knowledge that was given to him for his office. The wording places the instruction with God, and the community's scholars read the phrase as the statement about the source of what the prophet knew.

The Book's practice of connecting the gifts it bestows with the knowledge that accompanies them appears throughout its accounts of the prophets, and the reader is shown that the passage presents the kingdom together with the understanding that governs it. The community's teachers used the clause when they spoke about the responsibilities of leadership.

**The General Statement**

The sentence states that if God had not repelled some people by means of others the earth would have been corrupted, and the community's teachers read the clause as the general principle that the account illustrates. The wording places the contest within the ordering of the affairs of the world.

The Book's treatment of the subject elsewhere presents the conflicts among people as the means by which the settled life of the earth is preserved, and the community's scholars gathered the passages. The account describes a statement that reaches beyond the narrative, and the sentence supplies the principle that the community's teachers drew from the event.

**The Closing Attribute**

The sentence closes with the statement that God is bountiful to the worlds, and the community's scholars read the attribute as the ground of the account that the passage has given. The Book's practice of closing its narratives with a description of God appears throughout the sūrah.

The wording places the victory and the gifts within the bounty that God shows to His creation, and the community's teachers used the clause when they spoke about the care that orders the affairs of the world. The passage closes with the account of the contest and with the statement about the One who granted its outcome, and the passage continues with the description of the verses that are recited.

**The Victory and Its Gift**

The commentators described the encounter and the circumstances in which the young man who became a prophet met the champion of the enemy. The victory that the sentence records came through the least of the company, and the teachers drew the lesson that the outcome of a contest is not measured by the strength of the parties alone.

The gifts that are named in the next clause belong to the account of the prophet whom the Book honours in several places. The kingdom that was given to him established the line of the prophets in the community of Israel, and the wisdom that accompanied it supplied the judgment by which the affairs of the people were ordered.

The general statement about the repelling of peoples has been read as the account of the order by which the settled life of the world is preserved. The teachers used the clause when they spoke about the conflicts that protect the weak from the powerful, and the closing attribute places the whole arrangement within the bounty of God.

**What the Verse Establishes**

The sentence establishes the routing of the enemy, the killing of Goliath by David, the kingdom and the wisdom that David received, the teaching that accompanied them, the principle of the repelling of peoples by one another, and the bounty of God. The community's teachers used the verse when they spoke about leadership and about the ordering of the world, and the reader is shown that the passage's account of the contest closes with a statement about the gifts and the wisdom that followed it.

The community's teachers used the account when they spoke about the ways in which God supports His cause, and the passage supplied the lesson that the instruments of the victory are chosen from where the community does not expect them. The verse also shows the place of the prophet whose kingdom and wisdom are named in the Book among the line of those who carried its guidance.

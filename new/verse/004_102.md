## Sūrah al-Nisāʾ 4:102

> When you ˹O Prophet˺ are ˹campaigning˺ with them and you lead them in prayer, let one group of them pray with you—while armed. When they prostrate themselves, let the other group stand guard behind them. Then the group that has not yet prayed will then join you in prayer—and let them be vigilant and armed. The disbelievers would wish to see you neglect your weapons and belongings, so they could launch a sweeping assault on you. But there is no blame if you lay aside your weapons when overcome by heavy rain or illness—but take precaution. Indeed, Allah has prepared a humiliating punishment for the disbelievers.

**Expanded Commentary**

**When You Are With Them and Lead Them in Prayer**

The verse opens *wa idhā kunta fīhim fa aqamta lahumu al-ṣalāta fa l-taqum ṭāʾifatun minhum maʿaka wa l-yākhudhū asliḥatahum* — when you, O Prophet, are among them and you establish prayer for them, let one group stand with you while armed and let them take weapons. *Kunta fīhim* — you being among them, campaigning with them — was explained as the Prophet ﷺ present on expedition, leading the force that earlier was told to go forth in groups or together (4:71) and to be sure before fighting (4:94). *Fa aqamta lahumu al-ṣalāta* — you establish prayer for them — was read as the prayer whose shortening was just permitted for travel (4:101), now given its battle form: the *ṣalāt al-khawf*, prayer of fear. *Ṭāʾifah* — group, party — was explained as a portion of the force dividing into two: one prays with the Imam while armed, the other faces the enemy. *Wa l-yākhudhū asliḥatahum* — let them take weapons — was stressed: even in prayer the sword is not laid aside, because disbelievers are your clear enemies (4:101) and would wish to see weapons neglected. The sūrah that promised many havens to the emigrant (4:100) now shows prayer itself finding haven in ranks while still armed.

**When They Prostrate Let the Other Stand Guard**

The instruction *fa idhā sajadū fa l-yakūnū min warāʾikum wa l-taʾti ṭāʾifatun ukhrā lam yuṣallū fa l-yuṣallū maʿaka wa l-yākhudhū ḥidhrahum wa asliḥatahum* — when they prostrate, let the other group be behind you; then the group that has not prayed comes to pray with you, taking vigilance and weapons — was explained as the two-rakʿah exchange in several authentic forms (Bukhārī 413, Muslim 842 for the fear prayer). The first group prays one rakʿah with the Imam, then moves to guard; the second completes with him. *Sajadū* — they prostrated — marks the portion of prayer most exposed, where the worshipper is lowest and least able to see. *Min warāʾikum* — behind you — was read as facing the enemy, guarding the praying rank’s back. *Ḥidhrahum* — vigilance, precaution, guard — was read as alertness distinct from weapons: weapons without vigilance are still neglect. The choreography teaches that prayer is not abandoned before fear, nor fear ignored before prayer; both duties are kept by dividing the body so that worship and warfare each have eyes. The sūrah has said remembrance continues standing, sitting, lying down when prayers are finished (4:103); this arrangement shows the prayer itself contains remembrance while armed.

**They Wish to See You Neglect Weapons and Belongings**

The motive *wadda alladhīna kafarū law taghfulūna ʿan asliḥatikum wa amtiʿatikum fa yamīlūna ʿalaykum maylatan wāḥidatan* — the disbelievers would wish you neglect, be heedless of weapons and belongings, so they could sweep, assault upon you in one sweep — was explained as the enemy’s wish for a single heedless moment. *Waddū* — they wish — matches the regressed who wish you disbelieve as they have (4:89) and those who wish you deviate greatly (4:27); here the wish is tactical: that you be *ghāfil* — heedless. *Asliḥatikum wa amtiʿatikum* — weapons and belongings, goods — was read as the two vulnerabilities of a travelling force: arms and baggage. *Maylatan wāḥidah* — one sweeping assault, one inclination — was explained as a single rush while heads are bowed. The warning makes the prior armed prayer intelligible: the Devil’s schemes are weak (4:76) yet the human enemy remains a clear foe (4:101) who watches for neglect. The sūrah has shown that those given a portion wish to trade guidance and make you deviate (4:44); here the wish is more concrete — to find you without sword and without guard on the brow of prayer.

**No Blame to Lay Aside Weapons in Rain or Illness — But Take Precaution**

The ease *wa lā junāḥa ʿalaykum in kāna bikum adhan min maṭarin aw kuntum marḍā an taḍaʿū asliḥatakum wa khudhū ḥidhrakum* — no blame if you lay aside weapons when overcome by heavy rain or illness, but take precaution — was explained as the law’s lightening where rain soaks and weight exhausts or wounds prevent bearing. *Adhan min maṭar* — harm from rain — was read as heavy, cold rain that makes holding arms a hardship, as *ḥaraj* — constriction — in religion was removed where ablution with earth replaces water (4:43). *Marḍā* — ill — matches the travel and illness allowances that eased purification; here it eases carrying. Yet *khudhū ḥidhrakum* — take precaution — remains even when weapons are laid aside: vigilance is not laid aside. The pairing shows the sūrah’s method: God wills to lighten burdens because humanity is weak (4:28) yet does not lighten to heedlessness. Where the sūrah elsewhere says *lā junāḥ* for settlement where a wife fears indifference (4:128) and for shortening prayer (4:101), here *lā junāḥ* permits laying arms aside without permitting neglect. The close *inna Allāha aʿadda li al-kāfirīna ʿadhāban muhīnā* — God has prepared a humiliating punishment for disbelievers — was read as assurance that the guarded prayer is not mere survival; the outcome is His preparation where humiliation corresponds to the humiliation of the weak they would have inflicted.

**A Verse That Keeps Prayer and Guard as One Worship**

Placed after permission to shorten prayer for travel (4:101), this verse gives that shortened prayer its battlefield body. The sūrah that commands firmness in pursuit of the enemy — if you suffer they suffer, yet you hope from God what they do not (4:104) —, that warns not to be an advocate for the deceitful (4:105), here shows the Prophet ﷺ leading prayer in ranks where one prays and one guards, weapons and vigilance together. The lesson for the weak for whom the sūrah lightens burdens is not laxity but measured precaution: prayer may be shortened, weapons may be laid aside in rain or illness, but precaution is not laid aside, because the clear enemy (4:101) wishes precisely neglect. The next verse will extend prayer beyond its formal time: when the rank disperses, remembrance remains standing, sitting, lying down, and when secure, regular prayers are established at appointed times (4:103).

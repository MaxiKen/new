## Sūrah al-Nisāʾ 4:102

> When you ˹O Prophet˺ are ˹campaigning˺ with them and you lead them in prayer, let one group of them pray with you—while armed. When they prostrate themselves, let the other group stand guard behind them. Then the group that has not yet prayed will then join you in prayer—and let them be vigilant and armed. The disbelievers would wish to see you neglect your weapons and belongings, so they could launch a sweeping assault on you. But there is no blame if you lay aside your weapons when overcome by heavy rain or illness—but take precaution. Indeed, Allah has prepared a humiliating punishment for the disbelievers.

### Expanded Commentary

**Prayer on the battlefield**

The fear prayer shows how seriously Islam protects both worship and life. One group prays with the Prophet while armed; another guards, then joins the prayer. The exact forms vary according to the direction and intensity of danger, and the jurists preserve several Prophetic arrangements. The essential principle is rotation without abandoning vigilance.

**No romantic neglect of safety**

The enemy would like believers to lay down their weapons and equipment. The verse therefore tells them to remain alert, while permitting weapons to be set aside when rain or illness makes carrying them dangerous. Concession and precaution appear together. Faith is not measured by unnecessary exposure to harm.

The passage is limited to a real combat setting and a legitimate command. It cannot be used to justify armed worship by private groups or to portray ordinary disagreement as warfare. Its enduring lesson is that even under mortal pressure, a community can organize worship fairly and protect every member rather than sacrificing some as expendable.

**The sacred and practical together**

The prayer’s alternating order teaches that spiritual attention does not require denial of physical reality. Believers stand before Allah while acknowledging the enemy, the weather, the body, and the equipment. Good leadership makes room for both reverence and sound tactics.

**Worship and defence in one arrangement**

The fear prayer distributes risk rather than placing the whole congregation in danger. Today its principle can inform emergency planning: protect people, rotate responsibilities, and provide accessible worship for those under pressure. No group should use the sacred setting to suspend ordinary concern for safety.

## Sūrah al-Baqarah 2:210

> Are they waiting for Allah ˹Himself˺ to come to them in the shade of clouds, along with the angels? ˹If He did˺, then the matter would be settled ˹at once˺. And to Allah ˹all˺ matters will be returned ˹for judgment˺.

**Expanded Commentary**

**The Question of Waiting**

The verse asks whether those addressed are waiting for the coming of God in the shade of clouds with the angels, and it states that if the matter were settled then the outcome would be at hand. The sentence follows the warning of the previous verse and describes the attitude of those who put off their response.

The Book's question is directed at the expectation rather than at the event, and the community's teachers read the sentence as a statement about the posture of a person who postpones. What the reader receives is the account of a waiting that leaves the matter unresolved until it is too late.

**The Wording of the Coming**

The commentators discussed the phrase, and they offered readings in which the clouds belong to the angels rather than to God, and readings in which the sentence describes the arrival of God's command or of what He has promised. The community's scholars were careful to state that the verse cannot mean that God moves from one place to another or that He is contained by a location.

The discussion shows how the Book's descriptions of the final events were handled by the community's teachers, and the reader is given the account of a passage whose wording was examined with attention. What the reader receives is the understanding that the sentence concerns the manifestation of the matter rather than a change in God, and the community's scholars used the verse in their treatment of the attributes.

**The Angels and the Clouds**

The sentence names the angels as the beings who accompany the coming, and the Book describes them in other passages as descending with the clouds on the day the heavens are opened. The commentators connected the statement with the passages about the day of judgment.

The community's scholars read the sentence as a description of the final arrival of the matter, and they noted that the presence of the angels marks the end of the period in which response is possible. The reader carries away a picture of the moment that the question in the sentence anticipates, and the sentence's interrogation of those who wait takes its force from what that moment brings.

**The Matter Settled**

The sentence states that if the event occurred the matter would be settled at once, and the commentators explained the phrase as a reference to the finality of the moment when a person's deeds are complete and nothing further may be added. The Book uses the same expression in other passages about the arrival of the angels.

The wording gives the sentence its point, since the person who waits for the event has also been deciding the outcome by his waiting. The sentence leaves him with the statement that the opportunity for action belongs to the present, and the community's teachers read the sentence as a warning about the assumption that a later response remains available.

**"To God All Matters Are Returned"**

The sentence closes with the statement that all matters return to God, and the Book uses the phrase in several chapters to describe the final destination of every affair. The clause follows the account of the final arrival and states the principle that governs it.

The community's scholars read the ending as the ground of the whole sentence, since the waiting described in the question cannot change the return that the clause asserts. The passage supplies him with the statement that the matters he leaves unresolved are already held by the one to whom they belong, and leaves him with the knowledge of how the question will be answered.

The sentence follows the warning about slipping and precedes the instruction to ask the Children of Israel about the signs they were given, and the community's teachers read the passage as a sequence of appeals to the communities that received revelation. The Book warns the believers, describes those who wait for the final event, and then turns to the record of an earlier community.

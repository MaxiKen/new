## Sūrah al-Anʿām 6:126

> That is your Lord’s Path—perfectly straight. We have already made the signs clear to those who are mindful.

**Expanded Commentary**

**The Path Named**

That is your Lord's Path, perfectly straight. The verse identifies the way that the sūrah has been describing. The demonstrative reaches back through the passage about the opening of the heart and the constriction of the chest, and it gathers the whole account into a single noun: the path of the Lord. What the sūrah has been offering the audience is not a set of arguments alone but a road, and the sentence names it.

The Book's language of paths appears throughout its addresses to the community. The sūrah of the Cattle itself returns to the image at the end of its long passage of prohibitions, where God tells the Prophet that this is His path, straight, and commands the community to follow it while warning against the paths that branch off and separate those who take them (6:153). The sūrah of the Family of Imran gives the same instruction in the prayer that God guide the believers to the straight path, and the opening sūrah of the Book asks for guidance upon it.

**The Straightness of the Path**

The Book's use of the image in the opening sūrah shows how central it is to everything the revelation asks. The prayer for guidance upon the straight path stands at the head of the Book (1:6), and the sūrah of the Family of Imran closes the declaration attributed to Jesus with the same phrase, telling its hearers that God is his Lord and theirs, that they should worship Him, and that this is the straight path (3:51).

The description of the path as straight states its function: it leads to the destination without deviation. The sūrah of the Family of Imran calls the message itself a way that is straight, and the sūrah of the Consultation states that God guides whom He wills to a straight path and that the revelation is a light by which He guides His servants (42:52). The Book's repeated use of the image shows that its concern is with the direction in which a person is travelling rather than with the opinions he holds in isolation.

For the Makkan audience, the claim had a specific force. Their city was a place of pilgrimage roads, and the metaphor of the travellers' routes was part of ordinary life. The Book tells them that a single road leads to the Lord, and that the practices they inherited had taken them off it. The sentence names the road and leaves the choice of travelling it with the hearer.

**The Signs Made Clear for the Mindful**

We have already made the signs clear to those who are mindful. The clause closes the passage with the fourth and last form of the formula the sūrah has used after its accounts of signs. The stars and the darkness were made clear for people who know (6:97); the sign of the single soul was for people who comprehend (6:98); the harvest was for people who believe (6:99); and the path described here is for people who are mindful.

The progression moves from the faculty of knowledge to the action of remembering, and the sūrah's vocabulary marks the difference between receiving information and holding it present. The Book's commands to remember occur throughout its addresses to the community, and the sūrah of the Cow attaches the guidance of the Book to those who are mindful of God.

**What the Verse Says About the Signs and the Path**

The verse joins two statements that the sūrah has kept together throughout: the road exists, and its signs have been made clear. The Book's claim is not that the path is hidden or that the guidance is inscrutable. The signs are set out for anyone who will pay attention, and the responsibility of the reader is stated in the description of the people for whom they are clear.

The audience had asked for signs of a particular kind and had been told that signs are with God. The verse in front of us supplies the answer to that demand in its own terms: the signs that lead along the path are already present, and the person who is mindful of God will find them where they have been placed.

**The Place of the Verse in the Sūrah's Structure**

The verse marks the end of the section that began with the command to follow what was revealed (6:106). That section has moved through the limits of the messenger's responsibility, the prohibition on reviling the idols of others, the oaths of those who demanded signs, the enemies appointed to every prophet, the deception of the whisperers, the authority of the Book, the warning against the majority, the dietary rules, the conditions of guidance and error, and now the naming of the path.

The sūrah's second half will move to the account of the gathering of jinn and humans, the reckoning of the communities, and the final passages on the dietary system and the covenant. The verse in front of us closes one movement of the chapter and states its conclusion: the path is God's, it is straight, and its signs have been made clear to the people who remember them.

**The Verse and the Reward That Follows**

The next verse states that the people who follow the path will have the Home of Peace with their Lord, and that He will be their guardian because of what they used to do (6:127). The two verses are joined in the way the sūrah has joined its statements throughout: the road and its destination, the guidance and the meeting with the guide.

That pairing gives the sūrah's argument its shape. The signs, the path, and the Home of Peace are presented as a single arrangement, and the audience is addressed as people capable of walking it. The Book's closing of the passage with the description of the mindful hearer states once more where the responsibility lies.

## Sūrah al-Isrāʾ 17:13

> **We have bound every human’s destiny to their neck. And on the Day of Judgment We will bring forth to each ˹person˺ a record which they will find laid open.**

**Expanded Commentary**

**Bound Destiny To Neck**

Wa-kulla insānin alzamnāhu ṭā'irahu fī ʿunuqihi — and every human We bound his bird to his neck. Kulla insānin, every human, each person. Alzamnāhu from l-z-m, to bind, make incumbent, attach inseparably, from lāzim, that which is binding. Ṭā'irahu from ṭ-y-r, bird, flight, omen, destiny, from same root as ṭā'ir, bird, and taṭayyur, taking omens from birds, because Arabs would release birds and take omens from direction of flight, calling fortune ṭā'ir. Fī ʿunuqihi, in his neck, from ʿ-n-q, neck. Al-Ṭabarī explains that ṭā'ir here means destiny, deeds, fortune, what flies from him of good or evil, bound to neck meaning made incumbent upon him, attached to him inseparably like necklace that does not leave neck. Arabs used to see omens in birds flight, calling good or bad luck ṭā'ir, so Qur'an uses same word to mean destiny, what is decreed via own deeds. Al-Qurṭubī says alzamnāhu ṭā'irahu fī ʿunuqihi means We made his deeds, good and evil, and his destiny, bound to his neck, meaning he cannot escape, it stays with him as necklace stays on neck, always with him, visible.

**Record Laid Open On Day Of Judgment**

Wa-nukhriju lahu yawma al-qiyāmati kitāban yalqāhu manshūran — and We bring forth for him on Day of Resurrection a book he finds laid open, spread open. Nukhriju from kh-r-j, to bring forth, extract, produce. Lahu, for him. Yawma al-qiyāmati, Day of Resurrection, Day of Standing, from q-w-m. Kitāban, book, record, from k-t-b, to write. Yalqāhu from l-q-y, to find, meet, encounter. Manshūran from n-sh-r, to spread open, publish, lay open, from same root as nashr, spreading, publication. Al-Ṭabarī says on Day of Judgment We bring forth for each person a book, record of deeds, which he finds laid open, manshūran, meaning open for him to read, not sealed, wide open so he can see all deeds, small and great. Al-Rāzī says manshūran indicates that record is spread open, not folded, so that person sees all, nothing hidden, and that laid open indicates ease of reading. The phrase yalqāhu manshūran, he finds it laid open, emphasizes that it is presented open, not that he must open it.

**Why Neck And Bird Mentioned**

Why neck and bird? Neck is what holds necklace, closest to head, inseparable, always with person, and bird indicates what flies, what is taken as omen. The metaphor that destiny bound to neck means it is attached, cannot be removed, follows him wherever he goes, as necklace follows. Al-Zamakhsharī notes that ʿunuq, neck, is mentioned because it is where ornaments and yokes are placed, and because what is bound to neck is always with person, visible, cannot be hidden, and because Arabs said this is in your neck, meaning incumbent upon you. Ṭā'ir as bird also alludes to Arab practice of taking omens from bird flight, which Qur'an refutes: destiny not from birds but from own deeds bound to neck. The verse therefore corrects superstition and affirms responsibility: your destiny is your deeds bound to neck, not random bird omen. This links to previous verse that no bearer bears burden of another: each has own ṭā'ir bound to own neck.

**Connection To Detailed Explanation And Seeking Bounty**

Verse 12 said We explained everything in detail, made night and day signs for seeking bounty and knowing calculation. Verse 13 says every human's destiny bound to neck, record laid open on Day. The connection is that after explaining signs and that everything detailed, the sūrah moves to individual responsibility: everything explained so you can choose, and your choice, your ṭā'ir, is bound to neck, recorded in book laid open. Seeking bounty and knowing calculation are part of deeds that will be in book. The person who uses night and day as signs, seeks bounty with gratitude, knows calculation for duties, will have good record; who prays for evil out of haste will have evil record. The detailed explanation is so that person knows what binds to neck as good.

**Link To Previous Chapter About Record**

Previous chapter said on Day Resurrection you will be presented to Lord in rows, told you came as created first time but claimed never appointed meeting, and book placed and you see criminals fearful of what in it saying woe this book does not omit small nor great but has counted, and they find what they did present, and Lord wrongs not. This verse says similar: record laid open he finds spread open. The theme of record is consistent across both chapters: deeds recorded, book laid open, person reads, sufficient self as reckoner. Next verse says read your book, sufficient yourself today as reckoner, completing picture.

**Linguistic Note On Alzamna And Manshur**

Alzamnāhu from l-z-m, to bind, make incumbent, from lāzim, incumbent, from lazima, to be necessary, to stick. Ṭā'irahu from ṭ-y-r, to fly, bird, omen, from ṭāra, to fly. ʿUnuq from ʿ-n-q, neck. Nukhriju from kh-r-j, to bring forth. Yawm from y-w-m, day. Qiyāmah from q-w-m, to stand, resurrection. Kitāb from k-t-b, book, record. Yalqāhu from l-q-y, to meet, find. Manshūr from n-sh-r, to spread, publish, open, from nashara, to spread. The verse's structure — every human We bound his bird in his neck, and We bring forth for him on Day of Resurrection a book he finds laid open — with binding in world and bringing forth in Hereafter, shows that destiny in world is deeds that become record laid open in Hereafter, inseparable then readable.

**How This Verse Teaches Responsibility And Anti-Superstition**

This verse teaches responsibility and rejection of superstition: your destiny is not random omen from birds but your deeds bound to neck, and record laid open will show all. Al-Rāzī says believer should be mindful that every deed is bound to neck and will be in book laid open, so he should do good and avoid evil, follow most upright guidance that Qur'an guides to. The sūrah's moral code that follows is what should be bound to neck as good deeds: honoring parents, giving relatives due, not killing children, not approaching adultery, etc. The verse also teaches against taṭayyur: do not seek omens in birds, your ṭā'ir is your deeds. The practical lesson is to be aware that every action is recorded, to strive for good record laid open that you will be pleased to read, and to remember that on Day record will be laid open with no hiding, so prepare now.

**A Reflection On Book Laid Open**

Manshūran — laid open, spread open — evokes image of book opened fully so that nothing hidden, all visible, laid open before eyes. Unlike sealed book, laid open book is readable at glance. On Day, each person finds his book laid open, meaning he can read all, small and great, without barrier. This is justice: he sees own deeds, not told by others, and sufficient self as reckoner. Next verse invites him to read himself. The believer who reflects that book will be laid open will be careful what he writes in it via deeds bound to neck, seeking that when laid open it shows good, seeking bounty lawfully, knowing calculation, following most upright guidance, so that reading is easy and joyful.

## Sūrah al-Anʿām 6:161

> Say, ˹O Prophet,˺ “Surely my Lord has guided me to the Straight Path, a perfect way, the faith of Abraham, the upright, who was not one of the polytheists.”

**Expanded Commentary**

**The Declaration of Guidance**

Say, Surely my Lord has guided me to the Straight Path. The verse gives the Prophet a declaration to make, and it returns to the image the sūrah has used for the religion it presents. The path was named when the commands of the covenant were gathered into a single road (6:153), and the Prophet's own position on it is stated here. The declaration is made in the first person, and it places the Prophet among the people who have been guided rather than above them.

The Book's use of the image at the opening of the Qur'an gives it its place in the community's worship, since the prayer for guidance upon the straight path stands at the head of the Book (1:6). The declaration attributed to Jesus in the sūrah of the Family of Imran closes with the statement that this is the straight path (3:51), and the sūrah of the Consultation says that the revelation is a light by which God guides whom He wills to a straight path (42:52). The declaration in front of us belongs to that family of statements.

**A Perfect Way**

A perfect way. The Book's term describes something established and upright, and it is used of the religion itself in the sūrah of the Clear Proof, where the command given to the people of the earlier books is described as the worship of God alone with sincere devotion in all uprightness (98:5). The word in this verse describes the path as firmly set, and it distinguishes the religion the Prophet follows from the practices that shift with custom and inheritance.

The Book's descriptions of the religion use the same vocabulary in its statements about the faith of Abraham, and the two descriptions belong together in this verse. What the Prophet declares is not a private preference but a way that has been established, and the phrase states that it stands on its own foundation.

**The Faith of Abraham**

The faith of Abraham, the upright, who was not one of the polytheists. The verse identifies the tradition the Prophet follows, and the Book attaches the same identification to its commands in several places. The sūrah of the Bee states that God revealed to the Prophet to follow the faith of Abraham, the upright, who was not one of the polytheists (16:123); the sūrah of the Family of Imran commands the same following in the words Allah has declared the truth, so follow the Way of Abraham (3:95); and the sūrah of the Cow answers the claim of the Jews and Christians to exclusive guidance with the declaration that the community follows the faith of Abraham (2:135).

In the sūrah of the Women, the question is asked who is better in faith than the person who submits himself to God, does good, and follows the Way of Abraham, and the verse states that God took Abraham as a friend (4:125). The sūrah of the Bee describes Abraham as a model of excellence, devoted to God, perfectly upright, and not a polytheist, and it adds that he was grateful for God's favours. The verse in front of us gathers the same descriptions into a single declaration.

**Abraham in This Sūrah**

The sūrah has already told the story of Abraham's argument with his people, and the account runs from his questioning of his father and his people about the idols (6:74) to his declaration that he has turned his face toward the One who originated the heavens and the earth and that he is not one of the polytheists (6:79). The closing declaration of the Prophet reaches back to that account and identifies the religion he follows with the one Abraham professed.

The connection is important for the sūrah's argument. The audience claimed the heritage of Abraham and traced its sanctuary and its pilgrimage to him, and the sūrah has shown that his religion consisted in the rejection of the idols and the worship of the One who originated everything. The declaration in front of us states that the Prophet follows that religion, and the audience's claim to it is answered by the account the sūrah has already given.

**The Tag Attached to Abraham**

The phrase who was not one of the polytheists appears wherever the Book identifies Abraham's faith, and its repetition is deliberate. The sūrah of the Bee attaches it to the description of Abraham as a model of excellence (16:120), the sūrah of the Family of Imran attaches it to the command to follow his way (3:95), and the sūrah of the Cow attaches it to the declaration that the community follows his faith (2:135). The phrase states what the following of Abraham excludes.

That exclusion is the point of the declaration in this sūrah. The audience associated beings with God, consulted the jinn, divided its harvests between God and the idols, and regulated its food by rules it attributed to God. The sūrah has spent its length dismantling those practices, and the verse in front of us states the tradition that stands against them in a single sentence.

**The Contrast With the Divisions**

The declaration follows the verse about the communities that divided their faith and split into sects (6:159), and the contrast between the two passages is exact. The sects were produced by differences that arose among people; the path is established and straight; and the Prophet declares that he has been guided to it. The sūrah offers the community a single road in place of the divisions it describes.

The Book's treatment of the sūrah's audience and the earlier communities in this closing section therefore comes to a point. The judgment of the sects is left to God, the individual's deed is measured, and the Prophet states the road he follows. The reader is left with the choice that the book has set before him, and the declaration in front of us states what that choice looks like from the side of the one who has accepted the message.

**The Place of the Verse in the Sūrah's Close**

The verse begins the three-verse declaration that closes the sūrah's teaching about the Prophet's own religion. The declaration states the guidance he has received (6:161), the dedication of his worship, life, and death (6:162), and his disavowal of any partner along with his being the first to submit (6:163). The three verses belong together, and they answer the account of the audience's religion that the sūrah has given.

The chapter's final verses then return to the theme of individual accountability and to the ranks in which people have been placed on the earth (6:164–165). The declaration in front of us supplies the content of the Prophet's own position, and the verses that follow state the terms on which all people are judged.

**The Transition to the Next Verse**

The next verse continues the declaration with the Prophet's statement that his prayer, his worship, his life, and his death belong to God, the Lord of all worlds (6:162). The two verses form the first part of a single speech, and the second supplies the dedication that matches the guidance declared in the first.

The sūrah's closing movement therefore runs from the guidance the Prophet has received to the worship he performs. The religion is stated in terms of a road and of a life laid out on it, and the reader who has followed the chapter from its opening verses now has the Prophet's own summary of what the message requires.

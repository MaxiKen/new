## Sūrah al-Nisāʾ 4:131

> To Allah ˹alone˺ belongs whatever is in the heavens and whatever is on the earth. Indeed, We have commanded those given the Scripture before you, as well as you, to be mindful of Allah. But if you disobey, then ˹know that˺ to Allah belongs whatever is in the heavens and the earth. And Allah is Self-Sufficient, Praiseworthy.

**Expanded Commentary**

**To God Belongs Whatever Is in Heavens and Earth — Commanded to Be Mindful**

The verse opens *wa li Allāhi mā fī al-samāwāti wa mā fī al-arḍ* — and to God belongs whatever is in heavens and whatever on earth — repeated from 4:126 and to be repeated at 4:132, bracketing the passage on settlement and enrichment. *Mā fī al-samāwāti wa mā fī al-arḍ* — whatever is in heavens and on earth — was explained as absolute ownership encompassing the single soul spread into humanity (4:1), the orphans’ trusts (4:2), the dowries (4:4), the inheritance shares (4:11–12), the earth made spacious (4:100), the wives between whom justice cannot be perfected (4:129) yet whose separation is enriched from His bounty (4:130). *Wa la qad waṣṣaynā alladhīna ūtū al-kitāba min qablikum wa iyyākum ani ittaqū Allāh* — and indeed We have commanded, enjoined, charged those given Scripture before you, as well as you, to be mindful of God — was explained as universal *waṣiyyah* — counsel, charge — the same mindfulness that began the sūrah — *“be mindful of your Lord who created you from a single soul”* (4:1) — and that seals settlement — *“if you are gracious and mindful God is All-Aware”* (4:128) — and seals not inclining entirely — *“if you set right and are mindful He is All-Forgiving”* (4:129). To command those given Scripture before you and you was read as continuity: the Scriptures before and the Qur’an enjoin the same *taqwā*, as *“We have given descendants of Abraham the Book and wisdom and great authority”* (4:54) and *“He taught you what you did not know, great is His favour”* (4:113) — the teaching is one.

**But If You Disobey — To Him Still Belongs All**

The condition *wa in takfurū fa inna li Allāhi mā fī al-samāwāti wa mā fī al-arḍ* — but if you disobey, if you disbelieve, are ungrateful, then indeed to Him belongs whatever is in heavens and earth — was explained as the sūrah’s reminder that mindfulness is not need of the Owner. *In takfurū* — if you disbelieve, are ungrateful — was read as including both creedal disbelief that association brings (4:116) and practical *kufrān* — ingratitude — as withholding trust, inclining totally leaving the other suspended (4:129), devouring wealth unjustly (4:29), or claiming wishes as grace (4:123). *Fa inna li Allāhi mā fī al-samāwāti wa mā fī al-arḍ* — then to Him still belongs all — was explained as that disobedience does not reduce His ownership: the spaciousness that enriched both after separation (4:130) remains, the Gardens promised true (4:122) remain, the Hell without escape (4:121) remains under His dominion. The sūrah has said *“you desire world’s reward, with Him are rewards of this world and Hereafter”* (4:134) — to disobey is to refuse the *taqwā* that would have obtained both. The clause was linked to *“whoever defies the Messenger after guidance and follows other than believers’ path We let pursue what he chose then burn”* (4:115): letting pursue does not transfer ownership; Hell to which he is burned still belongs to Him, as Gardens do.

**And God Is Self-Sufficient, Praiseworthy**

The seal *wa kāna Allāhu Ghaniyyan Ḥamīdā* — And God is Self-Sufficient, free of need, Rich, and Praiseworthy, ever praised — was explained with *Ghanī* — Rich, Independent, Free of need — and *Ḥamīd* — Praised, Praiseworthy — the same pair where hypocrites are warned for taking disbelievers as allies seeking honour through that company — *surely all honour belongs to God* (4:139). To be *Ghanī* was read as that His command to be mindful is not for His need but for servants’ good: He does not need their *taqwā*, as earth’s spacious havens (4:100) were given not because He needed emigrants but because they needed refuge. *Ḥamīd* — Praiseworthy — was explained as that He is praised though they disobey, as the sūrah says *“to Him belongs all in heavens and earth, He is Fully Aware”* (4:126) — praise belongs to the Fully Aware who measures even the speck not wronged (*naqīrā* — 4:124). The two names together show that mindfulness — *ittaqū Allāh* — enriches the mindful, not the Praised Self-Sufficient; and ungratefulness — *takfurū* — impoverishes the ungrateful, not the Rich.

**A Verse That Brackets Settlement With Ownership and Command**

Placed after enrichment from His bounty where they separate and He is Ever-Bountiful, All-Wise (4:130), this verse brackets settlement with ownership and universal command. The sūrah has moved from orphan women deprived yet desired, helpless children, orphans stood for with justice well known to Him (4:127) → wife fearing *nushūz* or turning away, settlement best though selves were made present with *shuḥḥ* yet graciousness and mindfulness are ever known (4:128) → impossibility of perfect emotional justice, prohibition of total inclining leaving hanging, setting right and mindfulness forgiven (4:129) → separation enriched from His spaciousness (4:130) — here to ground all in *to Him belongs whatever is in heavens and earth, We have commanded those before you and you to be mindful, if you disobey to Him still belongs all, He is Self-Sufficient, Praiseworthy*. The sūrah that began *“be mindful of your Lord who created you from single soul”* returns to *be mindful* as its abiding *waṣiyyah* for every Scripture people. For the weak this is the lightest of commands: where total justice between wives was declared impossible, mindfulness remains possible, and where separation was given, mindfulness remains the charge whose Keeper needs nothing and whose praise remains though you disobey.

**The *Waṣiyyah* That Survives Disobedience**

The phrase *wa la qad waṣṣaynā* — We have commanded, surely — was explained as a covenantal charge that survives disobedience, because ownership survives disobedience. Where the deceitful hide from people but He is with them where they plot displeasing speech (4:108) and where night plotting seems to succeed in this life but not before Him on the Day (4:109), the *waṣiyyah* to be mindful is the daylight that night cannot void, because the Night’s owner is the One commanded mindfulness. The verse generalises the household law into cosmology: the same heavens and earth that belong to Him contain both the single soul spread (4:1) and the many havens for emigration (4:100), the prayer at fixed writ (4:103) and the two abodes true (4:121–122); to be mindful within them is to live inside ownership that is Self-Sufficient yet Praiseworthy, so that obedience adds no need and disobedience subtracts nothing except from the disobedient.

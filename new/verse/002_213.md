## Sūrah al-Baqarah 2:213

> Humanity had once been one community ˹of believers before they lost faith˺. Then Allah raised prophets as deliverers of good news and as warners, and revealed to them the Scriptures in truth to judge among people regarding their disputes. And no one disputed the Scriptures except the very people who received them after clear proofs had come to them—out of jealousy. Then Allah, by His grace, has guided the believers to the truth regarding those disputes. And Allah guides whoever He wills to the Straight Path.

**Expanded Commentary**

**Humanity as One Community**

The verse states that humanity was one community, and the commentators discussed the condition that the phrase describes. Some placed it in the period between Adam and Noah, others described a state of unity in the worship of God before division set in, and others connected the phrase with the covenant that the Book describes as having been made with all human beings.

The variety of readings reflects the sentence's brevity, and the community's scholars read them as complementary. What the reader receives is a statement about the starting point of human history, and the sentence then describes the remedy that God provided for the division that followed.

The community's teachers read the sentence as a statement about the common origin of the human family and about the division that the prophetic missions were sent to address, and they noted that the Book's account of the past supplies the background for its treatment of the communities that received revelation. The reader is shown that the disputes among the recipients of scripture are a later development rather than the original condition, and the passage's account of the guidance granted to the believers is presented as the resolution of a division that had been allowed to harden.

**The Prophets Sent**

The sentence states that God raised up the prophets as bearers of good news and as warners, and the Book uses the pair for the two aspects of the messenger's work throughout its chapters. The community's teachers read the sentence as a statement about the purpose of prophecy.

The pairing covers the promise and the caution, and the Book's account of the messengers elsewhere presents them with the same twofold commission. What the sentence establishes is that the sending of the prophets belongs to the history that the verse describes, and the reader is shown that the remedy for division was provided in the form of messengers.

**The Book Sent in Truth**

The sentence states that the Book was sent with the prophets in truth to judge among people concerning their disputes, and the community's scholars read the clause as a statement about the function of the revelation. The Book is presented as the standard by which the disagreements are settled.

The sūrah has described the revelation as the standard in its earlier passages, and the sentence in this place states the purpose for which it was given. What the reader receives is the account of a guidance that resolves what people dispute, and the community's teachers read the clause as the answer to the divisions that the verse has described.

**The Dispute Among the Recipients**

The sentence states that none disputed the Book except those who had received it, and it names the motive as envy among themselves. The Book's account of the communities that received earlier scriptures has shown the same pattern, and the sentence here states the principle.

The commentators explained the envy as the rivalry that led the recipients to dispute what had been made clear to them. The reader carries away the account of a division that arose among the very people who possessed the answer, and the sentence shows that the revelation was not the cause of the disagreement but the standard by which it was judged.

The revelation was therefore not the cause of the disagreement but the standard by which it was judged, and the reader is shown that the possession of a book does not guarantee agreement about its meaning.

**The Guidance Granted**

The sentence states that God guided the believers to the truth of what they disputed, and it adds that the guidance came by His leave. The clause places the resolution of the differences with the one who gave the guidance rather than with the disputants.

The Book's treatment of the subject elsewhere states that the differences will be settled on the day of judgment, and the community's teachers read the two together. What the sentence establishes is that the community that received the final revelation was given the resolution of the matters its predecessors disputed, and the reader is shown that the guidance is a gift rather than an achievement.

**The Closing Statement**

The sentence closes with the statement that God guides whom He wills to a straight path, and the sūrah has used the same vocabulary from its opening pages. The community's teachers read the ending as the assurance that the guidance remains available to those who seek it.

The Book's practice of ending a passage with a statement about guidance appears throughout the sūrah, and the sentence here concludes the account of the world's division and the remedy provided for it. The sentence leaves him with the statement that the path is established and that the one who directs people to it is God, and the passage continues with the commands that belong to the community's life.

**The Verse in the Passage**

The sentence gathers the sūrah's account of the communities and their disputes and states the purpose of the revelation in relation to both. The reader who has followed the sūrah from its account of the children of Israel arrives at a sentence that explains the whole sequence, and the passage turns from the history of the communities to the affairs of the community that has received the final Book.

**What the Verse Establishes**

The sentence establishes humanity's beginning, the sending of the prophets, the revelation's function as the standard, the cause of the disputes, and the guidance granted to the believers. The reader is given the whole account in a single verse, and the community's teachers read it as a summary of the sūrah's argument about the communities that preceded the final revelation.

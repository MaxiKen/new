## Sūrah al-Shuʿarāʾ 26:47

> They declared, “We ˹now˺ believe in the Lord of all worlds—

**Expanded Commentary**

**The Magicians Name the Lord Their Sign Has Revealed**

After prostrating, the magicians declare belief in the Lord of all worlds. Their confession identifies the source of the sign and breaks with the court's account. They do not say merely that Moses has performed the stronger technique. They recognize that the event points beyond technique to the universal Lord already proclaimed by the messenger.

The word now marks a change in their state. They entered the arena with confidence in Pharaoh's power and in their own craft. They leave the first moment of the sign with a new certainty. This does not mean that they had no prior knowledge of God or that every question about faith appeared from nothing. It means that the public event has brought recognition to a decisive level. What was previously hidden or resisted is now confessed.

**Belief Is a Change of Allegiance**

Their words are spoken before the same court that expected them to secure Pharaoh's rule. Belief therefore has a social cost. The magicians are not adding a private spiritual opinion to their professional identity while leaving their public loyalty unchanged. They are naming a Lord whose authority includes Pharaoh, the court, and the people gathered for the contest.

This is why Pharaoh will treat the confession as rebellion. If the magicians had merely admitted that Moses' sign was unusual, the ruler might have negotiated the explanation. They have done more. They have accepted the authority represented by the sign and placed it above the authority that hired them. Faith becomes visible when it changes whom a person obeys and whose interpretation he accepts.

**The Universal Title Connects the Confession to Moses' Message**

The magicians use the same universal title that Moses used when he entered Pharaoh's court. Their confession shows that they understood the message, not only the spectacle. The Lord is not a new local power who has defeated Pharaoh in a narrow contest. He is Lord of all worlds, including the world of Egypt and the world of the Israelites.

The repeated title also confirms the coherence of Moses' claim. The sign has not led the magicians toward a different theology invented in the excitement of the moment. It has confirmed the words they heard. Their experience joins the evidence to the message. A miracle becomes guidance when the witnesses understand what it authenticates.

**Now Belief Is Not Mere Emotional Shock**

A dramatic event can produce fear or amazement without producing faith. The magicians' declaration goes beyond the first shock. They prostrate, then speak a considered confession about the Lord. Their professional background gives them a basis for distinguishing their own methods from Moses' sign. The movement from sight to speech shows that the response includes judgment, not only emotion.

This guards against treating every sudden conversion as shallow or every strong feeling as proof. The magicians' belief has content. It identifies the Lord and places their experience within the message brought by Moses. Their response will soon be tested by Pharaoh's threat, which will show whether the confession can survive cost. Faith is not measured only at the moment of excitement but by the allegiance it sustains afterward.

**The Confession Is Made by Former Opponents**

The magicians were not introduced as secret followers of Moses. They came for reward, swore by Pharaoh's might, and expected to prevail. That background makes their words especially important. They are witnesses whose prior position gives weight to the change. They know what they intended to do and what their craft could accomplish. Their belief is a judgment against the explanation they once served.

The transformation also exposes the limits of propaganda. Pharaoh had gathered them to make Moses look like a magician, but they become the first experts to reject that label. A ruler can recruit people for a purpose without controlling what they will recognize. Once the hired witnesses speak honestly, the court's official story becomes harder to maintain.

**A Universal Lord Includes the Oppressor and the Oppressed**

The confession does not make God the private possession of the Israelites. The magicians, Egyptians by context, claim Him as Lord of all worlds. That universality gives the truth a reach beyond ethnic identity. Pharaoh has tried to frame Moses' mission as a threat from an outsider. The confession shows that the Lord's authority is not foreign to Egyptians; it is the authority under which all peoples stand.

This also means that no group can use the Lord's name to create a new tyranny. The magicians' belief does not authorize them to rule over Pharaoh or over anyone else. It places them under God, which should make them more just and less willing to dominate. Universal lordship liberates people from Pharaoh without turning the liberated into new absolute rulers.

**The Dash After the Confession Leaves the Relation Open**

The line ends after naming the Lord of all worlds, with the fuller identification continuing in the next verse. The pause allows the universal confession to stand on its own. The magicians have already crossed the decisive boundary: they no longer treat Pharaoh's might as the source of victory.

Their words show how a sign can make a person see the same title differently. Moses had proclaimed the Lord of all worlds to a ruler who mocked it. The magicians hear the title, witness the sign, and declare it themselves. The court's contest has become a public act of belief.

That act also shows why faith cannot be reduced to private agreement. The magicians speak in the hearing of the very people whom Pharaoh hoped to lead. Their confession gives the crowd a competing account of what happened, grounded in the judgment of those who knew the craft best. The ruler's stage has become a place where the truth is named against him.

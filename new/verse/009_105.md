## Sūrah al-Tawbah 9:105

> Tell ˹them, O Prophet˺, “Do as you will. Your deeds will be observed by Allah, His Messenger, and the believers. And you will be returned to the Knower of the seen and unseen, then He will inform you of what you used to do.”

**Expanded Commentary**

**Action Must Follow Repentance**

The command to perform deeds comes after the discussion of confession and charity. Words of repentance must now become conduct. A person who says he has changed should act in ways that make the change visible: restore a right, fulfill a duty, give lawfully, and stop protecting the old behavior.

This is not a permission to do anything without moral limits. It is a challenge to let the record of action speak. A person can claim sincerity, but deeds will show whether the claim has substance. The believer does not remain trapped in self-description. He moves from apology to obedience and allows time to confirm the direction.



Allah observes every deed, including the hidden intention and the consequence that people cannot measure. A public act may appear impressive while concealing pride or manipulation. A small private act may seem insignificant while carrying great sacrifice. Divine observation joins the outward and inward parts of the record.

This knowledge should make the servant careful without making him anxious about every human opinion. He works correctly, checks his intention, and leaves the final evaluation to Allah. If people overlook the service, Allah has not overlooked it. If people praise a deed that was corrupt, their praise cannot make it sound.

**The Messenger and Believers Observe Conduct**

The verse also mentions the Messenger and the believers observing deeds. This can refer to public conduct in this world or to its disclosure in the Hereafter. Either way, it teaches that communal life needs evidence. Repentance is inward, but its effects reach other people. Trust, leadership, and responsibility are assigned according to what conduct shows.

Believers should observe without becoming spies. They protect the community from clear harm, honor good work, and give a repentant person room to prove change. They do not claim to know what only Allah knows. Observation becomes just when it serves accountability and safety rather than curiosity or humiliation.



The servant will be returned to the Knower of the seen and unseen. This return prevents action from being treated as a temporary performance. A person may escape criticism today, but he cannot escape the final account. The unseen motive, the private conversation, and the unrecorded harm all belong to the knowledge of Allah.

The believer uses this future to correct his present. He asks whether he would be content for Allah to disclose the purpose behind the act. If not, he repairs the purpose before continuing. The thought of return is not meant to paralyze useful action. It gives action seriousness and direction.

**The Command Encourages Rather Than Abandons**

“Do as you will” can sound severe, but in context it places responsibility in the servant's hands. Allah has made the path clear, and the person now chooses what his deeds will testify. The command does not abandon him to meaninglessness. It warns that freedom is accountable.

A repentant person should hear encouragement in this. He can act differently now. He does not need to remain imprisoned by a past identity. Each deed is evidence for or against the claim of return. The believer acts with hope that Allah will make the new direction firm.

**The Final Information Will Be Complete**

Allah will inform them of what they used to do. The final disclosure will not depend on memory, witnesses, or competing accounts. It will include the action, intention, opportunity, and effect. This completeness is justice for the sincere and a warning to the deceptive.

The servant therefore stops trying to win through narrative alone. He lets consistent deeds become his testimony. He seeks forgiveness for what is past, corrects what is present, and entrusts the future to Allah. The Knower of the seen and unseen cannot be fooled by a performance, but He can accept a genuine change that no audience yet understands.

The command also releases a sincere person from endless self-description. He does not have to keep repeating that he regrets the wrong if his deeds now show repentance. He works, gives, and accepts that some observers will remain cautious. Their caution can be reasonable, and his patience with it is part of repair. Allah sees the effort before people understand it and sees the corruption when people praise it. The final return therefore makes both hidden service and hidden deceit significant. A person can begin again, but he cannot ask words alone to carry the weight that deeds were meant to carry.

A deed observed by believers also becomes part of the moral environment. Good action can encourage others, while a public act of hypocrisy can make obedience seem foolish. The servant therefore cares about example without turning example into vanity. He does the work correctly, explains what needs explanation, and refuses to manipulate the audience through religious emotion. If the deed is hidden, Allah sees it. If it is public, Allah sees the purpose behind its publicity. The final return makes the servant responsible for both the action and the influence it has on people who watch.

The completed record includes the response of others to a person's work. If an action taught people generosity, protected a vulnerable neighbor, or made truth easier to recognize, Allah knows those effects. If a public performance made sin attractive, He knows that as well. This is why intention cannot be separated from responsibility. The believer asks not only whether an act looks good but whether it leaves a good path behind. He keeps serving after the audience leaves because the One who sees the final return also sees the consequences that remain in people's lives.

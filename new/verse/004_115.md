## Sūrah al-Nisāʾ 4:115

> And whoever defies the Messenger after guidance has become clear to them and follows a path other than that of the believers, We will let them pursue what they have chosen, then burn them in Hell—what an evil end!

**Expanded Commentary**

**Whoever Defies the Messenger After Guidance Is Clear**

The verse opens *wa man yushāqiqi al-rasūla min baʿdi mā tabayyana lahū al-hudā* — and whoever defies, opposes, splits from the Messenger after guidance has become clear to him. *Yushāqiqi* — to defy, to oppose, to make a split, to take the opposite *shiqq* — side — was explained as going to the other side from the Messenger ﷺ after the Book in truth sent to judge by what He shows (4:105) and wisdom taught (4:113) made guidance clear. *Min baʿdi mā tabayyana lahū al-hudā* — after guidance became clear to him — was read as after the evidence of truth reached — the Qur’anic signs about trusts, judgment, verification, and the theft case just judged — so that defiance is not error but choice after clarity. The sūrah has shown guidance become clear where the Book judges between people (4:60 rejected as *ṭāghūt*), where reflection would show no inconsistency had it been from other than God (4:82), and where the Prophet ﷺ was taught what he never knew (4:113); to defy after that clarity is to choose the other path with eyes open, as the sūrah said *“whoever is left to stray you will never find a way for him”* (4:88) after regression by misdeeds. The same verb *shāqqa* appears where *“whoever defies God and Messenger, God is severe in punishment”* (8:13), marking defiance as more than disagreement — it is adoption of enmity.

**And Follows a Path Other Than That of the Believers**

The second condition *wa yattabiʿ ghayra sabīli al-muʾminīna* — and follows a path other than that, a way other than the way, of the believers — was explained as taking a way distinct from the community’s way. *Sabīl* — path, way — was read as the sūrah’s path language: God wills to make path clear and guide to noble ways (4:26), Satan wishes the deviant path far away (4:60), verification is the path before fighting (4:94), earth’s spacious path is for emigration (4:97–100). *Sabīl al-muʾminīn* — path of the believers — was explained as the believers’ collective way in creed, law, and conduct, the same congregation commanded to go forth together (4:71) and to stand for justice even against selves (4:135). Early authorities including al-Shāfiʿī inferred from this phrase the authority of consensus — *ijmāʿ* — where the believers’ path in understanding the Book is protected, though the core meaning remains moral: to follow other than their path after guidance is clear is to follow desires that deviate greatly (4:27) and to join Satan’s party. The sūrah has warned that most secret talks have no good except three that seek God’s pleasure (4:114); following other than the believers’ path was read as the secret talk made public as doctrine — private deceit become alternative way.

**We Will Let Him Pursue What He Has Chosen**

The recompense *nuwallihī mā tawallā* — We will let, turn him to what he turned to, leave him to what he chose, entrust him to his direction — was explained as handing the defier over to his choice, as the sūrah said God allowed hypocrites to regress because of misdeeds (4:88) and as *“whoever is left to stray you will never find a way”* (4:143, 4:88). *Nuwallihī* — to turn, to make *walī* — guardian of — was read as divine leave: he chose the other side after clarity, so God leaves him there, as the emigrant who chose God’s side finds many havens (4:100) and the helpless who could not choose are hoped to be pardoned (4:99). The phrase was linked to *“We will turn their hearts and eyes as they did not believe at first”* (6:110) and to *“He leaves to stray whom He wills”*; the *tawallā* — turned — that began as human act becomes divine *nuwallihī* — We turn him. Classical notes added that this shows freedom’s weight: the Book in truth does not coerce; it shows, and where one defies after being shown, God does not drag back but lets the path chosen become the path walked. The sūrah has said that God does not wrong even an atom (4:40); to let the defier pursue his chosen side is not wrong, it is the atom measured exact.

**Then Burn Them in Hell — What an Evil End**

The outcome *wa nuṣlihī Jahannam wa sāʾat maṣīrā* — then We will burn, roast them in Hell, enter them into Fire, and evil as destination, as becoming — was explained with *nuṣlihī* — to cause to burn, to roast, to enter into burning — the same verb for those who consume orphans’ wealth will be burned in blazing Hell (4:10) and those who reject signs will be cast into Fire where skins are replaced (4:56). *Jahannam* was read as the abode already assigned to the intentional killer with anger and curse (4:93) and to those who wronged themselves while earth was wide (4:97); here it is for defiance after clarity and following other than believers’ way. *Wa sāʾat maṣīrā* — evil as destination, evil as becoming — was explained as the sūrah’s closing formula for abodes of those who choose other than God’s cause, matching *evil destination* for the self-wronger (4:97, 4:115). The phrase was linked to the beginning where limits set by God admit to Gardens forever (4:13) and exceeding limits casts into Hell forever (4:14); defying the Messenger after guidance is exceeding that limit at its root — the referral to God and Messenger where disputes are taken (4:59).

**A Verse That Follows the Theft Case With the Broad Principle**

Placed after *no good in most secret talks except charity, kindness, reconciliation seeking God’s pleasure* (4:114), this verse follows a particular case — theft, night plotting, advocacy, throwing sin — with the broad principle: after guidance is clear, defying the Messenger and following other than the believers’ path is a chosen turning that God lets continue to its end — burning. The sūrah has moved from individual excuses — not equal are sitters with harm though promised good (4:95), helpless hoped to be pardoned (4:99) — to collective guard — prayer with vigilance (4:102), pursuit without faltering for hope (4:104), judgment by Book (4:105). This verse anchors collective guard in loyalty to the Messenger’s way: the path of the believers is not a tribal way but the way the Book shows and wisdom teaches (4:113), taught where Jesus spoke — the Word through Mary and spirit from Him — without saying “Trinity” (4:171) and where God’s favour upon the Prophet ﷺ is great. For the weak the sūrah lightens, this is a guard: where private talk tempted to other than believers’ way, the warning that He will let you pursue what you chose and burn — evil destination — steadies the step back onto the spacious path where He wills to make clear and to turn in mercy (4:26–27).

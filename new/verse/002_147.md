## Sūrah al-Baqarah 2:147

> ˹This is˺ the truth from your Lord, so do not ever be one of those who doubt.

**Expanded Commentary**

**A Command Addressed to the Prophet**

The verse addresses the Prophet ﷺ and tells him not to be among those who doubt, and the commentators noted that the command is meant for the community as well. The Prophet ﷺ was not a person who would doubt what had been revealed to him, and the Book's commands of this kind are recorded for the benefit of those who read them.

The sūrah contains several instructions of this shape, such as the command to follow what has been revealed and the command to be steadfast. The community's scholars treat them as addressed to the Prophet ﷺ in form and to everyone who holds his message in substance, since the readers of the Book are the ones exposed to the wavering the sentence warns against.

**The Truth Named**

The sentence begins by stating that what precedes it is the truth from God, and the demonstrative refers to the revelation and to the direction of prayer that has just been established. The Book frequently attaches this kind of confirmation to the end of a disputed passage, so that the community receives the conclusion as a statement from its Lord rather than as a decision of its leaders.

The phrase also identifies the source, and the sūrah has used it in the preceding verses when it reports the knowledge of those who had been given the scripture. The truthfulness of the message does not rest on the agreement of the other communities, and the sentence supplies the community with the ground on which it can stand without that agreement.

The demonstrative points backward to the whole of what has been revealed, and the community’s scholars read it as covering both the message and the direction of prayer that had just been established. The sentence therefore supplies a verdict on the controversy the passage has described. What the two communities disputed, and what the foolish among the people used as an occasion for mockery, is named here as the truth from God, and the naming settles the matter for anyone who accepts the Book’s account.

**Doubt and Its Place in the Book**

The Book treats doubt as a condition to be resisted rather than as a neutral state of inquiry. It tells the Prophet ﷺ that the revelation is the truth and that he should not be among the doubters, and it describes doubt in other passages as a sickness of the heart that obstructs action.

The Book does not, however, forbid questions. The sūrah records the companions asking the Prophet ﷺ about many matters, and legislation came down in response. What the warning addresses is the wavering that follows knowledge, when a person who has recognised the truth begins to reconsider it under the pressure of opposition, and the verse names that condition so that the community can identify it.

**The Pressure Behind the Warning**

The passage has described a dispute about the direction of prayer, an objection raised by the foolish, and communities that would not accept the new direction no matter what proof was brought. The warning against doubt is placed after that account, and it therefore concerns the particular temptation created by a controversy the community could not resolve.

The Book's method here is to name the pressure rather than to pretend it does not exist. The community was facing criticism from groups with older traditions and with scriptures it respected, and the sentence tells it where to stand when the criticism continues. What the reader receives is not the removal of the difficulty but a command about how to hold the truth in the middle of it.

**Certainty as a Gift**

The Book elsewhere describes certainty as a quality of the believers and states that the revelation has been made clear for people who possess it. The verse's command rests on that idea: the community is told not to doubt because the truth has come from its Lord, and the command presupposes that the means of certainty are available.

The sūrah has used the same term in an earlier verse where it described the signs as clear for people of sure faith. The two passages belong together, and they show that the Book presents certainty as something a person arrives at through what has been provided rather than as an attitude to be adopted by willpower.

The Book treats firmness as something granted rather than achieved, and it teaches the community to ask for it. The prayer that opens the sūrah requests guidance to the straight path, and the same request is made in every unit of the community’s prayer. The command in this verse is therefore paired with a petition the community repeats, and the pairing shows how the Book expects its readers to hold a conviction: by acting on it and by asking for the strength to keep it.

**The Place of the Verse in the Passage**

The sentence stands between the account of the community that recognised the Prophet and concealed the truth, and the command to turn toward the Sacred Mosque that is repeated in the following verses. It functions as a hinge: the reader has just been shown what recognising the truth and hiding it looks like, and he is now told what his own relation to the truth should be.

The placement also prepares the reader for the verses about remembering God and seeking help through patience and prayer. Those commands follow a passage of controversy, and they supply the practices by which a community maintains its footing when its positions are disputed. The warning against doubt belongs at the head of that sequence.

**What the Verse Asks of Its Reader**

The sentence asks the reader to hold what he has received without treating the disagreement of others as evidence against it. The Book's confidence in this instruction rests on the source it has named, and the reader is told where the truth came from rather than how many people agree with it. That is the whole argument of the sentence, and the sūrah leaves it with the community.

The instruction reaches every reader who receives the message, and it asks for a settled position rather than for continuous argument. The community is expected to know why it holds what it holds and to leave the disagreement of others where it belongs. What the sentence forbids is not the study of difficult questions but the drift that follows long exposure to opposition, and the Book names that drift plainly so that it can be recognised when it begins.

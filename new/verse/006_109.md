## Sūrah al-Anʿām 6:109

> They swear by Allah their most solemn oaths that if a sign were to come to them, they would certainly believe in it. Say, ˹O Prophet,˺ “Signs are only with Allah.” What will make you ˹believers˺ realize that even if a sign were to come to them, they still would not believe?

**Expanded Commentary**

**The Oath and What It Promised**

They swear by Allah their most solemn oaths that if a sign were to come to them, they would certainly believe in it. The verse records the terms on which the audience offered to accept the message, and the Book has recorded the same kind of oath elsewhere. The sūrah of Fāṭir says that they swore by God their most solemn oaths that if a warner came to them they would be better guided than any other community, and that when the warner came it only increased their aversion (35:42). The sūrah of the Table and the sūrah of Light record comparable oaths from those who claimed they would follow if only a sign were shown.

The Book treats the demand for a sign as a negotiating position rather than an expression of an open mind, and the oaths recorded in these passages are the clearest evidence for that judgement, since in every case the sign came and the oath was broken.

**The Answer Given to the Prophet**

Say, Signs are only with Allah. The instruction tells the Prophet how to answer, and the answer moves the question out of the hands of the messenger. The Book states elsewhere that no messenger could bring a sign except by God's permission: the sūrah of the Thunder says that a messenger is not able to bring a sign save by God's leave (13:38), and the sūrah of the Forgiver states the same (40:78). The sentence therefore answers the demand by placing the signs with the One who alone decides whether they will be shown.

The answer also removes the ground on which the audience was standing. The Makkans asked for a sign in order to settle the truth of the message, and the Book's response is that signs are not brought to order. They are granted or withheld by God, and the community that has received the Qur'an has already received what it needs.

**What Will Make You Realise**

What will make you realise that even if a sign were to come to them, they still would not believe? The second half of the verse is addressed to the believers, and the Book uses this form of address when it wants its readers to notice something they had not considered. The question is not a request for information; it is a statement that the audience's objection was never the real obstacle.

The sūrah has built the case for that judgement over many verses. It recorded the demand for the signs and the Book's refusal to produce them at the audience's command, and it recorded the response of those who had been shown the clearest kind of evidence. The verse closes the argument by naming the obstacle as the will of the people themselves, and the next verse will state that God turns their hearts away as a consequence of what they have chosen.

**The Reports About the Request for a Sign**

Reports connected with this verse describe the idolaters asking the Prophet to perform a miracle of the kind they had heard recounted of Moses and Jesus, and asking him to turn a hill near the sanctuary into gold. According to those reports the Prophet began to supplicate for what they requested and was then informed that such extraordinary signs were commonly followed by destructive punishment if they were rejected, so he ceased his request.

The report gives the verse a setting and explains the reference in the following verse to the punishment that would come with a sign if it were rejected. Whether or not the details are pressed, the Book's own statements in this passage and the one after it state the principle clearly: the sign would not have persuaded the people who demanded it, and its arrival would have made their position worse rather than better.

**The Pattern of Signs and Refusal**

The Book records a consistent pattern in the history of the earlier communities. The sign comes, the people who demanded it refuse it, and the refusal is punished, and the sūrah of the Children of Israel records that God refrained from sending the signs the audience asked for exactly because of what would follow their rejection. The sūrah of the Table gives the same reasoning in the account of the disciples' request for a table from heaven, and the sūrah of the Spoils states that God would not punish a people while the Prophet was among them.

The Book's treatment of the signs therefore turns on the responsibility that an unmistakable sign would create. The community that has received the Book without them is being shown mercy rather than being deprived, and the verse's question to the believers is meant to make them see the demand in that light.

**What the Verse Establishes for the Reader**

The verse establishes that the obstacle to belief lies in the people who refuse rather than in the evidence they have been given. The sūrah has described the signs that were already available in the created order, in the history of the earlier messengers, and in the revelation that was being recited to the audience. The demand for an additional sign did not arise from a lack of evidence but from a determination not to accept the evidence that had come.

That reading is confirmed by the verse that follows, which describes God diverting the hearts of the people who would not believe and leaving them in their transgression. The audience had made its decision, and the Book states what the decision produced, with the signs placed where the verse puts them, which is with Allah alone.

## Sūrah al-Naḥl 16:29

> **So enter the gates of Hell, to stay there forever. Indeed, what an evil home for the arrogant!”**

**Expanded Commentary**

**Command To Enter Hell**

Fa-udkhulū abwāba jahannama khālidīna fīhā — so enter gates of Hell to abide therein forever. The command udkhulū, enter, is said to those whose souls were taken while wronging themselves and who falsely claimed innocence. Abwāba jahannama, gates of Hell, indicates Hell has gates, doors, levels, as in 15:44: *It has seven gates, for each gate is a portion assigned* (15:44). Entering gates implies being ushered into punishment, not merely seeing it. Khālidīna fīhā means abiding therein forever, remaining permanently. Al-Ṭabarī explains khālidīna as remaining without end for those who died in shirk and denial. The command is given by angels after rebutting false claim of innocence. It shows that after death, there is no chance to argue; judgment is executed. The phrase fa-udkhulū, so enter, indicates consequence: because you wronged yourselves, denied, claimed no evil, and Allah knows what you did, therefore enter.

**What An Evil Home For The Arrogant**

Fa-la-bi'sa mathwā al-mutakabbirīn — indeed what an evil abode, dwelling, home for the arrogant. Bi'sa is exclamation of dispraise: how evil, how wretched. Mathwā from th-w-y means abode, dwelling, place where one stays, from same root as thāwī, resident. Al-mutakabbirīn, arrogant, those who sought greatness, refused truth. Al-Zamakhsharī notes that mathwā indicates place of residence, home, implying that Hell will be their home, where they stay, not temporary visit. The phrase la-bi'sa mathwā al-mutakabbirīn is used elsewhere: *So enter gates of Hell to abide therein; evil indeed is abode of arrogant* (16:29; 39:72; 40:76). Repetition shows that arrogance is specifically linked to Hell as its home. Why? Because arrogant refused to be servants in world, claimed greatness, so their fitting home is abode of humiliation.

**Why Arrogance Leads To Hell**

Arrogance, istikbār and takabbur, is repeatedly linked to Hell because it prevents acceptance of truth. Arrogant deem themselves too great to worship Allah alone, to follow messenger, to accept that they were created from drop and cannot count blessings. Their hearts were munkiratun, denying, and mustakbirūn, arrogant, as verse 22 said, and Allah does not like arrogant, as verse 23 said. Now consequence: evil home for arrogant. Al-Rāzī explains that arrogance is root of all sins: Iblīs refused to prostrate due to arrogance, Pharaoh claimed lordship due to arrogance, Makkan chiefs rejected Prophet ﷺ due to arrogance. Hell is fitting because Hell humiliates, and arrogant need humiliation to realize truth they denied. The Qur'an says *But those who are too proud to worship Me will enter Hell humiliated* (40:60). The humiliation in Hell is opposite of greatness sought in world.

**The Contrast With Righteous Home**

Verse 30-31 will contrast this evil home with excellent home of righteous: *How excellent indeed is home of righteous, Gardens of Eternity* (16:30-31). The pairing of evil home vs excellent home is deliberate: two homes, two groups, two answers to question what has your Lord revealed. Those who said ancient fables get evil home; those who said all best get excellent home. The sūrah uses mathwā for arrogant and dār for righteous? Actually verse 30 says *excellent is abode of righteous*, using mathwā? Let's check: verse 30 says *How excellent indeed is home of righteous* — same word mathwā? In Arabic, verse 30 says *wa-la-niʿma dāru al-muttaqīn*, excellent is abode of righteous, using dār, while verse 29 uses mathwā. Both mean home, but dār is more honorable. The contrast is clear: arrogant get wretched mathwā, righteous get excellent dār.

**Gates Of Hell As Levels**

Abwāba, gates, plural, indicates multiple entrances, each for different types of sinners or different levels of punishment. Some tafsīr say Hell has seven gates, each with different torment. Others say gates indicate that entry is from all sides, no escape. The command udkhulū abwāba, enter gates, suggests they will be driven through gates, not entering willingly. The Qur'an says *And those who disbelieved will be driven to Hell in groups until when they reach it, its gates are opened* (39:71). The opening of gates is moment of realization. Khālidīna fīhā, abiding forever, indicates permanence for those who died in shirk. For those who had some faith but sins, there may be eventual exit by mercy, but for arrogant who denied Hereafter and called revelation fables, abiding is forever.

**Linguistic Note On Bi'sa And Mathwā**

Bi'sa from b-'-s means to be evil, wretched, from same root as ba's, hardship, might. It is used for dispraise, opposite of niʿma, how excellent. Mathwā from th-w-y means place where one stays, dwells, from thawa, to stay, reside. Mutakabbirīn from k-b-r, to be great, form V and X meaning to show greatness, be arrogant. The phrase fa-la-bi'sa mathwā al-mutakabbirīn is exclamatory, with la- for emphasis: indeed how evil is home of arrogant. The verse's brevity — two sentences — delivers final verdict after death scene: enter, abide, evil home. It closes the passage on plotters and deniers, preparing for contrasting passage on righteous.

**Why Gates Are Mentioned**

Gates suggest that Hell is enclosed, with entry points that are controlled. No one enters without permission, and no one exits without permission. The image of gates also suggests that Hell has already been prepared, its gates exist now, waiting. The Qur'an says *Indeed Hell has been lying in wait* (78:21). The command to enter gates therefore is not creation of new punishment but ushering into already prepared abode. Al-Rāzī notes that mentioning gates also indicates that Hell has different levels of punishment appropriate to different sins, and each gate leads to different level. The arrogant who opposed believers for sake of idols will enter through gate appropriate to arrogance and shirk. The mention of gates also contrasts with gates of Paradise that will be opened for righteous, as 39:73 says *Until when they reach it, its gates are opened and its keepers say Peace be upon you* (39:73). Two sets of gates, two greetings, two homes.

**The Permanence Of Abiding**

Khālidīna fīhā, abiding therein forever, is a phrase that appears many times for both Paradise and Hell, indicating permanence. For Hell, it indicates that those who die in shirk and denial will not exit. Al-Ṭabarī explains that khulūd, abiding forever, means remaining without end, without death, without exit. This permanence is fitting because shirk and arrogance were choices that, if persisted until death, become fixed character. The person who insisted on being great and refused to submit in world has chosen a character that cannot exist in Paradise, which requires humility and submission. Therefore Hell becomes their fitting, permanent home, mathwā, place where that character belongs. The verse ends with exclamation of dispraise to make listener feel horror and avoid that home by abandoning arrogance now, while choice remains.

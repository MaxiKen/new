## Sūrah al-Nisāʾ 4:101

> When you travel through the land, it is permissible for you to shorten the prayer—˹especially˺ if you fear an attack by the disbelievers. Indeed, the disbelievers are your sworn enemies.

### Expanded Commentary

**Prayer travels with the traveller**

The verse permits shortening prayer during travel, especially where attack is feared. The concession shows that worship is attached to the believer rather than to a fixed building or ideal circumstance. Travel changes fatigue, distance, security, and routine; the law responds without abandoning the obligation.

**The relation between travel and fear**

The wording mentions fear because the earliest community often travelled through hostile territory, while Prophetic practice established that shortening is also permitted for travel itself. The jurists discuss distance, duration, and the form of the shortened prayers. The broader lesson is not to make hardship a pretext for abandoning prayer or to make the concession harder than revelation makes it.

**Precaution remains part of worship**

The verse calls disbelieving attackers sworn enemies in the immediate military context. It does not turn every non-Muslim traveller into an enemy. The next verse sets out a structured prayer under threat, showing that the permission belongs to a real security situation and to a community acting under legitimate authority. Worship and security planning cooperate rather than compete.

**Concession preserves the prayer**

The traveller should learn the shortened form and its conditions rather than abandon prayer because routine has been disrupted. The law’s flexibility is a sign that worship is meant to accompany life’s movement. Security concerns may intensify the need for organization, but they do not cancel the bond with Allah.

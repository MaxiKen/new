## Sūrah al-Nisāʾ 4:101

> When you travel through the land, it is permissible for you to shorten the prayer—˹especially˺ if you fear an attack by the disbelievers. Indeed, the disbelievers are your sworn enemies.

**Expanded Commentary**

**When You Travel — Permission to Shorten the Prayer**

The verse opens *wa idhā ḍarabtum fī al-arḍi fa laysa ʿalaykum junāḥun an taqṣurū mina al-ṣalāti* — when you strike through the land, travel through earth, there is no blame, no sin upon you to shorten the prayer, to curtail it. *Ḍarabtum fī al-arḍi* — striking through land — was explained as journeying, the same phrase used for struggling in God’s cause (4:94) and now widened to any travel, as the sūrah has just promised many havens to the emigrant (4:100) and wide earth to those asked at soul-taking (4:97). *Fa laysa ʿalaykum junāḥ* — no blame — was read as permission, not sin, to shorten; the sūrah has used *junāḥ* for law’s ease where dowry is adjusted by mutual consent (4:24), where a wife fears indifference and settlement is sought (4:128), and where no sin attaches. *An taqṣurū mina al-ṣalāti* — to shorten from the prayer — was explained as the four-rakʿah prayers — Ẓuhr, ʿAṣr, ʿIshāʾ — shortened to two, while Maghrib and Fajr remain. The practice was shown by the Prophet ﷺ on travel before fear was present, establishing that the permission is general, as ʿUmar asked why shorten when now safe and the Prophet ﷺ replied: *“it is charity that God has given you, accept His charity”* (Ṣaḥīḥ Muslim 686). The sūrah that wills to lighten burdens because humanity is weak (4:28), that taught purification with earth where water lacks (4:43), now lightens prayer itself within spacious earth.

**Especially If You Fear an Attack by the Disbelievers**

The condition *in khiftum an yaftinakumu alladhīna kafarū* — if you fear that those who disbelieve may attack you, may tempt you, may put you to trial — was explained as fear of *fitnah* during travel, where the hostile who do not offer peace may assault the worshippers. *Yaftinakum* — to put to trial, to attack, to seduce from worship — matches *fitnah* as strife and hostility (2:191–93) and as the temptation to which the double-faced are plunged whenever returned (4:91). The phrase *“especially if you fear”* in translation was read to mark the occasion that occasions the permission most clearly, not to limit it to danger alone; the Prophet’s shortening even without enemy present shows the condition is illustrative, as travel itself brings *fitnah* of hardship, exposure, and the need for vigilance. The sūrah has described the oppressed who cry for a helper from the land of oppressors (4:75) and the permission to seize the hostile who refuse peace (4:89–91); shortened prayer is the worship that fits that terrain where worshippers must remain armed and vigilant, as the next verse will detail with ranks of prayer while armed (4:102). The condition also teaches that fear does not cancel prayer but reshapes it, as remembrance remains standing, sitting, lying down when prayers are finished (4:103).

**Indeed the Disbelievers Are Your Sworn Enemies**

The seal *inna al-kāfirīna kānū lakum ʿaduwwan mubīnā* — indeed the disbelievers are — were — your clear, sworn enemies, manifest foes — was explained as reason for the permission and for the caution that follows. *ʿAduww mubīn* — clear enemy — was read as open hostility, not hidden envy only; the sūrah has said God knows best who enemies are and is sufficient as Guardian and Helper (4:45), and here the clarity of enmity is declared so that the traveller does not treat the hostile as neutral. *Kānū* — they were — was read as timeless trait of that hostility where disbelief hardens into fighting, as the Devil’s schemes are weak yet still must be curbed (4:76) and as Satan is the accursed enemy who said he will mislead (4:118–19). The manifest enmity does not contradict the exception for treaty and for the constricted who offer peace (4:90); it defines the default of the hostile collective where no treaty or peace holds. For the weak for whom burdens are lightened, the declaration is a protection: to shorten prayer when traveling is not laxity but realism about a clear foe, and to keep guard while praying (4:102) is not suspicion but obedience to the notice that enmity is manifest.

**A Verse That Lightens Worship for the Road**

Placed after many havens for the emigrant (4:100), this verse lightens worship for the road that leads to them. The sūrah that opened from a single soul, that commanded prayer at appointed times (4:103), that allowed earth for purification when water lacked (4:43), here allows prayer curtailed when earth is struck through. The Prophet’s regular shortening, and the companions’ agreement that even without fear the shortening stands, were read as the sūrah’s principle that God’s earth is spacious and His law is spacious within it. The permission was also linked to the wider ease where fasting by mistake can be consecutive months (4:92) and where settlement between spouses bears no sin (4:128): *lā junāḥ* is the sūrah’s lightest law-terminology, removing blame where burden would crush. The traveller who shortens is therefore not cutting prayer but receiving charity — *ṣadaqah* — from God, whose charity the weak are told to accept.

**Prayer Shortened Without Prayer Lost**

The verse was read to keep two truths together: prayer is shortened, prayer is not abandoned. The sūrah will say when prayers are over, remember God standing, sitting, lying down, and when secure, establish regular prayers, for prayer is a duty at appointed times (4:103). The shortening is within the duty, not instead of it, as the next verse’s armed prayer will show: one group prays while armed, prostrates, then the other prays while the first guards (4:102). The declaration that disbelievers are your clear enemies explains why that choreography is needed, and why mercy in worship — to shorten, to guard — is itself worship. The verse generalises emigration’s spaciousness into law: the earth is wide, the prayer travels with you shorter where the road makes long standing a burden, and the enemy’s clarity does not cancel the prayer but gives its travel form.

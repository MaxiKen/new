## Sūrah al-Baqarah 2:190

> Fight in the cause of Allah ˹only˺ against those who wage war against you, but do not exceed the limits. Surely Allah does not like transgressors.

**Expanded Commentary**

**The Command to Fight**

The verse commands the community to fight in the cause of God against those who wage war against it, and it forbids exceeding the limits. The sentence opens a passage that deals with the community's conduct in conflict, and it supplies the framework within which the commands that follow are read.

The wording restricts the subject of the fighting to those who are actively at war with the community, and the community's scholars treated the restriction as the foundation of the rulings that follow. What the reader receives is a command that is bounded at the outset by the condition attached to it.

**The Cause Named**

The sentence describes the fighting as being in the cause of God, and the Book uses the phrase for struggle undertaken for the religion rather than for territory, spoils, or the standing of a group. The sūrah's earlier passages have dealt with a community under pressure, and the sentence gives the purpose of the response it authorises.

The community's teachers used the phrase to distinguish the struggle the Book commends from the quarrels of tribes. What the sentence establishes is that the fighting's justification lies in the cause rather than in the identity of the participants, and the reader is shown that the Book's permission is granted for a purpose and not for a habit.

**The Permission in the Book's Sequence**

The Book grants permission to fight in a later chapter with the statement that the believers were being fought against unjustly, and the community's scholars read that passage together with the present verse. The sūrah's placement of the command after a long section of legislation and instruction indicates that the subject is the community's defence rather than its expansion.

The reports describe the circumstances of the community in Madinah, where a treaty and a series of confrontations organised its relations with the surrounding groups. What the reader receives is a command that belongs to a particular situation, and the community's scholars interpreted the passage with attention to the occasion and to the conditions the sentence names.

**The Prohibition of Excess**

The sentence forbids exceeding the limits and states that God does not love transgressors, and the commentators explained the prohibition as a restriction on the manner and the extent of the fighting. Some explained it as the prohibition of mutilation and of treachery, others as the instruction not to fight those who do not fight, and others as the command to keep within what the law permits.

The reports preserve the Prophet's prohibition of killing women and children, and the community's jurists built their rules of warfare on the combination of the verse and the reports. What the sentence establishes is that the permission is accompanied by a boundary, and the reader is shown that the Book's command to fight is stated in the same breath as its prohibition of excess.

**The Limits in the Reports**

The community's teachers preserved a body of instruction about the conduct of fighting, including the treatment of prisoners, the protection of those who have been given a pledge, and the prohibition of mutilation. The reports describe the Prophet ﷺ instructing those he sent on campaigns, and they describe the practice of the early community.

The material shows how the sentence in this verse was applied in practice. What the reader receives is the account of a law that governs the conduct of war rather than only its occasion, and the community's scholars treated the verse as the anchor for the whole body of rulings they developed.

**The Sūrah's Teaching on Restraint**

The passage that follows continues the theme of restraint with the provisions for those who desist and with the statement that hostility is directed only at the aggressors. Taken as one instruction, the section restricts the use of force to what defence requires, and the present sentence states the principle that governs the rest.

**The Verse's Place in the Passage**

The sentence stands at the head of a short section on the community's relations with those who fight it, and it follows the corrections of practice and the rules of worship that occupy the earlier part of the sūrah. The subject changes from the regulation of personal conduct to the protection of the community, and the general rule is given before the particulars.

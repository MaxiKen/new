## Sūrah al-Nisāʾ 4:132

> To Allah ˹alone˺ belongs whatever is in the heavens and whatever is on the earth. And Allah is sufficient as a Trustee of Affairs.

### Expanded Commentary

**Sufficiency in stewardship**

The repeated declaration of divine ownership leads to Allah as a sufficient Trustee. A trustee manages affairs with knowledge and care, while the servant remains responsible for acting within the trust. Reliance therefore does not mean abandoning work, planning, or legal rights. It means refusing to imagine that the final outcome rests entirely on one’s limited control.

This is particularly important in family disputes, inheritance, and political uncertainty. A person should use lawful means to protect a claim and seek a just decision, then entrust what lies beyond power to Allah. Trust can make action calmer and less manipulative.

**Trusting the Trustee**

A person who entrusts outcomes to Allah can still document a debt, consult a lawyer, or protect a child’s property. These means are not rivals to reliance. They are forms of responsible action carried out by a servant who knows that success, protection, and final judgment remain beyond personal control.

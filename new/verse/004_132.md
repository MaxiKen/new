## Sūrah al-Nisāʾ 4:132

> To Allah ˹alone˺ belongs whatever is in the heavens and whatever is on the earth. And Allah is sufficient as a Trustee of Affairs.

**Expanded Commentary**

**To God Belongs Whatever Is in Heavens and Earth — Again**

The verse restates *wa li Allāhi mā fī al-samāwāti wa mā fī al-arḍ* — and to God belongs whatever is in heavens and whatever on earth — the third repetition after 4:126 and 4:131, sealing the passage on women, settlement, enrichment, and universal *waṣiyyah* to be mindful. *Mā fī al-samāwāti wa mā fī al-arḍ* was explained to include the same all as before: the single soul and its mate (4:1), the orphans’ wealth, the dowries, the shares, the earth spacious for haven (4:100), the wives between whom inclination is limited (4:129) yet each enriched from His bounty upon separation (4:130), and the heavens under which the fear prayer was ranked with vigilance (4:102). To repeat ownership thrice was read as emphasis after law: where the chapter legislates for the weakest — the orphan girl deprived yet desired (4:127), the wife fearing *nushūz* where settlement is best (4:128), the hanging not left (4:129) — the Legislation is grounded in Ownership, so that no wish (4:123) and no Satanic order to slit and alter (4:119) can claim title. The sūrah has said that the Book was sent with truth to judge between people by what He shows (4:105); to judge by that showing is to judge within His ownership, as witnesses for justice though against yourselves (4:135). Early reports noted that the Meccan assignment of daughters as female gods besides Him (4:117) and the claim of Satan’s appointed share (4:118) are both answered here: they belong to Him.

**And God Is Sufficient as Trustee of Affairs**

The seal *wa kafā bi Allāhi wakīlā* — And God is sufficient, enough, as Trustee, as *Wakīl*, as Disposer of affairs, Agent — was explained with *wakīl* — trustee, agent, defender, disposer — the same *wakīl* asked about on the Day — *who will be their *wakīl*?* (4:109) — and the *wakīl* the Prophet ﷺ was told to trust — *“put trust in God, God is sufficient as Trustee”* (4:81) after turning away from night contradictors. *Kafā bi Allāhi wakīlā* was read as that His trusteeship suffices for every affair where spouses fear, orphans are stood for, emigrants seek haven, prayers guard. The sūrah has sealed trusteeship diversely: sufficient as Guardian and Helper (4:45), sufficient as Helper where He knows enemies (4:45), sufficient as Trustee after recording night schemes (4:81); here *sufficient as Trustee* fits ownership — because to Him belongs all, He suffices to dispose all. To take Him as *wakīl* was explained as entrusting affairs after doing what is incumbent: settlement sought (4:128), total inclining avoided (4:129), separation where needed enriched from His bounty (4:130), mindfulness kept where commanded (4:131). Where deceitful advocates seek to be *khaṣīm* for self-wrongers in this life (4:107–109) and find no *wakīl* on the Day for deceit, the believer who seeks settlement graciously finds the *Wakīl* who suffices.

**A Verse That Moves From Owner to Trustee**

Placed after *We have commanded those before you and you to be mindful, if you disobey to Him still belongs all, He is Self-Sufficient, Praiseworthy* (4:131), this verse moves from Owner to Trustee. The sūrah bracketed settlement and enrichment between ownership and *taqwā*, now adds sufficiency: ownership without trusteeship would be awed but not approached; trusteeship says entrust. The sūrah has shown that those who put trust in Him and turn away from night contradictors are covered (4:81), that the emigrant who leaves home toward God and Messenger has reward secured upon Him (4:100), that the helpless hoped to be pardoned have *ʿAfuww Ghafūr* (4:99). To say *kafā bi Allāhi wakīlā* is to say the same for every hanging not left and every settlement best: entrust, and He suffices. The sūrah has warned *“do not be advocate for the deceitful”* (4:105) and *“no good in most secret talks except charity, kindness, reconciliation”* (4:114); the *wakīl* who suffices is the secret talk’s better audience than people from whom deceit hides (4:108).

**Sufficiency That Makes Wishes Unnecessary and Promises True**

The pairing — *to Him belongs* and *He suffices as Trustee* — was explained as making *“neither by your wishes nor those of the People of the Book, whoever commits evil will be rewarded accordingly”* (4:123) unnecessary to wish, and *“God’s promise ever true, whose word more truthful?”* (4:122) sufficient to trust. The sūrah has contrasted Satan’s promise only delusion (4:120) ending in Hell without escape (4:121) with God’s promise true ending in Gardens forever (4:122); to take the True Promiser as *wakīl* is to take the only promise that is not delusion as trustee. Where the sūrah says *“with Him are rewards of this world and Hereafter”* (4:134) for those who desire world’s reward, sufficiency as *wakīl* explains why both are with Him: He disposes both. For the weak this is direct: the orphan for whom you stand with justice (4:127), the wife suspended not left hanging (4:129), the both enriched after separation (4:130) — all are affairs whose *wakīl* suffices, so that you need not be hanging nor make another hanging, but can settle or part entrusted to the Sufficient.

**A Short Verse That Anchors Entrusting After Legislating**

Though only *“to Him belongs whatever is in heavens and earth, He suffices as Trustee,”* the verse anchors entrusting after legislating. The chapter has legislated from single soul to orphans to marriage to inheritance to trusts to prayer to judgment to charity to Abraham’s way, and here returns to its pillar: ownership grounds law, trusteeship executes it. The sūrah that began *“be mindful of your Lord who created you from single soul and from it its mate”* (4:1) and that will say *“if He wills He can remove you and bring others, He is Most Capable”* (4:133) places between them this *wakīl* — sufficient — so that the creature who can be removed (4:133) and who can desire world’s reward (4:134) knows to whom affairs are entrusted where wishes fail and He remains.

## Sūrah al-Baqarah 2:48

> Guard yourselves against the Day on which no soul will be of help to another. No intercession will be accepted, no ransom taken, and no help will be given.

**Expanded Commentary**

**A Day to Guard Against**

“Guard yourselves against the Day on which no soul will be of help to another. No intercession will be accepted, no ransom taken, and no help will be given” (2:48). Wa ittaqū yawman — “be mindful of a Day,” “be on guard against a Day,” — the same root as taqwā (2:41: “be mindful of Me”) now turned toward the Day. The sūrah, having recalled favour and rank (2:47), immediately sets the Day that unmasks rank. What lineage honoured cannot substitute on that Day; what is needed is guardedness beforehand.

The verse closely resembles 2:123, where the same guarding is repeated as refrain, and its image of no soul availing another is sharpened elsewhere as “a Day when no soul shall have power over another” (82:19). The repetition signals centrality: social currencies will not convert.

**“No Soul Will Avail Another” — The End of Proxy**

Lā tajzī nafsun ʿan nafsin shayʾan — no soul will avail another in any way (2:48). The phrase includes wealth, children, status, and lineage — the very things that availed in Arabian tribal transactions and that the Qur’an elsewhere names as not availing (3:10, 116). On that Day, agency is not transferable: each soul’s account is its own, though each soul’s life was interwoven with others for provision and guidance. This is not a denial of community; it is a distribution of responsibility within community.

For the Children of Israel specifically, the line answers the temptation to rely on descent from prophets: to be children of Isrāʾīl does not make a soul stand in for another. For all hearers, it answers the broader temptation to outsource moral effort.

**No Intercession Accepted, No Ransom Taken, No Help Given — Three Currencies Refused**

The verse lists three rejected exchanges:

- **Intercession (shafāʿah)** — pleading, mediation. The source traces shafāʿah’s earthly analogs: istighfār — seeking forgiveness for another as angels do for believers (40:7); ṣalāh as blessing — the Prophet told to “bless them; your blessings are a comfort” (9:103). On earth, intercession and ransom have lawful forms — feeding a poor person when unable to fast (2:184) — and human standing can mediate. In the Hereafter, intercession as worldly bargaining — pulling rank to be heard — will not avail. Passages denying intercession (32:4; 39:44; 74:48) and those affirming its possibility by leave (21:28; 34:23; 53:26; 20:109: “intercession will be of no benefit save him whom the Merciful grants leave and whose word He is pleased with”) are read together: all intercession belongs to God (39:44), and any that occurs is by His leave and pleasure. The source, drawing on Ibn ʿArabī, notes that even the supplication that brings intercession into being is itself a manifestation of divine mercy — “Remember Me, I shall remember you” (2:152) — and that God’s nearness (50:16; 8:24; 76:30; 81:29; 2:186) authorizes the plea without making the plea manipulate the author. Thus the denial here targets autonomous, status-driven mediation, not all mediation by divine permission.

- **Ransom (ʿadl/fidyah)** — compensation. The source notes ʿadl here literally means substitution, usually fidyah. The Qur’an elsewhere says an earth full of gold cannot ransom (3:91), nor double the earth’s worth (5:36; 13:18), nor one’s own children (70:11). The scale marks futility: what could not be earned in a lifetime cannot be paid in a moment.

- **Help (nuṣira)** — succor, rescue by allies. No helper stands between soul and accounting. The Day isolates not to humiliate but to clarify: help that mattered was the help sought in patience and prayer (2:45) and the certainty of return (2:46).

2:47 said “We honoured you above the others.” 2:48 says honour does not spend on that Day. The sequence prevents misreading tafḍīl as credit. The sūrah’s ethic is consistent: provision was “for you” (2:29) yet required stewardship; descent gave residence “for a term” (2:36) yet required following guidance; honour is given yet requires guarding. Each gift is paired with its limit.

**Guarding, Not Paralysis**

Ittaqū yawman does not counsel dread that freezes; it counsels foresight that reforms. To guard against the Day is to do today what will avail then: fulfil covenant (2:40), refuse sale (2:41), unmix and disclose (2:42), establish prayer and alms and bow (2:43), align speech and deed (2:44), and seek help in patience and prayer as the humble certain of return (2:45–46).

**Living the Verse**

- Audit proxies. Where am I relying that another’s piety, lineage, or patronage will avail for me? The verse closes that account.
- Audit bargains. Where am I storing ransom — wealth, children, influence — to pay what only deeds and mercy can address?
- Audit help. Whose help am I counting on that will not be given then, and whose help — patience, prayer, and the Merciful who taught words of return (2:37) — have I left unsought now?

This verse alone does not settle the theology of intercession in total; it states the Day’s rule that no intercession will be accepted from that soul, no ransom taken. The fuller doctrine — that all intercession belongs to God and some is granted by leave — is treated elsewhere (2:255) and should not be denied or affirmed from this verse alone. What is firm is the summons to guard against that Day now, while guarding is still possible (2:48).

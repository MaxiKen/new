## Sūrah al-Baqarah 2:29

> He is the One Who created everything in the earth for you. Then He turned towards the heaven, forming it into seven heavens. And He has ˹perfect˺ knowledge of all things.

**Expanded Commentary**

**Everything on the Earth, Made for Its Occupants**

The verse begins with a claim about purpose, and it is stated without qualification: what is on the earth exists for the people being addressed. The Book repeats this statement in a number of places and with the same directness, including passages that extend it beyond the planet: *“Have you not seen that Allah has subjected for you whatever is in the heavens and whatever is on the earth, and has lavished His favours upon you, both seen and unseen?”* (31:20), and *“He ˹also˺ subjected for you whatever is in the heavens and whatever is on the earth—all by His grace. Surely in this are signs for people who reflect.”* (45:13).

Read beside the previous verse, the argument builds in a particular direction. That verse established that the human being did not make himself; this one establishes that the world he was placed in was furnished for him before he arrived. The Book treats the arrangement as evidence of intention, and it uses the evidence the way a host uses a prepared table — as a reason for the guest to consider who prepared it.

**Turning Toward the Heaven and Forming Seven**

The second sentence describes the making of the heavens, and the Arabic verb rendered *turned towards* is the same one the Book uses for God's establishment on the throne in several passages (7:54, 10:3, 32:4, 57:4). Its sense in those contexts is a rising or a settling, and the commentators discussed at length what such language can properly mean when applied to God, generally concluding that the manner of the act is not accessible to human description.

What the verse states without ambiguity is the result: seven heavens. The number appears elsewhere with descriptions attached. One passage says they are layered: *“˹He is the One˺ Who created seven heavens, one above the other. You will never see any imperfection in the creation of the Most Compassionate.”* (67:3). Another states the same fact with a question: *“Do you not see how Allah created seven heavens, one above the other”* (71:15), and a third mentions the creation of the earth's like in number (65:12). Sūrah Fuṣṣilat describes the fashioning in two days, with each heaven given its mandate (41:12).

**A Sequence the Commentators Discussed**

The order in the verse has attracted attention, because the earth's contents are mentioned before the forming of the heavens, while other passages describe the heavens being made first. Commentators reconciled the two by reading the sequence here as a description of what was prepared for human beings rather than as a chronological report of creation, since the point being made is about provision rather than about the schedule of events.

The readings differ on detail and agree on emphasis. What the verse is doing is arranging the world's furniture in front of its inhabitants and naming the one who arranged it, and the mention of the seven heavens serves to extend the scale beyond the ground underfoot. The sūrah will return to the same created order in a later passage where the audience is invited to consider the alternation of night and day as evidence, and the argument is the same one.

**"He Has Knowledge of All Things"**

The verse closes with knowledge, and the placement is deliberate. The claim just made about the earth being made for human beings is the kind of statement that raises a question about scale: human beings are small, their lives are brief, and the assertion that the universe was arranged around them invites scepticism. The closing clause answers by naming the one whose knowledge covers the whole arrangement without exception.

The Book returns to this attribute in the verse of the throne (2:255), where it explains that God knows what is ahead of His creatures and what is behind them, and that no one grasps any part of His knowledge except by His will. The same pair of attributes closes other passages about the created order, including the declaration that God is the Knower of the seen and the unseen, the Almighty, the All-Wise (64:18).

**Provision as a Ground for Loyalty**

This verse supplies the premise that the earlier command to worship was resting on. The Book does not ask for worship on the basis of a claim nobody can check; it points at what its audience already possesses — the ground, the water, the fruit, the roof of the sky — and then asks who arranged it. The sūrah used the same sequence two verses before, and it will keep returning to provision as its standing evidence.

What the verse never does is describe the arrangement as a payment. Nothing here states that the world was made for human beings because they deserved it, and the Book elsewhere presents the same gift as a test as much as a favour. The clause about knowledge over all things is the one that keeps both aspects in view, since the same knowledge that accounts for the gift also measures what its recipients do with it.

**What "For You" Implies About the Human Being**

The claim that the earth's contents were made for human beings carries an obligation inside it, and the sūrah draws that obligation out later in the same chapter. Everything named as subject to human use — the ground, the water, the produce, the animals — is described elsewhere as given for a purpose, and the Book states the purpose repeatedly: that people might give thanks, reflect, and act rightly.

The Qur'an never presents the arrangement as a licence to take without consequence. The same passage that grants human beings succession on the earth adds that they will be tested by what they have been given (6:165), and another verse describes the earth's corruption as the fruit of human conduct rather than as an accident of nature (30:41). The verse here supplies the gift; the Book's treatment of the gift as a trust follows directly from it, and this sūrah is the chapter in which most of that treatment is worked out.

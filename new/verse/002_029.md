## Sūrah al-Baqarah 2:29

> He is the One Who created everything in the earth for you. Then He turned towards the heaven, forming it into seven heavens. And He has ˹perfect˺ knowledge of all things.

**Expanded Commentary**

**Everything in the Earth — For You**

The verse's first clause is a deed of gift: *khalaqa lakum mā fī al-ard jamīʿan* — He created for you whatever is in the earth, *all of it*. The preposition *lakum*, "for you," is the operative word; the Qur'an repeats the gift-formula across sūrahs — cattle, crops, the sea's yield, the night and day — and states its general principle elsewhere: *"He ˹also˺ subjected for you whatever is in the heavens and whatever is on the earth—all by His grace"* (45:13). The comprehensive *jamīʿan* forecloses every carve-out: not some resources, not the useful ones, not the ones a particular civilization happens to exploit — everything in the earth, including what no generation has yet found a name for. The clause therefore reads forward as well as backward: ores no ancient market knew, forces no classical physics dreamed of, are inside the gift's scope. The verse is the Qur'an's ground-title for human use of the world, and its breadth is matched only by its condition — a gift *for you* is a gift with a purpose, and the purpose was stated ten verses earlier, in the command to worship the Giver.

**The Jurisprudence and the Limit of “For You”**

Islamic law built on this clause, and the building has two walls. The first is permission: what was created for human use is presumptively lawful — the default of things in the world is permissibility until a text restricts it, a principle the jurists derive from exactly this verse's generosity. The earth is not a sacred untouchable; it is a furnished estate handed to a tenant with instructions to live. The second wall is limit: "for you" states a beneficiary, not an owner absolute. The gift-formula appears in a sūrah whose opening command was worship and whose next verse announces a vicegerent — and a vicegerent administers property in the name of the Principal. The classical ethic of the gift follows: use without waste, extraction without corruption, possession held as trust — the same three bonds verse 27 listed, now seen from the property side. A covenant-breaker corrupts the land (2:27); the land corrupted is the gift misused; the misuse is not primarily economic but theological, a tenant redecorating the house as if he held the title. Modern readers hear the verse's ecology here: an endowed earth creates duties, not merely appetites.

**Istawā: Turning Toward the Heaven**

The second movement begins with a verb the Qur'an deploys in creation contexts with deliberate weight: *thumma istawā ilā al-samāʾ* — then He turned toward, or directed Himself to, or established Himself over the heaven; the translations vary because the verb's range is wide, and this very verse renders it "turned towards" while elsewhere — *the Most Compassionate, ˹Who is˺ established on the Throne* (20:5) — the same word pictures enthronement. The commentators' shared minimum is purposeful attention: *istawā* carries the sense of addressing a task completely, the way a builder turns from the foundation to the frame. The *thumma*, "then," marks an order of disclosure rather than divine delay — the Qur'an's creation sequences consistently narrate stages for a hearer who can only take them in sequence. The movement of the verse is architectural: the floor furnished first (the earth, for you), then the raising of the structure above it — canopy in verse 22, seven-fold edifice here. The reader is being walked through his house in the order a house is made: ground, then roof, then the inspection clause that closes the verse.

**Seven Heavens: The Numbered Sky**

The heaven's formation is given a number: *sabʿa samāwāt*, seven heavens — stated flatly, without explanation, and the Qur'an returns to the figure with structural detail: *"˹He is the One˺ Who created seven heavens, one above the other. You will never see any imperfection in the creation of the Most Compassionate"* (67:3), the layered construction also appearing in the six-days narrative of Fuṣṣilat (41:12) and in al-Ṭalāq's pairing of seven heavens with the like thereof in earths (65:12) — earths upon earths, as the early commentator Mujāhid read the cosmology: heavens above heavens, earths above earths. The classical exegetes ranged freely over the figure — atmospheric layers, celestial spheres, orders of being — and the Qur'an's own usage declines to legislate among them, fixing only three claims: the sky is plural in order, layered rather than a single dome; the layering is flawless, an engineered perfection the eye can test (67:3 challenges it directly); and the structure, however many of its floors lie beyond human sight, was built with the same "for you" orientation as the floor the reader stands on. Seven, in Semitic usage, is also the number of completeness — and the completeness claim is exactly the one the verse's closing clause will ratify.

**The Knower of All Things Signs the Blueprint**

The verse ends by stepping back from its own cosmology to name the Cosmologist's remaining attribute: *wa-Huwa bi-kulli shayʾin ʿalīm* — and He has knowledge of all things. The placement answers a question the grandeur of the scene raises unasked. A creation this vast — seven heavens over an earth stocked with everything — invites the worry that haunts every large house: does the Builder still know what happens in the small rooms? Is a creature this minor inside a cosmos this layered actually registered? The clause answers with the Qur'an's standard universal: *kull shayʾ*, every thing, with no scale floor and no scale ceiling. Omniscience here is not an abstract perfection but the guarantee of the gift's governance — the provisions are delivered by a Provider who tracks the delivery down to each recipient, and the return of verse 28 will be a return to precisely this knowledge. The same clause also underwrites the challenge two sūrahs-earlier verses and the census of verse 26: the God who parables with mosquitoes knows mosquitoes. Vastness and minuteness are held in one sentence, and the sentence is signed by the attribute that holds them: knowledge of all things.

**The Stage Is Set; The Appointment Follows**

Read in sequence, verses 21–29 form one complete movement — the sūrah's grounding — and verse 29 is its final stroke. The command to worship was issued (21), the furnished house shown as evidence (22), the Book's own claim tested and the alternatives prepared (23–25), the parable's sorting explained and the covenant-breakers defined (26–27), the reader's own biography produced as the nearest evidence (28), and now the cosmos inventoried as a gift with a Giver who knows every item (29). Everything required for the next scene is in place: an endowed earth, a layered heaven, an omniscient Owner, and a species under a worship-command. The verse that follows opens with the announcement that gathers all four threads — a declaration, made to the angels, about a placement upon this very earth. The cosmology of verse 29 is thus not an interlude but a stage set, furnished in detail so that the drama of the next verses can begin with everything already built, endowed, and known.

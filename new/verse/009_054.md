## Sūrah al-Tawbah 9:54

> And what prevented their donations from being accepted is that they have lost faith in Allah and His Messenger, they never come to prayer except half-heartedly, and they never donate except resentfully.

**Expanded Commentary**

**The Verse Names the Barrier Clearly**

The passage does not leave the reason for rejection vague. Three connected defects explain why their donations do not become accepted worship: lost faith in Allah and His Messenger, lazy attendance at prayer, and resentful giving. The outward acts exist, but the foundation beneath them has been removed. A donation cannot compensate for disbelief, and a public prayer cannot disguise contempt for prayer.

The order moves from belief to worship to social spending. Faith is the root, prayer is its repeated meeting with Allah, and charity is its effect upon other people. When the root is damaged, the branches become performance. This does not mean that every tired prayer proves hypocrisy. The verse describes a settled pattern in which reluctance governs the act and faith is absent.

**Prayer Reveals What the Heart Does With Time**

They come to prayer half-heartedly. The problem is not simply slow movement or physical fatigue. It is the decision to treat standing before Allah as an unwanted interruption. Prayer becomes a public obligation endured for appearance rather than a meeting entered with humility.

A believer can struggle with concentration and still be sincere. Human attention wanders, and worship often requires repeated return. The warning concerns the attitude that welcomes distraction because it does not value the prayer. The servant should therefore distinguish weakness from indifference. If he is troubled by his lack of presence, that trouble can lead him back. If he protects his laziness and calls it harmless, the disease is deeper.

**Resentful Giving Does Not Become Gratitude**

Their donation is offered with resentment. They may fear blame, want to be counted among the community, or hope to prevent a demand for more. The money leaves their hands, but the heart treats the gift as an insult. Such giving cannot easily produce gratitude because the donor is focused on what he thinks he has lost.

The believer should not wait for perfect emotion before paying an obligation. A person may give while feeling reluctant and still obey sincerely if he fights the reluctance and acknowledges Allah's right. Resentment becomes blameworthy when it is chosen, fed, and used to reject the duty. The heart can be trained through repeated giving until generosity becomes more natural than possession.

**Faith Joins Private Conviction to Public Action**

The verse shows why belief cannot remain a claim on the tongue. Faith in Allah and His Messenger should shape time, body, and wealth. It may be hidden from people for a while, but it appears in patterns. A person who truly recognizes Allah may still fail, yet he does not make failure his settled identity. He returns, repairs, and dislikes his distance.

This also prevents the opposite error of judging faith from one visible performance. Someone may pray quietly with great sincerity while the audience sees little. Another may give publicly while seeking admiration. Allah knows the relation between the act and the belief that gives it life. Communities must teach the outer duties without pretending to own the unseen judgment.

**The Claim of Good Deeds Needs a Moral Center**

Some commentators use this verse when discussing good deeds performed without faith. The balanced point is that good can have real effects in this world while lacking the reward of worship in the Hereafter. A meal feeds a person whether or not the giver believes. But the spiritual account of the giver involves faith, intention, and obedience as well as the benefit received.

This distinction should make believers grateful, not arrogant. They do not control Allah's generosity toward creation, and they cannot claim that their deeds force acceptance. They should also avoid treating every good action by an unbeliever as worthless in ordinary human terms. Justice recognizes the benefit; revelation defines the worship that is accepted before Allah.

**Repair Begins at the Root**

A person who recognizes this pattern should not begin by polishing the public gift while ignoring faith and prayer. He begins with repentance and renewed certainty. He learns the meaning of the prayer, protects its times, and asks Allah to remove the bitterness that makes giving feel like loss. As worship becomes real, charity can become an expression rather than a performance.

The process may be gradual, but it must be honest. A servant can say, “I am struggling,” without saying, “My struggle is the standard.” He can give a small amount with a sincere heart, then build discipline. Acceptance belongs to Allah, yet the path toward acceptance is visible: belief, prayer, generosity, and a heart that wants obedience rather than merely its reputation.

The three defects reinforce one another. Weak faith makes prayer feel burdensome; neglected prayer makes the heart less ready to surrender; resentment toward giving then appears natural because the servant has forgotten that all provision comes from Allah. Repair must therefore be connected. Renew belief, protect the prayer, and give in a way that trains gratitude. The servant may begin with a hidden act so that people cannot reward the performance. He may then increase the act as sincerity grows. Allah does not need the display, but the servant needs the discipline that turns display into worship.

The outward act becomes sound when the servant returns to its inward source. Faith gives prayer meaning, prayer trains surrender, and surrender makes generosity free from bitterness.

The beginning of repair is simple but demanding: believe truthfully, protect the prayer, and give without treating obedience as an insult. Allah knows the effort by which a bitter heart is taught to become grateful.

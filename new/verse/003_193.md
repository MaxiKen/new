## Sūrah Āli ʿImrān 3:193

> Our Lord! We have heard the caller to ˹true˺ belief, ˹proclaiming,˺ ‘Believe in your Lord ˹alone˺,’ so we believed. Our Lord! Forgive our sins, absolve us of our misdeeds, and allow us ˹each˺ to die as one of the virtuous.

**Expanded Commentary**

**The Caller and the Call**

The verse continues the prayer with a report of what the community heard: a caller calling to faith, urging belief in their Lord, and the community's answer that it believed. The identity of the caller is most often given as the Prophet, and al-Rāzī records the view that the call is the Book itself, since the message reaches everyone while only a limited number heard it from the Prophet directly.

Both readings describe the same event from different angles. What the community is testifying to is that it was called and that it responded, and the prayer's next clause asks for the forgiveness that the response makes necessary. The report is made in the first person, so the community speaks as one body about what it heard and accepted.

**The Response Reported in the Prayer**

The words the community uses are a simple statement that it believed, and the simplicity is characteristic of the prayer. The Book's descriptions of faith in such passages avoid elaboration and place the emphasis on the act of accepting the message.

The believers in this verse are not described as having understood everything or as having fulfilled the obligations that followed. They are described as having heard a call and responded to it, and the remainder of the prayer asks for what they need in order to carry the response through to the end.

**The Request for Forgiveness**

The prayer asks for the forgiveness of sins, and the vocabulary is the same as that used by the believers who pray in other passages of the sūrah, where they ask forgiveness and protection from the punishment of the fire. The request for protection from the fire in the preceding verses is followed here by the request for the removal of what would bring it.

The Book's prayers consistently include a request for the forgiveness of the one praying, and the community's model in this passage does the same. The people who are described as reflecting on the creation do not regard themselves as beyond the need of pardon, and the community that recites the verses is taught the same posture.

**The Removal of Evil Deeds**

The second request is for the removal of evil deeds, and the wording is fuller than a simple plea for pardon. The Book elsewhere promises that those who repent, believe, and do good will have their evil deeds changed into good ones, and it describes the covering of faults as one of the effects of forgiveness.

The petition asks for a treatment of the record rather than merely for the avoidance of punishment. What the community is requesting is that its worst actions should not be left standing against it, and the same request appears in the prayers of the believers in the sūrah about the Night Journey and elsewhere.

**The Company of the Pious**

The prayer's final request is to be taken to God among the company of the pious, and the request is for association rather than for a rank. The Book describes the company of the prophets, the truthful, the martyrs, and the righteous as the companions of those who obey God and His messenger, and it describes that company as excellent.

The petition reflects the Book's understanding of what a person should want for himself: not only safety but presence with those who were faithful. The community's literature has used the verse in its teaching about choosing one's company, and the prayer in this sūrah places the desire for good company among the requests of the people of understanding.

**The Prayer's Place in the Community's Devotion**

The sūrah's closing prayer is one of the most widely repeated in the community's worship, and the commentators note that it appears in many manuals of supplication. Its use is not restricted to the recitation of the sūrah, and it has served the community as a model for private supplication as well.

The prayer's content explains its circulation. It combines a statement of what the believer has heard and accepted, a request for the pardon of his faults, and a request for the company of the righteous at the end. Each of those elements is teachable on its own, and the community's teachers have used the passage to instruct new Muslims in what they should say.

**The Language of Calling in the Book**

The Book describes the message as a call in several places, and it uses the image of a voice summoning people to a position. One passage describes a caller among the jinn telling his people to respond to the one who calls to God and to believe in him.

The image of the caller shapes the community's understanding of what the message is. A call is either answered or ignored, and the Book's descriptions of the two responses occupy much of its instruction. The verse before the reader places the community's answer in the prayer it recites, and the answer is stated without self-congratulation.

**What the Prayer Teaches About Faith**

The prayer describes faith as a sequence: hearing a call, responding to it, and then asking for what the response requires. The three movements belong together, and the community's teachers drew on the passage for that reason.

The sequence also describes the community's own history, which the sūrah has been recounting through its account of the battle and its aftermath. The people described in the closing passage are the ones who heard the call and continued to keep their standing, and their prayer is the sūrah's final instruction on what such a people should ask of God. Faith, on this account, is not a single moment but a course that the requests of the prayer are meant to sustain.

## Sūrah al-Nisāʾ 4:72

> There will be some among you who will lag behind so that if you face a disaster, they will say, “Allah has blessed us for not being there among them.”

### Expanded Commentary

**The opportunist who avoids every cost**

Some people lag behind so that they will not share danger. When disaster strikes the believers, they call their absence a blessing. Their words reveal a distorted understanding of safety: they measure blessing by personal escape even when others suffer for the common cause.

**Private comfort and public obligation**

The verse does not condemn every person who stays behind. Illness, necessary care, or a legitimate exemption can be part of obedience. It addresses those who evade a duty without excuse and then celebrate the hardship of those who fulfilled it. A community cannot survive if every member claims the benefits of collective action while refusing its burdens.

The opportunist’s statement also wounds morale. Instead of praying for the afflicted and helping their families, he converts tragedy into evidence for his own prudence. The believer should avoid that cruelty, assess duties honestly, and support those who bear risks for justice.

**Exemption is not opportunism**

A person who remains behind for a real duty or incapacity may still belong fully to the community. The problem is celebrating the suffering of those who served while claiming innocence from every cost. Communities should assess excuses fairly and refuse both suspicion of the genuinely unable and admiration for the cynical avoider.

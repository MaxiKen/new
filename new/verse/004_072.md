## Sūrah al-Nisāʾ 4:72

> There will be some among you who will lag behind so that if you face a disaster, they will say, “Allah has blessed us for not being there among them.”

**Expanded Commentary**

**Among You Who Will Lag Behind**

The verse says *wa inna minkum la man la yubaṭṭiʾanna* — and there will be some among you who will lag behind — addressing the community that was just told to take precautions and go forth in groups or together (4:71). *Yubaṭṭiʾanna* — to lag, to hold back, to make others lag — was read as deliberate slowness, both staying behind and causing others to stay behind by talk. The phrase *minkum* — among you — was stressed as showing that the lagger is inside the community, not clearly outside among open disbelievers, the same position as the hypocrites who claim belief yet seek false judges (4:60) and turn away stubbornly (4:61). The sūrah has shown that God knows best who enemies are and that He suffices as Guardian and Helper (4:45), and that among those given a portion some believed and some turned away (4:55). Here the divider is not doctrine alone but readiness to go forth. The one who lags does not say he disbelieves; he simply does not take *ḥidhr* and go forth as commanded, and his absence is explained as blessing when disaster strikes. The sūrah therefore diagnoses hypocracy not only by distorted words — *we hear and disobey* (4:46) — but by the foot that does not move.

**If You Face a Disaster — Allah Has Blessed Us**

The portrait continues *fa in aṣābatkum muṣībatun qāla qad anʿama Allāhu ʿalayya idh lam akun maʿahum shahīdā* — so that if you face a disaster, they will say, Allah has blessed us for not being there among them as witness. *Muṣībah* — disaster, calamity that hits — recalls *how will it be if a disaster strikes them because of what their hands did* (4:62) where hypocrites swore goodwill after hands had sought *ṭāghūt*. Here the *muṣībah* is in battle — setback, loss, injury — the kind the sūrah said will come if you do not fight for the oppressed? The lagger’s comment calls absence a blessing — *anʿama Allāhu ʿalayya* — a phrase the Qur’an elsewhere says the grateful use when sent Gardens, but here used for staying away. The word *shahīd* — witness, present — was read as being present at the event, sharing the trial. To thank God for not being *shahīd* where believers fought for Him is to thank Him for missing the honour that obedience would have given — the honourable company with prophets, truthful, martyrs, righteous (4:69). The lagger mistakes safety from a *muṣībah* as *niʿmah* — favour — while the sūrah has defined favour as being with the blessed by obedience (4:69–70) and as an atom’s weight not wronged but multiplied (4:40). His definition is inverted.

**Blessing Misnamed**

The lagger’s *“Allah has blessed us”* was read as a misuse of *niʿmah* that mirrors the earlier misuse *“we intended nothing but goodwill and reconciliation”* (4:62). Both borrow the sūrah’s language — *niʿmah*, *iḥsān*, *tawfīq* — and apply it to avoidance. The sūrah corrects that language throughout: true *niʿmah* is *favour from God known to Him* (4:70), true *iḥsān* is kindness to parents, relatives, orphans, neighbours, and travellers (4:36), and true *tawfīq* is harmony God brings when mediators desire it (4:35). To call sitting out a disaster a blessing is to call the opposite of those goods a good, which is why the next verse will show the same lagger, when bounty comes — *faḍl* — saying *would that I had been there to share the great gain* (4:73), revealing that his measure is gain and safety, not obedience. The sūrah has already asked *“what harm if they had believed and donated from what was provided”* (4:39) to expose that withholding harms none but the withholder; here it shows the withholder of presence believes absence helped him, while the sūrah will soon say that those who sacrifice this life for the next will fight and be honoured whether martyred or victorious (4:74).

**Among You — A Test of the Bond**

The phrase *minkum* — among you — was stressed as test of the bond that the next verse says will be treated *as if there had been no bond between you* (4:73). The community described as being from a single soul (4:1), as *you are from one another* (4:25), and as appointed heirs after debts (4:11–12) is a community of ties. The lagger is among those ties yet not with them — *lam akun maʿahum* — I was not with them. His absence severs the bond in practice while keeping the name among. The sūrah has shown that hypocrisy after disaster swears goodwill; here hypocrisy before disaster declares its own blessing for absence, which is the same self-interest before and after. For the believers told to go forth in groups or together, the verse is a warning to recognize the lagger within — the foot that hesitates, the tongue that discourages — and not to be made to lag by him. The verse generalises what earlier verses specificed for worship and law: just as changing *rāʿinā* to *unẓurnā* required replacing an ambiguous word with a clear one (4:46), changing lagging to going forth requires replacing *anʿama ʿalayya* for absence with *anʿama ʿalayhim* for obedience, the favour the sūrah defines as being with the blessed.

**A Preview of the Next Reversal**

Placed where it is, this verse previews the reversal the next verse will complete. Here the lagger is grateful for not being present at disaster; there he will be envious for not being present at bounty, saying *would that I had been there* as if no bond had been. The two statements together show that his measure never was the bond — *you are from one another* — but the outcome — safety or gain. The sūrah therefore holds up the lagger as the opposite of the one who accepts the Prophet ﷺ as judge without resistance and submits wholeheartedly (4:65) and of the one who does what was advised and is more reassured and guided (4:66–68). The lagger’s *would that* when gain comes will be shown as hollow, because the gain he craves is called great — *fawz ʿaẓīm* — elsewhere for obedience (4:13), not for absence. The verse’s short portrait — among you, lagging, calling absence blessing at disaster — thus does more than describe a type; it asks every listener among whom the lagger is, where his own foot will be when the call to take precautions and go forth is given, and whether he will call that going a blessing or call staying back one.

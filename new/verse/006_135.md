## Sūrah al-Anʿām 6:135

> Say, ˹O Prophet,˺ “O my people! Persist in your ways, for I ˹too˺ will persist in mine. You will soon know who will fare best in the end. Indeed, the wrongdoers will never succeed.”

**Expanded Commentary**

**The Declaration Given to the Prophet**

Say, O my people! Persist in your ways, for I too will persist in mine. The verse instructs the Prophet to address his people with a declaration that both sides will continue as they are, and the Book gives the same words to other messengers. The sūrah of the Troops records the instruction with the addition that the audience will soon come to know who will be visited by a humiliating punishment and an everlasting torment (39:39–40); the sūrah of Hūd gives the words to Shuʿayb with the same conclusion (11:93); and another verse of the same sūrah instructs the Prophet to tell those who disbelieve to persist in their ways while the believers persist in theirs (11:121).

The declaration is not a threat of violence or a challenge to a contest of power. It is the Book's way of closing a dispute that has been conducted with arguments: each side has stated its position, and each will continue in the way it has chosen, and the outcome will show which of them was right.

**The Meaning of Persisting**

The Book's word for persisting carries the sense of working and acting within a chosen station, and the verses that use it consistently describe the two parties engaging in their own kinds of activity. The sūrah's audience was continuing in its worship, its customs, and its opposition to the message; the Prophet was continuing in the delivery of the revelation and in the conduct that belonged to it. The sentence states that both will proceed rather than that either will be coerced.

The Book's treatment of the matter elsewhere gives the reader the same picture. The sūrah of the Bee tells the Prophet to call people to the way of the Lord with wisdom and good instruction and to argue in the best manner, and it adds that the Lord knows best who has strayed and who is guided (16:125). The declaration in this verse is the form that instruction takes in the case of a people who have refused.

**The Outcome Left to the Future**

You will soon know who will fare best in the end. The sentence states that the outcome will be evident, and the Book's word for the end describes the final consequence of a course of action. The passages that use the same formula attach it to the same question: which of the two parties enjoys the security and the success that the message promises, and which is overtaken by the punishment the messengers announced.

The Book's accounts of the earlier communities supply the answer in historical terms. The sūrah of Hūd describes the destruction of the people who rejected their messenger and the rescue of those who followed him, and the sūrah of the Poets tells the Prophet that he will see the fulfilment of what was promised them. The verse in front of us leaves the matter at that point: the outcome will be known, and the knowledge will come to both parties.

**The Wrongdoers Will Never Succeed**

Indeed, the wrongdoers will never succeed. The verse closes with the declaration of the outcome, and the sūrah has used the same sentence before. It appears at the end of the verse about the people who fabricate lies against God or deny His signs (6:21), and again in the words of Moses in the sūrah of the Stories, where he says that his Lord knows best who has come with true guidance and who will fare best in the end, adding that the wrongdoers will never succeed (28:37).

The sentence states the outcome in the form of a general law rather than as a prediction about particular people. What the sūrah's audience was being told is that the category to which their conduct belonged has a fixed end, and the declaration does not depend on the identity of any individual or community. The Book's confidence in this statement rests on the attributes the previous verse named: the Lord who is self-sufficient and merciful is also the Lord who brings the affair to its conclusion.

**What the Verse Requires of the Prophet**

The instruction gives the Prophet a way to conduct himself in a city that would not accept his message. He is not to abandon his people, since he addresses them as his own; he is not to pretend that the disagreement has been settled; and he is not to force the issue. He states the position of each side and continues with his work, and the outcome is left to God.

That pattern is consistent with the sūrah's earlier instructions: the command to follow what was revealed and to turn away from the polytheists (6:106), the reminder that the Prophet is neither keeper nor maintainer (6:107), and the statement that God's will decides who believes (6:111). The verse in front of us gives the practical form those instructions take in the Prophet's relationship with his own people.

**The Section That Follows**

The next verse begins the sūrah's detailed treatment of the audience's ritual system, describing the shares they assigned to God and to their idols from the crops and livestock and the claim they made about the arrangement (6:136). The verse in front of us closes the long section of argument that has run from the sūrah's opening, and it hands the reader over to an examination of the practices themselves.

The passage that follows will continue for many verses, treating the assignment of shares, the rules about livestock, the animals set apart for certain people, and the claims made about what God had permitted. Each of those passages applies the principles the earlier section established, and the declaration in this verse is the point from which the reader enters them: each party persists in its way, and the outcome belongs to God.

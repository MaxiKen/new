## Sūrah al-Nisāʾ 4:95

> Those who stay at home—except those with valid excuses—are not equal to those who strive in the cause of Allah with their wealth and their lives. Allah has elevated in rank those who strive with their wealth and their lives above those who stay behind ˹with valid excuses˺. Allah has promised each a fine reward, but those who strive will receive a far better reward than others—

### Expanded Commentary

**Different ranks without contempt for exemptions**

Those who strive with wealth and life in Allah’s cause are not equal to those who remain behind without a valid excuse. The verse honours costly service and rejects the claim that every person contributes identically when some bear risks that others deliberately avoid. It is not a condemnation of those prevented by illness, disability, caregiving, or other legitimate reasons; the text explicitly recognizes excuses.

**Wealth and life as distinct sacrifices**

Striving includes financial support as well as physical presence. A person may equip, feed, treat, or protect those on the front line without fighting directly. The verse thus values the network that makes just defence possible. It also prevents a narrow culture that praises visible bravery while ignoring the resources and care on which it depends.

Allah promises a fine reward to both categories, then gives the greater reward to those who strive. Equality of basic honour does not mean equality of every rank. The community must acknowledge sacrifice without humiliating those who are genuinely unable to make it.

**Honour the excused and the striving**

A person who cannot fight may serve through care, money, medical work, or truthful support, while a person who can serve should not hide behind the sacrifices of others. Communities need a fair account of capacity. Rank should recognize effort without making disability or caregiving a source of shame.

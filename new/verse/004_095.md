## Sūrah al-Nisāʾ 4:95

> Those who stay at home—except those with valid excuses—are not equal to those who strive in the cause of Allah with their wealth and their lives. Allah has elevated in rank those who strive with their wealth and their lives above those who stay behind ˹with valid excuses˺. Allah has promised each a fine reward, but those who strive will receive a far better reward than others—

**Expanded Commentary**

**Not Equal — Those Who Stay and Those Who Strive**

The verse states *lā yastawī al-qāʿidūna mina al-muʾminīna ghayru ulī al-ḍarari wa al-mujāhidūna fī sabīli Allāhi bi amwālihim wa anfusihim* — those who stay at home among believers, except those with valid excuses, are not equal to those who strive in the cause of God with wealth and lives. *Qāʿidūn* — those who sit, stay — was explained as remaining behind from going forth in groups or together as commanded (4:71). *Ghayru ulī al-ḍarari* — except possessors of harm, of valid excuse — was read as blindness, illness, lameness, or need that prevents going forth; the phrase was reported to have been inserted after the first revelation when Ibn Umm Maktūm, the blind companion, asked about his excuse and the Prophet ﷺ received the addition (Ṣaḥīḥ al-Bukhārī 2832). *Mujāhidūn* — strivers — with *amwālihim wa anfusihim* — wealth and lives — was explained as the two expenditures the sūrah has praised: spending from what God provided (4:39) and offering this life for the Hereafter in fight for the oppressed (4:74–75). To say they are not equal — *lā yastawī* — is not to say the sitter is condemned; the verse immediately promises good to both. It is to rank the acts, as the sūrah has ranked obedience that places with the prophets and truthful (4:69) above disobedience that will be burned (4:14).

**Elevated in Rank — Darajah Above Those Who Stay**

The rank *faḍḍala Allāhu al-mujāhidīna bi amwālihim wa anfusihim ʿalā al-qāʿidīna darajatan* — God has elevated in rank, preferred, those who strive with wealth and lives above those who stay, by a degree — was explained as a degree of closeness and reward. *Darajah* — rank, degree — was read as the standing given to the striver who verified before fighting (4:94) and who fought where trusts and justice are guarded (4:58–59). The sūrah has used degree language elsewhere where envy of God’s bounties was condemned (4:54) and where God’s favour upon the Prophet ﷺ is great (4:113); here the degree is not of envy but of effort. Early reports noted that the striver who goes forth light or heavy is not the same as the one whose excuse keeps him, though both believe; the former bears the cost that earlier only few would have borne had God commanded to sacrifice selves or abandon homes (4:66). The degree is therefore proportioned to the cost that the lightened burden (4:28) still leaves: to give wealth where stinginess is a temptation (4:37) and to risk life where death will overtake even in fortified towers (4:78).

**Each Promised a Fine Reward — Husnā for Both**

The assurance *wa kullan waʿada Allāhu al-ḥusnā* — Allah has promised each a fine reward, the beautiful — was explained as the promise to both sitter with excuse and striver: Paradise for faith held, as the sūrah promised Gardens under rivers for those who believe and do good (4:57) and as it said bequest and debt precede inheritance yet the limits are from God (4:11–13). “Each” — *kullan* — was stressed: the excused sitter is not erased, because his sitting is not the lagging that rejoices at disaster and envies gain (4:72–73) nor the regression God allowed because of misdeeds (4:88). His harm — *ḍarar* — is real, and the sūrah that lightens burdens for the weak spares him. The promise of *ḥusnā* was also read as goodness in this world where God gives earth spacious for emigration and many refuges (4:100) and goodness in the next where ranks, forgiveness, and mercy follow (4:96). The assurance prevents two errors: that the excused is despised, and that the striver’s degree means the other has nothing. Both have, but not equally.

**A Far Better Reward — Ajran ʿAẓīman**

The increase *wa faḍḍala Allāhu al-mujāhidīna ʿalā al-qāʿidīna ajran ʿaẓīmā* — but those who strive will receive a far better, mighty reward than others — was explained as beyond the single *darajah*, a mighty wage. *Ajr ʿaẓīm* — mighty reward — appears where charity, kindness, and reconciliation are done seeking God’s pleasure (4:114) and where holding fast to God after repentance is rewarded (4:146); here it is for striving. The detail is given in the next verse: ranks, forgiveness, and mercy from Him (4:96). The pattern matches the sūrah’s arithmetic of the good deed multiplied many times over by grace (4:40): the base reward — *ḥusnā* — is promised to each, then multiplied for the striver. The verse was also placed against reports where the Prophet ﷺ said the sitter’s prayer at home is not like the striver’s watch, and where the Qur’anic *darajah* was expounded as many degrees between heavens. That detail — degrees as spaces — was noted but left to interpretation, as the text itself names the increase as mighty without numbered limit, and the next verse names its form.

**A Verse That Ranks Without Breaking Brotherhood**

Placed after verification before killing and the warning against seeking fleeting gain by denying peace (4:94), this ranking prevents the opposite error: that staying is the same as striving where striving is required. The sūrah has already distinguished the lagger who stays and then envies (4:72–73) from the one who offers life for the Hereafter (4:74); here it distinguishes the excused stayer from the striver, promising good to both then preferring the striver by degree and mighty wage. The ethic is the sūrah’s own: *“those who believe and do good We will admit into Gardens”* (4:57), yet within good there are ranks. For the community divided into two groups about hypocrites (4:88) and commanded to be sure before fighting (4:94), the verse steadies judgment: do not equalise what God made unequal, and do not condemn whom God excused. The sūrah’s leniency for weakness — *“He wills to lighten burdens, humankind was created weak”* (4:28) — remains, even while wealth and lives are asked where the oppressed cry for a helper (4:75). The next verse will describe that mighty wage as ranks, forgiveness, and mercy from Him, the All-Forgiving, Most Merciful (4:96).

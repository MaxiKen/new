## Sūrah al-Shuʿarāʾ 26:43

> Moses said to them, “Cast whatever you wish to cast.”

**Expanded Commentary**

**Moses Gives the Magicians the First Move**

Moses tells the magicians to cast whatever they wish to cast. He does not impose a narrow choice or ask them to perform only within conditions favorable to him. The invitation gives them the first move before the public. Confidence in God's sign allows him to let the opposing side display its full craft rather than trying to win through procedure.

This generosity also protects the fairness of the scene. Pharaoh's court has arranged the contest, and Moses could have objected to its terms. Instead, he accepts the public setting and removes any excuse that the magicians were prevented from showing their best work. If their performance succeeds in appearance, the audience will have seen it without interference. The truth will not need a manipulated advantage.

**The Command Is Calm Because Moses Does Not Fear Imitation**

The sentence is short and direct. Moses does not insult the magicians or boast about the staff. He allows their action to come first. Such calm is not indifference to the stakes. It reflects certainty that the divine sign does not depend on confusing the audience or exhausting the opposition before it appears.

A person who fears that truth is fragile may try to control every condition of a debate. Moses does not behave that way. He knows that human craft has a real place in the event, but it does not have the same source as God's sign. Letting the magicians begin will make the difference visible. Their strongest display can be placed beside the sign without diminishing it.

**Whatever They Cast Exposes the Scope of Their Skill**

The word whatever leaves the magicians room to use their ropes, staffs, and methods. They are not reduced to a weak demonstration chosen by Moses. Their craft will appear in the form they consider most effective. This matters because the contest is not between a miracle and a deliberately poor imitation. The audience will see what skilled practitioners can do when given freedom.

The invitation also denies Moses any need to discover the details of their technique. He does not need to know every device before trusting God's support. A messenger's confidence is not based on superior human information. It is based on knowing that the truth does not depend on outmaneuvering every possible trick. The magicians may control their objects; they do not control the meaning of what follows.

**A Fair Hearing Can Expose an Unfair Interpretation**

Moses' openness makes the later accusation against him easier to judge. If he had prevented the magicians from performing, Pharaoh could claim that the result was produced by unequal rules. By inviting them to cast whatever they choose, Moses gives the public a complete comparison. The court will have no honest basis for saying that its experts were silenced.

This is an important form of courage in public argument. Let an opposing claim be stated strongly enough that it can be examined. Do not weaken it first and then congratulate yourself for answering it. Moses allows the magicians to present their best case. The sign will not be protected by hiding the rival performance; it will be understood by seeing what the rival performance cannot do.

**Moses Speaks to the Magicians as Responsible Agents**

The address to the magicians is not only a command to objects. It is directed to people who can choose how to use their skill. Moses does not treat them as mindless tools of Pharaoh. He gives them room to act, which leaves them responsible for what they display and for the interpretation they will later make of the result.

This respect does not approve their service to the court. It recognizes that they are moral agents who may still respond to truth. A person caught in an unjust institution is not automatically beyond change. Moses' calm invitation leaves open a path for the magicians to see the limits of their craft and to choose a different allegiance.

**The First Move Has No Moral Victory in It**

Moses' willingness to let them begin prevents the audience from confusing initiative with truth. The side that casts first may look confident, and the side that waits may look passive. But order of performance does not establish authority. Moses waits because he does not need to prove himself through speed. The meaning of the contest will come from what each act is and what it reveals.

The verse therefore separates stage control from real power. Pharaoh's court has arranged the time and gathered the experts. Moses controls neither the crowd nor the first cast, yet he remains confident because his authority is not created by the event's choreography. He allows the opposing side its turn, knowing that God's sign will speak when the time for it arrives.

**Trust Makes Room for the Truth to Appear**

The instruction to cast whatever they wish is a practical expression of tawakkul, reliance upon God. Reliance is not passivity. Moses has spoken, presented the signs, and entered the public contest. Now he refuses to add manipulation to the task. He makes room for the result that God will bring.

That habit can guide anyone who must defend truth under pressure. Prepare honestly, state the claim clearly, and do not depend on controlling every audience reaction. A fair process may seem risky when the opponent has more resources, but truth is not strengthened by dishonest advantage. Moses' sentence gives the magicians freedom to perform and gives the crowd a clearer view of the difference between craft and sign.

He also refuses to let fear dictate the order of the encounter. The side that trusts God can wait without surrendering authority. Moses does not need to interrupt, expose, or intimidate the magicians. Their own display will be enough for the sign to answer, and the audience will be able to compare both acts without wondering whether Moses hid the opposing skill.

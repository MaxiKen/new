## Sūrah al-Naḥl 16:52

> **To Him belongs whatever is in the heavens and the earth, and to Him ˹alone˺ is the everlasting devotion. Will you then fear any other than Allah?**

**Expanded Commentary**

**To Him Belongs Whatever In Heavens And Earth**

Wa-lahu mā fī al-samāwāti wa-l-arḍi — and to Him belongs whatever is in heavens and earth. Lahu, to Him, belongs, possessive. Mā fī al-samāwāti wa-l-arḍi, whatever is in heavens and earth, includes all creation: angels, stars, humans, cattle, sea, mountains, etc. Al-Ṭabarī explains that everything in heavens and earth belongs to Allah as creation and possession; He created it, owns it, controls it. This is basis for tawḥīd: if everything belongs to Him, then no one else owns anything independently to be worshipped. The phrase appears many times in Qur'an to establish Allah's ownership and dominion, as in 2:284 and 10:68. In al-Naḥl, it comes after command not to take two gods and to be in awe of Me alone, to give reason: to Him belongs everything, so devotion should be to Him alone.

**Everlasting Devotion To Him Alone**

Wa-lahu al-dīnu wāṣiban — and to Him alone is everlasting devotion, religion, obedience. Dīn here means devotion, obedience, religion, submission, as well as recompense. Wāṣiban means everlasting, perpetual, constant, obligatory, from w-ṣ-b, to be constant, lasting, also to be sick, but here means perpetual. Al-Ṭabarī explains wāṣiban as always, perpetually, everlasting, meaning devotion to Allah is perpetual, not temporary, and also that Allah's religion is everlasting, not abrogated in its principle of tawḥīd. Al-Zamakhsharī says wāṣiban means constant, obligatory, everlasting, indicating that devotion to Allah is not for a time then stops, but is perpetual, and that Allah's dominion is perpetual. The phrase wa-lahu al-dīnu wāṣiban therefore means everlasting devotion belongs to Him alone, always, not only in hardship.

**Will You Fear Other Than Allah?**

A-fa-ghayra Allāhi tattaqūn — will you then fear other than Allah? Fa- is for consequence, then, so. Ghayra Allāhi, other than Allah. Tattaqūn from w-q-y, to fear, be mindful, guard, same root as taqwā. The question is rhetorical, expecting no, you should not fear other than Allah. Al-Ṭabarī says this questions why you fear idols, tyrants, or others besides Allah when everything belongs to Him and everlasting devotion is His. Fear should be of Allah alone, as previous verse said be in awe of Me alone. Al-Rāzī notes that fearing other than Allah is shirk in fear, which is part of worship. If you fear other than Allah as you fear Allah, or more, you have taken other as god. The question exposes irrationality: you fear idols that belong to Allah and cannot harm or benefit, while not fearing Allah who owns everything and whose devotion is everlasting.

**Why Ownership Implies Exclusive Fear**

If everything belongs to Allah, then nothing can harm or benefit without His permission, so fear should be of Him alone. Al-Qurṭubī explains that ownership includes power to dispose: owner can give, withhold, harm, benefit. If Allah owns everything, then only He can truly harm or benefit, so only He should be feared. Idols that are owned cannot harm or benefit independently. This is same argument as in 10:106-107: *Do not call besides Allah what cannot benefit nor harm you* (10:106) and *If Allah touches you with harm, none can remove it except Him* (10:107). The verse therefore links ownership to fear: because He owns all, fear Him alone.

**Connection To Blessings And Hardship**

Next verses will say whatever blessings you have are from Allah, then when hardship touches you, to Him alone you cry, then when He removes hardship, some associate others. The present verse about ownership and everlasting devotion and fearing other than Allah prepares for that: blessings are from Owner, hardship removal is from Owner, so fear and devotion should be to Owner alone. The sūrah's blessings catalogue showed that all blessings are from Allah; this verse says all things belong to Him, so devotion should be everlasting to Him. The question will you fear other than Allah anticipates that in hardship you cry to Him alone, showing you know He is Owner, but after hardship you associate others, which is inconsistent.

**Linguistic Note On Dīn And Wāṣib**

Mā fī al-samāwāti wa-l-arḍi, whatever in heavens and earth, general. Lahu, to Him. Dīn from d-y-n means religion, devotion, obedience, recompense, debt, from same root as dayn, debt, indicating that devotion is debt owed to Allah. Wāṣiban from w-ṣ-b means constant, perpetual, everlasting, from waṣaba, to be constant, to last. Some lexicons say wāṣib means obligatory, constant, from same root as waṣab, to be sick, but meaning here is perpetual. A-fa-ghayra Allāhi tattaqūn, will you fear other than Allah, with a-fa- for interrogative and consequence, ghayra for other than, tattaqūn from w-q-y, to fear, guard. The verse's structure — to Him belongs whatever in heavens and earth, to Him is everlasting devotion, will you fear other than Allah — moves from ownership to devotion to question, creating argument: because He owns all and devotion is His perpetually, you should not fear other than Him.

**How This Verse Teaches Tawḥīd In Fear**

Fear is part of worship, as hope and love are. To fear other than Allah as you fear Allah, or to fear other more than Allah, is to give to other what belongs to Allah alone. Al-Rāzī notes that tattaqūn here includes both fear of punishment and fear of displeasure, which should be directed to Allah alone. If you fear idol's displeasure, you have taken it as god. The verse teaches to fear Allah alone, which frees from fear of creation. When you fear One who owns all, you do not need to fear those owned. This is liberation: many fears collapse into one fear that is reverent awe, which brings peace, as angels fear Lord above and do what commanded, and are not too proud.

**A Lesson On Everlasting Devotion**

Everlasting devotion, al-dīnu wāṣiban, indicates that devotion to Allah is not seasonal, not only in hardship, but constant. In world, people often remember Allah in hardship and forget in ease, as next verses will describe. But devotion should be wāṣib, perpetual, everlasting. Al-Qurṭubī says wāṣiban also means that Allah's religion, tawḥīd, is everlasting, not abrogated, from first messenger to last. The core call worship Allah and shun ṭāghūt is everlasting, as verse 36 said messenger to every community. Therefore devotion to Him alone should be everlasting, not temporary. The question will you fear other than Allah then becomes: will you give temporary devotion to others while everlasting devotion belongs to Him alone?

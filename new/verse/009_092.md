## Sūrah al-Tawbah 9:92

> Nor ˹is there any blame on˺ those who came to you ˹O Prophet˺ for mounts, then when you said, “I can find no mounts for you,” they left with eyes overflowing with tears out of grief that they had nothing to contribute.

**Expanded Commentary**

**Desire to Serve Can Exist Without the Means**

The people described came to the Prophet seeking mounts so that they could join the campaign. They did not stay away because they preferred comfort. They wanted to go, but the means were unavailable. When they were told that no mounts could be found, they left grieving because they had nothing to contribute.

Their tears show the difference between inability and indifference. The hypocrites celebrated when they remained behind; these believers grieved when they were unable to go. Emotion alone does not prove sincerity, but here it agrees with the action: they came forward, asked for help, and accepted the answer rather than inventing an excuse. Allah knows the difference between a person prevented from service and a person who avoids it.

**The Mount Represents Access to Duty**

In a difficult expedition, a mount was not a luxury. It carried a person, supplies, and the ability to cross a harsh distance. Without transport, eagerness could not become participation. This detail teaches the community to understand the practical barriers that stand between intention and action.

A person may want to study but lack access to a teacher, want to work but lack transport, or want to help but lack the equipment. Leaders should not interpret every absence as laziness before asking what means are missing. Sometimes a small provision unlocks a large act of service. The community becomes generous when it looks for those barriers instead of judging only the visible result.

**Tears Can Be a Sign of Love, Not Weakness**

The tears of these believers are not condemned as emotional excess. They arise from grief that they cannot spend themselves for Allah. Their sadness reveals that the duty matters to them. A heart that loves obedience may feel pain when circumstances prevent it, even though Allah has removed the blame.

This does not mean that every tear should be displayed or that people should compete in emotional expression. The value lies in the inner direction, not in the performance. A servant can be sincere while remaining composed, and a person can cry without being sincere. Here the tears are part of a pattern of seeking, accepting, and grieving honestly.

**The Unable Person Can Still Belong**

The people who could not travel are not cast out of the community. The verse removes blame and preserves their dignity. Their inability does not make them spectators without value. They belong through faith, intention, and whatever support they can offer.

This is important for anyone living with limits. The community should not make a person feel that he must apologize for receiving an exemption Allah gave him. It should invite him into roles that match his strength. A person who cannot carry supplies may organize them, encourage those who go, or care for those left at home. Contribution changes shape without disappearing.

**A Sincere Intention Has Moral Weight**

The verse teaches that Allah judges more than completed movement. A person can receive the reward of an intention that circumstances prevented, while another person can perform the same outward act for a corrupt reason. The distinction does not make action unnecessary. It shows that action and intention belong together in the divine account.

The sincere servant therefore prepares when he can and accepts the limit when he cannot. He does not exaggerate his ability to earn praise, nor does he use inability as a reason to stop caring. He brings his grief to Allah and asks for another door of service. Intention becomes fruitful when it leads to whatever action remains possible.

**Communities Should Share Their Means**

The passage also makes a claim on those who possess transport and resources. If the campaign needs people and some are unable to reach it, those with means should help where they can. Shared equipment can turn private ability into communal strength. Hoarding the means while sincere people remain stranded is a failure of guardianship.

The believer therefore notices who is absent through lack rather than choice. He offers a ride, scholarship, tool, or introduction when such help is lawful and useful. If he cannot solve the whole problem, he should not despise the person who asks. Allah may accept the tears of the unable and the generosity of the one who opened the way.

The community's responsibility is visible in the fact that these people sought mounts. They did not quietly accept exclusion while wishing the problem would remain someone else's concern. They looked for a way to serve, and when no way existed they accepted the result with grief. Those who possess means should notice such people and remove barriers where possible. A community that ignores sincere eagerness because it is inconvenient loses talents Allah intended to benefit others. A community that helps without checking intention can also waste resources. The wise response combines generosity with knowledge and treats every person with dignity.

Their grief also teaches those who possess means not to assume that a person who stays behind prefers safety. Some people are excluded by a shortage they would gladly overcome. The strong should notice the difference and offer practical help instead of moral suspicion. A mount, scholarship, tool, or safe arrangement can transform a sincere intention into useful action. If help cannot be found, the person remains honored by Allah for the truth of his desire. The community should let him grieve without making him prove his sincerity through impossible promises. Mercy sees both the longing to serve and the limit that stopped it.

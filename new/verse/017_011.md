## Sūrah al-Isrāʾ 17:11

> **And humans ˹swiftly˺ pray for evil as they pray for good. For humankind is ever hasty.**

**Expanded Commentary**

**Praying For Evil As For Good**

Wa-yadʿu al-insānu bi-l-sharri duʿā'ahu bi-l-khayr — and human prays for evil as he prays for good, calls for evil as he calls for good. Yadʿu from d-ʿ-w, to call, pray, supplicate. Al-insān, human, generic, includes all. Bi-l-sharri, for evil, bad, from sh-r-r, evil. Duʿā'ahu bi-l-khayr, his supplication for good, prayer for good, from d-ʿ-w and kh-y-r, good. Al-Ṭabarī explains that human, out of haste, prays for evil as he prays for good: when angry, he prays against himself, children, wealth, with evil, like saying may Allah curse me, destroy me, etc., as he prays for good when in need. The phrase bi-l-sharri duʿā'ahu bi-l-khayr indicates that he prays for evil with same eagerness, swiftness, as he prays for good, without thinking. Al-Qurṭubī says this includes praying for punishment to be hastened, as Makkans said bring punishment if truthful, which is praying for evil as for good.

**Humankind Ever Hasty**

Wa-kāna al-insānu ʿajūlan — and human is ever hasty, hasty by nature. Kāna, was, is, indicates state. Al-insān, human. ʿAjūlan from ʿ-j-l, hasty, hastening, from same root as ʿajala, haste, and istiʿjāl, seeking haste, as previous chapter said command at hand so do not hasten it, and man was created of haste as 21:37 says. Al-Ṭabarī says human is hasty in seeking things before their time, praying for evil as for good out of haste, not reflecting. Al-Rāzī explains that haste is part of human makeup that needs training: to be patient, not hasty, to reflect before praying. The verse diagnoses human weakness that leads to praying for evil: haste.

**Why This Verse Follows Good News And Warning**

Verses 9-10 said Qur'an guides to most upright, gives good news to believers who do good of mighty reward, and warns those who do not believe in Hereafter of painful punishment. Verse 11 says human prays for evil as for good, is ever hasty. The connection is that after hearing good news and warning, human may out of haste pray for evil, like asking for punishment to be hastened, or praying against self when angry, which is evil. The sūrah therefore warns against haste that leads to praying for evil. Previous chapter opened with do not hasten command at hand, and said man was created of haste. This verse continues theme: human is ever hasty, so he prays for evil as for good. The remedy is patience, reflection, and following most upright guidance that teaches what is good to pray for.

**The Meaning Of Praying For Evil**

Praying for evil includes several forms: praying against self, family, wealth when angry; praying for punishment to be hastened out of mockery; praying for worldly gain that is actually evil for Hereafter; praying for evil for others out of envy. All are due to haste and lack of reflection. Al-Zamakhsharī notes that yadʿu bi-l-sharri indicates he calls for evil, supplicates for evil, thinking it is good or out of anger, without realizing it is evil. The phrase duʿā'ahu bi-l-khayr, his prayer for good, indicates that he prays for good with eagerness, but also prays for evil with same eagerness, without distinguishing. The cure is to be mindful, to reflect before praying, to follow Qur'an that guides to most upright, which teaches what is good to pray for.

**Connection To Haste And Command At Hand**

Previous chapter said command at hand so do not hasten it, and man was created of haste. This verse says human prays for evil as for good, is ever hasty. The connection is that haste leads to praying for evil, like asking for command, punishment, Hour to be hastened, which is evil because if hastened, no time to repent. The prohibition do not hasten command is to prevent praying for evil out of haste. The verse therefore explains why prohibition needed: because human is hasty and would pray for evil as for good. The remedy is patience, as verses about emigrants being patient and trusting Lord, and as final verses about patience with Allah's help.

**Linguistic Note On Duʿā' And ʿAjūl**

Yadʿu from d-ʿ-w, to call, pray, supplicate. Al-insān, human, from a-n-s, to be familiar, or n-s-y, to forget, indicating human forgets. Bi-l-sharri, for evil, from sh-r-r. Duʿā'ahu, his supplication, from d-ʿ-w. Bi-l-khayr, for good, from kh-y-r. Kāna al-insānu ʿajūlan, human is ever hasty, from ʿ-j-l, haste, with ʿajūl intensive form, ever hasty, very hasty. The verse's structure — human prays for evil as he prays for good, human is ever hasty — with two clauses, first describing action, second explaining nature, diagnoses weakness and invites training to be patient and to pray for good, not evil.

**How This Verse Teaches Patience And Mindful Supplication**

This verse teaches patience and mindful supplication: do not pray for evil out of haste, anger, or mockery; pray for good with reflection. Al-Rāzī says believer should be patient, not hasty, and should reflect before supplicating, asking whether what he asks is truly good for world and Hereafter. The sūrah's moral code that follows includes honoring parents, giving relatives due, not spending wastefully, etc., which are most upright ways that are good to pray for and do. The verse therefore calls to follow most upright guidance that teaches what is good, to be patient, not hasty, and to supplicate for good that benefits self and others in world and Hereafter.

**A Lesson On Human Nature**

Human is ever hasty — this is statement about human nature that is both description and warning. Haste leads to praying for evil as for good, to demanding punishment hastened, to making decisions without reflection. The Qur'an often mentions haste as weakness to be trained: *Man was created of haste, I will show you My signs, so do not hasten Me* (21:37) and *Man prays for evil as for good, man is ever hasty* (17:11). The remedy is patience, reflection, and trust, as emigrants were patient and trusted Lord. The believer who recognizes haste in self can train to be patient, to reflect, to follow most upright guidance, and to pray for good that is truly good.

## Sūrah al-Nisāʾ 4:153

> The People of the Book demand that you ˹O Prophet˺ bring down for them a revelation in writing from heaven. They demanded what is even greater than this from Moses, saying, “Make Allah visible to us!” So a thunderbolt struck them for their wrongdoing. Then they took the calf for worship after receiving clear signs. Still We forgave them for that ˹after their repentance˺ and gave Moses compelling proof.

**Expanded Commentary**

**The People of the Book Demand a Book From Heaven**

The verse opens *yasʾaluka ahlu al-kitābi an tunazzila ʿalayhim kitāban mina al-samāʾi* — the People of the Book ask you, demand of you, that you bring down upon them a book from the sky, a revelation in writing from heaven. *Ahlu al-kitābi* — People of the Book — was explained as Jews and Christians given Scripture before you — the same before you commanded to be mindful (4:131) — whose wishes are not the measure (4:123) and who were given a portion yet trade it for misguidance (4:44). *Yasʾaluka* — ask, demand — was read as challenging demand, as the sūrah has shown that they demand what is greater than this from Moses — *“make God visible”* — a demand that exceeds bringing a book from heaven. *An tunazzila kitāban mina al-samāʾ* — to bring down a book from heaven — was explained as requesting a written revelation descending as sheets, a physical book, as the Meccans demanded signs and as earlier they asked Moses to show God. The sūrah has shown that the Book was sent with truth to judge between people by what He shows (4:105) and that God bears witness to what He sent with His knowledge and angels bear witness (4:166); to demand a book from heaven as condition is to condition belief on spectacle. Early reports noted this demand followed the Prophet’s recitation of comprehensive faith (4:152): they said, if you are among messengers, bring a book as Moses brought tablets, otherwise we make distinction as before (4:150) — the sūrah exposes demand as continuation of distinguishing.

**They Demanded Greater Than That From Moses — Show Us God Visible**

The reminder *fa qad saʾalū Mūsā akbara min dhālika fa qālū arinā Allāha jahratan* — yet they demanded of Moses greater than that, saying show us God plainly, visibly, openly — was explained with *akbara min dhālika* — greater than that — the book from heaven. *Arinā Allāha jahratan* — show us God manifest, visibly, openly — was explained as demand for theophany, to see the Lord with eyes, as the sūrah elsewhere recounts *“they said, O Moses, we will not believe until we see God plainly, so the thunderbolt seized them”* (2:55). *Jahratan* — openly, plainly, manifestly — was read as that they demanded vision of the Ownership declared *to Him belongs whatever is in heavens and earth and He is Fully Aware* (4:126) — to see the Fully Aware plainly before believing the revealed Book. Elsewhere the Book notes that God suffices as Witness (4:166) and that disbelievers will be asked *what were you doing?* at soul-taking (4:97); to demand visible God is to refuse the witness of Book and angels. Classical notes added that Moses’ people demanded this after being delivered through sea and given manna, showing that spectacle does not produce faith where distinction remains.

**So a Thunderbolt Struck Them — Then They Took the Calf After Clear Signs**

The consequence *fa akhadhat-humu al-ṣāʿiqatu bi ẓulmihim* — so the thunderbolt seized them, struck them, for their wrongdoing — was explained with *ṣāʿiqah* — thunderbolt, striking — the same punishment that seized others who demanded signs with wrongdoing. *Bi ẓulmihim* — for their wrong, injustice — was read as that demand for visible God was injustice, as associating is far straying (4:116) and defying the Messenger after clarity is left to what he chose (4:115). *Thumma ittakhadhū al-ʿijla min baʿdi mā jāʾathumu al-bayyinātu* — then they took the calf for worship after clear signs came to them — was explained with *ʿijl* — calf — the golden calf worshipped while Moses was on the Mount, after receiving *bayyināt* — clear signs, evidences — the staff, the sea parted, the Mount raised over them (4:154). To take the calf for worship after clear signs was read as the chapter’s illustration of distinguishing: they demanded visible God and, when not given, took a visible calf — the worst substitution: from demanding the Most High visible to worshipping the lowest. Elsewhere the chapter notes they invoke female gods besides Him as Satan (4:117) and slit ears and alter creation (4:119); calf worship is the same — altering creation of respected animal into object of worship, then worshipping it.

**Still We Forgave Them and Gave Moses Compelling Proof**

The mercy *fa ʿafawnā ʿan dhālika wa ātaynā Mūsā sulṭānan mubīnā* — still We pardoned them for that, after their repentance, and We gave Moses clear, compelling, manifest authority, proof — was explained with *ʿafawnā* — We pardoned, effaced — the same root *ʿAfuww* — Ever-Pardoning (4:99, 4:149) — who pardons though Most Capable (4:149). The chapter has already noted that whoever wrongs themselves then seeks forgiveness finds Him All-Forgiving (4:110) and that the helpless hoped to be pardoned (4:99); here the calf-takers were forgiven after repentance where *“kill yourselves”* after calf was commanded (2:54). *Sulṭānan mubīnā* — clear warrant, compelling proof — was explained as the manifest authority Moses was given: tablets, the Mount raised, the *bayyināt* — clear signs — the sūrah will enumerate as Mount raised for covenant, *“enter the gate with humility, do not break Sabbath, firm covenant”* (4:154). The phrase *sulṭān mubīn* appears where hostile turners are given clear warrant over them (4:91) and where giving God clear warrant against yourselves is warned (4:144); here Moses’ *sulṭān mubīn* is the proof against those who demanded greater than a book.

**A Verse That Frames Demand With Forgiveness**

Placed after comprehensive belief rewarded with forgiveness and mercy (4:152) versus distinction as true disbelief with humiliating preparation (4:151), this verse frames demand for a book from heaven with history of greater demand — visible God — thunderbolt, calf after clear signs, yet pardon and compelling proof. The sūrah has shown that the Book was sent with truth to judge between people and that God bears witness with His knowledge (4:166); to demand a book from heaven as condition is to condition acceptance of that witness on another witness. The sūrah that warns *“do not go to extremes regarding your faith, say nothing about God except truth, the Messiah was but messenger, Word through Mary and spirit from Him, do not say Trinity”* (4:171) here shows that extremes begin as demands for spectacle and end as calf worship — the visible made god when the Invisible is demanded visible. For the weak this is opening: where the calf after clear signs was forgiven after repentance, the sincere repentant with believers (4:146) is within that forgiveness, and Moses’ *sulṭān mubīn* is the proof that distinguishes demand from faith.

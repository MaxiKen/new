## Sūrah al-Nisāʾ 4:153

> The People of the Book demand that you ˹O Prophet˺ bring down for them a revelation in writing from heaven. They demanded what is even greater than this from Moses, saying, “Make Allah visible to us!” So a thunderbolt struck them for their wrongdoing. Then they took the calf for worship after receiving clear signs. Still We forgave them for that ˹after their repentance˺ and gave Moses compelling proof.

### Expanded Commentary

**Demanding signs and repeating old errors**

The People of the Book ask the Prophet for a written revelation from heaven, and the verse recalls that the Israelites demanded from Moses an even greater sign: to see Allah openly. The issue is not that every request for evidence is illegitimate. It is that demands can become a way to postpone obedience after sufficient signs have been given.

The calf followed clear signs, proving that spectacular evidence does not cure a heart attached to an idol. A person can ask for a miracle and then build an alternative god the next day. The verse therefore directs attention from endless demands to the moral response already possible.

The Mount raised over them, the covenant, and the compelling proof given to Moses show that revelation comes with authority and mercy. Allah forgave after repentance, but forgiveness did not make the rebellion wise. The narrative is a mirror for every community that turns religious questioning into a shield against submission.

**Evidence and the will to obey**

Not every request for evidence is bad faith, but a person should notice when new demands appear immediately after an answer to the old one. Signs do not help a heart determined to keep an idol. The wise response is to study the evidence already provided and ask what obedience it requires.

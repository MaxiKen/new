## Sūrah al-Naḥl 16:49

> **And to Allah ˹alone˺ bows down ˹in submission˺ whatever is in the heavens and whatever is on the earth of living creatures, as do the angels—who are not too proud ˹to do so˺.**

**Expanded Commentary**

**Universal Prostration To Allah**

Wa-li-Allāhi yasjudu mā fī al-samāwāti wa-mā fī al-arḍi min dābbatin wa-l-malā'ikatu — and to Allah bows down whatever is in heavens and whatever on earth of living creatures, and angels. Yasjudu from s-j-d means to prostrate, bow down, submit. Mā fī al-samāwāti wa-mā fī al-arḍi, whatever is in heavens and earth, includes all creation. Min dābbatin, of living creatures, from d-b-b, to crawl, creep, move, meaning all animals that move on earth, including humans, animals, insects. Wa-l-malā'ikatu, and angels, mentioned separately though they are in heavens, to emphasize their prostration despite high rank. Al-Ṭabarī explains that everything prostrates to Allah, willingly or unwillingly, as 13:15 says *To Allah prostrates whoever is in heavens and earth willingly or unwillingly* (13:15). Living creatures prostrate by subjection to Allah's command, by following natural laws, by being under His control. Angels prostrate willingly, constantly, without pride.

**Angels Not Too Proud**

Wa-hum lā yastakbirūn — while they are not too proud, not arrogant. Yastakbirūn from k-b-r, to be arrogant, as in previous verses about arrogant. Angels are not too proud to prostrate to Allah, despite being near to Allah, pure, powerful. Al-Qurṭubī says this is to shame humans who are too proud to prostrate, while angels who are greater in creation are not too proud. The phrase lā yastakbirūn appears in description of angels in 21:19: *Those near Him are not too proud to worship Him* (21:19). Angels' lack of pride is model for humans: if angels who have no sin are not too proud to bow, how can humans who have sins be too proud? The verse therefore contrasts angels' humility with humans' arrogance that leads to Hell as evil home.

**Why Living Creatures Are Mentioned**

Min dābbatin, of living creatures, emphasizes that prostration includes moving, living beings, not only inanimate shadows as previous verse. Dābbah includes all that moves on earth: humans, animals, insects, as 11:6 says *There is no living creature on earth but its provision is upon Allah* (11:6). Their prostration is by subjection to Allah's command in their creation, movement, life, death. Al-Rāzī explains that dābbah prostrates by following instincts and laws that Allah placed, like bee inspired to make homes in mountains as verse 68 will say, and by being under Allah's control. Even if they do not prostrate like humans with forehead on ground, they prostrate by submission to divine decree. The verse invites to see all moving creatures as submitting, so human who moves should also submit.

**The Connection To Shadows**

Verse 48 said shadows incline right and left prostrating to Allah in humility. Verse 49 says whatever is in heavens and earth of living creatures and angels bow to Allah, not too proud. The progression is from non-living shadows to living creatures to angels, showing universal prostration across all categories: inanimate, animate, spiritual. Shadows prostrate, living creatures prostrate, angels prostrate. Who remains who does not prostrate? Only arrogant humans and jinn who refuse out of pride. The sūrah thus isolates arrogant as exception to universal submission, making their refusal appear unnatural and foolish. All creation bows; why do you not?

**A Verse That Prepares For Command Not To Take Two Gods**

Verse 51 will say *Do not take two gods, there is only One God, so be in awe of Me alone*. The universal prostration to Allah alone prepares for that command: if everything prostrates to Allah alone, then taking two gods contradicts universal prostration. The verse also prepares for verse 52: *To Him belongs whatever is in heavens and earth and to Him alone is everlasting devotion*. If everything belongs to Him and prostrates to Him, then devotion should be to Him alone. The sūrah's argument is consistent: creation, subjection, prostration all point to tawḥīd.

**Linguistic Note On Dābbah And Istikbār**

Dābbah from d-b-b means to crawl, move slowly, used for any living creature that moves on earth, including humans in some usages. Yasjudu from s-j-d means to prostrate, bow, submit. Wa-l-malā'ikatu, and angels, from m-l-k, angel. Lā yastakbirūn, not too proud, from k-b-r, to be great, form X meaning to deem self great, be arrogant. The verse's structure — and to Allah bows whatever in heavens and earth of living creatures and angels, while they are not too proud — with universal quantifier mā and specific mention of angels and negation of pride, emphasizes that prostration is universal and humility is characteristic of those who prostrate, especially angels who are high yet not proud.

**How This Verse Teaches Humility**

The verse teaches humility by showing that even angels, who are near Allah and pure, are not too proud to bow. If angels bow, human should bow. The phrase wa-hum lā yastakbirūn, they are not too proud, is both description of angels and indirect criticism of humans who are too proud, as verses 22-23, 29 described arrogant. The remedy for arrogance is to join universal prostration: bow to Allah alone, as everything else does. The verse therefore invites to be part of universal submission rather than exception that will be humiliated on Day as verse 27 said.


**A Lesson That Connects To Gratitude And Reflection**

This verse, like all verses in Sūrat al-Niʿam, ultimately calls to gratitude and reflection. Whether it describes emigration, patience, trust, human messengers, clear proofs, earth swallowing, or angels fearing, the purpose is to make reader recognize blessings and respond with thanks and reflection. Al-Qurṭubī notes that every warning in this sūrah is also a blessing because it warns before punishment, giving chance to change. Every promise is also a blessing because it encourages good. The sūrah weaves warning and promise, blessing and accountability, to cultivate heart that is mindful, humble, grateful. The reader who reflects on this verse with that mindset will find both knowledge and motivation to be among those who say goodness when asked what Lord revealed, and who are greeted with peace at death and enter Gardens.

**How This Verse Fits The Sūrah's Argument**

The sūrah moves from blessings to argument against shirk to warnings to examples to guidance. This verse fits in that flow by showing either reward for patience and trust, or principle of human messengers, or modes of punishment, or universal submission. Each verse adds layer to argument that Allah alone creates, sustains, knows, and deserves worship. The verse's placement after previous warnings and before next commands is deliberate: it shows consequence of previous attitude and prepares for next command. The reader is invited to see connection and to respond with gratitude, patience, trust, and asking knowledgeable, rather than with denial, arrogance, and plotting.

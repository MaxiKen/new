## Sūrah al-Nisāʾ 4:109

> Here you are! You ˹believers˺ are advocating for them in this life, but who will ˹dare to˺ advocate for them before Allah on the Day of Judgment? Or who will come to their defence?

**Expanded Commentary**

**Here You Are — Advocating for Them in This Life**

The verse opens *hā antum hāʾulāʾi jādal­tum ʿanhum fī al-ḥayāti al-dunyā* — Here you are, you believers are, you are these who have disputed for them, advocated for them, in the life of this world. *Hā antum* — behold you, here you are — was explained as summons and gentle rebuke, as the sūrah summons witnesses for justice though against yourselves (4:135). *Jādaltum ʿanhum* — you disputed for them, advocated — repeats the same *jādala/jādal[tum]* as *“do not advocate for those who wrong themselves”* (4:107) and *“do not be advocate for the deceitful”* (4:105), showing the community indeed did dispute after the theft. *Fī al-ḥayāti al-dunyā* — in the nearer life — was read as the place where advocacy can seem to work because judges must weigh oaths and eloquence — *“some of you may be more eloquent”* (Bukhārī 2680) — and where kinship pulls. The sūrah has shown that enjoyment of this world is little and the Hereafter better for the mindful (4:77), that fleeting gain is sought by denying peace (4:94) and that most secret talks have no good except charity, kindness, reconciliation (4:114); disputing for the deceitful in this life is another fleeting gain — a kinsman saved from shame — sought where speaking truth would have cost shame but gained the Book’s favour. The phrase was also read as the sūrah’s reminder that this life is the only court where human advocacy can affect verdict before divine.

**But Who Will Advocate for Them Before God**

The question *fa man yujādilu Allāha ʿanhum yawma al-qiyāmati am man yakūnu ʿalayhim wakīlā* — but who will dispute, advocate for them before God on Resurrection Day? Or who will be their defender, trustee, agent over them — was explained as two challenges. *Man yujādilu Allāha ʿanhum* — who will dispute God on their behalf — was read as impossible: there is no disputant against God’s knowledge, for He is Fully Aware of what they do (4:108) and Encompassing of it, and He records night plotting while with them at what displeases Him. *Yawma al-qiyāmati* — on the Day of Standing — was explained as the Day about which there is no doubt where He will gather you all (4:87) and where each will be judged by the Book sent in truth (4:105). *Man yakūnu ʿalayhim wakīlā* — who will be their *wakīl*, defender, trustee, agent — was read as the same *wakīl* the sūrah commanded to trust: *“God is sufficient as Trustee of Affairs”* (4:81), *“God is sufficient as Trustee”* (4:132). To ask who will be their *wakīl* on that Day is to answer: no disputant can argue against the Encompassing, and no *wakīl* can protect against the Sufficient *Wakīl* who already knows. The verse was linked to *“on that Day those who denied and disobeyed the Messenger will wish they were dust”* (4:42), for advocacy will have no ground where every hidden night saying is present.

**A Verse That Measures This Life’s Advocacy by the Next**

The comparison — *fī al-ḥayāti al-dunyā* versus *yawma al-qiyāma* — was read as the sūrah’s measurement of advocacy. In this life to dispute for the self-wrongers may succeed where a story is coached, where armour is said to be with a Jew, where oaths are divided; on the Day, the Judge is the One who was with them while they plotted by night (4:108) and who will not be disputed because He was never absent. The sūrah has said that God will gather all together — about which there is no doubt (4:87) — and that those who were oppressed and wronged themselves will be asked *“what were you doing?”* at soul-taking (4:97); advocacy that might deflect a human question will not deflect that. The sūrah has also shown that seeking forgiveness is still open — *“whoever wrongs themselves then seeks forgiveness finds Him”* (4:110) — which is the only advocacy that works on the Day: not a pleader for deceit, but a sinner’s own seeking that meets the All-Forgiving. The question therefore reframes advocacy: the advocate’s eloquence in this life is real, but the forum is small; the forum that matters has no *khaṣīm* for deceit and no *wakīl* besides Him who does not love the deceitful sinner (4:107).

**Why the Question Is Double — Disputant and Defender**

The two questions — who will dispute, who will be defender — were explained as covering both roles advocates play: the voice that argues and the guardian who manages affairs after argument. *Yujādil* covers speech — to dispute, to contend, to answer charges; *wakīl* covers standing — to be trustee, to defend, to take responsibility. The sūrah has distinguished speech — *mā lā yarḍā mina al-qawli* — night speech displeasing to Him (4:108) — from act — theft, moving goods, throwing guilt on the innocent (4:112). Both need a defender on the Day; neither will have one for deceit that persists. The double question also mirrors the sūrah’s double prohibition — *do not be advocate for deceitful* (4:105) and *do not advocate for self-wrongers* (4:107) — showing that neither prohibition is light, because the Day’s lack of advocate is heavier. Where the sūrah elsewhere promises honourable company for the obedient (4:69) and humiliating torment prepared for disbelievers where prayer was guarded (4:102), here the company on the Day for the deceitful’s advocate is solitude: no pleader, no trustee.

**A Short Verse That Anchors Justice Beyond Eloquence**

Though only *“you advocate for them in this life, who will advocate before God or be defender on Resurrection Day?”* the verse anchors justice beyond eloquence. The sūrah that warns not to take disbelievers as allies for honour (4:139), that says hypocrites deceive God but He outwits them when they stand for prayer to be seen (4:142), and that declares *“do not let your desires cause you to deviate from justice”* (4:135), here pulls the advocate’s desire — loyalty to kin — into daylight: your dispute works here, it will not there, so let your dispute here be for what would still work there — charity, kindness, reconciliation (4:114), or seeking forgiveness that will find Him All-Forgiving, Most Merciful (4:110). For the weak for whom the sūrah lightens burdens, the verse relieves a burden: you need not be your kinsman’s *khaṣīm* in this life, because you cannot be his *wakīl* in the next, and the only burden removed from him now is the one he removes himself by seeking.

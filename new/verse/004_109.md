## Sūrah al-Nisāʾ 4:109

> Here you are! You ˹believers˺ are advocating for them in this life, but who will ˹dare to˺ advocate for them before Allah on the Day of Judgment? Or who will come to their defence?

### Expanded Commentary

**Temporary advocacy and permanent judgment**

People may defend wrongdoers in this life through family loyalty, money, rhetoric, or legal skill. But the verse asks who will defend them before Allah on the Day of Judgment. The question exposes the limits of worldly advocacy: it can affect a human verdict but cannot erase a fact from divine knowledge.

**Fair defence is not moral endorsement**

The verse does not prohibit representing the accused or ensuring a fair process. It prohibits advocacy that knowingly protects deceit. A just advocate can insist that the prosecution prove its case while refusing to claim that a false story is true. This distinction protects both defendants and truth.

The final court will not be swayed by the same alliances that influence the present. The wise person seeks forgiveness, returns rights, and refuses to rely on social protection as though it were salvation.

**The limits of worldly defence**

A powerful advocate can obtain a favourable judgment, but cannot give the wrongdoer a clean record before God. This should make the influential cautious about lending their name to a false cause and make the vulnerable hopeful that a failed earthly case is not the last word.

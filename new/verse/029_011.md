## Sūrah al-ʿAnkabūt 29:11

> Allah will certainly distinguish between those who have ˹sure˺ faith and the hypocrites.

**Expanded Commentary**

**The Same Audit, a New Pair of Names**

The sūrah has already announced that God will separate the truthful from the liars (29:3). This sentence runs the same procedure again with a different pair of terms: those who have sure faith and the hypocrites. The repetition is a form of tightening. The first statement sorted people by whether their claim was backed; this one sorts them by whether their conviction was firm. Between the two sits the man described in the previous verse, whose loyalty rose and fell with the news, and the verse is best read as his verdict. A reader who thought the earlier warning was addressed to people entirely outside the community now sees the audit conducted inside it, with a name attached to the failing. What the sentence promises is not punishment in the first place but clarity: the distinguishing will be done, and it will be done by God, whose knowledge of the interior cannot be contested.

**The Burrow With More Than One Door**

The Arabic noun for hypocrisy carries an image from the animal world. Lexicographers and commentators connect it to the *nāfiqāʾ*, the burrow a jerboa digs with several concealed exits, so that a hunter entering by one hole finds his quarry gone by another. Al-Qurṭubī uses exactly this likeness to explain the name, and the fit is exact. A hypocrite is a man with more than one way out. He moves between two positions with passages behind him, never committing to a room he cannot leave. The root's basic sense of tunneling, of moving underground rather than in the open, supplies the rest of the picture. The word was not invented as an insult; it was borrowed from a description of how certain creatures survive, and the sūrah's use of it here charges the reader to notice that survival is precisely what the person concerned is pursuing. A man who never intends to stay has no reason to defend the house he is visiting, and the Book's later warnings about the type are variations on that single observation.

**A Word That Names an Attitude Before It Names a Faction**

Commentators observe that this is probably the earliest appearance of the term in the order of revelation, and that organized hypocrisy only became a feature of the community after the migration to Madinah, when the Book devotes an entire sūrah to the type. In Makkah there was no party to belong to, no faction to hide inside, and the two camps stood openly opposed. What the word names at this stage is a personal condition rather than a social club: the wavering man, half in and half out, unwilling to pay the full price and unwilling to leave the market. The Book's later portrait of the type adds detail but changes nothing essential. Whether a person hides inside a party or inside himself, the trouble is the same — a claim maintained without the commitment that would make it true.

**Faith That Does Not Doubt**

The phrase rendered as "sure faith" fixes the opposite condition with precision. The Book defines the true believers as those who believe in God and His Messenger and then never doubt, expending their wealth and their lives in the cause of God, and calls them the ones true in faith (49:15). Two elements are joined in that definition and in this verse: the interior absence of hesitation and the exterior expenditure that follows from it. Certainty in the Book is not a feeling of confidence; it is a settled state that makes a person spendable. The man in the previous verse lacked exactly that, and his condition under pressure proved the absence. The sentence here therefore separates two ways of handling the same claim: one man's belief is load-bearing and another man's is ornamental, and the difference becomes public the first time the structure takes weight.

**The Vocabulary the Book Reserves for This Condition**

The Book's later chapter on the same subject supplies the name that this verse's audience would have recognized the type by, and it opens with the charged word attached to it (63:1). The term's use in the Makkan period, before that chapter existed, is instructive: the Book is describing a condition rather than a party. Sūrah al-Ḥujurāt distinguishes the two registers in a single exchange, telling a group of nomads who claimed faith that they had not believed but had submitted, since faith had not yet entered their hearts (49:14). The distinction between claiming and holding is the one this verse draws.

**Why the Sentence Ends in Distinction, Not Fire**

The verse stops short of the punishment it clearly implies, and the restraint is instructive. What God promises here is the sorting itself — the exposure of the two groups as what they are. That is a mercy for the community, which needs to know whom to trust in matters of survival, and it is a mercy for the waverer, who is given the chance to see himself before the final accounting closes the file. Sūrah al-Nisāʾ states the destination of the unrepentant type and then immediately opens a door: the hypocrites are in the lowest depths of the Fire, except those who repent, mend their ways, hold fast to God, and make their devotion sincere, who will be with the believers (4:145–146). The Book's habit of attaching an exit to every verdict explains why this sentence issues a distinction and leaves the sentence of the sort closed.

**The Sūrah That Dedicates Itself to the Type**

One chapter of the Book takes its name from this failing and opens with the portrait complete: when the hypocrites come to the Prophet, they bear witness that he is the Messenger of God, and God, who knows the witness to be true of the man they address, bears witness that they are liars (63:1). A few lines later the cause is given — they believed, then abandoned faith, and their hearts were sealed so that they could not understand (63:3). Set beside the present verse, the later chapter supplies the anatomy of what is being distinguished here. The exterior form had never been the problem; the Book is full of people saying the words. What is being tested, in Makkah and in Madinah alike, is the part of a person that no exit from a burrow can protect. The community is promised a proof here rather than a purge, and the proof is conducted by the One whose knowledge of the interior no defense can obscure.

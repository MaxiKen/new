## Sūrah al-Nisāʾ 4:70

> This is Allah’s favour, and Allah fully knows ˹who deserves it˺.

**Expanded Commentary**

**This Is Allah’s Favour**

The verse states *dhālika al-faḍlu min Allāh* — this is Allah’s favour — referring to the promise that whoever obeys God and the Messenger will be with the blessed: prophets, people of truth, martyrs, righteous, what honourable company (4:69). *Faḍl* — bounty, surplus, favour beyond exact recompense — was read as the surplus by which a servant’s obedience places him among ranks he could not claim by deed alone, the same surplus the sūrah told the envious to stop craving in others’ hands and to ask from God (4:32), and the same bounty the stingy conceal (4:37) yet God multiplies an atom’s weight many times over and adds a great reward from grace (4:40). To call that companionship *faḍl* is to say it is not a wage for hours; it is favour that crowns obedience. The sūrah has shown favour as *“We have given the descendants of Abraham Book and wisdom and great authority”* (4:54) — not earned by the recipient’s claim but given — and as *“great reward by Our grace”* (4:67) for doing what was advised. Here favour is the fellowship that guidance to the straight path (4:68) yields. Obedience walks the path; favour admits to the company. The pronoun *dhālika* — this, that — points back to the entire promise, making the whole companionship, not only a part, the favour.

**And Allah Fully Knows Who Deserves It**

The clause *wa kafā bi Allāhi ʿalīmā* — and Allah fully knows who deserves it — was read as both reassurance and warning. *ʿAlīm* — All-Knowing — was explained as knowing the deed, the intention, and the capacity behind obedience, the same knowledge that never wrongs even by an atom’s weight (4:40) and that is perfect knowledge of them (4:39). To say He knows who deserves the favour is to say the distribution is not arbitrary and not based on claim. The sūrah has exposed those who falsely elevate themselves — *“it is Allah who elevates whomever He wills, and none will be wronged even by a thread”* (4:49) — and those who fabricate lies against Him as blatant sin (4:50), and those who believe in *jibt* and declare disbelievers better guided (4:51). Each claims a status the verse denies; the true elevation to the honourable company is by *faḍl* distributed with perfect knowledge. Early authorities linked *kafā bi Allāhi ʿalīmā* — sufficient is Allah as Knower — to the sūrah’s refrain of sufficiency: sufficient as Guardian and Helper (4:45), sufficient as Witness (4:79, 4:166), sufficient as Trustee (4:81). Here sufficiency is of knowledge: no other auditor is needed to sort who walks the straight path with firmness (4:66) and who turns away stubbornly (4:61).

**Favour That Rests on Knowledge, Not on Claim**

The pairing of *faḍl* and *ʿalīm* corrects two errors at once. If favour were without knowledge, it would be random; if knowledge were without favour, the weak would despair. The sūrah has held both since it said humankind was created weak and therefore burdens were lightened (4:28), and that God wills to make clear and turn toward you (4:26–27). Favour is given where knowledge sees obedience — not self-elevation — and weakness met with doing what was advised. The sūrah has also said *“We would have granted a great reward by Our grace and guided to the straight path”* (4:67–68) had they obeyed; to call that reward and guidance *faḍl* from God is to remind that the straight path itself is a gift, not only the company at its end. The hypocrites who sought false judges while claiming belief (4:60) claimed a favour — alliance and ease — that knowledge would not ratify. The believers who accept the Prophet ﷺ as judge without resistance and submit wholeheartedly (4:65) receive favour that knowledge does ratify. The verse therefore draws a line between favour claimed and favour given, between *yuzakkūna anfusahum* — they declare themselves pure — and *yuzakkī man yashāʾ* — God purifies whomever He wills.

**A Short Verse That Holds the Sūrah’s Economy**

Placed where it is, this short verse holds the sūrah’s economy of favour together. The economy was stated as: do not crave what God gave some over others; ask Him for bounty (4:32) — because the distributor is not the envied person but the All-Knowing. It was shown as: those who spend to be seen without belief take Satan as associate — an evil companion (4:38) — while those who believe and donate from what was provided would have harmed no one and would have found perfect knowledge remembering it (4:39). It was measured as: not an atom wronged, but multiplied many times, with a great reward from grace (4:40). To call the companionship of the blessed *faḍl min Allāh* and to add *Allah fully knows who deserves it* is to say that entire economy ends here: obedience places with the blessed by favour, and the favour is distributed by perfect knowledge that misses no thread and no atom. The sūrah has warned that the evildoers who fight for the Devil have weak schemes (4:76) and that the hypocrites’ disaster comes because of what their hands put forward (4:62); the favour of honourable company is the opposite destiny for hands that put forward referral, fairness, and submission. Sufficiency as Knower guarantees both.

**The Invitation Inside the Statement**

The statement *this is God’s favour* is also invitation. The sūrah does not list the deeds that earn the company as unattainable acts reserved for prophets; it lists them as the advised acts that would have been better and more reassuring — return trusts, judge fairly, refer disputes, accept judgment (4:66) — and as obedience to God and the Messenger as the sūrah has detailed them. To call the result favour is to tell the weak that the distance between his obedience and the prophet’s company is bridged not by his own rank but by God’s giving. That giving is known, witnessed, and measured with the same precision that guards the orphan’s sixth and the wife’s eighth, so that the weak who fears his atom is too small hears *Allah fully knows* as reassurance: his atom is known and will be favoured. The verse therefore follows the exclamation *what honourable company* (4:69) with the quiet reason company is honourable: it is from God, and it is known. The community that was told to take precautions and go forth (4:71) can thus go forth not with the boast that it has taken favour, but with the trust that favour will be given by the One who knows who deserves it, and that the knowledge that suffices is also the knowledge that elevates without wrong by even a thread.

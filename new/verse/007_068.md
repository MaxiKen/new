## Sūrah al-Aʿrāf 7:68

> conveying to you my Lord’s messages. And I am your sincere advisor.

**Expanded Commentary**

**The Delivery of the Message**

The verse states that the messenger conveys the messages of his Lord, and the description repeats the account of Noah's work in the same sūrah (7:62). The Book presents the delivery of the revelation as the substance of the prophetic office, and the sūrah of the Family of Imran states that the messenger's duty is only to convey (3:20), while the sūrah of the Table repeats the same limit twice. The verse in front of us places the delivery before the counsel, and the order states which of the two is the foundation.

The messages in the plural are the successive communications that the messenger received, and the sūrah's narratives present the prophets as transmitters rather than as authors. The Book's accusations against those who conceal the message belong to the same understanding, and the sūrah of the Cow warns the people of the earlier books against covering what has been revealed. The verse in front of us states the work that the messenger performs among a people who have called him a liar.

**The Sincere Adviser**

The second half of the verse describes the messenger as a sincere adviser, and the phrase repeats the term that the sūrah has used for Noah's counsel (7:62) and will use again for Ṣāliḥ (7:79) and Shuʿayb (7:93). The Arabic term for the adviser describes one who wishes the good of the person advised without any mixture of self-interest, and the word for sincerity in front of us adds the sense of purity and unmixed intention. The two terms together state a counsel that seeks nothing from the one who receives it.

The Book's treatment of counsel among the believers appears in its instructions to the community, and the sūrah of the Declining Day states that the people who are not at a loss are those who urge one another to the truth and to perseverance (103:3). The verse in front of us supplies the pattern of that practice at the level of a messenger addressing a whole community, and the sūrah's narratives repeat the pattern with each people.

**Why the Counsel Is Stated After the Charge**

The messenger states his sincerity immediately after his people have accused him of folly and falsehood, and the juxtaposition gives the verse its force. The charge denied his honesty; the statement in front of us asserts it as the purpose of his work. The sūrah's accounts of the communities repeatedly place the false accusation next to the messenger's true description of himself, and the arrangement lets the reader see who is speaking the truth.

The Book's descriptions of the messengers' patience under abuse appear in its accounts of the prophets who continued their warning after being dismissed, and the sūrah of Hūd reports Hūd continuing his appeal to the people after the leaders had rejected him. This verse states the motive that sustained the continuation, and the narrative then turns to the reminder that the messenger gives his people.

**The Counsel and the Community's Answer**

The Book's accounts of the communities show the counsel being refused in the same terms that the leaders used, and the sūrah's record of the destruction states the outcome of the refusal. The sūrah of the Heights will report the messenger turning away from his people at the end of the account with the statement that he conveyed the message and gave the counsel and that the people did not like advisers (7:79; 7:93). The verse here supplies the middle of the sequence, and the closing statement of the messenger comes at the end of the account.

The rejection in front of us is therefore a rejection of the counsel as well as of the message, and the Book treats the two as a single offer. The messenger's work consisted of the warning and the well-wishing that accompanied it, and the community's refusal covered both. The present verse states the full extent of what was offered before the sūrah reports the exchange that follows.

**The Sincerity the Book Admires**

The Book's use of sincerity as a virtue appears in its descriptions of the worship that is offered to God alone, and the sūrah of the Clear Proof describes the command given to the people of the earlier books as the worship of God with sincere devotion. The term in front of us applies the same quality to the counsel that is offered to people, and the verse therefore joins the two directions of the sincere life: toward God and toward one's community.

The sūrah's contemporaries knew the practice of advice in their tribal affairs, and the messenger's claim in front of us uses a term they understood. The Book's later instructions to the community adopt the practice for the religion, and the sūrah of the Declining Day makes the mutual urging of truth and patience the mark of the saved. The verse under discussion supplies the model in the prophetic mission.

**Where the Verse Sits**

The verse stands sixty-eighth in the chapter, and it completes the messenger's reply to the leaders of ʿĀd. The verse that follows reports his reminder to the people of the favours they had received, and the account then proceeds through the challenge of the leaders to the destruction of the community (7:70–72). Here the verse states the work that the messenger performed before the argument reached its end.

The reader who heard the leaders charge the messenger with folly and falsehood now hears him describe his own work. He delivers the messages and he counsels sincerely, and the people's answer to both is recorded in the verses that follow; and the sūrah's next verse states the reminder with which he turned from the leaders to the community.

**The Transition to the Next Verse**

The next verse asks the people whether they find it astonishing that a reminder should come to them from their Lord through a man of their own, and it reminds them that God made them successors after the people of Noah and increased them in stature, closing with the command to remember the favours of God so that they may succeed (7:69). The statement of the messenger's work in front of us is followed by the reminder that the work contained, and the account then reports the leaders' challenge.

The two verses together state the messenger's approach to his people. He conveys and counsels, and then he reminds them of what they have been given; and the sūrah's following verses state the demand that the leaders made and the destruction that answered their refusal.

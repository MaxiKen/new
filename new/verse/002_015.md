## Sūrah al-Baqarah 2:15

> Allah will throw their mockery back at them, leaving them to continue wandering blindly in their defiance.

**Expanded Commentary**

**Mockery Returned With the Same Word**

The hypocrites had said, *“We were only mocking”* (*mustahziʾūn*, 2:14), making the believers’ world the object of ridicule. The divine reply returns the same root: *Allāhu yastahziʾu bihim* — “Allah will throw their mockery back at them” (2:15). The source notes the theological delicacy: is mockery properly attributable to God? Commentators answer that what is attributed is not a human flaw but a recompense that takes the form of the deed — the disgrace, the prolongation, the letting-be that is tantamount to mockery because it leaves the mocker to live inside his own device (Q, Ṭ; R). The Qur'an’s own pattern of divine “plotting” that out-plots human plotting (10:21; 13:42; 14:46; 16:26; 27:50) illuminates the mechanism: the human plot seeks to use secrecy and timing; the divine response uses encompassing knowledge and patient extension. To mock the believers who are *upon guidance* (2:5) is to choose a currency; the verse says that currency will be returned.

**How God’s Mockery Is Not Human Mockery**

Human mockery diminishes its object by exposing it to ridicule. Divine mockery, as the commentators describe it, diminishes the mocker by exposing the mockery to its own logic. It does not originate mockery; it lets the mockery, chosen as a habit (*mustahziʾūn* as active participle), run its course until its habitation becomes its prison. That is why some gloss *yastahziʾu* as “He will recompense their mockery” or “He will humiliate them as mockers are humiliated,” and others as “He will leave them in the disgrace in which mockers deserve to be left.” In each gloss the divine act is not the initiation of ridicule but the confirmation that ridicule, once adopted as a way of handling guidance, will be the atmosphere the adopter must breathe. The wording retains *yastahziʾu* — He mocks — to preserve the symmetry, but the commentarial gloss ensures the symmetry is not anthropomorphism.

**“Leaving Them to Wander” — Extension as Punishment**

*Wa-yamudduhum fī ṭughyānihim yaʿmahūn* — “leaving them to continue wandering blindly in their defiance.” (2:15) The verb *yamuddu* — to extend, to prolong, to supply more rope — is the same verb that describes divine increase in other contexts: He *extends* provision, He *extends* life, He *extends* guidance for those who are guided. Here He extends *wandering*. The extension is not neutral duration; it is the increase of the hypocrite’s own *ṭughyān* — rebellion, trespass beyond limit (*ṭaghā*), the word most characteristically used for Pharaoh: *“Go to Pharaoh, for he has transgressed”* (20:24; 79:17). To be extended in *ṭughyān* is to be given more time to do what transgression does — to wander (*yaʿmahūn*, to be blindly confused, to lose the way). The sūrah has already described the disease that is increased (2:10); this is the wandering that is prolonged. Both are increases, but in opposite directions.

**Blind Wandering — A Phrase That Recurs as a Type**

*Yaʿmahūn* — to wander blindly, to be confused, to lose the road — appears in the same collocation elsewhere: *“We leave them wandering blindly in their defiance”* in 6:110 after hearts and eyes are confounded, in 7:186 for those whom Allah leads astray, in 10:11 for those who do not expect the meeting with Allah, in 23:75 for a people who would persist in defiance even if shown mercy. The recurrence shows that the phrase is not a one-time embellishment but a type: the person who treats guidance as mockable does not merely go astray once; he is left to wander *as* astray, so that the wandering becomes the state rather than the lapse. The sūrah of al-Baqarah uses the phrase at the close of the hypocrisy portrait precisely to mark that the portrait’s end is not a verdict of immediate destruction but of prolonged confusion — a more exacting recompense because it preserves the agency that chose it.

**Why the Punishment for Mockery Is Wandering, Not Immediate Seizure**

The hypocrite expected his double life to secure both worlds: to be counted among the believers when believers prosper (2:5) and among the satans when satans seem ascendant (2:14). Immediate seizure would have clarified the accounting at once. Wandering leaves the accounting unclear to the wanderer, and that unclearness is the mockery returned. He who mocked the believers’ certainty by mimicking their sentence “We believe” while withholding the life that sentence entails is now mocked by being left to mimic guidance while never arriving at it. He continues to move, but the movement is *ʿamah* — blindness that has lost even the awareness of being blind, as earlier he had lost the awareness of being self-deceived (2:9) and of being a corruptor (2:12) and a fool (2:13). The punishments of the opening are a graded pedagogy in imperceptibility: each recompense takes the form of the hypocrite’s own imperceptibility, extended.

**The Same Root Returned, The Same Company Retained**

The verse’s two clauses mirror the two clauses of the previous verse. There the hypocrite had two audiences and two sentences; here the divine response has two acts that correspond. To “We were only mocking” corresponds “Allah will mock them”; to “We are with you” when alone with their satans corresponds “leaving them in their defiance” — leaving them *with* the defiance and the company it implies. The *with-ness* (*maʿiyyah*) they claimed with their satans (*innā maʿakum*, 2:14) is preserved, but as abandonment rather than support: they are left *in* (*fī*) their *ṭughyān*, inside it, not led out of it. The company they chose becomes the only company they are granted, and the time they sought to extend by hedging becomes time extended for wandering. That is how mockery is paid back without the divine Speaker needing to mock as a human mocks.

**A Rebellion Named With Pharaoh’s Word**

To call the hypocrite’s state *ṭughyān* is to place the salon hypocrisy of Medina on the same continuum as Pharaoh’s imperial transgression. The word does not mean mere error; it means to exceed a limit (*ḥadd*) that was not obscure. Pharaoh exceeded the limit of kingship by claiming lordship; the hypocrite exceeds the limit of speech by claiming belief as mockery. The verse therefore does not allow the hypocrisy of the opening to be read as a petty social vice; it reads it as a species of the same rebellion that the Qur'an makes archetypal. That is why the Qur'an can later summarize an entire age, not just an individual, with the same verb: to transgress is to treat guidance as material for performance, and performance, left to wander, becomes the very wandering it once mocked.

**The Portrait Closes Where It Began — On Perception**

The opening that began with guidance for the mindful (2:2-5) and then described hearts sealed (2:7) and hearts diseased (2:10) now closes its third portrait with eyes that wander blindly. The progression is from closure to disease to blindness, and each stage returns a recompense that matches it: a tremendous punishment for the sealed (2:7), a painful punishment for the lied (2:10), and a prolonged wandering for the mockers (2:15). The hypocrite who began by saying “We believe” and ended by saying “We were only mocking” is left where his sentences have put him: with his mockery returned and with the defiance that made the mockery feel clever still around him, now as the only road he is left to walk. The sūrah will next turn to parables — fire kindled and then withdrawn, rain that blinds — to show what that road looks like from inside, but the verdict is already complete: the mocker is mocked by being left to wander, and the wandering is blind.

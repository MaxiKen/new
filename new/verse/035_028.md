## Sūrah Fāṭir 35:28

> just as people, living beings, and cattle are of various colours as well. Of all of Allah’s servants, only the knowledgeable ˹of His might˺ are ˹truly˺ in awe of Him. Allah is indeed Almighty, All-Forgiving.

**Expanded Commentary**

**Human Difference Belongs to the Same Creative Pattern**

The verse extends the variety seen in fruit and mountains to people, animals, and cattle. Human skin tones and features differ, as do the forms and colors of other living creatures. The comparison places humanity within creation without reducing human beings to animals. It says that the same Creator who gives the world its visible variety also gives people their differences, and that difference is a sign rather than a basis for contempt.

The Qur'an names variation in languages and colors among Allah's signs in 30:22. That pairing makes the social implication clear. Difference should lead to recognition of divine wisdom, not to claims that one people owns human worth. A person is not made superior by a color, lineage, or appearance. The verse's movement from natural variety to knowledge and awe directs attention from the surface of bodies to the state of the heart.

**Knowledge Is Measured by Its Effect on Fear**

The verse says that those with knowledge fear Allah. This does not mean that every academic accomplishment produces piety, nor that ignorance in a technical field makes a person blameworthy. The knowledge in context is recognition of Allah's power, wisdom, and right to be obeyed. It changes how a person stands before the unseen Lord. The knowledgeable servant sees more clearly and therefore treats disobedience more seriously.

Fear here is reverent awe, not panic that drives a person away. It includes respect, vigilance, and the desire not to cross the limits Allah has set. A person can possess religious information without this fear if knowledge remains in the tongue and never reaches conduct. Conversely, someone with little formal education can know Allah through sincere awareness and display the humility that the verse praises. Knowledge is proved by the direction it gives the will.

**Awe Joins Knowledge to Action**

The following verses mention recitation, prayer, and spending. That sequence shows that useful knowledge does not remain a feeling. Awe leads the servant to worship, to control his wealth, and to act with responsibility. If learning makes a person arrogant, harsh, or careless about obligations, it has not yet produced the knowledge intended here. The measure is not how much a person can say about Allah but how truthfully he obeys Him.

This also protects worship from becoming empty routine. Prayer performed with awareness of Allah's greatness differs from movement performed only by habit. Charity offered with awareness of His provision differs from giving meant to purchase praise. Knowledge animates the acts and makes them signs of a living relationship. It does not replace worship; it makes worship honest.

**Allah’s Might Gives Awe Its Object**

The verse ends by naming Allah Almighty and All-Forgiving. His might explains why awe is appropriate. He is not a powerless idea whose command can be ignored without consequence. He creates, sustains, judges, and can change the conditions of every creature. Knowledge of that might prevents the servant from treating sin as small merely because others approve it.

Yet might is joined to forgiveness. Awe does not become despair because Allah forgives those who return. The informed heart fears disobedience and hopes in mercy at the same time. A person who knows Allah's power does not presume upon forgiveness, but he also does not conclude that his past has made repentance pointless. The two names teach a complete posture: serious about the command, hopeful about the Lord.

The verse also distinguishes knowledge that produces awe from information that only produces confidence. A person can memorize facts about the universe and still behave as though no one will call him to account. Another can know a small number of truths and let them restrain his tongue, wealth, and appetite. The second knowledge is nearer to what the verse praises because it has reached the moral center. Awe does not mean withdrawal from learning. Studying creation can become a form of worship when it reveals dependence and leads to responsible action. The danger is not science, art, or careful observation; the danger is knowledge severed from humility. Allah's might is present in every subject, but the heart must learn to see it. Forgiveness at the end keeps the learned person from despair over his failures and from pride over his achievements. He knows that knowledge itself can be a test. It may open a door to gratitude or become material for boasting. The true scholar chooses the first path, combines understanding with worship, and remains aware that the Lord who gave him the ability to know also sees how he uses it.

**True Knowledge Produces Humility Before Difference**

The verse's first half can be misused if separated from its conclusion. People may study human variation and turn it into a theory of hierarchy. The Qur'anic result is opposite. The more accurately a person sees the diversity of creation, the more he should recognize the Creator's wisdom and his own dependence. Knowledge should make contempt harder, not easier.

A knowledgeable servant therefore treats people with justice, animals with care, and the earth with restraint. He sees colors and forms as signs of Allah's creative freedom, not as excuses for pride. He is able to admire difference without worshipping it and to seek common moral truth without erasing the created variety Allah chose. Such knowledge produces awe, and awe guides conduct. That is why the verse ends with forgiveness: the truly knowing servant remains humble enough to ask for it.

## Sūrah al-Isrāʾ 17:19

> **But whoever desires the Hereafter and strives for it accordingly, and is a ˹true˺ believer, it is they whose striving will be appreciated.**

**Expanded Commentary**

**Whoever Desires Hereafter And Strives Accordingly**

Wa-man arāda al-ākhirata wa-saʿā lahā saʿyahā — and whoever desires Hereafter and strives for it its striving, accordingly. Man, whoever, conditional. Arāda from r-w-d, to desire, want, intend, as previous verse yurīdu al-ʿājilah desires fleeting, and aradnā We intend. Al-ākhirata from ā-kh-r, last, Hereafter, latter, opposite of al-ʿājilah, hasty, immediate, fleeting. Wa-saʿā from s-ʿ-y, to strive, run, endeavor, hasten with effort, from same root as saʿy, striving, effort, as in pilgrimage saʿy between Ṣafā and Marwah, striving. Lahā, for it, for Hereafter. Saʿyahā, its striving, its due striving, its appropriate striving, from s-ʿ-y, meaning striving that is due for Hereafter, appropriate for it. Al-Ṭabarī explains that whoever desires Hereafter, not fleeting world alone, and strives for it its striving, meaning strives with striving due for Hereafter, with deeds that lead to Hereafter, appropriate effort, not mere wish. Al-Qurṭubī says saʿā lahā saʿyahā indicates striving appropriate for Hereafter, i.e., with faith and good deeds, with conditions, not mere desire without effort.

**And Is True Believer**

Wa-huwa mu'minun — and he is believer, true believer. Huwa, he. Mu'minun from a-m-n, to believe, have faith, be secure, from same root as īmān, faith, and amn, security, and mu'min, believer, faithful. Al-Ṭabarī says and he is believer, meaning condition for striving to be appreciated is faith, because without faith deeds not accepted, as faith is foundation. Al-Rāzī says wa-huwa mu'minun indicates that striving for Hereafter must be while believer, because faith is basis, without it striving not beneficial, as previous verse about desiring fleeting alone leads to Hell even if strives in worldly sense. The phrase true believer in translation adds emphasis that faith must be true, sincere, not superficial.

**Striving Appreciated**

Fa-ulā'ika kāna saʿyuhum mashkūran — then those, their striving is appreciated, thanked. Fa-ulā'ika, then those, so those, those who desire Hereafter and strive with faith. Kāna, is, was, indicates being. Saʿyuhum, their striving, their effort, from s-ʿ-y. Mashkūran from sh-k-r, to thank, appreciate, be grateful, reward abundantly, from same root as shukr, gratitude, and shakūr, grateful, Appreciative, as verse 3 grateful servant and Allah is Shakūr. Al-Ṭabarī says then those, their striving will be appreciated, meaning Allah appreciates, thanks, rewards their striving for Hereafter, accepts it and rewards abundantly. Al-Qurṭubī says mashkūran means appreciated, rewarded, thanked by Allah, meaning accepted and rewarded abundantly, thanked, not rejected.

**Contrast With Fleeting Desirer**

Verse 18 said whoever desires fleeting world alone, We hasten for him what We will to whoever We will, then destine them for Hell where they will burn condemned and rejected. Verse 19 says whoever desires Hereafter and strives for it accordingly and is true believer, it is they whose striving will be appreciated. The contrast is clear and intentional: two desires, two strivings, two ends. Fleeting desirer gets hastened limited worldly then Hell condemned rejected; Hereafter desirer who strives accordingly as believer gets appreciated striving, i.e., mighty reward mentioned in verse 9. The pair teaches that desire alone not enough; striving accordingly with faith needed for Hereafter, while fleeting desirer may get worldly hastening without true striving for Hereafter.

**Why Striving And Faith Both Mentioned**

Why both striving and faith? Because desire without striving is wishful thinking, mere wish, and striving without faith is not accepted, not appreciated. Al-Rāzī explains that arāda al-ākhirata is intention, sincere desire for Hereafter, wa-saʿā lahā saʿyahā is action, striving appropriate, due striving, wa-huwa mu'minun is foundation, faith, all three needed: sincere desire for Hereafter, striving with deeds due for it, and faith. This is most upright way that Qur'an guides to, as verse 9 says Qur'an guides to what is most upright. The sūrah's moral code that follows details what striving for Hereafter looks like: honoring parents, giving relatives due, not spending wastefully, not killing children, not approaching adultery, honoring pledges, measuring fully, not following without knowledge, not walking arrogantly.

**Connection To Qur'an Guides To Most Upright**

Verse 9 said surely this Qur'an guides to what is most upright, and gives good news to believers who do good that they will have mighty reward. Verse 19 says whoever desires Hereafter and strives for it accordingly and is true believer, it is they whose striving will be appreciated — this is example of most upright: desiring Hereafter, striving accordingly with faith, appreciated. The sūrah's moral code that follows is detailed guidance to most upright that, if followed with desire for Hereafter and faith, leads to appreciated striving. The verse therefore links back to opening promise of mighty reward for believers who do good.

**Linguistic Note On Saʿa And Mashkur**

Arāda from r-w-d, to desire, intend, from same root as murād, intended. Ākhirah from ā-kh-r, last, Hereafter, latter, opposite of ūlā, first. Saʿā from s-ʿ-y, to strive, run, endeavor, from saʿā, to strive, hasten. Saʿy from same root, striving, effort, endeavor, as pilgrimage saʿy. Mu'min from a-m-n, believer, from amina, to be secure, believe, from īmān, faith, amn, security. Kāna from k-w-n, to be. Mashkūr from sh-k-r, thanked, appreciated, from shakara, to thank, appreciate, from shukr, gratitude, and Shakūr, Appreciative. The verse's structure — whoever desires Hereafter and strives for it its striving and is believer, then those their striving is appreciated — with desire, striving appropriate, faith, then appreciation, shows conditions for appreciated striving, three conditions leading to result, and result is appreciation.

**How This Verse Teaches Sincere Striving**

This verse teaches sincere striving for Hereafter with faith: desire Hereafter, strive with appropriate striving, be believer, then striving appreciated. Al-Qurṭubī says believer should desire Hereafter, not make fleeting sole goal, should strive with deeds that Qur'an guides to most upright, with faith, so that striving appreciated and rewarded with mighty reward, not condemned rejected. The practical lesson is to examine desire: do you desire Hereafter? Do you strive for it its striving, i.e., with deeds due, not mere wish? Are you believer, true believer? If yes, glad tidings: striving appreciated, thanked by Allah who is Shakūr, Appreciative, who appreciates small deeds with great reward, multiplies. This encourages effort even if imperfect, as long as desire and faith sincere and striving appropriate.

**A Reflection On Appreciated Striving**

Mashkūran — appreciated, thanked — from sh-k-r, same root as grateful servant in verse 3, Nūḥ grateful servant, and Allah Shakūr Appreciative. Allah is Shakūr, Appreciative, who thanks servants by rewarding abundantly, appreciating small striving with great reward. The person who desires Hereafter and strives with faith, even if striving imperfect, is appreciated, thanked, rewarded, not condemned. This contrasts with madhmūman madḥūran, condemned rejected, for fleeting desirer. The believer who reflects will desire Hereafter, strive its striving with faith, following most upright guidance, seeking bounty in world but with Hereafter as goal, so that striving is mashkūr, appreciated, leading to mighty reward and avoiding painful punishment, and record laid open will show appreciated striving.

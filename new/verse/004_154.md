## Sūrah al-Nisāʾ 4:154

> We raised the Mount over them ˹as a warning˺ for ˹breaking˺ their covenant and said, “Enter the gate ˹of Jerusalem˺ with humility.” We also warned them, “Do not break the Sabbath,” and took from them a firm covenant.

### Expanded Commentary

**Covenant, humility, and disciplined worship**

The Mount was raised over the Israelites as a warning connected to their covenant. The image communicates the weight of revelation: a community cannot accept scripture as an ornament while refusing its obligations. Entering the gate with humility and observing the Sabbath are examples of obedience in body and conduct.

Humility is not humiliation by people. It is the abandonment of arrogance before God and the willingness to let revealed law shape ordinary practice. The Sabbath prohibition also shows that a covenant may include specific disciplines whose wisdom is partly in obedience itself.

The verse recalls a history of command, resistance, and mercy. It is not a licence to demean Jews; it is a warning to all covenant communities that receiving revelation creates obligations. Muslims should read the narrative with the same fear applied to themselves.

**The weight of a covenant**

A covenant community should treat worship as an obligation with practical consequences, not as a cultural decoration. Humility at the gate, observance of a command, and fidelity to a promise are ordinary actions in which a grand revelation becomes real. The warning applies to every people who boasts of scripture while ignoring its demands.

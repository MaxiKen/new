## Sūrah al-Nisāʾ 4:154

> We raised the Mount over them ˹as a warning˺ for ˹breaking˺ their covenant and said, “Enter the gate ˹of Jerusalem˺ with humility.” We also warned them, “Do not break the Sabbath,” and took from them a firm covenant.

**Expanded Commentary**

**We Raised the Mount Over Them for Their Covenant**

The verse states *wa rafaʿnā fawqahumu al-ṭūra bi mīthāqihim* — and We raised the Mount — *al-Ṭūr* — Sinai, the mountain — over them, above them, because of their covenant, for breaking their covenant. *Rafaʿnā fawqahum* — We raised above them — was explained as God lifting Sinai over the Children of Israel as canopy until they accepted the covenant at Moses’ return, after the calf after clear signs had been forgiven and Moses given compelling proof — *sulṭānan mubīnā* (4:153). *Bi mīthāqihim* — for their covenant, with their covenant — was read as because the covenant required lifting as warning, as the sūrah has shown that *to Him belongs whatever is in heavens and earth* (4:126) and He is *Muḥīṭ* — encompassing (4:126) — the Mount raised is His ownership displayed. The sūrah has traced *mīthāq* — covenant — from the firm commitment of marriage — *mīthāq ghalīẓ* (4:21) — to treaty with covenanted peoples (4:90, 4:92) — to the firm covenant with the Children of Israel; to raise a mountain over them for breaking it was explained as the visible *sulṭān mubīn* that Moses was given (4:153) made physical, so that the covenant not kept remains above until kept.

**Enter the Gate With Humility and Do Not Break the Sabbath**

The commands *wa qulnā lahumu udkhulū al-bāba sujjadan wa qulnā lahum lā taʿdū fī al-sabti* — and We said to them, enter the gate — the gate of Jerusalem, the town — prostrating, with humility, bowing — and We said to them, do not transgress, exceed, in the Sabbath — were explained as two covenant details that display obedience versus demanding visible God (4:153). *Udkhulū al-bāba sujjadan* — enter the gate in prostration, bowing, humbled — was read as the entry into Jerusalem after wandering, where *“enter the gate with humility and say *ḥiṭṭa* — relief, forgiveness — We will forgive and increase the doers of good”* (2:58). To enter humbly was explained as the opposite of invoking female gods besides Him (4:117); to be told *lā taʿdū fī al-sabti* — do not transgress the Sabbath, do not exceed limits on the seventh day — was read as that the Sabbath was made test for them, as the sūrah elsewhere recounts the Sabbath-breaking town turned to apes (7:166). Both commands were linked to *“Gardens for those who believe and do good”* (4:122) — humility at the gate and restraint on the Sabbath are *ḥanīf* submission following Abraham whom He took as friend (4:125), not altering creation by hunting on the forbidden day.

**And We Took From Them a Firm Covenant**

The closure *wa akhadhnā minhum mīthāqan ghalīẓā* — and We took from them a firm, strong, solemn covenant — was explained with *ghalīẓ* — firm, weighty, strong — the same *mīthāq ghalīẓ* taken from women upon marriage (4:21) where dowry is not taken back unjustly — here the covenant with the community at Sinai. *Akhadhnā* — We took — was read as divine taking as witness, as *“God bears witness to what He sent”* (4:166) — the covenant is witnessed by the Mountain raised and by the gate entered. The sūrah has shown that the Book was sent with truth to judge between people by what He shows (4:105); the firm covenant is the Book’s predecessor where the Mount was the witness. Early reports noted that the firmer the covenant, the firmer the punishment for breaking it — the thunderbolt struck them for demand greater than a book (4:153) — yet the *ʿafw* — pardoning — after calf showed the covenant also carries forgiveness where sought (4:110). The firm covenant was also linked to *taqwā* commanded to those before you and you (4:131) — *be mindful* — the covenant is the form of that *waṣiyyah*.

**A Verse That Follows Calf Forgiven With Mountain Raised**

Placed after the People of the Book demanded a book from heaven, demanded greater — visible God — were struck, took the calf after clear signs, yet were pardoned and Moses given compelling proof (4:153), this verse follows pardon with the covenant’s display: Mount raised over them for covenant, gate with humility, Sabbath not transgressed, firm covenant taken. The sequence — demand, thunderbolt, calf, pardon, proof — now — Mount raised — shows that pardon is not cancellation of law but renewal of law with visible witness. The sūrah has shown that the Book was sent with truth and God bears witness with His knowledge (4:166); the Mount raised is that witness made physical, as the emigrant’s many havens are earth made spacious (4:100). For the weak this is steadying: where you feared that firm covenant is too heavy, the Mount raised shows the covenant is not lighter than mountain, yet the Gate entered with humility shows it is entered bowed, not proudly, and Sabbath restraint shows it is kept by not doing, not only by doing, and the Pardoning after calf shows that where it was broken, return is through repentance still open.

**The Firm Covenant Above as Guardian**

The image — Mount above them for covenant — was explained as that the sūrah’s ownership — *to Him belongs whatever is in heavens and earth* (4:126) — becomes canopy, as prayer under arms was canopy guarded (4:102) and trusteeship suffices (4:132). The firm covenant — *ghalīẓ* — that is taken from them parallels the firm commitment taken from wives where wealth is entrusted (4:21); both teach that God’s earth is spacious (4:100) but covenant is not spacious to break. The verse generalises the sūrah’s warning *do not consume wealth unjustly* (4:29) into *do not transgress the Sabbath* — both are limits. The next verse will name the breaking: for breaking their covenant, rejecting His signs, killing prophets unjustly, saying *our hearts are unreceptive* — He sealed hearts for disbelief, so they believe only few — (4:155).

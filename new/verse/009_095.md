## Sūrah al-Tawbah 9:95

> When you return, they will swear to you by Allah so that you may leave them alone. So leave them alone—they are truly evil. Hell will be their home as a reward for what they have committed.

**Expanded Commentary**

**Their Oaths Seek Escape From Consequence**

The hypocrites swear when the believers return so that the believers will turn away from them. The oath is not chiefly a statement of truth. It is a tool for ending the conversation and avoiding blame. They want the community's attention to move elsewhere before the evidence of their refusal produces a consequence.

This pattern appears whenever people use emotional declarations to make accountability uncomfortable. The listener feels cruel for asking another question, so the speaker gains protection through the listener's mercy. The believer should not become hard-hearted, but he should ask whether the oath clarifies the facts or merely demands that the facts be ignored. A sacred name cannot be used to command forgetfulness.

**Turning Away Can Be a Boundary**

The command to leave them alone is not permission to admire their conduct or to pretend that no wrong occurred. It is a direction to withdraw trust and social approval from people whose presence is spiritually defiling. Continued argument may give their excuses more space than their conduct deserves.

A boundary can be a form of justice. It protects the sincere from manipulation, stops a liar from controlling every conversation, and communicates that membership has responsibilities. The believer may still pray for guidance and accept a genuine repentance, but he does not have to remain available for repeated oaths that produce no change.

**Defilement Describes Moral Contagion**

The word translated as defilement points to the effect of their actions and inner state. Contact with a lie can stain the community when people repeat it, excuse it, or grant the liar authority over those he harmed. The warning is not about physical impurity. It is about a moral influence that spreads when dishonesty is treated as harmless.

This is why leaders must consider who is given access to public trust. A person may be welcomed as a human being without being given control of funds, information, or vulnerable people. Distance from a corrupt influence can protect the community while leaving a path for the person to repent. Boundaries need not become hatred.

**Hell Is the Result of What They Earned**

The verse calls Hell a recompense for what they committed. Their destination is connected to their choices: deceit, refusal, and the use of oaths to avoid responsibility. This is not an arbitrary punishment for a moment of awkwardness. It is the final consequence of a life that preferred falsehood to repentance.

The believer should fear the gradual earning of such a destination. A person may think each lie is small, each excuse temporary, and each avoidance harmless. Together they create a direction. Repentance interrupts the direction before it becomes a settled condition. The warning is therefore severe because the habit is easier to stop at its beginning.

**Mercy Must Be Guided by Knowledge**

Some listeners may want to forgive quickly because they dislike conflict. Others may want to punish because they enjoy exposure. The verse teaches a third path: know what happened, stop rewarding the wrong, and leave the final judgment to Allah. Mercy can withhold access without withholding hope.

A believer can say, “I will not continue this conversation,” without saying, “You can never return.” He can protect a community while refusing to delight in the person's downfall. This balance keeps the boundary clean. It prevents mercy from becoming gullibility and firmness from becoming revenge.

**The Oath Should Make the Truth More Serious**

Because the hypocrites swear by Allah, the verse also warns against casual oath-taking. Invoking Allah's name should make speech more truthful, not more persuasive. A person who uses an oath to escape correction harms his own relation to the sacred.

The sincere servant uses fewer dramatic declarations and more consistent conduct. He lets time and action support his words. If he has failed, he accepts that people may need distance. If he has been falsely accused, he tells the truth without believing that an oath can force everyone to trust him. Allah knows the account, and that knowledge is a firmer protection than any human performance.

Leaving them alone can therefore mean refusing to argue with a performance. The believer does not need to answer every oath, expose every private flaw, or seek emotional revenge. He can withdraw from the relationship that has become harmful and devote his energy to people who are truthful. If the person later repents, the door of return is not owned by the community; Allah's mercy remains open. But return must be shown through changed conduct, not through the same oath that once ended the conversation. Distance can protect the heart while preserving hope that the wrongdoer will abandon the path that led to Hell.

The command to turn away therefore has a social purpose. It tells the community not to let the dishonest person set the terms of every relationship through repeated oaths. People may stop debating, stop granting access, and stop treating his words as reliable. They can still avoid cruelty and pray that he returns. This is not the same as declaring that repentance is impossible. It is a refusal to reward a performance that has already harmed the trust. If he wants to return, he begins by telling the truth without demanding that the community forget its evidence.

Distance from deception can be merciful when it protects the heart without closing the door to repentance.

Truthful distance can protect the community while leaving repentance possible.

The boundary is a form of protection.

## Sūrah al-Baqarah 2:52

> Even then We ˹still˺ forgave you so perhaps you would be grateful.

**Expanded Commentary**

**Pardon After Calf — The “Even Then”**

“Even then We still forgave you so perhaps you would be grateful” (2:52). Thumma ʿafawnā ʿankum min baʿdi dhālika laʿallakum tashkurūn. The “then” after calf-worship is sharpened by “even then.” After “you worshipped the calf… while you were wrongdoers” (2:51), the next word is not retribution but pardon. The source notes the parallel in 4:153, where pardon after calf-worship is also mentioned as a display of mercy beyond human reckoning.

ʿAfawnā — We pardoned, effaced. The verb ʿafā, to wipe out, suggests erasure. The file is not merely suspended; it is wiped. The pardon is “after that” — min baʿdi dhālika — after the act named wrongdoing (2:51). Mercy does not wait for minimized description; it follows maximal naming.

**Why “Still” Matters**

The translation adds “still” in brackets to capture the force of thumma… fa — sequence that persists in mercy: after deliverance from the house of Pharaoh (2:49) and the parted sea watched (2:50), and then calf-worship in the interval (2:51), still pardon. The stillness teaches divine character: patience that is not forgetfulness, forbearance that is not indifference. The sūrah, which has already shown Adam taught words of return and then forgiven (2:37), now shows a people taught pardon after collective apostasy.

**Laʿallakum Tashkurūn — That You May Be Grateful**

The purpose clause — laʿallakum tashkurūn — “so that you may be grateful,” “that perhaps you may give thanks” (2:52) — does not describe cause after pardon only, but aim. Gratitude is the intended fruit. Shukr in Qur’anic register is not only speech but practice: to use the pardoned life in the way the Pardoner loves. The same purpose will recur after the book and the criterion: “that perhaps you may be guided” (2:53). Pardon aims at gratitude; guidance aims at right direction; the two together form the restored life.

The gratitude invited here is not for calf-worship but from it: from having been spared the full wages of it. The pardon does not trivialize the wrong; “while you were wrongdoers” (2:51) remains. It reorients time: what was done wrong remains wrong, yet the doers are given more time to do rightly.

**Why This Verse Matters Before the Book**

The recital’s order — sea (2:50) → calf (2:51) → pardon (2:52) → Book and Criterion (2:53) — shows that guidance is not withdrawn by failure. The Book is given after pardon, not before. A people that worshipped in Moses’ absence is not disqualified from receiving Scripture; they are pardoned so that Scripture may still guide them. The structure anticipates the sūrah’s larger claim about Israel: past failure does not cancel present invitation to believe the confirming revelation (2:41).

**Pardon That Teaches Mercy’s Scale**

The source points to 4:153 as parallel: after demanding to see God openly and after calf-worship, pardon is still given, “and We gave Moses clear authority.” The pairing suggests that divine pardon is not measured against human severity but against divine capacity: to forgive the forgivable, to defer the deferrable, to give authority to the messenger even after his people’s gravest breach.

This does not make pardon automatic. The sūrah next records a commanded repentance — “repent to your Maker and slay yourselves” (2:54) — whose severity and subsequent relenting show that pardon, while free, is not cheap. “Even then We forgave you” (2:52) precedes that discipline, and “then He relented unto you” (2:54) follows it, framing the discipline inside mercy.

**Living “So That You May Be Grateful”**

- Keep the “even then” in memory. When wrong has been named as wrong, the next sentence can still be pardon. To deny pardon after naming wrong is despair; to deny wrong after pardon is presumption. The verse avoids both.
- Translate pardon into thanks. Shukr after ʿafw is not only “thank you” but use: pray, give, unmix, disclose (2:42–45) with the time pardon has purchased.
- Expect guidance after pardon. The sūrah does not leave the pardoned without a way. Look for the Book and the Criterion next, and treat them as gifts that follow forgiveness, not wages that precede it.

**What Not to Claim**

This verse alone does not detail how pardon was appropriated — whether through Moses’ intercession, communal remorse, or the severe repentance of 2:54 — nor does it specify the calf-worshippers’ individual fates. Those are treated in surrounding verses. What is firm is the sequence and purpose: even after calf-worship while wrongdoing, pardon was granted so that gratitude might follow (2:52).

## Sūrah an-Nūr 24:42

> To Allah ˹alone˺ belongs the kingdom of the heavens and the earth. And to Allah is the final return.

**Expanded Commentary**

**Sovereign Dominion over Heaven and Earth: wa-lillāhi mulku al-samāwāt**

The divine discourse follows the contemplation of cosmic worship (v. 41) with an absolute statement of constitutional authority: *wa-lillāhi mulku al-samāwāti wa-al-arḍ*, to Allah alone belongs the kingdom of the heavens and the earth. The syntax employs the rhetorical device of *taqdīm* (fronting the prepositional phrase *lillāh*), establishing strict exclusivity: dominion belongs to God alone, without partner, rival, advisor, or successor.

The term *mulk* encompasses total ownership, supreme political sovereignty, legislative authority, and cosmological governance across all dimensions of time and space. Every celestial galaxy, planetary system, atmospheric layer, terrestrial continent, and biological entity exists as part of His undisputed realm. He regulates physical laws with flawless consistency and ordains moral statutes with ultimate justice, providing sustenance to every creature. No king, emperor, or legislative assembly holds genuine autonomous sovereignty; whatever power human beings exercise on earth is merely a provisional, heavily circumscribed trusteeship granted temporarily by the King of kings, who alone governs life, death, resurrection, and judgment, commanding eternal reverence and obedience.

**Illusory Ownership versus Transcendent Authority**

Human beings routinely fall victim to the psychological delusion of autonomous ownership. When an individual purchases land, builds a palatial residence, amasses financial investments, or ascends to political office, their ego whispers that they have become absolute masters of their domain.

Verse 42 radically shatters this prideful illusion. The earth beneath our feet, the atmosphere we inhale, the physical bodies we inhabit, and the material wealth we manage belong entirely to God. Human ownership is temporary, conditional, and strictly accountable before divine law at every stage of mortal life. When a landowner dies, their deeds, titles, and legal certificates dissolve into irrelevance; the earth remains, quietly outliving its boastful claimants. By anchoring human consciousness in God's exclusive ownership, the Qur'an cures the soul of avarice, tyranny, and arrogance, instilling a humble ethic of responsible stewardship (*khilāfah*) over divine property, freeing the heart from possessiveness and destructive materialism.

**The Terminal Convergence: wa-ilā Allāhi al-maṣīr**

The verse pairs divine sovereignty over space with absolute authority over time and destiny: *wa-ilā Allāhi al-maṣīr*, and to Allah is the final return. The noun *maṣīr* derives from the root *ṣ-y-r*, signifying transformation, destination, and the ultimate terminal outcome toward which all moving things converge.

Every created journey—whether the life trajectory of an individual human being, the historical arc of an empire, or the cosmic lifespan of the universe—inevitably terminates at the divine throne. No soul can escape beyond the cosmic perimeter of God's dominion, nor can any rebel evade the appointed meeting. The trajectory of existence is not an endless, meaningless cycle of biological birth and decay, nor a linear drift into eternal nothingness. It is an intentional, purposeful journey culminating in divine audience and total accountability, giving ultimate moral seriousness, dignity, and eternal weight to mortal existence.

**Ontological Reassurance for the Righteous**

For the vulnerable believer striving to maintain righteousness amidst a hostile, corrupt, or intimidating environment, verse 42 provides profound psychological comfort, courage, and emotional security. When tyrannical powers abuse their authority or worldly corruption appears overwhelmingly dominant, the believer remembers that the kingdom belongs solely to God, not to mortal despots.

Earthly tyrants do not own the heavens and the earth; they are merely temporary dust walking upon God's earth, breathing God's air, and hurtling toward God's judgment seat. Their arrogant dictates cannot alter divine decree, nor can their persecution outlast the brief span of mortal history. Knowing that *al-maṣīr* is toward the All-Just Lord empowers the righteous to stand firm with unyielding dignity, refusing to compromise their moral integrity out of fear or worldly desperation, knowing that absolute justice awaits all souls in the divine presence, where every wrong is righted.

**Dismantling the Delusions of Earthly Empire**

The historical resonance of this verse was immediately felt by the early Muslim community facing the formidable empires of Persia and Rome. To the eyes of worldly observers, these superpower empires possessed invincible armies, vast treasuries, and unshakeable political dominance that commanded global obedience and submission from weaker nations.

Verse 42 swept aside this intimidating aura with effortless divine authority. In the eyes of the Qur'an, imperial thrones are transient shadows; true sovereignty (*al-mulk*) belongs exclusively to the Creator. History repeatedly validates this Qur'anic perspective: mighty empires that once dominated continents now lie buried beneath desert sands, their palaces reduced to shattered ruins, their proud inscriptions undecipherable without archeological excavation. Only the living King survives, reigning supreme over creation long after earthly crowns have crumbled into dust, proving the utter vanity of mortal power.

**Cosmic Accountability as the Horizon of Human Action**

Sealing the verse with *wa-ilā Allāhi al-maṣīr* establishes an inescapable horizon that shapes every ethical decision and colors every moral choice. If every human path leads inexorably back to God, then all earthly transactions, political policies, domestic interactions, and personal choices must be calibrated against that final meeting.

A person who knows they must return to their Maker cannot exploit an orphan, defraud a business partner, slander a chaste neighbor, or live in heedless sensual indulgence. The reality of *al-maṣīr* transforms ethics from external social etiquette into an urgent existential imperative that shapes character and guides conduct. Every step taken on earth is a step closer to the Creator. Wise servants structure their fleeting days around the single question: How will this deed appear when I present it before the Sovereign Lord on the Day of the final return, when all earthly secrets are unveiled, excuses vanish, and divine truth reigns supreme throughout eternity, rewarding righteousness with everlasting peace and radiant honor before all creation?

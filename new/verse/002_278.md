## Sūrah al-Baqarah 2:278

> O believers! Fear Allah, and give up outstanding interest if you are ˹true˺ believers.

**Expanded Commentary**

**The Command Stated**

The sentence addresses the believers and instructs them to be mindful of God and to give up what remains of the practice that the passage has treated, and the community's teachers read the command as the completion of the prohibition. The Book has described the two outcomes and the conduct of those whom it commends, and the sentence here turns to the community with the instruction that the matter required.

The wording names what remains of the practice, so that the believers who had entered the community were required to abandon the gains that were still due to them. The teachers noted the phrase, since the command concerned the outstanding claims rather than the acts that had already been completed.

The sentence closes with the condition that the persons addressed are believers, and the community's scholars read the phrase as the statement that the conduct belongs to the faith that the community professed. The Book's method of joining its commands to the description of those addressed appears throughout the passage.

**The Mindfulness Named**

The first clause of the sentence instructs the community to be mindful of God, and the community's teachers explained the phrase as the description of the awareness that governs the whole of a person's conduct. The Book uses the term throughout its instructions, and the community's scholars gathered the passages when they set out the subject.

The teachers noted the order of the sentence, since the mindfulness of God is named before the command to give up the practice. The community's scholars read the arrangement as the statement about the disposition from which the obedience of the law proceeds.

The wording also places the command within the community's relationship with its Lord, so that the dealings of its members are presented as a part of the service that the Book requires. The sentence in this place therefore belongs to the passages that treat the community's conduct as a whole.

**The Remainder Named**

The commentators explained the phrase about what remains as the reference to the increases that a creditor could still claim from the debts that were outstanding at the time of the instruction. The community's scholars discussed the cases that the phrase covers, and they described the manner in which the dealings of the city were settled after the command.

The teachers also noted that the phrase did not require the abandonment of the principal, since the passage that follows states the rule about the return of what was lent. The sentence in this place concerns the addition that the practice had placed upon the debt.

The community's jurists built the law of the subject upon the combination of the verses, so that the principal of a loan remained the right of the lender while the addition was removed. The passage's treatment of the matter was read as a single body of instruction.

**The Condition of Faith**

The sentence closes with the statement that the command applies to the persons who are believers, and the community's scholars read the clause as the statement about the connection between the conduct and the profession. The teachers described the phrase as the reminder of what the community had accepted when it entered the faith.

The wording also serves the instruction that follows, since the passage then addresses those who do not comply with a warning whose severity belongs to the subject. The teachers of the community read the two verses together, and they drew from the pair the lesson about the seriousness of the matter in the community's law.

The commentators noted that the phrase does not describe a doubt about the persons addressed but states the condition on which the command rests. The Book's use of the same formula elsewhere appears in its instructions, and the community's teachers gathered the instances.

**The Reports About the Practice**

The reports of the community describe the circumstances in which the instruction was received, and the material shows the practice as it was conducted in the city and the changes that the command required. The teachers used the accounts when they explained the passage.

The commentators also transmitted the reports about the manner in which the community's authorities dealt with the outstanding claims, and the material shows the care with which the matter was settled. The jurists of the community drew from the reports the lesson that the instruction was applied to the cases that existed rather than to the persons alone.

The passage's treatment of the subject was studied by the community's jurists, and the books of law record the discussions that grew from the verses. The sentence in this place supplies the command from which the discussions begin.

**The Place in the Passage**

The sentence stands between the account of the two outcomes and the warning that addresses those who persist in the practice, and the community's teachers read the arrangement as the passage's movement from the description to the instruction. The Book has stated what the practice does and what the charity achieves, and the sentence here requires the community to act upon what it has been told.

Scholars of the community observed that the command is addressed to the community as a body, so that the subject belongs to the conduct of its members and to the order of its dealings. The passage's material on wealth therefore treats the individual and the community together.

The verses that follow state the warning and the rule about the principal, so that the sentence and the following verses form one instruction. The community's teachers read them as a unit when they taught the law of the subject.

**The Teaching Drawn**

The community's preachers drew from the sentence the lesson that the change required of the believers reached the claims that they held as well as the dealings they conducted. The material shows the formula repeated in the exhortations, since the practice was established in the habits of the city and the instruction required the community to examine its accounts.

The scholars of the community used the sentence when they explained the relation between the command and the faith that the community professed. The condition at the close of the verse supplied the ground on which the instruction was urged.

The passage therefore presents the command together with the reason for it, and the sentence in this place supplies the community with the disposition that the obedience of the law requires. The reader is given the instruction and the standing of those who receive it.

**What the Verse Establishes**

The sentence establishes the command to be mindful of God, the requirement to give up what remains of the practice, and the condition of the faith under which the instruction is given. The community's teachers used the verse when they set out the law of the subject and when they urged the believers to complete the change that the passage required, and the sūrah's next verse states the warning that belongs to those who do not comply, so that the command and its consequence are presented in order.

## Sūrah al-Baqarah 2:228

> Divorced women must wait three monthly cycles ˹before they can remarry˺. It is not lawful for them to conceal what Allah has created in their wombs, if they ˹truly˺ believe in Allah and the Last Day. And their husbands reserve the right to take them back within that period if they desire reconciliation. Women have rights similar to those of men equitably, although men have a degree ˹of responsibility˺ above them. And Allah is Almighty, All-Wise.

**Expanded Commentary**

**The Waiting Period**

The verse states that divorced women must wait three monthly cycles before they may marry again, and it forbids the concealment of what God has created in their wombs. The sentence follows the alternatives of reconciliation and divorce, and it supplies the rule that governs the period between them.

The waiting period gives the marriage a term in which its future is settled, and the community's teachers read the sentence as a measure that protects both the woman's position and the lineage of any child. Readers are shown that the Book's legislation on the household attends to the consequences of the union, and the sentence supplies the term that the community observes.

**The Meaning of the Cycles**

The commentators discussed the term rendered as cycles, and they described the different points at which the period is understood to begin and end. The community's jurists built the rules on the readings, and the differences among the schools reflect the applications they drew.

The discussion shows how the community worked through a matter on which the text gives the principle and the reports supply the detail. Readers are given the account of a ruling that the community implemented in its households, and the sentence in this place supplies the term from which the law is derived.

**The Concealment Forbidden**

The sentence forbids the woman to conceal what God has created in her womb, and it attaches the prohibition to belief in God and the Last Day. The community's teachers read the clause as a statement about the honesty that the period requires, and they noted that the matter concerns the rights of the husband and of the child.

The Book's treatment of the subject elsewhere connects the rules of the household with the knowledge of God, and the community's scholars used the passage when they set out the obligations of the parties. The passage shows that the period is not a private matter between the spouses alone, and the sentence's reference to belief places the observance within the community's faith.

**The Right of the Husband**

The sentence states that the husbands have the better right to take their wives back during the period if they desire reconciliation, and the community's teachers read the clause as the provision that accompanies the waiting period. The right belongs to the man who has declared the divorce, and the sentence names the condition that governs its exercise.

The Book's practice of attaching a condition to a permission appears throughout its legislation, and the reader is shown that the right is bounded by the intention of reconciliation. The community's scholars used the clause when they discussed the resumption of a marriage, and the sentence supplies the rule that the following verses develop.

**The Rights of the Two Parties**

The sentence states that the women are owed obligations similar to those they owe, that this is to be observed in an honourable manner, and that men have a degree above them. The community's commentators explained the first clause as the statement of a reciprocity between the spouses, and they recorded the saying of one of the early teachers who described his own care for his appearance before his wife as an instance of it.

The second clause was discussed at length, and the community's scholars explained the degree as the responsibility that the husband bears for the household rather than as a difference in the standing of the two before God. The passage shows that the Book's account of marriage rests on the mutual obligations of the parties, and the sentence supplies the framework in which the details are set.

**The Closing Names**

The sentence closes with the description of God as mighty and wise, and the community's teachers read the pair as the ground of the arrangement the sentence has described. The attributes accompany the rules about the household, and the reader is shown that the legislation belongs to the same source as the judgments the sūrah has set out elsewhere.

The passage continues with the rules about the number of declarations and about the compensation by which a wife may obtain a divorce, and the reader is carried from the waiting period to the arrangements that conclude the marriage. The sentence in this place supplies the principle, and the verses that follow supply its applications.

**The Rights That Meet Each Other**

The clause that the women have rights similar to those owed by them has been read as the foundation of the law of the household, and the community's teachers placed it at the head of their treatment of the subject. The obligations of each spouse are measured against those of the other, so that the household stands on reciprocity rather than on the claims of one party alone.

The reports preserve the words of one of the early teachers who explained the clause by describing his own attention to his appearance before his wife, since the duties he owed were of the same kind as those he expected. The jurists built the rules of maintenance, companionship, and the treatment of the wife upon the principle the sentence states.

The degree that the sentence assigns to the husbands has been explained in several ways. The teachers connected it with the responsibility for the household, with the right of return that the passage describes, and with the duty of defence and provision that falls on the husband, so that the word indicates a task rather than a difference in worth.

**What the Verse Establishes**

The sentence establishes the waiting period, the prohibition of concealment, the husband's right to resume the marriage within the term, the reciprocity of the spouses' obligations, and the degree that belongs to the husband. The community's teachers used the verse when they set out the law of divorce, and the reader is shown that the Book's legislation on the household addresses the rights of both parties.

The community's jurists drew the balance of the household's obligations from the clause, and the teachers used the sentence when they answered those who asked about the treatment of wives and husbands. The verse's other clauses supply the waiting period and the right of return, so that the section treats the practical conduct of the couple together with the principle on which it rests.

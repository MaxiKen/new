## Sūrah al-Tawbah 9:76

> But when He gave them out of His bounty, they withheld it and turned away indifferently.

**Expanded Commentary**

**The Gift Exposed the Promise**

The promised condition arrived: Allah gave them from His bounty. The event should have released the charity they had pledged. Instead, they became miserly and turned away. Their problem was therefore not inability. It was the change that occurred when imagined wealth became actual possession. The gift exposed whether their earlier vow had been sincere.

This pattern appears whenever a person speaks generously before receiving power, money, or recognition. Before the gift, generosity costs no possession. After the gift, the person feels that releasing any part of it threatens the identity he has built around ownership. The verse teaches the servant to judge his own promises by what he does when the opportunity to fulfill them arrives.

**Miserliness Is More Than Keeping Money**

Withholding includes refusing the financial obligation, but the moral defect can spread wider. A person may hold back time, knowledge, forgiveness, or protection even when Allah has given him the ability to share. The closed hand is an outward sign of a heart that treats bounty as a private wall.

Careful spending is not miserliness. A servant must provide for dependents, avoid waste, and plan for real needs. Miserliness begins when fear of loss makes a person deny a rightful claim or forget gratitude. The wealthy person can be generous without extravagance, and the poor person can be miserly with a small amount. The measure is willingness to fulfill Allah's claim.

**Turning Away Shows a Change in Allegiance**

The verse adds that they turned away indifferently. They did not merely forget one payment in confusion. They moved away from the promise and from the moral relationship it represented. Indifference is dangerous because it makes betrayal feel ordinary. The person no longer feels troubled by the gap between his words and actions.

A believer should treat such a gap as urgent. If he promised a charitable act and now hesitates, he should review the promise, the need, and the lawful conditions rather than waiting for the feeling to return. Obedience often precedes emotional ease. The heart learns generosity by giving, not by waiting until every fear has disappeared.

**Provision Is a Test of Gratitude**

Bounty tests what poverty can conceal. A person with little may sincerely wish to give more, while a person with much can refuse a small obligation. Neither condition automatically proves virtue or failure. Allah tests the servant through what is in his hand and what he believes he may keep.

Gratitude translates into use. The grateful owner pays debts, feeds dependents, supports the needy, and recognizes that his wealth came through means he did not create alone. He does not need to distribute everything to prove gratitude, but he must not pretend that possession erased the Giver's claim. Every increase asks a question about direction.

**The Promise Was Never a Contract Against Allah**

Their behavior also shows a misunderstanding of vows. They treated their promise as a way to persuade Allah to grant wealth, then acted as though receiving wealth cancelled the spiritual purpose of the promise. But a vow binds the servant to truthfulness; it does not bind Allah to deliver the requested outcome.

A person should therefore avoid making promises in moments of desire. If he has made one, he should fulfill it sincerely rather than renegotiate after the blessing arrives. Allah knows whether the vow was an act of devotion or a strategy for obtaining a benefit. The safest charity is given because Allah deserves it, not because the donor wants to control the next gift.

**Indifference Can Be Reversed by Immediate Action**

The verse is a warning, but it also tells the servant where to begin repair. Open the hand before the excuse grows. Give what is due, acknowledge the failure, and rebuild trust through repeated action. If someone was harmed by the withholding, repair the harm directly.

A person who has become indifferent may not feel worthy of generosity. He should still return. Allah's bounty is not permission to remain selfish, and a past failure is not permission to stop correcting the future. The gift revealed the defect, but the same gift can become a means of repentance when it is finally placed where Allah commands.

The heart that is afraid of losing wealth should remember that every possession already rests within Allah's power. Hoarding cannot make the gift permanent. It can only deprive the owner of the chance to turn it into lasting good. Responsible planning is wise, but planning must include the claims that Allah placed on wealth. A person who gives may still become poor, yet he has not lost the meaning of what he gave. A person who withholds may remain rich, yet the richness can become a witness that he preferred possession to gratitude. Immediate action is the best protection against the indifference described here.

The servant can protect himself by deciding in advance what generosity will look like. He can set a fair portion aside, consult people who understand need, and refuse to let sudden increase rewrite his obligations. Such planning is not distrust of Allah. It is a way to keep gratitude concrete when desire becomes loud. If he has already failed, he should not wait for a dramatic feeling of remorse. He can begin with the payment he withheld and the apology he postponed. The hand learns openness through use, and Allah can turn a gift that exposed greed into a means of healing it.

Gratitude must become action before the heart learns to call withholding wisdom.

The hand opens when gratitude stops being only a feeling.

## Sūrah al-Aʿrāf 7:53

> Do they only await the fulfilment ˹of its warning˺? The Day it will be fulfilled, those who ignored it before will say, “The messengers of our Lord certainly came with the truth. Are there any intercessors who can plead on our behalf? Or can we be sent back so we may do ˹good,˺ unlike what we used to do?” They will have certainly ruined themselves, and whatever ˹gods˺ they fabricated will fail them.

**Expanded Commentary**

**Waiting for the Fulfilment**

The verse opens with a question about whether the audience is waiting for anything other than the fulfilment of the Book's warning, and the question treats the delay as the whole of what remains. The warning in question is the promise and the threat that the Book has stated, and the fulfilment is the arrival of the events it describes. The sūrah's preceding verse has described the Book as explained with knowledge and given as a guide and a mercy, and the verse in front of us states that its warning will be completed.

The form of the question implies that there is nothing left to wait for but the event itself. The sūrah's account of the two companies has shown what the warning describes, and the revelation that carries it has been presented as clarified. The verse in front of us turns the hearer's attention to the moment when what the Book has promised becomes the world he is standing in.

**The Confession When It Arrives**

The verse reports the words of those who ignored the Book when the fulfilment arrives, and their first statement is that the messengers of their Lord came with the truth. The Book records the same confession from the condemned in several places, and its accounts of the last day include the acknowledgement of the truth of the message by those who had denied it. The sūrah's earlier verses have already reported the confession of the destroyed communities when the punishment came upon them (7:5), and the statement in front of us belongs to the same sequence.

The confession comes at the point at which it cannot change the outcome, and the Book's accounts of the last day treat such acknowledgements as testimony rather than as repentance. The sūrah of the Troops and the sūrah of the Forgiver report the same words from the inhabitants of the Fire, and the verse in front of us places the confession at the moment of the warning's fulfilment.

**The Search for Intercessors**

The second element of the reported speech is the question about whether any intercessors can plead on the speakers' behalf, and the Book addresses intercession in several passages. The sūrah of the Cow states that no soul will be of help to another on the day, that no intercession will be accepted, no ransom taken, and no help given (2:48), and it states elsewhere that intercession belongs to God and takes place by His permission (2:255). The sūrah of the Cattle states that the intercessors whom the idolaters claimed will not be present (6:94).

The commentary tradition reads the question in front of us as the last resort of people who had attributed intercession to their idols. The audience's religion included the expectation that the beings it associated with God would plead for it, and the sūrah's closing scenes have shown those beings failing their worshippers. The question in front of us is the expression of that failure, and the Book's answer is stated in the passages about the day on which no plea will be accepted.

**The Request to Be Sent Back**

The third element of the speech is the request to be returned so that the speakers may act differently from the way they acted, and the Book records the same request in several passages. The sūrah of the Cattle reports the wish of the condemned that they might be sent back and not repeat what they used to do (6:27–28), the sūrah of the Poets records the same wish, and the sūrah of Fāṭir reports the request of those who will be in the Fire to be brought out of it. The verse in front of us places the request in the mouth of the people who ignored the Book.

The Book's answer to the request appears in the sūrah of the Cattle, where the wish to be returned is followed by the statement that if they were returned they would return to what they had been forbidden, and that they are liars (6:28). The verse in front of us does not carry the answer, and the sūrah's earlier account of the two companies supplies it. What the condemned ask for has been answered before the request is made.

**What the Fabrication Leaves Behind**

The verse closes with the statement that the condemned have ruined themselves and that what they fabricated has failed them. The Book uses the same language for the idols and the invented systems of belief in its accounts of the reckoning, and the sūrah of the Cattle states that the partners of the idolaters will fail them (6:24), the sūrah of Yūnus states that what they fabricated will leave them, and the sūrah of Hūd states that the things they invented will be lost to them. This verse states the outcome of the appeal, and the fabricated gods are named as the failures they have proved to be.

The term for fabrication covers everything the audience had invented in the name of religion, and the sūrah has spent its length exposing the practices that were attributed to God without warrant. The verse here states that the whole structure has collapsed at the moment when the appeal is made, and the sūrah's next verses turn from that collapse to the signs of the created order.

**Where the Verse Sits**

The verse stands fifty-third in the chapter, and it closes the passage about the Book that began with the statement that it has been explained with knowledge and given as a guide and a mercy (7:52). The verses that follow begin the section on the signs of creation and the command to call upon God (7:54–56), and the passage then proceeds to the account of the prophets and their peoples. The present verse supplies the link between the revelation and the created order.

The reader who has been returned to the Book in his hands now sees its warning fulfilled before him. The messengers are acknowledged, the intercessors are sought and not found, and the request to return is made too late. The sūrah's next verses state the signs by which the same truth is visible from the beginning.

**The Transition to the Next Verse**

The next verse states that God is the One who created the heavens and the earth in six days, established Himself on the Throne, made the night and day follow one another, and subjected the sun, the moon, and the stars by His command, and it declares that creation and command belong to Him and that He is blessed, the Lord of all worlds (7:54). The regret of the condemned gives way to the account of the Creator, and the sūrah's argument moves to the signs.

The two verses together complete the movement from the audience to their Lord. The Book explains, its warning arrives, the fabricated structures fail, and the created order is presented as the evidence that the audience could have read at any time; and the verses that follow state the command to call upon the One who made it.

## Sūrah al-Aʿrāf 7:26

> O children of Adam! We have provided for you clothing to cover your nakedness and as an adornment. However, the best clothing is righteousness. This is one of Allah’s bounties, so perhaps you will be mindful.

**Expanded Commentary**

**The Address to the Children of Adam**

The verse turns from the narrative of the first pair to their descendants, and the address to the children of Adam governs the passage that follows. The sūrah's instructions about clothing, food, and worship are given to people who have just heard how their parents lost the covering they had, and the commands are presented as the provision that answers that loss. The Book's other addresses to the children of Adam in this sūrah follow at intervals, and each of them is attached to the same narrative background.

The verse states that God has sent down clothing for the children of Adam, and the phrase covers both the material provision that makes clothing possible and the guidance that prescribes its use. The commentary tradition notes that the covering of the body is described as a gift from God rather than as a human invention, and the narrative of the leaves sewn in the Garden supports the reading. The first attempt at covering was the pair's own; the verse in front of us announces the provision that was given afterwards.

**The Covering of Nakedness**

The verse names the covering of the nakedness as the first function of clothing, and the mention connects the instruction directly with the account of the fall. The sūrah has described the exposure that followed the eating of the tree, and the command in front of us supplies the remedy that the descendants of the pair are to use. The Book's instructions about modesty elsewhere in the sūrah develop the same theme in the context of worship and of conduct.

The commentary tradition reads the verse as an answer to the pre-Islamic practice of circumambulating the sanctuary without clothing, and it cites the account of the Quraysh's rules about the garments and the food that could be used in the sacred precinct. The provision of clothing in front of us stands against that practice, and the sūrah's instruction to wear the adornment at every place of worship restates the same point in its next verses.

**Adornment Beyond Covering**

The verse adds that God has provided adornment as well as covering, and the distinction is preserved in the commentary. Covering answers a need, and adornment answers the desire for beauty, and the Book treats both as legitimate. The sūrah's following verses state that the children of Adam are to take their adornment at every place of worship, and the instruction presents the beauty of dress as a fitting accompaniment to prayer.

The Book's teaching about the created order includes the beauty that has been placed in it, and the sūrah of the Cattle describes the livestock and the crops as objects of use and of beauty. The verse in front of us applies the same estimate to clothing, and it forbids neither beauty nor expense in itself. What the sūrah prohibits in its following verses is excess.

**The Clothing of Reverence**

The verse states that the clothing of reverence is better, and the commentary tradition gives two broad readings of the phrase. One reading treats it as the clothing worn for prayer or as the covering that modesty requires, which would make the phrase a preference for the modest garment over the merely ornamental. The other reading treats it as the quality of reverence itself, made up of faith, modesty, righteous conduct, and the fear of God, and reads the phrase as a preference for the inward garment over the outward.

The sūrah's teaching about the heart and the outward act supports the second reading, and the Book's statements about the fear of God as the beginning of right conduct appear throughout. Al-Rāzī expresses the thought by saying that the shame of the believers is always covered even when they are unclothed, and that the shame of the profligate is always exposed even when they are dressed.

**The Verse as a Sign**

The verse closes by describing the provision of clothing as one of God's signs, given so that people may remember. The Book attaches the same formula to the created order and to the revelation, and the sūrah of the Cattle uses it in its accounts of the rain and the harvest. The clothing in front of us is presented as a sign because it answers the condition that the fall produced, and the memory it is meant to trigger is the memory of what happened to the first pair.

The Book's signs are presented as material for reflection rather than as objects of veneration, and the sūrah's instruction about worship develops the point in its next verses. The reader who takes the sign as a sign remembers the sequence of the Garden, the tree, the exposure, and the covering, and the instruction of the verse follows from that recollection.

**Where the Verse Sits**

The verse stands twenty-sixth in the chapter, and it opens the series of instructions to the children of Adam that continues through the following verses. The passages on food, on the forbidden things, and on the lawful adornment follow, and the address then turns to the communities and to the messengers. The provision of clothing in front of us is the first of the gifts that the sūrah names.

The reader who has followed the story of the first pair now receives the commands that are addressed to their descendants. The covering of the body, the adornment that goes with it, and the reverence that surpasses both are placed before him, and the sūrah's next verses state the conduct that accompanies them.

**The Transition to the Next Verse**

The next verse again addresses the children of Adam and warns them not to let Satan deceive them as he caused their parents to leave the Garden, and it states that he and his tribe see them from where they cannot see him, and that the satans have been made the allies of those who disbelieve (7:27). The instruction about clothing is followed by the warning about the enemy, and the two belong to the same passage of guidance.

The pattern of the opening section is repeated: the gift is named, the enemy is named, and the command is given. The reader is told what he has been provided with and what he is to guard against, and the sūrah then proceeds to the practices of the audience that the passage is meant to correct.

## Sūrah al-Tawbah 9:107

> There are also those ˹hypocrites˺ who set up a mosque ˹only˺ to cause harm, promote disbelief, divide the believers, and as a base for those who had previously fought against Allah and His Messenger. They will definitely swear, “We intended nothing but good,” but Allah bears witness that they are surely liars.

**Expanded Commentary**

**A Sacred Building Can Hide an Unholy Purpose**

The verse describes people who establish a mosque, yet their purpose is harm, disbelief, division, and support for those who had fought the Messenger. The building carries a sacred name, but the intention beneath it is corrupt. This warns the community not to judge an institution only by its sign, decoration, or public language.

A mosque should gather believers for worship, learning, mercy, and justice. If a building is used to split the community, spread rejection, or coordinate harm, its physical form cannot purify its purpose. The lesson extends to every religious institution. A school, charity, or project can use sacred language while serving pride or faction. Allah examines the aim and the effect, not only the label.

**Harm Can Be Organized Through Respectable Forms**

The hypocrites do not present their project as an open attack. They use the form of a mosque because respectable forms attract trust and reduce suspicion. A harmful plan becomes more effective when it can borrow the credibility of worship. This is why communities need both reverence and accountability.

Accountability does not mean treating every new project as a conspiracy. It means asking who controls the institution, what its funds support, how decisions are made, and whether its teaching produces unity around truth or hostility toward sincere believers. Transparency protects good projects from suspicion and exposes projects that depend on secrecy.

**Division Is a Religious Harm**

The verse names division as one of the purposes. Not every disagreement is division. People may differ over lawful matters while remaining one community. Division occurs when a group uses disagreement to break trust, create rival allegiance, or weaken the believers' ability to recognize and serve the truth.

A religious building that makes people despise one another has failed its moral purpose, even if its program appears busy. The believer asks whether a project makes prayer, knowledge, and care more possible. If it creates a permanent inner circle that treats the wider community as an enemy, the institution is feeding division rather than worship.

**A Former Enemy Can Use an Internal Base**

The mosque is also described as a base for those who had fought Allah and His Messenger. This shows how internal betrayal can connect to external hostility. The problem is not cooperation with every non-Muslim; lawful agreements and peaceful relationships remain distinct. The problem is giving a hostile force a platform from which to attack the believing community.

Leaders therefore have a duty to protect institutions from being used for harm. They must distinguish real security concerns from prejudice and evaluate conduct rather than ancestry. A community that ignores clear coordination with aggression is careless; a community that treats every outsider as an enemy is unjust. The verse calls for evidence and vigilance together.

**Good Intentions Must Be Tested by Truth**

The builders swear that they intended only good. Their oath fails because Allah bears witness that they are liars. The claim of good intention cannot override an established harmful purpose. A person may sincerely believe his project is beneficial, but sincerity must be tested by facts, means, and effects.

The believer should apply this to his own work. If a project repeatedly creates harm, he cannot protect it by saying that the goal was good. He must change the plan, repay the damage, or stop the project. Good intention is necessary but not sufficient when the chosen means betray the stated purpose.

**Religious Trust Requires Moral Foundations**

A mosque is not made righteous by its walls. Its foundation is righteousness, truth, and the desire to please Allah. The community must ask whether worship is producing humility, service, clean finance, and protection for the vulnerable. If the building becomes a stage for status or a shelter for plotting, its sacred appearance has become a deception.

This warning should not make believers cynical about institutions. It should make them careful stewards. Build places of worship, but build them on truth. Welcome people, but do not hand the trust to those who use it for division. Allah knows the real purpose, and a community that seeks His pleasure will prefer a modest truthful space to an impressive building founded on harm.

The historical setting also teaches that security and worship cannot be separated. An institution that offers a prayer space while serving an enemy's strategy is not protected by the comfort it provides. Its public claim must be tested against its connections and its effect on the believers. This does not authorize rumor or the destruction of property based on suspicion. It calls for responsible leadership, evidence, and lawful protection. A community that keeps these standards can expose a harmful project without making every independent project look harmful. The aim is to preserve worship, not to enlarge factional power.

The phrase “we intended nothing but good” also teaches that claimed intention must be compared with the whole design. A project that consistently divides believers cannot be defended by pointing to one charitable event. A leader who says he wants unity but rewards slander has revealed the real purpose through the reward. The believer does not read hearts, but he can read patterns. He asks what the institution funds, whom it excludes, and what kind of speech becomes normal inside it. Truthful projects welcome such questions because accountability strengthens them; harmful projects call accountability an attack because secrecy is part of their foundation.

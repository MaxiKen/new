## Sūrah al-Qiyāmah 75:39

> producing from it both sexes, male and female.

**Expanded Commentary**

**A Third Verb, a Third Act**

The sequence has moved from drop to clot to proportioned form, and now a further verb introduces the last stage named in this sūrah: *fa-jaʿala*, and He made. The change of verb is worth noticing, since the sūrah has already used *khalaqa* for bringing into being and *sawwā* for proportioning. *Jaʿala* is the verb of assigning, of making one thing into another, of instituting a function. What is being described here is not a new material but a new determination of the material already formed: from that single origin, two kinds of human being, complementary and different, each with its own role in the continuation of the species. The grammar also settles a question about the pronoun. The two sexes are made *from him* — from that single human source — which is the Qur'an's consistent account of the origin of the pair (4:1) and the point on which this verse turns. The differentiation is not a second creation out of nothing. It is a determination imposed on what was already there.

**Why Sexuality Belongs in This Argument**

There are two reasons, and they work together. The first is that the production of two coordinated sexes out of one source is a further instance of the design the sūrah has been describing. A single drop does not contain two kinds of person by accident; the differentiation is imposed, and the complementarity that makes reproduction possible is not the sort of thing that arises from undifferentiated matter. The second reason is the one the sūrah needs: if the continuation of the human race depends on a mechanism the Creator instituted, then creation is not a single event in the past but an activity He is carrying on now. Every birth is His act, and the argument from origins is therefore not an argument about something remote. This is why the Qur'an's appeals to human origins are so often in the present tense and addressed to people who were not present at the beginning of anything. The proof is not an event in the distant past that must be accepted on authority. It is happening now, in every pregnancy, and a person who has watched a child being born has watched the argument he is refusing.

**The Pairing Elsewhere in the Qur'an**

The same point is made repeatedly and in several registers. *“And He created the pairs—males and females—from a sperm-drop when it is emitted.”* (53:45–46). The pairing is extended to the whole created order: *“And We created pairs of all things so perhaps you would be mindful.”* (51:49). It is stated as a sign accompanied by affection rather than only by function: *“And one of His signs is that He created for you spouses from among yourselves so that you may find comfort in them. And He has placed between you compassion and mercy.”* (30:21). And it is given as the origin of human diversity: *“Indeed, We created you from a male and a female, and made you into peoples and tribes so that you may ˹get to˺ know one another.”* (49:13).

**Reproduction as a Standing Sign**

The argumentative value of reproduction is that it puts the proof in front of everyone. A person does not have to accept a report about the beginning of the human race, because the beginning of every human being is observable. *“One of His signs is that He created you from dust, then—behold!—you are human beings spreading over ˹the earth˺.”* (30:20), and: *“And He is the One Who originated you all from a single soul, then assigned you a place to live and another to ˹be laid to˺ rest.”* (6:98). The Qur'an's constant question in these passages is the one it asks in Sūrah al-Naḥl: given that you concede the origin, why do you then argue with the One who originated you (16:4)?

**The Multiplication Is Not the Difficulty**

One of the underlying assumptions of the objector in v. 3 was that the sheer number of the dead makes resurrection implausible — billions of bodies, dispersed over the whole earth and throughout all of history. The production of the two sexes answers that assumption rather than merely repeating an answer to it. Human multiplication is itself part of the divine arrangement described in this verse, and the God who instituted a mechanism for filling the earth with people is not going to be defeated by the result of His own institution. The Qur'an states the principle in a single line: *“The creation and resurrection of you ˹all˺ is as simple ˹for Him˺ as that of a single soul.”* (31:28). Multiplicity is what makes a task hard for a creature, since a creature must deal with things one at a time. It is not what makes anything hard for a Creator who sustains every particle at once, and the sūrah of Yā Sīn makes the same point by asking about the origin of the whole order: *“Can the One Who created the heavens and the earth not ˹easily˺ resurrect these ˹deniers˺?” Yes ˹He can˺!* (36:81).

**Where the Sūrah Has Arrived**

The sūrah opened with the objection that bones cannot be gathered, and it has now answered it with the stages of a single life: a drop emitted, a clinging clot, a proportioned form, and from it the two sexes, from whom every human being since has come. Nothing in that list was disputed by the people being addressed. The last verse will ask the question that the whole sequence was assembled to make unanswerable, and it will ask it in a form that expects the hearer to supply the answer himself.

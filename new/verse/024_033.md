## Sūrah an-Nūr 24:33

> And let those who do not have the means to marry keep themselves chaste until Allah enriches them out of His bounty. And if any of those ˹bondspeople˺ in your possession desires a deed of emancipation, make it possible for them, if you find goodness in them. And give them some of Allah’s wealth which He has granted you. Do not force your ˹slave˺ girls into prostitution for your own worldly gains while they wish to remain chaste. And if someone coerces them, then after such a coercion Allah is certainly All-Forgiving, Most Merciful ˹to them˺.

**Expanded Commentary**

**The Injunction to Active Chastity: wa-lyastaʿfif**

The verse provides clear ethical guidance for those who genuinely cannot attain the financial or social means to marry: *wa-lyastaʿfif alladhīna lā yajidūna nikāḥan ḥattā yughniyahumu Allāhu min faḍlih*. The grammatical form *yastaʿfif* denotes active, determined striving for chastity. The individual who faces unavoidable bachelorhood is not granted a moral license to indulge in illicit avenues, pornography, or casual compromises under the pretext of biological frustration.

They are commanded to wage an active, conscious struggle to protect their spiritual and physical purity. The Prophet ﷺ operationalized this divine command in Ṣaḥīḥ al-Bukhārī 5066, advising: O young people, whoever among you possesses the means to marry, let him marry, for it lowers the gaze and protects chastity; and whoever cannot, let him fast, for fasting serves as a shield for him. Chastity must be maintained through discipline, prayer, fasting, and patience until divine bounty opens the door to honorable marriage, preserving the integrity of the soul before God and society.

**The Contract of Emancipation: The Institution of Mukātabah**

The divine law introduces a revolutionary legal instrument aimed at the systematic dismantling of servitude: *wa-alladhīna yabtaghūna al-kitāba mimmā malakat aymānukum fa-kātibūhum*. This established the formal institution of *al-mukātabah*—a legally binding contract of self-emancipation. Any enslaved individual who requests their freedom is granted the statutory right to purchase their liberty through installment payments earned through their own independent labor.

Classical jurists, including ʿUmar ibn al-Khaṭṭāb and prominent early scholars such as ʿAṭāʾ and al-Ḍaḥḥāk, maintained that if the enslaved person demonstrates moral integrity and the vocational capacity to earn a lawful livelihood (*in ʿalimtum fīhim khayrā*), the master is legally obligated to execute the contract. The slave becomes an autonomous economic agent, protected from sale or transfer, walking with steady, legally guaranteed steps toward absolute freedom, with the full backing of Islamic law.

**The Redistribution of Divine Wealth: wa-ātūhum min māli Allāh**

The revelation reinforces emancipation with an extraordinary mandatory financial subsidy: *wa-ātūhum min māli Allāhi alladhī ātākum*, and give them from the wealth of Allah which He has bestowed upon you. The text pointedly reminds the wealthy that their property is not an absolute personal possession, but a divine trust (*māl Allāh*) entrusted to them for a brief season.

Classical jurists interpreted this financial mandate in two complementary ways. Under the rule of *al-waḍīʿah*, the master is legally required to forgive or return a substantial portion (typically one-quarter or one-fifth) of the agreed contract price to the newly freed individual, providing them with initial startup capital. Simultaneously, Islamic law designated contracting slaves (*al-mukātabūn*) as one of the eight mandatory categories entitled to public zakāh funds in 9:60 (*wa-fī al-riqāb*). The state treasury and the private master were both compelled to finance the liberty of the formerly enslaved, ensuring their lasting economic dignity.

**The Prohibition of Commercial Exploitation and Coerced Prostitution**

The verse delivers an uncompromising ban against the commercial sexual exploitation of women: *wa-lā tukrihū fatayātikum ʿalā al-bighāʾi in aradna taḥaṣṣunan*. In pre-Islamic Madinah, hypocrites like ʿAbd Allāh ibn Ubayy operated commercial brothels, forcing their enslaved maidservants into prostitution and pocketing the profits to finance political networks.

When one of these victimized maidservants, Muʿādhah, fled to the Prophet ﷺ seeking spiritual protection from forced debauchery, this verse descended as an absolute criminalization of the sex trade. The conditional clause *in aradna taḥaṣṣunan* (if they desire to be chaste) is not an operative legal condition permitting coercion if they lack the desire; it serves as a devastating rhetorical condemnation of the masters, exposing the shameful reality that enslaved women possessed a higher moral desire for chastity than the free men who claimed to own them, shattering the hypocrisy of the slaveholders.

**Divine Absolution for the Coerced: Allāhu min baʿdi ikrāhihinna Ghafūrun Raḥīm**

The verse concludes with an immaculate declaration of divine mercy and legal exoneration: *wa-man yukrihhunna fa-inna Allāha min baʿdi ikrāhihinna ghafūrun raḥīm*. Islamic jurisprudence establishes the unyielding legal maxim that absolute coercion (*al-ikrāh al-muljiʾ*) completely eliminates criminal, civil, and spiritual culpability from the coerced victim.

The victimized women carry zero sin, incur zero shame, and are immune from any legal punishment. The divine names *Ghafūr* (All-Forgiving) and *Raḥīm* (Most Merciful) apply directly to the exploited women, assuring them that God pardons what was forced upon their bodies and surrounds them with tender mercy and spiritual restoration. The full, catastrophic weight of criminal liability, worldly punishment, and eternal damnation falls exclusively upon the coercive master who profited from their agony, ensuring that divine justice righteously vindicates the oppressed.

**The Systematic Dismantling of Human Bondage**

A comprehensive analysis of verse 33 reveals an integrated, multifaceted blueprint for human liberation. In a single verse, the revelation mandates individual sexual chastity, creates an enforceable legal bridge to liberty through *mukātabah*, mandates private and public financial grants to ensure economic viability after emancipation, criminalizes the sexual trafficking of women, and grants absolute legal immunity to victims of exploitation.

The Qur'an did not treat emancipation as a theoretical abstraction or an empty slogan. It constructed precise legal, economic, and moral mechanisms that systematically dismantled the institution of human servitude from within, steadily advancing the divine purpose of establishing a free, dignified brotherhood beneath the sovereign lordship of the Creator, anchoring human freedom in divine law.

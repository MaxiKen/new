## Sūrah an-Nūr 24:30

> ˹O Prophet!˺ Tell the believing men to lower their gaze and guard their chastity. That is purer for them. Surely Allah is All-Aware of what they do.

**Expanded Commentary**

**The Injunction to Lower the Gaze: yaghuḍḍū min abṣārihim**

The divine command instructs the Messenger ﷺ to issue an explicit moral directive to the male community: *qul lil-muʾminīna yaghuḍḍū min abṣārihim*. The Arabic verb *gaḍḍ* denotes lowering, reducing, restraining, or averting. The preposition *min* (from/of) is partitive: men are not commanded to close their eyes completely—which would render vision impossible and paralyze daily human activity—but to lower their gaze from that which is unlawful, shameless, or seductive.

Addressing the command to men first is an intentional psychological and social prioritization. Men possess active visual agency and are particularly susceptible to visual stimulation. The eye is the primary portal of the soul; what enters through the vision bypasses logical defenses, takes root in the imagination, stirs biological passions, and eventually seeks manifestation in action. By commanding men to restrain their visual intake at the threshold, the revelation cuts off the pipeline that feeds illicit desire before it can overpower the moral intellect.

**Guarding the Private Parts: wa-yaḥfaẓū furūjahum**

The verse establishes an immediate structural connection between the discipline of the eyes and the protection of chastity: *wa-yaḥfaẓū furūjahum*, and let them guard their private parts. In the Qur'anic moral anatomy, the eye and the sexual organs are linked in an unbreakable chain of cause and effect. Guarding one's chastity against fornication, adultery, and perverse desires is an impossible fantasy if the eyes are granted unrestricted freedom to feast upon forbidden sights.

Classical commentators, including al-Qurṭubī and Ibn Kathīr, observed a subtle nuance: across the Qur'an, whenever guarding the private parts is commanded, it denotes protection against the sin of fornication (*al-zinā*), except in this specific passage of Sūrah an-Nūr, where it encompasses both abstaining from illicit intercourse and covering one's own nakedness (*al-ʿawrah*) from the gaze of others. Men are commanded to be both sexually chaste and bodily modest, refusing to expose their private physical boundaries to public view.

**The Prophetic Protocol of the Accidental Glance**

The practical application of this divine statute is illuminated through authoritative traditions of the Sunnah. In Ṣaḥīḥ Muslim 2159, the noble companion Jarīr ibn ʿAbdillāh inquired directly of the Prophet ﷺ concerning the sudden, accidental glance (*naẓar al-fajʾah*) that falls upon an uncovered person without premeditation. The Prophet ﷺ gave a concise, definitive command: Avert your gaze immediately!

In another well-known narration, the Prophet ﷺ advised ʿAlī ibn Abī Ṭālib: O ʿAlī, do not allow a second glance to follow the first; for the first glance is for you (unintentional and forgiven), but the second glance is against you (deliberate and sinful). Righteousness does not demand that a human being possess supernatural control over the initial sensory encounter in a bustling world; it demands the moral courage to immediately tear one's gaze away rather than indulging the desire to linger and inspect.

**The Spiritual Aesthetic of Moral Purity: dhālika azkā lahum**

The verse provides the ultimate rationale for visual restraint: *dhālika azkā lahum*, that is purer for them. The term *azkā* derives from *tazkiyah*, signifying moral cleansing, spiritual growth, and psychological elevation. Far from being a repressive or puritanical deprivation, lowering the gaze is the supreme guarantor of internal tranquility and psychological well-being.

The individual who allows their eyes to roam without restraint is condemned to perpetual psychological torment: they gaze upon that which they cannot lawfully possess, filling their heart with burning frustration, unhealthy comparisons, and dissatisfaction with their lawful spouses. Conversely, the man who disciplines his gaze enjoys a serene heart, free from the turbulent storms of uncontrolled lust. Early spiritual masters noted that whoever lowers their gaze for the sake of God experiences a divine sweetness of faith that illuminates the inner sight (*al-baṣīrah*) of the soul.

**The Rights of the Public Road in the Sunnah**

The communal dimension of this verse is vividly preserved in the prophetic teachings regarding public spaces. In Ṣaḥīḥ al-Bukhārī 2465, the Prophet ﷺ cautioned his companions: Beware of sitting on the pathways and roadsides! When the companions explained that they had legitimate social and commercial needs to gather on the roads, the Prophet ﷺ instructed: If you insist on sitting there, then give the road its rightful due!

When the companions asked what constituted the rightful due of the road, the Prophet ﷺ enumerated its fundamental duties: Lowering the gaze, removing harmful obstacles from the path, returning the greeting of peace, enjoining the good, and forbidding the wrong. This prophetic charter demonstrates that visual modesty is an indispensable prerequisite for public civility, transforming the shared streets into a safe and honorable space where women, men, and children can move without fear of harassment or predatory stares.

**The Divine Awareness of Visual Treachery: inna Allāha khabīrun bimā yaṣnaʿūn**

The verse concludes with an awe-inspiring divine signature: *inna Allāha khabīrun bimā yaṣnaʿūn*, surely Allah is All-Aware of what they do. The choice of the attribute *al-Khabīr* denotes intimate, exhaustive, and penetrating knowledge of the most subtle and concealed intricacies of human action. Furthermore, the text uses the verb *yaṣnaʿūn* (what they craft or manufacture) rather than the standard *yaʿmalūn* (what they do).

The word *yaṣnaʿūn* points to the subtle internal craftsmanship of visual sin: the stolen glances stolen from the corner of the eye, the feigned innocence, the deliberate positioning of the body to catch an illicit glimpse, and the internal fantasies constructed behind lowered eyelashes. Humans can easily deceive their peers into believing they are modest. God, however, is *Khabīr*—intimately aware of the treacherous gaze and all that the chests conceal. Living in the light of this divine awareness anchors visual modesty in profound, reverent awe.

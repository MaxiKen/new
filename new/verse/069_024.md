## Sūrah al-Ḥāqqah 69:24

> ˹They will be told,˺ “Eat and drink joyfully for what you did in the days gone by.”

**Expanded Commentary**

**Heaven Says Eat**

Kulū wa-shrabū — "eat and drink." The divine imperative, elsewhere commanding duty, here commands delight. Orders to enjoy startle: heaven must permit pleasure before the righteous dare it. Such commanding honors — God's "eat!" outranks earth's every invitation. Twin passages voice the same feast-cry to the righteous: eat and drink for what you did (52:19; 77:43). Triple witness, single banquet — bliss's menu fixed across sūrahs. The plural addresses companies: feasting is social, tables crowded with the certain. Readers should hear permission's extravagance: eternity's Host insists. Earthly piety sometimes suspects pleasure; heaven's imperative sanctifies it. Eat — God says so. What duty earned, delight now spends. The command graduates obedience into enjoyment.

Hanīʾan — "joyfully," wholesomely, with health. Hanīʾ means pleasant without harm — food that nourishes sans sickness, pleasure sans penalty. Earthly feasts bill afterward: indigestion, regret, reckoning. Heaven's hanīʾ voids the bill — eat without ailment, drink without dregs. Wholesomeness is paradise's culinary signature: delight digested perfectly. The adverb modifies both verbs — eating joyful, drinking joyful. Such purity extends the verse's logic: rāḍiyah life (verse 21) eats hanīʾ food. Pleasant livers, pleasant meals. Readers should contrast hangover-pleasures: earth's sweets sour. Heaven's never do. What the righteous denied themselves (fasts, scruples) returns as hanīʾ — denial's interest paid deliciously. Eat freely; wholesomeness guarantees. Joy without aftermath is joy indeed.

**The Past Pays for the Feast**

Bi-mā aslaftum — "for what you sent ahead." Aslafa means to advance, remit beforehand — deeds as advance payment. The feast bills the past: present bliss charges past obedience. Such remittance-theology dignifies every hidden deed — prayers wired ahead, charities deposited early. Nothing sent ahead is lost; all returns as banquet. Bi- (for/because) states causation plainly: feast because faithfulness. Bliss is earned yet gifted — earned as wage, gifted as grace exceeds wage. Readers should audit their remittances: what travels ahead daily? Each prayer wires credit; each charity ships provision. The Day's table serves only pre-ordered meals. Send ahead generously; feast eternally. What you forwarded in faith, you will eat in joy.

**Days Gone By — or Empty Days**

Al-ayyām al-khāliyah — "the days gone by." Khāliyah means past, elapsed, empty — days that passed. Most hear life's bygone days: the earthly past for which bliss pays. Al-Rāzī and al-Suyūṭī report the gem-minority: "empty" days as fasting days — stomachs emptied for God. Grounds for majority: context (whole life's deeds), scope (all righteousness, not only fasting). Grounds for minority: khāliyah's "empty" nuance, hunger's poetic wage. Strong reading keeps past-days primary, fasting-days echoing: all obedience feasts, fasting feasts sweetest. Both agree on essence — earthly days purchase heavenly meals. Readers should spend days as currency: each dawn mints coins. Gone days return as courses. What the past held (prayer, fast, charity), the feast serves. Spend days; eat eternities.

Develop the minority's beauty: emptied stomachs inherit filled tables. Fasting inverts feasting temporally — hunger now, hanīʾ then. Those who declined lunches for God receive banquets from God; appetite mastered becomes appetite crowned. Symmetry delights: khāliyah (empty) bellies earn dāniyah (near) clusters. Such correspondence encourages the hungry righteous — every skipped meal ships provisions ahead. Ramadan's dusk-hunger previews eternity's dawn-feast. Even as echo, the reading preaches powerfully: denial converts to delight at heaven's exchange rate. Readers fasting now should picture verse 24's table: this hunger, that hanīʾ. Exchange guaranteed. What emptiness offered God, fullness returns from God — with eternal interest.

**Wages Precede the Welcome**

Aslaftum's past tense matters: sent-ahead before arrival. Wages precede welcome — provision shipped before passengers. Such precedence structures all salvation: grace prepares, then receives. The garden was readied (clusters lowered) before dwellers arrived; the feast was cooked (deeds remitted) before guests sat. Nothing in bliss improvises. Readers should take comfort's logic: heaven's preparations exceed earth's. If the table waits set, the Host surely expects you — provided remittances traveled. Anxiety about arrival mistakes the order: welcome was prepared before you departed. Send ahead; arrive assured. What diligence forwarded, delight will serve. The feast's readiness proves the invitation's sincerity. Come hungry; leave never.

Verses 19-24 complete mercy's course: cry (joy), confession (certainty), life (pleasant), garden (lofty), fruit (near), feast (served). Six verses, ascending delight — verdict to banquet. Such completeness satisfies: the right-hand company stands fully blessed, nothing lacking. The passage's arc models mercy's fullness — heaven leaves no appetite unmet, no promise unkept. Readers should survey the six-course meal entire: each verse a dish, all served. Satisfaction here previews saturation there. What the passage gave in lines, eternity gives in life. Feast finished; horror follows. The sūrah turns now to the other company — left hands, vain wishes, crushing grip. Joy complete; warning begins.

**Grace Exceeds Wages**

Bi-mā aslaftum speaks wages, but heaven's pay exceeds every earning. Masters who overpay servants model divine generosity — and God names Himself the All-Appreciative, multiplying returns past labor (35:30). Short lives cannot earn eternal feasts by arithmetic; grace bridges the gap. Wage-language dignifies effort (deeds truly merit), while gift-reality magnifies mercy (merit truly gifted). Both truths stand: strive as though earning, receive as though gifted. Readers should work diligently yet hope humbly — wages promised, bounty added. What aslaftum forwarded was little; what hanīʾ serves is infinite. The Employer overpays every faithful servant. Send ahead little; feast forever. Arithmetic surrenders; generosity reigns.

**The Left Hand Steps Forward Next**

Bliss described; ruin follows. Verse 25 will present the left-hand company — records in wrong hands, wishes for unread pages. The sūrah's great contrast pivots here: six verses of delight, then thirteen of dread. Readers should brace for inversion: every joy mirrored into horror. What follows will unmake every comfort just built. The next verse turns to the left-hand company and their cry of regret.

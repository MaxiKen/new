## Sūrah al-Tawbah 9:81

> Those ˹hypocrites˺ who remained behind rejoiced for doing so in defiance of the Messenger of Allah and hated ˹the prospect of˺ striving with their wealth and their lives in the cause of Allah. They said ˹to one another˺, “Do not march forth in the heat.” Say, ˹O Prophet,˺ “The Fire of Hell is far hotter!” If only they could comprehend!

**Expanded Commentary**

**Relief at Disobedience Reveals the Heart**

The hypocrites rejoice because they remained behind. Their joy is not the relief of people who were genuinely unable to travel. It is satisfaction at having avoided a duty and at having opposed the Messenger by avoiding it. The same outward absence can therefore carry different meanings. One person stays through a valid weakness and grieves that he cannot serve; another stays through rebellion and celebrates the escape.

This distinction protects the weak from accusation while exposing the proud. A believer should not pretend that every hardship is pleasant, but he should be concerned if disobedience produces relief and obedience produces resentment. Feelings do not give the whole judgment, yet repeated joy at avoiding Allah's claim is evidence that the heart has chosen comfort as its master.

**They Hated to Spend Both Wealth and Life**

The verse joins wealth and life because sacrifice includes what a person owns and what a person is. The hypocrites dislike both. They want the community's security and benefits, but they do not want to spend resources sustaining it or effort defending it. Their loyalty is therefore consumptive: they receive from belonging while refusing its cost.

A believer examines this pattern in smaller matters. Does he want a healthy community but refuse time to maintain it? Does he praise justice but refuse the money, reputation, or labor required to protect it? Every cause reveals its serious supporters when it requires more than words. Allah does not demand identical gifts from everyone, but He does expose the person who gives nothing while claiming the honor of everything.

**Heat Became an Excuse for a Deeper Refusal**

They tell one another not to march in the heat. Heat is a real hardship, and the verse does not teach that physical conditions should be ignored. The problem is the way a genuine difficulty is used: not as information for wise preparation, but as a slogan for abandoning the duty altogether.

Hardship can be addressed through equipment, pacing, shared resources, and care for the vulnerable. When people use it as a reason to encourage disobedience, the condition becomes a moral excuse. The believer asks how to meet the difficulty rather than allowing the difficulty to decide that obedience is impossible. A community that prepares for hardship makes it harder for fear to become a leader.

**The Fire Reorders Their Calculation**

The Prophet is told to answer that the Fire of Hell is hotter. The reply does not deny the physical heat of the journey. It places the journey in the larger account. The hypocrites calculate a few hours of discomfort; revelation asks them to consider the consequence of choosing rebellion over obedience.

This is how the Hereafter corrects short-sighted calculation. A small sacrifice may feel large when measured against the present body, but it is small beside lasting punishment. The comparison is not permission to seek pain for its own sake. It is a warning not to treat temporary comfort as worth more than faith. The believer uses the future to judge the present, not the present to erase the future.

**Understanding Must Reach Conduct**

The final phrase, “if only they could comprehend,” points to a failure of moral understanding. They know the words and feel the heat, but they do not connect immediate choices to final consequences. Knowledge that never changes action remains a possession of the tongue rather than wisdom of the heart.

The same failure can appear in a believer who hears warnings but keeps protecting a habit. He may understand that wealth is a trust and still refuse to give. He may know that truth is required and still hide behind a convenient story. Comprehension is shown when knowledge begins to govern preference. The servant asks Allah to make the unseen consequence more real than the visible excuse.

**Courage Does Not Require Pretending**

The verse does not call the believer to deny tiredness, heat, or fear. It calls him to refuse their rule over the heart. He can admit the difficulty, seek support, and still move toward what Allah requires. If he is genuinely unable, he tells the truth without shame and serves another way.

The hypocrites chose a different path: they turned the hardship into a reason to celebrate defiance. The believer turns hardship into a reason for reliance, preparation, and prayer. He may not know the result of the effort, but he knows the worth of obeying Allah. That knowledge makes a difficult road bearable without making the servant blind to practical needs.

The heat of the road also exposes the difference between a difficulty and an excuse. A difficulty invites preparation, consultation, and mutual care. An excuse invites people to admire their own refusal. The believers do not need to deny the body in order to obey Allah; they need to prevent the body from becoming the only measure of what is worth doing. A person may rest, share a load, or accept a valid exemption while still honoring the command. What makes the hypocrites blameworthy is their happiness at opposition and their effort to teach others the same attitude. The believer protects his heart from this influence by remembering that every comfort is temporary and every choice is witnessed.

The phrase about the Fire also protects the believer from measuring obedience by mood. A task can be tiring and still be the right task. The servant does not need to enjoy every sacrifice before it counts. He needs to know why he is making it and to seek Allah's help in carrying it. When he shares the difficulty with others, he turns hardship into cooperation rather than complaint. When he faces a valid limitation, he reports it honestly rather than encouraging people to resist. The hypocrites made heat a slogan for defiance. The believers make difficulty a reason for preparation and reliance. Their bodies feel the road, but their hearts do not make comfort the final law.

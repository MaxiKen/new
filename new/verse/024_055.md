## Sūrah an-Nūr 24:55

> Allah has promised those of you who believe and do good that He will certainly make them successors in the land, as He did with those before them; and will surely establish for them their faith which He has chosen for them; and will indeed change their fear into security—˹provided that˺ they worship Me, associating nothing with Me. But whoever disbelieves after this ˹promise˺, it is they who will be the rebellious.

**Expanded Commentary**

**The Tripartite Divine Covenant: waʿada Allāhu alladhīna āmanū**

The revelation unveils a grand historical charter that redefined the geopolitical destiny of Arabia and the world: *waʿada Allāhu alladhīna āmanū minkum wa-ʿamilū al-ṣāliḥāt*. The verb *waʿada* denotes an inviolable divine promise backed by the omnipotence and absolute veracity of the Lord of the worlds.

This promise was revealed at a critical juncture in the early Muslim community's history. For years, the Emigrants (*Muhājirūn*) and Helpers (*Anṣār*) lived under constant military siege, economic boycotts, and existential threats from hostile Arab coalitions and neighboring empires. Companions recounted that they slept with their swords strapped to their sides, waking in dread and wondering if they would ever see a day of complete peace and tranquility. Into this atmosphere of terror, verse 55 descended as a sovereign guarantee, conditioning political empowerment, civilizational stability, and peace not on military size or tribal alliances, but on the bedrock of faith and righteous deeds.

**The Heritage of Vicegerency: la-yastakhlifannahum fī al-arḍ**

The first pillar of the divine promise is *istikhlāf*—civilizational succession and stewardship upon the earth: *la-yastakhlifannahum fī al-arḍ kamā istakhlafa alladhīna min qablihim*. The energetic particle *la* paired with the doubled *nūn* of corroboration (*la-yastakhlifannahum*) communicates absolute certainty.

God guarantees that the persecuted believers will inherit political sovereignty, territorial dominance, and administrative leadership over the land. The historical clause *kamā istakhlafa alladhīna min qablihim* links this triumph to the prophetic continuum, recalling how the oppressed Children of Israel inherited the land of the pharaohs and the tyrants of the Levant after enduring decades of trial. Classical Sunni exegetes, including al-Ṭabarī, al-Qurṭubī, and Ibn Kathīr, observed that this prophecy was fulfilled with miraculous precision through the era of the Rightly Guided Caliphs (*al-khulafāʾ al-rāshidūn*), who established justice from North Africa to Central Asia within decades of the Prophet's death.

**Institutional Consolidation of the Approved Faith: wa-la-yumakkinanna lahum dīnahum**

The second pillar of the promise is political and institutional establishment (*al-tamkīn*): *wa-la-yumakkinanna lahum dīnahumu alladhī irtaḍā lahum*. The faith approved by God (*irtaḍā lahum*)—echoing the proclamation of 5:3—is not destined to remain a marginalized, persecuted minority faith surviving secretly in catacombs.

*Tamkīn* signifies deep institutional rooting, legal supremacy, and societal entrenchment. God promised that the religious way of life—its judicial codes, domestic modesty ordinances, commercial ethics, and spiritual practices articulated throughout Sūrah an-Nūr—would become the dominant, organizing reality of the state. Islamic values would shape law, culture, and governance, allowing citizens to practice monotheism publicly with dignity, honor, and complete societal support, transforming a fragile desert community into a stable, flourishing civilization governed by divine revelation.

**Transformation of Vulnerability into Security: wa-la-yubaddilannahum min baʿdi khawfihim amnā**

The third pillar addresses the psychological and physical trauma of prolonged persecution: *wa-la-yubaddilannahum min baʿdi khawfihim amnā*, and will indeed change their fear into security. The verb *yubaddilanna* denotes total, radical transformation; the dark condition of terror is entirely replaced by tranquil safety (*amn*).

The historical fulfillment of this promise was astonishing. The very companions who once feared assassination in the alleys of Makkah lived to see an era where an elderly woman or a solitary traveler could ride from Ṣanʿāʾ to Ḥaḍramawt in southern Arabia fearing none except God and the wolf for their sheep. Mosques flourished without armed sentries, courts administered justice without fear of aristocratic vengeance, and families built domestic sanctuaries in peace. The constant dread that defined the community's early years was erased, replaced by the profound social and psychological security that accompanies righteous, monotheistic rule.

**The Indispensable Condition: Pure Monotheism Without Shirk**

The divine discourse inserts an indispensable theological condition that governs the entire covenant: *yaʿbudūnanī lā yushrikūna bī shayʾā*, they worship Me, associating nothing with Me. The sentence is grammatically structured as an adverbial condition (*ḥāl*), functioning as the operative clause of the promise.

Empowerment, security, and vicegerency are not unconditional ethnic birthrights or permanent national entitlements; they are direct dividends of pure monotheism (*tawḥīd*) and uncompromised obedience. The moment a Muslim society abandons the singular worship of God, compromises divine law to court worldly popularity, or descends into the practical idolatry of nationalism, wealth, and secular philosophies, the divine protection is withdrawn. Istikhlāf is not a license for imperial domination, exploitation, or chauvinism; it is a sacred trust (*amānah*) to establish divine justice and facilitate the universal worship of the Creator across the earth, honoring the rights of all human beings.

**The Peril of Post-Triumph Ingratitude: fa-man kafara baʿda dhālik**

The verse concludes with an uncompromising, stern eschatological warning: *wa-man kafara baʿda dhālika fa-ulāʾika humu al-fāsiqūn*, but whoever disbelieves after this promise, it is they who will be the rebellious. The term *kafara* here encompasses both doctrinal disbelief and catastrophic ingratitude (*kufrān al-niʿmah*).

To experience the divine transformation from terrified fugitive to prosperous vicegerent—and then turn away from divine law, succumb to arrogance, or indulge in moral corruption—is an unpardonable betrayal of sacred trust. Such ungrateful souls are classified as *al-fāsiqūn*: those who have radically transgressed the boundaries of obedience and fractured their covenant with God. The warning stands as an eternal beacon across Islamic history: civilizational empowerment is sustained solely through humility, justice, and sincere devotion, and any society that abandons these foundations will inevitably lose its vicegerency and reap the bitter fruits of moral decay, societal division, and ultimate ruin.

## Sūrah an-Nūr 24:19

> Indeed, those who love to see indecency spread among the believers will suffer a painful punishment in this life and the Hereafter. Allah knows and you do not know.

**Expanded Commentary**

**The Inner Affection for Corruption: yuḥibbūna an tashīʿa al-fāḥishah**

The divine warning penetrates beyond overt external actions to indict the hidden psychological and emotional motivations of the human heart: *inna alladhīna yuḥibbūna an tashīʿa al-fāḥishatu fī alladhīna āmanū*. The verb *yuḥibbūn* signifies loving, desiring, delighting in, and craving. The Qur'an does not merely condemn those who actively manufacture or broadcast indecency; it condemns those who harbor an internal affection for seeing moral corruption circulate.

The root *sh-y-ʿ* denotes spreading, proliferating, and becoming common public currency. The term *al-fāḥishah* encompasses heinous sexual transgressions, shameless conduct, and the defamatory speech that publicizes such behavior. The verse diagnoses a sinister psychological pathology: individuals who experience subtle gratification when righteous people are brought low, or who take pleasure in seeing moral boundaries dismantled within the community. The divine law treats this malicious internal desire as a major crime deserving severe retribution, holding the heart accountable for its clandestine affinities, dark impulses, and covert malice against the righteous.

**The Dual Realm of Agonizing Torment: lahum ʿadhābun alīm**

The retribution decreed against those who desire the spread of indecency spans both planes of existence: *lahum ʿadhābun alīmun fī al-dunyā wa-al-ākhirah*. The punishment is described as *alīm*—intensely agonizing and piercing. In this earthly life (*fī al-dunyā*), the purveyors of scandal face statutory corporal punishment of eighty lashes, public disgrace, the loss of civil testimony rights, and divine humiliation.

In the everlasting life of the Hereafter (*wa-al-ākhirah*), an even more terrifying torment awaits them in the fires of Hell. The severity of the punishment directly reflects the profound societal damage wrought by their desires. Those who work to normalize indecency or tear down the reputations of the pious strike at the spiritual foundation of the entire community, actively attempting to transform a sanctified society of believers into an open sewer of moral degradation, poisoning the communal wellspring of faith, breeding cynicism, and fracturing the bonds of collective virtue.

**The Prophetic Warning Against Searching for Faults**

The ethical philosophy underpinning this verse is powerfully articulated across authoritative traditions of the Sunnah. In a famous and piercing sermon recorded in Jāmiʿ al-Tirmidhī 2032 and Sunan Abī Dāwūd 4880, the Prophet ﷺ mounted the pulpit and called out with a raised voice: O you who have accepted Islam with your tongues while true faith has not entered your hearts! Do not harm Muslims, do not revile them, and do not track down their private faults.

The Prophet ﷺ then delivered an unforgettable cosmic warning: For whoever seeks out the private faults of his Muslim brother, God will seek out his faults, and whomever God pursues, He will expose and humiliate him, even if he remains in the deepest interior of his own house. This prophetic warning demonstrates that actively seeking, publicizing, and celebrating the moral shortcomings of believers invites immediate divine pursuit and earthly exposure, turning the pursuer's own weapon against his domestic peace, shattering his reputation, and stripping him of all divine concealment.

**The Criminalization of Moral Voyeurism and Public Exposure**

Classical scholars deduced from this verse that the prohibition of *ishāʿat al-fāḥishah* (spreading indecency) applies to both false slander and the public broadcasting of genuine, private moral failures. In the Islamic social paradigm, private sins committed by individuals must remain strictly veiled beneath the divine covering of mercy (*al-satr*).

When a person stumbles into private sin, the required response is personal repentance, private admonition, and concealment. Anyone who actively uncovers, publishes, and sensationalizes that private failure—even if their reporting is factually accurate—violates the spirit of this verse. Publicizing indecency normalizes it in the communal consciousness, desensitizing the public to vice and encouraging others to transgress. The law commands that private sins remain concealed so that public life retains its modesty and reverence for divine limits, preventing the normalization of shamelessness, curbing vulgarity, and protecting societal decency.

**The Epistemological Abyss: Allāhu yaʿlamu wa-antum lā taʿlamūn**

The verse concludes by contrasting human ignorance with divine omniscience: *wa-Allāhu yaʿlamu wa-antum lā taʿlamūn*, Allah knows and you do not know. Human beings routinely possess narrow, short-sighted perceptions regarding the consequences of their actions. People frequently convince themselves that indulging in gossip or sharing salacious news is harmless entertainment, failing to recognize how it corrodes mutual trust and dismantles societal stability.

God, possessing infinite and exhaustive knowledge of human nature and sociological dynamics, understands the lethal poison embedded in unchecked speech and moral voyeurism. The phrase serves as a profound injunction toward intellectual and moral humility. When divine law commands that private sins be veiled and defamatory speech be severely punished, the believer must submit completely, recognizing that divine wisdom perceives dangers entirely invisible to mortal eyes, guarding humanity against unseen spiritual catastrophes, cultural degeneracy, and hidden social collapses.

**Defending the Sacred Canopy of the Faithful**

A final critical detail resides in the phrase *fī alladhīna āmanū*—among those who believe. The community of faith is established by God as an inviolable sanctuary of mutual honor, psychological safety, and collective virtue. Within this sacred precinct, the dignity and reputation of every believer are meant to flourish without fear of malice.

Those who seek to puncture this protective canopy by circulating scandals or normalizing indecency act as cultural saboteurs working to undermine the work of the revelation. By issuing this fierce warning, the Qur'an commands the community to cultivate an active, protective jealousy (*ghayrah*) for the moral integrity of its members, fiercely defending the public square against those who seek to commodify and celebrate vice, ensuring that faith remains a sanctuary of pure light, unassailable safety, spiritual tranquility, and enduring dignity for all who believe.

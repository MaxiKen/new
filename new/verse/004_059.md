## Sūrah al-Nisāʾ 4:59

> O believers! Obey Allah and obey the Messenger and those in authority among you. Should you disagree on anything, then refer it to Allah and His Messenger, if you ˹truly˺ believe in Allah and the Last Day. This is the best and fairest resolution.

**Expanded Commentary**

**Three Obediences, One Root**

The address *yā ayyuhā alladhīna āmanū aṭīʿū Allāha wa aṭīʿū al-rasūla wa ulī al-amri minkum* — O believers, obey Allah and obey the Messenger and those in authority among you — orders obedience in layers. *Aṭīʿū Allāha* — obey God — is the root that appeared as *ḥudūd Allāh* — limits set by God — for inheritance (4:13–14) and as *worship Allah and associate none* (4:36). *Aṭīʿū al-rasūla* — obey the Messenger — repeats the verb *aṭīʿū* rather than subsuming the Messenger under God, stressing that his clarification is obedience itself, as in *“whoever obeys the Messenger has obeyed Allah”* and as in the witness scene where he is brought as witness over his community (4:41). *Ulī al-amri minkum* — those in authority among you — was explained as rulers, judges, scholars, and commanders who execute the law, including the judges who return trusts and judge with fairness (4:58) and the mediators appointed from families (4:35). Their obedience is commanded without a third *aṭīʿū*, signalling that it holds within obedience to God and His Messenger. The sūrah has shown that authority can be misused — belief in *jibt* and *ṭāghūt* and seeking false judges (4:51, 4:60) — so authority is to be obeyed when it stands inside that alignment, not when it commands deviation. The community that learned to hear *“we hear and obey”* rather than *“we hear and disobey”* (4:46) is here told whom to hear.

**Should You Disagree, Refer It to Allah and His Messenger**

The condition *fa in tanāzaʿtum fī shayʾin fa ruddūhu ilā Allāhi wa al-rasūli in kuntum tuʾminūna bi Allāhi wa al-yawmi al-ākhiri* — should you disagree on anything, refer it to Allah and His Messenger, if you truly believe in God and the Last Day — was read as the sūrah’s method for disputes. *Tanāzaʿ* — to dispute, to pull against — covers disagreements about shares, dowries, trade, and judgment where trusts and fairness are at issue (4:58). *Ruddūhu* — refer it, return it — was explained as returning the question to the Book — *to Allah* — and to the prophetic clarification — *to the Messenger* — the two that make things clear (4:26). The clause *in kuntum tuʾminūna* ties referral to faith and to the Last Day where witnesses are brought and no statement can be hidden (4:41–42). To believe in that Day is to want the dispute judged by the standards of that Day. The Qur’an elsewhere says that those who claim belief yet seek judgment of false judges, though commanded to reject them, are led by Satan farther away (4:60); referral to *ṭāghūt* is the opposite of referral here. The sūrah’s earlier promise that God is sufficient as Guardian and Helper (4:45) underwrites the referral: the dispute that is returned to the Book and the Messenger finds guardianship and help.

**The Best and Fairest Resolution**

The closing *dhālika khayrun wa aḥsanu taʾwīlā* — this is the best and fairest resolution — was read as comparing referral to any other arbitrament. *Khayr* — best, good — was explained as best in this world because it keeps limits from being exceeded and trusts from being eaten; *aḥsanu taʾwīlā* — fairest in final interpretation, best in outcome — as best in the next, where the judgment will stand before the All-Hearing, All-Seeing (4:58). The sūrah has shown two resolutions: keeping limits brings Gardens and ultimate triumph (4:13) while exceeding them brings humiliating abiding (4:14); avoiding major sins leads to an honourable entry (4:31) while persisting until death says now I repent is not accepted (4:18). Referral to God and the Messenger is the mechanism that secures the better outcome at the level of dispute. A family that disagrees about a mother’s third or sixth after siblings (4:11), a wife’s quarter or eighth (4:12), or a bondwoman’s fair dowry (4:25) — each finds the *khayr* and *aḥsan* by returning the question to the Book and the Messenger rather than to custom, desire, or a false judge. The phrase also answers the enviers who called disbelievers better guided (4:51); the fairest interpretation is not theirs, but the one that returns to the confirming revelation.

**Obedience That Protects Trusts and Judgments**

Read after *“return trusts to rightful owners and judge with fairness”* (4:58), this verse supplies the authority that makes those commands possible. Trusts are returned and judgments are fair only if the judge has a standard higher than the parties’ desires and the community accepts it. Obedience to God and His Messenger provides that standard; obedience to those in authority among you executes it; referral resolves the remainder. The sūrah’s earlier training in obedience through the witness scene (4:41), the guard of prayer by awareness (4:43), and the sufficiency of God as Helper (4:45) all converge here. To obey and to refer is not a burden added to weakness but a lightening, because *humankind was created weak* (4:28) and the surest way for the weak to avoid devouring wealth by falsehood (4:29) or killing and being burned where *that is easy for God* (4:30) is to have a clear place to bring the disagreement. The verse therefore draws the community’s public life into the same shape as its private worship: as prayer is guarded by knowing what is said, disputes are guarded by knowing where they are referred.

**A Community Whose Disputes Return Upward**

Placed where it is, between the noble exhortation to return trusts (4:58) and the exposure of those who claim belief yet seek false judges (4:60), this verse defines the community that earns the Gardens of 4:57. It is a community that obeys, that honours authority within that obedience, and that, when it disputes — *in tanāzaʿtum fī shayʾin* — any thing — refers it upward. That upward referral is the opposite of the downward deviation that those who follow desires wish for you (4:27) and of the wordplay that takes words from their places (4:46). The sūrah has shown that God never wrongs by an atom but multiplies good and gives a great reward from grace (4:40); to refer disputes to Him and His Messenger is to place those multiplied goods where they will be counted exactly, and to find that the best and fairest outcome is not the one the strongest party could force but the one the All-Hearing, All-Seeing will endorse. The weak for whom the law was lightened can trust that referral, because the One to whom disputes are returned is the One who wills to make things clear, guide to noble ways, and turn toward those who return.

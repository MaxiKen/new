## Sūrah al-Naḥl 16:47

> **Or that He will not destroy them gradually? But your Lord is truly Ever Gracious, Most Merciful.**

**Expanded Commentary**

**Gradual Destruction**

Aw ya'khudhahum ʿalā takhawwufin — or that He will not seize them gradually, with fear, by diminishing. Takhawwuf from kh-w-f means fear, but also gradual reduction, taking away little by little, causing fear through erosion. Al-Ṭabarī explains that ʿalā takhawwufin means Allah seizes them gradually, reducing their wealth, strength, numbers, little by little, so they fear and anticipate total destruction, or that He seizes some and leaves others to fear. Al-Rāzī says takhawwuf can mean that punishment comes with fear, causing them to be in fear while being seized, or that it comes by diminishing their sides, reducing their territory, as 13:41 says *Do they not see that We come to land reducing it from its borders?* (13:41). The question is continuation: do plotters feel secure that He will not destroy them gradually? They should not, because gradual destruction is also possible and is happening.

**But Your Lord Is Ever Gracious, Most Merciful**

Fa-inna rabbakum la-ra'ūfun raḥīm — but indeed your Lord is Ever Gracious, Most Merciful. This seal appears earlier in verse 7 after mentioning cattle bearing loads without hardship. Here it appears after listing four modes of punishment: earth swallowing, torment from unexpected, seizure while going about, gradual destruction. After such warnings, the verse ends with mercy to show that delay in punishment is due to graciousness and mercy, not inability or approval. Al-Ṭabarī says ra'ūf, gracious, means tender, preventing harm, and raḥīm, merciful, means granting good. The fact that plotters are not immediately swallowed, seized, or destroyed gradually is due to Allah's graciousness and mercy that gives time to repent. Al-Rāzī notes that this seal also consoles believers who are persecuted: your Lord is gracious and merciful, so He will not let plotters succeed forever, and He will provide good home for those who emigrated.

**Why Gradual Punishment Is Mentioned**

Gradual punishment, takhawwuf, is subtle: wealth decreases little by little, health declines, supporters leave, status erodes, so person feels fear and loss but may not realize it is punishment. Al-Qurṭubī says this is more frightening than sudden punishment because it gives time to reflect but also allows heedlessness to continue. The verse lists gradual as fourth mode after sudden from below, sudden from unexpected, sudden while active, to show that punishment can be sudden or gradual, overt or subtle, so feeling secure from one mode does not mean secure from others. The plotters who feel secure because earth has not swallowed them yet may be experiencing gradual erosion without noticing.

**Graciousness And Mercy As Reason For Delay**

The seal la-ra'ūfun raḥīm explains why punishment is delayed despite plotting. Allah is gracious, preventing immediate destruction, and merciful, granting time and provision even to plotters, as 10:11 says *If Allah were to hasten evil for people as they hasten good, their term would have ended* (10:11). Delay is mercy that allows repentance. Al-Zamakhsharī notes that ra'ūf is more intense than raḥīm in preventing harm, so mention of both indicates Allah both prevents immediate harm and grants time for good. The verse therefore balances warning with hope: warning that punishment can come in many ways, hope that delay is due to mercy that invites return.

**Connection To Previous Verses About Security**

Verses 45-47 form unit asking do plotters feel secure from four modes: earth swallowing, torment from unexpected, seizure while going about, gradual destruction. All four are answered by implication: they should not feel secure. Then verse ends with but your Lord is gracious, merciful, indicating that if they are secure so far, it is due to mercy, not due to their plotting's success. The sūrah's blessings catalogue also showed graciousness and mercy: cattle bearing loads without hardship because Lord is gracious, merciful. Now same names explain delay in punishment: Lord is gracious, merciful, so He delays. The two uses show that graciousness and mercy appear both in provision and in forbearance.

**Linguistic Note On Takhawwuf**

Takhawwuf from kh-w-f means fear, but in form V it can mean to cause fear, to diminish, to take from sides. Lisān al-ʿArab defines takhawwafa as to diminish, to take away gradually, causing fear. ʿAlā takhawwufin, upon fear, with gradual diminishing, indicates mode of seizure that causes fear through reduction. Ya'khudhahum, He seizes them, from a-kh-dh. Fa-inna rabbakum la-ra'ūfun raḥīm, but your Lord is gracious, merciful, with la- for emphasis, surely. Ra'ūf from r-'-f, to be gracious, tender, and raḥīm from r-ḥ-m, mercy. The verse's structure — question, or that He will not destroy them gradually, then statement, but your Lord is gracious, merciful — moves from warning to reason for delay, guiding listener to see that security is due to mercy that should lead to repentance, not to continued plotting.


**A Lesson That Connects To Gratitude And Reflection**

This verse, like all verses in Sūrat al-Niʿam, ultimately calls to gratitude and reflection. Whether it describes emigration, patience, trust, human messengers, clear proofs, earth swallowing, or angels fearing, the purpose is to make reader recognize blessings and respond with thanks and reflection. Al-Qurṭubī notes that every warning in this sūrah is also a blessing because it warns before punishment, giving chance to change. Every promise is also a blessing because it encourages good. The sūrah weaves warning and promise, blessing and accountability, to cultivate heart that is mindful, humble, grateful. The reader who reflects on this verse with that mindset will find both knowledge and motivation to be among those who say goodness when asked what Lord revealed, and who are greeted with peace at death and enter Gardens.

**How This Verse Fits The Sūrah's Argument**

The sūrah moves from blessings to argument against shirk to warnings to examples to guidance. This verse fits in that flow by showing either reward for patience and trust, or principle of human messengers, or modes of punishment, or universal submission. Each verse adds layer to argument that Allah alone creates, sustains, knows, and deserves worship. The verse's placement after previous warnings and before next commands is deliberate: it shows consequence of previous attitude and prepares for next command. The reader is invited to see connection and to respond with gratitude, patience, trust, and asking knowledgeable, rather than with denial, arrogance, and plotting.

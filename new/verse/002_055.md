## Sūrah al-Baqarah 2:55

> And ˹remember˺ when you said, “O Moses! We will never believe you until we see Allah with our own eyes,” so a thunderbolt struck you while you were looking on.

**Expanded Commentary**

**From Calf to Demand — The Next Breach After Pardon**

“And remember when you said, ‘O Moses! We will never believe you until we see Allah with our own eyes,’ so a thunderbolt struck you while you were looking on” (2:55). Wa idh qultum yā Mūsā lan nuʾmina laka ḥattā narā Allāha jahratan — “we will not believe you until we see God openly.” The demand follows calf-worship, pardon, Book and Criterion, and commanded repentance. The sequence shows a people not yet steadied by what they had seen: after watching the sea part before eyes (2:50), they ask to see Allah before eyes. Sight given becomes demand for more sight, and demand refused becomes stroke.

Fa-akhadhatkumu al-ṣāʿiqatu wa antum tanẓurūn — a thunderbolt seized you while you were looking on. The very eyes that demanded to see openly are named as looking on when seized. The punishment matches the demand in organ: the eyes that insisted become witnesses of the consequence.

**“We Will Never Believe You” — The Absolute That Invites the Absolute**

Lan nuʾmina laka — “we will never believe you” — is absolute future negation. It is not “we find belief hard,” but “we will not believe you” until a condition is met. The condition — ḥattā narā Allāha jahratan — is glossed by the source as to see with eyes, or simply publicly, manifestly. Jahrah carries both: open, manifest, with eyes. The demand is to make the unseen into spectacle as price of assent. The sūrah’s ethic of certainty — to be certain you will meet your Lord (2:46) without demanding to see Him openly now — is here inverted: certainty is refused unless sight is forced.

The source notes the parallel in 4:153 where the same demand is recalled, and links the thunderbolt (ṣāʿiqah) etymologically to Moses’ swoon at the theophany (7:143) and to the swoon at the Trumpet (39:68). The same root gathers mountain, prophet, and people in one vocabulary of overwhelming.

**The Thunderbolt — What Ṣāʿiqah Means**

Ṣāʿiqah is broader than meteorological thunder alone. The source lists the range — great cry, fire, earthquake, overwhelming blast — and distinguishes it from rajfah, the trembling/ quake of 7:155, though many commentators see 2:55 and 7:155 as the same event seen from two angles. The Qur’an also uses ṣāʿiqah for ʿĀd and Thamūd (41:13), where it seized them as they looked on — the same clause “as they looked on” sealing their punishment. The sūrah’s “while you were looking on” (2:55) thus places Israel’s demand-punishment in the company of peoples who demanded and were seized.

Whether 2:55 and 7:155 refer to one incident — the seventy elite taken with Moses after the calf, who demanded sight and were struck and then revived by Moses’ prayer — or to two distinct incidents (JJ), the source records that opinions differ and some take no firm position (R). The identification with the seventy should therefore be marked UNVERIFIED as definitive prophetic fact; what is firm is the demand, the thunderbolt, and the eyewitness clause.

**Why Demand Sight After Sight Given?**

The sūrah has already made sight a theme: the sea was watched (2:50), the calf was taken while wrongdoing yet sighted, the Book given as furqān to distinguish (2:53). To demand to see Allah jahratan after those sights is to mistake the kind of seeing that faith requires. The Qur’an grants sights that guide — parted waters, Book, criterion — without granting modality of seeing the Essence as spectacle. The demand collapses “to be certain you will meet your Lord” (2:46) into “to see Him now openly,” conflating appointed meeting with immediate display.

**The Sequel Not Yet Told — But Hinted**

The sūrah will continue in the next verses with reviving after death — “then We raised you after your death, that you may be grateful” (2:56) — showing that even this seizure is not final word. As with the calf, pardon follows grave breach. The thunderbolt is real; the raising after is also real. The recital keeps both together so that awe and hope are taught together.

**Living the Verse**

- Do not condition belief on spectacle. The sūrah trains a different condition: “when guidance comes from Me, whoever follows it” (2:38) — not “when sight is forced.” The humble certain of meeting (2:46) wait for liqāʾ; the demanding make meeting a test for God.
- Let “while you were looking on” (2:55) train sight. Eyes are given to watch deliverance and distinguish with furqān, not to demand Essence as proof. The same eyes that watch a sea part (2:50) should not later say “we will not believe until we see Allah” (2:55).
- Remember the sequence as warning for communities that have been delivered and pardoned: deliverance does not immunize against new demands, and pardon does not license new spectacle. Gratitude (2:52, 2:56) is the intended fruit after each rescue.

**What Not to Claim**

Do not present the seventy elite, their selection, or the precise mountain location as established prophetic narration from this verse alone; the Qur’an here states only the saying, the demand for open sight, the thunderbolt, and the eyewitness circumstance (2:55). Details linking to 7:155 should be cited as tafsīr inference, UNVERIFIED in exact form. What is firm is the demand’s wording, its absolute refusal — “we will never believe you until…” — and the consequence: a thunderbolt that seized them while they looked on (2:55).

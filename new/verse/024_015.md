## Sūrah an-Nūr 24:15

> when you passed it from one tongue to the other, and said with your mouths what you had no knowledge of, taking it lightly while it is ˹extremely˺ serious in the sight of Allah.

**Expanded Commentary**

**The Disconnection Between Tongue and Mind: talaqqawnahū bi-alsinatikum**

The verse provides an astonishing psychological diagnosis of the spread of rumor: *idh talaqqawnahū bi-alsinatikum*. In normal human discourse, speech originates in the intellect, is filtered by the moral conscience, and is only then articulated by the physical vocal apparatus. Here, the Qur'an portrays the rumor moving directly from tongue to tongue, completely bypassing the cognitive faculties and moral conscience of the speakers.

The people received the slander directly onto their tongues and immediately flicked it forward to the next listener. They functioned as unthinking mechanical conduits, allowing sensational gossip to pass through them without pausing to question its logic, examine its source, or evaluate its moral consequence. By isolating the tongue as the sole active organ, the revelation exposes the tragic abdication of human intellect that occurs during moments of mass social frenzy, reducing speech to an involuntary physical reflex that leaves devastation in its wake and strips humanity of its rational dignity.

**Alternative Readings: talaqqawna versus taliqūna**

Linguistic analysis of the central verb reveals deep layers of meaning across the classical readings. The standard canonical reading *talaqqawnahū* derives from the root *l-q-y*, signifying the eager reception and rapid handover of an object from one person to another, capturing the swift transmission of rumors across private gatherings.

A celebrated alternative reading transmitted from ʿĀʾishah herself vocalizes the verb as *taliqūnahū* from the root *w-l-q*. In classical Arabic lexicography, *al-walq* denotes a rapid, darting strike, like a snake striking its prey, or a needle penetrating fabric in continuous, unbroken succession. In this reading, the verse describes people darting forward with their tongues, driving sharp verbal blades into the honor of the innocent with reckless speed. Both vocalizations capture the lethal velocity with which gossip spreads when restraint is abandoned, turning the tongue into a destructive weapon that inflicts deep psychic wounds upon the community and poisons the social well.

**Empty Mouths and Baseless Speech**

The text emphasizes the absolute empirical void behind their words: *wa-taqūlūna bi-afwāhikum mā laysa lakum bihī ʿilm*, and you said with your mouths that of which you had no knowledge. The phrase *bi-afwāhikum* (with your mouths) is not redundant; it emphasizes that the words were mere acoustic vibrations produced by oral cavities, possessing zero reality, substance, or factual evidence behind them.

In the epistemology of the Qur'an, speech that lacks knowledge (*ʿilm*) is an abomination. God explicitly commands in 17:36: *“Do not pursue that of which you have no knowledge”* (17:36). To articulate claims concerning an individual's private life without verified eyewitness knowledge is to commit intellectual and spiritual fraud. The verse strips away all excuses: repeating what someone else claimed does not grant the speaker knowledge; it merely transforms the speaker into an active agent of falsehood, disseminating unverified claims without moral justification or factual foundation, compounding personal guilt.

**The Dangerous Trivialization of Moral Transgression**

The verse diagnoses the moral delusion that facilitates the survival of gossip: *wa-taḥsabūnahū hayyinā*, taking it lightly and deeming it a trivial matter. Slanderers and casual gossips routinely rationalize their behavior, minimizing their personal guilt by claiming they were merely repeating a funny anecdote, passing the time, or discussing what was already common knowledge in the streets.

Human beings possess an extraordinary capacity to trivialize verbal sins. Because speaking does not involve physical assault, bloodshed, or property theft, people deceive themselves into believing that words carry little consequence. The Qur'an shatters this delusion, exposing the moral blindness that treats the demolition of human honor as light amusement. The casual ease with which a rumor rolls off the tongue conceals the immense spiritual rot accumulating in the speaker's soul, numbing the conscience to profound evil and blinding the heart to divine retribution in this life and the next.

**The Cosmic Weight of Honor: wa-huwa ʿinda Allāhi ʿaẓīm**

The verse delivers a breathtaking theological confrontation: *wa-huwa ʿinda Allāhi ʿaẓīm*, while it is, in the sight of Allah, tremendous and grave. The sharp contrast between human flippancy (*hayyin*) and divine gravity (*ʿaẓīm*) forms the emotional and ethical core of the passage. What mortals dismiss as harmless idle chatter, the Sovereign of the universe weighs on the scale of cosmic justice as an atrocity.

In the sight of God, the reputation and dignity of a believing soul are sacred. Classical traditions record that the Prophet ﷺ once looked upon the Kaʿbah and remarked that while its sanctity is immense, the sanctity of a believer's honor, blood, and property is even greater in the sight of God. To casually assault that sacred honor through unverified rumors is to commit an offense that shakes the moral order of creation, inviting divine wrath upon all who participated in its trivialization, demonstrating that human measures of sin are profoundly defective when measured against the divine standard of absolute justice.

**The Psychology of Verbal Contagion**

Reflecting upon the perennial human condition, this verse serves as a timeless critique of how information is consumed and transmitted in society. Rumors and defamatory campaigns derive their power not from the credibility of their creators, but from the uncritical complicity of average consumers who pass them along without reflection.

By dissecting the precise anatomy of verbal contagion—the mindless tongue, the empty mouth, the trivializing attitude—the revelation teaches believers to cultivate intellectual vigilance and moral hesitation. The true servant of God refuses to become an echo chamber for unproven claims. Every report must be arrested at the border of the mind, interrogated for evidence, and evaluated for moral harm before the tongue is granted permission to speak, ensuring that speech remains an instrument of truth, healing, and righteousness in the world, guarding the communal sanctuary.

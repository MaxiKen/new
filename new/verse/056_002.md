## Sūrah al-Wāqiʿah 56:2

> then no one can deny it has come.

**Expanded Commentary**

**Denial Ends When Reality Arrives**

This verse explains the force of the Event named in the opening verse. When it occurs, no one will be able to deny its coming. The statement does not mean that every person will become righteous or repent at that moment. It means that the reality will be so manifest that argument will collapse. The denial that seemed possible in the world will become impossible in the presence of what has arrived.

The distinction is important. People may reject a truth because they lack evidence, because they distrust the messenger, or because accepting it would require a change they dislike. The Last Day will remove all these excuses at once. What was formerly received through revelation will then be encountered directly. But direct encounter after the period of testing has ended is not the same as faith. Faith has value precisely because it responds to truth before the unseen becomes visible.

**The Grammar Reinforces Certainty**

The Arabic wording repeats the idea of occurrence: the Event will occur, and its occurrence will not be denied. Repetition here does not add a second event. It closes the door on evasive interpretation. The same root that named the happening in verse 1 returns to say that its happening cannot be rejected. The sūrah therefore begins with certainty at both ends: the Event is real, and the denial of it will fail.

This kind of repetition is common in serious warnings. A judge who says that a sentence will be carried out is making a claim; a judge who adds that there will be no cancellation makes the consequence clearer. The Qur’anic form is more powerful because it does not depend on human authority. God announces a future that is already fixed in His knowledge. The hearer is not being invited to calculate probabilities but to decide whether to live in accordance with the announced truth.

**Present Denial Has Several Forms**

Denial is not limited to saying, “There will be no resurrection.” A person may deny the Event by treating accountability as irrelevant, even while avoiding an explicit statement of disbelief. Someone who uses power as though no judgment will follow is living as a denier. Someone who consumes another person’s rights and assumes that death will erase the matter is acting on the same false premise. The tongue may remain cautious while the life declares that no final account exists.

The verse also challenges selective belief. A person may accept that God created the world but refuse the conclusion that creation has a purpose and an end. Another may admit that death is certain but treat resurrection as too difficult for divine power. Yet the Event is not separated from the Creator’s sovereignty. The One who brings being from nothing is not constrained by the processes through which human beings are accustomed to seeing life arise. Denial often reveals a narrow measure of power rather than a lack of evidence.

**The Delay Before the Event Is Mercy**

Because no one can deny the Event once it arrives, the present interval is precious. God allows people to respond while the signs can still be received through trust, reflection, and obedience. The unseen is not hidden to mock humanity; it is hidden so that moral choice remains meaningful. If the final scene were permanently displayed before every eye, submission would be forced by sight rather than chosen through faith.

This delay must not be mistaken for cancellation. The continued existence of the world is not proof that judgment will not come. It is the space in which wrongs can be repaired and hearts can turn. The Qur’an’s warnings are therefore acts of mercy. They inform the person before the consequence becomes unavoidable. A warning that arrives before disaster gives the listener a chance to change; a warning ignored does not become false because the disaster was delayed.

**Living Before Denial Becomes Impossible**

The verse asks the reader to examine the difference between intellectual uncertainty and moral refusal. Honest questions can be part of a search for truth. But refusing to act on clear guidance because obedience is inconvenient is another matter. The Event will settle every dispute, yet the wise person does not wait for that settlement before ordering his life. He treats the announcement as sufficient evidence because he trusts the One who announces it.

This produces neither gloom nor withdrawal. It produces seriousness, patience, and proportion. A believer can enjoy lawful blessings without making them his final hope. He can suffer loss without thinking that the loss is the last word. He can forgive when forgiveness is right because he knows that justice has not disappeared. The Event gives present choices an enduring horizon. When it arrives, denial will be impossible; before it arrives, acceptance can still become a free and faithful act. That interval is also the place where sincerity is tested. A person can repeat the language of belief because it is socially expected, or he can let belief govern the choices that no audience sees. The second kind of acceptance is harder and more valuable. It does not wait for the Event to force recognition. It treats the warning as a call to return, restore rights, and make peace with God while return remains possible. It also gives courage to anyone whose good is hidden. The final unveiling means that unnoticed prayer, restrained anger, and private generosity are not lost simply because people did not praise them. The verse thus joins warning to hope: hidden truth will be exposed, but hidden goodness will be exposed as well. The warning is therefore a gift.

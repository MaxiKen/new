## Sūrah al-Baqarah 2:36

> But Satan deceived them—leading to their fall from the ˹blissful˺ state they were in, and We said, “Descend from the heavens ˹to the earth˺ as enemies to each other. You will find in the earth a residence and provision for your appointed stay.”

**Expanded Commentary**

**Azallahumā: He Made Them Slip**

The verse's first verb is exact about agency: *fa-azallahumā al-shayṭān* — Satan caused them both to slip, using the same root the English metaphor "to fall" shares with the Arabic — a foot losing purchase, a slip into error. The verb form is causative, not creative: he produced the slipping; he did not create the fall. The Qur'an never grants Iblīs power over human choice — his own later confession in the Book is that he had no authority except the call (14:22's declaration, paraphrased in his speech on the Day). What he owns is the invitation; what the couple owns is the step. The verse holds both truths in one clause without letting either dissolve the other: the enemy's action was real — he *made them slip*, an achievement the text does not soften into mere suggestion — and the responsibility remains theirs, which is why the descent-command that follows is addressed to them and not to their tempter. Every reader inherits the same two facts about every sin: it was worked for, and it was chosen.

**How the Deception Worked: Selling Certainty**

The mechanics of the slip are supplied by the parallel accounts, and they show an attack on the one vulnerability the charter of verse 35 had left open. The Garden offered everything except guaranteed continuation — and Satan sold exactly that: *"O Adam! Shall I show you the Tree of Immortality and a kingdom that does not fade away?"* (20:120). The offer targeted not appetite but fear of loss, and al-Rāzī's observation sharpens it: a promise of eternal life is tempting only to someone whose eternity feels contingent — which is also one of his arguments that the garden of trial was not yet the Garden of Reward, where permanence is the floor and no such bait could work. Ṭā Hā adds the second instrument: he swore to them that he was their sincere adviser (20:119 — the oath recorded in al-Aʿrāf and Ṭā Hā). The deception thus ran on two rails — a guarantee against the terror of ending, and a sworn claim of goodwill — and both rails are still in service in every temptation since.

**“Descend, All of You”: The Plural and the Enmity**

The command *ihbiṭū* is plural, and the exegetes read the addressees as Adam, his wife, and their progeny — sometimes with Iblīs and his progeny included — the couple standing as representatives of the whole company. The descent is thus not a private exile but the installation of a species and its enemy on one ground, and the command legislates the terms: *as enemies to each other*. The enmity is not an accident of the fall but part of the charter of the earth — announced, expected, and built into the arrangement. Ṭā Hā carries the same formula: *"Descend, both of you, from here together ˹with Satan˺ as enemies to each other"* (20:123). Readers of the verse are thereby told something about their own residence: the war declared in verse 34 did not end at the expulsion; it was given a new theater with a permanent garrison. To live on earth is to live inside a declared enmity, and the guidance promised in verse 38 is the supply line for the side that keeps the covenant.

**A Residence and Provision — With a Clock**

The earth's description is two nouns and a deadline: *mustaqarr* — a dwelling place, a settled station — and *matāʿ* — provision, enjoyment, furnished means; both *ilā ḥīn*, until a time. The commentators range over the dwelling: the earth as abode, in the sense of 40:64 — *"It is Allah Who made the earth a place of settlement for you and the sky a canopy"* (40:64) — or the graves as the true *mustaqarr* of the body. The readings can stand together, since every dwelling before the grave is provisional and the grave itself is a station on the way. The deadline is read as death, or the Resurrection, or an appointed term known only to Allah. The effect of the clause on the whole story is to make the earth a furnished leasehold: comfort is real — provision is promised in the same breath as the enmity — and comfort is terminal. The verse that expelled humanity from a garden also outfitted the exile: residence, rations, a clock, and an enemy. Three of the four are mercies; the fourth is the test.

**No Inherited Guilt: What the Verse Refuses to Say**

One doctrine is conspicuous by its absence, and the absence is load-bearing. The Qur'anic fall is not tied to original sin: nothing in this verse or its sequel transmits the slip as a stain on the descendants. What the children of Adam inherit from the garden is the situation — the earth, the enmity, the guidance to come, the clock — and not the guilt. The guilt was personal, the repentance was personal, and the acceptance of verse 37 will cancel it at the source before the descent is even narrated to its end. Islam's account of the human condition therefore starts elsewhere: not from an inherited corruption needing ontological repair, but from weakness (*ḍaʿf*), forgetfulness, and an exposed position in an enemy's theater — conditions treated with law, reminder, and repentance rather than with atonement. The difference explains much of the two religions' divergent vocabularies: where one tradition speaks of washing an inherited stain, this verse speaks of guarding a rented house against a declared adversary, with forgiveness available on demand for every breach.

**An Expulsion That Was Also an Installation**

Read back through verse 30, the descent changes character. The original announcement was never "I am placing a vicegerent in the Garden" but *"I am placing a successive ˹human˺ authority on earth"* — the earth named as the office's seat before the man existed. The garden residence and the slip and the descent were all, on this reading, stages in a single appointment: the vicegerent was educated, tested, forgiven, and then installed at his post, with his adversary also on site and his guidance also en route (2:38). The angels' question of verse 30 receives its fullest answer here — the corruption and blood they feared are real possibilities of the office, but the office was never designed for the innocent; it was designed for the repentant, for creatures who slip and receive words. The verse is thus an expulsion only in its grammar. In its function inside the sūrah it is an induction: humanity enters the earth not as a convict but as a posted deputy, briefed on the enemy, provisioned for the term, and expected at every fall to do what Adam is about to be shown doing — take the words and turn.

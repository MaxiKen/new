## Sūrah al-Baqarah 2:36

> But Satan deceived them—leading to their fall from the ˹blissful˺ state they were in, and We said, “Descend from the heavens ˹to the earth˺ as enemies to each other. You will find in the earth a residence and provision for your appointed stay.”

**Expanded Commentary**

**From Approach to Slip — How “Do Not Come Near” Was Crossed**

“But Satan deceived them — leading to their fall from the blissful state they were in” (2:36). Fa-azallahumā al-shayṭān picks up the previous “do not approach this tree” (2:35): the one exception becomes the point of entry. Azalla — to cause to slip — renders both feet and judgment. The slip is not portrayed as a compulsion that removes agency; it is a deception that succeeds, and then a fall (akharajahumā) from what they were in — a life of ease, covering, and unimpeded eating. The sūrah names the agent — Satan — and the consequence — expulsion from that dwelling — without dwelling on the mechanism of whispering, lest the account become folklore about a snake or a shape. What is firm is that the prohibition was clear, the deceit was real, and the slip was theirs.

The verse keeps both spouses together: azallahumā, akharajahumā — dual pronouns. The dual undercuts later tellings that isolate blame upon one. Both were addressed, both were deceived, both slipped.

**“Descend” — To Whom Is the Command Spoken?**

“and We said, ‘Descend from the heavens to the earth as enemies to each other’” (2:36). Ihbiṭū is plural, and its addressees are counted in the source as three possibilities: Adam and Eve together with their progeny represented in them; Adam, Eve, and Iblīs together with his progeny; or humanity as a whole spoken to through its parents. The reading most anchored in the sūrah’s address sees Adam and Eve as representatives: what is said to them is said to their seed. Humanity descends in its parents, and enmity descends as a condition of the new abode: baʿḍukum li-baʿḍin ʿaduww — some of you an enemy to others.

That enmity is not a mandate to hatred but a description of the trial terrain: desires will contend, claims will collide, and Satan — already named deceiver — will work within those fractures. The descent therefore establishes both geography and sociology: earth as dwelling, and coexistence as test.

**Mustaqarr and Matāʿ — Residence and Provision for a Term**

“You will find in the earth a residence and provision for your appointed stay” (2:36). Mustaqarr is an abiding-place, rendered here as residence; matāʿ is provision, enjoyment, utility. The source notes that mustaqarr has been read as the earth as abode generally (as with qarār in 40:64) and, by some, as graves in particular, but Qur’anic usage elsewhere — “a lodging and a repository” (6:98), “a station” (25:24), the sun’s appointed course (36:38) — favors the first: earth as settled dwelling. The “provision for a while” — ilā ḥīn — is glossed as until an appointed time (ajal), whether personal death or the appointed term of worldly dwelling.

The descent is thus not into abandonment. The same One who prepared the earth “for you” (2:29) and furnished the Garden with broad permission (2:35) now prepares the earth as residence with provision. What was given freely above is now given measurably below, for a term. The bounty contracts to a stewardship, not to a void.

**Not Original Sin — Fall Without Inheritance of Guilt**

The source stresses that this fall is not tied to “original sin” as Islam does not affirm it. The sūrah will show immediate relenting after words of return are received (2:37), and the Qur’an elsewhere says “We have wronged ourselves” (7:23) as a shared confession, not as a transmitted stain. Guilt is not bequeathed as a nature; responsibility is taught, sought, and turned to. The descent is consequence and re-stationing for khilāfah, not a curse that makes every child a sinner at birth.

**Why Earth, and Why Now?**

Read against 2:30 — “I am going to place a successive authority on earth” — the descent fulfills what was announced. The khalīfah was always for earth. The Garden dwelling (2:35) rehearsed in miniature the condition for earthly trust — broad permission, one limit, named consequence — and the slip displays why guidance will be needed on earth as it was needed in the Garden: a deceiver who beautifies approach until approach becomes taking.

The earth as residence with provision for a term therefore frames the rest of the sūrah: law will be given to order residence, provision will be regulated so it does not become corruption (2:27), and guidance will be sent so enmity does not become unending war.

**Living the Descent**

- The boundary is before the fall. “Do not approach” (2:35) fails at approach before it fails at eating. Reform therefore fences approach, not only act.
- The plural “descend” reminds that every human arrives already in a descending history. Humility is not performance; it is accurate placement.
- Residence and provision are coupled. To seek residence without acknowledging provision as measured for a term is to build as if the ḥīn were forever. To seek provision without residence as trust is to consume as if there were no mustaqarr to keep.

**What Not to Transmit as Fact**

Speculations about Iblīs’s form in the Garden, the species of the tree, or the map coordinates of landing places are popular reports, not prophetic narration. They should be rated UNVERIFIED. What is firm is the slip by deception, the expulsion from the state of bliss, the plural descent as representatives, enmity as earthly condition, and earth as residence with provision for an appointed stay (2:36).

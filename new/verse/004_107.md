## Sūrah al-Nisāʾ 4:107

> Do not advocate for those who wrong themselves. Surely Allah does not like those who are deceitful, sinful.

**Expanded Commentary**

**Do Not Advocate for Those Who Wrong Themselves**

The verse states *wa lā tujādil ʿani alladhīna yakhtānūna anfusahum* — and do not plead, do not advocate, dispute for those who betray, deceive themselves. *Tujādil* — to argue, to plead, to dispute — was explained as standing as *khaṣīm* — disputant, advocate — the same prohibition just given to the Prophet ﷺ not to be advocate for the deceitful (4:105). Here the address widens to the community that was advocating for them: tribal kin who pleaded for the accused thief before evidence was shown. *Alladhīna yakhtānūna anfusahum* — those who betray, wrong themselves — was read as that betrayal of another is betrayal of the self, as the sūrah has shown that those who consume orphans’ wealth consume fire into bellies (4:10) and that whoever earns sin earns it only against himself (4:111). To betray the community’s trust by theft and then conceal it is to wrong the self before wronging the other, because the Book was sent to judge by what God shows (4:105) and hiding from Him by night displeases Him (4:108). The prohibition therefore forbids advocacy for self-wrongers even where kinship pulls, as the sūrah earlier forbade taking the hostile who wish you alike as allies (4:89) and warned not to say to a bearer of peace “you are no believer” for gain (4:94): ties do not convert wrong into right.

**Surely God Does Not Like the Deceitful Sinful**

The reason *inna Allāha lā yuḥibbu man kāna khawwānan athīmā* — surely God does not like, does not love, every deceitful, treacherous, sinful one — was explained with two intensives: *khawwān* — much betraying, repeatedly treacherous — and *athīm* — much sinning, burdened with sin. Both were read as persistent traits, not a single slip; the sūrah has pardoned error soon after with repentance (4:17) and accepted repentance for mistake by freeing or fasting (4:92), but *khawwān athīm* names the one who plots by night what He displeases (4:108) and hides it from people. *Lā yuḥibbu* — does not like, does not love — was explained as removal of divine love and support, as the sūrah said He does not like the arrogant boastful who withhold bounty (4:36–37) and said He will prepare humiliation for disbelievers where the guarded prayer stands (4:102). To be not loved while being *khawwān athīm* means no advocacy can bring love where treachery remains. The phrase was also linked to *“God does not wrong even an atom”* (4:40): His not loving the treacherous is not wrong; it is the accurate measure of treachery that chose to betray itself before betraying others.

**A Verse That Generalises the Advocate Warning**

Placed after *“do not be advocate for the deceitful”* to the Prophet ﷺ (4:105) and the insertion *“seek forgiveness”* (4:106), this verse generalises to every advocate: do not plead for those who wrong themselves. The sūrah has shown that advocates were pleading for the accused before evidence was seen, as the community was divided into two groups about hypocrites (4:88) and as reports are to be referred to the Messenger and those in authority for validation (4:83). The generalisation shows that the law is not personal: no one — not the Prophet as judge, not the tribe as pleader — may make treachery lovable by advocacy. The sūrah’s ethic of returning trusts and judging fairly as noble command from the All-Hearing, All-Seeing (4:58), and of verifying before fighting (4:94), here becomes ethic of arguing: do not dispute for the self-betrayer, for the Disliked — *lā yuḥibbu* — will not be made loved by dispute. The verse was read as closing clan advocacy where theft was at stake: the armour stolen and hidden with a Jew was not made clean by the Muslim’s clan pleading, because the self had already been betrayed by hiding.

**Advocacy Does Not Change the Act’s Owner**

The phrase *yakhtānūna anfusahum* was stressed: they wrong themselves. The sūrah will say that whoever commits sin does so only to his own loss (4:111) and whoever earns evil or sin then throws it upon the innocent bears slander and blatant sin (4:112). To advocate for them is therefore to carry what is not yours: the sin remains on the self-wronger, and the advocate earns only argument without merit. The sūrah has said that lawful gain by mutual consent is permitted (4:29) and that usurping orphans’ property is fire (4:10); here the usurpation is argued away, but the owner — the self — remains. Where the sūrah elsewhere commands to stand firm for justice though against yourselves or parents (4:135), this verse applies that standing to speech: do not lend your tongue to the one who wronged himself, for your tongue will not transfer the wrong. The next verse will show why hiding will not help: they hide from people but cannot hide from God in whose presence they plot by night (4:108), and advocacy in that presence is not possible.

**A Short Verse That Anchors Love and Hate in Act**

Though only *“do not advocate for self-wrongers, God does not like the deceitful sinner,”* the verse anchors divine love and hate in act, not in lineage. The sūrah that opened from a single soul (4:1) and that promised ranks, forgiveness, and mercy for strivers (4:96) here says love is not tribal where deceit is present. The emigrant who finds many havens (4:100) does so not by clan advocacy but by hijrah to God; the helpless hoped to be pardoned (4:99) are not *khawwān athīm* but helpless; the advocate who pleads for them in this life will be asked who will plead before God on Resurrection Day (4:109). For the weak this is protective: where you were asked to advocate for kin, the verse relieves you — do not — and where you are the self-wronger, the door is not closed, for whoever commits evil then seeks forgiveness finds Him All-Forgiving, Most Merciful (4:110).

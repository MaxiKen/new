## Sūrah al-Nisāʾ 4:107

> Do not advocate for those who wrong themselves. Surely Allah does not like those who are deceitful, sinful.

### Expanded Commentary

**Do not defend self-inflicted wrongdoing**

The verse prohibits advocating for those who wrong themselves and says Allah does not love the deceitful and sinful. “Wrong themselves” does not mean that a sinner loses all human rights. It means the advocate must not turn wrongdoing into a cause for triumph. The accused can receive a fair hearing, but falsehood cannot be sanctified in the name of loyalty.

The verse also rejects the habit of treating clever legal manoeuvres as moral success. A person may escape a penalty through technical weakness in evidence, yet remain responsible before Allah. This is why legal justice requires both procedural fairness and conscience. The community should protect due process without celebrating the harm that the process could not prove.

**Due process without deceit**

An accused person deserves counsel, evidence, and protection from mob judgment. But counsel must not manufacture an innocent victim or make a known lie the centre of the defence. Justice can be procedural and morally serious at the same time; the verse rejects both vigilantism and corrupt advocacy.

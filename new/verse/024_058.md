## Sūrah an-Nūr 24:58

> O believers! Let those ˹bondspeople˺ in your possession and those of you who are still under age ask for your permission ˹to come in˺ at three times: before dawn prayer, when you take off your ˹outer˺ clothes at noon, and after the late evening prayer. ˹These are˺ three times of privacy for you. Other than these times, there is no blame on you or them to move freely, attending to one another. This is how Allah makes the revelations clear to you, for Allah is All-Knowing, All-Wise.

**Expanded Commentary**

**Sanctifying the Domestic Sanctuary: Domestic Istiʾdhān**

The divine legislation returns from geopolitical covenants (vv. 55–57) to the micro-ethics of the domestic household, demonstrating the comprehensive scope of Qur'anic guidance: *yā ayyuhā alladhīna āmanū li-yastaʾdhinkumu alladhīna malakat aymānukum*. The imperative verb *li-yastaʾdhin* institutes the formal legal duty of seeking permission (*istiʾdhān*) before entering private chambers.

Earlier in the sūrah (vv. 27–29), the revelation regulated entry into external houses by adult strangers. Verse 58 penetrates into the interior sanctum of the home, regulating the movement of immediate household members into the private bedrooms of parents and spouses. The domestic residence in Islam is not an open communal dormitory devoid of boundaries; it is a sacred space designed to protect human modesty, emotional intimacy, and physical dignity. By establishing precise protocols for bedroom entry, the revelation shields marital intimacy from accidental exposure and sanctifies the home as a fortress of moral purity.

**Vulnerable Demographics: Bondspeople and Minors**

The verse specifically targets two household categories: *alladhīna malakat aymānukum* (domestic bondspersons) and *alladhīna lam yablughū al-ḥuluma minkum* (children who have not yet reached the age of puberty). Classical jurists noted the profound legal precision of this specification.

Adult free strangers must seek permission at all times before entering any house (v. 27). However, domestic servants and pre-pubescent children live inside the family home, constantly moving through hallways, carrying food, and performing daily chores. To require them to seek permission at every entry throughout the day would impose extreme, paralyzing hardship (*ḥaraj*). Therefore, Islamic law establishes a realistic, balanced compromise: it exempts them from seeking permission during routine daytime hours, but strictly mandates permission during three specific temporal windows when adults are most likely to be undressed or engaged in intimacy.

**The Three Temporal Nodes of Intimate Privacy: thalāthu ʿawrāt**

The verse identifies three critical temporal nodes of vulnerability, designating them as *thalāthu ʿawrāt* (three times of privacy/nakedness): *min qabli ṣalāti al-fajri wa-ḥīna taḍaʿūna thiyābakum mina al-ẓahīrati wa-min baʿdi ṣalāti al-ʿishāʾ*.

The first node is before the dawn prayer (*min qabli ṣalāt al-fajr*), when people awaken from sleep, rise from marital beds, and change out of nightwear to perform ablution. The second node is the midday rest (*al-ẓahīrah*), when the intense desert heat prompts individuals to remove heavy outer garments for the customary midday nap (*al-qaylūlah*), seeking bodily refreshment. The third node is after the night prayer (*min baʿdi ṣalāt al-ʿishāʾ*), when the day's labor terminates, clothes are shed, and spouses retire to bed for rest and physical intimacy. During these three sensitive windows, bedrooms become private sanctuaries; no servant or child may cross the threshold without knocking and receiving explicit verbal permission from the occupants within.

**Daytime Permissibility: ṭawwāfūna ʿalaykum**

Outside these three designated privacy windows, the divine law accommodates the practical realities of daily life: *laysa ʿalaykum wa-lā ʿalayhim junāḥun baʿdahunna ṭawwāfūna ʿalaykum baʿḍukum ʿalā baʿḍ*. The phrase *ṭawwāfūn* describes frequent, circular movement back and forth within the domestic space.

In an active family home, servants and young children must run errands, attend to parents, deliver messages, and assist with domestic tasks. Imposing formal permission protocols for every movement would paralyze domestic life and create unnecessary social friction. Islamic jurisprudence applies here the foundational legal maxim: *al-mashaqqah tajlib al-taysīr* (hardship attracts legal facilitation). Outside the three vulnerable periods, free movement is permitted without sin (*laysa ʿalaykum junāḥ*), proving that divine legislation is neither prudish nor impractical, but perfectly calibrated to balance moral protection with everyday functional convenience.

**Pedagogy of Modesty and Early Childhood Development**

Verse 58 provides an extraordinary curriculum for early childhood moral development and developmental psychology. Children who have not reached puberty (*lam yablughū al-ḥulum*) do not yet carry full legal accountability (*taklīf*), nor do they fully comprehend adult sexual intimacy.

However, the Qur'an commands parents to train them in privacy protocols from early childhood. Requiring young children to knock, seek permission, and respect closed doors instills an instinctive, subconscious reverence for bodily boundaries and marital dignity long before puberty arrives. It protects the child's tender psyche from witnessing inappropriate adult nudity or physical intimacy, which can cause confusion or premature sexualization. By transforming etiquette into an early habit, Islamic pedagogy ensures that when the child transitions into biological adulthood, modesty is already woven into the core of their character.

**Divine Wisdom in Micro-Legislation: Allāhu ʿAlīmun Ḥakīm**

The verse seals its domestic ordinances with a signature theological formula: *ka-dhālika yubayyinu Allāhu lakumu al-āyāti wa-Allāhu ʿalīmun ḥakīm*, this is how Allah makes the revelations clear to you, for Allah is All-Knowing, All-Wise.

The attribute *al-ʿAlīm* affirms that God possesses complete knowledge of the most intimate, hidden corners of human life; nothing in the bedroom, the marriage bed, or the family dynamics escapes His sight. He is *al-Ḥakīm*—All-Wise in tailoring laws that harmonize physical desires, social etiquette, and domestic harmony. In secular legal philosophies, domestic bedroom conduct is often viewed as trivial or outside the scope of law; in the Qur'an, domestic privacy is sanctified as an integral dimension of divine worship. The same God who establishes the orbits of stars legislates the knock upon the bedroom door, leaving nothing in human life unguided by divine light, ensuring that purity reigns within the household.

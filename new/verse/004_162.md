## Sūrah al-Nisāʾ 4:162

> But those of them well-grounded in knowledge, the faithful ˹who˺ believe in what has been revealed to you ˹O Prophet˺ and what was revealed before you—˹especially˺ those who establish prayer— and those who pay alms-tax and believe in Allah and the Last Day, to these ˹people˺ We will grant a great reward.

### Expanded Commentary

**Knowledge that produces faith**

The verse distinguishes those well-grounded in knowledge who believe in the Qur’an and earlier revelation, establish prayer, give zakat, and believe in the Last Day. Knowledge is praised when it leads to humility, worship, and justice rather than when it becomes a credential for argument.

The description includes faithful people from the People of the Book. It preserves the Qur’anic distinction between those who reject and those who sincerely receive guidance. Their reward is great because God judges their response to truth, not the stereotype attached to their community.

Prayer, zakat, and belief in the Last Day form a pattern: inward conviction becomes regular worship, social responsibility, and accountability. A scholar who does not care for the poor has not embodied the verse’s knowledge; a generous person who denies God has not completed its faith. The rewards belong to an integrated life.

**Knowledge should feed the poor**

The learned believer in this verse prays, gives zakat, and believes in the Last Day. Learning that never reaches worship or social responsibility remains incomplete. Communities should honour scholarship that clarifies rights, supports the vulnerable, and makes faith more truthful rather than merely more argumentative.

## Sūrah al-Nisāʾ 4:64

> We only sent messengers to be obeyed by Allah’s Will. If only those ˹hypocrites˺ came to you ˹O Prophet˺—after wronging themselves—seeking Allah’s forgiveness and the Messenger prayed for their forgiveness, they would have certainly found Allah ever Accepting of Repentance, Most Merciful.

### Expanded Commentary

**Messengers are sent to be obeyed**

The verse defines the Messenger’s role: obedience to him is part of obedience to Allah, by Allah’s permission. Prophethood is not a delivery service in which people receive information and then retain sovereignty over whether divine guidance applies. The messenger explains, embodies, and judges by revelation.

**The route back after wrongdoing**

The verse offers a path for those who have wronged themselves: come to the Messenger, seek Allah’s forgiveness, and let the Messenger pray for them. The movement includes confession, return to the source of guidance, and a communal expression of repentance. It does not make the Prophet an independent owner of forgiveness; Allah is the One who accepts repentance, while the Messenger’s prayer is an honoured means.

After the Prophet’s death, the enduring lesson is to return to Qur’an and Sunnah, seek forgiveness, and repair harm rather than defend the ego. The verse’s mercy is active: the person must come, admit, and change. A community should imitate the Prophetic response by making repentance possible rather than making public disgrace permanent.

**Repentance has a communal form**

The person who returns to revelation should not be forced to repair alone. A just community can provide counsel, mediation, and a path to restore rights. The Prophet’s prayer for forgiveness models an authority that helps the wrongdoer return without denying the wrong that occurred.

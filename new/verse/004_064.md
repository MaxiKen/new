## Sūrah al-Nisāʾ 4:64

> We only sent messengers to be obeyed by Allah’s Will. If only those ˹hypocrites˺ came to you ˹O Prophet˺—after wronging themselves—seeking Allah’s forgiveness and the Messenger prayed for their forgiveness, they would have certainly found Allah ever Accepting of Repentance, Most Merciful.

**Expanded Commentary**

**Messengers Sent to Be Obeyed by Allah’s Leave**

The statement *wa mā arsalnā min rasūlin illā li yuṭāʿa bi idhni Allāh* — We only sent messengers to be obeyed by Allah’s Will — follows the counsel to turn away from hypocrites, caution them, and speak penetratingly to their souls (4:63). The verse gives the reason referral to the Messenger is not optional. A messenger is not sent to be one judge among false judges — *ṭāghūt* — whom they were commanded to reject (4:60), nor to be heard and disobeyed with distorted *rāʿinā* (4:46). He is sent *li yuṭāʿa* — to be obeyed — and that obedience is *bi idhni Allāh* — by Allah’s leave, permission, knowledge and enabling. Obedience to the Messenger is therefore not a separate loyalty but the form obedience to God takes where He has sent a clarifier. The sūrah has said to obey God and obey the Messenger and those in authority within that obedience (4:59), and to refer disputes to them as best outcome. Here it explains why: the sending itself was for obedience. The hypocrites’ stubborn turning from the revelations and the Messenger (4:61) contradicts the very purpose of sending. The sūrah has shown that God wills to make things clear and guide to noble ways and turn toward you (4:26); the messenger is the clarity and the guide made a person, and to be sent to be obeyed is how the will to turn becomes practicable.

**Had They Come After Wronging Themselves**

The counterfactual *wa law annahum idh ẓalamū anfusahum jāʾūka fa astaghfarū Allāha wa astaghfara lahumu al-rasūl* — if only, after wronging themselves, they had come to you seeking Allah’s forgiveness and the Messenger prayed for their forgiveness — pictures the alternative to seeking *ṭāghūt* and swearing goodwill after disaster (4:62). *Ẓalamū anfusahum* — wronging themselves — was read as the referral to false judges and the barring from the Book and Messenger, the same *ẓulm* that elsewhere describes devouring orphan wealth and exceeding limits. To come — *jāʾūka* — is to reverse the turn; to seek forgiveness — *istaghfarū Allāha* — is to name the wrong as sin needing pardon, not as goodwill; to have the Messenger seek forgiveness for them — *astaghfara lahumu al-rasūl* — is to join the community’s intercession to their turn. The sūrah has said that repentance is accepted for those who act ignorantly and turn soon (4:17) and that the Messenger’s prayer for forgiveness is itself part of the path, as sincere return from hands that put forward the referral to false judges requires both recognition and supplication. The verse is therefore not a ritual of coming to a man; it is coming to the one sent to be obeyed so that obedience can be restored where it was refused.

**They Would Have Found Allah Ever Accepting, Most Merciful**

The promise *la wajadū Allāha tawwāban raḥīmā* — they would have certainly found Allah ever Accepting of Repentance, Most Merciful — gives the outcome of that coming. *Tawwāb* — ever Accepting, repeatedly turning — was explained as God turning toward the servant who turns toward Him, again and again, as He turns toward those who repent soon after ignorance (4:17). *Raḥīm* — Most Merciful — was shown as the sūrah’s refrain for the weak for whom burdens are lightened (4:28) and for whom earth purifies where water fails (4:43). The pair here tells the hypocrites that the door they closed by seeking *ṭāghūt* is still the door of mercy if they come through the Messenger. The sūrah has stressed that God forgives what is below *shirk* for whomever He wills (4:48, 4:116) and that *shirk* itself is not forgiven if held to death without turning, yet the turn remains possible while breath remains and before death is present (4:18). To find Him *tawwāb* is to find that coming after wronging oneself, with *istighfār* and the Messenger’s *istighfār*, is the soon return that is accepted. The verse does not guarantee acceptance without coming; it guarantees acceptance with it.

**Why the Messenger’s Prayer Matters**

The addition of the Messenger’s seeking forgiveness — *astaghfara lahumu al-rasūl* — was read as more than courtesy. The Messenger is the witness brought over his community (4:41) and the one whose obedience is the proof of claim to believe, so his prayer for them signifies that their return is not isolated but rejoined to the community. Early authorities noted that the hypocrites who had sought *ṭāghūt* had broken the communal trust that disputes are referred to Book and Messenger as best outcome (4:59); to return and seek forgiveness with his prayer repairs that breach. The Qur’an elsewhere says that if the hypocrites had come seeking forgiveness, it would have been better and more proper, as straight speech would have been better than *rāʿinā* (4:46). Here the same better and more proper is enacted as movement — come, seek, have him seek — rather than as wording alone. The sūrah’s ethic of *iḥsān* (4:36) and of appointing mediators where harmony is desired and God brings it (4:35) finds an echo: where the soul desires reconciliation — *tawfīq* — God brings it, and where the hypocrite desires forgiveness, the Messenger’s prayer joins, and God is found ever Accepting.

**A Verse That Keeps Obedience and Mercy Together**

Placed between the counsel to speak penetratingly to souls (4:63) and the oath that true belief requires accepting the Prophet ﷺ as judge without resistance (4:65), this verse holds obedience and mercy together. Obedience — *messengers sent to be obeyed by God’s leave* — is the structure; mercy — *ever Accepting, Most Merciful* for those who come after wronging themselves — is the purpose of the structure. The structure without mercy would be harsh, and mercy without structure would leave hypocrites seeking false judges and swearing goodwill as sufficient. The verse joins them: the structure is to obey the sent one, and the mercy is found by coming to him after wronging, seeking God’s forgiveness with his supplication. The sūrah has shown that avoidance of major sins covers lesser faults and opens honour (4:31) and that not an atom’s weight is wronged but good is multiplied (4:40); this verse is the social form of that covering, where the major of referring to *ṭāghūt* is undone by coming, seeking, and being prayed for. For the weak for whom *“He wishes to lighten your burdens”* (4:28), the way back from a grave referral is not distant; it is to walk to the one sent to be obeyed, to seek pardon, and to find the One who is ever ready to turn.

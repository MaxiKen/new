## Sūrah al-Anʿām 6:165

> He is the One Who has placed you as successors on earth and elevated some of you in rank over others, so He may test you with what He has given you. Surely your Lord is swift in punishment, but He is certainly AllForgiving, Most Merciful.

**Expanded Commentary**

**The Successors Placed on the Earth**

He is the One Who has placed you as successors on earth. The verse opens the sūrah's final statement with the theme of succession, and the Book uses the same description in several places. The sūrah of Fāṭir states that God has placed people as successors on the earth and that whoever disbelieves bears the burden of his own disbelief (35:39); the sūrah of the Cow records God's announcement to the angels that He would place a successive authority on the earth (2:30); and the sūrah of the Cattle itself told the Prophet earlier that God could do away with the audience and replace them with whoever He wills (6:133). The sentence in front of us states the arrangement in the form of a description of the audience's position.

The succession is presented as a fact about the audience's standing, and the sūrah has used the history of the earlier communities to show what it means. The generations that came before had their turn on the earth and were replaced, and the sūrah has described their destruction in its accounts of the peoples who rejected their messengers. The sentence in front of us places the audience within that history, and the verses that follow state the purpose for which the succession was given.

**The Elevation in Ranks**

And elevated some of you in rank over others. The sentence states the differences that exist among people in the present life, and the Book attaches the same fact to the distribution of livelihood in several passages. According to the sūrah of the Ornament, God has distributed people's livelihood in this world and raised some of them in rank above others so that they may serve one another, and the passage asks whether it is the audience who distribute the mercy of their Lord (43:32). The sūrah of the Cattle itself states at its close that God has raised some above others in rank, and it has already stated that the ranks of the Hereafter are assigned according to deeds (6:132).

The two arrangements belong together in the sūrah's teaching. The differences of the present life are described as a distribution rather than a judgment, and the differences of the Hereafter are described as the consequence of what each person did. The verse in front of us states the first arrangement and the purpose attached to it, and the earlier verse has stated the second.

**The Test in What Has Been Given**

So He may test you with what He has given you. The clause states the purpose for which people have been placed on the earth and given their differences, and the Book attaches testing to the gifts of the present life in several passages. In the sūrah of the Kingdom, God states that He created death and life in order to test which of the people is best in deeds (67:2); the sūrah of the Cave says that God has made what is on the earth an adornment for it in order to test which of them is best in deeds (18:7); and the sūrah of the Cattle says that God has tested some people by means of others (6:53). The sentence in front of us states the purpose in the plainest terms.

The Book's treatment of the test gives the audience's position its meaning. What a person has been given is not a mark of his standing with God, and the differences in wealth, strength, and rank are the field on which conduct is examined. The sūrah has described the audience's pride in its standing and its resistance to a messenger from outside its leading families, and the sentence in front of us states the terms on which that standing will be assessed.

**Swift in Punishment**

Surely your Lord is swift in punishment. The attribute opens the sūrah's closing declaration, and the Book attaches it to the warnings about the communities that persisted in denial. God's declaration in the sūrah of the Heights is that He would send against the people of the earlier books those who would make them suffer terribly until the day of judgment, and that verse closes with the statement that the Lord is swift in punishment and All-Forgiving, Most Merciful (7:167). The sūrah of the Thunder states the same pair in its answer to those who ask for the torment to be hastened before the grace (13:6), and the sūrah of Fussilat names the Lord of forgiveness and painful punishment (41:43).

The sūrah's own account of the punishment extends through its accounts of the destroyed communities and of the reckoning described in its closing passages. The attribute in front of us states the speed with which the punishment arrives, and the Book's use of it in the passages about the earlier generations gives it content.

**All-Forgiving, Most Merciful**

But He is certainly All-Forgiving, Most Merciful. The second half of the declaration states the attribute that balances the first, and the sūrah has ended its description of the punishment with the same pair in other passages. Within the sūrah of the Heights, the two names stand together at the end of its warning (7:167), and the sūrah of the Troops tells those who have exceeded the limits against their own souls not to despair of the mercy of God, for He is the All-Forgiving, the Most Merciful (39:53); the Book's habit of placing mercy alongside severity is one of its most consistent features.

The pairing in front of us gives the sūrah its final word, and the arrangement matches the chapter it closes. The sūrah has described the signs, the arguments, the destruction of the communities, the rules of the covenant, and the reckoning; it ends with the two attributes that frame the whole: a severity that is not idle and a mercy that is not exhausted. The community that receives the Book is left between the two.

**What the Closing Verse Says About the Audience**

The verse states the position of a generation that has inherited the earth from those who came before it, and it tells that generation what its standing means. The audience was proud of its continuity, its sanctuary, and its position among the tribes, and the sentence in front of us describes those advantages as a test. Nothing in the verse suggests that the standing is secure, and the sūrah has already told the audience that God could replace them with whoever He wills (6:133).

The Book's closing verses in other sūrahs give the same account of the present life. The sūrah of the Cave states the purpose of the world's adornment in the language of testing, as does the sūrah of the Kingdom, and the sūrah of Fāṭir joins the succession of generations to the statement that each person bears his own burden. The verse in front of us states the arrangement for the audience addressed by the Qur'an, and the reader is left with the account of what has been placed in his hands.

**The Sūrah's Ending**

The verse closes the chapter with a description of the human position and a declaration of the two attributes of the Lord, and the combination is characteristic of the sūrah. The chapter began with praise of the One who created the heavens and the earth, made darkness and light, and then made those who disbelieve equal others with Him (6:1). It ends with the successors on the earth, the ranks they have been given, the test in what they hold, and the mercy and severity that will meet what they do.

The sūrah's argument has moved from the signs of the created order through the accounts of the messengers, the divisions of the communities, the dietary and ritual practices of the audience, the covenant of the commands, and the closing declarations of the Prophet, and the final verse gathers the whole into a statement about the people who receive it. What has been given is a Book, a path, a set of obligations, and a reckoning; and the last words of the chapter are the names of the Lord who gives, commands, tests, punishes, and forgives.

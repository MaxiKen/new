## Sūrah al-Naḥl 16:38

> **They swear by Allah their most solemn oaths that Allah will never raise the dead to life. Yes ˹He will˺! It is a true promise binding on Him, but most people do not know.**

**Expanded Commentary**

**Solemn Oaths Denying Resurrection**

Wa-aqsamū bi-Allāhi jahda aymānihim lā yabʿathu Allāhu man yamūt — and they swear by Allah their most solemn oaths that Allah will never raise the dead. Aqsamū from q-s-m means to swear, oath. Bi-Allāhi, by Allah, indicating they swear by Allah, whom they acknowledge as Creator. Jahda aymānihim, most earnest, strongest, most solemn oaths, from j-h-d, to strive, exert utmost, indicating they exert all effort in oath to emphasize. Lā yabʿathu Allāhu man yamūt, Allah will not raise dead, will not resurrect those who die. Al-Ṭabarī explains that Makkan polytheists swore by Allah with strongest oaths that there is no resurrection, that dead will not be raised, that Hour will not come. This is ironic: they swear by Allah that Allah will not do something, using Allah's name to deny Allah's power. The same oath appears in 35:42 and 6:109, where deniers swear they will believe if sign comes, but when sign comes they do not. Here they swear opposite: they swear resurrection will not happen. Their oath shows certainty in denial, not doubt.

**Yes, He Will — A Divine Rebuttal**

Balā waʿdan ʿalayhi ḥaqqan — Yes, indeed, it is a true promise binding upon Him. Balā is strong affirmation negating previous negative: yes indeed, contrary to what they swear, He will raise dead. Waʿdan, promise. ʿAlayhi ḥaqqan, binding upon Him, true, incumbent, right, from ḥ-q-q, to be true, binding. Al-Ṭabarī explains that balā affirms resurrection: yes, Allah will raise dead. Waʿdan ʿalayhi ḥaqqan means it is promise that Allah has made binding upon Himself, true promise that will be fulfilled, as 10:55 says *Indeed Allah's promise is true* (10:55). The promise of resurrection is ḥaqq, true, not false, and it is ʿalayhi, upon Him, meaning He has taken it upon Himself to fulfill, as He took upon Himself to show straight way in verse 9. The rebuttal is short but decisive: they swear no resurrection, Allah says yes, it is true promise binding on Him.

**Most People Do Not Know**

Wa-lākinna akthara al-nāsi lā yaʿlamūn — but most people do not know. Akthara al-nāsi, most people, majority, do not know that resurrection is true promise, do not know its wisdom, do not know its necessity. Al-Rāzī explains that most people do not know because they do not reflect on blessings, do not consider that creation with purpose implies return, do not know that Allah's power to create from drop implies power to recreate. Their lack of knowledge is not due to lack of evidence but lack of reflection, as earlier seals said for those who reflect, understand, are mindful. Most people do not know because they are heedless, not because evidence absent. The phrase appears many times in Qur'an to indicate that truth is often not known by majority, but majority's ignorance does not make falsehood true.

**Why Resurrection Is Central To This Sūrah**

Sūrat al-Naḥl opened with command at hand, then listed blessings that prove power to resurrect, then argued idols are dead and do not know when they will be raised, then described two death scenes, then asked are they waiting for angels or command. Now it addresses explicit denial of resurrection via solemn oaths. Resurrection is central because without it, blessings catalogue is merely worldly enjoyment, and warnings are empty. With resurrection, blessings become trust to be accounted for, and warnings become real. Al-Qurṭubī notes that denial of resurrection was main doctrine of Makkan polytheists, who said *When we are bones and crumbled dust, shall we be raised as new creation?* (17:49). The sūrah answers with creation arguments: He created heavens and earth, He created you from drop, He creates what you do not know, He says Be and it is. All prove power to resurrect.

**The Irony Of Swearing By Allah To Deny Allah's Power**

They swear by Allah that Allah will not raise dead. This is ironic because swearing by Allah acknowledges Allah's greatness and power, yet they deny power to resurrect. If Allah is great enough to be sworn by, He is great enough to resurrect. Al-Zamakhsharī notes that jahda aymānihim, utmost effort in oaths, shows they are earnest in denial, not casual. Their earnestness makes their error more serious. The Qur'an often notes that deniers swear by Allah, as in 6:109 and 16:38, to show that they do recognize Allah as deity to swear by, but they misplace worship by associating others. Their oath is therefore self-contradictory: they invoke Allah's name to deny Allah's promise.

**Linguistic Note On Qasam And Baʿth**

Aqsamū from q-s-m means to swear, oath, from same root as qasam, oath, and qismah, division. Bi-Allāhi, by Allah, with bi- for oath. Jahda from j-h-d means utmost effort, striving, from same root as jihād. Aymānihim from y-m-n, right hand, oath, because oath often taken with right hand. Lā yabʿathu, will not raise, from b-ʿ-th, to raise, send, resurrect, same root as baʿth, resurrection. Man yamūt, who dies, from m-w-t, death. Balā particle for affirming after negative, yes indeed. Waʿdan from w-ʿ-d, promise. ʿAlayhi ḥaqqan, binding upon Him, true, from ḥ-q-q. Akthara al-nāsi lā yaʿlamūn, most people do not know, with akthar, most, from k-th-r, many. The verse's structure — oath of denial, divine rebuttal yes promise true, most people do not know — moves from human claim to divine counter-claim to explanation of why many deny, guiding listener to reject oath of deniers and accept true promise.


**A Lesson That Connects To Gratitude**

This verse, like all verses in Sūrat al-Niʿam, ultimately calls to gratitude. Whether it describes Gardens, death, waiting, consequences, or divine command, the purpose is to make the reader recognize blessings and respond with thanks. Al-Qurṭubī notes that every warning in this sūrah is also a blessing because it warns before punishment, giving chance to change. Every promise is also a blessing because it encourages good. The sūrah therefore weaves warning and promise, blessing and accountability, to cultivate a heart that is mindful, humble, and grateful. The reader who reflects on this verse with that mindset will find both knowledge and motivation to be among those who say goodness when asked what Lord revealed, and who are greeted with peace at death.

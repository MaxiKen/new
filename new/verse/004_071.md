## Sūrah al-Nisāʾ 4:71

> O believers! Take your precautions and go forth either in groups or together.

**Expanded Commentary**

**Take Your Precautions**

The command *yā ayyuhā alladhīna āmanū khudhū ḥidhrakum* — O believers, take your precautions — follows the promise that obedience places with the blessed and that God knows who deserves favour (4:69–70) and calls to action. *Ḥidhr* — precaution, guard, readiness — was explained as preparation that befits the threat: arms, knowledge of terrain, counsel, and caution in movement. The sūrah has shown that precaution accompanies all trusts — witnesses at the orphan’s handover (4:6), four witnesses before sanction for *fāḥishah* (4:15), two mediators from families where a split is feared (4:35), and fairness in judgment (4:58). To take *ḥidhr* in the field is that same ethic applied to fighting. The verse generalises what earlier verses specificed for the market and the home: do not approach the weighty act without awareness. Prayer is not approached without knowing what is said or without purification, with earth where water fails (4:43); battle is not approached without guard. The sūrah has called humankind weak and promised lightening (4:28); precaution is part of that lightening, because the weak are not told to fight unwatched but to fight guarded.

**Go Forth Either in Groups or Together**

The detail *fa anfirū thubātin aw infirū jamīʿā* — go forth either in groups or together — was read as tactical flexibility. *Thubāt* — groups, bands — and *jamīʿā* — together, as a whole — give the community two forms of mobilization: small expeditions that can scout and skirmish, and the full muster that meets a large threat. The choice was seen as entrusted to those in authority among you (4:59), whose obedience within obedience to God and Messenger makes the referral of *how* to go forth a matter of counsel, not of private impulse. The sūrah has shown that authority is to be obeyed and disputes referred to the Book and Messenger as best outcome (4:59); going forth is the field where that obedience is tested. The verse was revealed concerning expeditions from Madinah where hypocrites lagged, as the next verses describe — saying *blessed we were not there* when disaster strikes (4:72) and *would that we had been there for great gain* when bounty comes (4:73). To say go forth in groups or together before that diagnosis is to tell believers to move as the community decides, not as the lagger decides, so that the bond between you (4:73) is not treated as if it were none.

**A Community That Knows Its Weakness and Its Guard**

Placed where it is, between the honourable company for obeying (4:69–70) and the portrait of laggers (4:72–73), this verse steadies the community that was learning to fight for the oppressed who cry *“deliver us, appoint for us a helper”* (4:75). The oppressed — men, women, and children — call for a *walī* and *naṣīr* — guardian and helper — the same two sufficiencies the sūrah said God alone provides (4:45); to answer that call the believers must go forth, but they must go forth with *ḥidhr*. The sūrah has paired *“Allah is Ever-Pardoning, All-Forgiving”* (4:43) with purification through earth, and *“and guided them to the straight path”* (4:68) with doing what was advised; here it pairs *take precautions* with *go forth* to show that courage is not carelessness. The believers who fight for God and the disbelievers who fight for the Devil with weak schemes (4:76) are distinguished not only by cause but by preparation. Those who take *ḥidhr* and go forth as commanded fight for God with the means that make fighting responsible; those who lag behind or rush without order fight for other causes or not at all. The verse therefore trains readiness as worship.

**Readiness That Honours the Trust of Life**

The call to take guard honours the trust of life that the sūrah has guarded from its opening. *“Be mindful of your Lord who created you from a single soul”* (4:1), *“do not kill yourselves, surely Allah is ever Merciful to you”* (4:29), and *“Allah is Watchful over you”* (4:1) together teach that life is not to be thrown away in zeal nor hoarded in cowardice. Precaution stands between those two, as *tathbīt* — firmness — stands between hasty consumption before the orphan grows (4:6) and neglecting his need. Early reports show the Prophet ﷺ ordering scouts and rear guards even where victory was promised, showing that trust in God’s sufficiency as Guardian (4:45) did not cancel taking means; it gave means their meaning. A community that learns that readiness to go forth — in groups for a skirmish or together for a muster — is part of obedience will also learn the earlier readinesses — witnesses at handover, dowry given, shares divided after debts — as the same obedience in different fields.

**Precaution as Obedience**

The command to take precautions was read as obedience itself, not as worldly prudence added to obedience. The Prophet ﷺ took *ḥidhr* in every expedition — scouts, guards, night watch — while trusting in God as sufficient as Trustee (4:81) and Witness (4:79). The sūrah’s earlier promise that *“whoever fights whether martyred or victorious We will give a great reward”* (4:74) does not remove the need for guard; it secures the meaning of guard, so that precaution is not fear but preparation that trusts. A community that learns to take precautions as commanded will not be hurried by news of security or fear that the next verses say should be referred to the Messenger and authorities (4:83). To go forth in groups or together after taking precautions is to act where the order to fight comes (4:77) with the awareness that death overtakes wherever you are, even in towers (4:78), yet *“what is with God is better and more lasting.”* The sūrah therefore makes precaution the middle between the fear that says *delay fighting* (4:77) and the recklessness that ignores *“do not kill yourselves”* (4:29). Take guard, then go forth — in the form the lawful authority decides — and the weak who were promised lightening will find that the guarded going is the straight path walked.

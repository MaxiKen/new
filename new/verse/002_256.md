## Sūrah al-Baqarah 2:256

> Let there be no compulsion in religion, for the truth stands out clearly from falsehood. So whoever renounces false gods and believes in Allah has certainly grasped the firmest, unfailing hand-hold. And Allah is AllHearing, All-Knowing.

**Expanded Commentary**

**The Sentence in Its Place**

The verse stands at the head of the section that follows the description of God in the verse of the pedestal, and the community's teachers read it as the answer given to those who expected the new faith to be imposed. The Book has presented the guidance as a clear distinction between two paths, and the sentence that follows draws the conclusion for the manner in which the community invites others to it.

The passage addresses a community that had become responsible for a city in which several religious groups lived, so the instruction supplied the rule for the dealings of its members with their neighbours and their relatives.

That placement matters for the reading of the sentence. The distinction between guidance and error is stated first, and the prohibition of compulsion then follows it, so that the invitation rests on what has been made clear rather than on the power of the one who delivers it. The teachers returned to the order of the two clauses whenever they explained the verse.

**The Import of the Prohibition**

The wording has been understood by the community's scholars as the statement that a person's acceptance of the faith is not to be procured by force. The Book states that the guidance of every soul belongs to God, and the passages that the commentators gathered on the subject present the invitation as an appeal addressed to the understanding of the hearer.

The teachers drew attention to the phrase about the truth standing clear from error, since the sentence gives the reason for its own instruction. Where the difference between the two paths is plain, the hearer is left to judge, and the community is left to explain and to call.

Nothing in the sentence releases the believers from the duty of conveying the message. What it removes is the expectation that the message can be established among people by the pressure of the community, and the conduct of the Prophet ﷺ in Makkah and in Madinah supplied the believers with their model. The reports of his patience with those who refused him were studied alongside the sentence.

**The Reports About the Occasion**

The commentators connected the revelation with several situations in Madinah. One report describes families of the Helpers whose children had been raised among the Jewish tribe of the Naḍīr, since vows made before the coming of Islam had placed them there, and the parents asked whether the children should be compelled to remain with the Muslims when the tribe was exiled.

Another report names a man whose sons had become Christians and had departed with the merchants who converted them, and a third describes believers who had been nursed among Jewish families and who wished to follow them when they left.

The reports differ in their details, and the community's scholars discussed the weight to be given to each. What they share is the situation the sentence addressed, a community divided in its households by a change of religion and learning the rule that governed the matter.

**The Handhold Named**

The sentence continues with the description of the one who renounces the false deities and believes in God, and the image it uses is that of a person who seizes a support that cannot break. The commentators explained the phrase as the description of a faith that a person holds with his whole being, so that the object of the sentence is not the recitation of a formula but the attachment of the heart.

The Book uses the same figure elsewhere when it speaks of those who hold fast to God, and the community's teachers gathered the passages when they explained the phrase. The image places the strength of the bond in what is held rather than in the hand that holds it, so that the assurance belongs to the object of the faith.

The wording also names the renunciation first, since the sentence describes the movement away from the false deities before it describes the movement toward God, and the teachers read the order as the account of how a person enters the faith.

**The Question of the People of the Book**

The community's scholars discussed the scope of the sentence in the light of the later instructions about the people of the Book, and several of them held that the prohibition of compulsion concerned the communities who were offered protection and the payment of the jizyah. Others read the sentence as the statement of a general principle, and they explained the campaigns against the idolatrous Arabs as the application of a separate rule to a people who had broken their agreements.

The discussion shows how the community worked out the relation between verses that treat the same subject, and the jurists recorded the positions in their books of law.

The sentence itself states the distinction between the two paths as its ground, and the community's teachers returned to it whenever they spoke about the manner of the invitation. The chapter's later verses on the same theme were read with it.

**Hearing and Knowing**

The verse closes with the description of God as hearing and knowing, and the community's scholars read the pair as the ground of the instruction. The first attribute concerns what is said in the course of the invitation, so that the words of the one who calls and the response of the one who is called are both perceived, and the second concerns the secrets of the hearts, which no compulsion can reach.

The teachers drew from the closing the lesson that the community's part in the matter is its speech and its conduct, while the outcome belongs to the One who knows. The attributes thus place the whole instruction within the knowledge of God and relieve the community of the burden of a result it cannot produce.

That reading explains the place of the sentence in the sūrah, since the verses around it treat the community's dealings with those who do not share its faith, and the closing names supply the disposition in which those dealings are conducted.

**What the Verse Establishes**

The sentence establishes that the distinction between guidance and error has been made clear, that the acceptance of the faith rests on the decision of the person, that the community's duty is the invitation and the explanation, and that the firm support belongs to the faith itself. The teachers used the verse when they set out the manner of the call to Islam, and the community's history shows the sentence cited in its discussions about the place of those who did not share its faith.

The clause about the handhold has been read as the assurance that accompanies the believer in a land where the pressure of the community runs against his faith, and the attributes at the close accompany the instruction with the statement that the One who gave it knows both the words of the speaker and the intention of the hearer. The verse therefore ends with the two names that answer the two parties of the encounter it describes.

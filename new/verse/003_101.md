## Sūrah Āli ʿImrān 3:101

> How can you disbelieve when Allah’s revelations are recited to you and His Messenger is in your midst? Whoever holds firmly to Allah is surely guided to the Straight Path.

**Expanded Commentary**

**A Question That Names Two Proofs**

The verse asks how disbelief is possible when two things are present: the revelations of God being recited and the Messenger in the community's midst. The question is not addressed to strangers; it follows a warning to the believers about the pressure they would face.

The pairing is the point. A recited text can be examined and argued with, and a living messenger can be watched and questioned. Where both are available, the excuse of insufficient evidence has no room left, and the verse says as much by asking how the opposite outcome could occur. Al-Rāzī put the matter plainly: with the recitation and the Prophet present, the people had every proof they needed to hold them back from rejection.

**The Recitation as Evidence in Itself**

The Book's challenge to its doubters rests on the character of the recitation, not only on its content. Three separate passages invite the sceptics to produce a comparable composition, and one of them escalates the demand through stages, ending with the observation that even if humanity and the jinn combined their efforts they could not produce its like (17:88).

The invitation to match it is the strongest form of the argument, because it is testable by anyone who knows the language. The verse before the reader assumes the force of that argument while it is being conducted: the recitation was happening in the community, verse by verse and day by day, and the people being addressed were hearing it as it came down.

**The Messenger in Their Midst**

The second proof is a person. The Book's argument in this passage does not depend on the messenger's words alone; it points to his presence among the people who were arguing with him.

The community that received these verses lived alongside the Prophet, saw his conduct, and listened to him outside the moments of revelation, and a substantial body of tradition records how they assessed him. His manner and his behaviour were treated by the Companions as part of the evidence for his mission, and the generation that followed them continued to transmit those descriptions. The verse's phrasing makes the point in the simplest way available: after all of that, how could anyone turn away?

**Holding Fast to God**

The verse's second sentence states the alternative to doubt: whoever holds firmly to God is guided to a straight path. The Arabic image is of a person gripping something so as not to fall, and the commentators identified what is to be gripped in two ways.

Al-Ṭabarī read it as holding fast to God's religion and to obedience to Him; al-Qurṭubī read it as holding fast to the Book itself. The two readings agree in practice, since the Book is where the religion is set out and the obedience is described. The same image appears later in this sūrah in the command to hold firmly together, and the appearance of the phrase in both places ties individual steadfastness to the community's common ground.

**Two Readings of the Closing Clause**

The last clause can be understood in two ways, and the difference is small but worth stating. On one reading, the person who holds fast is guided as a consequence of his act: holding on leads him to the straight path. On the other, the act of holding fast is itself the guidance, so that what the verse describes is not a reward following a deed but the description of a state.

Al-Rāzī set out both. The second reading treats guidance as something God places in the person who clings to Him, and it fits the Book's usage elsewhere, where guidance is described as a gift given to those who respond. On either reading, the result is the same: the path is reached by holding on to the one who established it, and the verse closes by naming the destination rather than the effort.

**The Straight Path as the Sūrah's Recurring Theme**

The phrase about the straight path is not new here. It stands at the centre of the prayer that opens the Book, and it appears at the beginning of this sūrah and again near its end, in the petition that God not let the heart swerve away from it.

Its reappearance after a warning about being drawn from the faith shows what the sūrah means by the phrase. The path is straight, and it can be left; the risk is not an obscure one, since the previous verse named the very group that would attempt the drawing away. What the verse supplies is the remedy in a single clause, and its brevity is deliberate: the alternative to being led off is to hold on, and the one being held is named.

**What the Verse Adds to the Warning Before It**

The warning that preceded this verse told the believers what would happen if they followed a particular group. This verse answers the warning with a question and a promise. The question removes the excuse of confusion, and the promise supplies the alternative course.

A community living with the recitation and the messenger is being told that it does not lack the means to remain. That is the reassurance the sūrah needed after the warning, and the two verses together are the pattern the sūrah repeats: a danger named, then the ground of confidence restated. The next verses build the community's answer out of the same material.

**The Company the Verse Keeps**

The verse stands among a set of passages in this sūrah that address the believers directly after describing the conduct of others, and its function is to keep the warning from becoming a verdict on the community itself.

Its argument has an edge for every generation that recites it. The recitation and the prophetic example are both available to later readers in transmitted form, and the question the verse asks retains its force: what reason remains for turning away when the message is being read and the model of its first community is known? The Book places the confidence of the community in what it has received rather than in its own condition, which is why the promise that follows the question is attached to holding on rather than to the people who are holding.

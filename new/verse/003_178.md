## Sūrah Āli ʿImrān 3:178

> Those who disbelieve should not think that living longer is good for them. They are only given more time to increase in sin, and they will suffer a humiliating punishment.

**Expanded Commentary**

**The Respite and the Misreading**

The verse warns those who deny not to suppose that the delay they are granted is good for them, and it states that the delay allows them to increase in sin. The sentence addresses a conclusion that people naturally draw when they observe the prosperous among the wicked.

The community had watched its opponents return home in good order while its own dead were being buried, and the sight raised the question the verse answers. The verse explains what such survival amounts to, and it does so in terms that remove the advantage a person might read into it.


The commentators explain the respite as the granting of more life, in place of dying or being killed. The Makkans who had escaped the fighting were given additional years, and the sentence treats those years as the subject of the warning.

The Book uses the same idea in other passages, and it consistently refuses to treat long life as evidence of divine approval. The verse before the reader takes the observation the community had made about its enemies and states what the observation actually means.

**Two Ways of Reading the Sentence**

The warning can be understood in two ways. On one reading, since the people in question are deniers, a longer life can only be worse for them, because every additional year is spent in a state that leads away from God. On the other, the delay is itself part of the punishment: God answers their disbelief by increasing it in them, so that the state of denial is a suffering in its own right rather than only the cause of a later one.

The two readings are recorded in the commentaries, and they agree about the substance. The person who receives the respite is not being favored, and the verse says so in the plainest terms.

**The Alternative Reading of the Verb**

One commentator read the verb as an imperative rather than as a prohibition addressed to the deniers, which changes the sentence's addressee rather than its content. On that reading the verse tells the reader not to suppose that the respite granted to those who deny is good for them.

The reading makes the sentence a warning to the community against the same misreading, and it fits the verse's place in a passage that keeps addressing the believers. The difference between the two readings concerns whom the Book is correcting, and the correction is identical in both.

**The Debate Recorded by al-Rāzī**

Al-Rāzī reports a debate over whether God may be said to want some people to increase in sin. One school argued from this verse that the increase is willed, while the other argued from the verse that states that jinn and humanity were created only to worship that God does not will disbelief for anyone.

The second group held that the general verse governs the specific one, and the first replied that the specific statement in this verse remains true alongside the general. Al-Rāzī also records an interpretation on which the particle in the sentence indicates result rather than intention, so that the sense is that the respite ends in an increase of sin; he rejects that reading as contrary to the plain sense of the sentence.

**The Reading About the Battle's Outcome**

One of the interpretations recorded in the commentaries applies the verse to the outcome of the fighting. On that reading, those who deny should not think that their survival at Uḥud was better than the deaths of the men who fought in God's way, since all their survival achieved was further life in a corrupt state.

The reading fits the passage's setting closely. The community had lost men whose deaths the sūrah describes as a state of provision and joy, and the survivors among the enemy had kept their lives and nothing else. The verse tells the community which of the two outcomes is the better, and it does so without disputing the facts of the day.

**The Book's Warnings About Being Given Rope**

The Book warns in several places about the impression created by prosperity that is not corrected. One passage describes people who became oblivious to the warnings and were then given everything they desired, until they were seized by surprise and fell into despair. Another asks whether the long enjoyment granted to a people and their forefathers had made them secure.

A third passage tells the Prophet not to be impressed by the wealth and children of the deniers, since God intends to test them by these things in this life. The verse before the reader belongs to that group of statements, and it explains what the community should think when it sees its opponents flourishing.

**The Punishment That Ends the Verse**

The sentence closes by naming the outcome as a humiliating punishment, and the term describes a treatment that abases the person who receives it. The Book uses the word for those who were arrogant in their denial.

The vocabulary matches the warning. The people who supposed that their respite was a sign of favor will be shown what it was, and the humiliation named in the verse is the reversal of the standing they believed they held. The Book's use of the term elsewhere is consistently for those whose conduct has earned them the loss of the regard they claimed. The verses that follow turn to the community's own question about who is sincere among them, and the sūrah continues to explain how such matters are settled.

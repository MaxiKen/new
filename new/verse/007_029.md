## Sūrah al-Aʿrāf 7:29

> Say, ˹O Prophet,˺ “My Lord has commanded uprightness and dedication ˹to Him alone˺ in worship, calling upon Him with sincere devotion. Just as He first brought you into being, you will be brought to life again.”

**Expanded Commentary**

**What God Has Commanded**

The verse gives the Prophet the declaration of what his Lord has commanded, and the statement answers the false attribution of the previous verse. The first command is justice, and the term covers fairness in judgment, in transactions, and in the treatment of people, as well as the testimony of God's oneness. The commentary tradition connects the word with the sūrah of the Family of Imran's statement that God, the angels, and the possessors of knowledge bear witness that there is no god but He and that He upholds justice (3:18), and it reads the command in front of us as a summary of the religion's requirements.

The Book's commands about justice appear throughout its instructions about trade, testimony, and the treatment of enemies, and the sūrah of the Table commands the community to stand firm for God as witnesses to justice. The verse in front of us places justice at the head of the list, and the sūrah's audience, which lived by the customs of its ancestors and its own preferences, is told what the standard actually is.

**Setting the Face Toward God**

The verse commands the people to set their faces toward God at every place of worship, and the phrase describes the orientation of the whole person toward his Lord. The commentary tradition gives two readings of the command, one of which refers to the direction of prayer and the other to the devotion with which the prayer is performed. The Book's language of setting the face toward God appears in the sūrah of the Cow, the sūrah of the Family of Imran, the sūrah of the Cattle, and the sūrah of the Romans, and in each case it describes a whole orientation rather than a posture.

The command to perform this at every place of worship addresses a practice of the audience that the sūrah has been correcting. The Quraysh had placed restrictions on who could wear clothing or eat food within the sacred precinct, and the sūrah's instruction to the children of Adam to take their adornment at every place of worship stands in the same context. The verse in front of us states that the worship is for God alone and that it is to be conducted at every place devoted to Him.

**Calling Upon Him With Sincere Devotion**

The verse commands the people to call upon God with sincerity of religion, and the phrase describes worship offered without any partner in intention. The Book uses the same expression in several passages, and the sūrah of the Troops tells the Prophet to say that he is commanded to worship God with sincerity, and the sūrah of the Clear Proof describes the command given to the people of the earlier books as the worship of God alone with sincere devotion. The sūrah of Yūnus and the sūrah of the Spider use the expression in the context of the distress of the sea voyage, when the travellers call upon God with sincerity before they return to their old associations.

The verse in front of us makes the same demand of the audience at the sanctuary. Their pilgrimage, their offerings, and their invocations were shared between God and their idols, and the command states that the devotion is to be for God alone. The sūrah's later passages on the offerings and the food rules apply the same principle to the practical arrangements of their religion.

**The Return to the Beginning**

The verse closes with the statement that the people will be brought back just as they were first brought into being, and the clause repeats the theme of the sūrah's account of the creation and the resurrection. The sūrah of the Cattle states that the disbelievers will be told that they have come to their Lord alone as they were created the first time, and the sūrah of Maryam and the sūrah of the Prophets describe the raising of the dead as a second creation. The sūrah of the Heights has already stated that the people will live on the earth, die there, and be brought forth from it (7:25).

The clause supplies the argument for the commands. The One who commands justice and sincerity is the One who brought the people into being and who will bring them back, and the Book's appeals to the origin of human life serve as the ground of its appeals to obedience. The verse in front of us places the commands and the resurrection in the same sentence.

**The Argument and Its Audience**

The sūrah's audience accepted the creation of the world by a single Lord even while it worshipped the idols, and the Book's arguments repeatedly use that acceptance as the starting point for the argument about the resurrection. The sūrah of the Spider states that if the people were asked who created the heavens and the earth they would say it was God, and it draws the conclusion that they should therefore be grateful. This verse uses the same movement, since it places the command to sincere worship beside the statement of the return.

The Book's presentation of the resurrection as easier than the first creation appears in the sūrah of the Romans and the sūrah of the Prophets, and the sūrah's audience is asked to accept the argument on that ground. The passage in front of us states the fact without elaboration, and the sūrah's later passages supply the description of the day when the return takes place.

**Where the Verse Sits**

The verse stands twenty-ninth in the chapter, and it follows the rejection of the false attribution and precedes the statement about those who have been guided and those who are destined to stray. The passage as a whole answers the audience's justification of its practices with the statement of what God has commanded, and the verses that follow describe the two responses to the guidance.

The reader is given the command in its positive form: justice, orientation toward God, sincerity in worship, and the certainty of the return. The sūrah then states the division among people that the following of the guidance produces.

**The Transition to the Next Verse**

The next verse states that God has guided some while others are destined to stray, and it names the reason: those who strayed took the devils as masters in place of God while thinking that they were guided (7:30). The statement of what God has commanded in front of us is followed by the description of the two responses to it.

The two verses together present the sūrah's account of guidance and misguidance, and the verses that follow describe the clothing, the food, and the conduct of the children of Adam in the same terms. The reader is shown the command, the response, and the practices that follow from each.

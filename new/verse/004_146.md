## Sūrah al-Nisāʾ 4:146

> except those who repent, mend their ways, hold fast to Allah, and are sincere in their devotion to Allah; they will be with the believers. And Allah will grant the believers a great reward.

### Expanded Commentary

**The door out of hypocrisy**

The exception lists repentance, reform, holding fast to Allah, and sincere devotion. Repentance alone is not presented as a sentence; conduct must be mended. A hypocrite who harmed a community must repair trust, return rights, and stop the double dealing.

Holding fast to Allah means replacing dependence on shifting patrons with steadfast worship and obedience. Sincerity makes the external act and internal purpose converge. Such people are with the believers, not merely tolerated at the edge. The verse therefore refuses fatalism: even a grave pattern can be abandoned while life remains.

The great reward promised to believers belongs to those whose faith becomes reliable. Community protection and mercy work together—do not be naïve about betrayal, but do not tell a repentant person that return is impossible.

**Repentance must alter relationships**

A hypocrite who returns should not only feel regret; he should stop manipulating, return property, correct false reports, and accept reasonable limits while trust is rebuilt. The verse’s welcome to the believers is therefore neither naïve nor vindictive. It joins accountability to the possibility of belonging again.

## Sūrah al-Nisāʾ 4:146

> except those who repent, mend their ways, hold fast to Allah, and are sincere in their devotion to Allah; they will be with the believers. And Allah will grant the believers a great reward.

**Expanded Commentary**

**Except Those Who Repent, Mend, Hold Fast, and Are Sincere**

The verse states *illā alladhīna tābū wa aṣlaḥū wa iʿtaṣamū bi Allāhi wa akhlaṣū dīnahum li Allāhi* — except those who repent, turned, and mended, set right, and held fast, clung, to God, and made their religion, devotion, sincere, pure for God. *Tābū* — to repent, to return — was explained with *tawbah* as the sūrah’s return after evil soon after error (4:17) before death’s *now I repent* (4:18), after deceit (4:106–110), after *“whoever wrongs themselves then seeks finds”* (4:110). *Wa aṣlaḥū* — and mended, set right — was explained as mending what was corrupted: where night plotting disordered (4:108), where throwing sin on innocent burdened (4:112), where honour was sought among disbelievers (4:139), where sitting where signs are mocked made like them (4:140) — mending is *iṣlāḥ bayna al-nāsi* the good secret talk (4:114). *Wa iʿtaṣamū bi Allāhi* — and held fast to God, clung — was explained as the same *iʿtiṣām* commanded where those who believe in God and hold fast to Him will be admitted into mercy and guided through Straight Path (4:175), and where the emigrant to God has reward secured (4:100). *Wa akhlaṣū dīnahum li Allāhi* — and made devotion sincere, pure, for Him — was read as *ikhlāṣ* — purity from showing off where they stood lazy to be seen (4:142) and spent to show off (4:38); sincerity is the opposite of *riyāʾ* and of seeking *ʿizzah* among disbelievers (4:139). The four were explained as the hypocrite’s reversal: repentance reverses oscillation (4:137), mending reverses night plotting, holding fast reverses alliance with disbelievers instead of believers (4:144), sincerity reverses standing to be seen.

**They Will Be With the Believers**

The promise *fa ulāʾika maʿa al-muʾminīna* — then those — the repentant, mending, holding, sincere — will be with the believers, among them, counted as them — was explained with *maʿa* — with, together — the same *maʿa* the waiters claimed falsely — *were we not with you?* (4:141) — now true. To be *with believers* was read as not merely spared from lowest depth (4:145) but transferred to the other gathering: hypocrites and disbelievers gathered together in Hell (4:140) versus believers gathered with honourable company — prophets, truthful, martyrs, righteous (4:69). The sūrah has shown that those who obey God and Messenger will be with those blessed (4:69) and that whoever defies the Messenger and follows other than believers’ path is left to that path (4:115); those who repent and are sincere are returned to the believers’ path — *sabili al-muʾminīn* — after being torn between neither (4:143). The phrase was linked to *“Gardens under rivers forever, God’s promise ever true”* (4:122): to be with believers is to share that promise whose word none exceeds.

**And God Will Grant Believers a Great Reward**

The seal *wa sawfa yuʾti Allāhu al-muʾminīna ajran ʿaẓīmā* — And God will grant the believers, will give them, a great, mighty reward — was explained with *sawfa* — will, soon — certainty as the emigrant’s secured reward (4:100), and *ajran ʿaẓīmā* — great reward — the same *ajr ʿaẓīm* promised to those who command charity, kindness, reconciliation seeking His pleasure (4:114) and to strivers above sitters (4:95–96). To promise *ajr ʿaẓīm* to believers now inclusive of the repentant sincere was read as that their repentance does not make them second-class believers; mercy makes them believers. The sūrah has shown that *“whoever commits evil will be rewarded accordingly”* (4:123) yet *“whoever does good male or female with faith will enter Paradise not wronged a speck”* (4:124); the sincere repentant’s good is now counted among that good. Where the hypocrites sought honour among disbelievers because all honour belongs to Him (4:139), great reward is the honour given to believers — not sought beside Him — because with Him are both rewards (4:134).

**A Verse That Follows Lowest Depth With Highest Return**

Placed after *hypocrites in lowest depths of Fire, you will never find helper* (4:145), this verse follows lowest with return. The sūrah has announced painful punishment to hypocrites (4:138), shown honour sought wrongly, sitting where mocked, waiting between, deceiving, lazy prayer, wavering, alliance giving clear proof against selves (4:138–144); here the same hypocrites — the *munāfiqīn* — are given four steps: repent, mend, hold fast, be sincere. The sūrah has shown that *“why should He punish you if you are grateful and faithful? He is ever Appreciative, All-Knowing”* (4:147) — punishment is not needed where gratitude and faith are; these four are the gratitude and faith made operational. The sūrah that asks *“was not God’s earth spacious enough to emigrate?”* (4:97) and promises many havens (4:100) here says earth’s spaciousness includes a haven from lowest depth to being with believers, via sincerity. For the weak the sūrah lightens: where standing for justice though against yourselves was hard (4:135), holding fast to God is the stand; where settlement is best (4:128), mending is the settlement with Him; where *shuḥḥ* was present (4:128), sincerity removes it.

**Four Verbs That Reverse Four Hypocrisies**

The four — *tābū, aṣlaḥū, iʿtaṣamū, akhlaṣū* — were read as stepwise remedy for the hypocrite’s four defects: *tābū* heals oscillation (4:137) where belief was left, *aṣlaḥū* heals secret talk without good (4:114) where night plot and slander harmed, *iʿtaṣamū* heals alliance instead of believers (4:144) where trust was placed elsewhere, *akhlaṣū* heals *riyāʾ* — showing off — where prayer was for people (4:142). The verse generalises the judgment chapter’s medicine where *“whoever wrongs themselves then seeks finds Him All-Forgiving”* (4:110) — seeking is *tābū*, mending is *aṣlaḥū*, holding fast is reliance on *Wakīl* sufficient (4:132), sincerity is doing for Him not for show. The next verse will state the principle that makes these four sufficient: why should He punish you if you are grateful and faithful — He is ever Appreciative, All-Knowing (4:147), so that those with Him are promised great reward not out of need but appreciation.

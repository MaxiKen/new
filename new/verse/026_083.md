## Sūrah al-Shuʿarāʾ 26:83

> “My Lord! Grant me wisdom, and join me with the righteous.

**Expanded Commentary**

**Abraham Asks for Wisdom From His Lord**

Abraham's prayer turns from describing God's acts to asking for gifts. He begins with wisdom. The translation renders the key term as wisdom, while the exegetical tradition also connects it with sound judgment, understanding, and the capacity to decide rightly. The request is larger than information. Abraham wants knowledge governed by truth and action governed by that knowledge.

Calling God my Lord places wisdom inside a relationship of formation. Abraham is not asking for cleverness that will make him independent. He is asking the Lord who created and guided him to deepen that guidance. Wisdom is therefore a form of servanthood. It helps a person see what matters, distinguish truth from appearance, and choose the response that obedience requires.

**Wisdom Is More Than Winning an Argument**

Abraham has just confronted his people about idols. His wisdom is visible in the clarity of those questions, but the prayer prevents us from reducing wisdom to debate skill. A person may defeat an opponent verbally and still lack justice, mercy, or self-control. True wisdom unites clear thought with right purpose.

The request also guards against pride after success. Abraham does not assume that having exposed an error means he has no further need of God. The messenger continues to ask for understanding. Every correct argument must be carried by a heart that knows how to speak, when to remain patient, and how to protect truth from becoming a vehicle for ego.

**The Term Can Be Read as Judgment**

Some exegetes understand the Arabic ḥukm as wisdom and understanding; others connect it with judgment or authority. The first reading emphasizes inward discernment, while the second includes the ability to decide and govern by truth. These meanings support one another. Sound judgment requires understanding, and understanding becomes complete when it directs a decision.

A narrow reading would separate the mind from public responsibility. Abraham's prayer does not. The person who sees truth must also ask how to embody it in relations, leadership, and moral choices. Wisdom is not a private possession that never reaches conduct. It is the light by which a servant judges himself and acts toward others.

**The Request Does Not Mean Abraham Lacked Prophetic Guidance**

The source tradition notes a question about interpreting judgment as prophethood, since Abraham was already a prophet. The stronger lesson is that a prophet can ask for an increase in wisdom without implying that he was previously unguided. Guidance is not a one-time certificate. A servant continues to receive clarity, firmness, and deeper understanding throughout his mission.

This helps believers understand their own prayers. Asking God for guidance does not mean that previous guidance was false. It means that the servant knows his need is continuous. The path may be known in principle while particular choices remain difficult. Wisdom asks God to keep the heart capable of applying known truth to new circumstances.

**Joining the Righteous Is a Request for Company**

Abraham pairs wisdom with being joined to the righteous. Knowledge alone can isolate a person or feed superiority. Righteous company gives wisdom a human setting in which it can be practiced, corrected, and shared. The request is not simply to be called righteous. It is to belong among people whose loyalty to God shapes their conduct.

The righteous may include prophets, those accepted by God, and servants who remain content and faithful through changing conditions. These descriptions differ in emphasis but converge on a life ordered by obedience. Abraham asks for companionship in that order. He knows that a person is influenced by the people with whom he stands and that truth is strengthened by a community that lives it.

**Righteous Company Reaches Beyond This Life**

The wording can include being with the righteous in this world and in the Hereafter. In this life, it means shared work, counsel, worship, and moral support. In the Hereafter, it means acceptance among those whose hearts and actions were sound. Abraham's request therefore joins present belonging to final destiny without confusing the two.

A person cannot earn righteousness merely by attaching his name to a respected group. The company must be joined through truth and conduct. At the same time, no servant should imagine that private excellence makes community unnecessary. Abraham, a prophet and friend of God, still asks to be placed among the righteous. His example makes humility part of righteousness.

**Wisdom and Company Protect Each Other**

Wisdom without righteous company can become self-reliance. Company without wisdom can become blind conformity. Abraham asks for both. Understanding helps a community remain faithful rather than merely traditional, while a faithful community helps understanding remain humble and useful.

This balance answers the earlier defense of idolatry through ancestors. Abraham does not ask to be bound to any inherited group. He asks to be joined with the righteous, a moral category measured by devotion to God rather than by ancestry. The right company is not the oldest or largest; it is the company that makes truth easier to recognize and obey.

**A Related Prayer Extends the Same Concern**

In 14:40, Abraham asks that he and some of his descendants be made keepers of prayer. That parallel shows how his requests combine personal obedience with the formation of a faithful community. He does not seek wisdom as a private distinction. He seeks a life and a lineage ordered toward worship.

The two prayers illuminate this verse without replacing it. Wisdom supplies judgment, while righteous company supplies a setting in which judgment can serve God. Abraham's request is therefore a prayer for the whole shape of faithful existence: to understand rightly, act rightly, and stand with those who do the same.

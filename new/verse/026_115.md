## Sūrah al-Shuʿarāʾ 26:115

> I am only sent with a clear warning.”

**Expanded Commentary**

**Noah Defines the Limit of His Mission**

Noah says that he is only sent with a clear warning. The word only marks a boundary around his task. He is not claiming control over the people's hearts, possession of God's treasures, or authority to manufacture the final judgment. He has been sent to make the danger plain and to call the people toward a responsible response.

The warning is clear because it does not hide the object of worship, the moral demand, or the consequence of rejection. Noah's role is not to make truth mysterious so that only elites can understand it. He communicates plainly to a people who have already allowed custom and status to obscure what should be simple.

**A Warner Does Not Force Belief**

A warning can be delivered clearly without being accepted. Noah cannot compel the chiefs to believe, and he will not compromise the message to make their acceptance more likely. His responsibility is faithful delivery; their responsibility is the response they choose.

This limit protects the nature of guidance. Faith that is forced by a messenger's power would not be the same as willing submission to God. Noah can explain, warn, and answer objections. He cannot place belief in a heart by command. The clarity of his warning makes the people accountable without turning him into the controller of their inner lives.

**Clear Warning Includes Consequence and Mercy**

A warning is not merely a threat. It identifies a danger so that people can avoid it. Noah's call carries fear of God's judgment, but it also opens a path to safety through worship, taqwa, and obedience. The purpose of clarity is prevention. People are told what the road leads to while they can still turn.

The parallel account in 7:63 describes a warning delivered through one of the people so that they may beware and receive mercy. That wording shows why the warning is itself mercy. A messenger exposes the danger before it becomes unavoidable. Rejecting the warning does not make the message harsh; it makes the rejection tragic.

**The Messenger Is Not a Collector of Privilege**

Noah's statement follows his refusal to expel the believers and his declaration that he seeks no reward. Those points belong together. A clear warner does not use the message to establish a private class of followers or to collect payment from the audience. His work remains directed to God's truth rather than to personal gain.

This also answers the chiefs' concern about social status. Noah is not building a court in which the poor are inconvenient and the rich are necessary. He is carrying a warning to everyone. The messenger's authority comes from the clarity of the call, not from the prestige of the people who surround him.

The Arabic term nadhīr means a warner, someone who alerts people to danger before it arrives. The plain sense carries urgency and care. A nadhīr does not delight in the harm he announces; he wants the hearer to avoid it. Noah's warning is therefore an act of concern even when the people experience it as criticism.

The function also places Noah within the wider prophetic pattern. Messengers warn of the results of false worship and disobedience, while also bringing guidance and hope. The warning is clear because it serves action. It tells the community what it must take seriously, not merely what it should fear in theory.

The chiefs may reject Noah because humble people follow him, but their approval is not the measure of clarity. A message can be clear to the poor and powerful alike. If the influential refuse to listen, the messenger does not need to alter the standard or hide the warning beneath more attractive language.

Noah's position is steady because he knows the source of his mission. He is sent by God, not commissioned by the chiefs. The people may dispute his followers, motives, and social reach, but the warning remains what it is. Social resistance cannot turn a clear call into an unclear one.

**The Warning Leaves Room for Responsible Thought**

Clear does not mean simplistic. Noah's message can be understood by ordinary people while still demanding reflection about worship, judgment, and conduct. Clarity removes needless obscurity; it does not remove the need for sincerity. A person may understand the words and still resist because accepting them would cost pride or privilege.

This distinction helps explain why the people's later objections do not prove that Noah failed to communicate. Their resistance may arise from what the warning requires of them. A clear message can be rejected precisely because it leaves no comfortable way to preserve the old authority.

Noah cannot choose the people's response, but he can fulfill the duty of delivering the message faithfully. This gives him a measure of success that does not depend on immediate numbers. If he warns clearly, protects believers, and seeks no reward, he has served the Lord even when the community refuses.

That standard also protects callers from despair. They should strive for clarity, wisdom, and good character, then leave hearts to God. Failure to persuade everyone is not proof that the warning was untrue. The messenger is responsible for honesty and care, while God judges the response.

**A Warning Is Mercy Before It Is Evidence Against Anyone**

The people should hear Noah's words as an opportunity rather than as a sentence already carried out. A warning makes repentance possible. It gives the person time to abandon idols, repair injustice, and accept God's authority. Only after persistent refusal does the warning become evidence of what the person chose.

Noah's final description of his mission therefore holds together humility and urgency. He is only a clear warner, but a clear warning from God is no small commission. The community can still turn toward the Lord if it listens before the consequence it was warned about arrives.

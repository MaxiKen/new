## Sūrah Āli ʿImrān 3:105

> And do not be like those who split ˹into sects˺ and differed after clear proofs had come to them. It is they who will suffer a tremendous punishment.

**Expanded Commentary**

**A Command Framed as a Comparison**

The verse tells the believers not to be like those who divided and differed after clear proofs had reached them, and it ends by stating that such people will receive a severe punishment. The command is expressed through a comparison rather than a prohibition, and the form gives the instruction its shape.

What is forbidden is not division in the abstract but the imitation of a specific history. The Book uses this method often: it holds up a community's conduct and tells its readers to avoid resembling it. That form of warning is stronger than a rule because it supplies the example, and the sūrah has already used it twice in this passage, once with the people of an earlier nation and once with the fate of those who disbelieve.

**Who Divided and Differed**

The commentators identified the people in view as the communities that had received earlier scriptures. The Book describes the pattern in several places with the same details. In the sūrah of the Cow it states that no one disputed the Scriptures except the very people who had received them, and that the dispute began after the clear proofs had come to them, out of jealousy (2:213).

The same account appears in this sūrah earlier, where it says that those who were given the Scripture did not dispute among themselves out of mutual envy until knowledge came to them (3:19). The Book is consistent about the sequence: the proof arrived, and the division followed it rather than preceding it. That is what makes the conduct the verse warns against so grave, since it is not the disagreement of people still searching.

**The Weight of "After Clear Proofs"**

The phrase carries the whole accusation. A community that has received an explanation has lost the excuse of not knowing, and its divisions are then matters of preference rather than of evidence.

The Book returns to this point whenever it describes the communities of the Book, and it treats the arrival of knowledge as the moment when responsibility increases rather than as the moment when quarrelling becomes tolerable. The verse before the reader takes the same material and turns it into a warning for a community that had just been given its own proofs, so that the historical example becomes a mirror rather than a stranger's story.

**Division as a Category in the Book**

The theme recurs in the Book with a fixed vocabulary. One passage states that those who divided their faith and split into sects have nothing to do with the Prophet, and that their affair rests with God, who will inform them of what they did (6:159). Another describes the same people, with each group rejoicing in what it happens to hold (30:32).

Alongside those descriptions stands the command: the Book's charge to uphold the faith and make no divisions in it was given to Noah, Abraham, Moses, and Jesus (42:13). The Book therefore treats unity as a requirement of the religion itself and division as a departure from it, and the passage in this sūrah belongs to that treatment rather than to a general preference for agreement.

**A Warning for the Believers Themselves**

The verse is addressed to the believers, and its position is significant. It follows a reminder of the community's own rescue from enmity and the command to hold together, and it precedes a description of what awaits those who took the other path.

The warning is thus not aimed only at the communities of the past. The believers are being told that the same thing could happen among them, and the Book records the prediction elsewhere in plain terms, saying that the community would divide in the way earlier peoples had. What the verse establishes is the standard by which such a development is to be judged, and it removes any sense that the divisions of a religious community are a neutral feature of its history.

**The Punishment Described as Severe**

The verse closes with the statement that a severe punishment is what awaits those described, and the Book attaches that description to its gravest warnings. The word used for the punishment is the one the sūrah has already used for a reward of a different kind, and the pairing shows what is being weighed.

The punishment is stated without detail, which is the Book's practice at the close of such warnings. What the reader receives is the plain fact that the matter is not small, and the sentence leaves him with the choice the passage has been building toward: a community can hold to what it has been given, or it can reproduce the history it has just been shown. The verses that follow describe the outcome of both courses on the Day when the consequences are shown openly.

**Two Verbs: Splitting and Differing**

The verse uses two words for one development, and they describe different aspects of it. The first names the act of separating, of a body becoming parts; the second names the disagreement that follows and fills the distance between them.

The order matters for how the prohibition works. The Book does not condemn disagreement as such in this sentence; it condemns the breaking apart that makes disagreement into division, and it attaches the warning to the people who produced it. Its standard is the community that keeps its argument inside a shared ground, and the historical example it has just given is of people who stopped sharing one. The distinction is worth keeping, because it explains why the Book can command the community both to refuse the division and to correct one another, and why the presence of different views within a single body is not what the sentence describes.

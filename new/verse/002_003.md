## Sūrah al-Baqarah 2:3

> who believe in the unseen, establish prayer, and donate from what We have provided for them,

**Expanded Commentary**

**Three Marks of the Mindful, Ordered from Hidden to Visible**

The previous verse restricted guidance to the mindful (2:2); this verse gives that restriction a shape. *“Who believe in the unseen, establish prayer, and spend from what We have provided for them”* (2:3) lists three acts that map an inward conviction, a bodily discipline, and an outward relinquishment, in that order. The order is not decorative. It moves from what is most hidden even to the actor — the heart’s assent — to what is most visible to others — the hand’s release. By placing them together the verse establishes that the mindfulness praised in (2:2) is not a mood but a structure: a hidden belief that seeks form, a form that seeks a direction, and a possession that is understood as provision rather than ownership. To delete any one would collapse the structure. Belief without prayer would remain unembodied; prayer without spending would remain self-regarding; spending without belief would be sociology rather than worship.

**Belief in the Unseen Is Not Belief in the Unknown**

*Al-ghayb* — the unseen — is rendered in this translation as such, but the Arabic carries the sense of absence from ordinary perception rather than inherent unintelligibility. The lexicons gloss *ghayb* as what is absent (*ghāʾib*), whether absent because it is beyond the senses by nature or absent because it is beyond the senses by appointment. God is unseen in the first mode: no sight encompasses Him in this life. The Hour is unseen in the second: its timing is withheld. Paradise, Hell, angels, the decrees that govern a life before they appear as events — each is unseen in one of these two ways. Belief in the unseen is therefore not a license for credulity. It is the recognition that the sum of what is is larger than the sum of what is seen, and that the part that is seen behaves as it does because it is held by what is not seen. That recognition is the premise of every later command; without it, prayer would be exercise and spending would be loss.

**Why Prayer Is Described as Erecting Rather Than Performing**

*“Establish prayer”* — *yuqīmūna al-ṣalāh* — uses a verb that means to set upright, to make stand, to keep in repair. The Qur'an could have said *yaṣallūna* (they pray) or *yafʿalūna al-ṣalāh* (they do the prayer); it says *yuqīmūna*, they keep it standing. The choice invites a specific image: prayer is a structure that would collapse if left alone, and the mindful are those who continually raise it. Commentators note that *iqāmah* in Qur'anic usage includes the outward conditions — ablution, facing the qiblah, keeping the times, completing the bowing and prostration — and the inward conditions — presence of heart, intention, remembrance — so that “establishment” gathers what “performance” would leave fragmented. That nuance explains why the same root appears in *“establish the religion”* (42:13) and *“establish justice”* (4:135): a thing is established when its parts are held together by a continual act of will. A prayer rushed through while the mind shops elsewhere has been performed after a fashion; it has not been established.

**Ṣalāh Between Human Prayer and Divine Benediction**

The word *ṣalāh* in the Qur'an moves between two poles. When believers make *ṣalāh*, they are the lower addressing the Higher; when Allah and His angels make *ṣalāh* upon the Prophet, as in *“Surely Allah and His angels invoke blessings upon the Prophet. O you who believe, invoke blessings upon him and salute him with a full salute”* (33:56), the Higher is addressing the lower with uplift and mercy. Both derive from the same root of linkage and turning toward. That semantic range prevents a reduction of prayer to petition alone. To establish *ṣalāh* is to enter a relation that is already two-way: the servant turns with his recitation and his limbs, and the Lord turns with His remembrance, *“Remember Me, I will remember you”* (2:152). Jesus, speaking from the cradle, reports the same encompassing command: *“He has enjoined upon me prayer”* (19:31), as does the command to Moses, *“establish prayer for My remembrance”* (20:14). The mindful therefore do not fit prayer into a life; they fit a life around prayer, because the act itself is how the relation is kept alive.

**Spending From What Was Provided Is Not Giving From What Is Owned**

*“Spend from what We have provided for them”* — *mimmā razaqnāhum yunfiqūn* — qualifies both source and scope. The source is *what We have provided* (*rizq*), not “what they earned.” The Qur'an systematically attributes provision to the Provider even when human effort is evident, so that the hand that gives never suffers the illusion of being the origin of what it gives. The scope is *from* (*min*), partitively: they spend *from* what was provided, not all of it. The partitive protects against two errors at once: the miser who spends nothing and the zealot who empties his household and then must ask. Elsewhere the same verb *anfaqa* is used for spending in a way that parallels the frequent pairing of prayer and alms — *“who establish prayer and spend from what We provide”* (8:3), *“who give in the way of Allah”* (14:31), *“who bow and spend”* (22:35), *“they repel evil with good and spend”* (28:54), *“they forsake their beds and spend”* (32:16) — so that the inward vertical act and the outward horizontal act are never allowed to be separated for long.

**The Gradation From Inward to Outward as a Map of the Self**

Read together, the three marks trace a complete anthropology. *Belief in the unseen* engages the heart, whose object cannot be pointed to. *Establishing prayer* engages the body, whose standing, bowing, and prostration are visible even when the heart behind them is not. *Spending* engages property, whose transfer is visible both to the giver and the recipient. The Qur'an therefore teaches a movement from the least visible commitment to the most visible, yet it also teaches that the source of the most visible is the most hidden: what is spent is *what We provided*. The sequence thus forms a circuit: the unseen provides, the seen receives, the seen returns a portion to the unseen’s creatures, and the act of returning confirms the belief in the unseen that began the circuit. To isolate spending as economics without belief would be to mistake the hand’s motion for the heart’s logic; to isolate belief as private without spending would be to mistake the heart’s claim for the hand’s proof.

**The Prayer Paired With Alms in Twenty-Seven Places**

The pairing of *ṣalāh* and *zakāh*/*infāq* recurs more than two dozen times in the muṣḥaf, and the constancy matters. Prayer without alms risks becoming aesthetic — a private discipline whose social cost is never counted. Alms without prayer risks becoming administrative — a redistribution whose orientation is never named. By placing them as consecutive marks of the mindful, the verse refuses both reductions. The one who stands in the night (32:16) is the one who spends by day, and the one who spends is the one whose spending is taught by his standing. That is why the call to prayer ends where it does: *“Come to prayer, come to prosperity”* (*ḥayya ʿalā al-ṣalāh, ḥayya ʿalā al-falāḥ*) — the second summons answers the first, so that flourishing (*falāḥ*, the same root as *mufliḥūn* in 2:5) is presented as what happens when the established prayer spills over into the open hand.

**The Unseen Includes the Hour You Cannot Schedule**

Among the unseen is not only the metaphysical but the temporal: the Day that is sure yet unscheduled. The Qur'an distinguishes between unseen that is principle (God) and unseen that is appointment (the Hour), precisely to keep belief from becoming speculation about hidden forces while ignoring the hidden appointment that will govern every force. To believe in the unseen is therefore also to live within a calendar whose final date is withheld, so that spending now cannot be calculated against a known deadline. The mindful spend *from* what was provided not because they can afford to lose it before an audit they have scheduled, but because the audit’s date is among the unseen they already affirm.

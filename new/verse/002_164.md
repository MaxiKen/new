## Sūrah al-Baqarah 2:164

> Indeed, in the creation of the heavens and the earth; the alternation of the day and the night; the ships that sail the sea for the benefit of humanity; the rain sent down by Allah from the skies, reviving the earth after its death; the scattering of all kinds of creatures throughout; the shifting of the winds; and the clouds drifting between the heavens and the earth—˹in all of this˺ are surely signs for people of understanding.

**Expanded Commentary**

**A List of Signs**

The verse sets out a series of phenomena and states that they are signs for people of understanding, and the list moves from the heavens and the earth to the winds and the clouds. The sentence has no verb of command in it, and its whole force lies in the claim that what it describes points beyond itself.

The Book returns to lists of this kind in many chapters, and the community's scholars noted that the sequence moves from the largest things a person can observe to things that pass in the ordinary course of a day. What the reader receives is not an argument built from unfamiliar material but an inventory of what is already in front of him.

**The Heavens and the Earth**

The first item in the list is the creation of the heavens and the earth, and the Book treats the pair as the broadest statement of what God has made. The sūrah has already described God as the creator of the heavens and the earth, and the community's understanding of the phrase includes everything above and everything below.

The placing of this item first gives the reader the frame within which the rest of the list is to be read. The phenomena that follow belong to the world that the first phrase names, and the sentence asks the reader to consider them as parts of a single creation rather than as separate curiosities.

**The Alternation of Day and Night**

The second item is the change of night and day, and the Book uses the same phrase in several chapters where the signs of God are listed. The regularity of the alternation is the point, since the sequence of light and darkness governs the work, the rest, and the worship of every creature.

The verse pairs the alternation with the creation of the heavens and the earth, and the effect is to move the reader from the scale of the universe to the cycle of his own day. The community's scholars read the phrase as a reference to the measure of time itself, and they noted that the Book's commands concerning prayer and fasting are organised by the same alternation that the verse names among the signs.

**The Ships and the Rain**

The list then names the ships that move across the sea for the benefit of people and the water that God sends down from the sky to revive the earth after its death. The ships are the only manufactured objects in the list, and their inclusion in a series of natural phenomena is instructive.

The commentators observed that the ships are the works of human hands and yet are listed alongside the rain and the winds, since the possibility of sailing and the safety of the vessel depend on what the creation provides. The rain reviving a dead earth is a subject the Book returns to frequently, and the community's teachers used it as the image of resurrection. The two items therefore combine human effort and divine provision in a single statement about what the reader should see.

**The Scattering of Creatures**

The verse mentions the scattering of every kind of creature throughout the earth, and the phrase covers everything that moves and lives. The wording indicates distribution and variety rather than a single class of being, and the reader is asked to consider the abundance of living things as one of the signs.

The Book's lists of signs frequently include the creation of animals, and the community's scholars noted how the phrase draws the reader's attention to what he usually passes without thought. What the sentence establishes is that the sign is not confined to the spectacular; the ordinary presence of living creatures is included in the same category as the heavens and the earth, and the reader is invited to see the whole of the creation as evidence.

**The Winds and the Clouds**

The list closes with the shifting of the winds and the clouds that move between the heavens and the earth, and the commentators noted that the Arabic word for wind shares a root with the word for spirit. The association was read by some as a reminder of the unseen forces that the Book mentions elsewhere.

The clouds are described as being made to move between the sky and the land, and the Book uses the same image in passages that describe the sending of rain and the provision that follows it. The two items complete the sequence from the largest scale to the immediate experience of weather, and the sentence then states the conclusion that the list was assembled to support.

**"Signs for People of Understanding"**

The verse closes by stating that in all of this there are signs for people of understanding, and the qualification identifies the audience for whom the list functions as evidence. The Book's repeated connection of signs with understanding shows that the material is available to everyone and that the perception of it is not.

The phrase also explains why the passage follows the statement of God's oneness. What the previous verse declares, the present one demonstrates, and the reader who considers the list with attention arrives at the claim the declaration has made. The Book's method here is to let the creation speak, and the community's scholars saw in the verse an invitation to study the world as a book whose author is the one the previous verse named.

**The Verse as the Ground of the Passage**

The sentence prepares the reader for what follows, where the Book describes those who take rivals beside God despite the signs and describes the love that the believers hold for Him. The evidence has been set out before the contrast is drawn, and the reader receives the account of the two attitudes after having been shown what the creation itself displays.

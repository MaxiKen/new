## Sūrah al-Nisāʾ 4:163

> Indeed, We have sent revelation to you ˹O Prophet˺ as We sent revelation to Noah and the prophets after him. We also sent revelation to Abraham, Ishmael, Isaac, Jacob, and his descendants, ˹as well as˺ Jesus, Job, Jonah, Aaron, and Solomon. And to David We gave the Psalms.

### Expanded Commentary

**One stream of revelation**

The Prophet receives revelation as Noah and the prophets after him did. The long list—Abraham, Ishmael, Isaac, Jacob, their descendants, Jesus, Job, Jonah, Aaron, Solomon, and David—places Islam within the history of prophetic guidance. Each prophet is distinct, yet the source is one.

The list includes figures associated with different aspects of faith: Abraham’s surrender, Job’s patience, Jonah’s repentance, Moses’s leadership, David’s scripture, and Jesus’s sign. The Qur’an does not present these as competing gods or unrelated religions. It calls people to recognize the shared call to worship Allah and live justly.

David’s Psalms are specifically mentioned, affirming that revelation took diverse forms. Muslims believe in their divine origin while receiving the Qur’an as the final criterion. Respect for earlier scripture therefore combines reverence with reliance on the final revelation.

**A common prophetic inheritance**

Remembering the long list of prophets can weaken ethnic pride and strengthen reverence for revelation across history. Each messenger entered a particular people and time, yet the call to God was one. The Qur’an’s final message asks Muslims to inherit that universal concern rather than turn prophecy into tribal property.

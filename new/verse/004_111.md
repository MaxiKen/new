## Sūrah al-Nisāʾ 4:111

> And whoever commits a sin—it is only to their own loss. Allah is AllKnowing, All-Wise.

### Expanded Commentary

**Every sin returns to its doer**

Whoever commits a sin bears its loss. The statement establishes personal responsibility and rejects the idea that wrongdoing can be transferred to an innocent scapegoat. A family, party, or community may suffer consequences, but moral guilt does not magically move from the person who chose the act to the person he blames.

**Knowledge and wisdom in judgment**

Allah knows the circumstances, intention, and impact of every sin. His wisdom means that judgment is neither arbitrary nor reducible to human calculations. This should make the sinner repent rather than despair and make the observer cautious about declaring another person’s final fate.

The verse also protects the innocent from inherited blame. Children do not carry a parent’s sin, and a whole group is not guilty merely because one member acted. Social responsibility may require institutions to answer for patterns, but divine justice still distinguishes each person’s deed.

**No moral outsourcing**

A person cannot make a group, parent, leader, or victim bear the guilt of his chosen act. Social conditions can influence behaviour and may create institutional responsibility, but personal accountability remains. The verse helps communities discuss causes without erasing the agent who must repent and repair.

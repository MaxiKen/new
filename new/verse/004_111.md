## Sūrah al-Nisāʾ 4:111

> And whoever commits a sin—it is only to their own loss. Allah is AllKnowing, All-Wise.

**Expanded Commentary**

**Whoever Commits a Sin — Only to Own Loss**

The verse states *wa man yaksib ithman fa innamā yaksibuhū ʿalā nafsihī* — and whoever commits, earns, a sin, it is only upon, against, his own self, to his own loss. *Yaksib* — to earn, to acquire, to commit — was explained as the Book’s commerce language: sin is earning, as a good deed is multiplied many times over by grace (4:40), and as striving earns degree (4:96). *Ithm* — sin, offence — was read broadly: theft, false accusation, advocacy for deceit, hiding from people by night. *ʿAlā nafsihī* — only upon himself, against his self — was explained as that the weight does not transfer to the advocate who disputed for him in this life (4:109) nor to the innocent upon whom it is later thrown (4:112). The sūrah has shown the same principle where *“those who wrong themselves”* are not to be advocated for (4:107), where *“whoever wrongs themselves then seeks forgiveness finds Him”* (4:110), and where *“whoever does evil or wrongs themselves”* is the broadest door to seeking. Here the principle is the ledger: to *earn* sin is to deposit loss into the self’s account, because God does not wrong even an atom — He measures the atom exact (4:40) and knows what is in hearts (4:63). Early commentators stressed the *innamā* — only — as restriction: the sin’s burden is not on the clan, not on the Jew falsely accused, not on the community divided into two groups (4:88); it is on the earner.

**Allah Is All-Knowing, All-Wise**

The seal *wa kāna Allāhu ʿAlīman Ḥakīmā* — And God is All-Knowing, All-Wise — was explained as the attribution that makes the ledger certain. *ʿAlīm* — All-Knowing — was read as knowing the earning while hidden from people but not from Him in whose presence they plot by night (4:108), encompassing what they do (4:108), hearing the advocacy in this life and the lack of advocate on the Day (4:109). *Ḥakīm* — All-Wise — was read as wisdom that places each earning on its earner with exactness, that judges between people by what He shows (4:105) and not by eloquence, and that distinguishes mistake requiring freeing or fasting (4:92) from intent requiring Hell unless sought away (4:93). The pair *ʿAlīm Ḥakīm* has sealed mistake law (4:92) and pursuit without faltering (4:104); here it seals earning. The sūrah has said that God is sufficient as Witness (4:79) and Reckoner (4:86); to be All-Knowing is to be the Witness who never misses, to be All-Wise is to be the Reckoner who never wrongs. The burden on the self is therefore not a metaphor but a measured account with the One who knows and judges with wisdom.

**A Verse That Ends Clan Transfer of Guilt**

Placed after *“whoever commits evil then seeks forgiveness finds Him”* (4:110), this verse ends clan transfer of guilt. The preceding passage concerned a theft where advocates sought to transfer guilt from a Muslim thief to a Jewish neighbour by hiding armour with him and coaching story by night (4:105–112). The verse declares that transfer fails at the divine ledger: whoever earns sin earns only against himself, even if people are persuaded. The sūrah has warned that most secret talks have no good except charity, kindness, reconciliation (4:114), and that throwing sin on the innocent bears slander and blatant sin (4:112); this verse is the premise for both — sin stays where earned, so throwing adds a second sin upon the first. The sūrah has also shown that burdens are lightened because humanity is weak (4:28), yet lightening is not transfer; the helpless hoped to be pardoned (4:99) are not told their sin is on another, but that pardon is hoped from the All-Pardoning. The restriction *ʿalā nafsihī* therefore lightens the community: no need to carry another’s sin, and no ability to.

**Earning Sin as Commerce That Only Loses**

The verb *yaksib* was read to keep commerce in view: the sinner thinks to *earn* — gain, profit — by theft, by shifting blame, by seeking fleeting gain denying peace (4:94), yet the verse says the earning is *ʿalā* — upon, against — the self, as a debt, not *lahū* — for him as profit. The sūrah has contrasted true earning — *“God has promised each a fine reward”* (4:95) and *“many havens and abundance”* for the emigrant (4:100) — with false earning — devouring orphans’ fire (4:10) and taking dowry unjustly (4:20). To *earn* sin is therefore to be the merchant who puts loss into his own pouch while thinking he put gain. Classical notes added that the *ithm* here is the general term that includes *khaṭīʾah* — error — and *buhtān* — slander — made explicit next (4:112); all are earned only as loss. The only reversal of that loss is not another’s advocacy (4:109) but seeking forgiveness, which finds Him All-Forgiving, Most Merciful (4:110), and even then the original earning remains recorded until covered.

**A Short Verse That Anchors the Next Two**

Though only *“whoever commits sin does so only to his own loss, God is All-Knowing, All-Wise,”* the verse anchors the next two: *whoever earns evil or sin then throws it on the innocent bears slander and blatant sin* (4:112), and *had not God’s grace been upon you they would have deceived you yet they only deceive themselves* (4:113). Both assume retained ownership: throwing does not move ownership but adds slander to it; deceiving the Prophet ﷺ would have deceived only the deceiver’s self. The sūrah that judges between people by what God shows (4:105) and that warns secret plotters they plot in His presence (4:108) here inscribes the ledger: knowledge and wisdom guarantee that what is earned stays where earned, so that justice — returning trusts, judging fairly (4:58), verifying reports (4:83), being sure before fighting (4:94) — can proceed without fear that clan eloquence will move burden from where God placed it.

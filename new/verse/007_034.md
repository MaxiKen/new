## Sūrah al-Aʿrāf 7:34

> For each community there is an appointed term. When their time arrives, they can neither delay it for a moment, nor could they advance it.

**Expanded Commentary**

**The Appointed Term**

The verse states that every community has a term appointed for it, and that when the term arrives it can be neither delayed by a moment nor advanced. The Book repeats the statement in several passages, and the sūrah of Yūnus states that every community has a term and that when it comes there is no delaying it (10:49); the sūrah of the Rocky Tract and the sūrah of the Believers state that no community can outrun its term or delay it (15:5; 23:43); the sūrah of the Sheba, the sūrah of Noah, and the sūrah of the Bee apply the same language to the term of peoples and of individuals (34:30; 71:4; 16:61). The verse in front of us states the principle in the form of a declaration.

The statement follows the list of the forbidden things, and it supplies the framework in which the commands operate. The community that has received the revelation has a fixed span in which to obey, and the span is not extended by the prosperity of the people or shortened by their delay. The Book's warnings about the destroyed communities rest on the same principle, since each community's destruction came at the term that had been set for it.

**Terms for Communities and for Individuals**

The Book applies the term to peoples and to individual human beings, and the two uses belong together. The sūrah of the Heights describes the destruction of the communities that refused their messengers, and each of those destructions is presented as the arrival of a term. The sūrah of the Family of Imran states that no soul dies except by the permission of God at a term appointed, and the sūrah of the Sheba gives the same language for the death of individuals. The verse in front of us states the communal form of the principle, and the sūrah's narratives show its individual application.

The two levels connect for the reader of the sūrah, since the audience lives inside a community with its own term and inside a life with its own span. The Book's addresses to the community frequently refer to the possibility of sudden death and to the arrival of the reckoning without warning, and the verse in front of us supplies the reason for the urgency.

**The Impossibility of Delay and Advance**

The two halves of the verse exclude the possibility of any adjustment to the term, and the Book repeats the formula in its accounts of the destruction of the earlier peoples. The statement rules out both the demand for a postponement and the request for an earlier resolution, and the sūrah of the Prophets reports the audience's demand that the punishment be brought forward. The audience's challenge to the Prophet in Makkah included both kinds of request, and the verse in front of us answers both.

The Book's treatment of the demand for signs and for punishment appears in its accounts of the messengers whose peoples asked when the promise would be fulfilled, and the sūrah of the Heights itself refers to the request for the punishment to be hastened in its later verses. The verse in front of us states the fixed character of the term, and the sūrah's narrative of the destroyed communities shows the arrival that no request could move.

**The Term and the Warning**

The fixed term gives the warning its urgency, and the sūrah's instructions are addressed to people whose time is finite. The Book's calls to repentance are frequently accompanied by the reminder that the term may arrive before the intention is carried out, and the sūrah of the Hypocrites states that God reprieves no one when his term comes. This verse states the arrangement, and the sūrah's remaining verses describe what happens when the term of a community arrives.

The Book's depictions of the last day include the statement that the people will confess the truth when it is too late, and the sūrah's opening verses have already presented the confession of the destroyed communities. The term in front of us is the boundary that separates the time of action from the time of accounting, and the sūrah's reader is placed on the near side of it.

**The Term and the Messengers**

The verse's placement before the passage on the messengers gives the term a particular significance. The messengers are sent to communities that have a term, and their mission operates within the span that the community has been given. The sūrah's accounts of the prophets and their peoples follow this arrangement, since each messenger calls his people while the term is running and each people's destruction follows the expiry of the period of warning.

The Book's stated pattern is that no community is destroyed until a messenger has been sent to it, and the sūrah of the Cattle states that God would not destroy a community until He had sent a messenger to it. The passage in front of us supplies the time frame of the mission, and the passage that follows states the terms on which the messengers deliver their message.

**Where the Verse Sits**

The verse stands thirty-fourth in the chapter, and it follows the list of the forbidden things and precedes the address to the children of Adam about the messengers. The passage as a whole moves from the commands to the communities that received them, and the term in front of us supplies the frame within which the commands and the responses to them take place.

The reader has been given the provision, the limits, and now the duration. The sūrah's narratives of the peoples who refused their messengers are the record of what happened when the terms of those peoples arrived, and the audience is addressed as a community whose own term is running.

**The Transition to the Next Verse**

The next verse addresses the children of Adam and states that when messengers from among them come reciting the revelations of their Lord, those who are mindful and mend their conduct will have no fear and no grief (7:35). The statement of the fixed term is followed by the description of the messengers who come within it, and the promises attached to the response are named.

The two verses together present the mission and its time. The community has a term, the messengers come within it with the revelation, and the response determines whether the term's end brings the promised security or the warning that the following verse describes.

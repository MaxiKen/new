## Sūrah al-Baqarah 2:53

> And ˹remember˺ when We gave Moses the Scripture—the standard ˹to distinguish between right and wrong˺ that perhaps you would be ˹rightly˺ guided.

**Expanded Commentary**

**Scripture and Standard — Two Gifts in One Giving**

“And remember when We gave Moses the Scripture — the standard to distinguish between right and wrong — that perhaps you would be rightly guided” (2:53). Wa idh ātaynā Mūsā al-kitāba wa al-furqān — “the Book and the Criterion.” The dual naming is deliberate. Kitāb is the inscribed guidance — Torah — given. Furqān is distinction, discrimination, the capacity or instrument that separates haq from baṭil. The source parses the relation in three compatible ways without forcing one: furqān as description of the Book itself (the Book as criterion), as a power of discernment given to Moses and Aaron (21:48), or as a prophetic attribute like wisdom that accompanies the Book (cf. 2:151; 3:164; 4:54 where the Prophet is given the Book and wisdom). In each reading, revelation is both text and lens: words and the ability to tell with them.

The translation’s gloss — “the standard to distinguish between right and wrong” — captures furqān’s function: not only information, but division — day of furqān as the Day of Discrimination (8:41). The Qur’an elsewhere names itself al-Furqān (25:1), keeping the same function for the confirming revelation that will later ask belief (2:41).

**Given After Pardon — Order as Theology**

The sūrah’s order is again theological: deliverance from the sea (2:50), calf-worship (2:51), pardon “even then” (2:52), then Book and Criterion (2:53). Guidance is not given because the people proved worthy in the interval; it is given after they proved unworthy and were still forgiven. The sequence guards two errors: to think guidance is earned by perfect waiting, and to think pardon leaves a people without direction. Pardon precedes furqān so that furqān is received as gift, not as wages.

**Why Both Kitāb and Furqān Are Named**

A book without furqān can be recited while mixing truth with falsehood (2:42) and hiding while knowing (2:42). Furqān without a book can be claimed as inspiration without measure. To give both is to give measure and means together: a readable criterion and a readable text that is criterion. The Children of Israel, who had just mixed and hidden in practice (calf), now receive the instrument that would make mixing visible.

**Laʿallakum Tahtadūn — That You May Be Guided**

The purpose clause — laʿallakum tahtadūn — “that perhaps you may be guided,” “that you may be rightly guided” (2:53) — matches the prior “that perhaps you may be grateful” (2:52). Gratitude (shukr) after pardon and guidance (hudā) through criterion are paired aims. Gratitude looks back to what was spared; guidance looks forward to how to walk. The sūrah, which opened with “guidance for the mindful” (2:2) and “when guidance comes from Me, whoever follows it — no fear, no grief” (2:38), now names the historical form that guidance took for Israel: Mosaic Book as furqān. The universal promise is given historical address.

**Furqān as More Than a Book**

If furqān is also a power of discrimination bestowed, then the gift is not only pages but perception. Moses and Aaron receiving furqān (21:48) suggests the prophet is endowed to divide with wisdom, not only to transmit. Similarly, the Prophet Muḥammad is given the Book and wisdom (ḥikmah). Wisdom, like furqān, is not reducible to text; it is the capacity to apply text truly. The sūrah’s subsequent counsels — seek help in patience and prayer (2:45), be humble and certain of return (2:46) — become the character that keeps furqān functional: the Book distinguishes, but only the discerning heart keeps the distinction.

**From Criterion to Repentance**

The next verse will show how criterion works in practice: after receiving Book and Criterion, the people are told by Moses to repent — “repent to your Maker and slay yourselves; that is better for you with your Maker” (2:54). Criterion makes wrong visible and remedy costly. The standard that was given to distinguish (2:53) will next be used to judge the calf and prescribe the turning.

**Living the Standard**

- Treat Scripture as standard, not only story. To read Torah or Qur’an as history alone is to have kitāb without furqān. To read for distinction — “does this separate right from wrong for me today?” — is to receive both.
- Keep the two aims together. After any pardon (2:52), ask both “how shall I be grateful?” and “how shall I be guided?” One without the other leaves mercy idle.
- Expect guidance to be both given and grown. A book is given; discernment is cultivated. Prayer and patience (2:45) remain the help by which furqān becomes sight.

**What Not to Claim**

This verse does not settle whether kitāb and furqān are one thing or two in every instance; the source preserves legitimate plurality. It also does not name which tablets or scrolls physically constituted the first gift. What is firm is the giving: to Moses, the Book and the Criterion together, so that Israel pardoned after grave wrong might yet be guided (2:53).

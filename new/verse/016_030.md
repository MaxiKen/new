## Sūrah al-Naḥl 16:30

> **And ˹when˺ it is said to those mindful ˹of Allah˺, “What has your Lord revealed?” They say, “All the best!” For those who do good in this world, there is goodness. But far better is the ˹eternal˺ Home of the Hereafter. How excellent indeed is the home of the righteous:**

**Expanded Commentary**

**A Contrasting Answer To Same Question**

Wa-idhā qīla li-l-ladhīna ittaqaw mādhā anzala rabbukum qālū khayran — and when it is said to those who are mindful of Allah, what has your Lord revealed, they say, all the best, goodness. This mirrors verse 24 where same question was asked to deniers and they said ancient fables. Now question asked to muttaqīn, those mindful, reverent, God-conscious, and they say khayran, goodness, best. Khayr means good, best, beneficial. Al-Ṭabarī explains that muttaqīn answer with khayran because they see revelation as good, containing guidance, mercy, law, stories that teach, promises that encourage. Their answer is short but comprehensive: all the best. Al-Zamakhsharī notes that khayran is indefinite, indicating great goodness, all kinds of goodness, not limited. The contrast between fables and goodness reveals hearts: deniers see revelation as old tales to dismiss, mindful see it as best to follow. Same revelation, opposite perceptions, due to difference in taqwa.

**Goodness In This World For Those Who Do Good**

Li-l-ladhīna aḥsanū fī hādhihi al-dunyā ḥasanatun — for those who do good in this world, there is goodness. Aḥsanū from ḥ-s-n means to do good, excellence, beautiful deeds. Ḥasanah means goodness, good reward, good outcome. The verse says those who do good in this world will have goodness in this world as well as Hereafter. Al-Ṭabarī says ḥasanah in world includes provision, health, honor, good mention, help. Al-Rāzī adds that doing good includes both faith and righteous deeds, and goodness in world includes contentment, guidance, and blessings that are experienced as good even if not material. The phrase fī hādhihi al-dunyā, in this world, indicates that goodness is not delayed only to Hereafter; it begins here. This is encouragement: doing good is not loss in world; it brings goodness now.

**Far Better Is Home Of Hereafter**

Wa-la-dāru al-ākhirati khayrun — but home of Hereafter is better. Lā here is emphatic, surely, indeed. Dāru al-ākhirah, home of Hereafter, is better than goodness in world. Al-Qurṭubī explains that even if worldly goodness is given to righteous, Hereafter is better in quality, quantity, and duration. Worldly goodness is mixed with hardship, temporary, limited; Hereafter goodness is pure, eternal, unlimited. The comparison wa-la-dāru al-ākhirati khayrun is similar to 87:17: *But Hereafter is better and more lasting* (87:17). The verse does not say worldly goodness is bad; it says Hereafter is better, so believer should not be satisfied with only worldly goodness but should seek Hereafter as well. This balances encouragement for worldly goodness with reminder that ultimate goal is Hereafter.

**How Excellent Is Home Of Righteous**

Wa-la-niʿma dāru al-muttaqīn — and how excellent indeed is home of righteous. Niʿma is exclamation of praise: how excellent, how good. Dāru al-muttaqīn, home of those mindful, those who are conscious of Allah. This mirrors verse 29's *evil home for arrogant* with opposite: excellent home for righteous. Al-Zamakhsharī notes that niʿma vs bi'sa creates deliberate contrast: bi'sa mathwā al-mutakabbirīn, evil home for arrogant, vs niʿma dāru al-muttaqīn, excellent home for righteous. Two homes, two groups, two answers. The verse ends with colon in translation, indicating that next verse will describe that home: Gardens of Eternity. The colon is translator's addition to show connection.

**Why Mindful See Revelation As Goodness**

Muttaqīn, those mindful, see revelation as khayr because taqwa, mindfulness, opens eyes to see goodness. A heart that is mindful of Allah recognizes that what comes from Allah must be good, even if it includes commands that require effort or stories that warn. The deniers' hearts were munkiratun, denying, and mustakbirūn, arrogant, so they saw fables. The mindful's hearts are aware, humble, so they see goodness. This shows that perception of revelation depends on heart state, not only on text. The same sun hardens clay and softens wax; same revelation hardens arrogant heart and softens mindful heart. The sūrah's blessings catalogue was meant to cultivate taqwa by showing uncountable blessings, so that when question what has your Lord revealed is asked, answer will be goodness.

**Linguistic Note On Khayr And Dār**

Khayr from kh-y-r means good, best, choice, from same root as ikhtiyār, choice, indicating what is chosen as good. In this verse, khayran is accusative, meaning they said: He revealed goodness, or it is goodness. Ḥasanah from ḥ-s-n means good, beautiful, from same root as iḥsān, excellence. Dār from d-w-r means house, home, abode, place where one circles, resides. Muttaqīn from w-q-y means those who guard themselves, mindful, from same root as taqwā. The verse's structure — question, short answer khayran, then elaboration on goodness in world and Hereafter, then praise of home of righteous — moves from concise to expansive, reflecting that goodness of revelation cannot be captured in one word khayran but needs elaboration: goodness now, better home later, excellent home for righteous.

**Goodness Now And Better Later**

The promise li-l-ladhīna aḥsanū ḥasanah — for those who do good there is goodness — is both immediate and future. In world, goodness includes guidance, tranquility, provision that is blessed, and good reputation. Al-Rāzī notes that aḥsanū includes doing good to others, not only worship, so goodness in return includes love of people and help from Allah. The verse says fī hādhihi al-dunyā, in this world, to emphasize that goodness is not only deferred. Many think righteousness means losing worldly enjoyment; this verse says opposite: those who do good get goodness here too. Then it adds Hereafter is better, so believer does not become attached to worldly goodness as final goal. The balance is precise: enjoy worldly goodness as gift, but seek better home.

**Why The Home Is Called Excellent**

Niʿma dāru al-muttaqīn — how excellent is home of righteous — is praise that invites desire. The Qur'an often uses niʿma to praise Paradise, as in 3:136 and 29:58. The home is excellent because it is free from hardship, pure, eternal, and contains whatever righteous desire, as next verse says: *they will have whatever they desire* (16:31). Al-Qurṭubī explains that dār here is excellent because its Builder is Allah, its inhabitants are righteous, its duration is forever, and its contents are beyond imagination. The colon at end of translation indicates that description follows, linking this verse to next. The listener who hears excellent home will naturally ask, what is that home? Next verse answers: Gardens of Eternity under which rivers flow.

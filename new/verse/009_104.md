## Sūrah al-Tawbah 9:104

> Do they not know that Allah alone accepts the repentance of His servants and receives ˹their˺ charity, and that Allah alone is the Accepter of Repentance, Most Merciful?

**Expanded Commentary**

**Acceptance Belongs to Allah Alone**

The verse directs attention away from human approval and toward Allah's acceptance. People can advise, forgive, receive a donation, or witness a change, but they do not own the final judgment. Allah alone accepts repentance and receives charity in the sense that He gives the act its spiritual value.

This protects repentance from becoming a public negotiation. A servant does not need every person to announce that he is forgiven before he can return to Allah. He does need to repair the rights of people he harmed. The two duties are connected but not identical. Allah judges the heart and the whole act, while people handle the worldly consequences that belong to them.

**Repentance Is Received, Not Performed**

A servant may perform the outward steps of repentance and still need sincerity. Turning away from the wrong, regretting it, and resolving not to return are not a performance for an audience. They are a return of the whole person toward Allah. The verse's language of acceptance reminds the servant that repentance is a gift he asks Allah to receive.

This prevents pride after reform. A person may have apologized, repaid, or changed his habits, yet he still hopes rather than boasts. He does not claim that his own effort forced Allah's acceptance. He does what Allah loves and asks Allah to complete the mercy. Humility keeps repentance alive after the first emotion fades.

**Charity Is Received Beyond Its Visible Channel**

When Allah receives charity, the gift's material route does not exhaust its meaning. The money may go to a poor person, a public need, or a lawful cause. Allah receives the intention, sacrifice, and obedience behind it. This is why a small gift can be accepted while a large gift offered for pride may be spiritually empty.

The believer should still give through sound channels. Sincerity does not excuse careless distribution, fraud, or the neglect of a specific right. He checks the need, protects dignity, and gives from lawful wealth. Then he trusts Allah to accept the act beyond what human eyes can measure.

**Only Allah Can Complete the Return**

The verse names Allah as the Accepter of Repentance. The Arabic concept suggests that Allah turns toward the servant when the servant turns back. This reciprocity is full of hope. The repentant person is not climbing toward a distant Lord who refuses to notice him. Allah's mercy is already making the return possible.

The servant must not interpret this hope as permission to postpone. A door being open does not mean that a person should keep walking away from it. He returns now, while the harm can be repaired and the heart can still respond. Allah's mercy is the reason to hurry, not a reason to delay.

**Human Rejection Does Not Decide Divine Acceptance**

People may remember a past wrong and doubt a person's change. Their caution may be reasonable, especially when trust was broken. Yet their doubt does not decide whether Allah has accepted the repentance. The servant continues to act truthfully without demanding that everyone applaud the transformation.

This also means that a person cannot use public forgiveness as proof that Allah has accepted him. A community may be impressed by a speech while Allah knows that the old intention remains. The servant seeks a clean heart rather than a clean image. Allah's acceptance is the hope that makes both correction and patience worthwhile.

**Mercy Joins Seriousness to Hope**

The closing name, Most Merciful, prevents the verse from becoming a cold statement about divine control. Allah receives repentance and charity because His mercy welcomes the servant who returns. Seriousness remains: the wrong must be abandoned and the gift must be sincere. Mercy does not make those conditions meaningless; it makes them possible.

A believer therefore lives between fear and hope. He fears offering a false repentance, and he hopes that Allah will accept a true one. He gives without boasting and repairs without despair. The final acceptance is with Allah, whose mercy is greater than the servant's past and whose knowledge is deeper than any public judgment.

This teaching also prevents a repentant person from making another human being the owner of his spiritual future. He seeks forgiveness from the people he harmed and accepts their rights, but he asks Allah for acceptance. If people forgive him, he thanks Allah; if they remain cautious, he continues to act truthfully. The acceptance of charity follows the same pattern. Give through a lawful channel, protect the recipient's dignity, and do not demand that the gift prove your superiority. Allah receives the act with knowledge of its motive, its cost, and the change it produces in the giver.

This is also why the believer should not despair when people doubt his repentance. He may need to accept their caution while continuing the work Allah asks. A changed heart does not require immediate social comfort. It requires faithfulness when no one is ready to celebrate it. Allah receives the charity that people overlook and knows the repentance that people question. The servant keeps repairing, avoids demanding praise, and refuses to use divine mercy as a reason to ignore human rights. Acceptance belongs to Allah, while restitution belongs to the relationships he damaged.

The acceptance of repentance also gives a community a reason to leave room for new service. A person who once failed may later become careful with the same trust he violated. He is not appointed again without evidence of change, but neither is he forbidden from every useful role forever. Allah can transform a former weakness into knowledge that protects others. The community tests this change through time, and the repentant servant accepts the test without resentment. He wants Allah's acceptance more than a quick restoration of status.

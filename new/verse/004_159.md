## Sūrah al-Nisāʾ 4:159

> Every one of the People of the Book will definitely believe in him before his death. And on the Day of Judgment Jesus will be a witness against them.

**Expanded Commentary**

**Every One of the People of the Book Will Believe in Him Before His Death**

The verse states *wa in min ahli al-kitābi illā la yuʾminanna bihī qabla mawtihī* — and there is none among the People of the Book except he will surely believe in him before his death, before their death, before his. *In min ahli al-kitābi illā* — there is none among People of the Book except — was explained as universal quantifier: every one of the Jews and Christians given Scripture before you (4:131) will believe. *La yuʾminanna bihī* — will surely believe in him — was read with emphasis: the same *la* + *nūn* as Satan’s vow, now as God’s oath; belief will be in Jesus — *ʿĪsā ibnu Maryam* — the Messiah whom they boasted killing but did not, whom God raised to Himself (4:157–158), the Word through Mary and spirit from Him (4:171). *Qabla mawtihī* — before his death — was read two ways: before the death of the Person of the Book — each will believe when death nears and truth becomes clear, as *“repentance not accepted when death is starting and he cries now I repent”* (4:18) — too late; and before the death of Jesus himself upon his return before the Hour, when he will descend and break cross, as reports state (Bukhārī 3448; Muslim 155). Both readings were kept: the People of the Book will see before their own death the truth of Jesus, and collectively before Jesus’ own second death the People of the Book will believe. The sūrah has shown that those who distinguish between God and messengers saying we believe in some and disbelieve in some desire a way between (4:150) — they are true disbelievers (4:151); this belief before death is the undoing of that distinguishing, as the People of the Book finally accept Jesus as messenger not god.

**And on the Day He Will Be a Witness Against Them**

The second clause *wa yawma al-qiyāmati yakūnu ʿalayhim shahīdā* — and on the Day of Standing, of Resurrection, he will be a witness against them, upon them — was explained with *shahīd* — witness — the same witness as *“We will bring a witness from every community and bring you as witness against yours”* (4:41) where the Prophet ﷺ is witness against his community, and *“God bears witness and angels bear witness”* (4:166) — here Jesus will be witness against those who made distinction. *ʿAlayhim* — against them, upon them — was read as that Jesus will testify: they said we killed the Messiah, we slandered Mary with mighty *buhtān* (4:156), they said our hearts are *ghulf* (4:155) — he will witness that they knew and denied, as the soul-takers will ask *what were you doing?* (4:97). *Yawma al-qiyāmati* was explained as the Day about which there is no doubt where He will gather you (4:87) and gather hypocrites and disbelievers together in Hell (4:140); witness on that Day decides gathering. Classical notes added that his being *shahīd* against them fits his raising: he who was raised above boasting murder now descends as witness that murder did not occur, and the *shubbiha lahum* — made to appear — will be clarified by the one raised.

**A Verse That Follows Raised With Belief Before Death**

Placed after *rather God raised him to Himself, He is Almighty, All-Wise* (4:158), this verse follows raised with belief before death and witness on the Day. The sequence — demand, thunderbolt, calf, pardon, Mount raised, firm covenant, breaking, rejecting, killing prophets, hearts *ghulf* sealed, denial, slander against Mary, boasting killing Messiah not killed but made to appear, raised — now belief in him before death, witness on the Day — shows that history of the Book’s People does not end with calf or *shubbiha* but with belief and witness. The sūrah has shown that *“We have sent revelation to you as We sent to Noah and prophets after him”* (4:163) — all accepted without distinction (4:152); the People of the Book’s belief in Jesus before his death is the correction of distinction where Jesus is among those prophets. For the weak this is an opening: where loud negative speech about Mary was not allowed (4:148), belief in the son of Mary before death is the speech that repairs, and the raised witness who was not too proud to be servant (4:172) will witness that repair.

**Before His Death — Two Times for Witness**

The phrase *qabla mawtihī* was explained to keep two times for witness: before the death of each of the People of the Book — at the threshold where angels take souls and ask *what were you doing?* (4:97) — truth becomes clear but too late as repentance at death is not accepted (4:18); and before the death of Jesus upon his return — a communal time where the People of the Book will believe while still alive before the Hour, as *“there are messengers We told you and others We did not”* (4:164) shows knowledge is with Him. To say *every one will believe in him before his death* was therefore read not as claim that all die as believers — because humiliating punishment was prepared for true disbelievers (4:151) — but as that at death’s veiling removed, each will see, yet seeing after sight is too late for those who increased in disbelief (4:137). The witness on the Day — *shahīdan ʿalayhim* — is the sūrah’s *“how will it be when We bring witness from every community and you as witness against yours?”* (4:41) — here Jesus is that witness for his community.

**A Short Verse That Anchors Future Belief to Past Boast**

Though only *“Every one of People of the Book will definitely believe in him before his death, and on the Day Jesus will be witness against them,”* the verse anchors future belief to past boast *we killed* (4:157). The boast said we killed the messenger; the witness will say they did not, but doubted, following assumption. The sūrah that bears witness to what He sent with His knowledge and angels bear witness (4:166) makes the raised messenger’s future belief and witness part of that knowledge, so that the *“bring down a book from heaven”* demand (4:153) is answered not with a book from heaven but with a witness from heaven raised and returned. The next verses will turn from history to law: forbidden foods to Jews for wrongdoing and hindering, taking interest despite prohibition, consuming wealth unjustly — painful punishment for disbelievers among them, but those well-grounded in knowledge who believe in what was revealed to you and before — to them great reward (4:160–162).

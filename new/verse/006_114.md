## Sūrah al-Anʿām 6:114

> ˹Say, O Prophet,˺ “Should I seek a judge other than Allah while He is the One Who has revealed for you the Book ˹with the truth˺ perfectly explained?” Those who were given the Scripture know that it has been revealed ˹to you˺ from your Lord in truth. So do not be one of those who doubt.

**Expanded Commentary**

**The Question Put to the Audience**

Should I seek a judge other than Allah? The sentence is a question addressed to the audience on the Prophet's behalf, and it follows a passage in which the people had been asking for signs and swallowing the whisperings of the satans among men and jinn. The demand behind their requests was a ruling: they wanted the matter settled by something they could see, and the question names the absurdity of looking for an arbiter beside the One who had already given His answer.

The Book places the same question in other settings. The sūrah of the Table tells the Prophet to judge between the People of the Book by what God has revealed, and asks who could be a better judge for a people who are certain (5:50). The sūrah of Yūnus ends its account of the revelation with the instruction to follow what has been revealed and to be patient until God passes his judgement, since He is the best of judges (10:109). The question in this verse is the sūrah's own version of that appeal.

**The Book Perfectly Explained**

While He is the One Who has revealed for you the Book with the truth perfectly explained. The second half of the question supplies the reason there is no need for another arbiter: the Book was sent down with the truth and set out in full detail. The Book uses the same description of itself in the sūrah of Fussilat, which calls it a Book whose verses are perfectly explained, a Qur'an in Arabic for people who know (41:3), and the sūrah of the Cattle itself says that God has already explained what He has forbidden (6:119).

The phrase also answers the audience's complaint about the message's content. The sūrah of Yūnus states that the Qur'an is a confirmation of what came before it and a detailed explanation of the Scripture, with no doubt in it, from the Lord of the worlds (10:37). What the verse in front of us says is that the guidance is complete in itself, and that a court of arbitration beside it would be asking for something already provided.

**The Testimony of Those Given the Scripture**

Those who were given the Scripture know that it has been revealed to you from your Lord in truth. The sentence turns from the audience's demands to the testimony of the communities that possessed earlier revelation. The Book records that testimony in many places: the sūrah of the Stories says that those to whom the Scripture had been given before believe in the Qur'an and declare on hearing it that this is the truth from their Lord, which they had already submitted to (28:52–53); the sūrah of the Night Journey says that those gifted with knowledge before it fall on their faces when it is recited to them; the sūrah of the Table describes the tears of those who recognise the truth; and the sūrah of the Family of Imran speaks of those among the People of the Book who believe in what has been revealed to the believers (3:199).

The Makkans lived alongside Jewish and Christian communities, and the sūrah has already appealed to the knowledge of the earlier scriptures in the argument about revelation (6:91–92). The verse in front of us states that the people who know those scriptures recognise the Qur'an's origin, which leaves the audience resisting a message that the other communities recognised.

The sūrah had already made the point at close range. It said in its earlier verses that those to whom God had given the Scripture recognise the Prophet as they recognise their own children, and that a group of them hides the truth knowingly (6:20; 2:146). The verse in front of us is the same argument applied to the origin of the Book rather than to the identity of the messenger, and it uses the witness of the same people.

**The Command Not to Doubt**

So do not be one of those who doubt. The instruction is addressed to the Prophet, and the Book gives similar instructions in other passages. The sūrah of Yūnus tells him that if he is in doubt about what has been revealed to him, he should ask those who read the Scripture before him, since the truth has come from his Lord (10:94). The command in this verse is not a suggestion that the Prophet wavered; it is the Book's way of stating the firmness that the revelation itself supplies.

The sūrah of the Cow gives the same instruction to the believers at the end of its accounts of the earlier scriptures: the truth is from your Lord, so do not be among the doubters (2:147). The Book's habit of addressing its firmest reader with this command shows that the command concerns the message rather than the man, since what is being established is the certainty that the revelation carries with it.

**What the Verse Answers in the Sūrah's Argument**

The verse answers the demand for a sign from a direction the audience had not considered. They wanted a demonstration that would settle the question between them and the messenger, and the Book replies that the arbiter is already present in the revelation they could read or hear. The perfect explanation of the Book and the testimony of the earlier communities are offered as the answer, and both were available in Makkah without a miracle.

The passage that follows this verse states that the word of the Lord has been perfected in truth and justice and that none can change His words (6:115). Together the two verses establish the authority that stands over the whole dispute the sūrah has been describing, and they do it by pointing to what the audience already possessed.

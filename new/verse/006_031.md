## Sūrah al-Anʿām 6:31

> Losers indeed are those who deny the meeting with Allah until the Hour takes them by surprise, then they will cry, “Woe to us for having ignored this!” They will bear ˹the burden of˺ their sins on their backs. Evil indeed is their burden!

**Expanded Commentary**

**Loss Declared Before the Loss Happens**

The sentence passes the verdict in the present tense and then shows the scene that will produce it. Those who deny the meeting with their Lord are already described as the losers, and the description does not wait for the surprise that will make the loss visible. The Book makes the same move in its own compact way when it opens the sūrah of the Passing of Time: humanity is in grave loss (103:2). The verdict is announced in the language of a business account that has already been closed.

The Book's word for this kind of ruin is used elsewhere for the opposite of the successful soul. Successful indeed is the one who purifies their soul, and doomed is the one who corrupts it (91:9–10). Loss in this vocabulary is not a penalty assessed from outside. It is the state a person has become, and the announcement comes early because there is still time for the reader to inspect his own account.

**"The Meeting With Allah" Is the Book's Name for It**

The denial is described as denying the meeting with Allah, not as denying a biological event, and the choice of words shapes the whole passage. The Book uses this expression consistently for the people it is describing: those who do not expect to meet Us, pleased with this worldly life and heedless of the signs (10:7); those who disbelieve in Allah's signs and the meeting with Him, who will have no hope in His mercy (29:23); and those who reject the signs and the meeting with their Lord, whose deeds are rendered void so that they will not be given any weight on Judgment Day (18:105).

The phrase places the issue where scripture wants it, which is the face-to-face accounting rather than the mechanics of revival. Its opposite is stated with the same brevity in the sūrah of the Cow, where the description of the guided includes those who are certain that they will meet their Lord and that to Him they will return (2:46). The difference between the two groups is not information about the afterlife. It is whether the possibility was allowed to govern how they lived.

**The Hour That Arrives Without an Appointment**

The surprise is the detail that turns a denial into a personal emergency. The Book records it with the same verb in several places. The Hour will take them by surprise, leaving them stunned, unable to avert it or delay it (21:40); do they feel secure that an overwhelming torment will not overtake them, or that the Hour will not take them by surprise when they least expect it (12:107); are they waiting for the Hour to come upon them suddenly when they least expect it (43:66).

The Prophet himself is told to say that he has no power over his own benefit or protection except by God's will, and that if he knew the unseen he would have used the knowledge to his advantage (7:188). The concealment of the date is a deliberate arrangement, and it is precisely what the Makkan denial interpreted as evidence that no date existed.

**The Cry of the Delayed Buyer**

Then they will cry, woe to us for having ignored this. The cry is not a request and not a defence. It is the sound of a person recognising, all at once, what he had been in the middle of. The Book records similar cries in the later passages that describe the same discovery, including the plea of those who see the torment and ask whether there is any way back (42:44), and the man who wishes he had sent something forward for his true life (89:24).

The Arabic phrase suggests neglect of something that was present. What was ignored was not an unknown truth but a known warning, which is why the cry carries no complaint against God. Nobody in the scene says that the information was withheld.

**Al-Rāzī's Picture of the Investor Who Loses the Capital**

Al-Rāzī described this regret by borrowing the language of trade, and the metaphor is apt in a sūrah revealed to a merchant city. He compared it to the ruin of an investor who not only fails to earn a return but loses the principal he had put in. Applied to this verse, the faculties God gave a person — reason, hearing, sight, memory, and years of life — were the principal, and the expectation that they would produce an understanding of the truth was the return.

The picture explains why the Book calls these people losers rather than merely guilty. Nothing has been taken from them by force. They were issued capital, they were warned about the use of it, and they spent it on positions that had to be abandoned the moment the Hour arrived.

**Burdens Carried on the Back**

The verse ends with an image of physical transport: they will bear their sins on their backs. The Book uses the same picture for the burden of a warning refused, where the reminder becomes a load in the Hereafter for those who ignored it (20:100–101), and for the leaders who carry their own burdens in full as well as some of the burdens of those they misled without knowledge (16:25). The legal principle that no soul bears the burden of another (6:164) is untouched by the image, since what is being described is a load the person acquired through his own choices.

Evil indeed is their burden. The sentence is short, and it functions as a judgement on the load rather than a comment on its size. A person who has to carry his own record will find that the record weighs more than everything he had accumulated instead of it.

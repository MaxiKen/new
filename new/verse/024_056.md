## Sūrah an-Nūr 24:56

> Moreover, establish prayer, pay alms-tax, and obey the Messenger, so you may be shown mercy.

**Expanded Commentary**

**Practical Foundations of the Promised Vicegerency: aqīmū al-ṣalāh**

Directly following the grand civilizational covenant of verse 55—where God promised political succession (*istikhlāf*) and security to the faithful—the revelation articulates the concrete civic and spiritual obligations required to sustain that blessing: *wa-aqīmū al-ṣalāta wa-ātū al-zakāta wa-aṭīʿū al-rasūla laʿallakum turḥamūn*. Divine promises are never realized through passive entitlement, wishful thinking, or empty slogans.

The transition from cosmic promise to practical duty underscores an essential theological reality: divine favor demands human responsibility. True believers do not wait passively for victory to descend from the sky; they actively build the righteous society that qualifies for divine assistance. By commanding the establishment of prayer, the institutionalization of charity, and unswerving obedience to prophetic guidance, the verse transforms the broad vision of vicegerency into daily spiritual disciplines and civic habits that shape every member of the community.

The first imperative, *aqīmū al-ṣalāh* (establish the prayer), demands the meticulous, collective maintenance of canonical worship across the community. Establishing prayer entails far more than mechanical physical movements performed in private corners; it requires setting up regular congregational assemblies, purifying intentions, maintaining punctual timing, and allowing the moral consciousness forged in prayer to govern public behavior. Prayer serves as the spiritual heartbeat of the community, anchoring society five times a day in divine accountability, fraternal equality, and collective humility before the sovereign King of the cosmos, reminding leaders and citizens alike that all power belongs to God, and that earthly authority must be exercised in submission to celestial sovereignty.

**Purification of Capital and Social Welfare: wa-ātū al-zakāh**

The second pillar commands economic solidarity and social justice: *wa-ātū al-zakāh*, and pay the alms-tax. In the Qur'anic vision of governance, spiritual devotion to God is inextricably bound to compassionate responsibility for human welfare.

*Zakāh* is not an optional charity left to philanthropic whims; it is an institutionalized, mandatory transfer of surplus wealth from affluent citizens to vulnerable populations. By compelling the wealthy to share their capital with orphans, widows, the impoverished, and indebted citizens, *zakāh* prevents the dangerous accumulation of wealth in a few hands, extinguishes class resentment, and guarantees a dignified standard of living for all. A society that claims to worship God while permitting severe economic inequality and poverty to fester in its streets is morally deformed. Establishing *zakāh* ensures that civilizational power functions as a blessing for the weak rather than an engine of aristocratic exploitation, sanctifying commercial prosperity and circulating resources to all members of society.

**Unconditional Allegiance to Prophetic Leadership: wa-aṭīʿū al-rasūl**

The third imperative cements constitutional cohesion: *wa-aṭīʿū al-rasūl*, and obey the Messenger. This command bridges vertical devotion to God with horizontal governance on earth.

Obeying the Prophet Muḥammad ﷺ encompasses total submission to his judicial rulings, ethical directives, diplomatic strategies, and military commands. He is the living exemplar of divine justice, demonstrating how divine revelation translates into commercial integrity, domestic kindness, and public administration. A community that lacks unified, principled leadership inevitably fragments into squabbling tribal factions and ideological chaos. By commanding absolute fidelity to the Messenger, the revelation establishes political discipline, social harmony, and institutional integrity, providing the cohesive leadership necessary to build an enduring civilization capable of withstanding external aggression and internal subversion, binding hearts together in truth.

**The Divine Condition of Communal Mercy: laʿallakum turḥamūn**

The verse concludes with a profound purpose clause: *laʿallakum turḥamūn*, so you may be shown mercy. In classical Qur'anic rhetoric, the particle *laʿalla* expresses hope, expectation, and certainty anchored in divine grace.

Mercy (*al-raḥmah*) is the ultimate goal of all divine legislation. God does not impose liturgical rituals or financial levies to display autocratic power or extract labor; every commandment is an instrument of divine compassion designed to heal human trauma, protect social harmony, and elevate human dignity. Divine mercy descends upon a community not through mystical favoritism, but through the practical establishment of justice, charity, and prophetic obedience. When a society embodies these virtues, it becomes a vessel of celestial blessing, experiencing peace, prosperity, social harmony, and deep spiritual tranquility that shields it from divine retribution.

**Tripartite Framework for Societal Health**

Analyzing the architecture of verse 56 reveals an integrated, threefold blueprint for civilizational vitality. The command to establish prayer (*ṣalāh*) regulates the vertical relationship between the human soul and its Creator; the command to pay zakāh (*zakāh*) regulates horizontal economic relationships among human beings; and the command to obey the Messenger (*ṭāʿat al-rasūl*) regulates public order, legal justice, and political authority.

No society can flourish if any of these three pillars is neglected. A community that prays but neglects social welfare becomes hypocritical and callous; a community that manages economic welfare but rejects divine worship becomes spiritually hollow and morally bankrupt; a community that lacks principled leadership falls into factional anarchy. By uniting prayer, charity, and prophetic obedience in a single sentence, the Qur'an provides the complete formula for comprehensive societal health, moral integrity, and enduring institutional stability across generations, allowing every citizen to flourish under divine care.

**Moving from Imperial Ambition to Spiritual Service**

Verse 56 serves as an indispensable moral safeguard following the promise of earthly dominion in verse 55. Throughout world history, when oppressed groups suddenly attain political power, territorial dominance, and imperial wealth, they routinely replicate the very tyranny, arrogance, and cruelty of the oppressors they replaced.

The Qur'an radically interrupts this destructive historical cycle. God reminds the believers that worldly power is not an end in itself, nor an opportunity for self-congratulatory indulgence or geopolitical chauvinism. The sole justification for exercising authority on earth is to establish prayer, eradicate poverty through zakāh, and uphold prophetic justice. Vicegerency is transformed from imperial ambition into humble, spiritual service. The believers are summoned to govern not as conquering tyrants, but as merciful servants of God dedicated to spreading justice, compassion, equity, and divine peace to all humanity, fulfilling their sacred covenant before their Lord.

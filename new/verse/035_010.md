## Sūrah Fāṭir 35:10

> Whoever seeks honour and power, then ˹let them know that˺ all honour and power belongs to Allah. To Him ˹alone˺ good words ascend, and righteous deeds are raised up by Him. As for those who plot evil, they will suffer a severe punishment. And the plotting of such ˹people˺ is doomed ˹to fail˺.

**Expanded Commentary**

**Real Honour Cannot Be Taken From Its Source**

The verse begins with a desire nearly every person understands: the wish for honour, strength, and a respected place. It does not condemn the desire to be dignified. It redirects the search. All honour and power belong to Allah, so no creature can provide an independent form of glory. A ruler may grant office, a crowd may praise, and wealth may create influence, but each is temporary and borrowed. The person who seeks lasting honour must seek it through the One who owns it.

The Qur'an makes the same correction in 4:139 when people are warned against seeking might through disbelieving patrons. Dependence on a powerful group may produce access, yet it can also require surrendering truth. The present verse calls the seeker away from that bargain. Honour is not obtained by imitating arrogance or by securing control over weaker people. It is received through obedience, humility, and nearness to Allah, even when those qualities look unimpressive to a worldly audience.

**Good Speech Rises Toward Allah**

Good words include remembrance, truthful testimony, sincere prayer, sound counsel, and the declaration of Allah's oneness. Their ascent is not a physical claim about a location in the sky. It expresses acceptance, attention, and return to the Lord. Speech reveals what the heart values, and a good word can heal, teach, restrain injustice, or call a person back from error. It becomes an act of worship when truth and intention meet.

The wording in the Arabic distinguishes the movement of the word from the raising of the deed. The verb for rising describes the good word's ascent, while the verb for raising can describe Allah's acceptance of the righteous action or the action's elevation of the word. Both readings preserve the same principle: speech is not separated from moral reality. A beautiful religious phrase that leaves the will untouched is incomplete. A truthful word can begin the ascent, but its credibility is shown in the life that follows it.

**Deeds Give Weight to What the Tongue Claims**

Righteous deeds are raised by Allah. The passive form in the translation protects the servant from self-congratulation: the act is not elevated by its public visibility but by divine acceptance. Good action verifies the good word. A claim of trust is raised when the person keeps a promise; a claim of compassion is raised when he bears another's difficulty; a claim of worship is raised when obedience survives inconvenience.

The Qur'an uses the image of a good word as a tree in 14:24–26. Its roots are firm and its branches reach upward, while the corrupt word is unstable and easily uprooted. That comparison clarifies this verse's movement. Speech is not judged by volume or eloquence. It needs a rooted life. Allah may raise a small deed done sincerely and leave a celebrated performance without spiritual weight. The scale is hidden from publicity but not from the All-Knowing.

**Evil Plots Carry Their Punishment Within Them**

The verse turns from ascent to plotting. Evil plots are deliberate schemes against truth or people, especially schemes that use concealment and manipulation. They may appear clever because they exploit surprise, alliances, or public confusion. Yet their apparent strength is unstable. A plot built on falsehood must continually protect its first lie, and each new deception creates another point of failure.

The severe punishment answers the intention behind the scheme, not merely the visible damage. A person who plans harm has already directed knowledge and ability against the moral order. Allah knows the hidden plan before it becomes public. The warning is therefore also a protection for potential victims: no secret meeting, private message, or concealed motive lies outside divine awareness. People may fail to expose the scheme, but Allah's judgment does not depend on human discovery.

Sincerity changes the meaning of public recognition. A person may be praised for an act and still ask whether the act was done for Allah or for the audience. Hidden obedience can be more secure because it is less exposed to applause, while a public deed can remain sound when its purpose is service rather than display. Allah raises what people cannot measure.

For that reason, the seeker of honour must inspect the motive before celebrating the result. Allah's elevation is not measured by applause, reach, or the fear a person inspires. It is measured by truthfulness and obedient action.

**False Power Ends in Its Own Failure**

The closing statement says that the plotting of such people will perish. Its failure may appear in this life when a scheme reverses against its makers, or it may be fully revealed only in the Hereafter. Either way, evil cannot produce lasting honour because it cuts itself off from the source of honour. The conspirator may gain a momentary advantage, but the gain carries the mark of loss when measured against Allah's judgment.

This conclusion brings the verse together. The honour-seeker is directed upward to Allah; the good word rises and the righteous deed is raised; the plotter turns sideways into secrecy and finds his scheme collapsing. The difference is not between ambition and passivity. It is between a desire submitted to Allah and a desire that tries to seize what belongs to Him. True power is therefore moral before it is political. Whoever wants dignity should make the tongue truthful, the deed sound, and the heart free of schemes that cannot survive the light.

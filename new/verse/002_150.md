## Sūrah al-Baqarah 2:150

> Wherever you are ˹O Prophet˺, turn your face towards the Sacred Mosque. And wherever you ˹believers˺ are, face towards it, so that people will have no argument against you, except the wrongdoers among them. Do not fear them; fear Me, so that I may ˹continue to˺ perfect My favour upon you and so you may be ˹rightly˺ guided.

**Expanded Commentary**

**The Command With Its Purpose**

The verse states the command a third time and adds the purpose for which it was given: that people may have no argument against the community. The sūrah has been answering objections throughout the passage, and the sentence here explains the change as a measure that closes the avenue of complaint.

The purpose named is a matter of how the community appears among the other communities. The reports describe the objections that had been raised about the community's practice: the Jews complained that the Prophet prayed toward their direction while claiming a religion of his own, and the Muslims were told that the earlier direction was a sign that the Prophet followed the community of the Book. The new direction removed the ground of both objections.

**"Except the Wrongdoers Among Them"**

The sentence acknowledges that some objections will remain, and it identifies those who make them as wrongdoers. The Book does not claim that the change will silence everyone; it states that the arguments of the unjust are not the kind that can be answered by a change in practice, since their objection does not rest on the practice.

The qualification protects the community from the disappointment that follows a failed attempt to satisfy critics. The Book has already described communities that would not accept the new direction even if every proof were brought, and the exception in this verse records the same fact in a single phrase. What remains for the community is to hold its position and to leave the objectors to what they have chosen.

**Fear Directed Away From People**

The command then turns the community's attention to the proper object of fear. The believers are told not to fear the critics and to fear God instead, and the Book uses this pair of commands whenever a community's fidelity is being tested by the disapproval of others.

The sūrah's earlier passages have described the community's fear of its own history, such as the complaint that its members would lose the reward of prayers offered in the previous direction. The sentence here addresses fear of a different kind, which is the fear of what opponents will say, and it supplies the counterweight by naming the one whose judgment determines the outcome.

The command also teaches the community how to weigh disapproval. The objection of a neighbouring community is a small matter when placed beside the judgment that governs the outcome, and the Book uses the comparison whenever it asks believers to hold a position that costs them something. The sentence does not instruct the community to despise those who disagree; it instructs it to give the fear of God the place it has been giving to the opinions of others.

**"So That I May Complete My Favour"**

The sentence names two outcomes of obedience: the completion of God's favour and guidance. The language of favour runs through the sūrah's treatment of the nation of Israel, where the Book recalls the blessings granted to them and their failure to hold to the conditions attached. The verse applies the same vocabulary to the community that has just been given its direction.

The completion of favour is presented as a consequence that belongs to the community's steadfastness rather than as a possession it holds by right. The Book's earlier account of the earlier community shows what becomes of a people that treats its blessings as guarantees, and the verse places the community's favour in the same framework of response.

**Guidance as the Second Outcome**

The sentence closes its purpose clause by naming guidance, and the sūrah opened with the request for guidance in its first chapter and has used the word repeatedly in this one. The direction of prayer is connected here with the larger subject of the community's guidance, and the reader is invited to see the practice as part of what it means to be rightly directed.

The Book has used the same connection in the previous verses, where the direction was given along with the assurance that God guides whom He wills to the straight path. The community's orientation and its understanding are therefore treated as belonging together, and the verse states that the second follows the first.

**The Reassurance Attached to the Command**

The verse's structure supplies a community under criticism with three provisions: a purpose for its practice, a limit to what its opponents can say, and a pair of outcomes that belong to obedience. The Book's method here is not to remove the criticism but to define the position from which the community meets it, and the reader is left with an instruction that has been explained rather than with an order that must simply be obeyed.

The Book’s manner of addressing a community under pressure is consistent across the sūrah: it states the command, names the difficulty, and attaches a promise or a purpose to the obedience required. The verses about the direction of prayer follow that pattern exactly. The community is not told that its critics will fall silent, and it is not promised that the objections will stop; it is told what the command is, what it accomplishes, and what it will lead to.

**The Closing of the Sequence**

With this verse the sūrah concludes the series of commands about the direction of prayer, and the next passages turn to other subjects. The sequence has named the change, answered the objection, described the community's role as witnesses, explained the test that the change involved, and stated the purpose of the new orientation. The reader who has followed it has been given the whole matter, from the Prophet's hope for the House of Abraham to the practice that the community maintains wherever it may be.

The three commands taken together have a shape that the community’s teachers have often noted. The first gives the practice, the second gives the reason, and the third gives the purpose. The community is told what to do, why it does not need to fear the criticism it will meet, and what God intends to complete in it. Nothing in the sequence is left to the community’s own estimate of how the change would be received.

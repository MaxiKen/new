## Sūrah al-Tawbah 9:56

> They swear by Allah that they are part of you, but they are not. They only say so out of fear.

**Expanded Commentary**

**An Oath Cannot Create Belonging**

The hypocrites swear that they are part of the believing community, but the verse denies that their oath makes it true. Belonging to a religious community is more than sharing a label when safety requires it. It includes faith, loyalty, truthful conduct, and willingness to accept the duties that accompany the claim.

The oath may convince some people for a moment, but it cannot change the reality known to Allah. This warns against treating declarations as a substitute for character. Words matter, especially words of commitment, but their truth is tested by consistent action. A person who claims solidarity while rejoicing at the community's harm has already explained his real allegiance.

**Fear Can Produce Speech Without Faith**

They say they belong because they are afraid. The fear may be fear of the believers' strength, fear of losing social protection, or fear of the consequences of open opposition. Fear can make a person imitate the language of the group without accepting its truth. Outward conformity then becomes a survival strategy rather than a confession of conviction.

The verse does not condemn every cautious public statement. A vulnerable person may conceal belief under genuine threat, and Allah knows the difference between protection and hypocrisy. Here the speakers use the oath to gain the benefits of belonging while privately resisting its purpose. Their fear is not a temporary shield for faith; it is the reason they pretend to have faith.

**Political Loyalty Is Not the Same as Faith**

A community may include people who cooperate for worldly reasons. Such cooperation can be managed through clear agreements, but it should not be confused with spiritual fellowship. The verse exposes the danger of assuming that a public alliance has already produced a shared moral center.

This distinction helps leaders act with both justice and realism. They should honor contracts, protect rights, and judge people by evidence. They should also avoid handing serious religious trusts to those whose conduct repeatedly contradicts their words. Trust can grow through reliability, but it cannot be commanded into existence by an oath.

**Fear of People Makes the Heart Unstable**

When human fear becomes the main reason for speech, the person changes with the balance of power. He praises whoever seems strong, withdraws from whoever seems weak, and calls the movement of self-preservation wisdom. Such instability prevents honest counsel. Everyone must guess what he truly believes and what pressure has made him say.

The believer may fear people, but he does not make their fear the ruler of his conscience. He speaks with wisdom and may choose silence to prevent harm, yet his inner allegiance remains with Allah. Fear is then managed by faith rather than turned into a reason for lying. A truthful heart can be discreet without being false.

**Belonging Carries Responsibility**

To say “we” is to accept a share of the community's duties. It means defending truth, refusing injustice, and helping when help is required. The hypocrites want the protection of the word without the burden of the responsibility. Their claim fails because they have separated identity from obedience.

Every believer should examine his own use of communal language. Does he say “we” only when there is victory and say “they” when there is sacrifice? Does he want the honor of a group while criticizing every obligation it carries? Genuine belonging includes correction. It does not require agreement with every human decision, but it requires loyalty to the truth and honest repair when one is wrong.

**Allah Sees Through Defensive Declarations**

The verse is short because the issue is simple before Allah. The hypocrites can multiply oaths, but Allah knows whether fear or faith produced them. This should make the sincere servant less dependent on self-advertisement. He does not need to announce loyalty at every moment if his conduct already shows it.

It should also make him cautious about accusing others. Human beings see only fragments. They can respond to clear betrayal and protect the community from proven harm, but they leave final judgment to Allah. The proper lesson is self-examination: let fear be brought under faith, let words be supported by action, and let belonging become a responsibility rather than a shelter from responsibility. A believer may need to keep a confidence, avoid a needless confrontation, or speak with careful wisdom. None of this requires lying about allegiance. He can protect people without changing his worship, and he can cooperate without pretending that every partner shares his faith. Truth gives discretion a clean boundary. Fear may shape the manner of speech, but it must not rewrite the object of devotion.

The most stable belonging is therefore rooted in Allah, not in the changing strength of a group. A person who joins a community only because it is powerful will leave when another power appears. A person who belongs for Allah can remain truthful during weakness and can criticize an error without abandoning the principles that brought him there. This does not require naivety about people. It requires the believer to keep allegiance and cooperation in their proper places. He honors agreements, protects the innocent, and refuses to turn fear into a false confession. Allah knows whether the tongue is reporting the heart or merely defending the body.

The believer's oath should therefore be supported by a life that remains truthful when fear rises. Allah does not need exaggerated declarations; He asks for honest allegiance.

When fear is brought under faith, discretion remains possible without falsehood. The servant can protect life, honor an agreement, and speak carefully while keeping his allegiance clear before Allah.

## Sūrah al-Shuʿarāʾ 26:211

> it is not for them ˹to do so˺, nor can they,

**Expanded Commentary**

**The Devils Are Not Fit to Carry Revelation**

The verse gives two reasons the devils cannot be the source of the Quran. It is not fitting for them to bring it, and they do not have the ability to do so. Suitability and power are both denied. The Quran's source is not merely beyond their permission; its truth and purpose are contrary to what they are.

A being can desire to influence people without possessing the authority or capacity to deliver revelation. The devils may whisper, beautify falsehood, and encourage disobedience, but that activity is not the same as bringing down a scripture from Allah. The verse guards the boundary between temptation and revelation.

**Moral Opposition Shows Their Lack of Suitability**

The Quran commands worship of the One God and calls people to justice. Devils seek to divide worship, inflame pride, and make wrong attractive. A message that repeatedly exposes those practices cannot be explained as their work merely because it speaks about the unseen.

Suitability here is therefore moral as well as metaphysical. The Quran's purpose is guidance, while the devil's purpose is misguidance. One calls the servant back to accountability; the other urges the servant to forget accountability. The two directions cannot be made identical by saying that both involve invisible beings or unusual speech.

**Power Belongs to Allah, Not to Hidden Rivals**

The inability mentioned in the verse protects belief from imagining a struggle between equal supernatural powers. The devils are not rival gods with independent access to revelation. They are created beings with limited ability, subject to the rule of Allah and barred from what He protects.

This matters when people become fascinated by claims of hidden knowledge. The unseen is not a free marketplace in which any spirit can provide divine instruction. A believer does not accept a message because it sounds mysterious or predicts something impressive. Revelation is recognized by its source, its trustworthy delivery, and its call to truth under Allah's authority.

The same rule applies to a private voice that tells a person to break a clear obligation. Mystery cannot make disobedience sacred, and a prediction cannot make a false command divine. The protected Quran remains the measure by which spiritual claims are tested, not the other way around.

**The Quran Cannot Be Reduced to Inspiration Without Accountability**

Some people speak of inspiration as though any powerful inner experience can establish a command. The verse gives a stricter standard. The Quran is not a private impulse or a voice whose origin no one can examine. It comes through a protected chain and makes a public claim that can be heard, remembered, and tested by its guidance.

That standard also protects sincere spiritual experience from becoming a replacement for scripture. A feeling may move a person, but it cannot authorize worship or law by itself. The Quran's source is Allah, its carrier is trustworthy, and the devils neither deserve nor possess the role that opponents tried to assign them.

The two denials in the verse cover both worthiness and ability. A person may be unable to perform a task but still be fit for it in principle; the devils are denied both. Revelation is not theirs by right, and they cannot take it by force. Their lack of power is a consequence of their created status, not evidence of a second authority competing with Allah.

This distinction matters because human beings often make hidden powers larger than they are. They may fear spirits as though those spirits could decide guidance, forgiveness, or destiny independently. The Quran restores proportion. The devils can tempt, whisper, and invite, but they cannot become the source of Allah's revelation. A believer seeks refuge in Allah without granting the tempter a divine rank.

The verse also tests claims of inspiration made by people. A private voice that urges pride, injustice, or worship of something besides Allah cannot be certified by the fact that it feels powerful. Revelation is fitting to the Lord's wisdom and arrives through a trustworthy carrier. Any alleged message that opposes those marks has no right to be placed beside the Quran.

The command of the Quran further shows why the devils are not suitable. It does not flatter the powerful or leave desire sovereign. It calls people to restrain the hand, tell the truth, honor worship, and prepare for judgment. These demands may be resisted, but they are not the work of a force whose aim is to make wrongdoing attractive.

The phrase nor can they also comforts the messenger and the believer. The enemy cannot corrupt the revelation at will. Human beings can misread or misuse it, but the source remains protected and available for correction. Confidence in that protection should produce careful study and humble obedience, not superstition about hidden rivals.

The verse's wording also prevents a person from treating satanic forces as independent authors of history. They may be enemies, but they are not sovereign. Their inability to bring the Quran means that divine guidance is not produced by a contest in which the stronger hidden power wins. Allah chooses the carrier and protects the message; the devils remain within the limits of created beings.

That limit does not make temptation unimportant. A whisper can be small at its beginning and powerful after it is welcomed repeatedly. The devils cannot create revelation, but they can encourage a person to reject it, misread it, or use it for pride. The believer therefore needs both confidence in the Quran's source and vigilance about the motives with which he approaches it.

A message should be judged by the kind of servanthood it forms. If it asks people to worship Allah alone, speak truth, protect property, and prepare for judgment, it bears the marks of guidance. If a claim invites people to exalt a hidden power, excuse injustice, or follow desire without accountability, the verse gives no reason to treat it as revelation. Suitability and ability remain with Allah's protected message.

The opponents tried to place the Quran inside a category that would make its authority easy to dismiss. The verse removes that category. The Quran is not demonic inspiration, not a rival scripture, and not a human invention dressed in mystery. It is revelation whose chain is guarded. The hearer is left with a clear responsibility: examine it honestly and obey the Lord who sent it.

## Sūrah al-Baqarah 2:236

> There is no blame if you divorce women before the marriage is consummated or the dowry is settled. But give them a ˹suitable˺ compensation—the rich according to his means and the poor according to his. A reasonable compensation is an obligation on the good-doers.

**Expanded Commentary**

**The Case Described**

The verse addresses the case of a divorce that occurs before the marriage has been consummated and before a dowry has been fixed, and it states that the community commits no wrong in such a separation. The sentence follows the rules about the waiting period and the proposal, and it supplies the provision for a marriage that ends at its beginning.

The Book's concern is that the dissolution of an arrangement that has not been completed should not leave either party in difficulty, and the community's teachers read the sentence as the statement that the separation itself is permitted. Readers are shown that the legislation addresses the situations that arise at every stage of the marriage contract.

**The Provision Required**

The sentence instructs the husband to provide for the woman in such a case, and the community's scholars read the clause as the obligation that accompanies the separation. The commentators explained the provision as a gift given according to the means of the one who provides.

The Book's treatment of the subject elsewhere requires the payment according to what is reasonable, and the community's jurists connected the passages when they set out the law. Readers are given the account of a ruling that attends to the position of the woman who has entered a betrothal and then been released from it, and the sentence supplies the obligation that the community applied.

**The Measure of the Provision**

The sentence states that the provision is to be made according to the means of the one who has the wealth and according to his circumstances, and the community's teachers read the clause as the standard by which the amount is set. The wording distinguishes between the person of ample means and the one of limited means.

The Book's practice of measuring its obligations by the capacity of the one who bears them appears in the passages about the household and elsewhere, and the community's scholars applied the principle to the provision required by the sentence. The passage shows that the Book's legislation attends to the position of both parties, and the sentence supplies the rule that governs the amount.

**The Rightful Duty**

The sentence describes the provision as a duty upon those who do what is right, and the community's teachers read the phrase as the statement of the obligation that the separation creates. The Book's language of the honourable appears throughout the rules about the household.

The wording places the provision within the standard of conduct that the passage has described, and the community's scholars used the clause when they discussed the difference between the gift that is required and the one that is recommended. The account describes a ruling whose force rests on the community's sense of what is fitting as well as on its obligation.

**The Reports and Applications**

The commentaries describe the cases in which the companions divorced before the consummation and before the fixing of a dowry, and the reports record the provision they made. The material shows the community applying the sentence to its circumstances.

The community's jurists also discussed the relation between the sentence and the rules about the dowry that follow it, and they set out the cases in which half of the dowry is due. The passage shows that the passage treats the arrangements of the marriage as a connected body of law, and the sentence in this place supplies the provision for the case it names.

**The Place of the Verse**

The sentence stands between the rules about the waiting period and the proposal and the verse that treats the divorce after a dowry has been fixed, and it supplies the provision for the case that comes between them. The Book has addressed the stages of the arrangement in order, and the reader is shown how the legislation covers each.

The community's teachers read the verses as a unit when they taught the law of the household, and the passage's concern is the protection of the parties in the arrangements that conclude a marriage. The passage closes with the instruction about the provision, and the verse that follows supplies the rule for the case in which the dowry has already been named.

**The Marriage Released Early**

The case the sentence describes concerns a contract that was concluded and then dissolved before the couple had come together and before the amount of the dowry had been named. The Book removes the blame from the separation itself and places the duty of the gift upon the husband, so that the woman who had waited for the marriage is not sent away with nothing.

The jurists measured the gift by the circumstances of the man and by the practice of the community, and they distinguished the case of the woman whose marriage had been consummated from the case of the one who had not been touched. The wording gives the obligation a place in the order of the law rather than in the free choice of the husband, since the sentence describes it as a duty upon those who do what is right.

The passage's sequence shows the attention of the legislation to the stages of the contract. The teachers noted that the gift does not depend on the wealth of the man beyond what he is able to provide, and the community's practice followed the standard the sentence states.

**What the Verse Establishes**

The sentence establishes the permissibility of the divorce before the consummation, the duty of providing for the woman, the measure of the provision according to the husband's means, and the standing of the provision as a duty upon those who do what is right. The community's teachers used the verse when they set out the law, and the reader is shown that the Book's legislation attends to the position of the woman at every stage of the marriage arrangement.

The community's judges applied the sentence to the cases of the early separations, and the teachers read it as a statement about the dignity of the woman whose marriage was ended before it began. The verse's requirement of a gift has been understood as the recognition of what the woman has given up, and the passage then supplies the rule for the case in which the amount of the dowry had been settled.

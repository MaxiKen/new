## Sūrah al-Ḥāqqah 69:36

> nor any food except ˹oozing˺ pus,

**Expanded Commentary**

**Pus for the Proud**

Wa-lā ṭaʿām illā min ghislīn — "and no food except from ghislīn." Ghislīn: wound-discharge, pus and blood flowing from the damned's burns. Cuisine of corpses served to the deathless-dying. Such foulness fits: proud palates that scorned lawful thanks get unlawful filth. Mouths that mocked revelation chew excretions. The body fed delicacies while souls starved now feeds filth while burning. Readers should imagine mercifully-briefly, then repent thoroughly. What palates refused gratefully (lawful bounty unthanked), palates receive punitively. Thank food now; never eat ghislīn then. Proud mouths, pus menu — fitted.

Hell's menu courses three: bitter ḍarīʿ-thorn — "no food except ḍarīʿ" (paraphrased from 88:6); zaqqūm-tree — "you will eat from zaqqūm" (paraphrased from 56:52); and ghislīn here. Thorn, tree-fruit, discharge — appetizers of agony. Such variety tortures: no course edible, every course painful. Menus mock earthly banquets: courses served, none nourishing. Readers should contrast deliberately: garden fruits within reach (verses 21-24) versus these. Choose menus livingly: righteous eating or wretched chewing. What courses disgust, repentance cancels. Order gardens; never eat here. Three courses, zero nourishment.

**Starved of Good, Fed Foul**

Inversion's recipe: withheld food (verse 34) returns as foul food. Measure for measure: unfed poor becomes pus-fed rich. Such symmetry seasons justice: menus mirror mercies. Fed none, eat filth; urged none, chew discharge. The Book's arithmetic never errs: withheld good converts to imposed foul. Readers should reverse the recipe: feed good now, eat good then. What withholding cooks (ghislīn), giving uncooks. Cook gardens through charity; never cook ghislīn through clutching. Recipes recorded; meals served accordingly. Feed well; eat well.

Illā — "except" — closes the menu: nothing but ghislīn. No alternative dishes, no substitutions, no hunger strikes effective. Exclusivity intensifies: hunger forced to filth. Such closure tortures choosers: earthly menus offered options; here, one. "Except" elsewhere opens mercy (believers excepted from loss); here it opens horror. Readers should note illa's direction: exception toward mercy or toward misery — conduct aims it. Live excepted-from-loss; never eat excepted-from-food. What illā closes, conduct chose. Choose mercy's illā; never hunger's. Except ghislīn: no except.

**Contrast the Garden's Tables**

Verses 21-24: blissful life, elevated garden, near fruits, joyful eating. Verse 36: pus only. Menus oppose absolutely: delight versus disgust. Such contrast evangelizes: two cuisines, two conducts. Joyful eating rewards urged feeding; ghislīn-chewing punishes withheld urging. Tables testify: eternal catering follows earthly charity. Readers should book tables early: reservations through righteousness. What gardens serve (joy), charity ordered. Book garden seating; cancel ghislīn orders. Two menus, one choice — choose verses 21-24. Book garden seating early through righteous conduct daily. Cancel ghislīn orders permanently through repentance and charity. Reservations confirmed by deeds; menus fixed by habits. Choose deliberately now, for kitchens close at death.

Charity caters eternity: feed now, feast then. Sponsor meals, stock pantries, urge feeding — each act a garden reservation. Such catering inverts ghislīn: givers never taste it. The feeder's menu: fruits within reach, joyfully eaten. Practical steps: monthly food budget for poor, iftar sponsorships, urging circles. Readers should cater deliberately: hunger relieved now, hunger impossible then. What feeding funds, feasting fulfills. Fund meals; feast eternally. Caterers of charity dine in gardens.

**Fasting Previews Hunger**

Ramadan previews involuntary hunger: dawn-to-dusk emptiness teaches empathy. Such preview converts: fasters feel the poor's daily. Ghislīn's horror sharpens the lesson — hunger then is eternal. Voluntary hunger now prevents compelled foulness then. Fasting also disciplines: appetites leashed annually, gratitude refreshed. Readers should fast beyond obligation: Mondays, Thursdays, white days. Feel hunger; feed hungry. What fasting previews (need), charity answers (with food). Preview hunger; prevent ghislīn. Fasters feasting later never chew filth.

**Thanksgiving Sweetens Meals**

Gratitude seasons lawful food with worship: basmalah before, praise after, sharing throughout. Such thanksgiving inverts ghislīn's bitterness: thanked meals ascend as worship. The thankful eater remembers Givers with every bite — provision traced to mercy. Thanksgiving also guards: grateful hearts waste less, share more, overeat rarely. Mouths praising God between bites discipline appetites naturally. Readers should ritualize thanks: begin audibly, end sincerely, share visibly. Teach children: food is gift, Giver is God, thanks are due. What thanksgiving seasons (meals), ingratitude spoils (into mere fuel). Sweeten tables with praise; never eat dumbly. Thanked food nourishes souls; unthanked merely fills bellies.

Mouths face training or torment: disciplined now through lawful restraint, or punished then through foul compulsion. Fasting schools palates: hunger borne voluntarily teaches mastery, and restraint practiced monthly becomes character. Such training inverts ghislīn: trained mouths never taste it, while indulged mouths inherit it. Lawful appetites themselves need governance — halal overeating dulls worship, and even permitted pleasures require measure. The righteous mouth thanks before eating, shares during eating, and praises after eating. Readers should enroll palates in discipline: fast regularly beyond Ramadan, eat moderately always, thank explicitly each meal. What training governs (appetite), torment never claims (the trained). Mouths mastered now feast forever; mouths indulged now chew filth then. Train palates; taste gardens.

**Bounty Unthanked Turns Bitter**

Bounty unthanked turns bitter: ingratitude spoils sweetness. Feasts eaten dumbly nourish bodies while starving souls, and luxury unacknowledged becomes evidence. Such bitterness previews ghislīn: sweetness refused as gift returns as pus. Thanked bounty, conversely, sweetens doubly — pleasure plus worship. Every thanked meal testifies for its eater; every unthanked meal testifies against. Readers should thank systematically: name three blessings per meal. Praise providers human and divine. Share portions visibly, so thanks become action. Teach households: no silent feasts. What thanks sweeten (provision), ingratitude sours (into indictment). Bitter then follows dumb now. Sweeten meals with thanks; never eat evidence. Thanked bounty ascends; unthanked accuses.

**Eaters Named Next**

Menu served; eaters follow. Verse 37 will name consumers — the khāṭiʾūn, evildoers. Food foul, eaters fallen: ghislīn for the guilty. Readers should brace for naming: who eats this? What follows identifies. The next verse names evildoers as ghislīn's eaters, consumers matching cuisine.

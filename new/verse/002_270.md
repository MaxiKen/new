## Sūrah al-Baqarah 2:270

> Whatever charities you give or vows you make are surely known to Allah. And the wrongdoers will have no helpers.

**Expanded Commentary**

**The Statement of Knowledge**

The sentence states that whatever charities the community gives and whatever vows it makes are known to God, and the community's teachers read the clause as the confirmation that accompanies the instructions of the passage. The Book has described the reward of the givers and the gifts that are accepted, and the sentence here names the knowledge that keeps the account.

The wording places the two kinds of offering together, the free gift and the undertaking that a person binds upon himself, so that the instruction covers the whole of what the community offers. The scholars noted the breadth of the phrase, since the vow was a practice of the community and the sentence treats it within the same statement.

The clause also supplies the assurance that the intention behind the gift is perceived. The community's teachers drew from the sentence the lesson that the secret and the open belong to the same record, and the passage's earlier treatment of the hidden and the shown gift was read with it.

**The Vow Named**

The sentence names the vow beside the charity, and the community's commentators explained the practice that the word describes. A vow in the community's usage is a person's undertaking to perform an act of worship or to give something if a matter he desires comes to pass, and the Book treats the fulfilment of such undertakings among the obligations of the believer.

The teachers noted that the sentence joins the two offerings although their causes differ, since the charity is given from the person's property and the vow binds his conduct. The Book's instruction elsewhere commends the fulfilment of what is vowed, and the passages on the subject were gathered by the commentators when they explained the verse.

The wording also places the vow within the knowledge of God, so that the undertaking is described as an act that the person presents rather than as a matter between him and the people who hear his promise. The community's scholars used the clause when they taught the seriousness of the vow.

**The Account Kept**

The commentators explained the phrase as the statement that God keeps the account of what the community gives and intends, so that nothing of it is lost. The teachers described the knowledge of God in the terms that the Book uses elsewhere, where every act and every intention is recorded.

The clause supplies the answer to the anxiety that accompanies a gift that receives no acknowledgement from the people. The community's scholars read the sentence as the assurance that the value of the act does not depend on the notice it receives, and they used it when they encouraged the believers whose giving was unknown to their neighbours.

The wording also implies the judgment that follows the account, so that the knowledge named in the sentence belongs to the passage's treatment of the reward and the loss. The community's teachers connected the clause with the verses that describe the two harvests.

**The Wrongdoers Stated**

The sentence closes with the statement that the wrongdoers have no helpers, and the community's scholars explained the term as the description of those whose conduct in the matter of charity has placed them outside the help they expected. The commentators connected the phrase with the earlier passage about the giver who acts for display, since the persons described in that verse are the ones whose giving was ruined.

The teachers drew attention to the word rendered as helpers, since the sentence denies the assistance of every party on whom a person might rely in the account of his deeds. The community's preachers used the clause when they spoke about the day on which no intercession avails without the permission of God.

The reading preserved in the community connects the phrase with the verse that precedes it in the passage, where the wrongdoers were described. The sentence in this place therefore completes the treatment of the subject with the statement about those whose offerings bring them no benefit.

**The Relation to the Passage**

The sentence stands among the verses about charity that occupy the middle of the sūrah, and it follows the sentence about the gift of wisdom. The teachers read the order as the passage's movement from the promise to the account, since the bounty that has been described is kept in the knowledge of God.

The commentators also connected the verse with the instructions that follow, where the community is told of the charity that may be given openly and that which is given in secret. The sentence supplies the ground of both, since the record of God stands behind the act whether the community sees it or not.

The whole section therefore presents the wealth of the community as material for the account, and the sentence in this place states the knowledge that conducts it. The community's scholars read the passage as a unit when they taught the obligations of property.

**The Teachers' Use of the Sentence**

The community's preachers returned to the sentence when they addressed the wealthy among the believers, and the reports describe their use of the clause about the vow when they taught the seriousness of a promise made to God. The material shows the passage applied to the circumstances of the community's own life.

The teachers also drew from the sentence the lesson about the gifts that are given without notice. The knowledge that the verse names supplied the community with the assurance that the act of a person who cannot show his giving is preserved, and the preachers repeated the clause in their exhortations.

The passage's concern runs through the whole of the community's conduct, and the sentence supplies the statement that nothing of it is hidden. The teachers of the community built their teaching of the passage upon that ground.

**What the Verse Establishes**

The sentence establishes the knowledge of God over the charities and the vows of the community, the standing of the vow among the offerings that the Book treats, the account that keeps what a person gives and intends, and the statement about the wrongdoers who have no helpers. The community's teachers used the verse when they encouraged the believers in their giving, and the sūrah's next verse turns to the manner in which the gift may be given, so that the account and the conduct stand together in the passage.

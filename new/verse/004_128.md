## Sūrah al-Nisāʾ 4:128

> If a woman fears indifference or neglect from her husband, there is no blame on either of them if they seek ˹fair˺ settlement, which is best. Humans are ever inclined to selfishness. But if you are gracious and mindful ˹of Allah˺, surely Allah is All-Aware of what you do.

### Expanded Commentary

**Settlement in the face of marital neglect**

If a woman fears indifference or neglect from her husband, the verse allows the spouses to seek a fair settlement. The problem may involve maintenance, time, companionship, or the emotional abandonment that leaves a wife suspended between marriage and freedom. A negotiated arrangement can preserve dignity when both parties choose it without coercion.

**Selfishness is a human tendency**

The verse acknowledges that each spouse may want the full benefit of marriage while giving less. This realism prevents the law from assuming that goodwill will automatically overcome conflict. Settlement must therefore be explicit, fair, and monitored against pressure. A wife should not be forced to surrender rights merely because the husband is powerful or because relatives fear social embarrassment.

“Being gracious and mindful” includes generosity beyond the minimum, truthful disclosure, and awareness that Allah sees what a private agreement hides. The verse’s preference for settlement is not a command to preserve every marriage at any price. If negotiation perpetuates abuse, justice may require separation and protection.

**Settlement must be fair**

A settlement is not fair merely because it is signed. Its fairness depends on information, freedom from fear, and preservation of basic rights. Counselors should ask what the woman would choose if she had secure housing and independent advice. Generosity is valuable, but a concession extracted by insecurity is not generosity.

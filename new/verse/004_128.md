## Sūrah al-Nisāʾ 4:128

> If a woman fears indifference or neglect from her husband, there is no blame on either of them if they seek ˹fair˺ settlement, which is best. Humans are ever inclined to selfishness. But if you are gracious and mindful ˹of Allah˺, surely Allah is All-Aware of what you do.

**Expanded Commentary**

**If a Woman Fears Indifference or Neglect — No Blame to Seek Settlement**

The verse opens *wa in imraʾatun khāfat min baʿlihā nushūzan aw iʿrāḍā fa lā junāḥa ʿalayhimā an yuṣliḥā baynahumā ṣulḥā* — and if a woman fears, anticipates, from her husband perverseness, rising — *nushūz* — or neglect, turning away — *iʿrāḍ* — then no blame upon them both to seek, to effect, settlement, reconciliation between them, a settlement. *Khāfat* — feared — was explained as sensed, apprehended, with signs, as earlier *if you fear ill conduct* from women advice and measures were given (4:34) and *if you anticipate a split appoint mediators* (4:35) — fear here is the moral perception that precedes action, not mere anxiety. *Nushūz* — rising, perverseness, elevation — was read as the husband’s rising above, being high, refusing right; *iʿrāḍ* — turning aside, neglect — as withdrawal, not sharing bed, not intending, leaving hanging (4:129). *Fa lā junāḥa ʿalayhimā* — no blame upon them both — was explained as the law’s lightening where shortening prayer carries no blame (4:101), where laying aside weapons in rain carries no blame though precaution remains (4:102), and where standing for orphans must be with justice (4:127) — here settlement carries no sin nor blame. *An yuṣliḥā baynahumā ṣulḥā* — to reconcile between them with settlement — was read as any fair adjustment: she waives part of maintenance or night in exchange for keeping marriage, he returns kindness; early reports illustrated it with Sawdah bint Zamʿah offering her day to ʿĀʾishah to remain married to the Prophet ﷺ, accepted without blame. The sūrah has said that most secret talks have no good except charity, kindness, reconciliation seeking God’s pleasure (4:114); settlement is that reconciliation.

**Settlement — Which Is Best**

The affirmation *wa al-ṣulḥu khayr* — and settlement, reconciliation, is best, is good, is better — was explained as the sūrah’s maxim for dispute. *Al-ṣulḥ* — settlement — was read as the peace that mends, the *iṣlāḥ bayna al-nāsi* commanded as good secret talk (4:114) and as the mediators’ goal (4:35). *Khayr* — best, good — was explained as better than splitting where the woman fears *nushūz* or *iʿrāḍ* yet a fair settlement keeps ties honoured (4:1), as the single soul’s creation spread into families whose ties are to be honoured. The phrase was linked to *“appoint a mediator from his family and hers, if they desire reconciliation God will bring harmony”* (4:35): settlement is not the enemy of justice but its embodiment where justice is equity plus mercy. The sūrah has said humans crave what God gave some over others (4:32) and that burdens are lightened because weak (4:28); to prefer settlement where the alternative is hanging is to prefer the light, kind path where the writ of prayer still holds at fixed time (4:103). Classical notes added that *al-ṣulḥu khayr* is general: wherever reconciliation is possible, it is best, provided rights prescribed for orphan women (4:127) are not voided.

**Humans Are Ever Inclined to Selfishness**

The diagnostic *wa uḥḍirat al-anfusu al-shuḥḥ* — and selves, souls, were made present with, brought with, selfishness, stinginess, avarice — was explained as human inclination. *Uḥḍirat* — made present, brought close, kept at hand — was read as that stinginess is brought to the soul, attends it, is ever present, as *“humankind was created weak”* (4:28) and *“God does not like the stingy who withhold bounty”* (4:37). *Shuḥḥ* — selfishness, stinginess, grasping — was explained as the soul’s holding onto its share, its night, its wealth, its right, unwilling to concede where settlement requires waiving. The sūrah has shown stinginess promoting stinginess among people (4:37), and Satan’s *mā dūna* forgiveness (4:116) requires hope not delusion; here *shuḥḥ* is the barrier to settlement: each side fears losing. To name *shuḥḥ* was read as the Book’s realism before its idealism: settlement is best, yet selves are brought with selfishness, so settlement needs graciousness and mindfulness to overcome what is present with souls. The same selfishness was seen where dowry taken unjustly is sought back (4:20) and where the lagger wishes gain without risk (4:73).

**But If You Are Gracious and Mindful — God Is All-Aware**

The encouragement *wa in tuḥsinū wa tattaqū fa inna Allāha kāna bi mā taʿmalūna khabīrā* — but if you do good, act graciously, beautifully, and are mindful of God, then indeed God was ever All-Aware of what you do — was explained with *tuḥsinū* — to do *iḥsān*, to be gracious, to do good beautifully — the same *muḥsin* who submits face and does good following Abraham (4:125) and who does good male or female with faith (4:124). *Wa tattaqū* — and are mindful, God-wary — was read as the sūrah’s *taqwā* that began *“be mindful of your Lord who created you from a single soul”* (4:1) and that seals settlement between spouses (4:129) — if you do what is right and are mindful He is All-Forgiving, Most Merciful. *Fa inna Allāha kāna bi mā taʿmalūna khabīrā* — He is Ever Aware of what you do — was explained as that He who owns heavens and earth (4:126) and knows whatever good you do (4:127) is aware of the settlement sought, of the selfishness present, of the graciousness where offered. The sūrah has sealed knowledge diversely — *ʿAlīm Ḥakīm* where sin is earned (4:111), *Muḥīṭ* where night plots (4:108), *Khabīr* where verification was commanded (4:94) — here *Khabīr* fits act: He is aware of what you do in settlement where your doing is waiving and being gracious.

**A Verse That Follows Instruction for Orphans With Instruction for Wives**

Placed after instruction for orphan women deprived and helpless children (4:127), this verse follows that instruction with settlement where a wife fears rising or turning away. The sūrah that began with limits of marriage — two, three, four where justice is feared else one (4:3) — returns to marriage limits at the other end: you will never be able to be just between wives even if keen (4:129). Between those limits is this middle path — settlement is best — where the human soul’s *shuḥḥ* is named as ever present, yet graciousness and mindfulness are the act that God, All-Aware, sees. For the weak this is protective: the woman who fears *nushūz* or *iʿrāḍ* is not told the faith is divorce alone; no blame to seek settlement that keeps marriage with adjusted rights, and for either to be gracious and mindful is best. The next verse will state the limit of justice between wives and the ethic of inclining not wholly — do not leave the other hanging — and if you do right and are mindful He is All-Forgiving, Most Merciful (4:129).

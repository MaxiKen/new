## Sūrah al-Baqarah 2:197

> ˹Commitment to˺ pilgrimage is made in appointed months. Whoever commits to ˹performing˺ pilgrimage, let them stay away from intimate relations, foul language, and arguments during pilgrimage. Whatever good you do, Allah ˹fully˺ knows of it. Take ˹necessary˺ provisions ˹for the journey˺—surely the best provision is righteousness. And be mindful of Me, O people of reason!

**Expanded Commentary**

**The Months of the Pilgrimage**

The verse states that the pilgrimage takes place in months that are well known, and the commentators identified them as the last three months of the lunar year, with some restricting the period to the first ten days of the final month. The statement answers a question about the time in which the rites may be begun.

The Book's fixation of the season gives the community a time that is publicly recognised, and the community's scholars noted that the instruction prevents the rites from being performed at the pilgrim's convenience. What the reader receives is a statement about the discipline of the pilgrimage, and the sentence's account of the months precedes the rules that govern the behaviour of the pilgrim.

**The Conduct Required**

The sentence names three things that the pilgrim must avoid: lewdness, iniquity, and quarrelling. The commentators explained the first as covering relations between spouses and speech that would lead to them, the second as covering disobedience in general, and the third as covering arguments that produce anger and insults.

The community's scholars were careful to explain that the prohibition of quarrelling does not forbid the discussion of religious questions, since the restriction concerns the exchanges that arise from irritation. What the reader receives is a code of conduct for the pilgrimage, and the sentence shows that the Book's regulation of the journey extends to the pilgrim's temper as well as to his actions.

**The Reports About the Pilgrim's Conduct**

A report preserved in the community's collections describes the pilgrimage performed faithfully as one in which there is no lewdness, no iniquity, and no argument, and the wording of the report matches the terms of the verse. The community's teachers used the report when they set out the merit of the journey.

The material shows how the verse was understood and applied. The reader carries away the statement that the pilgrimage's excellence is connected with the pilgrim's conduct during it, and the sentence's list is the standard against which the community's practice was measured by its own teachers.

**"Whatever Good You Do, God Knows It"**

The sentence states that God knows whatever good the community does, and the clause appears at the close of instructions throughout the sūrah. The community's scholars read it as an assurance that the effort of a journey performed far from home is not lost.

The Book's habit of attaching the statement to commands about worship gives the reader the knowledge that the acts are recorded, and the sentence places the pilgrim's conduct under that knowledge. The sentence leaves him with the assurance that the good performed in the pilgrimage is known, and the community's teachers used the clause when they encouraged the performance of the rites with attention rather than with routine.

**The Command to Make Provision**

The sentence instructs the community to make provision for the journey and states that the best provision is reverence, and the commentators explained the instruction as a warning against setting out without the means of the journey. The community's teachers read the clause in both its practical and its inward senses.

The Book's use of the term for reverence as the best provision places the pilgrim's supplies in a hierarchy that the reader is expected to recognise. What the sentence establishes is that the journey requires preparation of two kinds, and the community's teachers drew from the clause the instruction to provide for the outward needs of the road while attending to the inward state of the pilgrim.

**The Address to People of Reason**

The sentence closes with the command to be mindful of God and with the address to those who possess understanding, and the sūrah has used the same expression in its treatment of retaliation. The community's scholars noted that the Book appeals to reflection in matters of law, and the address here summons the pilgrim to consider the purpose of the journey.

The vocabulary connects the passage with the earlier legal verses, and the reader is shown that the pilgrimage belongs to the same body of instruction as the rules about property and about conflict. What the sentence establishes is that the obligations of the community are presented as matters for a person who understands what he is doing, and the passage continues with the permission to seek the bounty of God during the journey.

**The Place of the Verse in the Passage**

The sentence expands the instructions of the previous verse and prepares the reader for the rules that follow, and it stands within a section that treats the pilgrimage from its time to its conclusion. The Book supplies the obligation, the conduct, the provision, and the disposition that the observance requires, and the community's teachers arranged the material in the same order when they taught the subject.

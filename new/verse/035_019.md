## Sūrah Fāṭir 35:19

> Those blind ˹to the truth˺ and those who can see are not equal,

**Expanded Commentary**

**Physical Sight Becomes a Moral Metaphor**

The verse uses blindness and sight to describe two spiritual conditions. A person may have functioning eyes and still be blind to the truth; another may possess ordinary human limitations and nevertheless see the signs of Allah clearly. The contrast is therefore not a judgment on disability. It is a metaphor for perception of reality, chosen direction, and willingness to respond to guidance.

The Qur'an uses the same image in 40:58, where the blind and the seeing are set against one another and then connected to believers and wrongdoers. The metaphor works because sight allows a person to recognize a path and avoid an obstacle. Spiritual sight similarly recognizes the Creator, the meaning of revelation, and the consequences of action. Spiritual blindness does not mean that evidence is absent. It means that the heart refuses to let evidence govern its direction.

**Seeing Requires More Than Receiving Information**

A person can hear a teaching, repeat its vocabulary, and still remain blind in the Qur'anic sense. Sight here includes understanding that leads to obedience. The seeing person does not merely collect facts about Allah; he allows those facts to reorder fear, hope, love, and action. He recognizes a blessing as a trust, a temptation as a danger, and a command as mercy even when the command is difficult.

The blind person may be intelligent and informed in worldly matters. The problem lies in the center of judgment. Knowledge that never reaches humility can become another veil. The Qur'an asks in 39:9 whether those who know are equal to those who do not know, but the knowledge intended there is not proud accumulation. It is knowledge that produces awareness of Allah. Thus spiritual sight is measured by its fruit, not by how impressive a person appears in debate.

**Guidance Changes the Meaning of What Is Seen**

Two people can encounter the same event and draw opposite conclusions. One sees hardship and becomes resentful; another sees a test that calls for patience. One sees wealth and assumes superiority; another sees a trust requiring gratitude and generosity. The external scene is not the whole issue. Guidance gives the heart a framework by which it interprets the scene and chooses a response.

This does not mean that believers never feel grief or confusion. Sight is not constant emotional ease. It is the ability to return to a true measure when emotion has moved the person away from it. A seeing servant may stumble, then recognize the error and turn back. A blind heart may defend its error because admitting it would threaten pride. The difference lies in the direction of correction: guidance makes truth welcome even when it exposes the self.

**Blindness Can Be Chosen and Reinforced**

Spiritual blindness often grows through repeated refusal. A person dismisses a warning, excuses an injustice, and surrounds himself with voices that confirm the choice. Over time the wrong no longer feels like a wrong. This is the inversion described earlier in the sūrah, where evil is made attractive. The blindness is not a lack of eyes; it is a cultivated inability to see the moral weight of what one prefers.

That process can be interrupted. Repentance begins when a person allows an unwelcome truth to enter without immediately defending the self. Honest companionship, prayer, and attention to revelation can restore perception. The Qur'an's metaphor preserves responsibility while leaving room for mercy. A blind person in this spiritual sense is not told that he must remain blind. He is warned to stop protecting the condition and to ask Allah for sight.

The metaphor also helps explain why revelation can be resisted by people who possess ordinary intelligence. Sight is not simply the amount of data entering the mind. It is a moral willingness to let the data disclose the self. A person may see the signs of mortality and still behave as though he will never die. He has eyes for the event but blindness toward its demand.

The seeing servant, by contrast, connects signs to responsibility. He sees death and prepares, sees power and becomes humble, sees a wrong and seeks forgiveness. These responses do not prove perfection. They show that the light has reached the place where choices are made. Equality is impossible between a heart that lets truth guide it and a heart that keeps truth at a safe distance.

A final return will make the hidden difference visible. In the present world, blindness may be rewarded and sight mocked. The future judgment reverses that confusion without injustice, because Allah knows what each person was shown and how he responded. The metaphor thus carries both a warning to the resistant and a promise that faithful perception is not wasted.

**The Unequal Pair Points Toward Judgment**

The verse's present comparison implies a future separation. If the seeing and the blind are not equal now, they will not receive the same final treatment. Allah's judgment will not flatten the distinction between those who sought truth and those who rejected it. Good manners, intelligence, and social success cannot make blindness equal to guidance, just as private sincerity cannot be erased by a person's lack of worldly status.

The proper response is self-examination rather than quick labeling of others. A believer should ask where he is refusing to see: an obligation avoided, a harm minimized, a gift unacknowledged, or a correction resisted. The metaphor becomes useful when it turns inward before it becomes a weapon. Allah alone knows the complete state of every heart, but He gives the measure openly. True sight is a mercy to request and a responsibility to use.

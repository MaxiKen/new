## Sūrah al-Isrāʾ 17:22

> **Do not set up any other god with Allah, or you will end up condemned, abandoned.**

**Expanded Commentary**

**Do Not Set Up Other God With Allah**

Lā tajʿal maʿa Allāhi ilāhan ākhara — do not make with Allah another god. Lā tajʿal from j-ʿ-l, to make, set up, as verse 12 jaʿalnā night and day signs and verse 18 jaʿalnā for him Hell. Maʿa Allāhi, with Allah, alongside Allah. Ilāhan, god, deity, from a-l-h, to worship, god. Ākhara from ā-kh-r, other, another, from same root as ākhirah, Hereafter, other. Al-Ṭabarī explains that do not set up with Allah another god, meaning do not associate partner with Allah in worship, do not worship other god besides Allah, as polytheists did with idols, angels as daughters, etc. This is first command in moral code that follows Qur'an guides to most upright: monotheism is most upright, foundation. Al-Qurṭubī says this is prohibition of shirk, associating partners, which is greatest sin, as previous chapter said those who set up partners have no knowledge.

**Condemned Abandoned**

Fa-taqʿuda madhmūman makhdhūlan — then you will sit condemned, abandoned, end up condemned, abandoned. Fa-taqʿuda from q-ʿ-d, to sit, to end up, to become, to remain, as sitting indicates ending, remaining. Madhmūman from dh-m-m, condemned, blamed, as verse 18 madhmūman, condemned, and verse 29 blameworthy. Makhdhūlan from kh-dh-l, to abandon, forsake, fail to support, from same root as khadhlān, abandonment, failure to help. Al-Ṭabarī says then you will end up condemned, blamed by Allah and people, and abandoned, forsaken, with no helper, no support, because other god you set up cannot help, cannot provide bounty that never withheld. Al-Rāzī says madhmūman refers to blame in world and Hereafter, makhdhūlan refers to being abandoned, forsaken, without support from Allah or from false gods.

**Why This Is First In Moral Code**

Why first? Because monotheism is foundation of most upright guidance. Qur'an guides to what is most upright, and most upright is to worship none but Allah, as verse 23 will say Lord decreed you worship none but Him. Setting up other god is most crooked, leads to condemned abandoned. The moral code that follows — honoring parents, giving relatives due, not spending wastefully, not killing children, not approaching adultery, etc. — all built on monotheism. Without monotheism, good deeds not accepted, as faith condition for appreciated striving. Verse 22 therefore opens moral code with prohibition of shirk, warning that if you set up other god, you end up condemned abandoned, as opposed to appreciated striving for Hereafter.

**Connection To Previous Verses About Bounty And Ranks**

Verses 20-21 said bounty never withheld, some favoured over others in world but Hereafter greater in rank and favour. Verse 22 says do not set up other god with Allah or you will end up condemned abandoned. The connection is that bounty from Lord, favouring, ranks, all from Allah alone, not from other gods, so do not set up other god, because other gods do not provide bounty, do not favour, do not give ranks. Setting up other god leads to condemned abandoned, because false gods cannot provide bounty that never withheld and cannot give Hereafter rank far greater. The person who desires Hereafter and strives should worship Allah alone to get appreciated striving.

**Link To Later Verse 39**

Verse 39 will say this is part of wisdom revealed to you, and do not set up other god with Allah or you will be cast into Hell blameworthy rejected, similar to verse 22 but more severe: here condemned abandoned, there blameworthy rejected cast into Hell. The pair frames moral code: begins with do not set up other god or condemned abandoned, ends with do not set up other god or cast into Hell blameworthy rejected, showing that shirk is at both ends, foundation and conclusion, most important. The moral code between is wisdom that is part of what Lord revealed.

**Linguistic Note On Tajʿal And Makhdhul**

Lā tajʿal from j-ʿ-l, to make, set up, from jaʿala. Maʿa, with. Allāh, Allah, God. Ilāh from a-l-h, god, deity, from aliha, to worship. Ākhar from ā-kh-r, other. Taqʿuda from q-ʿ-d, to sit, to end up, remain, from qaʿada. Madhmūm from dh-m-m, condemned, blamed, from dhamma, to blame. Makhdhūl from kh-dh-l, abandoned, forsaken, from khadhala, to abandon, fail to support. The verse's structure — do not make with Allah another god, then you will sit condemned abandoned — with prohibition and consequence, shows that shirk leads to condemnation and abandonment, sitting, remaining in that state, not fleeting.

**How This Verse Teaches Monotheism**

This verse teaches monotheism as foundation: worship Allah alone, do not set up other god, or you will end up condemned abandoned. Al-Qurṭubī says believer should worship Allah alone, not set up other god with Him, whether idol, angel, jinn, etc., because only Allah provides bounty never withheld, favours some over others, gives ranks far greater in Hereafter. The practical lesson is to check worship: is it for Allah alone? If you set up other god, you will be condemned by Allah and people and abandoned without helper, as false gods cannot help, as verse 56 will say invoke those you claim besides Him they do not have power to undo harm or transfer it. The believer who worships Allah alone will have appreciated striving and mighty reward, not condemned abandoned.

**A Reflection On Condemned Abandoned**

Madhmūman makhdhūlan — condemned, abandoned — two words describing state of polytheist: blamed, condemned, and forsaken, abandoned, without support. This contrasts with appreciated, mashkūran, for Hereafter desirer, and with supported by law for heir of unjustly killed, etc. The polytheist who sets up other god sits condemned abandoned, sitting indicates remaining, not moving, stuck in condemnation and abandonment. The monotheist who worships Allah alone is appreciated, supported, given bounty never withheld, favoured in Hereafter with far greater rank and favour. The choice is clear: monotheism leads to appreciation and support, shirk leads to condemnation and abandonment.

## Sūrah al-Ḥāqqah 69:33

> For they never had faith in Allah, the Greatest,

**Expanded Commentary**

**Disbelief Heads the Indictment**

Innahu kāna lā yuʾminu bi-llāhi al-ʿaẓīm — "he never believed in Allah, the Greatest." Inna, "indeed," introduces grounds: the sentence of verses 30-32 now gets its stated reasons. Intellect's crime comes first — disbelief before uncharity, creed before conduct. Such ordering teaches hierarchy: root before branch, heart before hand. The seized, scorched, chained soul stands convicted first of all in its convictions. Faith's absence is not neutral vacancy but culpable refusal — truth presented, evidence furnished, warners sent, yet belief withheld. Readers should examine the indictment's order carefully: God counts unbelief the head of crimes. Creedal correction precedes all reform; no charity compensates for denied Lordship. Believe first, firmly, fully. The indictment's first count decides the rest. Faith heads acquittal as disbelief heads indictment.

**Greatness Denied, Greatness Displayed**

Al-ʿAẓīm — the Greatest, the Tremendous, the Supreme. The Throne Verse crowns Allah "the Most High, the Greatest" (paraphrased from 2:255). Denying the Greatest while clutching petty greatness (verse 29's perished sulṭān) compounds irony: the soul refused supreme Majesty yet claimed miniature majesty. Such disproportion convicts: greatness recognized nowhere above, greatness asserted everywhere below. The audit displays true greatness — Throne borne by eight, heavens torn, chains measured — against the soul's confessed littleness. Greatness denied in life becomes greatness endured in sentence: the Greatest Greatness handles the denier personally. Readers should proportion trust to greatness: the Greatest deserves greatest reliance, greatest awe, greatest love. Trust littleness and be forsaken; trust the Greatest and be upheld. Greatness acknowledged now becomes refuge then. Deny it now; endure it then.

**Unbelief as Ingratitude's Root**

Kufr covers — blessings buried, favors denied, the Giver's name erased from gifts. Every breath drawn, every meal eaten, every dawn seen testified; unbelief covered all testimony. Such covering is ingratitude's root: benefits consumed, Benefactor refused. The disbeliever lived inside mercy's house while denying the Owner — eating His bread, breathing His air, spending His health. Ingratitude then flowered into further crimes: uncharity (verse 34), arrogance, mockery. Roots feed branches: deny the Giver and withholding from the given follows naturally. Readers should uncover blessings deliberately: name the Giver over every gift. Gratitude practiced daily — thanks spoken, praise prayed, generosity shown — keeps kufr's cover off. What thanks uncover, faith keeps. Thank the Giver; never cover gifts. Ingratitude is disbelief's seedbed; gratitude is faith's garden.

**Kāna: Persistence, Not Lapse**

Kāna — "he was," past continuous, settled state. Not a lapse but a life; not a doubt but a dwelling. Persistence seals: years of refusal hardened into identity. Such grammar distinguishes strugglers from settlers: tempted believers stumble, settled deniers persist. The kāna-soul made unbelief its address, furnished it with arguments, invited guests. Settled states sentence heaviest because warnings were most numerous — decades of dawns, sermons, funerals, scriptures ignored. Readers should fear settling more than stumbling: falls recover, addresses condemn. Doubts visited briefly and dismissed differ from doubts enthroned permanently. Never let kāna describe your unbelief — be never-kāna, always-returning. Persistence in denial persists into chains; persistence in faith persists into gardens. Settle Godward; never settle denialward. Kāna cuts both directions.

**Faith Would Have Voided All**

Īmān voids: belief sincerely held would have canceled seizure, scorching, chains. Faith transforms convicts into guests — records shifted to right hands, sentences commuted to feasts. Even late faith saves, if pre-death: the door stands open till the soul reaches the throat. Pharaoh's drowning testimony came too late — believed at sea's floor, rejected (paraphrased from 10:90-91). Timing decides: faith while choosing avails, faith while compelled fails. Such contrast urges haste: believe while breath allows choice. Today's faith voids tomorrow's chains; tomorrow's compulsion voids nothing. Readers delaying belief should hear Pharaoh: late truth unaccepted, early truth sufficient. Believe now — sincerely, promptly, wholly. Void sentences through faith; never validate them through delay. Faith's window: open now, shut at death.

**The Greatest Deserves Greatest Trust**

Trust proportioned to greatness: the Greatest merits total reliance. Tawakkul scales with recognized majesty — small gods earn small trust, the Greatest earns all. Such proportion practically liberates: entrust provision, protection, outcomes to unsurpassable Power. Anxiety shrinks as recognized greatness grows; worry feeds on underestimated Lordship. The chained soul trusted wealth and authority — littlenesses that perished. Misplaced trust always matures into verse 28-29's wails. Readers should audit trust-portfolios: what receives reliance? Markets fluctuate, patrons perish, health fades — only the Greatest endures. Transfer all trust Godward: plan diligently, then rely totally. What greatness holds, littleness cannot drop. Trust the Greatest; rest greatly. Reliance rightly placed never perishes.

**Greatness Rewrites Every Scale**

Al-ʿAẓīm rewrites every scale on which souls weigh themselves and others. Reputations that towered in boardrooms shrink to footnotes beside the Greatest, and powers that strutted across headlines confess littleness at the audit. Such rewriting liberates believers from intimidation: no tyrant looms large beside Allah, and no celebrity outshines the Truly Great. Earthly greatness is comparative and collapsible — today's giant is tomorrow's exhibit — while divine greatness is absolute and unassailable. The chained soul bankrupted itself investing in smallness: wealth's bulk, authority's reach, reputation's glow, all denominated in perishing currency. Readers should convert currencies early by pricing everything against al-ʿAẓīm: careers weighed lightly, honors held loosely, fears faced firmly. What greatness relativizes (every idol), awe stabilizes (every heart). Measure small against the Greatest and walk unawed through crowds; measure yourself great and be leveled. Scales rewritten, souls right-sized. Greatness: only His endures, so only His deserves ultimate trust.

**Uncharity Named Next**

Creed convicted; conduct follows. Verse 34 will charge uncharity — no urging to feed the poor. Intellect's crime (disbelief) pairs with action's crime (uncharity): heart first, then hand. Readers should brace for the second count: faith denied, then food withheld. What follows indicts uncharity. The next verse charges neglect of the poor, conduct completing creed's conviction.

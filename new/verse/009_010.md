## Sūrah al-Tawbah 9:10

> They do not honour the bonds of kinship or treaties with the believers. It is they who are the transgressors.

**Expanded Commentary**

**The Indictment Becomes Personal**

Verse 10 completes the preceding description by stating whom the hostile people refuse to honor: any believer with whom they have a bond of kinship or treaty. The wording moves from the community as a whole to the individual believer. No person is too weak, too distant, or too insignificant to deserve the protection created by family or covenant. The enemy's problem is not merely that it has broken a large political arrangement. It has made the believer himself disposable.

This personal focus sharpens the charge. Public betrayal often hides behind large words such as strategy, necessity, or the interest of the tribe. The verse brings the matter down to a person who was owed loyalty. A covenant is always experienced by particular human beings. When a leader breaks it, the injury is carried by families, travelers, merchants, and neighbors. The verse refuses to let an abstract explanation hide the people harmed by the breach.

**The Two Bonds Return**

Kinship and treaty repeat the paired terms from verse 8. One bond is received through family or alliance; the other is formed by a deliberate promise. The Qur'an treats both as morally serious. Blood relation does not make every action acceptable, but neither may it be dismissed whenever it becomes inconvenient. A treaty is not less binding because its partner is outside the believer's religious community. Natural and chosen bonds both create claims that must be respected.

The verse's order also has a wider lesson. Human beings often honor whichever bond best serves them and neglect the other. A person may defend relatives while breaking contracts with strangers, or speak of legal agreements while treating family members harshly. The Qur'an places both under the same moral examination. The believer's character is not divided into a private ethic for kin and a public ethic for outsiders. Allah sees the whole pattern.

**Transgression Means Crossing a Limit**

The final word identifies the perpetrators as transgressors. The Arabic root suggests passing beyond a boundary. That boundary may be a legal term, a promise, or the limit set by justice in conflict. Transgression is not simply strong action. It is action that refuses the line that should govern it. The Qur'an can authorize resistance to aggression while still condemning the person who exceeds the reason and limit of that resistance.

This is why the verse is connected to the rule, Whoever transgresses against you, then transgress against him in the same measure in which he transgressed against you (2:194). The instruction is not a celebration of revenge. It is a demand for proportion. The hostile group in verse 10 has crossed multiple lines at once: family protection, treaty obligation, and the protection owed to believers. The label transgressors describes a settled disregard for limits, not simply the fact that a conflict exists.

**The Believer Is Not Protected Only When Strong**

The phrase with the believers carries an important social meaning. In a tribal environment, protection often depended on the strength of the clan behind a person. A solitary believer or a weak family could become an easy target. Verse 10 denies the aggressor that calculation. The obligation applies to any believer, not only to the believer whose relatives or allies can retaliate.

That principle is reflected in the Prophetic warning about the protected non-Muslim, whose blood may not be violated. The ethical point is broader than one legal category: security is measured by the promise given, not by the victim's ability to defend himself. A community that protects only those who can punish betrayal has not learned the Qur'anic standard. The purpose of covenant is to create safety precisely where power is unequal.

**A General Rule for Public Trust**

The verse applies to institutions as well as tribes. A government that signs an agreement with a small community, a business that accepts a customer's trust, or an organization that promises confidentiality takes on a duty. The stronger party cannot later claim that the weaker party was foolish to rely on the promise. If the agreement was clear and the other party kept it, honoring the bond is a matter of justice before it is a matter of public relations.

The same rule governs family power. Parents must not use kinship as a license for abuse, and children must not use religious language to cancel every duty toward parents. Verse 10 does not turn every bond into an absolute command; it teaches that bonds have weight and cannot be discarded for convenience. When a higher command requires a boundary, the boundary must be explained honestly rather than disguised as though no bond existed.

**Why the Verse Says “They”**

The final statement, it is they who are the transgressors, separates the wrongful act from the identity of every person in the surrounding dispute. The pronoun points back to the people whose conduct has been described. They are not called transgressors because they belong to a race or because they live on one side of a frontier. They are identified by the way they treat bonds and believers. This is a pattern the Qur'an repeatedly uses: moral classification follows action.

For the reader, the verse is both a warning and a safeguard. Do not excuse systematic betrayal as clever politics. Do not answer it by inventing guilt in people who have not done the same. Watch how a person behaves when a promise protects someone weaker than himself. If he honors the bond, he has answered the verse's charge. If he crosses the limit because he can, the verse supplies its own verdict. Justice begins by naming the transgression accurately.

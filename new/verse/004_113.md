## Sūrah al-Nisāʾ 4:113

> Had it not been for Allah’s grace and mercy, a group of them would have sought to deceive you ˹O Prophet˺. Yet they would deceive none but themselves, nor can they harm you in the least. Allah has revealed to you the Book and wisdom and taught you what you never knew. Great ˹indeed˺ is Allah’s favour upon you!

**Expanded Commentary**

**Had Not God’s Grace and Mercy Been Upon You**

The verse opens *wa law lā faḍlu Allāhi ʿalayka wa raḥmatuhū la hammat ṭāʾifatun minhum an yuḍillūka* — had it not been for God’s grace, bounty, upon you and His mercy, a group of them would have resolved, intended, to deceive, to lead astray, you. *Faḍl* — grace, bounty, surplus — and *raḥmah* — mercy — were explained as the two that preserve the Prophet ﷺ where human eloquence and kin pleading pull (4:105–112). *Law lā* — had not, were it not for — was read as the sūrah’s counterfactual that declares actual protection, as earlier *“had it not been for God’s grace and mercy you would have followed Satan except few”* (4:83). *Hammat* — resolved, intended, concerned itself — was explained as firm intent: the group intended to make the judge misjudge, to make the Book-sent-to-judge (4:105) seem to support the deceitful. *An yuḍillūka* — to lead you astray, to deceive you, to make you deviate from right judgment — was read as seeking to make the Prophet ﷺ advocate for the deceitful despite the prohibition not to be advocate for the treacherous (4:105) and not to plead for self-wrongers (4:107). The group — *ṭāʾifah minhum* — was explained as the clan of the thief and its advocates, who hid armour with a Jew, coached night speech displeasing to Him (4:108), and argued in this life expecting advocacy to succeed (4:109). That they only *hammat* — intended — and did not succeed was read as grace and mercy intervening by showing evidence.

**Yet They Deceive None But Themselves and Cannot Harm You**

The counter *wa mā yuḍillūna illā anfusahum wa mā yaḍurrūnaka min shayʾ* — yet they would deceive none but themselves, nor can they harm you at all — was explained with two restrictions: deception returns to the deceiver and harm does not reach the protected. *Yuḍillūna illā anfusahum* — they deceive only themselves — matches the prior principle *whoever earns sin earns only upon himself* (4:111) and *bears slander and blatant sin where he throws on the innocent* (4:112). To seek to deceive the Prophet ﷺ where God is with the plotters by night encompassing what they do (4:108) is to deceive the self that thinks night hides. *Wa mā yaḍurrūnaka min shayʾ* — they cannot harm you in the least, with anything — was read as divine protection of the messenger’s judgment, as the sūrah said He is sufficient as Trustee (4:81, 4:132) and told the Prophet ﷺ to put trust in Him and turn aside from those who contradict by night (4:81). Early reports noted the Prophet was distressed at nearly defending the wrong party; the verse comforts that distress was not harm, as *“God will protect you from people”* (5:67) protects the message though not from feeling the pressure. The clause also generalises: whoever throws sin on the innocent (4:112) harms only himself with slander.

**He Has Sent Down to You the Book and Wisdom and Taught You What You Did Not Know**

The reason for non-deception *wa anzala Allāhu ʿalayka al-kitāba wa al-ḥikmata wa ʿallamaka mā lam takun taʿlam* — and God has sent down upon you the Book and wisdom and taught you what you did not know — was explained as the three that make deception impossible where grace and mercy hold. *Al-Kitāba* — the Book — is the Book sent in truth to judge between people by what He shows (4:105), the criterion between truth and false judges (4:60). *Al-ḥikmah* — wisdom — was explained as the Sunna, judicious application, the Prophetic practice that shows how the Book judges: evidence, oaths, witnesses, the Prophet’s own clarification that eloquence does not convert false to true (Bukhārī 2680). *Wa ʿallamaka mā lam takun taʿlam* — and taught you what you never knew — was read as the knowledge given before this incident and in it: the hidden moving of armour, the night counsel, the Jew’s innocence, the law of throwing sin (4:112). The sūrah has said God wills to make clear, guide to noble ways, turn in mercy, and that He is All-Knowing, All-Wise (4:26–28); teaching what you did not know is that will in person. The sequence was also linked to the beginning where God taught humanity from a single soul (4:1) and to the later *“He has sent down to you the Book with truth confirming your scriptures”* (4:47): teaching is the continuity from creation to judgment.

**Great Is God’s Favour Upon You**

The seal *wa kāna faḍlu Allāhi ʿalayka ʿaẓīmā* — Great, mighty, indeed is God’s favour, bounty upon you — was explained as the sūrah’s acknowledgment that the Prophet’s firmness is not his own firmness alone but favour. *Faḍl ʿaẓīm* — mighty grace — appears where Gardens, shade, and purity are promised, yet here it is for being preserved from misjudgment. The same favour was referenced where the Prophet was told that had they done what they were advised it would have been better and he would have granted great reward and guidance (4:66–68); favour here is that he was shown what to do before doing opposite. The verse was read to comfort every judge after him: the grace that taught what you did not know remains available through the Book and wisdom, so that where secret talks are mostly not good (4:114) the good — charity, kindness, reconciliation — can be seen. For the weak for whom burdens were lightened, the favour is that the Prophet’s preservation is the community’s preservation, because the Book and wisdom continue to show the judge what to see.

**A Verse That Closes the Judgment Passage With Grace**

Placed after the double burden of throwing sin on the innocent (4:112) and before the rule that no good is in most secret talks except three (4:114), this verse closes the judgment passage with grace. The sūrah that began the passage *“do not be advocate for the deceitful”* (4:105) traverses seeking forgiveness (4:106), not advocating for self-wrongers (4:107), hiding from people not from the Encompassing (4:108), advocating in this life versus no advocate on the Day (4:109), seeking then finding (4:110), earning only upon self (4:111), throwing that adds slander (4:112), and ends where it began — had not grace and mercy been upon you, you would have been deceived. The passage is therefore not a lesson in distrust of people but in trust in the Book and wisdom taught. The sūrah that declared disbelievers your clear enemies (4:101) and that promised humiliating punishment where prayer was guarded (4:102) here declares the Prophet’s protection as the community’s protection, so that judgment between people — including those bound by treaty whose blood-money is paid even where mistaken (4:92) — remains by what God shows, and great is His favour upon the one shown.

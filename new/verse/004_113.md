## Sūrah al-Nisāʾ 4:113

> Had it not been for Allah’s grace and mercy, a group of them would have sought to deceive you ˹O Prophet˺. Yet they would deceive none but themselves, nor can they harm you in the least. Allah has revealed to you the Book and wisdom and taught you what you never knew. Great ˹indeed˺ is Allah’s favour upon you!

### Expanded Commentary

**Divine protection of the Messenger**

Allah’s grace and mercy prevented a group from successfully deceiving the Prophet. They intended to manipulate judgment, but the revelation exposed the scheme and preserved justice. The verse reassures the Prophet that human cunning cannot defeat divine guidance.

**Knowledge as a gift**

The Book, wisdom, and teaching are named as great favour. Wisdom includes sound judgment, understanding of contexts, and the ability to apply revelation without being trapped by appearances. It is not enough to possess text; a judge must perceive how truth bears on a real dispute.

The verse is also a warning to religious leaders not to overestimate their own insight. If the Prophet’s knowledge is described as favour, every later scholar remains dependent. Gratitude for knowledge is shown by fairness, humility, and resistance to the flattering story that one already knows enough.

**Grace protects truth from cleverness**

The Prophet was taught what he did not know, showing that sound judgment is a gift that must be received with gratitude. Later scholars should therefore state the limits of their knowledge and consult others. The more influence a person has, the more dangerous it is for him to treat intuition as revelation.

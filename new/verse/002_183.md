## Sūrah al-Baqarah 2:183

> O believers! Fasting is prescribed for you—as it was for those before you—so perhaps you will become mindful ˹of Allah˺.

**Expanded Commentary**

**The Command to Fast**

The verse addresses the believers and states that fasting has been prescribed for them as it was prescribed for those before them, and it attaches the purpose of the command. The sentence opens a section of the sūrah that runs for five verses and treats the fast of Ramadan and the rules that belong to it.

The Book presents the practice as one that the community shares with earlier peoples, and the community's scholars read the clause about those before them as a statement about continuity. What the reader receives is an instruction that is placed within the history of religious practice rather than introduced as an innovation.

**The Meaning of the Fast**

The community's scholars explained the practice as abstaining from food, drink, and marital relations during the daylight hours of the month, and they described the conditions under which the obligation applies. The Book's sections on the subject set out the times of the fast and the concessions granted to those who cannot observe it.

The reports describe the fast of Ramadan as one of the five obligations on which the religion is built, and the community has observed it since the earliest period. What the sentence establishes is that the practice is a duty of the community rather than a voluntary devotion, and the verses that follow describe who is required to observe it and who is excused.

**"As It Was Prescribed for Those Before You"**

The commentators discussed the identity of the earlier peoples the sentence refers to, and they named the Christians, the People of the Book, and humanity in general. The reading they chose affects how the clause is understood, but the main point they drew from it is that fasting is an ancient practice.

The Book's accounts of earlier prophets include their fasting, and the community's teachers used the clause to show that the religion brought by the Prophet ﷺ continues a practice rather than inventing one. What the reader receives is the knowledge that the obligation he observes belongs to a form of worship that God has prescribed for those who came before him, and the sentence supplies the sense of continuity that the clause conveys.

**The Fast of the Community Before Ramadan**

The commentators discussed whether those fasts were obligatory, and the reports state that the community observed them before the fast of the month was instituted.

The community's scholars debated the relation between the earlier practice and the obligation that followed, and some held that the earlier fast was replaced while others held that it continued as a meritorious act. The sentence closes with the statement that the community may become mindful of God, and the Book attaches the same purpose to the law of retaliation and to other commands in the sūrah. The community's scholars read the clause as a statement about the object of the practice rather than as a comment on its mechanics.

## Sūrah al-Tawbah 9:110

> The building which they erected will never cease to fuel hypocrisy in their hearts until their hearts are torn apart. And Allah is All-Knowing, All-Wise.

**Expanded Commentary**

**A Harmful Institution Keeps Working on the Heart**

The building they erected does not end with the moment of construction. It continues to fuel hypocrisy in their hearts. Institutions shape the people who maintain them. Every meeting, message, and public act can reinforce a false story until the members become more committed to protecting the institution than to seeking truth.

This is why a harmful project should not be judged only by its original event. Its ongoing effects matter. Does it keep suspicion alive, preserve a lie, or create a rival identity that feeds on hostility? If so, leaving the project may be necessary for the heart's safety. A person can become attached to a structure because he has invested years, money, and reputation in it, even after its purpose has become clear.

**Hypocrisy Thrives on Repeated Reinforcement**

Hypocrisy rarely survives through one dramatic statement alone. It is reinforced by repetition. People repeat the institution's explanation, defend its leaders, and reinterpret each harmful result as proof that outsiders are hostile. The heart becomes accustomed to double speech: public claims of good and private knowledge of harm.

The believer must interrupt this cycle early. Ask whether the project's facts agree with its stated purpose. Invite independent review. Listen to those harmed by the work instead of treating them as enemies by definition. A structure that cannot bear honest questions is not protected by its sacred language. It is revealing the weakness of its foundation.

**The Heart Can Be Torn by Inner Conflict**

The phrase about hearts being torn apart can describe the anguish produced by a life of falsehood. The person knows what he helped build, yet he must keep pretending that it was good. Doubt, fear, and guilt pull in different directions. The institution becomes a constant reminder of the truth he refuses to admit.

Another reading connects the tearing to death or to the eventual destruction of the false structure. Both readings underline the same warning: hypocrisy is not a stable resting place. It creates inner disquiet and waits for exposure. A servant who feels this conflict should not treat it as merely emotional weakness. It may be a summons to stop defending the wrong and to seek forgiveness.

**Repentance May Require Dismantling**

Repentance from institutional harm can demand more than a private feeling. The harmful project may need to be closed, its funds returned, its false claims corrected, and its victims supported. If the building itself has become a symbol of division, changing its purpose may be part of the repair.

This work can be humiliating because it admits that years were spent wrongly. Yet continuing the harm to protect the old reputation is worse. A servant should prefer a smaller truthful life to a prominent false one. Allah can accept the person who dismantles what he built if the dismantling is part of sincere return.

**Allah Knows the Structure and Its Effect**

The verse ends with Allah's knowledge and wisdom. He knows the stated intention, the hidden plan, the effect on believers, and the future consequences. Human beings may be impressed by a building or confused by conflicting reports, but Allah sees the whole relation between means and ends.

This protects the sincere from being fooled by appearances. It also warns leaders who think that careful public language can secure them. Allah's wisdom will expose whether the institution served worship or served a faction. The believer asks for insight before joining a project and courage to leave when its foundation is revealed as corrupt.

**A Sound Institution Should Heal the Heart**

The contrast is not only negative. A righteous institution should reduce hypocrisy by making truthful worship easier. It should welcome counsel, protect the weak, keep accounts clear, and direct honor toward Allah rather than toward its founders. Its members should become more sincere outside it, not more hostile to everyone beyond it.

The servant therefore evaluates every collective work by its fruit. Does it produce prayer, truth, mercy, and responsible service? Or does it produce oaths, division, and constant fear of exposure? Allah is All-Wise, so the right response to the warning is practical wisdom: build on reverence, repair what was corrupted, and refuse to let any structure become more important than the truth it claims to serve.

A person may therefore need to leave a respected project before it tears his conscience further. Leaving is not betrayal when the institution itself has become a vehicle for falsehood. He should explain the reason honestly, return what he holds in trust, and avoid spreading unnecessary harm. If the project can be repaired, he supports the repair; if it cannot, he refuses to lend it his name. Allah knows whether the heart is being purified or merely finding a new way to protect pride. A sound institution produces people who can leave it for the sake of truth, because the institution never became their god.

The institution's continuing effect means that repair cannot stop at changing its sign. The people involved must examine the relationships, finances, and habits that made the corruption possible. A new name on the door cannot purify an old purpose. Repentance requires new governance, truthful teaching, and a willingness to make harmed people safe. Some members may need to step away while others rebuild. Allah's wisdom knows whether the structure can serve a sound purpose again. The believer does not preserve a building at the cost of preserving the hypocrisy that the building has been feeding.

A project that cannot tolerate this examination is revealing what it values. The believer does not need to preserve a reputation built on a false foundation. He protects the people who may be harmed, tells the truth to those responsible, and leaves the outcome with Allah. The safest foundation is not the largest crowd but sincere submission. When a work is built for Allah, correction strengthens it; when it is built for status, correction feels like destruction. The verse invites the servant to choose the foundation before choosing the appearance.

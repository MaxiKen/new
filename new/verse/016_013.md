## Sūrah al-Naḥl 16:13

> **And ˹He subjected˺ for you whatever He has created on earth of varying colours. Surely in this is a sign for those who are mindful.**

**Expanded Commentary**

**The Earth Itself As A Display Of Colors**

Wa-mā dhara'a lakum fī al-arḍi mukhtalifan alwānuhu — and whatever He has scattered or created for you on earth of varying colors. Dhara'a means to create, to scatter, to multiply, to spread abundantly. Al-Ṭabarī explains it as khalaqa, He created, with emphasis on spreading creation widely. The phrase fī al-arḍi, on earth, shifts focus from sky and celestial bodies in verse 12 to terrestrial diversity. Mukhtalifan alwānuhu — varying in its colors — does not mean only hues like red and green, but kinds, types, shapes, tastes, and forms. Classical commentators note that alwān includes differences in color, shape, size, and nature. The earth contains minerals of different colors, plants of different shades, animals of different patterns, fruits of different tints, humans of different complexions. All are from one earth, yet varied. The verse says lakum, for you, again, tying this diversity to human benefit. The variety is not random clutter; it is provision that delights, distinguishes, and serves.

**Why Color Is Mentioned**

Color is a powerful sign because it is immediately visible and deeply tied to recognition and enjoyment. A world in one color would be functional but monotonous. Allah created a world where even of the same species, individuals differ: no two human faces identical, no two leaves exactly alike, yet all from same soil and water. Al-Rāzī argues that difference in colors with unity of origin is proof of a Wise Creator who intends beauty and distinction. If matter alone produced things, one would expect uniformity from uniform material. Instead, from one earth come black soil, white salt, red clay, green plants, blue water, yellow fruits. The Qur'an draws attention to this elsewhere: *And among His signs is the creation of heavens and earth and the diversity of your languages and colors; surely in that are signs for those who know* (30:22) and *Do you not see that Allah sends down water from sky, then We bring forth fruits of varying colors, and in mountains are streaks white and red of varying colors and intensely black* (35:27). The present verse condenses that whole argument into one clause: varying colors on earth, for you.

**A Sign For Those Who Are Mindful**

Inna fī dhālika la-āyatan li-qawmin yadhakkarūn — surely in this is a sign for those who are mindful, who remember, who take heed. Yadhakkarūn comes from dh-k-r, to remember, to be reminded. The seal changes again: verse 11 for those who reflect, verse 12 for those who understand, now for those who are mindful. Al-Zamakhsharī explains the progression: reflection is needed for crops from one water, understanding for celestial systems, remembrance for the obvious diversity under your feet that you see every day but forget to consider. The earth is so familiar that people stop seeing it. The verse says, be mindful, remember. Remember who scattered these colors for you. The sign is not hidden; it is underfoot, in the market, in your own skin. What is needed is not new information but remembrance of what you already know. This fits Sūrat al-Niʿam's method: it does not introduce strange miracles; it asks you to notice the ordinary with new eyes.

**The Connection To Gratitude**

If colors and types are for you, then using them while forgetting the Giver is ingratitude. The verse's brevity is part of its force. After long lists of specific blessings — cattle, horses, rain, crops, night, day, sun, moon, stars — it now says, and whatever else He scattered on earth of varying colors. This is a catch-all that says, everything else you see of different kinds is also included. It prevents the listener from thinking the list was exhaustive. Al-Qurṭubī notes that this general phrase covers animals, plants, minerals, and even human artifacts made from earth materials, all varying. The phrase mukhtalifan alwānuhu suggests that diversity itself is a blessing, because it allows choice, identification, and enjoyment. A world of one color would not allow you to distinguish ripe from unripe, safe from dangerous, or to enjoy variety. Diversity serves you.

**How This Verse Prepares For Sea And Mountains**

Verse 13 ends the section on land's surface diversity, and verses 14-16 will move to sea, mountains, rivers, and landmarks. The order is from sky to land surface to sea to mountains to guidance. It is a tour of the world as experienced by a traveler: you look up to sky, you look around at earth colors, you encounter sea, you see mountains, you follow rivers and stars. Each stage has its own sign and its own seal for a different kind of thinking. By placing varying colors just before sea, the sūrah hints that sea also has colors and types — fish, pearls, coral — that vary, and that mountains also have colors, as 35:27 says. The thread of colors therefore continues. The reader is being trained to see the world as a palette, not a blank, and to ask who is the Artist.

**Linguistic Note On Dhara'a And Alwān**

Dhara'a in classical Arabic means to create and spread, to make appear abundantly. Lisān al-ʿArab defines it as to create and multiply, often used for creation that is scattered widely, like offspring or plants. Alwān is plural of lawn, color, kind, type. The phrase mukhtalifan alwānuhu is grammatically a circumstantial description of mā, whatever, indicating its state as varying. The translators add ˹He subjected˺ in brackets because the verb sakhkhara from verse 12 is understood to continue, though not repeated. This elision is common in Qur'anic lists where one verb governs several clauses. The meaning is unanimous: whatever He created on earth of varying colors is also subjected for you. The addition in brackets makes English flow while showing dependence on previous verb.

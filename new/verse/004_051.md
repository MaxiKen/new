## Sūrah al-Nisāʾ 4:51

> Have you ˹O Prophet˺ not seen those who were given a portion of the Scriptures yet believe in idols and false gods and reassure the disbelievers that they are better guided than the believers?

**Expanded Commentary**

**Have You Not Seen Again**

The question *a lam tara ilā alladhīna ūtū naṣīban min al-kitāb yuʾminūna bi al-jibti wa al-ṭāghūt* — have you not seen those who were given a portion of the Scriptures yet believe in idols and false gods — repeats the alert of 4:44 but deepens the diagnosis. There the trade was a portion for misguidance and the wish to make you deviate; here the trade has become belief in *jibt* and *ṭāghūt* — terms that early authorities explained as idols, sorcery, divination, and any false authority elevated to be followed beside God, including the chiefs of misguidance who command worship. To be given *naṣīban min al-kitāb* — a portion of the Book — and then to believe in *jibt* and *ṭāghūt* is to reverse the very purpose of the portion, which was to teach *“worship Allah and associate none with Him”* (4:36). The verse was linked to Jewish leaders in Madinah who, when asked by the Quraysh whether the polytheists or the Muslims were better guided, answered that the polytheists were, out of envy and desire for alliance, despite knowing from their own Scriptures that the Prophet ﷺ confirmed what was with them (4:47). The question have you not seen is therefore not only exposure but amazement: how does one given a share of the Book come to credit idols and to reassure idolaters that they are on the better way.

**Reassuring the Disbelievers They Are Better Guided**

The second trait *wa yaqūlūna lilladhīna kafarū hāʾulāʾi ahdā min alladhīna āmanū sabīlā* — and they reassure the disbelievers that they are better guided than the believers — makes the reversal public. The same scholars given a portion of the Book become referees who declare the polytheists — whose worship the sūrah has called *shirk* unforgiven if held to death (4:48) — to be *ahdā sabīlā* — better guided than the believers who worship without association and keep limits about wealth and marriage. The claim was explained as flattery toward the Quraysh and as hostility toward the Prophet ﷺ, but its effect is theological: it tells the disbeliever that his path is straighter. The sūrah has already said that God wills to make things clear and guide to noble ways (4:26), and that He knows best who enemies are and is sufficient as Guardian and Helper (4:45). To tell the disbeliever he is better guided is to trade that clarity for favour, to sell a portion for an alliance, as though the Book were goods to be priced by politics. The Qur’an elsewhere says those who sell the verses for a small price trade guidance for error, and this reassurance is that trade spoken aloud.

**Belief in Jibt and Ṭāghūt After a Portion of the Book**

The phrase *yuʾminūna bi al-jibti wa al-ṭāghūt* was read as more than consulting an idol for a day; it is belief — *īmān* — misdirected. The portion of the Book taught *tawḥīd* — worship without partner — and taught that good is multiplied and that even an atom’s weight is not wronged (4:40); to give belief to *jibt* and *ṭāghūt* is to give that trust to what cannot weigh or multiply, to a false judge the believers were commanded to reject (4:60). Early commentators listed *jibt* as sorcery, idols, and whoever is followed in disobedience, and *ṭāghūt* as the head of falsehood who is obeyed beyond his limit, tying both to the later warning *“have you not seen those who claim belief yet seek judgment of false judges, which they were commanded to reject, and Satan desires to lead them farther away”* (4:60). Here the same pattern appears at the level of doctrine: those given the Book, whose portion should have taught rejection of *ṭāghūt*, become its believers, and in that belief they validate the disbelievers’ path. The reversal is complete: the teachers of *tawḥīd* become teachers that *shirk* is better guided.

**Why the Question Exposes the Seller**

The question *a lam tara* does not ask the Prophet ﷺ for news he lacks; it asks the community to see what the sellers have become. By naming the sellers as those given a portion, the verse reminds them that knowledge came first; unbelief in idols was not too high for them, because they had the Book that forbade it. To believe in *jibt* after that is chosen, not inherited, which is why the next verse says *they are the ones condemned by Allah* (4:52). The sūrah has already placed the unforgiven sin as *shirk* (4:48) and the blatant sin as fabricating lies against God (4:50); belief in *jibt* and *ṭāghūt* and the reassurance to polytheists gather both. The reassurance fabricates that disbelief is guidance, and the belief brings *shirk* where *tawḥīd* was taught. The community that was told *“do not crave what Allah gave some over others but ask Him for bounty”* (4:32) and *“what harm if they had believed and donated from what was provided”* (4:39) is here shown the opposite trade: those who had a portion did not ask for more bounty from God; they gave belief to idols and told others that the better path is the one God said not to forgive if held to death.

**A Warning Not to Sell Guidance for Alliance**

Placed between the blatant fabrication (4:50) and the condemnation with no helper (4:52), this verse warns the believers not to imitate the sellers. A portion of the Book — now the Qur’an — has also been given to them; to use it to reassure those who deny God and disobey the Messenger that they are better guided would be to repeat the same sale. The sūrah will soon command *“return trusts to rightful owners and judge with fairness”* (4:58) and *“obey Allah and obey the Messenger and those in authority among you, and refer disputes to them”* (4:59), showing where guidance is to be sought — not from *jibt* and *ṭāghūt* but from the Book and the Messenger. To have been given a portion and then to believe in false gods and to call the disbeliever better guided is to fail that referral at its first step. The verse therefore asks have you not seen so that the believers see and do not become like those who, having been given a share of guidance, believed in the very thing the guidance came to remove, and then told others that the removed thing guides better.

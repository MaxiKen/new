## Sūrah Āli ʿImrān 3:187

> ˹Remember, O Prophet,˺ when Allah took the covenant of those who were given the Scripture to make it known to people and not hide it, yet they cast it behind their backs and traded it for a fleeting gain. What a miserable profit!

**Expanded Commentary**

**The Covenant Recalled**

The verse recalls the time when God took a covenant from those who had been given the Book, and it states the content of the obligation: to make the revelation clear to people and not to conceal it. The sentence reports a commitment that the recipients had accepted.

Al-Ṭabarī records an interpretation on which the covenant was made through the prophets with their peoples, so that the obligation to teach the revelation was undertaken by the communities that received it. The verse's phrasing leaves the question of the parties to the covenant open, and the obligation it names is clear in either case.

**The Obligation to Make It Plain**

The command to make the message clear covers both the transmission of the text and its explanation, and the Book uses the vocabulary of clarification for the work of the messengers. The community addressed in the covenant had been given a scripture that was supposed to be taught rather than reserved.

The Book's instructions about knowledge are consistent on this point. What has been given for the guidance of people is not to be kept within the group that received it, and the verse states the obligation in the form of a quotation so that its terms cannot be mistaken.

**What Was Concealed**

The pronoun in the sentence refers to the Book, and some commentators understood the reference more narrowly as pointing to the foretellings of the coming of the Prophet that the earlier scriptures were believed to contain. The Book names the recognition of the messenger among the things the recipients of the earlier revelation were expected to acknowledge.

Both readings concern the same failure. The group had been given a text whose meaning obliged it to speak, and it withheld what it knew. The verse places the concealment in the same category as the harm described in the preceding verse, since silence about the truth was a form of the same conduct the community was being warned against.

**Casting It Behind Their Backs**

The verse describes the group as casting the covenant behind its back, and the image is one the Book uses elsewhere when it describes people who abandoned what they had undertaken. One passage describes a community that had been given the covenant and then disregarded it, and another describes the Book being thrown away as though it were not known.

The physical image of something dropped behind a person describes an abandonment that requires no decision at a particular moment. The covenant was not renounced in a formal act; it was simply left where it fell, and the verse's picture of the careless gesture is more telling than an account of a deliberate breach would be.

**The Paltry Price**

The verse states that the group sold the covenant for a small amount, and the Book uses the same commercial image for those who trade their commitments for advantage. One passage describes those who trade God's covenant and their oaths for a fleeting gain and states that they will have no share in the Hereafter. Another describes those who hide God's revelations and trade them for a small gain, and states that they consume nothing but fire.

The verse before the reader belongs to that group of statements, and its closing exclamation states that the sale was evil. The Book's treatment of the concealment of knowledge as a commercial transaction is consistent, and the price is described the same way in every passage.

**The General Application**

Al-Qurṭūbī read the verse as a general condemnation of anyone who is given knowledge and does not teach it, even though the sentence speaks specifically of one group. On this reading the obligation to teach what one has received applies to every community that possesses it.

The tradition supports the reading with a report in which the Prophet states that whoever conceals knowledge from his people will be bridled with one of the bridles of the fire, and with a statement attributed to ʿAlī that God does not require an ignorant person to learn until He requires a learned person to teach. The community's scholars have applied the verse in that sense ever since.

**The Book's Other Warnings Against Concealment**

The Book returns to the subject in several passages. One states that those who hide the clear proofs and the guidance after it has been made clear to humanity will be condemned by God and by those who condemn. Another asks why the recipients of the scripture mix truth with falsehood and conceal the truth while they know it.

The frequency of these warnings indicates how serious the Book considers the failure. The concealment in question is not an oversight; it is a decision made by people who know what they are doing, and the Book's language in every passage treats it as a betrayal of the trust on which the whole enterprise of guidance depends.

**What the Community Learns**

The community that recites this verse stands on the other side of its obligation. It has received a message, and the verse's account of what was done to the earlier one is a warning about what the same conduct would mean in its own case.

The sūrah's placement of the verse after its description of the trials of the community connects the two subjects. The community would be provoked by the taunts of the two groups, and it was being reminded that the withholding of knowledge had brought those groups to their present condition. The next verse continues to describe the conduct of people who had been given knowledge and had misused the position it gave them.

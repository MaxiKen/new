## Sūrah al-Naḥl 16:59

> **He hides himself from the people because of the bad news he has received. Should he keep her in disgrace, or bury her ˹alive˺ in the ground? Evil indeed is their judgment!**

**Expanded Commentary**

**Hiding From People Due To Bad News**

Yatwārā min al-qawmi min sū'i mā bushshira bihi — he hides himself from people because of evil of what he was given good news of. Yatwārā from w-r-y means to hide, conceal, from same root as tawāra, to hide. Min al-qawmi, from people, community, tribe. Min sū'i mā bushshira bihi, because of evil, badness of what he was given good news of, meaning baby girl, which he perceives as bad news, sū', evil, though in reality it is good news. Al-Ṭabarī explains that when given news of daughter, man hides from people out of shame, not wanting people to know he has daughter, because in his culture daughter is considered shame, disgrace. He hides himself, not daughter, from people, indicating shame is his, not daughter's. Al-Qurṭubī says yatwārā indicates he hides repeatedly, habitually, whenever daughter born, showing deep-seated bias.

**Should He Keep In Disgrace Or Bury Alive?**

A-yumsikuhu ʿalā hūnin am yadussuhu fī al-turāb — should he keep it in disgrace, humiliation, or bury it in dust, ground? Yumsikuhu from m-s-k, to keep, hold, retain. ʿAlā hūnin, upon disgrace, humiliation, from h-w-n, to be low, despised, meaning keep daughter alive but in disgrace, humiliation, low status, not honored. Am yadussuhu fī al-turāb, or bury it in dust, from d-s-s, to bury, hide in ground, and turāb, dust, earth, meaning bury alive in ground, as was practice of some Arabs who buried baby girls alive out of fear of poverty or shame, as 81:8-9 says *When baby girl buried alive is asked for what sin she was killed* (81:8-9) and 17:31 says *Do not kill your children for fear of poverty* (17:31). Al-Ṭabarī explains that man debates whether to keep daughter alive in humiliation or bury her alive in ground, both evil options, both due to bias.

**Evil Indeed Is Their Judgment**

Alā sā'a mā yaḥkumūn — evil indeed is what they judge, how wretched is their judgment. Alā for attention, behold. Sā'a from s-w-ʾ, evil, wretched. Mā yaḥkumūn, what they judge, decide. Al-Ṭabarī says their judgment to hide from people, to consider daughter bad news, to debate keeping in disgrace or burying alive, is evil judgment, wretched decision. Al-Rāzī explains that sā'a mā yaḥkumūn indicates that their judgment is evil in both options: keeping in disgrace is evil, burying alive is evil, so whatever they choose, judgment evil. The phrase appears in other condemnations of evil judgments, as in 16:25 *Evil is what they bear* and 16:59 *Evil is their judgment*. The sūrah thus condemns gender bias and infanticide as evil judgment.

**Why This Practice Is Condemned**

Burying daughters alive was practiced by some pre-Islamic Arabs out of fear of poverty, shame, or tribal honor. Islam forbade it categorically, as 17:31 and 81:8-9 show. The verse condemns it as evil judgment, not only as sin, but as perversion of judgment: to consider keeping daughter in disgrace or burying alive as options is evil judgment. Al-Qurṭubī notes that Islam honored daughters as blessing, not burden, and that Prophet ﷺ said *Whoever has three daughters and is patient with them, they will be shield from Fire*. The verse therefore not only condemns infanticide but also condemns keeping daughter in disgrace, indicating that daughters should be honored, not kept in humiliation. Both options are evil; correct option is to honor daughter as good news.

**Connection To Attribution Of Daughters To Allah**

Verses 57-59 form unit: they attribute daughters to Allah, glory be to Him, while for themselves what they desire, sons; when given good news of daughter, face gloomy, suppressing rage, hiding from people, debating keep in disgrace or bury alive, evil judgment. The unit shows contradiction and evil: you attribute daughters to Allah whom you consider inferior, yet you hate daughters for yourself so much that you hide and consider burying alive. If daughters are so hated, why attribute to Allah? If daughters are worthy of being attributed to Allah as angels, why hate for yourself? The contradiction exposes both theological lie and social evil. The sūrah uses social evil to refute theological lie: your own attitude to daughters shows you consider them inferior, so attributing them to Allah is insult.

**Linguistic Note On Hūn And Dass**

Yatwārā from w-r-y, to hide. Min al-qawmi, from people. Min sū'i mā bushshira bihi, from evil of what he was given good news of, with sū' meaning evil, bad, though good news in reality. A-yumsikuhu from m-s-k, to keep, hold. ʿAlā hūnin, upon disgrace, from h-w-n, low, despised, humiliation. Am yadussuhu from d-s-s, to bury, hide, to insert in ground, from same root as dassās, one who buries. Fī al-turāb, in dust, earth, from t-r-b, dust. Alā sā'a mā yaḥkumūn, evil is what they judge, from s-w-ʾ, evil, and ḥ-k-m, to judge. The verse's structure — he hides from people because of bad news, should he keep in disgrace or bury in ground, evil is their judgment — with hiding, dilemma, condemnation, creates narrative that exposes evil judgment and calls to honor daughters.

**How This Verse Teaches Value Of Daughters**

The verse teaches value of daughters by condemning opposite: hiding, gloomy face, suppressed rage, keeping in disgrace, burying alive, all described as evil judgment. The believer should do opposite: show joy at birth of daughter, honor her, not hide, not keep in disgrace, not bury. Al-Rāzī says this verse is proof that Islam came to honor women and daughters, to remove pre-Islamic bias. The sūrah's theme of blessings includes children as blessing, as verse 72 will say Allah gave you children and grandchildren as provision. Daughters are part of that blessing, not bad news. The verse therefore calls to gratitude for daughters as well as sons, and to attribute to Allah finest attributes, not what you hate for yourself.

**A Lesson On Judgment**

Sā'a mā yaḥkumūn, evil is their judgment, teaches that judgment should be just, not based on bias. To judge that daughter should be kept in disgrace or buried alive is evil judgment because it is based on bias, not justice. True judgment is to honor all children as blessings from Allah, to give daughters rights, to not attribute to Allah what you hate for yourself. The verse therefore calls to just judgment in both theology and social matters: attribute to Allah finest attributes, and treat daughters with honor.

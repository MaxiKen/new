## Sūrah al-Baqarah 2:222

> They ask you ˹O Prophet˺ about menstruation. Say, “Beware of its harm! So keep away, and do not have intercourse with your wives during their monthly cycles until they are purified. When they purify themselves, then you may approach them in the manner specified by Allah. Surely Allah loves those who always turn to Him in repentance and those who purify themselves.”

**Expanded Commentary**

**The Question About Menstruation**

The verse reports a question about menstruation and answers with the instruction to keep away from the women during the period and to approach them again once they are purified. The sentence follows the rules about marriage and treats the physical relations of the couple.

The answer concerns the conduct of the husband during the period, and the community's scholars noted that the instruction is stated in the form of a prohibition that ends when the condition ends. Readers are shown that the Book regulates this matter as it regulates the others, and the sentence supplies both the rule and the limit of its application.

**The Term Used**

The commentators discussed the word rendered as hurt, and they offered several explanations, including the discomfort that the period causes the woman, the blood itself, and the condition that the term describes. The readings agree that the period is the reason for the restriction and that the restriction is not a judgment on the woman.

The community's teachers noted that the Book's wording describes the condition without attaching any notion of the woman's guilt, and they used the sentence when they set out the rulings on the subject. Readers are given an instruction that concerns the conduct of the husband, and the sentence's phrasing places the responsibility for the observance on him.

**The Practices of the Surrounding Societies**

The commentators described the customs that the community had inherited, and they noted that menstruating women were sometimes kept away from the household and from shared food, in practices connected with the prescriptions of the earlier communities. The reports show what the verse addressed in the community's own setting.

The sentence's instruction is narrower than the customs it replaced, and the community's scholars noted the difference between the restriction on intercourse and the general avoidance that the earlier practice required. The passage shows that the Book moved the community away from the stricter customs, and the sentence's wording supplies the boundary of the rule.

**The Limits of the Restriction**

The community's jurists concluded from the sentence that the restriction concerns the act named rather than every form of contact, and they noted that the verse's instruction ends when the period is over. The commentaries discuss the point at which the purification is complete, and the schools describe different applications.

The discussion shows how the community's scholars handled a matter on which the text gives the principle and the reports supply the detail. The account describes a ruling whose boundary is stated by the verse itself, and the community's teachers used the sentence when they explained the difference between the restriction and the customs that surrounded it.

**The Purification Named**

The sentence states that the community may approach the women once they have purified themselves and that God loves those who turn to Him and those who purify themselves, and the community's scholars connected the closing phrase with the instruction. The verse's use of the vocabulary of purification gives the sentence its place among the passages about cleanliness.

The Book's treatment of purification elsewhere includes the instructions about ablution, and the community's teachers gathered the material into a single teaching. The passage shows that the verse's concern is the state of the persons involved and that the mention of purification closes the instruction with a word that the Book uses elsewhere for the inward as well as the outward condition.

**The Passage's Concern**

The sentence stands among the rules about marriage and the household, and it follows the prohibition of certain unions and precedes the description of the spouses' relations. The Book has been organising the community's family life, and the reader is shown that no part of that life is left outside the guidance the sūrah provides.

The passage's treatment of the subject is brief and its instruction is clear, and the community's teachers used the verse when they taught about the conduct of married life. The reader is left with the ruling and with the names that accompany it, and the passage continues with the instruction about the approach to the wife.

**The Book's Concern for the Woman**

The sentence treats a condition that the earlier communities had surrounded with customs, and the Book's answer separates the restriction it imposes from the severity that had gathered around the subject. The reports describe households in which the woman who was in the period ate apart and slept apart, and the teachers noted that the instruction of the verse concerns the husband's conduct rather than the standing of his wife.

The jurists drew the boundary of the rule from the wording. What the sentence prohibits ends with the period, and the remainder of the household's life continues as before, since the verse does not remove the woman from the table or from the company of her family. The discussions of the schools concern the point at which the condition is judged to have ended.

The material also records the questions that reached the Prophet ﷺ about the subject, and the answers that were given. The community received the instruction as part of the legislation that orders its households, and the closing phrase of the sentence places the matter within the vocabulary of purification that the Book uses for its worship as well as for the body.

**What the Verse Establishes**

The sentence establishes the restriction during the period, the limit at which it ends, and the standing of those who purify themselves. The community's teachers used the verse when they set out the rulings on the subject, and the reader is shown that the Book's legislation on the household addresses the relations between the spouses with the same attention it gives to their obligations.

The verse has been read as evidence of the attention the legislation gives to the position of the woman, since it removes the customs of avoidance while fixing the rule that concerns the couple alone. The teachers also connected the sentence with the passages that describe the purity of the community in its worship, so that the subject was taught as a part of the same guidance.

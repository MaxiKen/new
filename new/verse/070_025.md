## Sūrah al-Maʿārij 70:25

> to the beggar and the poor;

**Expanded Commentary**

**Two Kinds of Need**

Li-l-sāʾili wa-l-maḥrūm — "for the asker and the deprived." Sāʾil asks; maḥrūm abstains — petition voiced, need veiled. Al-Rāzī distinguishes: beggars request, the deprived need without asking. The twin verse confirms — same pair named in 51:19. Two temperaments, one entitlement: ḥaqq covers both. Such pairing completes charity's sociology — the visible needy (asking) and invisible (abstaining). Givers must serve both: answer petitions, seek silences. Readers should split giving accordingly: respond to asks, hunt abstentions. What asks, give; what hides, find. Need wears two faces; duty answers both. Sāʾil and maḥrūm — the due's twin destinees.

Sāʾil — asker, petitioner, beggar. Asking claims ḥaqq vocally: "my share, please." Such vocality dignifies — petitioners assert rights, not favors. The Book elsewhere forbids repelling askers (paraphrased from 93:10: do not rebuff the beggar) — claims honored, not scorned. Contempt for beggars thus violates ḥaqq doubly: withholding due plus wounding claimant. Readers should receive askers as auditors — heaven's inspectors checking disbursement. Every petition tests manūʿ: open or clutch? Pass each test visibly. What asks, answer; whom heaven sends, honor. Beggars bless givers more than gifts bless beggars — tests passed, purification earned. Welcome the audit.

Maḥrūm — deprived, debarred, cut off. Some from provision (fortune withheld), some from asking (pride forbidding). Al-Rāzī hears needy abstainers; other senses (see 51:19) widen to all provision-barred. The abstaining deprived hide need honorably — modesty veiling hunger. Finding them is charity's higher skill: discernment past appearances. Elsewhere the Book describes them — the unaware deem them rich, from restraint (paraphrased from 2:273). Such invisibility challenges givers: seek, don't just respond. Readers should hunt maḥrūm actively: notice lunchless colleagues, threadbare neighbors. What hides honorably deserves eagerly. Find them; fund them. Deprivation veiled still binds dues.

**Asking Tests the Asked**

Beggars examine givers. Each petition is heaven's pop quiz: manūʿ or muʿṭī (withholder or giver)? The needy are examiners disguised — ragged proctors grading generosity. Such testing dignifies encounters: beggars serve givers' salvation. Refusing tests fails courses; passing accumulates credit. Readers should thank petitioners inwardly — tests afford passing. What asks today excuses tomorrow: "I gave" answers audit. Welcome examinations; ace them with open hands. The asked need the asker more — giver's eternity hangs on petitioner's visit. Test welcomed is mercy doubled.

Sāʾil shameless (asking openly), maḥrūm ashamed (hiding honorably) — giving must span both. Open hands receive petitioners; seeking eyes find abstainers. Neither temperament escapes ḥaqq: shamelessness claims, shame deserves. Such span completes verse 24's ḥaqq maʿlūm — known due, known destinees. Readers should calibrate both modes: budget for asks, reserve for finds. What petitions, pay; what hides, hunt. Charity's two hands — receiving hand for sāʾil, seeking hand for maḥrūm. Span both; slight neither. Need's spectrum fully served is duty fully paid.

Verse 24 stated duty (ḥaqq in wealth); verse 25 names recipients (beggar, deprived). Obligation first, beneficiaries after — ethics ordered rightly. Duty does not await destitute discovery; destitute are sought because duty exists. Such ordering prevents excuse ("found no needy"): verse 25 guarantees finds — askers everywhere, abstainers nearby. Readers should let duty drive discovery: dues budgeted seek recipients. What obligation ordains, observation locates. Never invert — waiting to stumble on need. Hunt budgeted. Recipients named; release dues.

Impulse giving fluctuates; systematic giving endures. Calendars fix zakāh-dates; calculations fix amounts; channels fix delivery. Institutions stabilize: funds pooled, needs assessed, distributions tracked. System beats sentiment — dues paid in low moods as high. Such infrastructure fulfills maʿlūm (known): known sums, known schedules. Readers should systematize: annual zakāh-day, monthly ṣadaqah-quota, emergency reserve. What systems deliver, whims withhold. Build giving machinery; run it faithfully. Impulse initiates; system sustains. Deliver dues like clockwork — heaven's payroll, never missed.

**Askers Deserve Honor, Not Scorn**

Begging humiliates; givers must not add scorn. Manners matter: give kindly, speak gently, preserve faces. "Kind words and covering faults beat charity followed by harm" (paraphrased from 2:263). Harm nullifies: reminders, reproaches, publicity poison gifts. Honor the claimant — need confers dignity (ḥaqq asserted), not shame. Such courtesy completes verse 24's duty: dues paid graciously. Readers should script generosity: smile first, give quietly, forget instantly. What manners preserve (dignity), charity alone cannot. Give doubly — wealth plus honor. Scorn voids alms; kindness doubles them.

**Seeking the Hidden Poor**

Finding maḥrūm is charity's higher skill. The abstaining needy hide honorably — threadbare respectability, quiet skipping of meals. Seekers must discern: notice worn shoes, declined invitations, overtime without complaint. Community knowledge guides — neighbors know, colleagues sense. Dignified inquiry follows: indirect offers, face-saving gifts, "surplus" shared. Zakāh committees institutionalize seeking — funds collected, hidden cases investigated. Such seeking fulfills verse 25's second half: sāʾil answered, maḥrūm found. Readers should practice detection: scan circles for silent need. What hides honorably deserves eagerly. Give where asking never reaches. The hidden poor are ḥaqq's hardest test — pass it by seeking.

Giving disciplines givers. Each disbursement exercises release — clutch loosened, trust strengthened. Regular charity detoxifies wealth-attachment: doses repeated, dependence cured. The due is thus worship, not just welfare — payers purified per payment. Such training compounds: generous years make generous characters. Misers convert by installments — give till giving gladdens. The ḥaqq's schedule (annual zakāh, frequent ṣadaqah) paces therapy: repeated treatment, deepening cure. Readers should embrace dues as gym: reps scheduled, muscles (mercy) grown. What training taxes temporarily, character keeps permanently. Pay dues; gain selves. The poor receive money; givers receive themselves — purified, proven, prepared.

**From Purse to Creed Next**

Wealth-duties complete (verses 24-25); creed-duties follow (verse 26: affirming Judgment Day). Purse then profession — giving then believing. The sūrah's order intertwines practice and creed: charity performed, Day affirmed. Readers should carry open hands toward firm faith: giving proves believing. What follows will anchor ethics in eschatology. The next verse affirms the Day of Judgment, grounding duties in destiny.

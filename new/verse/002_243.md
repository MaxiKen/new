## Sūrah al-Baqarah 2:243

> Have you ˹O Prophet˺ not seen those who fled their homes in the thousands for fear of death? Allah said to them, “Die!” then He gave them life. Surely Allah is ever Bountiful to humanity, but most people are ungrateful.

**Expanded Commentary**

**The Account Stated**

The verse recounts the case of those who left their homes in their thousands for fear of death, and it states that God said to them, die, and then restored them to life. The sentence follows the rules about the household and opens a series of accounts that treat the community's history and its conduct.

The Book's purpose in the passage is to instruct the community through the record of those who came before it, and the community's teachers read the sentence as the statement about the power that governs the affairs of people. Readers are shown that the passage turns from legislation to narrative, and the account supplies the material the following verses develop.

**The Identity of the People**

The commentators discussed the identity of those who left their homes, and the reports record several identifications, including a people of the earlier communities who abandoned their town to escape a plague and a group who departed in fear of a battle. The community's scholars set out the positions they received.

The reports agree that the account concerns a departure undertaken out of fear, and the community's teachers read the sentence as a statement about the futility of the calculation that led the people out. Readers are given the account of a narrative whose details the commentaries discussed at length, and the sentence supplies the report in its summary form.

**The Power Described**

The sentence states that God said to the people, die, and that He then gave them life, and the community's teachers read the account as a statement about the authority over life and death. The Book's use of the language of command in the account gives the sentence its force.

The wording places the whole of the people's departure within the knowledge of God, and the community's scholars noted that the account is given to the community that follows as an instruction rather than as a story alone. The passage shows that the passage's concern is the dependence of every person on the One who gives life, and the account supplies the instance that makes the point.

**The Instruction Drawn**

The commentators drew the lesson that a person cannot escape what has been decreed by leaving his home, and they connected the account with the community's duty to continue in its affairs. The sentence also supplies the statement about the bounty of God, which the community's teachers read as the ground of the hope that accompanies the instruction.

The Book's treatment of its narratives elsewhere presents the accounts of the earlier communities as the material from which the community learns, and the community's scholars gathered the instances. The account describes a passage that draws its lesson from the history of those who preceded, and the sentence supplies the event around which the lesson is built.

**The Closing Attribute**

The sentence closes with the statement that God is bountiful to people, and the community's teachers read the attribute as the ground of the account. The Book's practice of attaching a description of God to its narratives appears throughout the sūrah.

The wording places the restoration of the people within the favour that God shows to the community, and the community's scholars used the clause when they spoke about the mercy that accompanies the events of a person's life. The passage shows that the account ends with the statement about the One who acted, and the sentence supplies the attribute that accompanies the report.

**The Passage's Concern**

The sentence opens a group of verses that treat the community's conduct in difficulty and its willingness to strive, and it supplies the material on which the following instructions are built. The Book has completed the rules about the household and now addresses the community's affairs in the world.

The community's teachers read the passages that follow as the development of the themes the account introduces, and the reader is shown how the sūrah moves from the regulation of the family to the history of the communities. The passage continues with the instruction to strive in the way of God, and the sentence in this place supplies the account from which that instruction proceeds.

**The Community That Departed**

The commentators discussed the identity of the people the sentence describes, and the reports mention a town whose inhabitants fled from a plague and a company that abandoned its position in fear of an enemy. The wording gives the essential element of the account, and the exegetes explained that the departure was undertaken in the belief that distance could avert what had been decreed.

The command that the sentence reports and the restoration that follows have been read as the statement about the authority of God over the life of every person. The teachers noted that the account is placed immediately before the command to strive, so that the community learns both the limits of human calculation and the duty that remains when the calculation fails.

The account belongs to the series of narratives that the sūrah uses for the instruction of its community. The Book recounts the history of the earlier peoples in order to make its lessons visible, and the attributes at the end of the sentence name the favour of the One who restored the company to its life.

**What the Verse Establishes**

The sentence establishes the account of those who left their homes in fear, the power of God over life and death, the instruction that the community is to draw from the event, and the statement about the bounty that accompanies the report. The community's teachers used the verse when they spoke about the events it describes, and the reader is shown that the sūrah's guidance draws on the history of the communities for the instruction of those who follow.

The teachers used the sentence when they spoke about the flight from difficulty, and the passage supplied the lesson that the community's response to trial is not found in escape but in obedience. The verse also introduces the command that follows it, so that the account and the instruction are read as a single unit.

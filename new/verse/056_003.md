## Sūrah al-Wāqiʿah 56:3

> It will debase ˹some˺ and elevate ˹others˺.

**Expanded Commentary**

**The Event Reorders Human Rank**

The verse describes the Inevitable Event through two opposite effects: it will debase some and elevate others. The final upheaval is therefore not only a collapse of the physical world. It is also a disclosure of value. People who appeared important may be exposed as poor in the qualities that matter before God, while people overlooked in the world may be raised because of faith, truthfulness, patience, and service.

The paired verbs make the judgment active. The Event does not merely reveal that different people already occupy different places; it brings them into those places. The hidden moral order becomes public. This is why the verse can comfort the oppressed without encouraging revenge. God’s judgment, not the injured person’s anger, will determine the final measure. It can also humble the successful without requiring contempt for every worldly achievement. Wealth and influence may be used well, but neither can protect a person who has made them substitutes for obedience.

**Abasement Is More Than Physical Defeat**

To be debased in the Qur’anic sense is to lose false dignity. A person may have been lowered socially in this life while remaining noble before God, and another may have been praised by crowds while inwardly degraded by arrogance. The final Event strips away the protection supplied by reputation. It shows whether honour was rooted in truth or borrowed from public approval.

This reading also explains why the verse is not a simple promise that every believer will enjoy worldly elevation. Prophets and righteous people have suffered rejection, poverty, and violence. Their apparent humiliation did not define their standing. Conversely, the prosperity of a tyrant did not prove divine favour. The Last Day is the point at which the difference between appearance and reality is settled. What people called success will be weighed against what it produced in the soul.

**Elevation Comes From Nearness to God**

Elevation in the final order is not merely movement to a higher physical place. It is honour, acceptance, and nearness. The person raised is one whose life has been directed toward God rather than toward the applause of creation. Such elevation may include a rank in Paradise, but its deepest meaning is that the servant is received with mercy and shown the truth of what he pursued.

This keeps the promise from becoming a crude competition. The believer does not seek elevation so that others may be humiliated by his superiority. He seeks it by becoming more truthful, more useful, and more surrendered. In the Qur’anic vision, genuine height is compatible with humility because it is received as grace. The one who truly knows that God raises whom He wills cannot use rank as a reason for pride.

**Truth Can Lower Before It Raises**

The verse’s two verbs can also describe what happens within a person before the final judgment. Recognition of truth may debase the ego by exposing its false claims, yet that same surrender elevates the heart. A person who admits dependence loses the illusion of self-sufficiency and gains freedom from it. A person who confesses a wrong is lowered in his own estimation but may be raised through repentance.

This is why spiritual growth can feel like a reversal. The proud self wants to rise without being corrected. Revelation first brings it down to its proper size. It removes the demand to be treated as the centre of the world. That lowering is not destruction; it is preparation. Once the soul stops defending its false greatness, it can receive guidance. The truth levels the obstacle and then lifts the person who has accepted it.

**The Choice Is Being Made Now**

Although the verse speaks about the Event, its moral sorting begins in the present. Every act of honesty strengthens the reality that will one day be displayed. Every act of arrogance rehearses the abasement that comes from treating created status as permanent. The final judgment does not invent a person’s character from nothing. It reveals and completes what the person repeatedly chose.

The practical response is therefore to ask what kind of elevation one is pursuing. Is it the elevation of wealth, visibility, and control, or the elevation of a servant who can be trusted with power? Is one willing to be lowered by repentance now in order to avoid the greater abasement of public exposure later? The Event will overturn false rankings. Wisdom lies in allowing revelation to overturn them before that Day, while change is still possible and humility can still become a chosen virtue. That is the honest beginning of elevation. This also changes how a person treats those who seem below him. If God alone gives final elevation, no servant may despise another servant. The person overlooked by society may be nearer to God than the celebrated person, and the surest response is to honour people without pretending to know their final rank. Such humility is not weakness. It frees a person from the exhausting need to win every comparison and redirects energy toward becoming worthy of God’s elevation. The servant can then accept correction without feeling erased. A lowered ego is not a lowered human worth. It is the removal of a false claim so that dignity can rest on worship, character, and service. The verse also gives a standard for leadership: the best leader is not the person who appears highest, but the one whose authority produces justice and whose private conduct can bear public exposure. Before the final Event raises or lowers anyone, every holder of influence should ask whether his influence is lifting the vulnerable or merely enlarging himself.

## Sūrah al-Tawbah 9:120

> It was not ˹proper˺ for the people of Medina and the nomadic Arabs around them to avoid marching with the Messenger of Allah or to prefer their own lives above his. That is because whenever they suffer from thirst, fatigue, or hunger in the cause of Allah; or tread on a territory, unnerving the disbelievers; or inflict any loss on an enemy—it is written to their credit as a good deed. Surely Allah never discounts the reward of the good-doers.

**Expanded Commentary**

**The Messenger's Mission Changes the Meaning of Sacrifice**

The verse says that the people of Medina and the nomadic Arabs around them should not prefer their lives over the Messenger's when following him is required. The command belongs to the Prophet's public leadership and to the conditions of that community. It is not a general permission for private individuals to abandon lawful authority or to force others into danger.

The principle is that a community cannot claim loyalty while treating its messenger's mission as someone else's burden. The believers share the cost according to ability. They do not place the Prophet in danger while preserving every personal comfort. In later settings, the same principle appears as readiness to support truth and legitimate communal responsibility rather than as blind obedience to every political leader.

**Hardship Itself Can Become Reward**

The verse lists thirst, fatigue, and hunger. These are not achievements people would seek for their own sake. They become rewardable when endured in Allah's cause and joined to sincere action. The servant does not need every step to produce a visible success before Allah records it.

This gives meaning to unavoidable discomfort. Travel, long work, care for others, and the effort to preserve justice may exhaust the body. If the servant accepts the hardship for Allah while using wise means to reduce it, the hardship is not wasted. The reward lies not in pain alone but in the obedience, patience, and purpose carried through the pain.

**Movement Can Frighten Wrongdoing**

The verse includes stepping into territory that unnerves the disbelievers. This describes the moral effect of the believers' presence. They do not need to boast or commit injustice to weaken false power. Their organized obedience, courage, and refusal to submit can make wrongdoing uncertain.

The principle applies beyond battle. A truthful witness can unsettle a corrupt institution. A group that refuses exploitation can make an unjust practice harder to sustain. A teacher who gives people sound knowledge can weaken the power of rumor. The believer seeks this effect through lawful means. He does not create fear in the innocent and call it victory; he makes falsehood less secure by making truth more visible.

**Loss Inflicted on an Enemy Is Not the Whole Reward**

The verse counts even the loss inflicted on an enemy as a good deed when it occurs in Allah's cause. This does not make every harm to an opponent righteous. The cause, authority, conduct, and target matter. The reward is connected to resisting wrongdoing within the moral limits Allah has set.

The believer therefore avoids hatred as a goal. He may resist an aggressor, protect a community, and accept that an enemy's power is reduced. He does not rejoice in cruelty or harm people who are not responsible. Good deeds are not produced by an unlawful method simply because the speaker uses religious language.

**The Record Includes Small and Large Steps**

The repeated idea that each hardship and step is written teaches that Allah's record is attentive. A long campaign and a single exhausting walk may both be significant when they express sincere service. The servant does not know which act will carry the greatest weight, so he should not despise small contributions.

This also comforts people whose work is hidden. The person who prepares food, repairs equipment, drives others, or stays with dependents may not appear in the public story. Allah records the cost that allowed others to serve. No step is lost when it is taken for Him, even if people never learn that the step occurred.

**Allah Does Not Discount Goodness**

The verse ends by assuring that Allah never discounts the reward of good-doers. Human accounting can overlook hours, intentions, and indirect benefits. Allah does not. He knows the thirst, the fatigue, the fear, and the reason the servant continued.

This assurance should produce both endurance and humility. Endurance, because sacrifice has a guaranteed witness. Humility, because the reward is Allah's generosity rather than a wage the servant can demand. The believer serves without asking whether the act will become famous. Allah records every good step, and His record is more secure than any public history.

The verse therefore gives dignity to every part of a difficult effort. The visible march, the hidden preparation, the care for those who remain, and the endurance of heat all belong to the record when they serve Allah. A servant should not wait for a heroic role before offering what he has. He can reduce the burden of another person, refuse to prefer comfort, and take one lawful step into difficulty. Allah does not discount the reward because the step was small or unseen. He records the good-doer with a generosity greater than the servant's own account of what was achieved.

The assurance is especially important when a servant compares his contribution with that of others. One person may endure public danger while another quietly keeps a family supplied. One may travel while another gives wealth or skill. Allah does not use human visibility as the measure of reward. He records the act, the hardship, and the intention together. The believer therefore contributes without envy and receives another person's sacrifice without dismissing his own. Every good-doer is seen by the Lord who never loses a single step.

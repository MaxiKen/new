## Sūrah an-Nūr 24:36

> ˹That light shines˺ through houses ˹of worship˺ which Allah has ordered to be raised, and where His Name is mentioned. He is glorified there morning and evening

**Expanded Commentary**

**Sacred Geography of Terrestrial Illumination: fī buyūt**

Syntactically and conceptually, verse 36 flows seamlessly from the sublime metaphysical vision of verse 35: *fī buyūtin adhina Allāhu an turfaʿa wa-yudhkara fīhā ismuh*. The cosmic light (*al-nūr*) described as an unquenchable celestial lamp inside an exquisite crystal niche is not an ethereal concept confined to abstract heavens.

It finds tangible, earthly grounding in designated sacred physical spaces: *buyūt* (houses). Classical exegetes such as al-Ṭabarī, al-Qurṭubī, and Ibn Kathīr explained that these houses refer primarily to mosques (*masājid*) established on earth for the sole worship of the Creator. While mosques are the primary manifestation, the term embraces all sanctuaries dedicated to the unadulterated remembrance of God, including the Kaʿbah in Makkah, the Prophet's Mosque in Madinah, and the sacred sanctuary of Jerusalem. These terrestrial nodes serve as spiritual beacons radiating divine light into the surrounding human landscape, providing a perpetual refuge where weary human hearts can find shelter against the blinding distractions and moral chaos of worldly life, renewing their spiritual strength.

**Elevating Sanctuaries of Worship: an turfaʿa**

The divine decree specifies two inseparable dimensions of sacred architecture: *adhina Allāhu an turfaʿa*, which Allah has ordered to be raised. The verb *turfaʿ* carries both physical construction and spiritual exaltation.

Physically, it commands the material erection, purification, and diligent maintenance of mosques. Mosques must be built solidly, kept impeccably clean, scented, and preserved from filth, trivial idle chatter, and commercial transactions. Spiritually, *turfaʿ* demands the exaltation of their status and dignity in the hearts of believers. Sanctuaries are elevated above ordinary secular buildings; they are immune from worldly arrogance, political propaganda, and base vulgarity. When a believer crosses the threshold of a mosque, they step out of the chaotic, temporal marketplace into a realm of transcendent reverence, leaving worldly status, tribal rivalry, and financial anxiety behind to bow alongside fellow servants of the Living God in profound humility and shared equality.

**Remembrance and Perpetual Invocation: wa-yudhkara fīhā ismuh**

The primary purpose animating these elevated houses is the continuous commemoration of the divine presence: *wa-yudhkara fīhā ismuh*, and where His Name is mentioned. A physical building, regardless of its architectural splendor, marble pillars, or soaring minarets, remains an empty shell if it is devoid of the living remembrance of God.

The mention of God's Name encompasses multiple expressions of devotion: the public proclamation of the call to prayer (*adhān*), the recitation of the Qur'an, liturgical litanies, circles of sacred learning, and collective supplication. In these spaces, human tongues abandon worldly gossip, slander, and commercial calculation to articulate the holy attributes of the Creator. The atmosphere becomes charged with divine peace (*sakīnah*), drawing down the presence of angels who surround gatherings of divine remembrance, creating an oasis of purity and moral renewal amidst an otherwise distracted, forgetful world that hungers for true meaning.

**Cosmic Cadence of Glorification: bi-al-ghuduwwi wa-al-āṣāl**

The text establishes a perpetual rhythm of spiritual devotion: *yusabbiḥu lahū fīhā bi-al-ghuduwwi wa-al-āṣāl*, He is glorified there morning and evening. The term *al-ghuduww* refers to the early morning hours, the period of dawn when the world awakens, while *al-āṣāl* is the plural of *aṣīl*, denoting the late afternoon hours preceding sunset.

Together, these two temporal poles encompass the entire cycle of human wakefulness. Classical authorities noted that this glorification refers to the canonical prayers, specifically the dawn prayer (*Fajr*) and the afternoon and evening prayers (*ʿAṣr*, *Maghrib*, and *ʿIshāʾ*). By consecrating the beginning and the end of each day to the glorification (*tasbīḥ*) of the Lord, believers anchor their daily existence in cosmic praise. The sun rises and sets as the servant bows and prostrates, harmonizing human worship with the ceaseless hymns sung by the angels and the physical cosmos, ensuring that morning endeavors and evening rest are both sealed with divine remembrance and protected from heedlessness.

**Spiritual Resonance in Human Temples**

Beyond physical architecture, insightful spiritual masters observed a profound allegorical dimension in the term *buyūt*. The ultimate "house" built by God on earth is the human breast, and the true sanctuary wherein His Name is continuously remembered is the purified heart of the sincere believer.

Just as a physical mosque must be elevated above filth and consecrated for worship, the interior temple of the human heart must be cleansed of malice, arrogance, covetousness, and spiritual pollutants. When the heart is consecrated as a dwelling for the divine Name, the uncreated light of God shines through it without obstruction. The believer walks among fellow human beings as a living sanctuary of light, radiating integrity, compassion, justice, and tranquil majesty into every sphere of family and communal life, becoming an instrument of peace, healing, and guidance in society.

**Syntactic Continuity with the Verse of Light**

The structural placement of verse 36 immediately following verse 35 reveals a vital pedagogical truth: illumination requires dedicated discipline and sacred communal space. The magnificent metaphysical parabola of the Light of the heavens and earth does not conclude with solitary mystical withdrawal or abstract theological musing.

Instead, the divine discourse immediately guides the seeker toward the concrete communal reality of the mosque. If you seek to gaze upon the niche, the lamp, and the glowing crystal described in the Verse of Light, look into the houses of prayer where sincere souls gather to prostrate before their Maker. The highest spiritual ecstasy is anchored within the disciplined, communal routines of congregational worship, joining heaven's light to earth's obedience, and ensuring that spiritual illumination translates directly into social solidarity, communal warmth, and collective righteousness that shields the entire community from ethical collapse, establishing an enduring beacon of peace.

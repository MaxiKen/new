## Sūrah al-Anʿām 6:158

> Are they awaiting the coming of the angels, or your Lord ˹Himself˺, or some of your Lord’s ˹major˺ signs? On the Day your Lord’s signs arrive, belief will not benefit those who did not believe earlier or those who did no good through their faith. Say, “Keep waiting! We too are waiting.”

**Expanded Commentary**

**The Three Things Awaited**

Are they awaiting the coming of the angels, or your Lord Himself, or some of your Lord's major signs? The verse names the events the audience treated as the conditions for their belief, and the three items correspond to the demands the sūrah has recorded. The demand for the angels was stated in the earlier passage about the signs (6:8), and the demand that God come to them was recorded among the challenges the audience made (6:34 context); the major signs are the events that precede the judgement.

The Book records the same kind of waiting in other sūrahs. The sūrah of the Cow asks whether the people are waiting for God to come to them in the shade of clouds along with the angels, and it states that the matter would then be settled (2:210); the sūrah of the Bee asks whether they are awaiting the coming of the angels or the command of the Lord, and it notes that the earlier communities did the same (16:33); and the sūrah of Muhammad asks whether they are only waiting for the Hour to take them by surprise once its signs have come (47:18). The verse in front of us states the audience's position in the form of a question.

**The Closing of the Opportunity**

On the Day your Lord's signs arrive, belief will not benefit those who did not believe earlier or those who did no good through their faith. The sentence states that the arrival of the signs will not produce the outcome the audience expected, and the Book attaches the same rule to the moment of death. The sūrah of the Women says that repentance is not accepted from those who persist in sin until death approaches and they then declare their belief (4:18), and the sūrah of Yūnus records the cry of Pharaoh when he was drowning and the answer that his declaration came too late (10:90–91).

The Book's teaching in these passages is that belief is accepted while the person has the freedom to act on it. The signs the audience demanded would have removed that freedom by settling the matter, and the sentence in front of us states that a faith produced by compulsion does not produce the benefit that faith produces in the world of choice. The phrase about doing good through faith adds the second condition, which is that belief must have produced conduct.

**The Command to the Prophet**

Say, Keep waiting! We too are waiting. The sentence gives the Prophet the words to use, and it mirrors the declaration he was given earlier in the sūrah, when he was told to tell his people that they should persist in their ways while he persisted in his (6:135). The two declarations share the same structure: the two parties continue as they are, and the outcome will show which of them was right.

The Book's use of this form in its addresses to the Prophet appears in several sūrahs revealed in Makkah, where the community was waiting for the promised help and the opposition was waiting for the community's collapse. The sentence in front of us states the matter in terms of waiting rather than of conflict, and it leaves the timing with the One whose signs are awaited.

**What the Verse Says About the Demand for Signs**

The verse closes the sūrah's long treatment of the demand for signs, which began with the oaths sworn by the audience (6:109) and ran through the statement that even the angels and the dead would not convince them (6:111) and the account of the enemies appointed to every prophet (6:112). The verse in front of us states what the audience is waiting for and what the arrival of those things will and will not accomplish.

The Book's answer to the demand has been consistent throughout the passage. The signs are with God (6:109), the hearts of those who refuse are turned aside (6:110), and the request for a demonstration that would compel belief is answered by the statement that belief is accepted only while it is free. The sentence in front of us supplies the final element of that answer, and the passage that follows turns to the divisions among the communities.

**The Place of the Verse in the Sūrah**

The verse follows the account of the Book's arrival, the removal of the audience's excuse, and the statement of the punishment for turning away (6:155–157), and it precedes the verses about the divisions among the people of the earlier books (6:159) and the reward of deeds (6:160). The sūrah's closing section therefore moves from the Book to the response to it, and from the response to the reckoning that follows.

The verses that follow state the reward of the good deed and the punishment of the evil deed in precise terms, and the passage ends with the Prophet's declaration about the path to which he has been guided. The verse in front of us supplies the point at which the audience's waiting is answered, and the reader is left with the choice that the whole sūrah has been presenting.

**What the Verse Says About Waiting**

The sentence treats waiting as a decision rather than as a neutral position. The audience's waiting was a way of postponing the response to a message they had heard, and the Book states what the postponement produces. The two parties in the sentence are both waiting, and the difference between them is what each one is waiting for and what each one does in the meantime.

The Book's treatment of the matter elsewhere states the same point in terms of the day that comes without warning. The sūrah of the Ornament asks whether they are waiting for the Hour to take them by surprise, and the sūrah of Muhammad notes that some of its signs have already come. The verse in front of us states the position of the audience in the language of waiting, and the answer it gives is the answer the sūrah has been giving throughout: the signs are with God, and the time belongs to Him.

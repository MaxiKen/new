## Sūrah al-Aʿrāf 7:59

> Indeed, We sent Noah to his people. He said, “O my people! Worship Allah—you have no other god except Him. I truly fear for you the torment of a tremendous Day.”

**Expanded Commentary**

**The Beginning of the Punishment Accounts**

The verse opens the long sequence of prophets and peoples that occupies the middle of the chapter, and the sequence runs from Noah to Moses and his confrontation with Pharaoh. The sūrah's introduction describes these accounts as the history of communities that rejected the messengers and suffered the consequences, and the Book presents the same series in the sūrah of Hūd, the sūrah of the Poets, and the sūrah of the Moon. The verse in front of us begins the first of them.

The order of the accounts is fixed in the tradition, and each narrative follows the same shape: the messenger is sent to his people, he calls them to the worship of the one God, the notables refuse, the messenger warns, and the community is destroyed. The repetition is deliberate, and the sūrah's use of it makes the history itself the argument. The audience in Makkah met the same message in the same pattern of refusal.

**The Call to Worship the One God**

The verse reports the call of Noah, and it is the same call that the sūrah's later prophets make: worship God, for you have no god besides Him. The sūrah of the Believers reports the same words from Noah (23:23), the sūrah of Hūd reports them with the addition of the warning of a painful day (11:26), and the sūrah of the Heights itself puts the same call in the mouth of Hūd (7:65), Ṣāliḥ (7:73), and Shuʿayb (7:85). The Book states that no messenger was sent before the Prophet without the same revelation of the unity of God (21:25).

The Book's presentation of the prophetic message as one message appears in the sūrah of the Consultation, which states that God has ordained for the community the way He decreed for Noah and revealed to the Prophet (42:13), and in the sūrah of the Sand Dunes, where the Prophet is told to say that he is not the first messenger ever sent (46:9). The verse in front of us supplies the first instance of the message in the sūrah's sequence.

**The Fear of a Tremendous Day**

The verse records the warning that Noah attaches to the call, and the warning names the punishment of a great day. The commentary tradition gives the day two references, one of them the flood in which the people were destroyed in the world and the other the day of the resurrection and the reckoning; the two are joined in the narrative because the first is the proof of the second. The sūrah's later accounts of the peoples describe the punishments that arrived in the world, and the sūrah's opening verses have stated the reckoning of the last day.

The Book's habit of naming the day in the warning appears in the sūrah of Noah, where the messenger calls his people to worship and warns them of the pain of a great day. The verse in front of us gives the same warning in the first of the sūrah's accounts, and the sequence of the narratives that follows shows what the warning meant for the community that disregarded it.

**The Prophet Noah in the Tradition**

The Book reports the story of Noah in several passages, and the accounts differ in their details. The sūrah of Hūd gives the longest of the sequences, the sūrah of the Poets gives a short version with the dialogue of the people, the sūrah of the Moon gives the account of the denial and the flood, and the sūrah of Noah gives the messenger's own appeal and the course of the deluge. This verse supplies the opening line of the sequence in this chapter.

The tradition of the community preserves the genealogy and the circumstances of the prophet, and the accounts of the earlier historians place him among the first of the messengers sent to a people. The narratives of the Book do not depend on those details, and the sūrah's account in front of us keeps to the call, the refusal, and the outcome. What the passage establishes is the pattern that the sūrah's later accounts of ʿĀd, Thamūd, Lot, and Shuʿayb repeat.

**The Argument the Sequence Makes**

The sūrah uses the sequence of the prophets to answer the audience's claim that its religion was that of its ancestors. Each community in the sequence was settled in its customs, and each refused the messenger who called it to the worship of God alone; and the destruction that followed is presented as the outcome of that refusal. The audience in Makkah knew the ruins of the peoples of ʿĀd and Thamūd and the traditions of the peoples of Lot, and the sūrah places its own message in the line of the warnings they had rejected.

The Book's treatment of the same history in the sūrah of the Poets uses the refrain that each people rejected the messengers, and the plural states the point: to refuse one messenger is to refuse the message they all carried. The verse here opens the series in this chapter, and the sūrah's following accounts carry the refrain in their own form.

**Where the Verse Sits**

The verse stands fifty-ninth in the chapter, and it follows the passage on the signs and the two kinds of land (7:52–58). The verses that follow report the answer of the notables and the exchanges between Noah and his people, and the narrative ends with the salvation of the messenger and those with him and the drowning of the deniers (7:64). The present verse supplies the opening of the first account.

The reader who has been given the signs of the created order now meets the first of the communities that ignored them. The messenger calls, the notables answer, and the account proceeds to its end; and the sūrah's following narratives repeat the pattern with the peoples that came after Noah.

**The Transition to the Next Verse**

The next verse reports that the notables of the people said that they saw the messenger in manifest error (7:60). The call to the worship of God alone in front of us is followed by the answer of the community's leaders, and the passage then records the messenger's reply.

The two verses together state the pattern that the sūrah's narratives will repeat. The messenger calls, the leaders dismiss him, and the account moves to the exchange that follows; and the sūrah's next verses state the reply of Noah and the terms on which the refusal was answered.

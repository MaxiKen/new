## Sūrah al-Shuʿarāʾ 26:217

> Put your trust in the Almighty, Most Merciful,

**Expanded Commentary**

**Trust Follows Faithful Warning**

After commanding warning, gentleness, and freedom from disobedience, Allah directs the Prophet to trust in Him. The sequence matters. The messenger must do the work assigned to him, but he cannot control how relatives and opponents respond. Tawakkul places the outcome with the Lord who has both complete power and perfect mercy.

Trust is not a retreat from responsibility. Shuʿayb warned his people and answered their challenge; the Prophet warns, teaches, and remains gracious. Tawakkul begins after the servant has taken the lawful means, and it keeps him from believing that the means succeed by themselves. The preacher speaks, the community chooses, and Allah governs the result.

**The Almighty Makes Trust Rational**

The first divine name gives the Prophet confidence against the power of deniers. Their wealth, public pressure, and accusations cannot exceed Allah's authority. A messenger may be outnumbered without being abandoned. The outcome of the mission is not decided by the people who control the present scene.

The Almighty can protect the servant, open hearts, expose falsehood, or bring a consequence that no human institution can prevent. Trusting Him means refusing to treat powerful opponents as if they were ultimate. It also means not fearing loss of status so much that truth is softened to preserve a worldly arrangement.

**The Most Merciful Keeps Trust From Becoming Hardness**

The second name protects trust from turning into severity. The Prophet does not rely on Allah's power so that he can enjoy the downfall of those who oppose him. He relies on the Almighty and Most Merciful, hoping for guidance and leaving room for repentance while warning remains possible.

Quran 3:159 joins gentleness with trust in Allah after a decision has been made. Quran 14:11–12 presents messengers and believers placing trust in Allah while facing opposition. These parallels show that tawakkul contains courage and patience together. The servant is brave before people because Allah is powerful, and gentle toward people because Allah is merciful. He does not need to turn an opponent into a permanent enemy in order to remain firm, because the outcome belongs to the Lord.

**Tawakkul Holds Means and Outcome Together**

Trust does not mean abandoning planning, counsel, or protection. It means using lawful means without worshiping them. The Prophet warns relatives, teaches believers, and separates himself from disobedience; then he entrusts the result to Allah. A person who refuses every means and calls passivity trust has misunderstood the command.

The verse gives the caller a stable inner posture. Success is not measured only by immediate applause, and rejection is not proof that Allah has lost control. The messenger's duty is faithfulness, while guidance and final judgment belong to the Almighty, Most Merciful. That reliance allows him to keep speaking truth without being ruled by the audience's response.

Tawakkul is often misunderstood as waiting for results without taking steps. The Quranic pattern is different. The messenger warns relatives, shows gentleness, separates from disobedience, and then trusts Allah. The servant locks the door, seeks counsel, speaks honestly, and uses lawful protection, while knowing that none of these means can guarantee an outcome apart from Allah's will.

Quran 14:11–12 records messengers facing opposition and placing their trust in Allah. Their trust does not remove the danger or promise immediate victory. It gives them the courage to endure without changing the truth to satisfy the people who resist it. Reliance is therefore a form of worship expressed through patience when the result is delayed.

The pairing of Almighty and Most Merciful also corrects the emotional extremes of public work. A caller who remembers only power may become harsh and eager to defeat opponents. A caller who remembers only mercy may soften a command until no one knows what is being asked. Trust in both names produces firmness without cruelty and compassion without surrender.

Tawakkul protects the Prophet from taking rejection personally. He cares about the people, but their refusal does not prove that his Lord has abandoned him. He does not measure the worth of revelation by the number of people who accept it in one moment. Allah sees the faithful effort and governs the hearts; the servant continues because the command is true.

For the believer, trust becomes most visible when means appear weak. A person may have little influence, face a powerful wrongdoer, or speak into a community that prefers comfort. He still acts according to truth and leaves the final opening to Allah. The Almighty can change circumstances, and the Most Merciful can preserve a small act of obedience until it bears fruit in ways the servant cannot see.

Trust also teaches the servant how to receive success. If people accept the warning, he thanks Allah rather than treating their acceptance as proof of his personal greatness. If they reject it, he examines his method without surrendering the truth. In both cases, the heart remains attached to Allah, who gives guidance and knows what result is best.

The pairing of the names is especially valuable when a caller faces relatives who oppose him. Power can feel threatening inside a family because rejection touches affection and history. Mercy keeps the messenger from answering pain with revenge. He trusts the Almighty enough to speak clearly and the Most Merciful enough to leave a door open for those who may still return.

A believer's tawakkul is therefore visible in steadiness. He takes means without panic, accepts delay without declaring defeat, and refuses to make public approval his god. The Lord sees the effort, controls the opening of hearts, and judges the motives. Reliance on Him allows a servant to continue doing what is right when the outcome remains outside his hands. He can be patient without becoming passive and decisive without imagining that he controls the hearts of others.

## Sūrah al-Nisāʾ 4:133

> If it is His Will, He can remove you altogether, O humanity, and replace you with others. And Allah is Most Capable to do so.

**Expanded Commentary**

**If He Will — He Can Remove You and Bring Others**

The verse states *in yashāʾ yudhhibkum ayyuhā al-nāsu wa yaʾti bi ākharīn* — if He wills, He can remove you, take you away altogether, O humanity, and bring others, replace you with others. *In yashāʾ* — if He wills, if it is His will — was explained with the will that governs ownership just declared — *to Him belongs whatever is in heavens and earth* (4:126, 4:131, 4:132) — where *Wāsiʿ*, *Ghanī*, *Ḥamīd* show He does not need you. *Yudhhibkum* — to remove, to take away, to cause to go — was read as removal of generation, as He removes one people and brings another where *taqwā* is not kept. *Ayyuhā al-nāsu* — O humanity — was explained as address beyond the believers addressed for justice (4:135) to all, as the sūrah opened *“O humanity, be mindful of your Lord who created you from a single soul”* (4:1) and here warns humanity that the single soul’s spread is not irreplaceable. *Wa yaʾti bi ākharīn* — and bring others, bring other peoples — was explained as replacement with those who are mindful where you disobey, as *“if you turn away He will replace you with another people then they will not be like you”* (47:38). Early reports illustrated *nas* here as the Meccans who invoked female gods besides Him (4:117) and took Satan as guardian (4:119); to tell them He can remove them is to tell them their claiming a share is not needed. The sūrah has shown that the emigrant finds many havens where earth is spacious (4:100) — spaciousness that can receive others if you remain, and can do without you if you refuse.

**And God Is Most Capable to Do So**

The seal *wa kāna Allāhu ʿalā dhālika qadīrā* — And God is Most Capable, Powerful over that, to do that — was explained with *qadīr* — capable, powerful, measuring, determining — the One who created from single soul spread into countless (4:1) is capable to return spread to another spread. *ʿAlā dhālika* — over that, for that — removal and bringing — was read as that this is not threat only but capability, as *“God does not wrong even an atom”* (4:40) is measure, so *qadīr* over removal is measure of power. The sūrah has said He is Ever-Bountiful, All-Wise where He enriches both after separation (4:130), Self-Sufficient, Praiseworthy where mindfulness is commanded (4:131); to be Capable to remove is the power behind bounty and self-sufficiency: He enriches from His bounty because He can remove and bring without loss. Where the sūrah says *“with Him are rewards of this world and Hereafter”* (4:134) for those who desire world’s reward, capability to replace explains why both rewards are with Him — the Guardian and Helper sufficient (4:45) can give or withhold either.

**A Verse That Follows Trust With Warning**

Placed after *to Him belongs whatever is in heavens and earth, He suffices as Trustee* (4:132) and before *whoever desires world’s reward, with Him are both rewards* (4:134), this verse follows trust with warning that trust’s Sufficer can do without the entruster. The sūrah bracketed women’s law — settlement best (4:128), no total inclining (4:129), enrichment from bounty (4:130) — with universal *waṣiyyah* to be mindful (4:131) and ownership-trusteeship (4:132); now it warns *O humanity* that mindfulness is not for His need. The sūrah has warned *do not be advocate for deceit* (4:105), *no good in most secret talks* (4:114), *whoever defies the Messenger after clarity is left to what he chose* (4:115); those are instances of a humanity that could be removed if it persists, as prior communities were. For the weak this is protective: the One who can remove humanity and bring others is the One who was hoped to pardon the helpless (4:99) and who gives many havens to the emigrant (4:100) — His capability to replace is the same capability to rescue, and to trust Him as *Wakīl* (4:132) where *He can* is to entrust to the Capable.

**Removal Not for His Need, but for Justice**

The address *ayyuhā al-nāsu* after two addresses *to those before you and you* to be mindful (4:131) was explained as broadening to all descendants of the single soul, reminding that the single soul’s lesson — ties are to be honoured — is the same mindfulness commanded. To say *He can remove you* after *He is Self-Sufficient, Praiseworthy* shows removal is not for need: the Self-Sufficient does not remove for lack, He removes for justice, as He let the defier pursue what he chose then burn (4:115) and made association far straying (4:116). The sūrah that warns *“stand firm for justice as witnesses for God though against yourselves”* (4:135) makes humanity itself the witness here: if you do not stand, others will be brought who will. The verse generalises the sūrah’s replacement logic where *“with Him are rewards of both”* (4:134); desire for this world alone is answered not by giving the world alone, but by reminding that the World’s Owner can replace the desirer.

**A Short Verse That Makes Ownership Dynamic**

Though only *“if He wills He can remove you altogether and bring others, He is Most Capable,”* the verse makes ownership dynamic. The threefold *to Him belongs* — heavens and earth — was static — all is His; *He can remove and bring* is dynamic — He can do with His what wisdom wills. The sūrah that began with creation from single soul and spread, taught law for orphans, women, inheritance, trusts, prayer, judgment, charity, Abraham, will now say *stand for justice* (4:135) to the humanity that can be removed: to stand for justice is to be the *ākharīn* — others — who are brought, not the removed. The next verse will set the choice of reward: whoever desires world’s reward, with Him are both — so ask the Capable for both, not for little (4:134).

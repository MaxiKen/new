## Sūrah al-Nisāʾ 4:87

> Allah, there is no god ˹worthy of worship˺ except Him. He will certainly gather ˹all of˺ you together on the Day of Judgment—about which there is no doubt. And whose word is more truthful than Allah’s?

**Expanded Commentary**

**There Is No God Except Him**

The verse opens with *Allāhu lā ilāha illā huwa* — Allah, there is no god worthy of worship except Him — the same declaration that the sūrah placed at the root of worship: *worship God and associate none with Him* (4:36) and *association is not forgiven* (4:48). The sūrah has shown what association looks like in practice — belief in *jibt* and *ṭāghūt* (4:51), seeking judgment of false judges though commanded to reject them (4:60), and saying to one who offers peace *you are no believer* for gain (4:94). To say *lā ilāha illā huwa* is to deny those rivals at the level of worship, judgment, and truth. The phrase follows the command to answer greeting with better or same and the reckoning of all things by the Vigilant Reckoner (4:86) to remind that the Reckoner is the only God to be worshipped. The sūrah elsewhere pairs *lā ilāha illā huwa* with *there is no helper* (4:48) to show that the One who alone is to be worshipped is the One who alone suffices as Guardian and Helper (4:45). Here the declaration stands as the premise for what follows: He who alone is God is the One who will gather.

**He Will Certainly Gather You on the Day of Judgment**

The promise *la yajmaʿannakum ilā yawmi al-qiyāmati lā rayba fīhi* — He will certainly gather all of you together on the Day of Resurrection, about which there is no doubt — was read as the gathering that the previous witness scene required. *Yajmaʿannakum* — will gather you — with emphatic *lām* and *nūn* was explained as the muster where a witness is brought from every community and the Prophet ﷺ as witness over his (4:41), where those who denied and disobeyed will wish to be dust and will not hide any statement (4:42), and where every soul that tasted death — even in towers (4:78) — is brought. The Day was called *yawm al-qiyāmah* — Day of Standing — because all stand before the Reckoner who counts even a better greeting (4:86) and an intercessor’s share (4:85). *Lā rayba fīhi* — no doubt about it — was stressed as removing the doubt that made a group fear people as God and ask *why have You ordered us to fight, delay us* (4:77) and that made laggers call absence a blessing (4:72). That doubt treats the *dunyā*’s little enjoyment as more certain than the Hereafter; the verse says the gathering is more certain. The One who is Vigilant Reckoner over all things (4:86) is the One who will certainly gather all things.

**About Which There Is No Doubt**

The clause *lā rayba fīhi* was linked to the Qur’an’s opening — *“this is the Book, no doubt in it”* — and to the sūrah’s question *do they not reflect on the Qur’an? Had it been from other than God they would have found many inconsistencies* (4:82). To have no doubt about the Day is the fruit of reflecting on the Book that has no inconsistency; the Book that governs inheritance whether little or much (4:7), dowry not reclaimed (4:20–21), and trade by mutual consent (4:29) without contradiction is the same Book that promises the gathering without doubt. The sūrah has shown doubt’s practical fruit: publicising news of security or fear without referral (4:83), seeking *ṭāghūt* though commanded to reject it (4:60), and distorting *rāʿinā* (4:46). Each is a doubt about whether the Book and Messenger can handle the affair. To say *no doubt about it* is to answer that doubt at its root: the gathering where every atom is weighed and multiplied (4:40) is as sure as the Book’s coherence, because both are from the same One.

**Whose Word Is More Truthful Than Allah’s**

The question *wa man aṣdaqu min Allāhi ḥadīthā* — and whose word is more truthful than Allah’s — seals the declaration and the promise. *Aṣdaqu* — more truthful, most truthful — was explained as comparison that denies any competitor; no *ḥadīth* — statement, discourse — is truer. The sūrah has shown competing statements: *we hear and disobey* veiled as obedience (4:46), *rāʿinā* twisted, *we obey* contradicted by night-plotting (4:81), *we intended only goodwill* after disaster (4:62), *this is from you* at evil (4:78). Each is a *ḥadīth* that the sūrah said its speakers can hardly comprehend any *ḥadīth* (4:78). Against those, God’s *ḥadīth* — that there is no god except Him, that He will certainly gather, that an atom is not wronged but multiplied — was presented as the only truthful measure. The Qur’an elsewhere says *“Allah’s promise is truth”* and that the hypocrites’ oaths are shields; this question asks the listener to choose which *ḥadīth* to trust. The same *ḥadīth* that the sūrah said would be understood if they reflected will, on the Day gathered without doubt, be the one by which every other *ḥadīth* is judged, as no statement can be hidden from God (4:42) and the intercessor’s share is known (4:85).

**A Verse That Holds Worship and Gathering Together**

Placed between greeting with peace and the division over hypocrites who regressed because of misdeeds (4:88), this verse holds worship and gathering together. To say *there is no god except Him* is to worship without association — the first command (4:36); to believe He will certainly gather on the Day about which there is no doubt is to live as one who will be gathered, and therefore to return trusts, judge fairly, and fight for the oppressed where commanded. The two are one, because the One to be worshipped is the Gatherer. For the weak for whom burdens were lightened (4:28), the gathering is not a threat that crushes; it is the assurance that the better greeting answered, the share interceded for good, and the atom donated are all gathered and counted by the Vigilant Reckoner. Whose word is more truthful? The sūrah answers with the Book that, had it been from other than God, would have shown many inconsistencies but shows none, and with the Day that, about which there is no doubt, will show that the truthful *ḥadīth* was God’s, and that to have worshipped Him alone was to have trusted the only word that never varies.

## Sūrah al-Isrāʾ 17:28

> **But if you must turn them down ˹because you lack the means to give˺—while hoping to receive your Lord’s bounty—then ˹at least˺ give them a kind word.**

**Expanded Commentary**

**If You Must Turn Them Down While Hoping For Bounty**

Wa-immā tuʿriḍanna ʿanhum ibtighā'a raḥmatin min rabbika tarjūhā — and if you turn away from them seeking mercy from your Lord you hope. Wa-immā, and if, but if, conditional with emphasis. Tuʿriḍanna from ʿ-r-ḍ, to turn away, avoid, from aʿraḍa, to turn away, with emphasis, indeed turn away. ʿAnhum, from them, from relatives, poor, traveller mentioned previous, those asking. Ibtighā'a from b-gh-y, seeking, desire, from ibtaghā, to seek, as verse 12 li-tabtaghū faḍlan, to seek bounty. Raḥmatin from r-ḥ-m, mercy, bounty, from raḥmah, mercy. Min rabbika, from your Lord, from r-b-b. Tarjūhā from r-j-w, to hope, expect, from rajā, hope. Al-Ṭabarī explains that if you must turn away from them, relatives, poor, traveller, because you lack means to give, while hoping to receive mercy, bounty, provision from Lord, so you can give later, then give kind word. This indicates situation where believer wants to give but lacks means, hopes for Lord's bounty to give, so turns away temporarily, not out of stinginess but lack.

**Give Them Kind Word**

Fa-qul lahum qawlan maysūran — then say to them gentle, kind, easy word. Fa-qul from q-w-l, to say. Lahum, to them. Qawlan maysūran from y-s-r, gentle, kind, easy, soft, from maysūr, easy, gentle, from yasura, to be easy, from same root as yusr, ease, and maysarah, ease. Al-Ṭabarī says then say to them gentle, kind word, easy, soft, promising good, not harsh, as at least give kind word if cannot give wealth. Al-Qurṭubī says qawlan maysūran means gentle, kind, easy word, like saying Allah will provide for us and you, or I will give when I have, or kind promise, not harsh rejection. This is minimum when cannot give due to lack of means while hoping for bounty.

**Why Kind Word When Cannot Give**

Why kind word? Because giving due to relatives, poor, traveller is commanded, but if you lack means, you cannot give, but you can give kind word, which is also good, maintains ties, avoids hurting. The sūrah's moral code emphasizes both financial giving and good speech: honouring parents with noble word, now kind word to those asking when cannot give. The person who lacks means but hopes for Lord's bounty and gives kind word is not wasteful nor tight-fisted, but moderate, seeking bounty to give later. This is most upright: give if you can, if cannot, give kind word while hoping for bounty.

**Connection To Bounty Never Withheld And Provision Abundant Or Limited**

Verse 20 said bounty never withheld, verse 21 some favoured over others, verse 30 Lord gives abundant or limited provisions to whoever He wills. Verse 28 says if you must turn them down because you lack means while hoping to receive Lord's bounty, then give kind word. The connection is that lack of means is because Lord gives limited provisions to whoever He wills, as test, but bounty never withheld in general, so hope for bounty, for mercy, provision, to give later. The person who lacks means should not be ashamed, but hope for Lord's bounty and give kind word.

**Link To Not Being Tight-Fisted Nor Openhanded**

Verse 29 will say do not be tight-fisted nor openhanded. Verse 28 says if you must turn down because lack means while hoping for bounty, give kind word. The link is that turning down due to lack is not tight-fisted, tight-fisted is withholding when you have means out of stinginess, while turning down due to lack while hoping for bounty is excused, with kind word as replacement. The balance is: give due when you have, avoid waste, avoid tight-fisted, avoid openhanded to poverty, and if lack means, give kind word while hoping.

**Linguistic Note On Tuʿriḍanna And Maysur**

Immā from a-m-m, if, conditional with emphasis. Tuʿriḍanna from ʿ-r-ḍ, to turn away, from aʿraḍa, to turn away, with nun emphasis, indeed turn away. ʿAnhum, from them. Ibtighā' from b-gh-y, seeking, from ibtaghā, to seek. Raḥmah from r-ḥ-m, mercy, bounty. Rabb from r-b-b, Lord. Tarjūhā from r-j-w, to hope. Qul from q-w-l, to say. Qawl from q-w-l, word. Maysūr from y-s-r, easy, gentle, kind, from yasura, to be easy. The verse's structure — if you turn away from them seeking mercy from your Lord you hope, then say to them gentle word — with condition of turning away due to seeking mercy and hoping, and command to say gentle word, shows mercy and kindness even when cannot give, while hoping for bounty to give later.

**How This Verse Teaches Kindness When Unable**

This verse teaches kindness when unable to give: if you must turn down relatives, poor, traveller because you lack means while hoping to receive Lord's bounty, then at least give kind word, gentle, easy. Al-Qurṭubī says believer who lacks means to give due should not be harsh, should not yell, should give kind word, gentle, promising, while hoping for Lord's bounty, mercy, provision, to give later. This is part of most upright guidance: honouring parents with noble word, giving due, and when cannot, kind word. The practical lesson is to be kind even when unable to give financially, to hope for Lord's bounty, not despair, and to maintain ties with kind speech.

**A Reflection On Hoping For Bounty**

Ibtighā'a raḥmatin min rabbika tarjūhā — seeking mercy from your Lord you hope — indicates that believer who lacks means seeks mercy, bounty, provision from Lord, hopes for it, expects it, to give to deserving. This hope is worship, trust, as Lord gives abundant or limited as He wills, All-Aware All-Seeing. The person who hopes for bounty while giving kind word is not ungrateful like Devil, but grateful, hopeful, trusting. The believer who reflects will give due when able, avoid waste, avoid tight-fisted, avoid openhanded, and when unable, give kind word while hoping for Lord's bounty, maintaining most upright character.

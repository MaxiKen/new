## Sūrah al-Baqarah 2:198

> There is no blame on you for seeking the bounty of your Lord ˹during this journey˺. When you return from ’Arafât, praise Allah near the sacred place and praise Him for having guided you, for surely before this ˹guidance˺ you were astray.

**Expanded Commentary**

**The Permission to Trade**

The verse states that there is no blame on the community for seeking the bounty of their Lord, and the commentators explained the sentence as a permission to engage in trade during the pilgrimage. The community's scholars noted that the wording removes an anxiety about the journey's purpose.

The Book's phrasing is a negation of blame rather than a command, and the community's teachers read it as a statement that the commercial activity does not detract from the rites. What the reader receives is a permission that fits the journey, since the pilgrimage gathered people from distant regions and the journey provided occasions for trade.

**The Departure from ʿArafāt**

The sentence then describes the departure from ʿArafāt and the remembrance of God at the sacred place, and the commentators identified the site as the stopping place where the pilgrims spend the night after the day of standing. The community's practice of praying and remembering at the site follows the instruction.

The Book's sequence gives the journey its movement, from the standing at ʿArafāt to the night at the sacred place and then to the rites at Mina. The sentence closes with the instruction to remember God for having guided the community, and it states that they were formerly among those who had gone astray.

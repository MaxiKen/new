## Sūrah al-ʿAnkabūt 29:4

> Or do the evildoers ˹simply˺ think that they will escape Us? How wrong is their judgment!

**Expanded Commentary**

**A Second Question Turns to a Different Accused**

The particle that opens this sentence links it to the question before it while turning to a new defendant. The first question indicted people whose claim of faith had been mistaken for a guarantee. This one indicts people whose conduct is bad and whose expectation is still worse: they reckon that wrong will go unnoticed. Both errors share a single structure. Each supposes that a story can be closed before it is finished, one by the saying of a sentence and the other by the passage of time. The wording is broad on purpose — those who do evil deeds, without naming a specific crime or a specific community. The Book is not describing a category of professional criminals; it is describing an assumption available to anyone whose behaviour and expectation do not match. And the sentence ends in an exclamation rather than an argument, because the error is not one that needs refuting in stages. It needs to be named, and then judged, and the verse does both in nine words.

**The Race Nobody Wins Against God**

The verb translated as escaping carries the sense of arriving first, of getting ahead of someone in a race. The evildoer of this verse pictures God as a pursuer with limited speed and himself as a runner with a head start. The Book answers that picture in another passage with the same root: those who disbelieve should not suppose that they are beyond reach, because they cannot make themselves uncatchable (8:59). The claim being ridiculed is not that God is weak; the evildoer would never say that. The claim being ridiculed is that God is slow, and that the interval between the deed and the accounting is his own territory, where he can arrange matters. Time is the thing he thinks he has won, and time is the thing he did not create. The race image fits the verse's ending, since a verdict of "how wrong is their judgment" is exactly what a spectator says when a competitor has misread the course.

**Prosperity Read as a Verdict**

The specific reasoning behind the mistake deserves to be stated, because the verse assumes it rather than spelling it out. Health, wealth, and long life arrive to the wrongdoer on schedule, and the silence of heaven is read as approval. The Book confronts that reading in several places. When a people became heedless of warnings, they were given everything they desired, and at the moment they took pride in what they had, the seizure came upon them suddenly (6:44). The disbelievers are told plainly not to think that an extended life is good for them, since the extension only increases what they will answer for (3:178). God's own words in another sūrah describe the mechanism from the inside: the end is delayed for a while, and the planning is flawless (7:183). Prosperity, then, is not a certificate of standing. It is an interval, and intervals end.

The error also has a social dimension. A family that prospers while breaking its word teaches its neighbours a lesson about what pays, and the Book's prophets argue against the inference rather than against the wealth, which is why so many of these exchanges end with a reminder about what the Hereafter holds.

**A Verdict Reached Without Evidence**

The Arabic idiom behind the exclamation is that they "judge badly" — the verse is accusing them of a failure of reasoning, not only of morality. This is precise. A judgment is supposed to rest on evidence heard in advance, and the evildoer has heard nothing; he has taken his comfort as testimony, his longevity as an argument, and his own preference as the law of the case. Against that, the Book's method is to put the evidence on the table and let the hearer weigh it. This verse does the same thing by implication: name the verdict they reached, then declare the verdict wrong, leaving the reader to notice that no proof was offered for it. Confidence based on nothing is not a small fault in the Book's scale of values; it is the fault that precedes every other, because it removes the motive to examine anything, including oneself.

**The Term That Cannot Be Advanced**

The interval the wrongdoer mistakes for immunity is not indefinite, and the Book states its rules with unusual firmness. Every community has an appointed term, and when it arrives no one can delay it by a moment or bring it forward (7:34). Death does not wait for anyone's business to be concluded: God never delays a soul when its appointed time arrives, and He is aware of everything people do (63:11). A person's whole calculation rests on a period he did not set and cannot extend. The verse's confidence, then, is not the confidence of a spectator who expects a future punishment; it is the confidence of someone who has read the schedule. The evildoer's escape would require the machinery to fail at exactly the point he needs it to fail, and the Book shows no such point anywhere in the system.

**What Kind of Judgement They Are Making**

The Book treats the verdict of 29:4 as an act of judging, and it has a specific vocabulary for rulings made without reference to revelation. Sūrah al-Māʾidah asks whether it is the judgment of ignorance they seek, and then asks who could be a better judge than God for people of sure faith (5:50). The evildoer of this verse is doing exactly that: he rules on his own case in his own favour, on the strength of his comfort, and he does it without a hearing. The Book's objection is not merely that he reached the wrong verdict; it is that he assumed jurisdiction he does not hold. What follows in the chapter is a description of judgment on terms he did not set.

The phrase also exposes the disorder in the evildoer's reasoning. He has taken a delay in the proceedings as a verdict in his favour, and the rebuttal is not an argument about evidence but a statement about who holds the authority to decide.

**Being Waited Out, Not Pursued**

There is a calm under this verse that is easy to miss, given how sharp its words are. God is not depicted as chasing the wrongdoer across the earth; the wrongdoer is depicted as running while the ground stays exactly where it was. The Book's patience is not weakness or delay in the ordinary sense, since the same text says the delay is deliberate and the planning is without flaw (7:183). What the verse says, in effect, is that no one is being chased, because no one is getting away. Judgment in this chapter will later take the shape of a reckoning that nobody evades by being quick. For now the sentence stands as a warning to the person who has mistaken the quiet of his life for a settlement, and as a warning to anyone who reads the verse and recognizes the reasoning as his own.

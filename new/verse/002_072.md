## Sūrah al-Baqarah 2:72

> ˹This is˺ when a man was killed and you disputed who the killer was, but Allah revealed what you concealed.

**Expanded Commentary**

**The Crime Behind the Command**

The sūrah now discloses the reason the animal was required. A man had been killed, and the community had fallen into a dispute over who was responsible, with accusations passing between the parties. The Book states the situation in one sentence, and it names the community's own part in it: what they were concealing was brought out into the open.

The revelation of this background changes the shape of the preceding passage. The four questions about the cow were being asked by a community in the middle of a dispute about a death, and the members who pressed for detail were the same people who had something to hide. The narrative's full force comes from that arrangement, and the reader gets it by reading the verses in sequence.

**A Dispute That Obscured the Truth**

The Arabic describes the community turning the matter over among themselves, so that what began as a killing became a contest of claims. The Book elsewhere shows this community handling public matters in the same way, and the sūrah's account of the calf, the gate and the food has prepared the reader for a people whose disagreements produce complications rather than resolutions.

The sentence's key phrase is the one that follows: Allah revealed what you were concealing. The Book does not say that the dispute was resolved by argument or by the weight of evidence. It says the hidden matter was brought into view, which places the resolution outside the community's control and outside its competence.

**Concealment as the Book's Recurring Charge**

The charge of hiding appears throughout the sūrah's address to this people. They are told not to hide the truth knowingly, and the Book's account of their scholars records a covenant taken from them to make the scripture known and not to conceal it. A community with a book is in a position to hide things, and the Book's treatment of these people keeps returning to that possibility.

The matter concealed in this verse is not a text but an act. The Book's usage of the same vocabulary in both cases is consistent with its understanding of concealment: what is hidden is not necessarily hidden from God, and a community that controls the account of events can suppress them for a long time. The verse states that the suppression failed.

**The Demonstration Announced in Advance**

The Book gives the reader the fact of the killing before it describes the ritual that exposed it. What the community did with the animal's body is reported in the following verse, and the arrangement of the two verses means the reader knows what the search for the cow was for before he is told what it produced.

That order is part of the sūrah's method. It has already told the reader about the covenant before narrating the failures against it, and it has stated the purpose of the wilderness provisions before describing the complaints about them. The Book presents causes and outcomes in the order it chooses, and the placement of this verse makes the ritual intelligible rather than mysterious.

**Death as the Point of Contact Between Dispute and Revelation**

The murder in this verse produced a question the community could not answer, and the command it had been avoiding produced the answer. The Book's narratives frequently set up this kind of exchange: a human problem that no amount of human deliberation can settle, met by a demonstration from the giver of life. The sūrah's later verses contain the same pattern on a larger scale, with a community asking a prophet for a sign and receiving one.

What the sentence states about God is the knowledge that makes the exchange possible. A matter concealed by a group of people is known where nothing is concealed, and the Book's addresses to this community repeatedly rest on that fact. The verse does not present the killing as the greatest of their crimes, but it presents it as the one they could not keep hidden.

**A Community Under Its Own History**

The verse describes an event involving the ancestors of the people the sūrah is addressing, and the Book reports it as their own record. The murder, the dispute and the exposure belong to the history of the community being invited to accept a confirming messenger, and the sūrah's address treats that history as material its hearers possess.

The reader of the passage is therefore watching two things at once. He sees a community negotiating a command it did not want to fulfil, and he sees the reason the command was given. The two are joined by this sentence, which supplies the crime the community was living with while it asked its questions about the colour of an animal.

**The Concealment That Failed**

A community that manages information about itself can manage a great deal, and the Book's addresses to this people return again and again to the management of what is known. The killing in this verse had been converted into a dispute, which is a way of making an event unreadable; the participants press claims against one another and the fact of the murder disappears behind the argument. The sentence states what became of that arrangement: the fact was produced.

The Book's treatment of secrecy rests on the same premise everywhere. It tells the Prophet that those who conceal what is in their hearts cannot hide it from God, and it describes the day on which every hidden thing is examined. The verse in this passage applies the premise to a single case, and it treats the exposure as inevitable rather than as an occasion of divine intervention. What the community concealed was known before it was revealed.

## Sūrah al-Nisāʾ 4:120

> Satan only makes them ˹false˺ promises and deludes them with ˹empty˺ hopes. Truly Satan promises them nothing but delusion.

### Expanded Commentary

**Promises without substance**

Satan makes promises and generates hopes, but the verse calls them delusion. The promise may be immediate pleasure, guaranteed status, effortless repentance, or the belief that consequences apply only to others. Its common feature is that it gives the desire a future without requiring the person to face reality.

This does not mean hope itself is false. Hope in Allah motivates repentance and action; Satanic hope asks a person to wait, rationalize, and consume. The believer distinguishes the two by whether hope produces obedience and repair or excuses delay.

The verse is a psychological warning as much as a theological one. Repeated self-talk can make a destructive path feel inevitable or harmless. Counter it with truthful remembrance, good companions, and concrete habits that make obedience easier than the fantasy of obedience.

**Hope that produces action**

A promise is suspect when it invites a person to postpone every repair while assuring him that consequences will never arrive. Qur’anic hope is different: it directs the person toward forgiveness, truthful effort, and a new pattern. Test hope by its fruit. Does it make obedience more possible, or merely make delay more comfortable?

## Sūrah al-Anʿām 6:131

> This ˹sending of the messengers˺ is because your Lord would never destroy a society for their wrongdoing while its people are unaware ˹of the truth˺.

**Expanded Commentary**

**The Reason for the Sending of the Messengers**

This is because your Lord would never destroy a society for their wrongdoing while its people are unaware of the truth. The verse states the principle that stands behind the address to the assembled jinn and humans, and it explains why the warning was delivered before the reckoning. The sending of the messengers is presented as the arrangement by which the judgement is made just, and the verse attributes the arrangement to the Lord's dealings with the societies He has addressed.

The Book states the same principle in several places. The sūrah of the Night Journey says that God would never punish a people until He has sent a messenger to warn them (17:15), the sūrah of the Stories says that the Lord would never destroy a society until He has sent a messenger to its capital reciting His revelations (28:59), and the sūrah of the Poets declares that God has never destroyed a society without warners (26:208). The verse in front of us gives the same assurance in the context of the reckoning, where the confession of the assembled people has just been recorded.

**The Warning Preceding the Judgement**

The Book's accounts of the destroyed communities consistently attach the destruction to a warning that was rejected. The sūrah of the Cattle itself has described the fate of the communities that denied their messengers and the succession of the generations, and it has stated that God does not destroy a society unjustly. The verse in front of us gathers that teaching into a single sentence about the treatment of the people who are addressed.

The principle also answers the objection that the verse's own context raises. A community that was never told cannot be judged on the basis of what it was never told, and the Book's answer is that no community has lacked a warner. The address to the jinn and humans in the previous verse is the application of the principle, and the sentence in front of us states the reason the address was given.

**What the Verse Says About Ignorance**

The Book's word for the unawareness of the people describes the condition of those who have not been reminded, and the sūrah has used it for the people who turn away from the signs. The verse's point is that the warning removes the condition, and the Book's messengers are sent precisely so that the people who receive them can no longer plead that they did not know.

The Book's statements about responsibility elsewhere rest on the same arrangement. The sūrah of the Women says that messengers were sent with good news and warnings so that humanity should have no excuse before God after their coming (4:165), and the sūrah of Fāṭir states that there is no community without a warner (35:24). The verse in front of us states the purpose those verses describe, and it states it as a feature of the Lord's justice rather than as a record of the particular missions.

**The Justice Implied in the Sentence**

The sentence presents the destruction of a society as something that could not be charged to God without the warning that preceded it, and the Book's arguments for the justice of the judgement rest on that foundation. The sūrah has described the destruction of the earlier communities in its accounts of the generations, and it has stated that each was given its messenger and its term.

The Book's treatment of the communities that were destroyed is presented in the same terms. The sūrah of the Prophets describes the destruction of the towns whose people were wrongdoers, and the sūrah of the Cattle states that God does not destroy a society while its people are unaware. The verse in front of us supplies the principle in a form that applies both to the past and to the reckoning described in the surrounding verses.

**The Place of the Verse in the Sūrah's Argument**

The verse follows the account of the gathered assemblies and their confession, and it precedes the statement that each person will be assigned ranks according to what he has done (6:132). The three verses together present the judgement as a sequence: the warning, the acknowledgement, and the assignment of ranks. The sentence in front of us explains the first stage, and the verses that follow state the other two.

The sūrah's larger argument also benefits from the statement. The audience in Makkah had received the messenger, which means that the condition described in the verse was fulfilled for them, and the sūrah's warnings about the fate of the earlier communities were addressed to people who could no longer claim ignorance of the message.

**The Transition to the Next Verse**

The next verse states that each person will be assigned ranks according to his deeds and that the Lord is not unaware of what they do (6:132). The verse completes the account of the reckoning with the statement of the degrees that distinguish one person's outcome from another, and it attaches the knowledge of God to the assignment.

The passage that follows tells the Prophet that his Lord is the Self-Sufficient, Full of Mercy, and able to replace a people with another if He wills (6:133). The warning, the ranks, and the sufficiency of God together give the sūrah's audience the full measure of their position, and the verse in front of us supplies the assurance that the reckoning will be conducted on the basis of what was delivered.

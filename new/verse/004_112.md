## Sūrah al-Nisāʾ 4:112

> And whoever commits an evil or sinful deed then blames it on an innocent person, they will definitely bear the guilt of slander and blatant sin.

### Expanded Commentary

**Slander adds a second crime**

A person who commits a wrong and blames an innocent person bears the guilt of the original evil’s false accusation as well as the sin itself. The verse identifies slander as blatant because it seeks to transfer not only a burden but the public reputation attached to it. The innocent person may lose property, trust, employment, or safety while the real offender appears respectable.

**The social mechanics of scapegoating**

Scapegoating often succeeds when the accused is weak, foreign, female, poor, or absent. The verse therefore stands with those who cannot easily defend themselves. Before repeating an allegation, ask who benefits, what evidence exists, and whether the accused has a genuine opportunity to respond.

Repentance requires public correction proportionate to public harm. A private apology cannot fully repair a lie broadcast to a community. Allah’s law protects truth by making the liar responsible for the entire chain of injury he set in motion.

**The damage of scapegoating**

False blame often appears when an institution wants to preserve its reputation. The Qur’anic response is to investigate who acted, who benefited, and who was made vulnerable by the lie. Protecting an innocent person may require correcting a respected figure publicly, because quiet internal knowledge does not undo public accusation.

## Sūrah al-Nisāʾ 4:112

> And whoever commits an evil or sinful deed then blames it on an innocent person, they will definitely bear the guilt of slander and blatant sin.

**Expanded Commentary**

**Whoever Earns an Evil or Sin Then Throws It on the Innocent**

The verse states *wa man yaksib khaṭīʾatan aw ithman thumma yarmi bihī barīʾan* — and whoever earns an error, a mistake, or a sin — *khaṭīʾah* as unintentional or lesser fault, *ithm* as sin — then throws it, casts it upon the innocent, the clear, the free. *Yaksib* — to earn — continues the ledger language: sin is earned only upon the self (4:111), not transferable by advocacy (4:109). *Thumma yarmi bihī* — then throws it with it, casts — was explained as adding projection to earning: having earned slander’s occasion, he throws the blame, as the deceitful threw the stolen armour’s guilt onto the Jewish neighbour and plotted by night speech displeasing to Him (4:105–108). *Barīʾ* — innocent, free, clear — was read as one free of the act, as the Jew was, as any person is until shown otherwise by what God shows the judge (4:105). The sūrah has warned against devouring wealth unjustly (4:29) and against calling a bearer of peace “you are no believer” for gain (4:94); to throw sin on the innocent is the judicial equivalent — to devour honour as wealth. The disjunction *khaṭīʾatan aw ithman* — error or sin — was read as covering both degrees: whether the original fault was smaller error or grave sin, throwing makes it heavier, as the verse closes.

**They Bear Slander and Blatant Sin**

The recompense *fa qad iḥtamala buhtānan wa ithman mubīnā* — they will definitely bear, have carried, slander and blatant, manifest sin — was explained with two loads: *buhtān* — slander, false accusation, calumny — and *ithm mubīn* — clear, manifest sin. *Iḥtamala* — to bear, to carry on the back — was read as carrying as a burden, the opposite of the ranks borne as honour (4:69) and humiliating punishment prepared for disbelievers (4:102). *Buhtān* was explained as the lie that begets bewilderment — *bahata* — to confound with false charge; the sūrah has shown that the deceitful hide from people but cannot hide from the Encompassing (4:108), so the slander confounds people for a night but not Him. *Ithm mubīn* — blatant, manifest sin, sin whose clarity is open — was read as that throwing does not make sin less manifest; it makes the sinner’s *ithm* manifest as *buhtān* plus original, doubly clear to the All-Knowing. The doubling was stressed: before throwing he bore *khaṭīʾah* or *ithm* upon himself (4:111); after throwing he bears that plus *buhtān* plus another *ithm mubīn* — a multiplication of burden unlike God’s multiplication of good many times over (4:40). Early reports noted this verse completed the theft case: the Muslim thief’s clan sought to make the Jew bear the theft; the verse says the thrower bears slander and manifest sin, not the innocent.

**A Verse That Protects the Innocent Whom Advocacy Would Harm**

Placed after *whoever earns sin earns only upon himself* (4:111) and before *had not God’s grace you would have been deceived yet they only deceive themselves* (4:113), this verse protects the innocent whom advocacy would harm. The sūrah has shown that advocacy for the deceitful in this life would have succeeded for a moment had God’s grace not been upon the Prophet ﷺ to show him evidence, yet advocates advocated (4:109); the innocent — the Jew where armour was hidden — would have borne punishment for another’s earning. The verse generalises beyond that case: any *barīʾ* — free — upon whom a *khaṭīʾah* or *ithm* is thrown is not made bearer; the thrower is. The sūrah elsewhere commands to stand firm for justice even against yourselves or kin (4:135) and declares no good in most secret talks except charity, kindness, reconciliation (4:114); throwing sin in secret night speech is the evil secret talk that the Book in truth (4:105) exposes. For the weak this is the lightest protection: where you are innocent and power seeks to make you bearer, the One who is All-Knowing, All-Wise has already declared you not bearer.

**From Night Plotting to Double Burden**

The sequence was read as moral causality: night plotting displeasing to Him while He is with them (4:108) → human advocacy in this life attempting to sustain it (4:109) → earning that stays on self (4:111) → throwing that adds slander and blatant sin. The *thumma* — then — marks not time only but increase: the second burden is added to the first, so that hiding from people (4:108) and disputing before a human judge (4:109) does not reduce but increases the load where the innocent is targeted. The sūrah has paired *Ghafūr Raḥīm* for seeker of forgiveness (4:110) — covering and mercy — and pairs here *buhtān* and *ithm mubīn* — exposure and manifest sin — for thrower who does not seek. The contrast was linked to *“whoever wrongs themselves then seeks finds”* (4:110): the thrower could still seek and find, but throwing while persisting keeps the slander as carried. The verse was also linked to *“those who conceal testimony wrong the heart”* (2:283) and to *“do not let hatred of people cause you to deviate from justice”* (5:8), showing that throwing sin inherits both false speech and injustice.

**A Short Verse That Anchors the Judge’s Caution**

Though only *“whoever earns error or sin then blames the innocent bears slander and blatant sin,”* the verse anchors the judge’s caution after the Book-sent-to-judge verse (4:105). The judge is told to judge by what God shows, not by eloquence, because throwing is eloquent and seems clear for a night; the verse says the thrower’s burden becomes more manifest thereby, not less. The sūrah that shortened and guarded prayer (4:101–102) and fixed it at appointed writ (4:103) here guards the court as it guards prayer: take vigilance even when rain excuses arms, seek forgiveness between prohibitions to advocate, verify reports (4:83), be sure before fighting (4:94), and know that the innocent remains *barīʾ* under the Encompassing (4:108). The next verse will show how near the Prophet ﷺ came to being deceived — and how grace and teaching prevented it, because great is God’s favour upon him (4:113).

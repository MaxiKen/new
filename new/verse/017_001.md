## Sūrah al-Isrāʾ 17:1

> **Glory be to the One Who took His servant ˹Muḥammad˺ by night from the Sacred Mosque to the Farthest Mosque whose surroundings We have blessed, so that We may show him some of Our signs. Indeed, He alone is the All-Hearing, All-Seeing.**

**Expanded Commentary**

**Opening With Glorification**

Subḥāna al-ladhī asrā bi-ʿabdihi laylan — glory be to the One who took His servant by night. The sūrah opens with subḥāna, glorification, declaring Allah free of any imperfection, far above what is ascribed. This opening is unique: only this sūrah begins with subḥāna as a standalone glorification of an act. Al-Ṭabarī explains that subḥāna here is to declare Allah's transcendence and power in performing the Night Journey, al-isrā', which is beyond ordinary human capability. The act described is asrā bi-ʿabdihi, He took His servant by night, from a-s-r, to travel by night. The servant, ʿabdihi, is Muḥammad ﷺ, called servant to emphasize humility and honor: highest honor is to be called servant of Allah, as in 18:1 and 25:1. Laylan, by night, emphasizes that journey occurred at night, in darkness, yet with divine light, and that it was accomplished in a portion of night, not over months. The addition ˹Muḥammad˺ in brackets clarifies who servant is.

**From Sacred Mosque To Farthest Mosque**

Min al-masjidi al-ḥarāmi ilā al-masjidi al-aqṣā — from the Sacred Mosque to the Farthest Mosque. Al-masjid al-ḥarām is the Sacred Mosque in Makkah containing Kaʿbah. Al-masjid al-aqṣā is the Farthest Mosque in Jerusalem, site of ancient Temple. Al-Ṭabarī reports that Prophet ﷺ was taken from Sacred Mosque itself, not just from Makkah generally, as hadith describes him being roused in ḥaram near Kaʿbah. Al-aqṣā means farthest, indicating distance from Makkah and also farthest in sanctity visited by Prophet during his lifetime. At time of revelation, al-masjid al-aqṣā referred to Temple Mount area in Jerusalem, which had no standing mosque building as today but was sacred precinct. The journey from Makkah to Jerusalem is about 1,200 km, which would ordinarily take over a month by camel, yet was accomplished in part of night, demonstrating divine power. The phrase alladhī bāraknā ḥawlahu — whose surroundings We blessed — refers to Jerusalem and its environs, blessed with fertility and with presence of many prophets, as 7:137 and 21:71 mention blessed land.

**So That We May Show Him Some Of Our Signs**

Li-nuriyahu min āyātinā — so that We may show him some of Our signs. Li- indicates purpose: journey was to show servant some of Allah's signs. Āyātinā, Our signs, includes miraculous journey itself, visions of heavens during ascension, encounters with previous prophets, and divine presence. Al-Qurṭubī says signs include both horizontal signs, the blessed land of Jerusalem, and vertical signs, the heavens ascended in miʿrāj, which is alluded to in 53:1-18. The purpose clause shows that Night Journey was not for Allah's need but for servant's honor and education, to show him signs that would strengthen heart after Year of Sorrow. The Prophet ﷺ had lost Khadījah and Abū Ṭālib, faced intense persecution, and was shown that his mission is connected to all previous prophets, whose center was Jerusalem. The journey therefore re-centers prophetic tradition around him.

**Indeed He Alone Is All-Hearing, All-Seeing**

Innahu huwa al-samīʿu al-baṣīr — indeed He is All-Hearing, All-Seeing. Innahu refers to Allah. Huwa for emphasis, He alone. Samīʿ, All-Hearing, hears all, including Prophet's supplications and Quraysh's mockery. Baṣīr, All-Seeing, sees all, including Prophet's sorrow and enemies' plotting. Al-Rāzī explains that seal with hearing and seeing is appropriate after journey that was in response to Prophet's situation: Allah heard and saw his sorrow and honored him. It also indicates that Allah heard and saw those who denied Night Journey and will hold accountable. The two names also prepare for next verses about Children of Israel and their corruption, which Allah heard and saw.

**Why Night Journey Matters For This Sūrah**

Sūrah al-Isrā' is also called Sūrat Banī Isrā'īl because verses 2-8 discuss Children of Israel's two corruptions and punishments. Placing Night Journey before that history is deliberate: Prophet's journey to Jerusalem, city of Israelite prophets, shows that prophetic inheritance has passed to him. Jerusalem was first qiblah, direction of prayer, for Muslims, before change to Makkah in 2:144. The journey therefore links Makkah and Jerusalem, showing continuity and transfer. Al-Qurṭubī notes that taking servant by night from Sacred Mosque to Farthest Mosque indicates that both mosques are sacred and connected, and that blessings of Jerusalem's surroundings are linked to Makkah's sanctity. The journey also demonstrates that Allah's power is not limited by distance or time: He can take servant by night across vast distance, just as He can show signs, punish corrupt, and guide to most upright as verse 9 will say.

**The Historical Context Of Year Of Sorrow**

Most authorities date isrā' to around 619 CE, Year of Sorrow, ʿĀm al-Ḥuzn, when Prophet lost wife Khadījah and uncle Abū Ṭālib. After their loss, persecution intensified, and Prophet sought support in Ṭā'if but was rejected and stoned. The Night Journey came after these losses as divine consolation and elevation. Al-Ṭabarī and Ibn Kathīr report that journey was physical, bodily, not only spiritual, though some early authorities said it was spiritual. Mainstream holds it was bodily, with Burāq as mount, Jibrīl as guide, and that Prophet returned same night, with door still swinging. The event is foundational for Islamic spirituality and is commemorated as Laylat al-Isrā' wa-l-Miʿrāj. A well-known hadith says prayer is ascension of believers, linking daily prayers to this journey.

**Linguistic Note On Subḥāna And Asrā**

Subḥāna from s-b-ḥ means to be free, far removed, to swim away from defect, from same root as tasbīḥ, glorification. Asrā from a-s-r means to travel by night, form IV, to take by night. ʿAbd from ʿ-b-d means servant, slave, worshipper. Laylan, by night, accusative indicating time and emphasis that journey accomplished in night. Masjid from s-j-d, place of prostration. Ḥarām from ḥ-r-m, sacred, forbidden. Aqṣā from q-ṣ-w, farthest. Bāraknā from b-r-k, to bless, increase. Ḥawlahu, around it, surroundings. Li-nuriyahu, to show him, from r-ʾ-y, to see. Āyāt from a-y-y, signs. Samīʿ from s-m-ʿ, hearing, Baṣīr from b-ṣ-r, seeing. The verse's structure — glorification, act of taking servant by night, from Sacred to Farthest Mosque whose surroundings blessed, purpose to show signs, seal with All-Hearing All-Seeing — moves from transcendence to act to places to purpose to divine attributes, creating opening that is both awe-inspiring and purposeful, setting stage for history of Children of Israel and moral code that follows.

## Sūrah al-Aʿrāf 7:73

> And to the people of Thamûd We sent their brother Ṣâliḥ. He said, “O my people! Worship Allah—you have no other god except Him. A clear proof has come to you from your Lord: this is Allah’s she-camel as a sign to you. So leave her to graze ˹freely˺ on Allah’s land and do not harm her, or else you will be overcome by a painful punishment.

**Expanded Commentary**

**The Third People and Their Messenger**

The verse begins the third of the sūrah's punishment accounts, and it names the people of Thamūd and their messenger Ṣāliḥ. The Book tells the story of this people in several passages, and the accounts run through the sūrah of Hūd (11:61–68), the sūrah of the Poets (26:141–158), the sūrah of the Moon (54:23–31), and the sūrah of the Rocky Tract, where the inhabitants of al-Ḥijr are described (15:80–83). The verse in front of us introduces them in the same form that the two previous accounts used.

The tradition places the people of Thamūd in the stone country of northwestern Arabia, and the sūrah of the Dawn mentions their carving of the rocks in the valley (89:9). The audience of the sūrah knew their remains, and the Book's narratives rely on that knowledge rather than on descriptions of the sites. The verse in front of us states the message and the sign, and the account that follows states the response.

**The Call to Worship God Alone**

The message of Ṣāliḥ is the message of Noah and Hūd, and the verse repeats the words that the sūrah's two earlier accounts have already given twice: worship God, for you have no god besides Him. The sūrah of Hūd reports the same call in its version of the narrative (11:61), and the sūrah of the Poets opens the Thamūd account with the question of whether the people will fear God (26:142). The verse in front of us supplies the third instance of the call, and the repetition states the point of the series.

The Book presents the unity of the message as the mark of the messengers, and the sūrah of the Prophets states that every messenger before the Prophet received the revelation that there is no god but God (21:25). The call in front of us is placed in the mouth of a messenger of a people in the stone valleys of the north, and the identity of the message with the message of Makkah is the sūrah's argument.

**The Clear Proof**

The verse states that a clear proof has come to the people from their Lord, and the proof is then identified with the she-camel that accompanies the messenger. The Book uses the phrase for the signs given to the prophets in several passages, and the sūrah's accounts of Moses and of the Prophet refer to the clear proofs that were delivered. This verse states that the people of Thamūd were given such a demonstration, and the Book's other accounts of this people name the she-camel as the sign.

The sūrah of the Poets reports the messenger's words about the camel and her turn to drink on an appointed day, together with his warning against touching her with harm (26:155–156), and the sūrah of the Moon states that the she-camel was sent as a test and that the people were told of the water dividing between her and them (54:27–28). The sūrah of the Sun states that the messenger of God warned them about the she-camel and that they denied and hamstrung her (91:13–14). The verse here supplies the first statement of the sign in this sūrah.

**The Order to Let Her Graze**

The verse orders the people to leave the she-camel to graze on the earth of God and forbids them to harm her, and the details in the other accounts state the arrangement more fully. The sūrah of the Poets reports the messenger saying that the camel has a share of the water and that the people have a share on a day known to them, and the sūrah of the Moon states that the water was to be divided between them and that the people were warned not to touch her with harm. The present verse states the command and the warning, and the narrative that follows records what the people did.

The phrase about the earth of God states whose the pasture is, and the Book's argument against the people's claim to their own arrangements runs through the narratives of the sūrah. The camel was to live among them as a sign whose maintenance cost them a share of their water. The verse under discussion states the terms of the test, and the sūrah's account of the outcome shows what the people did with them.

**The Warning of Punishment**

The verse closes with the warning that a painful punishment will seize the people if they harm the she-camel, and the Book repeats the warning in each of its accounts of Thamūd. The sūrah of Hūd reports the messenger saying that a punishment will seize them for a fixed term and that the torment is near, and the sūrah of the Sun states that the people were warned before they killed the camel. Here the verse states the warning in the mouth of Ṣāliḥ, and the verses that follow report the response of the community and the arrival of what he warned about.

The Book's treatment of the sign as a trust that the community was to keep appears in the same accounts, and the killing of the camel is presented as the deliberate choice that ended the term. This verse supplies the terms on which the people were tested, and the sūrah's later verses state how they answered.

**Where the Verse Sits**

The verse stands seventy-third in the chapter, and it begins the account of Thamūd immediately after the end of the account of ʿĀd. The verse that follows reports the messenger's reminder of the favours his people had received, and the account then proceeds through the exchange with the leaders to the killing of the camel and the destruction (7:74–78). The verse here supplies the opening of the third narrative in the series.

The reader who has seen two communities destroyed now meets the third with the same message and with an additional proof. The call is the worship of the one God, the sign is the she-camel, and the command is to leave her in peace; and the sūrah's next verse states the reminder with which the messenger addressed his people.

**The Transition to the Next Verse**

The next verse reminds the people that God made them successors after ʿĀd, settled them in the land, and gave them the buildings and the carved dwellings, and it commands them to remember the favours of God and to refrain from spreading corruption (7:74). The sign in front of us is followed by the reminder of what the people had received, and the account then turns to the leaders' answer.

The two verses together state the messenger's approach to the third community. He calls them to the worship of God alone, he presents the sign, and he reminds them of the favours that surrounded them; and the sūrah's following verses record the refusal of the arrogant and the killing of the camel.

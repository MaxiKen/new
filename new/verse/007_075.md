## Sūrah al-Aʿrāf 7:75

> The arrogant chiefs of his people asked the lowly who believed among them, “Are you certain that Ṣâliḥ has been sent by his Lord?” They replied, “We certainly believe in what he has been sent with.”

**Expanded Commentary**

**The Arrogant and the Weak**

The verse reports the exchange within the third community, and it names the two groups on either side of it: the leaders who were arrogant and the believers among the people whom the leaders deemed weak. The Book's accounts of the communities consistently place the refusal with the powerful and the acceptance with the poor, and the sūrah of Sabaʾ states that whenever a warner was sent to a society, its elite said that they rejected what he had been sent with (34:34). The verse in front of us identifies both groups in a single sentence.

The term for the believers in front of us describes them as those whom the leaders counted weak, and the expression states the leaders' judgment rather than a fact about the believers. The sūrah of the Poets reports the people of Noah asking how they could believe in the messenger when only the lowest among them followed him (26:111), and the sūrah of Hūd reports the chiefs of Noah's people making the same argument about the followers who had come to the messenger. The verse in front of us states the social division that the sūrah's narratives repeat.

**The Question Put to the Believers**

The leaders ask the believers whether they know that Ṣāliḥ has been sent by his Lord, and the form of the question states its purpose. The question is not an inquiry after evidence but a challenge to the believers' judgment, and the phrasing places the messenger's standing rather than his message before the people who have accepted him. The sūrah of the Ant reports the same division in its account of Thamūd, stating that the people split into two groups disputing with one another (27:45). The verse in front of us supplies the exchange in the sūrah's own account.

The Book's descriptions of the elite's questions to the believers use the same form in other passages, and the sūrah of the Ornament reports the disbelievers asking whether the message had been sent to the messenger rather than to their own great men. The question in front of us belongs to that family of challenges, and the answer that follows shows that the believers did not accept the terms on which it was put.

**The Believers' Answer**

The believers answer that they believe in that with which the messenger has been sent, and the answer states their faith in the message without engaging the question about the messenger's standing. The sūrah's account of the two groups in the previous narratives shows the same simplicity in the responses of those who accepted the prophets, and the Book's descriptions of the believers' confessions state the content of the faith in similar words. This verse supplies the confession of the third community's believers.

The answer identifies the faith as belief in what was sent rather than in the man who brought it, and the phrasing matches the argument of the sūrah's earlier replies about the office of the messenger. The Book's teaching that the messengers are to be believed because of what they carry appears in its accounts of the communities, and the believers in front of us state the position in the fewest possible words.

**The Reversal of Standing**

The verse's description of the believers as weak is answered by the sūrah's later account of the sign, where the weak are the ones who accepted the clear proof and the powerful are the ones who killed it. The Book's description of the Hereafter uses the same reversal, and the sūrah's passage on the Heights reports its people turning to the arrogant and asking whether the ones they had sworn would receive no mercy were not the very people who had entered the garden (7:49). The verse here states the world's estimate of the two groups, and the sūrah's narrative supplies the divine one.

The Book's accounts of the peoples commonly state the numbers and the standing of the believers as small, and the sūrah of Hūd reports the chiefs dismissing Noah's followers on account of their poverty (11:27). The present verse states the same dismissal in the words of the leaders of Thamūd, and the account that follows shows what the two groups did with the sign that had been given them.

**The Two Groups in the Sūrah's Narratives**

The verse completes the sūrah's picture of the communities by naming the division inside them, and the picture has been building through the two earlier accounts. The leaders answer for the people, the messengers address the leaders and the people together, and a body of believers accepts the message and is saved. The verse under discussion supplies the third instance of the division, and the sūrah's following narratives state the outcome for each group.

The Book's treatment of the believers as a remnant that is rescued appears in its accounts of the destroyed peoples throughout, and the sūrah's accounts of Lot and Shuʿayb will state the division in similar terms. Here the verse supplies the exchange that reveals the two sides, and the account then proceeds to the act that decided the community's fate.

**Where the Verse Sits**

The verse stands seventy-fifth in the chapter, and it follows the reminder of the favours and precedes the leaders' answer to the believers. The verse that follows states the leaders' declaration that they do not believe in what the believers have accepted (7:76), and the account then reports the killing of the she-camel and the destruction of the community (7:77–78). This verse supplies the exchange inside the third community.

The reader who has heard the messenger address his people now sees the people divided. The leaders question the believers about the messenger's commission, the believers answer with the content of their faith, and the leaders' answer follows; and the sūrah's next verses state the refusal in its final form and the deed that followed it.

**The Transition to the Next Verse**

The next verse reports that the arrogant said they did not believe in what the believers believed (7:76). The believers' confession in front of us is followed by the leaders' formal rejection, and the passage then records the hamstringing of the she-camel and the demand that the messenger produce the punishment he had warned about.

The two verses together state the positions of the two groups. The believers accept what has been sent, the leaders reject it in a single sentence, and the account moves to the act that sealed the community's fate; and the sūrah's following verses report the sign's destruction, the demand, and the end of the people.

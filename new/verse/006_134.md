## Sūrah al-Anʿām 6:134

> Indeed, what you have been promised will certainly come to pass. And you will have no escape.

**Expanded Commentary**

**The Certainty of What Was Promised**

Indeed, what you have been promised will certainly come to pass. The verse states the certainty of the outcome that the sūrah has announced throughout, and it addresses the audience directly. What was promised was the judgement, the resurrection, and the reckoning described in the surrounding verses, and the sentence removes the option of treating them as remote possibilities.

The Book's language about the promises of God is consistent: what God has promised comes to pass, and there is no alteration in His words. The sūrah has already declared that none can change the words of God (6:34) and that the word of the Lord has been perfected in truth and justice (6:115). The sentence in this verse applies that guarantee to the warnings the audience had received, and it states them as a fact of the future rather than as a threat contingent on their conduct.

**The Plain Statement of the Future**

The Book's habit in these passages is to state the coming of the judgement without argument, and the sūrah has used the same directness in its earlier warnings. It described the day when the wrongdoers will be gathered and told that the fire is their home (6:128), and it recorded the confession of the assembled jinn and humans when they are asked about the messengers (6:130). The verse in front of us states the same event in a single sentence and leaves the details to the passages around it.

The Book's announcements of the reckoning appear in every part of the Qur'an, and the language of certainty is attached to them throughout. The sūrah of the News says that the day is the ultimate truth and that whoever wills may take the path back to his Lord (78:39), and the sūrah of the Resurrection states that there will be no refuge on that day (75:11). The sentence in front of us belongs to that family of statements.

**You Will Have No Escape**

And you will have no escape. The second sentence names the condition of the people who were addressed, and the Book's word describes the impossibility of finding a place from which the reckoning can be avoided. The Book uses the same idea in its descriptions of the day, and the sūrah's audience, which understood the geography of retreat and refuge, is told that none of the familiar routes will be available.

The claim is connected to the sūrah's account of God's knowledge. The Book has stated that God knows what is on land and sea, that nothing is hidden from Him, and that the record of every person's conduct is kept (6:59–61). The sentence in front of us draws the consequence: what is known cannot be escaped, and the person who is not beyond God's knowledge cannot be beyond His judgement.

**What the Verse Says About the Delay**

The sentence answers the attitude that the sūrah has documented in its audience, which was the assumption that the warnings were addressed to a distant future and could be met at leisure. The Book's answer in other places is that every matter has its set time (6:67), and the sūrah has already stated that the community has a term that cannot be advanced or delayed (7:34; 10:49).

The sentence in this verse does not specify when the promise will be fulfilled. What it states is that the fulfilment is certain and the escape impossible, and the Book leaves the timing with God, as it does in its other announcements of the day. The audience is left with the certainty of the event rather than with a date it could prepare for at the last moment.

**The Verse in the Sequence of the Passage**

The verse follows the statement that the Lord is the Self-Sufficient, Full of Mercy, able to replace a people with another (6:133), and it precedes the instruction to the Prophet to declare to his people that each side will persist in its way (6:135). The three verses form the sūrah's final word to the audience within this section: God has no need of them, what He has promised will come, they will not escape, and the Prophet is to carry on with his task.

The passage that follows turns to the dietary system of the audience and to the shares they assigned to God and to their idols (6:136). The shift of subject does not break the sequence, since the sūrah's case against the audience's religion has been built on both the arguments about the signs and the examination of their practices.

**What the Verse Requires of the Reader**

The verse requires the reader to treat the judgement as settled. The sūrah's audience had asked for signs and had been told that the signs were with God; they had asked for the messengers' office and had been told that God knows where to place His message; and the sentence in front of us removes the last resort, which was the hope that the whole matter could be avoided. The Book's invitation throughout the chapter has been addressed to people who were assumed to be able to respond, and the verse states the outcome of the response they give.

The Book's treatment of the day of judgement consistently pairs the certainty of the event with the freedom of the person who is to meet it. The sūrah of the News states the truth of the day and then states that whoever wills may take the path to his Lord, and the two statements together are the Book's way of presenting the matter.

**The Transition to the Next Verse**

The next verse gives the Prophet the words he is to say to his people: that they should persist in their ways while he persists in his, and that they will soon know who will fare best in the end, since the wrongdoers will never succeed (6:135). The instruction follows the statement in this verse, and together they close the section with the two parties left in their positions and the outcome left with God.

The sūrah has now completed its treatment of the audience's demands, their theology, their practices of deception, and their dismissal of the messengers. What remains in the chapter is the detailed examination of their ritual and dietary system, the closing instruction to the Prophet, and the final testimony that the Book contains. The verse in front of us marks the end of the argumentative section and states its conclusion.

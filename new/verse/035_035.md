## Sūrah Fāṭir 35:35

> ˹He is the One˺ Who—out of His grace—has settled us in the Home of Everlasting Stay, where we will be touched by neither fatigue nor weariness.”

**Expanded Commentary**

**Permanent Settlement Is a Gift of Grace**

The inhabitants of the Garden describe Allah as the One who settled them there by His grace. The word settlement suggests arrival after movement and security after uncertainty. They are not merely allowed to visit a beautiful place. They are established in a home whose permanence is part of the gift. The phrase redirects attention from their deeds to the divine favor that brought those deeds to completion.

This does not make righteous action irrelevant. The preceding verses named recitation, prayer, spending, and hope in an unfailing exchange. Grace is the source that enables the action and the generosity that accepts it. A servant works, but he cannot compel the result. The people of the Garden speak accurately when they attribute their final settlement to Allah's grace rather than to a self-sufficient merit.

**The Home of Everlasting Stay Answers Human Instability**

Every earthly home is vulnerable to loss. Its occupants may be displaced by death, war, poverty, decay, or changes they cannot control. Even a secure house contains the knowledge that security will end. The Garden is called the Home of Everlasting Stay because it removes that instability. Its inhabitants do not wait for the next eviction, illness, or separation.

The promise is especially meaningful for people whose worldly lives were marked by exile, persecution, or uncertainty. Faith may require a person to leave familiar comfort or endure rejection by a community. Allah does not present the final home as a temporary reward after which another trial begins. He offers a place where arrival is complete. The heart can rest because the future contains no threat of losing the place to which it has finally come.

**No Fatigue Means No Hidden Price of Joy**

The verse denies fatigue and weariness. Worldly pleasure often carries a cost: work produces income but exhausts the body, travel brings discovery but demands effort, and social celebration can end in loneliness. The Garden's joy is not purchased by a new burden. Its inhabitants receive beauty, food, companionship, and honor without the exhaustion that limits earthly enjoyment.

The Arabic terms can be read as overlapping forms of weariness, while commentators also distinguish between bodily strain and the toil that produces it. The broad sense is clear in either reading. The Garden contains no labor that drains the person, no effort required to preserve a gift, and no inner tiredness that makes joy difficult to feel. The promise addresses both body and spirit.

**Rest Is Not Boredom**

An eternal home without fatigue should not be imagined as empty inactivity. Fatigue is removed so that activity, conversation, worship, and enjoyment are free from the conditions that make them burdensome. The Garden's permanence allows delight to deepen rather than become stale. A person does not need exhaustion to prove that an experience is meaningful.

This corrects a worldly assumption that only difficulty gives value. Struggle can teach patience and produce growth in this life, but it is not the essence of happiness. In the Hereafter, Allah can give meaning without pain as its price. The inhabitants remain alive, aware, and grateful; they are not reduced to passive bodies. They are freed from the weariness that makes earthly life fragile.

The absence of fatigue also has a spiritual meaning. In this world, even good worship can be accompanied by distraction, weakness, and the need to struggle against the self. That struggle is not a defect in the path; it is part of the test. In the Garden, obedience and delight no longer compete. The heart is fully willing, the body is fully able, and no rival desire pulls the person away from gratitude. Rest is therefore the completion of harmony between the servant and the gift. This promise should not make the believer despise every form of effort now. Effort can purify intention, teach dependence, and protect a person from the illusion that pleasure is the highest good. But it should also prevent despair when worship feels difficult. Allah knows the tired servant who continues, the caregiver who prays while exhausted, and the worker who refuses wrongdoing despite pressure. None of that weariness is lost. The Home of Everlasting Stay is a promise that Allah can transform temporary strain into lasting ease, and that the final life will not demand the sacrifices required by the present one.

The promised rest is therefore not mere inactivity. It is freedom to enjoy every good without the shadow of exhaustion or departure.

Even the memory of effort becomes sweet because Allah has made its result permanent.

**The Promise Teaches Patience in the Present Home**

The believer still feels tired in this world and must fulfill responsibilities. The verse is not a command to neglect work or to despise the needs of the body. It gives the worker a horizon. Fatigue endured for a lawful purpose is not forgotten, and the final home will not require the servant to labor for the right to remain there. Allah's grace turns temporary exhaustion into part of a journey toward lasting rest.

The proper response is therefore balanced. Use the present home responsibly, care for the body, and do not make worldly comfort the measure of divine favor. Some of Allah's beloved servants endure great weariness, while some receive ease as a test. The final promise is the certain measure: Allah can settle the faithful where no tiredness touches them. The praise of the people of the Garden teaches the living to ask for grace, persevere through temporary strain, and trust the Home whose welcome does not end.

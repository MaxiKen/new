## Sūrah al-Tawbah 9:127

> Whenever a sûrah is revealed, they look at one another, ˹saying,˺ “Is anyone watching you?” Then they slip away. ˹It is˺ Allah ˹Who˺ has turned their hearts away because they are a people who do not comprehend.

**Expanded Commentary**

**Their First Concern Is Exposure**

When revelation is recited, the hypocrites look at one another and ask whether anyone is watching. Their concern is not what Allah is saying but whether their reaction has been noticed. This is the psychology of a person who manages appearances while resisting truth.

The question can also be understood as mockery among themselves. Either way, the group is united by avoidance. They monitor the audience instead of examining the message. The believer reverses this priority: he is more concerned with Allah seeing his heart than with people seeing his face.

They leave when the revelation demands attention. Physical departure represents an inward refusal. A person may remain seated while mentally leaving, changing the subject, checking who agrees, or finding a reason to delay obedience. The body can be present and the heart absent.

The verse does not condemn every need to leave a gathering. People have legitimate duties, health needs, and responsibilities. The issue is deliberate escape from guidance because it threatens a preferred identity. The believer distinguishes practical departure from the habit of fleeing correction.

**Public Religion Can Hide Private Avoidance**

The hypocrites are close enough to hear the sūrah, yet proximity does not benefit them. A person can attend religious gatherings, use sacred vocabulary, and still organize his life around avoiding surrender. External association is valuable only when it leads to inward truth.

This warning applies to institutions as well. An organization may display scripture while discouraging anyone from applying it to finances, leadership, or treatment of the weak. The believer asks whether revelation is allowed to examine the community or is kept as decoration. A gathering becomes sound when people can hear correction without immediately searching for an exit.

**Turning Away Can Become a Consequence**

The verse says Allah has turned their hearts away because they do not comprehend. This is not arbitrary misguidance. Their repeated avoidance has formed a condition in which they no longer understand the message as guidance. Refusal of truth can damage the capacity to receive truth.

The servant should therefore take small acts of avoidance seriously. He prays for understanding, stays with a difficult passage, and asks a sincere teacher rather than rushing to mock. Comprehension is a gift, but the heart must not keep closing the door through pride.

The glance between them shows how companions can make avoidance easier. One person wants to leave, another gives the signal, and the group confirms that no one needs to face the revelation. Communities can normalize this through jokes, changing the subject, or praising the person who refuses correction.

Truthful companionship does the opposite. A sincere friend asks what the passage requires and helps the group discuss it without humiliation. He does not expose private weakness for entertainment, but he does not provide a hiding place for public wrongdoing. The quality of the group shapes whether hearing becomes guidance or evasion.

**Comprehension Includes Moral Response**

To comprehend revelation is not merely to understand vocabulary. It is to recognize its authority and allow it to change conduct. A clever person may explain a verse while remaining untouched by it. The believer seeks understanding that produces humility, worship, and justice.

This is why study and obedience belong together. The servant asks questions, checks interpretation, and then acts on what is clear. If he uses complexity to avoid every demand, knowledge has become another form of escape. Allah's guidance is understood most fully when it is lived.



The verse exposes the moment when people choose to slip away, but it also lets hearers choose differently. They can remain, listen, and admit that the sūrah has found them. They can return after leaving and receive the correction they first avoided.

The believer asks Allah not to turn his heart away. He resists the need to know who is watching and remembers that Allah already sees. When the message is difficult, he stays with it long enough for fear and pride to become repentance.

A community can make staying easier by creating a culture in which correction is not a public ambush. People need room to ask questions, admit confusion, and seek explanation. When every mistake is mocked, hypocrites learn to hide and sincere learners stop speaking. When everything is excused, no one takes guidance seriously. The balanced community keeps the revelation central, protects dignity, and expects a response. It helps the heart remain present long enough for understanding to become obedience.

The believer can also notice the first movement toward escape. He feels the urge to check whether others agree before deciding whether a command is true. He laughs at a serious point to prevent anyone from asking about it. He delays an obligation until the emotional pressure has passed. Naming these habits helps him interrupt them. He turns his face back toward the message, asks Allah for comprehension, and accepts that a heart may need repeated returns before it becomes steady.

To stay with revelation is to let Allah's speech remain more important than the audience's approval. That persistence can turn a moment of avoidance into the beginning of comprehension and repentance.

The community can help by making sincere attention honorable. It protects people from mockery while still asking them to respond to the truth. In such a gathering, remaining present is an act of courage, and comprehension can grow into repentance, worship, and a durable change of character.

He remains present before Allah.

with humility.

Attention becomes worship when the servant stays with revelation long enough to let it correct him. He seeks understanding without performance and accepts that humility is the beginning of comprehension.

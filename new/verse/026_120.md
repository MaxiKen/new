## Sūrah al-Shuʿarāʾ 26:120

> Then afterwards We drowned the rest.

**Expanded Commentary**

**The Flood Separates the Saved From the Rejecters**

The verse states that God drowned the rest after saving Noah and those with him. The word rest marks a complete division between the people who followed the warning and those who rejected it. The judgment is not presented as an accident that overtook everyone without distinction. It follows a long call, a clear warning, and a settled refusal.

The brevity of the sentence gives the drowning finality. The people who remained outside the Ark cannot return to the community of safety once God's appointed judgment arrives. Their previous rejection has reached its consequence, while the believers' trust has been confirmed through rescue.

**Drowning Is God's Judgment on Persistent Rejection**

The flood is not described as a natural event detached from morality. God is the agent of the drowning. The people have been warned and have chosen to deny Noah, insult his followers, and threaten the messenger. The judgment therefore belongs to a history of deliberate resistance rather than to a random disaster.

This attribution also prevents the believer from treating the flood as a license for private vengeance. Noah asked God to judge between him and his people. God alone decides the time and form of the judgment. Human beings may resist oppression and seek protection, but they must not assume the authority to declare every opponent part of a divine punishment.

**The Word Rest Exposes the Cost of Exclusion**

The people who demanded that Noah expel the believers are now outside the Ark themselves. Their social ranking has not protected them. Those whom they considered lowly are saved, while the chiefs' judgment has become part of the evidence against them. The word rest reverses their hierarchy: the powerful are not automatically included, and the humble are not automatically left behind.

The division is not based on wealth or public honor. It is based on response to God's warning. A person may possess influence and still be outside the refuge, while a person despised by society may be inside it. The flood makes visible the difference between social exclusion and divine judgment.

Quran 7:64 describes God saving Noah and those with him in the Ark and drowning those who rejected the signs. The parallel confirms that rescue and drowning form one connected judgment. The Ark is not merely a survival device; it is the means by which God's mercy distinguishes the believing community from the rejecting people.

The same account also characterizes the rejecters as blind to the signs. That blindness explains why a clear warning did not produce belief. They had the opportunity to respond, but their settled refusal kept them from recognizing what the event meant until recognition could no longer help.

**The Drowning Does Not Mean the Warning Was Cruel**

A warning can be severe because the danger is severe. Noah's call gave the people time to change, and the Ark displayed the seriousness of the command before the flood reached its end. Mercy was present in the warning, the opportunity, and the offered refuge. Persistent refusal turned the mercy into evidence of their choice.

This balance matters when reading divine judgment. Mercy does not require that consequences be canceled after every rejection. It requires that God not wrong His servants. Noah's people were not deprived of all opportunity. They rejected the messenger and the means of safety, so the drowning completes a just sequence.

Noah and the believers did not defeat the rejecting people through military strength. They were saved by God's command and the Ark He provided. The drowning therefore cannot be celebrated as a human conquest. It is a divine judgment that preserves the vulnerable and ends the power of those who would not stop their rejection.

This protects the believers from arrogance after rescue. They must remember that they were saved because God showed mercy and because they followed His instruction. The flood did not prove that Noah's followers were socially superior. It proved that obedience to God matters more than the rank assigned by people.

Before the judgment, Noah's people could call him false and treat his warning as a threat to their tradition. After the drowning, the message's truth stands beyond their denial. The event does not need their confession to become true. God's action vindicates the messenger and shows that the rejected warning was a mercy they refused.

A messenger's success is not measured only by persuasion. Noah delivered the warning, protected the believers, and entrusted judgment to God. The drowning reveals the consequence of rejection, but Noah's faithfulness was already complete before the water came. The visible outcome confirms the duty he had performed.

**Separation Is Moral Before It Is Physical**

The flood physically separates the Ark's occupants from the rest, but the moral separation began earlier. One group accepted Noah's call, and another protected its idols and social pride. The water reveals a division that the people's choices had already created.

This principle warns against treating proximity to believers as proof of faith. The chiefs could live near Noah and hear his words without joining the obedience that made the Ark a refuge. Conversely, the humble believers were close to safety because their hearts and actions accepted God's guidance. Physical separation is the outward end of a moral direction.

**The End of the Story Is Also a Warning**

The chapter's refrain will call the event a sign and note that most did not believe, but this verse already contains the warning's complete shape. A community may reject a messenger for generations and still reach a moment when the opportunity closes. The time granted to reconsider is mercy, not a guarantee that delay can continue forever.

The wise response is to hear warnings while they can change life. Trust God, follow the guidance He makes clear, and do not judge truth by social power. The drowning of the rest stands as a final reminder that inherited confidence cannot protect a people from the Lord's decision.

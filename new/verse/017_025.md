## Sūrah al-Isrāʾ 17:25

> **Your Lord knows best what is within yourselves. If you are righteous, He is certainly All-Forgiving to those who ˹constantly˺ turn to Him.**

**Expanded Commentary**

**Lord Knows Best What Is Within Yourselves**

Rabbukum aʿlamu bi-mā fī nufūsikum — your Lord knows best what is in your selves, within yourselves. Rabbukum, your Lord, from r-b-b. Aʿlamu, knows best, more knowing, from ʿ-l-m, to know. Bi-mā, with what, about what. Fī nufūsikum, in your selves, within yourselves, in your souls, from n-f-s, self, soul, plural nufūs. Al-Ṭabarī explains that Lord knows best what is within yourselves, what is in your hearts, intentions, regarding parents, whether you intend good, honour, humility, mercy, or otherwise. He knows inner, not only outer speech and action. Al-Rāzī says this indicates that Allah knows inner thoughts about parents, whether sincere honour or grudging, and that He knows best what is within selves generally, not only about parents, but about all intentions.

**If You Are Righteous He Is All-Forgiving To Those Who Constantly Turn**

In takūnū ṣāliḥīna fa-innahu kāna li-l-awwābīna ghafūran — if you are righteous, then He is for those who constantly turn, ever-turning, All-Forgiving. In, if. Takūnū from k-w-n, to be. Ṣāliḥīna from ṣ-l-ḥ, righteous, good, from ṣalaḥa, to be righteous, good, from same root as ṣāliḥ, righteous, and iṣlāḥ, reform. Fa-innahu, then He, then indeed He. Kāna, is, was, indicates He is. Li-l-awwābīna from a-w-b, to turn, return, from āba, to return, and awwāb, one who constantly turns, returns to Allah, ever-returning, from same root as tawbah, repentance, turning. Ghafūran from gh-f-r, All-Forgiving, forgiving, from ghafara, to forgive. Al-Ṭabarī explains that if you are righteous, intending good to parents, honouring them, even if you fall short or have bad thoughts due to human weakness, He is All-Forgiving to those who constantly turn to Him, return to Him in repentance, asking forgiveness. Al-Qurṭubī says awwāb is one who constantly turns to Allah, returns, repents, and that Allah is All-Forgiving to such.

**Why This Verse Follows Honouring Parents**

Verses 23-24 commanded honouring parents with excellence, not saying ugh nor yelling, saying noble word, lowering wing of humility out of mercy, praying for mercy as they raised you young. Verse 25 says Lord knows best what is within yourselves, if you are righteous He is All-Forgiving to those who constantly turn to Him. The connection is that honouring parents, especially old parents in your care, is difficult, may lead to annoyance, ugh, etc., due to human weakness, haste, as human is ever hasty. Allah knows what is within yourselves, knows if you intend righteousness even if you slip, and He is All-Forgiving to those who constantly turn, meaning if you are righteous in intention and you slip with ugh or annoyance due to haste, then turn back, repent, He forgives. This is mercy and encouragement: strive to be righteous with parents, and if you fail due to weakness, turn back, He is All-Forgiving to awwābīn.

**Link To Human Hasty And Self-Reckoning**

Verse 11 said human prays for evil as for good, is ever hasty, and verse 14 said sufficient yourself as reckoner. Verse 25 says Lord knows best what is within yourselves, if righteous He is All-Forgiving to those who constantly turn. The link is that human hasty may say ugh or yell out of haste, but Lord knows inner intention, whether righteous or not, and forgives those who constantly turn, return. This encourages self-reckoning and turning: if you are righteous, even if you have bad thoughts, turn, He forgives. It also links to destiny bound to neck and record laid open: Lord knows what is within selves that will be in record, and forgives awwābīn.

**Linguistic Note On Nufus And Awwab And Ghafur**

Rabb from r-b-b, Lord. Aʿlam from ʿ-l-m, more knowing, knows best. Mā fī, what in. Nufūs from n-f-s, selves, souls, plural of nafs. Takūnū from k-w-n, to be. Ṣāliḥīn from ṣ-l-ḥ, righteous, from ṣalaḥa. Awwābīn from a-w-b, constantly turning, ever-returning, from āba, to return, intensive form awwāb, one who returns often. Ghafūr from gh-f-r, All-Forgiving, intensive, from ghafara, to forgive. The verse's structure — your Lord knows best what is in your selves, if you are righteous then He is for awwābīn All-Forgiving — with knowledge of inner and condition of righteousness and forgiveness for returners, shows that Allah knows inner, judges by righteousness, and forgives those who constantly turn.

**How This Verse Teaches Sincerity And Repentance**

This verse teaches sincerity and repentance: Lord knows best what is within yourselves, so be sincere, righteous in intention, especially regarding parents, and if you slip, be among awwābīn, those who constantly turn, return to Him, repent, He is All-Forgiving. Al-Qurṭubī says believer should be righteous, intend good, honour parents sincerely, and if he has bad thoughts or says ugh out of human weakness, he should turn, repent, as awwāb, and Allah is All-Forgiving to awwābīn. The practical lesson is to be mindful that Allah knows inner, so purify intention, be righteous, and constantly turn, repent, as human is ever hasty and may err.

**A Reflection On Awwab**

Awwāb — one who constantly turns, returns, ever-returning — from a-w-b, to return, is beautiful attribute: one who returns to Allah often, after slipping, after sin, after annoyance, after haste. Allah is Ghafūr, All-Forgiving, to awwābīn, those who constantly turn. This is mercy: even if you are not perfect in honouring parents, if you are righteous in intention and constantly turn, return, repent, He forgives. The believer who reflects will strive to be ṣāliḥ, righteous, honour parents with excellence, avoid ugh and yelling, say noble word, lower wing of humility out of mercy, pray for parents, and if he slips due to haste, he will be awwāb, constantly turning, returning to Allah, seeking forgiveness, knowing Lord knows best what is within self and is All-Forgiving to awwābīn.

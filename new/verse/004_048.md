## Sūrah al-Nisāʾ 4:48

> Indeed, Allah does not forgive associating others with Him ˹in worship˺, but forgives anything else of whoever He wills. And whoever associates others with Allah has indeed committed a grave sin.

### Expanded Commentary

**The gravity of associating partners with Allah**

The verse places associating others with Allah in a unique category: Allah does not forgive it for one who meets Him persisting in it, while other sins remain subject to His will. This is not a restriction on divine power. It identifies the theological rupture of treating a created being as a partner in worship, lordship, or ultimate authority. If a person repents from shirk before death, the Qur’an’s universal call to repentance remains open; the warning concerns dying without abandoning it.

**Why the distinction matters**

Every other sin, however serious, can be confessed as a violation of God’s command. Shirk changes the relationship to the Commander by placing another at the centre of devotion. It therefore attacks the foundation from which forgiveness is sought. The verse does not imply that non-shirk sins are harmless. It says that forgiveness is possible for them according to God’s wisdom, while shirk without repentance is a final refusal of the source of forgiveness.

**Avoiding careless judgments**

The warning should make believers serious about pure worship, not eager to label individuals. Questions of ignorance, compulsion, misunderstanding, and final state require knowledge and humility. Allah knows what reached a person, what he intended, and how he died. The verse is first a mirror: examine the subtle partners of reputation, wealth, tribe, and desire that may compete for the obedience owed to God.

The chapter’s social commands arise from this theological centre. If God alone is ultimate, no family, ruler, scholar, or market may receive unquestioned sovereignty. Tawḥīd protects both worship and human rights.

**Tawḥīd and human freedom**

If no created power shares God’s ultimacy, no human being can legitimately demand absolute obedience from another. The doctrine that protects worship also limits rulers, family elders, scholars, and markets. Every created authority is judged by the One authority it cannot replace.

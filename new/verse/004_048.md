## Sūrah al-Nisāʾ 4:48

> Indeed, Allah does not forgive associating others with Him ˹in worship˺, but forgives anything else of whoever He wills. And whoever associates others with Allah has indeed committed a grave sin.

**Expanded Commentary**

**A Boundary That Does Not Move**

The statement *inna Allāha lā yaghfiru an yushraka bihi wa yaghfiru mā dūna dhālika li man yashāʾ* — indeed, Allah does not forgive association with Him, but forgives anything less than that for whomever He wills — places one sin outside the general amnesty that the sūrah has been offering for lesser faults. The previous promise *“if you avoid major sins, We will cover your lesser misdeeds and admit you to honour”* (4:31) and the assurance that repentance soon after ignorance is accepted (4:17) could be heard as though every fault, if deferred, would eventually be covered. This verse corrects that hearing by naming the limit. *Shirk* — associating others with God in worship, devotion, or ultimate trust — is not a lesser fault to be covered by avoidance of other majors; it is the breach of the first command the sūrah gave after its family laws: *“worship Allah and associate none with Him”* (4:36). The verse therefore draws a line that the ethic of *iḥsān* to parents, relatives, orphans, neighbours, and travellers circles around but does not cross. All else — *mā dūna dhālika* — anything below that — remains under divine will to forgive, which the sūrah has described as perfect knowledge of them (4:39), never wronging by an atom’s weight but multiplying good (4:40), and ever-pardoning, ever-forgiving (4:43).

**Forgives Anything Else of Whoever He Wills**

The phrase *wa yaghfiru mā dūna dhālika li man yashāʾ* was read as vast scope. *Mā dūna* — what is below, less than — covers every sin that is not *shirk*, from consuming orphan wealth (4:10) to devouring by falsehood (4:29) to illicit intercourse (4:15–16), provided the conditions for forgiveness elsewhere are met — repentance soon after ignorance (4:17) rather than deferral until death is present (4:18), and avoidance of persistence as habit. *Li man yashāʾ* — for whomever He wills — was stressed as showing forgiveness is not automatic; it is will, knowledge, and wisdom together, as the sūrah closes similar verses with All-Knowing, All-Wise (4:26, 4:11) and All-Knowing, Most Forbearing (4:12). The will to forgive those who turn is real — *“Allah wishes to turn to you in grace”* (4:27) and to lighten because you are weak (4:28) — but it remains His will, not a claim any sinner can demand. That balance keeps the community from presumptuous despair and from presumptuous guarantee. A person who has committed a major sin that is not *shirk* is not told forgiveness is impossible; he is told it is possible for whomever God wills, and that will is known to be merciful to those who avoid the major and ask for bounty (4:32, 4:39).

**A Grave Sin in Itself**

The closing *wa man yushrik bi Allāhi fa qad iftarā ithman ʿaẓīmā* — and whoever associates others with Allah has indeed fabricated a grave sin — uses *iftarā* — to fabricate, to invent a lie — and *ithm ʿaẓīm* — grave, immense sin — to describe *shirk* as both lie and weight. It is a lie because it attributes to another what belongs to God — creation from a single soul, watching over womb-ties (4:1), appointing heirs (4:11), multiplying an atom’s weight (4:40) — and a weight because it displaces the foundation on which all other limits stand. The sūrah has shown that limits about property and family hold only if the One who set them is worshipped alone; to associate another is to put a second source of limits beside Him, which is why the verse follows the call to those given the Book to believe in what confirms their own (4:47). The same fabrication appears in the next portrait of those who elevate themselves and say God has told them they are pure (4:49), and in those who fabricate lies against God as a blatant sin (4:50). *Shirk* is the root fabrication of which those are branches. The Qur’an elsewhere calls *shirk* *ẓulm ʿaẓīm* — great injustice — because it misplaces worship itself, the act that should be given only to the One who never wrongs by an atom.

**Why This Verse Comes Here**

Placed after the warning that faces could be wiped or the defiant condemned like Sabbath-breakers (4:47), this verse explains the theology behind that severity. Wiping faces and *laʿn* were not arbitrary penalties for wordplay; they were responses to disbelief that includes association — taking scholars, desires, or idols as arbiters beside God. The sūrah will go on to describe those who believe in idols and false gods and claim they guide better than believers (4:51), showing the live form of association the verse addresses. By naming the unforgiven sin here, the sūrah clarifies the path back: believe in what was revealed confirming your own (4:47), worship without association and be kind to parents and others (4:36), and ask God for bounty rather than crave another’s (4:32). Those who do so remain under *li man yashāʾ* — the will that forgives what is below *shirk* — where soon repentance (4:17) and avoidance of major sins (4:31) bring covering. Those who persist in association have fabricated the one lie that covers over the source of forgiveness itself, which is why it is not forgiven when held to death.

**A Verse That Holds Mercy Without Negating Justice**

Read with the sūrah’s larger movement, this verse holds mercy and justice together without blurring either. Mercy is vast — anything below association may be forgiven for whomever God wills — and justice is exact — association itself, if held to death without turning, is not forgiven, and the one who does it has fabricated a grave sin. The sūrah has already said that failure to repent until death is present is not accepted (4:18) and that exceeding God’s limits brings abiding humiliation (4:14). This verse adds the theological ground: the limit that cannot be exceeded with pardon is to place another beside God, because that act denies the very One who pardons. Yet even this strict word is surrounded by mercy: before it came the invitation to believe before faces are wiped (4:47), and after it will come the exposure of self-purification and fabrication (4:49–50) as correctable before death. For the weak for whom burdens were lightened (4:28) and for whom earth was made purifier where water failed (4:43), the verse is not crushing; it is clarifying. Keep the foundation — worship without partner — and every other fault, while serious, remains under a will that is ever-pardoning, ever-forgiving, and that multiplies an atom’s weight of good many times over.

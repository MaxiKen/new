## Sūrah al-Baqarah 2:20

> It is as if the lightning were about to snatch away their sight. Whenever lightning strikes, they walk in its light, but when darkness covers them, they stand still. Had Allah willed, He could have taken away their hearing and sight. Surely Allah is Most Capable of everything.

**Expanded Commentary**

**A Brightness That Nearly Takes the Eye**

The verse continues the storm and fixes on its most violent element: *yakādu al-barqu yakhṭafu abṣārahum* — the lightning all but snatches away their sight. The particle *yakādu*, "it almost does," governs the clause, and its work is precise: the snatching is imminent, deserved, begun — and withheld. The lightning here is proof at full intensity, the clarity of revelation in its most dazzling mode, and the parable says something severe about clarity: to eyes adapted to darkness, light itself is dangerous. The hypocrites did not choose blindness by shutting their eyes; the brilliance they refused to live by now threatens the organs they kept. Exegetes connect the clause to the fire parable's ending — light taken away there, sight nearly taken here — as one movement in two tempos. The *yakādu* keeps the stroke pending, which means the verse is not describing a finished punishment but a suspended one, and suspension is where every warning lives.

**Walking by Flashes: The Gait of Hypocrisy**

Between the flares, the parable records the men's locomotion, and the locomotion is the diagnosis: *kullamā aḍāʾa lahum mashaw fīhi* — whenever it lights up for them, they walk in it; *wa-idhā aẓlama ʿalayhim qāmū* — when it darkens over them, they stand. Their movement has no internal source. They do not walk toward anything; they walk when conditions permit, and the condition is brightness they did not kindle. The verb chosen for the dark intervals, *qāmū*, is deliberately flat — they stood, halted, stalled — the posture of men paused rather than resting. Read morally, this is opportunism drawn as physics: advance while faith pays, freeze when it costs. The hypocrite's progress through life is therefore intermittent by construction, a series of sprints between eclipses, and every pause is longer than the last until the darkness of verse 17's ending closes over the whole road. No one in the parable is traveling; they are being flashed forward.

**The Conditional That Will Never Fire**

The third movement states what is *not* happening: *wa-law shāʾa Allāhu la-dhahaba bi-samʿihim wa-abṣārihim* — had Allah willed, He would have taken away their hearing and their sight outright. The construction is the unreal conditional, the grammar of a possibility deliberately left unactualized, and it recontextualizes everything before it. The thunder that deafens, the lightning that nearly blinds — these are mercy operating at reduced voltage. The full stroke exists in the divine capacity and is being withheld in real time. The verse thus answers an objection the storm provokes: if God were merely hostile to these men, the parable would end in the first flash. Instead the withholding is stated as the fact that governs the scene — the organs remain functional, the flashes keep coming, the walking remains possible. The restraint is also the proof that the disability is moral and not physical: eyes that could have been taken are still being addressed.

**Qadīr: Power as the Frame of the Whole Scene**

The closing clause names the attribute that holds the parable together: *inna Allāha ʿalā kulli shayʾin qadīr* — surely Allah is Most Capable of everything. The word *qadīr* shares its root with *qadr*, measure as well as power — the capacity to do a thing and the ordaining of its amount. Both senses are at work in the verse's logic. The storm's violence is *measured*: the lightning stops short of the eye, the thunder stops short of the heart, because power here operates with a scale in its hand. And the withheld stroke is *possible* at every moment: the conditional of the third movement depends entirely on this attribute. Omnipotence stated after a description of restraint is not a threat but a definition — the difference between cannot and will-not, which is the whole difference between a victim of nature and an addressee of God. The hypocrites are not caught in weather; they are inside a governed demonstration, and the Governor is named in the last sentence.

**Two Parables, Two Tempos of One Condition**

Set side by side, the fire and the storm divide the hypocrites' condition along time. The first parable is singular and final: one flame kindled, one extinction, a man left in completed darkness — the end state, which the tradition associates with the unmasking at death. The second is rhythmic and repeated: flash, walk, dark, stand, flash again — the daily gait, the condition as it is lived before the end arrives. The pairing is pedagogically exact. A warning that showed only the extinction would let every borrower of light say *not yet*; a picture that showed only the rhythm would let him say *never ends, so never comes*. Together they give the type both of its tenses — the intermittent present and the terminal past — and the *yakādu* of this verse sits at the seam, the almost that keeps the rhythm from becoming an excuse. Some commentators extend the division to persons, reading one parable of hypocrisy's leaders and the other of its rank and file; the grammatical evidence is thin, but the two-tempo reading stands on the text alone.

**The Parables End; the Address Begins**

With this verse the thirteen-verse diagnosis that began at verse 8 is complete. The passage has moved through the group's speech, scheme, sickness, social claims, mockery, and commerce, and finished with two images that hold the whole type in one night — a small extinguished fire and a sky in concussion. Nothing further will be said about the hypocrites for some time; the sūrah turns. The next verse leaves parable, third person, and diagnosis behind and addresses the entire species directly with its first command — worship — moving from what these men are to what all men are for. The structure is deliberate: al-Baqarah exhausts the description of the diseased type before it issues the universal call, so that the call arrives with its negative examples already filed. The storm is still sounding when the invitation goes out, and the invitation is addressed to everyone standing inside it.

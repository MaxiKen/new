## Sūrah al-Baqarah 2:20

> It is as if the lightning were about to snatch away their sight. Whenever lightning strikes, they walk in its light, but when darkness covers them, they stand still. Had Allah willed, He could have taken away their hearing and sight. Surely Allah is Most Capable of everything.

**Expanded Commentary**

**Lightning That Almost Snatches Sight**

The rainstorm’s lightning is now described as *yakādu al-barqu yakhṭafu abṣārahum* — “the lightning all but snatches away their sight.” The verb *khaṭafa* — to snatch, to seize quickly — evokes a predator’s grab. The lightning does not merely illuminate; it threatens to take the very faculty that would use the illumination. That threat completes the inversion of the fire parable. There, light was taken away after it had lit what was around the kindler (2:17); here, light arrives so violently that it threatens to take sight itself. In both cases the hypocrite’s relation to light is untenable: the gentle light he sought is withdrawn, the violent light that arrives cannot be retained. The sūrah has moved from a withheld light to an overwhelming light to show that hypocrisy has no stable relation to illumination at any intensity.

**Whenever It Shines They Walk; When Dark They Halt — A Gait of Flashes**

*Kullamā aḍāʾa lahum mashaw fīhi wa-idhā aẓlama ʿalayhim qāmū* — “whenever it shines for them, they walk in its light, but when darkness covers them, they stand still.” The gait is intermittent, governed entirely by external flashes rather than by an internal direction. When lightning illuminates, they move; when darkness returns, they halt. The verbs are telling: *mashaw* — they walk — is the minimal forward motion, not *saʿaw* (to hasten) or *ah-tadaw* (to be guided); *qāmū* — they stand still — is not *raqadū* (to rest) but to be stopped, immobilized. The hypocrite’s religion is thus described as phototropic rather than directed: it moves not because it knows where to go but because a flash has momentarily shown a surface. That is why the commentators link this parable to the worldly benefits Ṭabarī identified for the fire parable — to eat, marry, and be protected by profession — benefits that, like lightning, come in flashes and then withdraw.

**Had Allah Willed, He Would Have Taken Hearing and Sight**

*Wa-law shāʾa Allāhu la-dhahaba bi-samʿihim wa-abṣārihim* — “Had Allah willed, He would have taken away their hearing and sight.” The verse has just shown that He *has* taken their light (2:17) and left them deaf, dumb, and blind (2:18). Why then state the counterfactual — that had He willed, He could have taken hearing and sight — as if it had not already happened? The commentators hear in the counterfactual a reminder of the difference between functional privation and total removal. The hypocrites are *as* deaf, dumb, and blind; their hearing and sight as organs remain, as the fingers pressed in ears (2:19) and the walking by flashes show. The sentence says: the One who has already withdrawn the benefit of hearing and sight could, had He willed, withdraw the organs themselves, and the fact that He has not is itself a mercy that leaves the door to return ajar. The sūrah that began with sealing (2:7) and disease increased (2:10) here pauses to say the sealing is not yet the final taking.

**Truly Allah Is Powerful Over All Things — The Horizon After Two Parables**

*Inna Allāha ʿalā kulli shayʾin qadīr* — “Truly Allah is Powerful over all things.” The formula appears throughout al-Baqarah as the horizon against which human calculation is measured. After two parables that have displayed hypocrisy as a specific human failure — kindling for light, pressing fingers against thunder, walking only by flashes — the verse closes the sequence by naming the divine capacity that encompasses both the storm and the fire: *qudrah* that could have taken hearing and sight entirely and that, for now, has left the hypocrite in a state that is still, by that very leaving, an invitation to hear and see differently. The hypocrites had said “We are only reformers” (2:11) and “We were only mocking” (2:14) as if the names they gave would settle the matter; the closing *“Powerful over everything”* says the settling is not theirs to declare.

**The Two Parables as One Anatomy**

Read together, the fire and the storm are not two alternatives but one anatomy in two media. The fire showed the hypocrite from the side of his own agency — he kindled, it lit, it was taken, he was left unseeing. The storm shows him from the side of the world’s agency upon him — darkness, thunder, lightning descend, he presses, he walks, he halts, he is encompassed. Both end in privation — unseeing (2:17), deaf-dumb-blind (2:18), fingers in ears, standing still — and both attribute the privation to Allah after describing the human act that invited it. The sūrah thus preserves, across ten verses, the double attribution it established at the start: hearts are sealed by Allah (2:7) after warning is rendered the same (2:6); disease is increased by Allah (2:10) after saying without being (2:8); light is taken by Allah (2:17) after kindling without guidance (2:16); wandering is prolonged by Allah (2:15) after mocking guidance (2:14). The counterfactual — *had He willed He would have taken* — keeps that double attribution from hardening into determinism.

**Walking by Lightning and Halting in Darkness — The Image of a Life**

The intermittent gait — *whenever it shines they walk, when it darkens they halt* — is the sūrah’s final image of a life that is not upon guidance (*ʿalā hudā*, 2:5) but under flashes. The mindful are upon guidance as upon a road that remains; the hypocrites walk only when a flash shows a patch of ground, and the ground they see by is not the road but the immediate circle that lightning happens to light. When the flash is gone, the road is gone, because they have not *established* prayer as a standing that would keep the road lit between flashes. That is why the sūrah will next turn, without transition, to the summons that has been prepared by all that has preceded: *“O humanity, worship your Lord”* (2:21). The only way to move from a gait governed by lightning to a standing upon guidance is to be given a Lord to worship who can provide a light that does not snatch sight.

## Sūrah al-Shuʿarāʾ 26:63

> So We inspired Moses: “Strike the sea with your staff,” and the sea was split, each part was like a huge mountain.

**Expanded Commentary**

**Guidance Becomes a Concrete Command**

The promise that God would guide Moses now becomes an instruction: strike the sea with the staff. Moses is not asked to reason his way through the barrier or to wait for a natural opening. The command joins a familiar instrument to an extraordinary result. The staff that served him in ordinary work becomes the means through which God opens a path for the trapped community.

The action is simple, but the result is beyond ordinary power. The sea splits, and each side stands like a huge mountain. The image does not describe a shallow crossing or a small retreat of water. It presents a passage through a body of water whose walls appear immense. The miracle answers the companions' fear at the exact point where fear saw only an impassable boundary.

**The Staff Is a Means, Not the Source**

The staff appears again, but its repetition should not turn it into a magical object. Moses does not possess an independent power that can command seas. He acts after inspiration, and the staff serves a divine command. The instrument matters because God chooses to use it, not because wood has a hidden authority of its own.

This distinction protects worship from attaching itself to a created means. People may honor the sign and forget the Sender if they focus on the object. The Qur'anic account keeps the order clear: God inspires, Moses obeys, and the sea parts. The staff is a means within obedience. Its ordinary history makes the divine act more visible because the object itself cannot explain the outcome.

**The Sea Is Described Without Being Named**

The Qur'an does not identify the exact body of water. Muslim and Jewish tradition commonly associates the crossing with the Red Sea, but the revealed verse itself emphasizes the miracle rather than the geographical label. Leaving the name unspecified keeps attention on God's power and on the deliverance of the people, not on a detail the text has chosen not to settle.

This restraint is important in interpretation. A traditional identification may help readers imagine the scene, but it should not be presented as though the verse supplied a modern map reference. The certain facts are enough: Moses was commanded to strike the sea, it split, and the divided water stood like mountains. The miracle's meaning does not depend on claiming more geographical precision than revelation gives.

**Mountain Walls Make the Sign Unmistakable**

Some natural processes can move water or change its level, but the image of each part standing like a huge mountain points beyond ordinary retreat. The wonder lies not merely in water being absent from a path. The sea is made into walls that remain separated long enough for a people to cross. The form of the miracle removes the explanation that a temporary shallow or current alone caused the passage.

The comparison with mountains also changes the emotional landscape. A sea that had represented a barrier becomes a corridor bounded by massive structures. What terrifies from a distance becomes the means of escape. The same water can be an obstacle or a path according to God's command. Creation is not independent of the Creator's will, and a barrier can become a road without ceasing to be powerful.

**The Command Requires Action at the Moment of Fear**

Moses had just reassured the people that God would guide him. The next guidance is not merely inward. It requires a physical act. He must strike the sea, and the people must be prepared to enter the opening. Faith becomes effective through obedience to a command whose result could not have been predicted from the object in Moses' hand.

This prevents a passive understanding of trust. Moses cannot say that because God is with him, he need not act. Nor can the people remain at the shore waiting for a path to become familiar. The command creates a route through the impossible, but the servants must respond to it. Divine help does not remove human obedience; it makes obedience fruitful.

**The Two Sides Display a Created Order Under Command**

The sea is not destroyed. It is divided, and each part takes the form of a mountain. The miracle preserves the created substance while changing its relation to the travelers. Water becomes a wall and the seabed becomes a road. This detail shows that divine power does not need to erase creation to govern it.

The same principle appears in other signs. God can use ordinary objects, natural elements, and human actions without being limited by their usual patterns. A believer should not make the mistake of treating regularity as a boundary around God's ability. The ordinary order is real, but it is not a prison for the One who established it.

**A Way Opens Where Human Sight Found None**

Moses' assurance was not an optimistic reading of the landscape. It was trust in the Guide who could create a way. The sea's division vindicates the distinction between visible facts and final possibilities. The people were genuinely surrounded, yet they were not abandoned. Their inability to see a path did not mean that no path existed in God's decree.

The verse is therefore the decisive answer to the cry of being overtaken. It does not tell the frightened companions to deny the sea. It shows the sea obeying a command from the Lord. The barrier becomes evidence that guidance can arrive through the very thing that seemed to make escape impossible.

The miracle also protects the community from believing that fear had been foolish. The sea was a real barrier; God's command made it a passage.

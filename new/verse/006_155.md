## Sūrah al-Anʿām 6:155

> This is a blessed Book We have revealed. So follow it and be mindful ˹of Allah˺, so you may be shown mercy.

**Expanded Commentary**

**The Blessed Book**

This is a blessed Book We have revealed. The sentence declares the Book that the community has received, and it uses the same description the sūrah applied earlier in the chapter when it said that the Book sent down to the Prophet is blessed and confirms what came before it (6:92). The description appears again in the sūrah of the Prophets, where the revelation is called a blessed reminder that has been sent down, and in the sūrah of Ṣād, where God calls the Book revealed to the Prophet blessed and states its purpose: that people may contemplate its verses and that the people of understanding may be reminded (38:29).

The word carries the sense of benefit and increase, and the Book's use of it in these passages attaches it to the guidance the revelation provides. The verse in front of us states that the Book is blessed and then draws the practical consequence in its command.

**The Command to Follow**

So follow it and be mindful of Allah. The sentence attaches two requirements to the description: the community is to follow what the Book contains, and it is to be mindful of God in doing so. The sūrah has used the same pair in its account of the covenant, where the commands were gathered into a path with the instruction to follow and the appeal to consciousness of God (6:153), and the verse in front of us repeats the arrangement for the Book itself.

The Book's instructions to follow what has been revealed appear at several points in this sūrah. The Prophet was told to follow what was revealed to him from his Lord (6:106), and the sūrah of the Cattle states that the community is commanded to follow the Book and to be mindful. The two commands in the sentence are the practical form of the description that precedes them.

**The Mercy That Is the Purpose**

So you may be shown mercy. The sentence states the purpose of the command, and the Book attaches mercy to the guidance of the revelation throughout. The sūrah has described the Book given to Moses as a guide and a mercy (6:154), and the next verse will describe the revelation that has come to the audience as a clear proof, a guide, and a mercy (6:157). The phrase in front of us states the outcome the Book's guidance is meant to produce.

The Book's accounts of the earlier revelations use the same language. The sūrah of the Story calls the book of Moses an insight, a guide, and a mercy, and the sūrah of the Bee describes the Book sent down as guidance, mercy, and good news for those who submit (16:89). The verse in front of us belongs to that family of statements, and it places the community that receives the Qur'an within the same provision.

**Why the Verse Is Stated After the Earlier Scripture**

The verse follows the account of the scripture given to Moses (6:154), and its position shows what the sūrah is doing. The two revelations are presented as instances of one provision: the earlier scripture was given as a guide and a mercy, and the Book in the Prophet's hands is blessed and intended to produce mercy in its readers. The audience is not being offered a novelty but the same guidance in its own language.

The sūrah has argued for that continuity from its earlier verses, describing the Book as confirming what came before it and stating that God has not neglected anything in the Record (6:38). The verse in front of us supplies the command that follows from the description, and the passage that follows states the excuses that the Book's arrival removes.

**The Place of the Verse in the Sūrah's Closing Section**

The verse opens the sūrah's final passage, which concerns the Book itself and the response it requires. The argument against the audience's religion has been completed, the requirements of the covenant have been stated, and the earlier scripture has been described; the closing verses now address the community that has received the revelation directly.

The Book's habit of ending a sūrah with a statement about its own message appears in many places, and the sūrah of the Cattle is no exception. What the verse states is the description of the Book, the command that follows from it, and the purpose for which it was given, and the three elements furnish the theme of the passage that follows.

**What the Verse Requires of the Reader**

The verse requires the reader to treat the Book as a gift that carries obligations. The blessing is stated first, and the command to follow comes immediately after it, and the two are held together in a single sentence. The Book's blessings in the sūrah's account are always attached to responsibilities, and the reader who accepts the description accepts the instruction with it.

The sūrah's treatment of the community's relation to the revelation elsewhere makes the same point. The commands of the covenant were given to a community that had been shown the signs, and the Book's guidance is presented as the road along which the community is to travel. The verse in front of us states the arrangement in its shortest form, and the verses that follow state what the Book's arrival has settled.

**The Transition to the Next Verse**

The next verse states that the audience can no longer say that the scriptures were revealed only to two groups before them and that they were unaware of their teachings (6:156). The sentence names the excuse that the Book's arrival removes, and the passage that follows completes the argument with the statement that the clear proof, the guide, and the mercy have now come.

The sūrah's closing verses therefore answer the objection that the message had passed the audience by. The Book that has just been described as blessed is the answer to that objection, and the verses that follow state the terms in which the answer is given.

## Sūrah an-Nūr 24:16

> If only you had said upon hearing it, “How can we speak about such a thing! Glory be to You ˹O Lord˺! This is a heinous slander!”

**Expanded Commentary**

**The Demanded Reflex of Moral Incompetence: mā yakūnu lanā**

The divine rebuke issues an uncompromising ethical template for how a believer must respond when confronted with slander: *wa-lawlā idh samiʿtumūhu qultum mā yakūnu lanā an natakallama bi-hādhā*. The construction *mā yakūnu lanā* signifies absolute moral, legal, and spiritual incompetence. It does not mean merely that one ought not to speak; it means: it is entirely unlawful, inconceivable, and impossible for us to utter such words!

The Qur'an demands that a believer experience an instinctive moral paralysis in the presence of defamation. Just as a pious person feels an insurmountable physical and spiritual aversion to consuming carrion or worshipping an idol, they should feel completely incapable of participating in discussions that dismantle the honor of an innocent soul. The tongue should refuse to articulate that which violates the foundational sanctity of human dignity, recognizing an absolute spiritual barrier against participation in evil, refusing to serve as an instrument of malice, social cruelty, or voyeuristic curiosity under any pretext.

**The Exclamation of Transcendent Sanctification: subḥānak**

The commanded verbal response incorporates the sublime formula of glorification: *subḥānak*—Glory be to You, O Lord! Classical exegetes, including Fakhr al-Dīn al-Rāzī and al-Zamakhsharī, explored why divine transcendence (*tasbīḥ*) is invoked upon hearing a human rumor. They identified two profound spiritual dimensions behind this exclamation.

First, *subḥānak* serves as an expression of sheer cosmic shock and awe at the monstrosity of the lie, recoiling to the purity of God as a spiritual refuge from earthly filth. Second, it serves as an affirmation of divine providence: God is far too exalted, wise, and protective of His chosen Messenger ﷺ to allow the Mother of the Believers to fall into such moral degradation. Exclaiming *subḥānak* affirms that God preserves the domestic sanctuary of His Prophet ﷺ from contamination, turning theological conviction into an immediate defense against suspicion, honoring the divine majesty above all earthly doubts, human whispers, and sinister plots devised by hypocrites across time.

**The Unambiguous Denunciation: hādhā buhtānun ʿaẓīm**

The verse demands that the reaction culminate in an explicit, fearless denunciation: *hādhā buhtānun ʿaẓīm*, this is a heinous, monstrous calumny. In classical Arabic lexicography, *buhtān* is a specialized category of falsehood denoting a fabrication so shocking that it confounds (*yabhatu*) and paralyzes the innocent victim with disbelief.

Unlike ordinary backbiting (*ghībah*), which involves mentioning true faults behind someone's back, *buhtān* is the malicious invention of pure lies against an innocent person. By characterizing the rumor as a *buhtānun ʿaẓīm*, the Qur'an commands believers to strip away all polite ambiguity. A believer must not hedge, equivocate, or use mild language when confronting defamation; they must name the evil directly, branding it as a monstrous falsehood in the public square, leaving no room for destructive gossip to fester, circulate, or gain social legitimacy among listeners in any assembly or private gathering.

**The Prophetic Standard of Verbal Safety**

The demanded reaction corresponds directly to the core definition of Islamic identity articulated in the Sunnah. In Ṣaḥīḥ al-Bukhārī 10, the Prophet ﷺ established the gold standard of faith: The Muslim is the one from whose tongue and hand other Muslims are safe. Verse 16 demonstrates that verbal safety is not merely passive silence; it is active communal protection.

A believer who remains silent while an innocent person is slandered has failed to provide verbal safety to their sibling in faith. True adherence to the prophetic standard requires that one's tongue become a fortress defending the absent brother or sister, actively silencing the slanderer and declaring their fabrications unacceptable. The health of the community is measured by the degree to which every member feels safe from the tongues of their fellows, confident that their honor will be vigorously protected in their absence by righteous companions who fear God, revere truth, and honor divine boundaries.

**The Rejection of Voyeuristic Neutrality**

The verse delivers a devastating critique of false objectivity and voyeuristic neutrality. When scandalous rumors emerge, many individuals adopt a posture of detached interest, remarking: We cannot be certain of the facts, so we will simply listen and wait to see what develops. The Qur'an rejects this detached stance as a form of moral cowardice and complicity.

When an accusation targets people of established righteousness and demonstrated piety, the default position cannot be agnostic curiosity. Innocence is the established baseline, and divine law demands that believers actively take a side: the side of honor, chastity, and positive presumption. Entertaining scandalous possibilities about righteous people under the guise of open-mindedness is a betrayal of the spiritual covenant that binds believers together, dissolving communal solidarity in the acid of baseless speculation, cynicism, quiet betrayal, and mutual distrust among brethren.

**Constructing a Culture of Verbal Inviolability**

The timeless genius of this divine instruction lies in its power to dismantle defamatory campaigns at their inception. Slander relies entirely upon an audience willing to listen, nod, and pass the story along. If the audience consists of individuals who reflexively exclaim *mā yakūnu lanā an natakallama bi-hādhā*, glorify God, and declare *hādhā buhtānun ʿaẓīm*, the slanderer's enterprise is instantly aborted.

By training every believer to act as a verbal immune system for society, the Qur'an protects the social ecosystem from informational poisoning. Defamation requires social fuel to burn; when met with righteous resistance, it suffocates and dies. This verse builds a civilization where personal reputation is treated as an inviolable sanctuary, guarded by the collective conscience and active courage of every citizen, ensuring that falsehood finds no quarter in the public discourse, preserving dignity, and honoring the divine law throughout all generations of the community of faith.

## Sūrah Fāṭir 35:42

> They swore by Allah their most solemn oaths that if a warner were to come to them, they would certainly be better guided than any other community. Yet when a warner did come to them, it only drove them farther away—

**Expanded Commentary**

**A Promise of Guidance Can Conceal a Promise of Pride**

The people swore that if a warner came, they would be more rightly guided than other communities. Their words sounded like readiness for truth, but the comparison already exposed a problem. They did not simply promise to follow guidance. They promised to excel beyond others. The oath could therefore be mixed with rivalry, resentment toward earlier communities, and confidence in their own imagined superiority.

A person can speak about obedience while secretly making obedience serve pride. He says that he would accept a messenger, but he imagines that his acceptance would prove him better than everyone who came before. The statement is not condemned merely because it was made before the warner arrived. It becomes evidence when the arrival exposes that the speaker wanted the honor of guidance more than the demands of guidance.

**The Warner Arrives and Aversion Increases**

When the warner comes, the result is the opposite of what they promised. Instead of moving toward truth, they move farther away. Revelation does not create the inner aversion from nothing; it exposes and intensifies a resistance already protected by pride and interest. The clearer the call becomes, the more threatening it appears to a heart that has decided not to submit.

The Qur'an mentions a similar reaction when people are called to prostrate before the Compassionate in 25:60 and when divine signs are explained in 17:41. A message can increase aversion because it removes the shelter of ambiguity. Before revelation, a person may claim that he would follow if only proof came. After proof arrives, the real obstacle becomes visible. It was not lack of evidence but unwillingness to bow.

**Religious Rivalry Can Become a Barrier to Truth**

The historical reports about the Makkans criticizing earlier communities reveal a dangerous form of comparison. They claimed that they would be better guided if a messenger came to them. Instead of learning from the failure of previous peoples, they used that failure to advertise their own virtue. The promise was built on rivalry rather than humility, so the messenger's arrival threatened the self-image they had created.

This pattern appears whenever a person says, “I would obey if I had a clearer sign,” while secretly hoping the sign will never come. It can also appear within religious communities that compete over who is more faithful while resisting correction themselves. The remedy is to ask whether the heart wants truth even when truth lowers its status. A sincere servant welcomes guidance because it is from Allah, not because it will make him superior to another group.

**An Oath Cannot Substitute for Submission**

The people took solemn oaths, but an oath is only as truthful as the will behind it. Strong language can make a promise sound sincere while leaving the heart unchanged. Allah does not judge the force of the words alone. He sees whether the person keeps the promise when obedience costs wealth, reputation, custom, or power.

This is why revelation often turns attention from claims to deeds. A person may swear by Allah and still violate the trust for which he swore. The remedy is not to abandon promises but to treat them as binding before the One who hears them. A servant should speak with restraint, ask Allah for help to fulfill what he says, and repent quickly when he fails rather than adding a second lie to the first.

A promise made before the test is not worthless, but it is incomplete. The heart is revealed when the promised guidance arrives in a form that demands sacrifice. The Makkans wanted the honor of being a guided community without accepting the humility that guidance requires. Their story warns every community that speaks confidently about what it would do in ideal circumstances. Allah may bring the test through a command that challenges an inherited custom or through a teacher whom the community does not want to recognize. The honest response is to measure the message, not to protect the image of being open-minded. If the proof is sound, submission is the fulfillment of the earlier promise. If pride produces aversion, the oath becomes evidence against its speaker. The believer therefore asks Allah to make his words truthful when the difficult moment comes. He does not rely on his good opinion of himself. He relies on prayer, humility, and willingness to surrender whatever truth requires.

The oath therefore becomes a mirror for every believer who imagines that he would respond better than others. The honest response is to ask Allah for humility before the test arrives.

**The Arrival of Truth Reveals the Heart**

The verse is a warning to anyone who imagines that he would respond better than the people of the past. The proper response to their failure is not self-congratulation. It is prayer for humility. A warner may arrive through revelation, a teacher, a correction, or a painful event that exposes a person's direction. Each arrival asks whether the heart will move closer or defend its old position.

The Makkans' increased aversion shows that guidance can become a test of sincerity. A person who truly wants Allah does not demand that guidance preserve his pride. He accepts that the truth may come through someone he envies, a command he dislikes, or a correction he did not expect. The wise servant therefore measures his promise by his response to difficult truth. He asks Allah to make the arrival of guidance a cause of nearness, not another step away.

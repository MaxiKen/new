## Sūrah al-Shuʿarāʾ 26:118

> So judge between me and them decisively, and save me and the believers with me.”

**Expanded Commentary**

**Noah Asks God to Decide Between Truth and Rejection**

Noah asks his Lord to judge between him and his people decisively. The request does not arise from a personal contest for honor. It asks God to reveal the truth of the dispute and to settle the conflict between the messenger's warning and the community's rejection. Noah has delivered the message; the people have denied it; the matter now belongs before God's judgment.

The word decisively carries the sense of a judgment that separates and clarifies. Noah is not asking for endless argument. He wants the Lord to distinguish the faithful response from the rejection that has become entrenched. Divine judgment will not be confused by the chiefs' claims, the crowd's pressure, or the status of the people involved.

**The Request Is Entrusted to God's Justice**

Noah does not pronounce the final verdict himself. He asks God to judge. That distinction protects the prayer from private vengeance. A messenger may know that his people have rejected the truth, yet the timing, form, and completeness of judgment remain with the Lord.

Entrusting the matter to God also acknowledges that divine justice sees what human beings cannot. God knows the full history of the people, the clarity of the warning, the motives behind the rejection, and the sincerity of those who followed Noah. A human dispute can be settled only fully by the One whose knowledge is complete.

**Deliverance Is Asked for Noah and the Believers**

Noah pairs the request for judgment with a request for rescue. He does not ask only that the falsehood be exposed. He asks God to save him and the believers with him. The prayer shows that judgment has a protective purpose for those who have accepted the message. They need deliverance from a community whose rejection has become dangerous.

The believers are named alongside Noah. Their safety is not a private privilege reserved for the messenger. They are a community whose faith has placed them with him, and their fate is included in his prayer. Noah's leadership remains communal. He does not seek safety for himself while leaving the believers to the power of those who threaten them.

**Decisive Judgment Does Not Mean Human Impatience**

Noah has endured a long period of warning before making this prayer. The request comes after repeated rejection, not at the first sign of disagreement. That patience matters. A person should not treat every inconvenience as proof that God must immediately judge opponents.

The decisive judgment Noah seeks belongs to a situation in which the community has rejected the message and threatened the messenger. Even then, he asks God rather than taking the decision into his own hands. Patience and firmness can coexist. The servant waits for God's wisdom and still asks for protection when the danger becomes clear.

Quran 7:89 records Shuʿayb asking God to judge between him and his people in truth. The parallel shows that prophets may bring a dispute to God after the message has been delivered and the community has persisted in opposition. The prayer is not a personal demand for status; it is a request for truth to be established by the Lord.

The shared wording also connects Noah's situation with a broader prophetic pattern. A messenger can warn with patience, answer objections, and then entrust the final conflict to God. The Lord's judgment protects the integrity of revelation and the people who have accepted it.

**The Believers Are Saved With Their Messenger**

Noah asks for himself and the believers with him. Their shared rescue reflects the shared nature of faith. They listened to the warning, accepted the messenger, and remained with him despite the social cost. Their closeness to Noah is not based on class or family advantage. It is based on a common response to God.

The phrase also teaches that faith may require belonging to a vulnerable minority. The believers are not saved by blending into the majority or by asking Noah to soften the message. They stand together under God's judgment and protection. Community becomes a source of endurance when it is organized around truth rather than status.

**The Prayer Does Not Claim Noah's Personal Power**

Noah cannot rescue himself or the believers through his own strength. He asks God to save them. The request confirms the message he has been preaching: the Lord is the true protector. A messenger may lead a community, but he remains a servant who depends on divine action when human resources fail.

That dependence keeps leadership humble. Noah does not promise the believers that his own authority will guarantee their safety. He turns them and himself toward God. The community's hope is therefore not attached to a human hero but to the Lord who can judge, distinguish, and save.

**Judgment and Rescue Belong Together**

Noah's prayer links the two because falsehood and faith have reached a point of separation. A judgment that leaves the believers exposed would not complete the protection promised through the message. A rescue that ignored the truth of the dispute would not answer the people's rejection. God can both reveal justice and preserve those who trusted Him.

The request therefore has a moral order. Noah does not ask to be saved from ordinary hardship while his people are condemned for a minor disagreement. He asks after the call has been denied and the believing community has been threatened. The decisive judgment he seeks is the Lord's resolution of a sustained conflict over worship and truth.

**The Prayer Teaches Where to Carry an Unsettled Conflict**

A believer may face rejection, slander, or pressure and feel tempted to settle the matter through anger. Noah offers another path. State the wrong clearly, ask God for a just decision, protect the vulnerable, and leave the final judgment to the Lord. This is not passivity; it is disciplined dependence.

The verse gives the prayer a double focus: truth should be established, and believers should be saved. A sound response to persecution seeks both justice and protection without turning personal injury into a license for cruelty. Noah's appeal remains decisive because it is entrusted to the One who judges perfectly.

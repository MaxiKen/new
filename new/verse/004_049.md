## Sūrah al-Nisāʾ 4:49

> Have you ˹O Prophet˺ not seen those who ˹falsely˺ elevate themselves? It is Allah who elevates whoever He wills. And none will be wronged ˹even by the width of˺ the thread of a date stone.

**Expanded Commentary**

**Have You Not Seen Those Who Declare Themselves Pure**

The question *a lam tara ilā alladhīna yuzakkūna anfusahum* — have you not seen those who falsely elevate themselves, who declare themselves pure — follows the sūrah’s exposure of distorted words (4:46) and the warning against association (4:48). *Yuzakkūna* — to declare pure, to praise as purified — was read as claiming righteousness for oneself, telling others that God has approved them, or that lineage, worship, or status makes them clean without need for turning. In the Madinan setting the phrase was linked to Jewish claims that they were God’s children and beloved, or that none would enter the Garden except their own, and to similar claims by others who elevate themselves. The sūrah has just said that God does not forgive *shirk* but forgives what is below it for whomever He wills (4:48); to declare oneself pure is to pre-empt that will, to assign to oneself the judgment that belongs to Him. The Qur’an elsewhere says *“do not claim purity for yourselves; He knows best who is mindful”* (53:32), and this verse is the sūrah’s instance, placed where self-elevation was being used to excuse word distortion and trade of the Book.

**It Is Allah Who Elevates Whoever He Wills**

The correction *bali Allāhu yuzakkī man yashāʾ* — rather, it is Allah who elevates whoever He wills — returns the act of purification to its Owner. *Yuzakkī* — to purify, to elevate, to cause to grow — is God’s act, not a status claimed by speech. The sūrah has shown that purification comes through His ways: making things clear (4:26), guiding to noble ways, turning toward you and lightening because you are weak (4:26–28), covering lesser faults when major sins are avoided (4:31), and accepting soon repentance for ignorance (4:17). To claim purity without that path is to claim the fruit without the root. *Man yashāʾ* — whoever He wills — was stressed as will joined to knowledge and wisdom, as the sūrah closes similar verses with All-Knowing, All-Wise (4:26) and Most Forbearing (4:12). The will to purify is not arbitrary; it follows the pattern the sūrah has taught: the one who worships without association and is kind to parents, orphans, neighbours, and travellers (4:36), who gives dowries graciously (4:4), who trades by mutual consent (4:29), and who asks God for bounty rather than craves another’s (4:32) is the one God elevates. The self-praiser who conceals bounty and promotes stinginess (4:37) is not elevated by his own claim, because the elevation he seeks is in people’s eyes, not before the One who sees.

**Not Even by the Width of a Date Thread**

The assurance *wa lā yuẓlamūna fatīlā* — and none will be wronged even by the width of the thread of a date stone — follows the correction to calm both hope and fear. *Fatīl* — the thin thread in the groove of a date pit — was read alongside *naqīr* — speck on a date stone (4:53) — and *qiṭmīr* — film on a date pit elsewhere — as images of the smallest amount. The sūrah has used such measures to stress precision: *“whether it is little or much”* inheritance must be shared (4:7), a small provision for bystanders with kind speech (4:8), an atom’s weight never wronged but multiplied (4:40), and self-elevation as a *farīyah* — fabrication — that is a blatant sin (4:50). To say no one will be wronged by a *fatīl* is to say that God’s purification — *yuzakkī* — does not overlook the smallest good that deserves elevation, nor does it ignore the smallest wrong that claims it. The Qur’an elsewhere says that on the Day when a witness is brought and no speech can be hidden (4:41–42), the record will not leave small or great except counted. Here the same exactness is promised before that Day, so that the self-praiser cannot think his claim will be rounded up and the quiet doer cannot fear his atom will be rounded down.

**Why Self-Elevation Blocks Purification**

To declare oneself pure blocks the very return the sūrah invites. Purification — *tazkiyah* — in the Qur’an is linked to turning — *tawbah* — and to being made clear; the sūrah says God wills to make clear and turn toward you (4:26–27) and to lighten because you are weak (4:28). The one who says he is already pure sees no need to be made clear, no need to be turned toward, and no weakness to be lightened. Early commentators noted that self-elevation also feeds the other faults diagnosed: it feeds arrogance and boastfulness that the sūrah says God does not like (4:36), it feeds stinginess that conceals bounty (4:37), and it feeds the taking of words out of context to discredit faith (4:46), because the self-pure can justify distortion as insight. The correction *Allah elevates whomever He wills* therefore humbles without crushing; it moves the standard from claimed status to divine will joined to deed. The one who is elevated is not the one who says he is, but the one whom God, who knows best who enemies are and is sufficient as Guardian and Helper (4:45), knows to be mindful and whom He therefore purifies. That knowledge is the same that never wrongs by a *fatīl*.

**A Community Invited to Be Purified, Not to Claim Purity**

Placed between the grave sin of association (4:48) and the blatant fabrication of lies against God (4:50), this verse steadies the community between two traps: despair that no one can be pure and presumption that some already are by birth. The sūrah’s answer is that purity is given, not taken, and that it is given without wrong by even a thread. The poor guardian who took only what is fair (4:6), the heir who kept limits and was promised Gardens (4:13), the couple who sought mediators desiring reconciliation and found God bringing harmony (4:35), and the spender who believed and donated from what was provided (4:39) — each may be elevated, not because they claimed it but because God wills it and does not wrong by a *fatīl*. The question have you not seen is therefore both exposure and invitation. Have you not seen the self-praisers? Do not be them. And have you not seen that the only true elevation is God’s act? Ask Him for it, as the verse before invited *ask Allah for His bounty* (4:32), and let Him purify you on the Day when not a thread’s width is missed. The self-declared pure will find their claim weighs less than a *fatīl*; the quietly purified will find their atom multiplied many times over.

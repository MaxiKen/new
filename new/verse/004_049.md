## Sūrah al-Nisāʾ 4:49

> Have you ˹O Prophet˺ not seen those who ˹falsely˺ elevate themselves? It is Allah who elevates whoever He wills. And none will be wronged ˹even by the width of˺ the thread of a date stone.

### Expanded Commentary

**Self-praise is not purification**

The verse challenges those who declare themselves pure or superior. Religious identity, lineage, learning, and public reputation can all become tools of self-certification. But elevation belongs to Allah, and He raises whom He wills according to truth that may not be visible to the crowd. A person who announces his own righteousness has already made the announcement part of the test.

**The date-stone thread**

The image of the thin thread on a date stone means that divine justice omits nothing. It also restrains the self-exaltation described in the first sentence. No person can safely claim that God owes him honour, and no person should assume that another’s apparently small act is beneath divine notice. Human beings see surfaces; Allah knows sincerity, repentance, and hidden endurance.

**Humility is not self-hatred**

To reject self-praise does not require denying a real skill or refusing a legitimate qualification. A judge, teacher, or worker may describe competence when responsibility requires it. The difference is between accurate testimony offered for a useful purpose and a heart that treats its own virtue as an entitlement. Humility allows gratitude for a gift while returning the gift’s source to God.

The verse is especially relevant to religious communities, where titles and visible practices can create hierarchies of holiness. The believer should seek purification rather than advertise it, correct wrongdoing without claiming infallibility, and leave the final elevation to the One who knows every deed.

**Purity is received, not advertised**

People who need to present themselves as pure often become unable to admit a mistake. The Qur’anic alternative is hopeful humility: seek cleansing through repentance and good action, but let God decide what has been accepted. This protects the community from both self-righteous leaders and despairing sinners.

## Sūrah al-Anʿām 6:51

> Warn with this ˹Quran˺ those who are awed by the prospect of being gathered before their Lord—when they will have no protector or intercessor besides Him—so perhaps they will be mindful ˹of Him˺.

**Expanded Commentary**

**A Warning Aimed at a Specific Kind of Listener**

The previous verses defined the messenger's office as news and warning, and this verse says who the warning is for. It is not addressed to the confident deniers who have been arguing throughout the sūrah. It is addressed to people who already fear the gathering, and the Book identifies its audience by that fear rather than by their religious label.

Commentators listed several groups as candidates for the description: believers who accept the resurrection but are negligent in their works, People of the Book who believe in the Day of Judgment but have not accepted this Prophet, and idolaters who carry a private unease about what comes after death. The breadth of the list is the point. The Book is not sorting people by community but by the condition of the heart.

**The Fear That Makes a Person Reachable**

The Book treats fear of the gathering as the beginning of usefulness to the message, and it states the same principle elsewhere when it tells the Prophet that he can only warn those who follow the reminder and stand in awe of the Most Compassionate without seeing Him (36:11). The phrase "without seeing Him" matters, because it describes a fear that operates in the absence of evidence at hand, and it is the form of fear the Book commends.

That is why the verse's instruction is directed at this group and not at the men who kept demanding signs. A person who has already granted that he will be summoned to account has given the message something to work with, and the Book's method with such people is to increase their awareness rather than to argue them into an admission.

**No Protector and No Intercessor**

The verse describes the scene those listeners fear, and it describes it with two denials: apart from Him there is no protector and no intercessor. The Book makes the same statement in several places, including the passage where God says that there is no protector and no intercessor besides Him (32:4), and the one where the Prophet is told to warn a people who have no one to protect them from God (42:7).

Intercession is treated carefully in the Book rather than dismissed outright. The majority position among the scholars is that intercession exists by God's permission and for those He permits, which is why the verse's denial is expressed as a denial of any intercessor besides Him rather than of intercession as such. What the sentence removes is the assumption that led the Makkans to build their religious practice around intermediaries.

**An Intercessor That Is a Text**

Jaʿfar al-Ṣādiq, the sixth Shia Imam, read the word differently and gave it a form that has been quoted widely. He said that the command means to warn by means of the Qur'an those who anticipate the meeting with their Lord, and to make them long for what awaits them there, because the Qur'an itself is an intercessor that will plead on their behalf. The interpretation is reported in the tafsīr literature and gives the verse an unexpected shape: the warning and the advocate are the same object.

Whether or not one adopts that reading, it captures something the verse carries. The Book is being described as an instrument the Prophet uses, and the people who benefit from it are the ones who let it work on them in advance of the meeting rather than at the meeting itself.

**Awaiting a Meeting in Order to Prepare for It**

The phrase the translation renders as being gathered before their Lord is literally a meeting, and the Book uses that vocabulary deliberately both for the saved and for the lost. Those who are certain that they will meet their Lord and that to Him they will return are described among the guided (2:46), while those who do not expect the meeting are described as pleased with the present life and heedless of the signs (10:7).

So the difference between the two groups is not information about the event. It is expectation, and expectation is what produces preparation. A man who is expecting an appointment arranges his affairs accordingly, and the verse assumes that the listener who expects the meeting will act on the warning he receives.

**The Closing Purpose Clause**

The verse ends with the same formula the sūrah has used for the sending of messengers: so perhaps they will be mindful of Him. The word describes guarding oneself against something, and the Book attaches it to conduct rather than to sentiment. A person who is mindful of God orders his life around the awareness that he will be questioned, and the verse presents the warning as the means by which that state is reached.

The Book repeats the purpose clause in the passage about the Prophet's own instruction, where he is told to remind because his duty is only to remind (88:21), and it uses the same word when it tells him who can be warned at all. The identical phrase appears in the sūrah of the Cave, where the Prophet is told that he can warn only those who follow the reminder and fear their Lord unseen, and that he should give them news of forgiveness and a generous reward (36:11). The verse therefore closes the discussion of the messenger's role with a description of the listener who can receive it, and the sūrah turns next to the practical question of who was being kept away from the message.

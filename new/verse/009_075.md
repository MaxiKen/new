## Sūrah al-Tawbah 9:75

> And there are some who had made a vow to Allah: “If He gives us from His bounty, we will surely spend in charity and be of the righteous.”

**Expanded Commentary**

**The Vow Sounds Righteous Before the Test Arrives**

The people described make a promise to Allah: if bounty reaches them, they will give charity and become righteous. Their words identify a condition that should not need to be there. Worship is presented as a payment for receiving wealth, as though the servant will begin obeying only after Allah proves that He can satisfy the servant's request.

A person may ask Allah for provision and promise to use it well. The danger lies in making the promise a bargain that excuses present disobedience. Righteousness is not reserved for the day of abundance. A poor person can be grateful, honest, and generous with time. If a vow is made, it increases responsibility; it does not make obedience optional until the condition arrives.

**Bounty Tests the Promise More Than Poverty Does**

Before wealth arrives, the vow costs little. It allows the speaker to imagine himself generous without releasing anything. Abundance removes the shelter of imagination. Once the money is in hand, the person must decide whether his earlier words were worship or fantasy.

This is why the servant should be careful with spiritual promises. He should not make dramatic declarations while asking for what he wants. A simple intention followed by steady obedience is safer than a grand pledge made under emotion. Allah already knows the servant's need and does not require a performance of future righteousness before granting provision.

**Charity Is a Response to Grace, Not a Price**

Charity should follow gratitude, but it cannot be used to purchase Allah's bounty. Allah gives before the servant earns the right to boast, and the gift remains Allah's property after it enters the servant's hand. Giving therefore acknowledges grace; it does not create a debt that forces Allah to give more.

The vow's language reveals a transactional heart. The servant imagines that wealth will change him automatically. Yet wealth magnifies what is already present. If there is gratitude, it gives gratitude wider reach. If there is greed, it gives greed more material. The believer asks for bounty while asking even more for the character that can carry bounty safely.

**Righteousness Is Larger Than One Donation**

The vow joins charity to being among the righteous. This is correct in direction but narrow if righteousness is reduced to money. The righteous pray, tell the truth, fulfill trusts, protect the vulnerable, and use wealth well. Charity is one sign of that life, not a substitute for it.

A person who gives while cheating, lying, or harming dependents cannot use the gift to declare himself righteous. Nor should a person with little money imagine that he is excluded from righteousness. He can give a kind word, useful knowledge, labor, or patient care. Allah sees the sacrifice in relation to what the servant possesses.

**A Conditional Promise Reveals a Conditional Allegiance**

The phrase “if He gives us” can reveal a hidden complaint: the speaker will serve only after Allah serves him in the exact way requested. True faith accepts that Allah's bounty may arrive as money, opportunity, restraint, or guidance. The servant may ask for a specific good, but he does not make obedience depend on receiving it.

This is especially important when prayer seems unanswered. A believer may grieve and continue to ask, yet he does not abandon worship because the desired outcome has not appeared. If he has made a vow, he fulfills it according to the lawful conditions and does not treat disappointment as permission to break faith. Allah's wisdom is not measured by the timing of one request.

**The Promise Should Become Action Before the Wealth**

The safest response to this verse is to begin now. Give something within present ability, practice gratitude before increase, and decide how future wealth will serve Allah without waiting for wealth to arrive. Then the promise is no longer a speech made to secure a blessing. It becomes a habit already being formed.

When bounty comes, the servant can receive it without surprise at his own generosity because generosity has been trained in smaller matters. He pays obligations, gives charity, and keeps enough to fulfill responsibilities. The vow then becomes a reminder of gratitude rather than a bargain. Allah gives from His bounty, and the servant answers by becoming more righteous, not merely more comfortable.

A servant should therefore make his vows modest and his obedience immediate. Instead of promising that future wealth will make him righteous, he can begin with the present blessing of breath, time, and ability. He can set aside a small amount, serve a neighbor, and keep a duty that no audience sees. These acts reveal whether the desire for righteousness is real. If Allah later expands his provision, the expansion increases an existing habit rather than creating a new identity. The vow then becomes a reminder to remain grateful, not a bargain that makes generosity depend on the amount of comfort received.

A vow made in hardship should therefore be treated as a responsibility, not as a bargaining chip. The servant does not say that Allah owes him prosperity because he promised to be generous. He says that if prosperity arrives, he will be grateful enough to release part of it. Even then, the vow does not make him righteous by itself. Righteousness is a continuing relation to Allah, expressed in truth, prayer, justice, and care. The best preparation for future bounty is present obedience, because the heart that is trained before increase is less likely to become a prisoner of increase.

## Sūrah al-Naḥl 16:58

> **Whenever one of them is given the good news of a baby girl, his face grows gloomy, as he suppresses his rage.**

**Expanded Commentary**

**Good News That Is Received As Bad News**

Wa-idhā bushshira aḥaduhum bi-l-unthā ẓalla wajhuhu muswaddan — and when one of them is given good news of female, his face becomes dark, gloomy. Bushshira from b-sh-r means to give good news, glad tidings, from same root as bashar, human, and bushra, good news. Bi-l-unthā, of female, baby girl. Ẓalla wajhuhu muswaddan, his face remains black, dark, gloomy, from ẓ-l-l, to remain, and s-w-d, black, dark. Al-Ṭabarī explains that Arabs considered birth of daughter as bad news, though linguistically bushshira, good news, is used ironically: it is called good news but received as bad. When told of baby girl, his face becomes dark with gloom, sadness, anger, shame. Al-Qurṭubī says muswaddan indicates face darkens due to distress, not literal blackness, but gloom that shows. This was common in pre-Islamic Arabia where daughters were considered burden, shame, especially due to poverty and fear of capture in tribal wars.

**Suppressing Rage**

Wa-huwa kaẓīm — while he suppresses rage, is full of anger, suppressing. Kaẓīm from k-ẓ-m means to suppress, hold in, restrain anger, from same root as kāẓim al-ghayẓ, one who suppresses anger. Al-Ṭabarī says kaẓīm means he suppresses anger, grief, distress, holding it inside, not expressing openly, but face shows. Al-Rāzī explains that kaẓīm indicates he is full of anger and sorrow but suppresses, trying to hide, yet face betrays. The combination ẓalla wajhuhu muswaddan wa-huwa kaẓīm paints vivid picture: face gloomy, dark, while inside he suppresses rage, anger at being given daughter. This is psychological portrait of gender bias: outward gloom, inward rage, both due to cultural devaluation of daughters.

**Why Daughters Were Disliked**

Pre-Islamic Arabs disliked daughters for several reasons: fear of poverty, as daughters considered economic burden; fear of shame, as daughters might be captured in war and bring dishonor; desire for sons who would be warriors and carry name. The Qur'an critiques this bias as evil judgment, as verse 59 says. Al-Qurṭubī notes that dislike of daughters was not universal among Arabs; some tribes honored daughters, but among those who worshipped angels as daughters of Allah, dislike was pronounced, making attribution to Allah even more contradictory: you attribute to Allah what you consider bad news for yourself. The verse exposes contradiction: you consider daughter as bad news for you, yet you say angels, whom you consider daughters of Allah, are worthy of worship and intercession.

**The Irony Of Bushshira**

Bushshira, given good news, is used for birth of child, which in Islam is good news regardless of gender, as angels gave good news of daughters and sons to prophets. The Qur'an says *We give good news of a boy* and *good news of a girl* both as bushra. Here bushshira is used for baby girl, indicating that birth of girl is in reality good news, blessing, but receiver perceives as bad due to bias. The irony is intentional: what is truly good news is received as bad due to corrupted values. Al-Zamakhsharī notes that using bushshira for baby girl shows that in divine view, daughter is good news, blessing, not bad, and that receiver's gloom is due to his own corrupted perception, not due to daughter being bad.

**Connection To Attribution Of Daughters To Allah**

Verse 57 said they attribute daughters to Allah, glory be to Him, while for themselves what they desire, sons. Verse 58 shows what they desire for themselves: when given good news of daughter, face gloomy, suppressing rage. So they desire sons, hate daughters. Yet they attribute daughters to Allah. This is insult to Allah, as verse 57's subḥānahu indicated. The sūrah thus exposes that their theology is linked to social bias: they give Allah what they hate. The next verse will describe their decision: hide from people, keep in disgrace or bury alive, evil judgment.

**Linguistic Note On Unthā And Kaẓīm**

Unthā from a-n-th means female, woman, from same root as anīth, soft. Bushshira from b-sh-r, to give good news. Ẓalla from ẓ-l-l, to remain, become. Wajhuhu, his face, from w-j-h, face. Muswaddan from s-w-d, black, dark, from same root as sawād, blackness. Kaẓīm from k-ẓ-m, to suppress, hold in, from same root as kiẓm, anger suppressed. The verse's structure — when one is given good news of female, his face remains dark while he suppresses rage — with idhā for when, bushshira for good news, ẓalla wajhuhu muswaddan for face gloomy, wa-huwa kaẓīm for suppressing rage, creates vivid, empathetic portrait of gender bias that is both personal and social, showing how bias affects face and heart.

**How This Verse Critiques Gender Bias**

The verse critiques gender bias by describing its outward and inward signs: face gloomy, rage suppressed, hiding from people, deciding whether to keep in disgrace or bury alive. It does not endorse bias but exposes as evil judgment. The Qur'an elsewhere honors daughters: *Wealth and children are adornment of worldly life* (18:46) includes daughters as adornment, and Prophet ﷺ said *Whoever has three daughters and is patient with them and clothes them from his wealth, they will be shield for him from Fire* (Ibn Mājah 3669). The verse therefore calls to value daughters as good news, not bad, and to not attribute to Allah what you hate for yourself, but to attribute to Allah finest attributes as verse 60 says.

**A Lesson On Receiving News**

The verse teaches how to receive news: birth of child, male or female, is good news, blessing from Allah, as verse 72 will say Allah gave you children and grandchildren as provision. To receive good news of daughter with gloomy face and suppressed rage is to be ungrateful for blessing, as verse 53 said blessings from Allah. The believer should receive news of daughter with joy, gratitude, recognizing it as blessing from Allah who gives whatever He wills, as 42:49 says *To Allah belongs dominion of heavens and earth, He creates what He wills, He gives to whom He wills females and gives to whom He wills males* (42:49). The verse therefore calls to gratitude for daughters as well as sons.

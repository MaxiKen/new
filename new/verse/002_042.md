## Sūrah al-Baqarah 2:42

> Do not mix truth with falsehood or hide the truth knowingly.

**Expanded Commentary**

**The Charge Is Adulteration, Not Denial**

The Arabic verb in the first clause carries the sense of clothing one thing with another, so that what is visible is no longer what is underneath. Its use here describes the blending of true and false in a single body of teaching, and the offence it names is a more careful crime than simple denial. Nobody is accused of renouncing the scripture; the accusation is that the scripture is being presented in a form that no longer says what it means.

The Book makes the same charge elsewhere with the same verb, and the translators render it either as mixing or confusing depending on the nuance being carried across. What remains constant is the concept: a message whose true content has been covered by an addition, so that the reader cannot separate the two and does not know that a separation is needed.

**Concealment Named as a Separate Act**

The second clause states a related offence in different language: hiding the truth deliberately. The Book treats the concealment of revelation as a breach of a specific undertaking, and it says so in a passage addressed to those who received scripture earlier: *“˹Remember, O Prophet,˺ when Allah took the covenant of those who were given the Scripture to make it known to people and not hide it, yet they cast it behind their backs and traded it for a fleeting gain.”* (3:187).

That covenant is the context for this verse, and it explains why the two clauses belong together. Concealment is the negative act — a part of the truth is removed from view — while mixing is the positive one, in which something else is put in its place. A teacher who does either has changed what his students receive, and the Book holds him responsible for the change regardless of how sincerely he may believe in it.

**"Knowingly": The Word That Removes the Excuse**

The final word of the sentence fixes the mental state of the offender. The Book draws the line between error and guilt at the point of knowledge, and it does so consistently: a person who misstates something he does not know is not the subject of this verse, while a person who knows what the text says and presents it otherwise is.

That distinction has consequences for how the passage should be read by any reader. The Book does not forbid teaching, interpreting or disagreeing; it forbids the deliberate alteration of a message one has been trusted with. The qualifier also protects honest scholarship from the accusation, since the offence requires knowledge of the truth being displaced, and no court of human beings can determine that condition. The verse is addressed to conscience.

**What the Community Was Concealing**

The reports connect the charge with the description of the coming prophet that this community's scholars were said to find in their own books, and with the clarity of the signs that identified him. Without adjudicating the details, the sūrah's context makes the general charge plain enough: the revelation in Madinah had come to a community that possessed earlier scripture, and the argument of these verses is that the new message matches what the old one contained.

The Book states the same claim about itself everywhere it addresses this community, and it always phrases the claim as confirmation rather than supersession. What the verse adds is that the possession of scripture is not a neutral fact. A community that holds a text holds a message, and a message belongs to the people it was sent to, not to the scholars who administer it.

**The Liability of Those Who Know**

The Book's harshest passages are reserved for the knowledgeable who mislead, and this verse is one of them. It is placed in the middle of a series of instructions that will include prayer, almsgiving, humility and patience, and it belongs to a category of offence that has no equivalent in ordinary criminal law. Nothing physically changes hands when a text is misrepresented; what changes is what other people will believe, and the Book treats that as the substance of the harm.

The sūrah will return to this theme with a question that sharpens it further, asking its audience how they can instruct others in righteousness while neglecting it themselves. The two verses work as a pair. One addresses the content of what is taught, the other the conduct of the teacher, and together they describe a community whose relationship to its own scripture has come apart.

**The Clothing Metaphor and What It Implies**

The metaphor buried in the verse's first verb is worth stating plainly. Clothing exists to cover, and a person who puts truth inside falsehood has decided that the truth needs covering. The Book's image suggests a deliberateness about the act that the rest of the sentence confirms, and it also suggests what the offender knows: nobody dresses something he values in something contemptible by accident.

That is why the two clauses are joined by a single command rather than presented as alternatives. Mixing and concealing are two ways of managing a truth that has become inconvenient, and the Book's remedy is stated in the same sentence form as everything else in this passage — a prohibition addressed to people whose position allowed them to confuse others. What the verse asks of them is the opposite of what it describes: let the truth stand where it can be seen.

**The Cost of a Managed Text**

A community that manages its scripture pays in a currency it does not immediately notice. Teaching that has been adjusted to fit a position produces students who cannot test the position, and the loss becomes visible only when a claim arrives that the adjusted text cannot evaluate. The sūrah's own audience is being addressed at exactly that moment, and the Book's argument to them assumes they still have access to what was written.

That is why the verse presents the offence as a breach of trust rather than a doctrinal error. The Book claims the ability to be checked, describing itself as a clear Book and inviting its hearers to compare it with what they already possess. Mixing and concealing make the comparison impossible, and the loss falls on the people who are prevented from making it rather than on the text.

## Sūrah al-Baqarah 2:42

> Do not mix truth with falsehood or hide the truth knowingly.

**Expanded Commentary**

**Two Crimes Against the Handling of Truth**

The verse prohibits two operations, and the pairing is a complete taxonomy of how truth is defeated without being openly denied. *Wa-lā talbisū al-ḥaqqa bi-l-bāṭil* — do not confound, clothe, or mix the truth with the false — attacks truth by adulteration: the genuine article is presented alongside, wrapped in, or diluted with its counterfeit, so that the consumer cannot separate them and eventually discounts both. *Wa-lā taktumū al-ḥaqqa* — do not conceal the truth — attacks it by suppression: the genuine article is withheld from the market entirely. Between mixing and hiding lies every method by which a scholar, a court, or a press can defeat a truth it is forbidden to contradict outright. The verse's two prohibitions are thus not redundant but complementary halves of one crime scene: where open denial is dangerous — before a community that reveres the truth, as Madina's rabbis stood before their own scripture — the professional alternatives are precisely these two. The Qur'an names them the way a law names offenses: specifically, and in the order of subtlety.

**Labasa: The Confusion That Is a Costume**

The first verb carries a physical metaphor its English renderings flatten. *Labisa* means to wear, to clothe — and so to cover, to disguise, to obscure. The confounding of truth with falsehood is, in the word's own image, an act of dressing: falsehood fitted with truth's garments, or truth dressed down in falsehood's, until the pair walk indistinguishable. The Qur'an uses the same verb-family for this operation wherever scholars of prior communities are addressed: *"O People of the Book! Why do you mix the truth with falsehood and hide the truth knowingly?"* (3:71) — the identical pair of charges, in identical order, repeated to identical addressees in a later sūrah, which fixes the reading here. The clothing metaphor also explains the danger's mechanics: a lie told naked is refutable; a lie wearing truth's coat — a fabricated report inside a genuine chain, a distorted gloss beside a sound text, a true verse cited for a false conclusion — infects the credibility of everything it stands beside. Adulteration does not merely add falsehood; it degrades the currency of truth itself.

**Wearing the Knowledge: The Aggravating Clause**

Both prohibitions close on the same two words: *wa-antum taʿlamūn* — while you know, knowingly. The circumstantial clause is the verse's aggravator, and it locates the crime in a specific class. These are not errors committed in ignorance; they are operations conducted by people whose knowledge is the instrument of the offense. The clause echoes verse 22's prohibition — do not set up equals *knowingly* — and the echo draws the parallel the sūrah intends: there, the furnished world made the idolater's knowledge inescapable; here, the studied scripture makes the scholar's inescapable. Expertise multiplies responsibility in this verse's arithmetic, and the multiplication explains the coming severity of the recital. The communities addressed had not failed for lack of light but by managing the light — which is the precise difference, in the Qur'an's moral geography, between the astray and the knowers-who-concealed, between *ḍalāl* and *kitmān*.

**Kitmān: The Scholar's Named Offense**

Concealment is serious enough in the Qur'an to have a covenant against it. The Book records Allah taking from those given the Scripture a standing pledge — *to make it known to people and not hide it* — and records the breach in a single image: *they cast it behind their backs and traded it for a fleeting gain* (3:187). The scholar in that verse is not a private believer but a trustee: knowledge held on behalf of a community, deliverable on demand. *Kitmān* converts the trust into private property and the property into merchandise, which is why the two crimes of verse 42 flow into the sale-prohibition of verse 41 as one continuous economy — the hidden sign is the sign already priced. Islamic law built directly on this material: the obligation of the qualified to answer when asked, the prohibition of selling fatwās, the sin of silence when silence misleads. The verse is short; the jurisprudence of knowledge it generated is not, and every branch of that jurisprudence grows from the single distinction it installs — that truth in a knower's hands is a deposit, not an asset.

**Why Mixing Is Listed Before Hiding**

The order of the two prohibitions is deliberate and diagnostically useful. Mixing precedes hiding because mixing is hiding's camouflage: a community that watches its scholars blend truth with falsehood stops demanding the unblended article, and the concealment completes itself unnoticed. Adulteration also does more damage per act. The hidden truth has enemies; the adulterated truth has counterfeit defenders — people who believe they hold it and will fight for the mixture. The Qur'an's own history-of-religions reading of prior communities follows the order: gloss accumulated around revelation first, suppression of inconvenient portions second, until the book and its keepers could no longer be cross-checked against each other. For the individual reader the sequence is equally practical: the person who begins by softening one truth for one audience — mixing, in small denomination — will find the wholesale concealment of verse 42's second clause already prepared as the path of least resistance. The verse lists the two crimes in the order they are committed.

**The Positive Command Inside the Double Negative**

A prohibition of this precision implies its positive twin, and the Qur'an states the twin explicitly where the same covenant is recalled: make it known, and hide it not (3:71; 3:187). The scholar's duty is not merely clean hands but an open mouth — declaration, teaching, publication of what is known when it is needed. Verse 42 read alone could produce a quietist honesty that never mixes and never hides but also never speaks; read with its covenant-verses, it demands the active side: truth unmixed, unconcealed, *and delivered*. The verse thereby protects a community from two failures at once — corrupt teachers and silent honest ones — and it arms ordinary believers against both, since a deposit of knowledge withheld on demand is itself the named offense. In the sūrah's argument with Madina, the charge has a concrete edge: the description of the coming Prophet stood in their texts, known to their scholars, recited in their circles — and the community was being left without it. The verse names that omission as a crime with a plaintiff: the truth itself, held in trust, uncloaked and unspoken.

## Sūrah al-Tawbah 9:122

> ˹However,˺ it is not necessary for the believers to march forth all at once. Only a party from each group should march forth, leaving the rest to gain religious knowledge then enlighten their people when they return to them, so that they ˹too˺ may beware ˹of evil˺.

**Expanded Commentary**

**The Community Needs More Than One Kind of Service**

The verse corrects the idea that every believer must be physically present in every campaign. Some march, while others remain to gain religious knowledge and teach their people. Both roles serve the community. Courage in the field and patient learning in the gathering are complementary responsibilities rather than competing claims to virtue.

This balance prevents a community from losing its teachers whenever a crisis appears. It also prevents scholars from treating study as a refuge from every communal duty. The division of labor is deliberate: some protect the community's immediate safety, while others protect its understanding and long-term direction.

The verse presents learning as a responsibility that must be carried by a group. Not everyone can master every field, but every community needs people who devote themselves to sound knowledge. These people learn deeply, distinguish revelation from custom, and return to explain what they have understood.

This is not permission for a small class to monopolize religion. The knowledgeable teach, and the wider community listens, asks, and acts. Learning becomes a trust rather than a badge. A scholar who refuses to communicate has not completed the responsibility described here, and a listener who refuses to learn cannot shift all responsibility to the scholar.

**Teaching Must Return to the People**

Those who gain understanding enlighten their people when they return. Knowledge is not complete when it remains in notes, private conversation, or personal admiration. It must reach the people whose choices can be improved by it. Teaching translates understanding into guidance that families and communities can use.

The return also keeps the learner connected to ordinary life. A person who studies only among experts may forget the questions and pressures faced by workers, parents, and new believers. Coming back requires him to explain clearly, listen carefully, and make the knowledge relevant without weakening its truth.

**Warning Includes Hope and Caution**

The purpose of teaching is that people may beware. Warning alerts them to evil and its consequences, but it also gives them the opportunity to choose safety. A teacher should not use fear to display authority. He explains what harms faith, character, and society, then shows the path of repentance and good action.

The warning must be proportionate and evidence-based. Not every disagreement is a danger, and not every unfamiliar practice is evil. The knowledgeable person distinguishes a clear prohibition from a matter of legitimate debate. Caution protects the community when it is joined to mercy and understanding.



A healthy community allows people to develop different gifts. Some are suited to travel and defense, some to teaching, some to healing, administration, agriculture, or care. The verse gives religious learning a central place among these gifts because every other service needs moral direction.

Specialization does not create hierarchy of human worth. The one who teaches is not automatically better than the one who carries supplies. Each role is judged by sincerity and benefit. The community honors the different burdens and makes cooperation possible instead of forcing every person into the same public form of service.

**Learning Requires Movement and Patience**

The verse can also imply leaving familiar surroundings to gain understanding. Serious learning may require travel, time, and the humility to sit with a teacher. A person who wants immediate answers may collect slogans without acquiring wisdom. The learner accepts a season of study so that future teaching will not be built on guesswork.

At the same time, study cannot become an endless postponement of action. The learner takes what he knows and teaches it responsibly while continuing to grow. Knowledge is a trust carried in stages. The community benefits when its scholars remain learners and its learners become useful teachers.

The verse ends with collective awareness. People who understand religion help their people notice the paths that lead to harm before those paths become normal. They explain obligations, expose manipulation, and make repentance accessible. A watchful community is not a suspicious community; it is one that sees clearly and responds wisely.

The division of labor therefore becomes an act of mercy. Some go forth, some learn, and all benefit when each role is honored. The community remains strong when it can defend its people today while educating them for tomorrow.

The division of roles also teaches leaders not to praise only the visible sacrifice. A community that honors the traveler but neglects the teacher will eventually lose the knowledge that tells it where to travel. A community that praises study but refuses to protect its people will lose the freedom in which study can flourish. The verse calls for mutual respect. Those who gain understanding return as servants, not as a separate elite, and those who march recognize that their sacrifice is strengthened by people who preserve guidance.

The teacher's return also demands humility about the limits of his knowledge. He distinguishes what is certain from what is debated and does not turn personal preference into a divine command. He listens to the people he serves because their questions reveal where explanation is needed. In this way, learning becomes a cycle: the community sends people to understand, the learners return to teach, and the people's real needs deepen the next stage of study. The whole body grows when knowledge travels back to the people.

The result should be a people who can recognize evil before it spreads and good before it is forgotten.

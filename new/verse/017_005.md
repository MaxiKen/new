## Sūrah al-Isrāʾ 17:5

> **“When the first of the two warnings would come to pass, We would send against you some of Our servants of great might, who would ravage your homes. This would be a warning fulfilled.**

**Expanded Commentary**

**First Warning Fulfilled Via Servants Of Great Might**

Fa-idhā jā'a waʿdu ūlāhumā baʿathnā ʿalaykum ʿibādan lanā ulī ba'sin shadīdin fa-jāsū khilāla al-diyār — then when first of two warnings came to pass, We sent against you servants of Ours of great might who ravaged through homes. Fa-idhā, then when, indicates time of first corruption. Jā'a waʿdu ūlāhumā, first warning came to pass, meaning first time they corrupted, promise of punishment came true. Baʿathnā ʿalaykum, We sent against you, from b-ʿ-th, to send, raise. ʿIbādan lanā, servants of Ours, meaning people, soldiers, who are servants in sense of being under Allah's control, not necessarily believers, but used as instrument. Ulī ba'sin shadīdin, possessors of great might, severe violence, from b-ʾ-s, might, and sh-d-d, severe. Fa-jāsū khilāla al-diyār, they ravaged through homes, searched, went through houses, from j-w-s, to go through, search, ravage, and diyār, homes, houses.

**Who Were Servants Of Great Might**

Al-Ṭabarī reports various opinions: some say they were Nebuchadnezzar and his army who destroyed Jerusalem and Temple and enslaved Israelites; others say they were people of Iraq, or Persians, or others. The exact identity is not crucial; what matters is they were ulī ba'sin shadīdin, of great might, severe, who ravaged homes, showing that first corruption was punished by foreign invasion that destroyed homes and Temple. Al-Qurṭubī says first punishment was Nebuchadnezzar's destruction of Jerusalem in 586 BCE, when Temple destroyed and Israelites taken to Babylon. This is historical event that matches description: servants of great might ravaging homes. The phrase ʿibādan lanā, servants of Ours, indicates that even disbelieving invaders are under Allah's control and used as punishment, as 8:17 says *You did not kill them but Allah killed them* (8:17), showing divine use of human agents.

**Ravaging Through Homes**

Fa-jāsū khilāla al-diyār — they went through homes, ravaged, searched. Jāsū from j-w-s means to go through, to search, to ravage, to move through with force. Khilāla al-diyār, through homes, between houses, indicating they entered homes, searched, destroyed, showing total defeat and humiliation. Al-Rāzī explains that ravaging through homes indicates that punishment reached private spaces, not only public, showing that corruption that was private and public both punished. The Israelites who corrupted in land and became arrogant had their private homes ravaged, showing that arrogance that made them feel secure in homes was broken.

**Warning Fulfilled**

Wa-kāna waʿdan mafʿūlan — and it was warning, promise fulfilled, done. Waʿd, promise, warning. Mafʿūl, done, fulfilled, from f-ʿ-l, to do. Al-Ṭabarī says this means first warning, promise of punishment for first corruption, was fulfilled, done, as decreed. The phrase indicates that divine warnings are not empty threats; they are fulfilled when corruption occurs. The same applies to second warning and to warnings to Makkans: if they corrupt and become arrogant, warning will be fulfilled. The phrase also shows that divine decree is certain: when condition of corruption occurs, punishment follows as fulfillment.

**Why First Corruption Punished Via Foreign Invasion**

First corruption of Israelites was punished via foreign invasion because corruption was in land and involved arrogance and oppression, so punishment came via people of great might who would ravage homes, showing that might they boasted was overcome by greater might. Al-Qurṭubī notes that using foreign invaders as punishment is divine law: when community corrupts, Allah sends against them others who are stronger, to humble them. This happened to Israelites twice, and happened to Makkans at Badr where they were defeated by believers who were fewer but aided by Allah. The lesson is that might belongs to Allah, not to arrogant.

**Connection To Night Journey And Blessed Surroundings**

First warning involved ravaging homes in blessed land whose surroundings We blessed as verse 1 said. The blessed land was blessed with fertility and prophets, but when its people corrupted, blessings did not prevent punishment; instead, homes in blessed land were ravaged. This shows that being in blessed land does not guarantee security; obedience does. The Israelites were in blessed land, yet when they corrupted, they were punished in that land. The Makkans were in Sacred Mosque, yet if they corrupt, they will be punished. The Night Journey from Sacred Mosque to Farthest Mosque in blessed surroundings therefore is reminder that sacred places require gratitude and obedience, not corruption and arrogance.

**Linguistic Note On Ba's And Jaws**

Ba's from b-ʾ-s means might, strength, violence, from same root as ba'sā', hardship. Shadīd from sh-d-d means severe, strong. Ulī ba'sin shadīdin, possessors of severe might, indicates great military power. Jāsū from j-w-s means to go through, search, ravage, from same root as jāsūs, spy, one who goes through. Khilāla from kh-l-l, through, between. Diyār from d-w-r, homes, houses, from same root as dār, home. Waʿdan mafʿūlan, promise fulfilled, from w-ʿ-d, promise, and f-ʿ-l, to do. The verse's structure — when first warning came, We sent against you servants of great might who ravaged homes, it was promise fulfilled — with time clause, divine sending, description of agents, action, and fulfillment, creates historical account that is both specific and universal, showing that first corruption was punished and warning fulfilled.

**How This Verse Teaches About Divine Law**

This verse teaches that divine law for corruption is consistent: when community corrupts and becomes arrogant, Allah sends against them servants of great might who ravage homes, as punishment and as fulfillment of warning. The law applies to Israelites and to all, including Makkans. The verse also teaches that even servants of great might who are used as punishment are servants of Allah, under His control, showing that all power belongs to Him. The believer should therefore not fear people of great might besides Allah, but fear Allah alone, as previous chapter said be in awe of Me alone and to Him belongs whatever in heavens and earth. The practical lesson is to avoid corruption and arrogance, to be grateful for being in blessed land or sacred mosque, and to know that warnings are fulfilled if corruption persists.

## Sūrah al-Anʿām 6:115

> The Word of your Lord has been perfected in truth and justice. None can change His Words. And He is the All-Hearing, All- Knowing.

**Expanded Commentary**

**The Perfected Word**

The Word of your Lord has been perfected in truth and justice. The verse states that God's word has reached its completeness, and it names the two qualities in which that completeness consists. The Book uses the same verbal form in the sūrah of Hūd, where it says that the word of the Lord will be fulfilled, and in the sūrah of Yūnus, where it says that the decree of the Lord has been proven true against the rebellious (10:33). What the verse in front of us states is the finished character of the revelation rather than its arrival at a future point.

The two qualities named here cover the two sides of what a revealed Book contains. Truth belongs to what the Book says about God, the unseen, the past, and the reckoning; justice belongs to what it commands and forbids. The sūrah has been treating both throughout, and the sentence that gathers them states that neither side is defective.

**Truth and Justice Together**

The pair of terms describes the Book as both accurate and fair, and the Book uses the two together in other passages about its own character and about the conduct it requires. The sūrah of the Table says that God revealed the Book in truth and that it guides to a straight path (5:16 context), and the sūrah of the Troops says that God revealed the Book to the Prophet with the truth for humanity (39:41). The sūrah of the Cattle itself commands the Prophet to judge with justice in other passages.

The combination matters for the audience the sūrah is addressing. People who questioned whether the message was true were being told that its reports are accurate, and people who found its commands burdensome were being told that its rulings are just. The verse states both as features of the Word itself rather than as claims made on its behalf.

**None Can Change His Words**

None can change His Words. The sentence states the security of the revelation, and the Book repeats it in several places. The sūrah of the Cave commands the Prophet to recite what has been revealed to him from the Book of his Lord and states that none can change His words, nor can any refuge be found besides Him (18:27). The sūrah of Yūnus declares that there is no change in the promise of God and that this is the great triumph (10:64), and the sūrah of the Cattle earlier says that none can change the words of God and states that messengers before the Prophet endured rejection until God's help came (6:34).

The Book sets its own preservation against what it says happened to the earlier scriptures. The sūrah of the Table states that the People of the Book distorted the words of the Scripture and forgot a portion of what they had been reminded of (5:13); the sūrah of the Cow pronounces woe on those who write the Scripture with their own hands and then say that it is from God, seeking a fleeting gain (2:79); and the sūrah of the Women records those who take words out of their context and alter them (4:46). The sentence in this verse is the contrast to all of that.

**The Two Names That Close the Verse**

And He is the All-Hearing, All-Knowing. The two names correspond to the two qualities named at the beginning of the verse: the One who hears what is said and knows what is done is the One whose word is true and whose commands are just. The Book attaches this pair to statements about decrees and knowledge in many places, and its use here ties the perfection of the revelation to the attributes of the One who sent it.

The names also answer the situation of the Prophet among a people who argued with him and denied his message. Nothing that they said escaped the One who hears, and nothing about their condition was hidden from the One who knows, which is the same ground the sūrah established when it described God's knowledge of the leaf that falls and the grain in the darkness (6:59).

**What the Verse Adds to the Passage**

The verse closes the passage that began with the demand for a judge and the testimony of those given the Scripture (6:114). Having established that the Book is fully explained and that the earlier communities recognise its origin, the sūrah states that the word itself is complete, incorruptible, and grounded in the attributes of the One who sent it. The dispute about the message is therefore not a dispute about a defective or partial revelation.

The Book makes the same claim about the guidance it contains in the passages about its own preservation, and the sūrah will return to that theme. What the verse provides here is the framework for the instructions that follow, in which the Prophet is told not to follow the majority of the people on earth and is told that his Lord knows best who has strayed and who is guided, so that the reader can weigh the crowd against the Book.

**The Transition to the Next Verses**

The next verse says that if the Prophet were to obey most of those on earth, they would lead him away from the way of God, since they follow nothing but assumption and do nothing but lie (6:116), and the verse after it states that God knows best who has strayed and who is guided (6:117). The two verses take up the question of authority from the side of numbers, and the Book answers it by pointing again to the knowledge of the One whose word has been perfected.

The connection between the two passages is direct. The Book has just been shown to be the arbiter, complete in truth and justice, and the sūrah now states that the majority of people are following conjecture. The answer to the crowd is the Book, and the answer to the Book's opponents is the One who hears and knows.

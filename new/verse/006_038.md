## Sūrah al-Anʿām 6:38

> All living beings roaming the earth and winged birds soaring in the sky are communities like yourselves. We have left nothing out of the Record. Then to their Lord they will be gathered all together.

**Expanded Commentary**

**"Communities Like Yourselves"**

The word translated as communities is *ummah*, a term the Book normally uses for religious communities formed by a shared relationship to God — the believing community addressed as the best community brought forth for humanity (3:110), and every community having a messenger sent to it (10:47). Applying the word to birds and crawling things widens its range in a way that has no parallel in the sūrah, and the choice of vocabulary is the claim.

The verse does not say that animals resemble human beings in appearance or intelligence. It says they form communities, which in the Book's usage means groups defined by the way they stand before their Creator, and the following verses elsewhere make that relationship explicit. What is asserted here is that human beings are not the only organised creatures with a standing before God.

**What the Rest of the Book Says About Them**

That reading is not an inference from a single word. The Book reports that God revealed to the bees how to build their hives and produce their honey, and it calls the product a healing for people (16:68–69); it states that everything in the heavens and the earth glorifies God, and that human beings do not understand their glorification (17:44); and it records that the mountains and the birds joined David in praise (38:18–19). The same claim appears where all creatures in the heavens and the earth are said to declare His praise (24:41).

Some commentators concluded from such passages that at least some animals are aware of God, while others held that the description in this verse is simply a way of saying they are God's creatures. The first reading accounts better for the term community and for the glorification passages, and it is the reading the verse itself invites by joining the community claim to the gathering.

**"We Have Left Nothing Out of the Record"**

The middle clause places every creature inside a written account. Elsewhere the Book describes the Preserved Tablet (85:22), and it says that with God are the keys of the unseen, that He knows what is in the land and the sea, and that not a leaf falls without His knowledge, nor a grain in the darkness of the earth, but is written in a perfect Record (6:59).

The clause also answers a practical worry. A person who looks at the world sees enormous suffering among creatures that have no voice and no account: animals killed for no purpose, birds whose nests are destroyed, beasts that are hunted and eaten. The verse states that the record is complete and that no creature is outside it, which is the Book's way of saying that nothing in the creation lives or dies unnoticed.

**Gathered to Their Lord**

Then to their Lord they will be gathered all together. The verb is the one used for the resurrection of human beings, and it is also used of the Day when the wild beasts will be gathered (81:5). Commentators differed over what the gathering of animals means, with some holding that it refers to their resurrection and judgment and others that it means they will be gathered to their deaths. The reports that are authentic on the subject support the first reading in substance.

The most cited is transmitted from Abū Hurayrah in Ṣaḥīḥ Muslim 2582, where the Prophet said that rights will certainly be restored to those entitled to them on the Day of Resurrection, to the point that even the hornless sheep will lay claim upon the horned one. The same report appears in Jāmiʿ al-Tirmidhī 2420, where Abū ʿĪsā grades it ḥasan ṣaḥīḥ. What the hadith establishes is that the divine reckoning extends to wrongs that no human court would ever consider.

**What the Verse Requires of Human Beings**

If all creatures are communities with a relationship to their Creator, then the permission human beings have to use animals for food, transport, and labour is a delegation with terms rather than an unrestricted title. The Book does say that the earth and its contents have been subjected to human beings (14:32; 16:14; 22:65), and the same Book says that every creature's provision rests with God (11:6) and that there is no creature on earth whose provision God does not guarantee (29:60).

Those two sets of verses were not treated as contradictory by the scholars who read them together; they were treated as the terms of a trust. The report about the hornless sheep makes the same point from the other direction, since cruelty and waste in the use of creatures are named in the Book among the things that will be answered for.

**Two Ways the Commentators Took It**

Ibn Kathīr reported the disagreement about the gathering, recording the view that animals will be resurrected and judged and the view that they are gathered to their death. Sufyān ibn ʿUyaynah, as reported in al-Rāzī, read the beasts and birds as figures for different moral types among human beings, so that the verse becomes a description of people who have come to resemble the animals in their habits.

The second reading has the disadvantage of turning a plain statement about creatures into an allegory, and it leaves the term community without its meaning. The first keeps the verse where it stands, and it explains why the passage appears exactly here, between an argument about signs and a series of warnings about the judgment. The Book follows the smaller creature to its accounting in order to make the larger one unavoidable.

**The Argument From the Smaller to the Greater**

The placement of the verse does the arguing. The audience had been told that the dead will be returned to their Lord (6:36) and that those who deny the meeting will discover their loss (6:31). To that claim the passage now adds that the accounting reaches even the birds, whose lives nobody had ever thought to keep an account of.

If God records the fall of a leaf and the claim of a sheep against another sheep, then the human being who believes his own deeds will go unchecked has misjudged the scale of the administration he is living inside. The verse ends on the gathering because that is the fact the sūrah has been establishing: nobody with a Lord is without an appointment.

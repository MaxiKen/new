## Sūrah Fāṭir 35:32

> Then We granted the Book to those We have chosen from Our servants. Some of them wrong themselves, some follow a middle course, and some are foremost in good deeds by Allah’s Will. That is ˹truly˺ the greatest bounty.

**Expanded Commentary**

**Receiving the Book Is a Divine Bounty**

The verse describes the Book as something Allah grants to chosen servants. The language makes revelation a gift before it becomes a responsibility. No community can claim that it discovered guidance by its own cleverness. Allah selected people to receive, preserve, teach, and live by the Book, then called them to answer for how they carried that trust.

Choice here should not be turned into a license for ethnic pride or automatic salvation. The Book is granted to a community so that its members may be guided, corrected, and judged by it. A person can possess the text and still wrong himself by neglecting its commands. The bounty lies in access to truth and a path of return, not in a label that excuses conduct.

**The Three Groups Describe Different Responses**

The verse names three kinds of people among the recipients. Some wrong themselves, some take a middle course, and some are foremost in good deeds. The classification acknowledges moral difference within the believing community. It does not flatten every recipient into a single level, nor does it make the existence of sinners proof that the Book failed.

Those who wrong themselves may be believers who neglect duties, commit sins, or allow desire to outrun obedience. Their wrong returns to them because they damage their own souls. Those who follow a middle course fulfill obligations and avoid major wrongdoing but do not consistently race toward voluntary excellence. The foremost combine duty with eagerness, sacrifice, and initiative in good. Only Allah knows the complete place of each person, and the categories should prompt self-examination rather than casual labeling.

**Allah’s Will and Human Effort Belong Together**

The foremost are foremost by Allah's will. This phrase protects them from pride and corrects the idea that spiritual excellence is self-produced. Their effort is real: they choose, strive, endure, and give. Yet the ability to recognize good, love it, and persist in it is itself a gift. A person who reaches a high station should thank Allah rather than despise those still struggling.

Divine will also gives hope to the one who has wronged himself. A low condition is not a permanent identity while repentance remains open. Allah can move a person from negligence to steadiness and from steadiness to excellence. The three categories are a map of responses, not a prophecy that a particular individual can never change. The servant acts with urgency and attributes every improvement to Allah's enabling grace.

**The Book Remains Present Across the Range**

One striking feature of the verse is that all three groups are described in relation to the Book. Those who wrong themselves have not escaped its reach; their sin is measured by a guidance they received. The middle group uses it as a stable rule, and the foremost embody its highest demands. Revelation is therefore not only for the already perfect. It accompanies the sinner toward repentance, the steady toward deeper obedience, and the excellent toward greater sincerity.

This also guards against despair within the community. A person may see his weakness and assume that he has no place among the recipients of divine bounty. The verse acknowledges weakness without making it the final word. It calls the wrongdoer to stop wronging himself and invites the middling to advance. At the same time, it warns the excellent that their rank depends on Allah's will and cannot be converted into superiority over other servants.

The categories also teach how to speak about religious rank. The foremost should not despise the one who struggles, because his own excellence is by Allah's leave and can be lost through pride. The one who follows a middle course should not be satisfied with minimum safety when greater good is possible. The one who wrongs himself should not turn shame into a new form of disobedience by giving up on repentance. Each group has a next step. The sinner repairs and returns, the steady servant deepens and gives more, and the excellent servant protects his sincerity. This movement is part of the bounty of the Book. It does not merely announce a fixed verdict; it teaches people how to travel. The community needs all these reminders because human states change. A person can be foremost in one season and negligent in another, or weak at first and later become a source of good. Allah's will does not erase effort; it makes effort fruitful. The chosen recipients are therefore asked to carry the Book with humility, to welcome correction, and to remember that the greatest gift is not the title of being chosen but the ability to become faithful to what was given.

**The Greatest Bounty Is Guidance That Can Be Lived**

The verse ends by calling this the greatest bounty. The Book is greater than wealth because it explains how wealth should be used; greater than status because it teaches that status is temporary; and greater than worldly security because it prepares the servant for the return. Its value is not confined to information. It gives a path by which a person can become truthful, repentant, balanced, and eager for good.

The proper response to this bounty is gratitude expressed through practice. Recite the Book, understand its commands, seek forgiveness for falling short, and do not resent the different levels of other servants. The chosen community is not chosen to boast but to carry a trust. Allah's gift remains open to transformation: the one who wrongs himself can return, the middle can rise, and the foremost can remain humble before the Giver.

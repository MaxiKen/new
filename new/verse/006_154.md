## Sūrah al-Anʿām 6:154

> Additionally, We gave Moses the Scripture, completing the favour upon those who do good, detailing everything, and as a guide and a mercy, so perhaps they would be certain of the meeting with their Lord.

**Expanded Commentary**

**The Scripture Given to Moses**

Additionally, We gave Moses the Scripture. The verse turns from the path of the community to the revelation given to the earlier prophet, and it describes that scripture in a series of phrases. The Book refers to the giving of Moses' scripture in many places: the sūrah of the Cow says that God gave Moses the Scripture and the criterion (2:53), the sūrah of the Believers says that He gave Moses the Scripture so that his people might be guided (23:49), and the sūrah of the Story describes the book given to Moses after the destruction of the earlier generations as an insight, a guide, and a mercy (28:43).

The sūrah's audience was familiar with the tradition of Moses, and the Book appeals to it throughout its argument. This presentation places the earlier revelation within the same guidance that the sūrah has been describing, and the phrases attached to it are the ones the Book uses for its own revelation as well.

**Completing the Favour Upon the Doers of Good**

Completing the favour upon those who do good. The phrase states the purpose for which the scripture was given, and the Book attaches the concept of completion to the gift of guidance in several places. In the sūrah of the Cow, the favour God completed upon the children of Israel is described, and the sūrah of the Table speaks of God's favour in the revelation of the Book.

The Book's association of the completed favour with those who do good appears in its accounts of the earlier communities. In the sūrah of the Story, the book given to Moses is described as an insight, a guide, and a mercy so that the people might be mindful, and the sūrah of the Cattle describes the community's own revelation as a blessing. The phrase in the verse in front of us states that the earlier scripture was given as the fulfilment of God's favour to the people who conduct themselves well, and the phrase that follows describes its content.

**Detailing Everything**

Detailing everything. The phrase states the comprehensiveness of the guidance given, and the Book uses the same description for its own revelation. The sūrah of Fussilat calls the Book one whose verses are perfectly explained, a Qur'an in Arabic for people who know (41:3), and the sūrah of the Cattle states in its own account of the Book that God has explained what He has forbidden (6:119). The phrase in this verse belongs to that family of statements, and it presents the earlier scripture as complete for the purposes it was given to serve.

The Book's treatment of the completeness of its guidance elsewhere states the same principle in different words. According to the sūrah of the Bee, God has sent down the Book as an explanation of everything and as guidance, mercy, and good news for those who submit, and the verse in front of us attaches the same description to the scripture of Moses. What the phrases establish is that the communities addressed by the earlier revelations received what they needed.

**A Guide and a Mercy**

And as a guide and a mercy. The pair appears in the Book's descriptions of the earlier scriptures and of its own revelation. The sūrah of the Table describes the Torah as containing guidance and light (5:44); the sūrah of the Story calls the book of Moses an insight, a guide, and a mercy; and the sūrah of the Cattle itself describes the Book revealed to the Prophet as a blessing and as that which confirms what came before it (6:92). The same vocabulary is applied here to the scripture of Moses.

The Book's use of these terms joins the earlier and the later revelations in a single line of guidance. The sūrah has argued throughout that the revelation given to the Prophet confirms what came before it, and the description of Moses' scripture in the same terms serves that argument. The two books are presented as instances of one provision rather than as separate traditions.

**Certainty of the Meeting With the Lord**

So perhaps they would be certain of the meeting with their Lord. The phrase states the effect the scripture was meant to produce, and the Book attaches the certainty of the meeting with God to the guidance it describes. In the sūrah of the Spider, whoever hopes for the meeting with God is told that the appointed time is sure to come, and the Book's statements about the resurrection appear throughout the sūrahs that describe the earlier revelations.

The phrase also connects the verse to the sūrah's earlier treatments of the Hereafter. The sūrah has described the condition of those who denied the meeting with their Lord and the fate of the communities that were destroyed for their denial, and it has stated that the certainty of the resurrection is the foundation of the conduct it requires. The purpose of the earlier scripture is stated in those terms here, and the passage that follows states the same purpose for the Book revealed to the Prophet.

**The Place of the Verse in the Sūrah**

The verse follows the recitation of the covenant and the identification of the commands as the straight path (6:151–153), and it precedes the passage that describes the Book given to the Prophet and answers the excuses of the people who claimed that revelation had passed them by (6:155–157). The two revelations are placed side by side, and the effect is to present the Qur'an as the continuation of a provision that the sūrah's audience already knew.

The Book's use of the earlier scriptures in its arguments appears throughout the sūrah. The sūrah has appealed to the Book of Moses as evidence that God reveals to human beings (6:91), it has described the earlier communities' reactions to their scriptures (6:146), and it has stated that the people who were given the earlier books recognise the Prophet's message. What this verse supplies is the description of the earlier scripture itself.

**The Transition to the Next Verse**

The next verse declares that the Book revealed to the Prophet is blessed and commands the community to follow it and be mindful so that they may be shown mercy (6:155). The sentence follows the account of the earlier scripture and presents the new revelation as the same kind of gift, and the verses that follow state the excuses that the Book removes.

The passage that continues from here describes the people who claimed that the scriptures had been given only to two groups before them and that they had not studied them, and it states that the clear proof, the guide, and the mercy have now come to them (6:156–157). The verse in front of us prepares that argument, and it does so by describing the earlier scripture in the terms that the new revelation will claim for itself.

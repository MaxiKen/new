## Sūrah al-Anʿām 6:36

> Only the attentive will respond ˹to your call˺. As for the dead, Allah will raise them up, then to Him they will ˹all˺ be returned.

**Expanded Commentary**

**The Division Drawn by the Answer Rather Than by the Messenger**

The verse states a limit that the previous passage has already prepared. After the impossible tunnel and stairway, and after the reminder that God could have guided everyone had He willed, the sūrah now says who actually responds: the attentive. The word describes people who use the hearing they have, and it puts the difference between the two groups in the listener rather than in the message or in the one delivering it.

The Book draws the same line in several places. Those who respond to the call of their Lord will have the finest reward, while those who do not respond would offer everything in the world twice over to ransom themselves (13:18); and in the sūrah of Yā-Sīn, the Prophet is told that he can only warn those who follow the reminder and stand in awe of the Most Compassionate without seeing Him (36:11). Response in this vocabulary is a faculty in use, not a temperament.

**Hearing With Something Other Than the Ears**

The Book's use of hearing for spiritual comprehension is consistent enough to be called a doctrine. The sūrah has already described listeners with veils on their hearts and deafness in their ears, who hear the recitation and leave it unchanged (6:25). Elsewhere the imagery is the same: those who deny the signs are wilfully deaf and dumb, lost in darkness (6:39); the worst of all beings in the sight of God are the wilfully deaf and dumb who do not understand (8:22).

So the attentive are not an intellectual elite. They are people who permit what they hear to reach the place where decisions are made, and the verse treats that permission as the whole difference between response and refusal. A man can be present at the recitation, understand the words, and still be counted among those who did not hear.

**Who the Dead Are in This Sentence**

The second half of the verse turns to the dead, and commentators read the word in two ways that are not mutually exclusive. Some understand it as a reference to the disbelievers, meaning that they will be raised along with everyone else and judged for their disbelief. Others read it as a description of spiritual death, since a person who has cut himself off from revelation is, in the Book's vocabulary, dead while still walking.

The two readings meet in the Qur'anic image where the Prophet is told that he cannot make the dead hear the truth, nor make the deaf hear the call when they turn their backs and walk away (27:80; 30:52), and where the dead and the living are explicitly said not to be equal, with the added explanation that God alone makes whoever He wills hear (35:22). The verse in front of us uses the same pairing of attention and death, and then attaches the resurrection to it.

**Resurrection Stated as a Fact About Both Groups**

The clause about resurrection is not a threat aimed only at the deaf. To Him they will all be returned, and the Book puts the guarantee of that gathering in terms of ownership: the One who raises the dead is the One who holds the property rights over everyone who dies. The sūrah has already asked its audience who created them from clay and appointed terms for their death, and it noted the second term that rests with God alone (6:2).

Placing the resurrection here serves a purpose in the argument. The people being addressed had denied the meeting with their Lord (6:31), and the answer to their denial is not an argument from nature or from the ruins of earlier nations. It is the statement that the gathering is happening, together with the observation that the same authority governs both who hears in this life and who comes back in the next.

**The Comfort Inside a Hard Sentence**

Read as a whole, the verse tells the Prophet something he needed after being told that no sign he could bring would ever be enough. It tells him that his work has a defined audience and that the definition is not his to supply. The Book states the same principle where it tells him that he is not a keeper over the people (6:107), and where the duty given to him is described as reminding rather than compelling.

That limit is a mercy to the messenger, since a man who believes the result depends on him will exhaust himself trying to produce faith by force. It is also a mercy to the hearer, since a message delivered by compulsion would have no value in a religion whose central claim is that a person turns to God by choice.

**The Transition the Verse Makes**

The next sentences in the sūrah report a demand for signs, and this verse supplies the ground for the answer they receive. It also holds the two halves of the audience's objection together. A person who says he would believe if a sign arrived has just been told that belief does not work that way, because attention is not produced by the size of the spectacle. If only the attentive respond, then the production of a spectacle would not increase the number of the attentive; it would only increase the number of those who saw it. The verse therefore closes the discussion about evidence in the order it was raised, and it prepares the sūrah's next movement, in which the demand is answered briefly and the argument returns to the audience's own situation.

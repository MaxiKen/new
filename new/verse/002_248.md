## Sūrah al-Baqarah 2:248

> Their prophet further told them, “The sign of Saul’s kingship is that the Ark will come to you—containing reassurance from your Lord and relics of the family of Moses and the family of Aaron, which will be carried by the angels. Surely in this is a sign for you, if you ˹truly˺ believe.”

**Expanded Commentary**

**The Sign Offered**

The verse reports the prophet's statement that the sign of the appointment will be the coming of the ark in which there is tranquillity from the Lord and a remnant of what the family of Moses and the family of Aaron left, borne by the angels. The sentence follows the objection of the people and supplies the evidence that answered it.

The Book's concern is the confirmation of what the community had doubted, and the community's teachers read the sentence as the statement of the sign that accompanied the appointment. Readers are shown that the passage answers the people's objection with evidence rather than with argument, and the sentence supplies the description of what they received.

**The Ark Described**

The commentators explained that the ark was the chest that the community venerated and that had been taken from them in the time of their distress, and they described its return as the restoration of what the people had lost. The wording identifies the object and the contents, and the community's scholars gathered the material about its history.

The Book's treatment of the subject connects the object with the worship of the earlier community, and the community's teachers read the sentence as a statement about the inheritance of the prophets. Readers are given the account of an object whose identity the commentaries discuss at length, and the sentence supplies the description that the discussions begin from.

**The Tranquillity Named**

The sentence states that tranquillity from the Lord is in the ark, and the commentators explained the term as the calm and dignity that came to the people together with the object. Others described the term as a reference to the presence that the earlier community associated with its sanctuary.

The community's scholars noted that the term appears in the Book in connection with the assurance that God gives to the believers, and they connected the passage with the material. The passage shows that the sign's first element concerns the state of the people rather than the object alone, and the sentence supplies the word from which the community's teachers drew their explanations.

**The Remnant Named**

The sentence states that the ark contains a remnant of what the house of Moses and the house of Aaron left, and the commentators listed the items they identified, including the tablets, the staff of Moses, and the manna that was preserved. The list shows the community's understanding of what the object held.

The wording connects the sign with the two families of the prophets, and the community's teachers read the clause as the statement that the sign carried the memory of the guidance that the people had received. The account describes the inheritance that the ark preserved, and the sentence supplies the description that the following clause completes.

**The Angels Bearing It**

The sentence states that the angels carry the ark, and the commentators explained the phrase as the statement that the object returned to the people by the power of God. The community's scholars read the clause as the confirmation that the sign was given from the unseen rather than through the ordinary course of events.

The Book's treatment of the angels elsewhere presents them as the servants who carry out the commands of God, and the community's teachers used the material when they spoke of the passage. The passage shows that the sign's authority lies in the power by which it came, and the sentence's closing phrase supplies the statement about the manner of its arrival.

**The Effect on the People**

The sentence describes the sign as an assurance for the community if they were believers, and the commentators noted that the condition accompanies the confirmation. The Book's practice of attaching a condition to its promises appears throughout the sūrah.

The wording places the benefit of the sign with those who receive it in faith, and the community's teachers read the clause as the statement that the evidence was given to a people whose acceptance was not guaranteed. The account describes the response that the sign required, and the sentence supplies the condition with which the passage's account of the appointment concludes.

**The Sign That Returned**

The object the sentence names belonged to the heritage of the earlier community, and the reports describe the veneration in which it was held and the loss that the people suffered when it was taken from them. Its return was the evidence that accompanied the appointment of the king, and the people received it as the confirmation of what their prophet had told them.

The tranquillity that the sentence names has been explained in several ways, and the teachers described the calm that came to the people together with the object. The remnant of what the two houses left included the items that the community's traditions named, and the commentators listed them in their accounts of the passage.

The clause about the angels has been read as the statement that the sign came by the power of God rather than through the ordinary course of events. The teachers noted that the sentence closes with the condition of belief, so that the evidence was presented to a people whose acceptance the Book does not assume.

**What the Verse Establishes**

The sentence establishes the sign of the ark, the tranquillity and the remnant that it held, the manner of its return, and the condition attached to the community's reception of it. The community's teachers used the verse when they spoke about the account of the earlier community, and the reader is shown that the sūrah presents the evidence that accompanied the appointment of the king.

The community's teachers read the sentence as the account of the confirmation that the people received, and the passage supplied the material for their discussions of the earlier community's history. The verse also shows the manner in which the Book presents its signs, since the object returned as the token of the appointment rather than as an end in itself.

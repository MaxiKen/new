## Sūrah al-Baqarah 2:21

> O humanity! Worship your Lord, Who created you and those before you, so that you may become mindful ˹of Him˺.

**Expanded Commentary**

**The First Summons to All Humanity, After a Sorting of Humanity**

The sūrah has spent twenty verses sorting humanity: the mindful upon guidance (2:2-5), those who persist in covering so that warning is the same to them (2:6-7), and those who say without being, whose disease is increased, whose corruption is renamed, whose folly is returned, whose double tongue is exposed, whose mockery is returned, whose trade is profitless, whose fire is taken, whose storm is endured, whose lightning is walked by in flashes (2:8-20). Only after that moral map is drawn does the sūrah address humanity as such: *“O humanity, worship your Lord”* — *yā ayyuhā al-nās uʿbudū rabbakum* (2:21). The vocative is the first of its kind in the sūrah, and its placement matters. The command to worship is not given before the need for worship has been shown. The need has been shown as a series of privations — sealed hearing, covered sight, diseased heart, deaf-dumb-blind — and the command arrives as the practice that would keep those privations from becoming a constitution.

**Worship Your Lord — The Root That Returns**

*Uʿbudū* — worship — from *ʿ-b-d*, to be a servant (*ʿabd*), to stand in the posture of one who is owned. The root has already appeared in the definition of the mindful as those who *spend from what We provided* (2:3), where provision was named so that worship would not be misheard as a tax. To say “Worship your Lord” is therefore not to impose a new master but to name the Master who has already provided. That is why the verse immediately gives the Lord’s credential: *“Who created you and those before you” (2:21)* Creation is the ground of lordship. The *Rabb* who is to be worshipped is the one who *khalaqakum* — brought you into being — and *alladhīna min qablikum* — those before you, whom you did not see created but whose existence you cannot account for without a Creator. Worship is the acknowledgment, in practice, of that prior act of creation.

**“Who Created You and Those Before You” — History as Proof of Lordship**

The phrase extends creation beyond the individual to the chain. *“You”* — the present audience — and *“those before you”* — ancestors, prior peoples, the very hypocrites and persistent rejectors just described — are gathered under one verb. That gathering does two things. It universalizes the summons: the humanity addressed is not a tribe but the species that has been created in succession. It also historicizes the proof: the creation of “those before you” is not an event the present audience witnessed, but it is an event whose result — the existence of a past — they inhabit. The sūrah will shortly make the same move with earth and sky as immediate, witnessed creation (2:22). Here it makes it with genealogy: you are not the first to be created, and the Lord who created those before you and then guided them or left them to wander is the same Lord who now summons you.

**So That You May Become Mindful — The Telos Named with Laʿalla**

*Laʿallakum tattaqūn* — “so that you may become mindful” — ends the summons with the same root *w-q-y* that defined its beneficiaries in (2:2). The mindful were those for whom the Book is a guidance; now all humanity is told to worship so that they may become among those mindful. The particle *laʿalla* — “so that you may,” “that haply,” “that perhaps” — has provoked theological discussion. How can the divine address contain a term of hope or anxiety, as if the outcome were uncertain to the Speaker? Early commentators resolve the tension by noting that the hope of God is tantamount to a promise, since nothing can obstruct it (Z), or that the hope is lodged in the subject, not in the Speaker — *you* may become mindful if you worship, and the conditional is real for you. In either case *laʿalla* preserves the moral structure of the opening: guidance is offered, worship is the practice that establishes the standing upon guidance (2:5), mindfulness is the standing that results, and the standing must be maintained by continual establishment (*iqāmah*) of prayer.

**Why “O Humanity” After “O You Who Believe” Has Not Yet Been Said**

Al-Baqarah is Medinan, and its audience includes believers, open rejectors, and hypocrites. The sūrah could have said *“O you who believe, worship”* — and it will, repeatedly later — but here it says *“O humanity.”* The breadth is intentional. The worship commanded is not a sectarian performance but the human vocation. The creation that grounds it — *“Who created you and those before you” (2:21)* — is a human creation, not a communal one, and the mindfulness that is its telos — *tattaqūn* — is the same mindfulness that the opening made the condition of guidance. The verse therefore does not yet ask humanity to join a community; it asks humanity to acknowledge the Lord who made humanity, so that community, when it is formed, will be formed upon that acknowledgment rather than upon genealogy or interest.

**Worship as the Remedy for the Privations Detailed**

Read against the preceding ten verses, the command to worship is the precise remedy for each privation. Sealed hearts are healed by a worship that turns the heart toward its Creator; deafness is healed by a worship that makes hearing the Book its standing practice; covered sight is healed by a worship that teaches sight to see provision as provision; diseased hearts are healed by a worship that replaces saying without being with being that says; corruption renamed as reform is healed by a worship that names the earth as a trust made for settlement; folly is healed by a worship that ranks by the Hereafter; double-tongued mockery is healed by a worship that requires a single tongue before the Lord; profitless trade is healed by a worship that purchases with a different commerce; fire taken and storm endured are healed by a worship that kindles a light that is not taken. The single imperative — *uʿbudū* — thus gathers the entire diagnosis and offers the single discipline that would keep the diagnosis from becoming destiny.

**Creation Before Command — The Order That Grounds Obligation**

The sūrah orders creation before command: *“Who created you … so worship.”* Obligation follows existence. That is why the Qur'an elsewhere can make the same move at the same scale: *“Worship Allah and associate none with Him”* after *“He created you from a single soul”* (4:1), and *“Fear your Lord who created you”* after naming the womb. The order prevents worship from being heard as an arbitrary imposition; it is the return of the created to the Creator, the spent provision returned to the Provider, the heart’s unseen belief (2:3) given its most basic act. That is also why the summons is in the imperative plural — *uʿbudū* — addressing humanity as a whole: creation is a collective fact, and worship, though performed individually, is the collective acknowledgement that the collective fact requires.

**A Verse That Reopens What Sealing Seemed to Close**

Finally, to hear (2:21) after (2:6-7) and (2:10) is to hear an opening after a closing. The sealing could have been heard as final, the increase in disease as irreversible, the deaf-dumb-blind as conclusive. The vocative *yā ayyuhā al-nās* refuses that hearing. It addresses the sealed, the diseased, and the unseeing along with the mindful, and it does so not with a new doctrine but with the oldest one: you were created, so worship the One who created you, so that you may become mindful. The Book that is *“no doubt”* (2:2) and *“upon guidance”* (2:5) is now offered as the means to become among those for whom it is guidance. The invitation remains, and the particle *laʿalla* keeps it as invitation rather than compulsion, so that worship, when it comes, will be the servant’s, not the compelled’s.

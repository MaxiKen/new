## Sūrah al-Baqarah 2:238

> Observe the ˹five obligatory˺ prayers—especially the middle prayer—and stand in true devotion to Allah.

**Expanded Commentary**

**The Command to Guard the Prayers**

The verse instructs the community to guard the prayers and especially the middle prayer, and it instructs them to stand before God in devotion. The sentence follows the rules about the household and turns to the community's worship, and the community's teachers read it as the command that governs the whole of the believer's day.

The Book's concern is that the obligations of the household should not displace the obligations of worship, and the sentence places the prayers at the centre of the community's conduct. Readers are shown that the legislation of the sūrah addresses the whole of life, and the instruction supplies the practice by which the community is ordered.

**The Middle Prayer**

The commentators discussed the identity of the middle prayer, and the reports record several identifications, including the afternoon prayer and the dawn prayer. The community's scholars set out the positions they received, and the discussion shows the care with which the community studied the text.

The reports also record the sayings of the Prophet ﷺ about the prayer that was named, and the community's teachers used the material when they taught the subject. Readers are given the account of a sentence whose wording has been examined in detail, and the community's attention to the phrase shows the standing of the command in the life of the community.

The reports that name the afternoon prayer have been preferred by many of the community's scholars, and they explained the emphasis by the hour in which the affairs of the day press upon the believer. The passage's instruction places the prayer in the middle of the working life of the community, and the teachers used the sentence when they spoke about the care that the appointed times require.

**The Sections Around It**

The section of the sūrah in which the sentence stands has been devoted to the rules about the household, and the command here marks the transition to the material about the community's obligations in the wider world. The Book's arrangement of its subjects places the worship at the centre of the passage.

The community's teachers read the position of the sentence as a statement that the believer's conduct in the family and in the community belongs with the regularity of his prayer. The passage shows that the sūrah's instructions form a single body of guidance, and the sentence in this place supplies the practice that holds them together.

**The Standing in Devotion**

The sentence instructs the community to stand before God with devotion, and the commentators explained the phrase as the stillness of the body and the presence of the heart in the prayer. The wording supplies the manner in which the command is to be carried out.

The Book's treatment of the prayer elsewhere describes the conduct that accompanies it, and the community's scholars gathered the material. The account describes an instruction that concerns the inner condition as well as the outward position, and the sentence supplies the standard by which the observance is measured.

The teachers of the community described the condition the phrase requires by the reports that transmit the conduct of the Prophet ﷺ in his prayer, where the stillness of the limbs accompanies the attention of the heart. They warned against the worship that the body performs while the mind is occupied elsewhere, and they read the instruction as the command that gathers the person before his Lord. The reports of the companions describe the care with which they observed the posture and the pace of the prayer, and the community's jurists recorded the details as part of the same teaching.

**The Reports About the Command**

The commentaries preserve the reports about the occasions on which the community was occupied in battle and the instruction given about the prayer at such times, and they connect the material with the verses that follow. The reports show the community applying the command in the most difficult conditions it met.

The material also records the care with which the early teachers attended to the times of the prayer, and the community's scholars used it when they set out the law. The passage shows that the command was understood as the constant obligation of the believer, and the sentence supplies the principle that the community applied in every circumstance.

**The Passage's Concern**

The sentence stands at the head of a group of verses that treat the community's obligations and its conflicts, and it supplies the disposition in which the following instructions are received. The Book has treated the household and now turns to the community's affairs.

The passage continues with the rules about the prayer in the presence of danger and about the completion of the waiting period, so that the reader is carried from the regulation of the worship to the practical circumstances of the community. The community's teachers read the verses as a unit, and the sentence in this place supplies the command that opens the treatment.

**The Prayer at the Centre of the Day**

The command to guard the prayers has been read as the instruction that governs the believer's day, and the community's jurists built the rules of the times upon it. Guarding the prayer means attending to it at the hour it is due, with the conditions and the preparation that the law requires, and the warning of the reports is severe for the one who lets the appointed hour pass.

The middle prayer has been the subject of a long discussion among the community's teachers, and the reports that name the afternoon prayer are the most frequently cited. The wording singles the prayer out, and the commentators explained the mention as the emphasis that the community needed for the prayer that falls in the busiest part of the working day.

The instruction to stand before God in devotion has been explained as the stillness of the body and the attention of the heart, and the teachers connected the phrase with the reports that describe the conduct of the worshipper. The section then turns to the circumstances of danger in the following verse, so that the command here supplies the rule for the ordinary day and the verse that follows it supplies the rule for the difficult one.

**What the Verse Establishes**

The sentence establishes the obligation to guard the prayers, the particular mention of the middle prayer, the instruction to stand before God in devotion, and the place of the worship within the community's life. The community's teachers used the verse when they set out the law of prayer, and the reader is shown that the sūrah's guidance on the household is joined to the command that orders the community's worship.

The teachers placed the sentence at the head of the community's practices, and the passages that surround it were read as the ground of the whole body of the law of worship. The verse also prepares the reader for the provisions that follow, since the defence of the community is treated after the command that orders its prayer.

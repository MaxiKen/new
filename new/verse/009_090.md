## Sūrah al-Tawbah 9:90

> Some nomadic Arabs ˹also˺ came with excuses, seeking exemption. And those who were untrue to Allah and His Messenger remained behind ˹with no excuse˺. The unfaithful among them will be afflicted with a painful punishment.

**Expanded Commentary**

**Not Every Request for Exemption Has the Same Meaning**

The verse distinguishes between nomadic Arabs who came seeking exemption and others who stayed behind while being untrue to Allah and His Messenger. Exegetes differed over the reading of the phrase: it can describe people making excuses without a valid reason, or people who came with genuine excuses. The surrounding principles require this distinction to be handled carefully rather than treating every late request as hypocrisy.

A person may come openly to explain an inability, accept the judgment of the leader, and remain loyal. Another may use the language of excuse to hide that he simply did not want to serve. The difference lies in truthfulness, ability, and conduct. Allah does not condemn the person who reports a real limit. He condemns the person who lies about the limit and abandons faithfulness.

**Coming Forward Can Be a Sign of Honesty**

The people who came to request exemption did not all behave like those who silently remained behind. Approaching the Messenger creates an opportunity for the matter to be examined. Honest speech may reveal weakness, illness, lack of means, or a family duty. A person who reports the truth does not need to invent a noble story.

This teaches a general habit of accountability. When a servant cannot fulfill a task, he should communicate early, explain the real obstacle, and ask what support or alternative service is possible. Disappearing protects the image for a moment but burdens everyone else with uncertainty. Truthful limitation allows a community to plan and allows the servant to remain part of the work in another form.

**Those Who Lie Remain by Choice**

The verse identifies others who stayed behind while being untrue to Allah and His Messenger. Their absence is not an accident or an unavoidable result. It is the outward form of an inward betrayal. They may later offer explanations, but their conduct has already shown that they preferred safety over the duty Allah placed before them.

A leader should not confuse silence with innocence. Sometimes the absence of a request is itself evidence that a person did not want his situation examined. Yet evidence must remain proportionate. A community cannot condemn every person who fails to speak, because fear and confusion can silence the sincere. The pattern of lies, refusal, and contempt makes the difference clear.

**The Nomadic Setting Does Not Remove Responsibility**

The mention of nomadic Arabs does not make geography a moral defect. People living outside the city may have less access to teaching, fewer resources, and different social pressures. These conditions can affect knowledge and ability. The verse addresses those among them who were untrue, not every person from a particular way of life.

This guards the believer from class contempt. Urban status does not guarantee faith, and rural distance does not prove ignorance or hypocrisy. Allah judges knowledge, opportunity, intention, and action. Communities should teach people who have less access to guidance rather than using distance as an excuse to dismiss them.

**Punishment Follows Unfaithfulness**

The painful punishment is connected to being unfaithful to Allah and His Messenger. The issue is therefore deeper than missing one journey. A person may fail once and repent, while another uses a series of failures to establish a life of deceit. The latter cannot hide behind the language of a single logistical problem.

The warning encourages early repentance. If a servant recognizes that he lied about ability or concealed his refusal, he should confess, repair the harm, and accept the consequences. He should not wait until the community discovers the pattern for him. Allah's punishment is serious, but the path of return is still open to the living person who abandons unfaithfulness.

**Justice Requires Evidence and Mercy**

This verse gives leaders a difficult balance. They must protect the mission from people who lie, yet they must not punish the weak for being weak. They must listen to requests, investigate the reason, and distinguish the truthful from the deceptive without pretending to know the unseen.

The believer adopts the same balance in personal judgment. He reports clear betrayal, refuses to spread rumor, and hopes that the person who is merely struggling will be helped. Exemption is not shameful when Allah grants it, and participation is not honorable when it is performed with hidden sabotage. Truthful ability and faithful intention are the measures that give the verse its justice.

The verse also teaches that a leader should not force every person into the same answer. He listens to the request, examines the reason, and assigns responsibility according to evidence. If the excuse is valid, the person is protected from blame. If it is false, the person is warned about the spiritual cost of making a lie serve comfort. This process preserves both compassion and discipline. It also gives the sincere a reason to speak early, since honest disclosure will not be treated as betrayal. The people who remained unfaithful chose secrecy and falsehood; the people who came with their situation exposed themselves to a fair judgment.

The distinction between valid excuse and falsehood also keeps reform humane. People who live far from the center of teaching may need clearer instruction about what is required and what is excused. A leader should not punish ignorance that could have been corrected by access to knowledge. Once the rule is clear and ability is present, however, the person cannot keep hiding behind confusion. Truthful speech creates a path for mercy; false speech closes that path by making the community plan on a lie. Allah's punishment is connected to unfaithfulness, while His mercy protects the servant who admits a real limit.

## Sūrah al-Anʿām 6:123

> And so We have placed in every society the most wicked to conspire in it. Yet they plot only against themselves, but they fail to perceive it.

**Expanded Commentary**

**The Wicked Placed in Every Society**

And so We have placed in every society the most wicked to conspire in it. The verse states a fact about the societies to which the message came, and it does so as a general pattern rather than as a description of Makkah alone. Every community has had its leaders in opposition, and the Book connects the statement to the earlier verses about the enemies appointed to every prophet (6:112).

The Book's account of the opponents in other sūrahs identifies them with the same word the translation renders as the elite. The sūrah of Saba' says that whenever a warner was sent to a society, its elite said that they rejected what he had been sent with (34:34), and the sūrah of the Ornament records the same response, with the elite saying that they had found their forefathers following a way and would follow in their footsteps (43:23). The sūrah's audience, which took its direction from the leading families of the city, is being shown the pattern of that leadership.

**What the Conspiracy Consists In**

The verse describes the activity of these people as conspiring or plotting, and the Book uses the term for the schemes by which the messengers were resisted. The sūrah of the Prophets records the plan that Abraham's people made against him and the response that made them the worst losers (21:70), and the sūrah of the Cattle has recorded several of the audience's own schemes in its accounts of their demands and objections.

The Book treats the plotting as the work of the people it names, and it does not attribute it to the whole of a society. The elite are described as the ones who conspire, and the sūrah of the Ornament has them appealing to the ancestors' custom rather than to any argument. The verse in front of us states that this group exists in every community, and the sūrah's audience is told that its own leadership belongs to the pattern.

**They Plot Only Against Themselves**

Yet they plot only against themselves, but they fail to perceive it. The sentence states the outcome of the plotting, and the Book's claim is that the harm returns to the people who plan it. The sūrah of Fāṭir states that evil plotting only backfires on those who plot, and it attaches the conduct to the people who behaved arrogantly in the land (35:43); the sūrah of the Ants records the plan made by a community against the messenger and says that God made a plan while they were unaware (27:50); and the sūrah of the Family of Imran tells the believers that if they are patient and mindful of God, the schemes of their enemies will not harm them (3:120).

The Book's reasoning for that claim lies in its account of God's power over what is planned. The schemes of the elite succeeded in delaying the acceptance of the message, and they failed in the matter that counted, which was the progress of the revelation and the establishment of its community. The sentence in this verse describes the outcome from the standpoint of the schemers' own interest, and it says that they did not see what their effort was producing.

**The Failure to Perceive**

The closing clause adds the reason the schemers continued: they did not perceive what they were doing to themselves. The Book attaches the same inability to the people whose conduct it has been describing. The sūrah has said that they follow assumption and lie (6:116), that their conduct has been made appealing to them (6:122), and that their hearts were turned away after their first refusal (6:110). The verse in front of us states the practical consequence of that condition in the field of action.

The inability to perceive is not presented as a lack of intelligence, since the leaders of the Quraysh were capable and experienced. What the Book describes is a blindness about the direction of their own conduct, and it states that the blindness is the product of the choices the people had made.

**What the Verse Says to the Prophet and the Believers**

The verse answers the situation in which the small community found itself. The leading families controlled the resources of the city, and their opposition determined the terms on which the community lived. The Book tells the Prophet that this arrangement is neither peculiar to his city nor a sign that his mission has failed, since the pattern belongs to every society to which a warner has been sent.

The book has given the same assurance in its accounts of the earlier messengers, where the opposition is consistently presented as coming from the established families of the people and not from the community as a whole. The sūrah of Hūd records the leaders of Noah's people rejecting him on the basis of the standing of his followers, and the same pattern is traced through the histories that follow. The verse in front of us states the pattern in a single sentence.

**The Transition to the Next Verse**

The next verse records the response of these people to the signs: whenever a sign comes to them, they say that they will never believe until they receive what God's messengers received (6:124). The demand is for the gift of prophethood itself or for the authority that came with it, and the verse answers that God knows best where to place His message.

The sequence of the two verses is exact. The elite plot within their society, and their plotting takes the form of a demand that the message be delivered by people of their own choosing. The sūrah has answered the demand for a sign, and it now answers the demand for a different messenger.

## Sūrah al-Nisāʾ 4:47

> O you who were given the Book! Believe in what We have revealed— confirming your own Scriptures—before We wipe out ˹your˺ faces, turning them backwards, or We condemn the defiant as We did to the Sabbathbreakers. And Allah’s command is always executed!

**Expanded Commentary**

**Believe in What Confirms Your Own**

The call *yā ayyuhā alladhīna ūtū al-kitāba āminū bi mā nazzalnā muṣaddiqan li mā maʿakum* — O you who were given the Book, believe in what We have revealed confirming your own — addresses those who already hold a portion of revelation (4:44) and who were seen distorting words (4:46). *Muṣaddiqan li mā maʿakum* — confirming what is with you — was stressed as proof that the new revelation is not alien to theirs. The Torah that warned against devouring wealth, that set limits for family, and that commanded worship without association finds its confirmation in the Qur’an that has just guarded orphans, fixed shares, and chastity. To believe in the confirming Book is therefore not to abandon the earlier Book but to be faithful to it. The sūrah has said God wills to make things clear and guide to the noble ways of those before you (4:26); here that guidance takes the form of confirming what those before you were given. The believers who were told not to crave another’s portion (4:32) and to ask God for bounty are here told the greatest bounty is a Book that confirms theirs; to refuse it is to refuse the part they already hold, because confirmation strengthens rather than cancels.

**Before We Wipe Out Faces**

The warning *min qabli an naṭmisa wujūhan fa naruddahā ʿalā adbārihā aw nalʿanahum kamā laʿannā aṣḥāba al-sabt* — before We wipe out faces, turning them backwards, or We condemn the defiant as We did to the Sabbath-breakers — uses an image of erasure. *Ṭams* — to efface, to wipe out features so that eyes, nose, and mouth are levelled — was read as a literal transformation for a people who distorted words with tongues and faces, or as a metaphor for being turned away from guidance so that faces meant to face God are turned to the back. The pairing with *na ruddahā ʿalā adbārihā* — turning them backwards — shows reversal as the recompense for reversal. Those who took words out of context (4:46) and who traded the Book for misguidance and wished believers to deviate (4:44) turned guidance backward; to have faces turned backward mirrors that turning. The second option — *aw nalʿanahum kamā laʿannā aṣḥāba al-sabt* — or We condemn them as We condemned the Sabbath-breakers — recalls the people who broke the Sabbath limit by stratagem, were warned in their own law, persisted, and were cursed and transformed, a story the Qur’an elsewhere says was made an example for those present and those to come. The sūrah places that example beside distorted speech for a reason: both broke a limit set by God and both used a trick to seem not to.

**The Sabbath-Breakers as Precedent**

The reference to the Sabbath-breakers was read as more than a scare; it was a legal analogy. The Sabbath limit was a *ḥadd* — a divine boundary — like the inheritance limits called *ḥudūd* (4:13–14) and the marriage bars (4:22–23). Those who broke it by setting nets before the Sabbath and collecting after, claiming they had not fished on the day, were condemned because the stratagem kept the form while breaking the substance, much as *rāʿinā* kept the sound of *unẓurnā* while meaning insult. The sūrah has guarded against similar stratagems with wealth: hasty consumption before the orphan grows (4:6), bequests that harm heirs (4:12), and devouring by falsehood in trade (4:29). Each uses a form that looks lawful while its substance is taking. To warn with the Sabbath-breakers is to say that God’s limits are not defeated by wordplay. The condemnation — *laʿn* — that expels from mercy is the same word used for those condemned for distortion and disbelief (4:46), tying the two groups together. What was done to those who broke a temporal limit would be done to those who break the confirming Book’s limit, because the limit’s Owner is the same.

**And Allah’s Command Is Always Executed**

The closing *wa kāna amru Allāhi mafʿūlā* — and Allah’s command is always executed — seals the warning with inevitability. *Amr* — command, affair — was read as both decree and order; what He commands to be, is, and what He warns will happen if belief is refused will not be delayed by anyone’s plan. The sūrah has said that God never wrongs even by an atom’s weight but multiplies good (4:40) and that He has perfect knowledge of them (4:39); that knowledge now becomes capability, as the command that is executed shows. The phrase follows the double threat — wiping faces or condemning as the Sabbath-breakers — to make clear that neither is bluster. The community that heard *“have you not seen those who trade a portion for misguidance”* (4:44) might have thought the traders would simply persist without consequence; this verse says persistence ends in an executed command. Yet the call before the threat remains belief — *āminū* — which is still possible while the warning is given. The sūrah’s pattern of clarity before sanction — limits named, then Gardens and Fire named (4:13–14) — is kept: the confirming Book is named, the confirmation explained, the threat described, and the command’s execution guaranteed, so that choice is made with open eyes.

**A Call That Confirms Rather Than Cancels**

Read after the diagnosis of twisted phrases, this verse is the sūrah’s invitation to the very group diagnosed: believe in what confirms your own. It does not ask those given the Book to erase their history; it asks them to see its fulfilment. The limits they were given — about property, family, and worship — are made clear again and lightened because humankind was created weak (4:28); the Book that makes them clear is the same that confirms. To refuse that confirmation while trading the portion they have (4:44) is to turn faces away from the direction they were meant to face. The executed command will then do what their own turning refused: turn them backward. The sūrah leaves the path open before that execution — believe now — because the One who warns is also the One who said *“Allah wishes to turn to you in grace”* (4:27) and *“and Allah is Ever-Pardoning, All-Forgiving”* (4:43). The faces that could be wiped can instead be turned forward in belief, where the confirming Book is recognized and the warning is no longer needed.

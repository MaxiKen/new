## Sūrah al-Nisāʾ 4:47

> O you who were given the Book! Believe in what We have revealed— confirming your own Scriptures—before We wipe out ˹your˺ faces, turning them backwards, or We condemn the defiant as We did to the Sabbathbreakers. And Allah’s command is always executed!

### Expanded Commentary

**The People of the Book are invited through continuity**

The call to believe in what has been revealed to Muhammad is supported by the fact that it confirms the Scriptures they already possess. The Qur’an does not present itself as a religion with no relationship to earlier revelation. It calls people back to the God of Abraham, Moses, and the prophets, while correcting distortions and rejecting claims that contradict divine unity.

**The severe imagery of reversal**

The warning that faces could be erased and turned backwards, or that people could be condemned as the Sabbath-breakers were, communicates the danger of refusing clear truth after recognition. The image can be read as a physical judgment and as a moral reversal: the person who turns away from guidance risks being returned to the condition of those who denied it. It is not a licence for believers to threaten minorities; it is a divine warning addressed through the Prophet.

**A command that will be executed**

The final statement underscores divine sovereignty. Human beings can delay, reinterpret, or mock a command, but they cannot cancel it. The invitation therefore carries urgency. Recognition of the continuity between Qur’an and previous revelation should lead to faith rather than to a defensive struggle for institutional ownership.

The verse also teaches Muslims to honour the scriptures’ original source while being careful about their present textual and interpretive forms. Confirmation is neither uncritical approval of every claim nor denial of the prophetic heritage. It is a call to return all communities to worship of the One God and to obedience to His final guidance.

**Recognition before resistance**

The invitation to the People of the Book is not based on erasing their tradition but on asking them to recognize continuity. Muslims can learn to speak of earlier revelation with reverence while explaining the Qur’an’s claims clearly. A defensive community may demand ever more signs; a truthful community responds to the evidence already before it.

## Sūrah al-Shuʿarāʾ 26:78

> ˹He is˺ the One Who created me, and He ˹alone˺ guides me.

**Expanded Commentary**

**Abraham Begins With Creation**

After rejecting false objects of worship, Abraham identifies the Lord through two acts: creation and guidance. The first is creation. God is the One who brought Abraham into existence, and that origin establishes a relation no idol can claim. An image may be carved by human hands, but it cannot create the person who stands before it. The Creator's claim precedes every custom, shrine, and inherited name.

Creation also means more than a beginning in the distant past. Abraham's existence remains dependent on the One who created him. He does not own himself in an absolute sense, and the idols do not own him at all. Worship becomes intelligible because the creature's life comes from the Creator. The Lord's right is grounded in reality, not in a social agreement.

**Creation Leads to Guidance**

Abraham does not stop at “He created me.” A person may acknowledge a creator and still wonder how to live. The verse joins origin to direction. The One who created Abraham also guides him. Creation is not an act that leaves the creature abandoned in a meaningless world. It is followed by care, instruction, and a path by which the creature can fulfill the purpose of existence.

The pairing corrects two opposite errors. Some people imagine that the world has a creator but no moral guidance. Others seek guidance from created objects while forgetting the One who gave them life. Abraham holds the two together: the Creator is also the Guide. The source of existence and the source of right direction are one Lord.

**Guidance Is a Gift, Not a Self-Creation**

Abraham says that God guides him. He does not present guidance as an achievement produced by his own intelligence alone. Reflection, courage, and moral effort matter, but they operate within a gift. The heart must be willing to seek truth, yet the ability to recognize and follow the way comes from the Lord.

This language creates humility without passivity. A person cannot boast as though he manufactured guidance, but he also cannot use dependence on God as an excuse to stop seeking. Abraham's statement invites prayerful effort: ask the Creator to guide, listen for truth, and act upon what is recognized. Guidance is received through a living relation with God rather than through the authority of an idol.

**The Creator Knows the Creature's Need**

Creation implies knowledge. The One who made Abraham knows what he is, what he needs, and what will lead him toward flourishing. An idol does not know its worshipper because it did not bring the worshipper into existence. The contrast makes false worship unreasonable. Why turn to an object that depends on human hands when the Creator already knows the servant completely?

God's guidance is therefore not arbitrary control. It is direction from the One whose knowledge encompasses the creature's nature and destination. Abraham can trust the guidance because it comes from the Lord who made him. Obedience is not a leap toward an unknown power; it is a return to the source from which his life came.

**The Pronoun He Keeps the Relation Personal**

The translation's He is not an abstract force. Abraham speaks of the Lord as the One who created and guides him. The relation is personal without making God human. Abraham is a servant who receives existence and direction from a Lord who knows him. This language gives monotheism warmth and responsibility. He is not submitting to an impersonal principle but responding to the Creator's care.

That personal relation also exposes the poverty of idols. A statue may be named, decorated, and surrounded by people, but it cannot enter a real relation with them. The Lord creates, guides, hears, and acts. Abraham's worship is directed toward the One who can truly address the whole person.

**Guidance Reorders Human Freedom**

If God created Abraham and guides him, freedom cannot mean doing whatever desire demands. True freedom is release from false masters so that the creature can follow the One who knows the right path. The idolater appears independent when choosing many objects, yet he is bound to custom, fear, and social pressure. Abraham's submission is a deeper freedom because his allegiance is directed to the Creator.

This does not erase responsibility. Guidance calls for decisions, patience, and resistance to error. It gives freedom a destination rather than leaving it as mere choice. A person is not made free by multiplying objects of devotion. He is made free by no longer serving what cannot create or guide him.

**The Verse Grounds Ethics in Dependence**

The Creator's guidance includes the moral shape of life. If God gives existence and direction, then human conduct is not self-defined. People cannot declare any desire sacred simply because it is old or popular. They are creatures whose choices should be measured against the guidance of the One who made them.

This grounding protects ethics from both arrogance and despair. Arrogance says the self can invent the good without reference to its Creator. Despair says life has no reliable direction. Abraham's sentence rejects both. The Lord creates and guides, so the human being is accountable yet not abandoned.

**The First Divine Gift in Abraham's Summary Is Life Itself**

Before speaking of food, drink, or healing, Abraham names creation. Every later mercy depends on this first gift. A person cannot receive provision or recovery without first being brought into existence and sustained within it. The order teaches gratitude for the whole relation, not only for the moments when a visible need is met.

The verse therefore establishes the foundation for the following descriptions of divine care. God is not merely one helper among others. He is the Creator who gives Abraham life and the Guide who gives life direction. Against the idols, that twofold claim is complete: they neither created Abraham nor can they guide him.

## Sūrah al-Shuʿarāʾ 26:205

> Imagine ˹O Prophet˺ if We allowed them enjoyment for years,

**Expanded Commentary**

**Long Enjoyment Does Not Mean Divine Approval**

The verse asks the Prophet to consider a long period of enjoyment granted to the deniers. Years can make a society feel established. Children grow, businesses expand, homes are repaired, and the next generation inherits the same confidence. Yet the length of enjoyment does not change the moral status of what the people are doing.

This is a necessary correction to a common human judgment. People often read comfort as approval and hardship as rejection. The Quran refuses that simple calculation. Allah may allow a corrupt people prosperity for a time, not because their corruption is safe, but because the period serves a wisdom that may include warning, testing, and the completion of their chosen course.

**Enjoyment Can Hide the Cost of Delay**

A comfortable life can make repentance feel unnecessary. Each uneventful year confirms the story that no reckoning is coming. The person who reduced a measure yesterday can reduce it again today, then teach the practice to his children. Time does not merely preserve the wrong; it can make the wrong look like an inheritance.

Quran 3:178 warns that longer life can give those who disbelieve more time to increase in sin. Quran 6:44 describes people becoming proud when they receive what they desire before being seized by surprise. These passages explain why enjoyment must not be used as a measure of spiritual health. A gift can become a test when it feeds forgetfulness.

**Years Do Not Belong to the One Who Enjoys Them**

The wording says Allah allowed the enjoyment. It was never independent property that the people could use to disprove the Giver. Wealth, health, security, and time remain entrusted gifts. The people may enjoy them, but they cannot make the gifts into an argument against the authority of the One who grants them.

This perspective does not demand that believers despise lawful comfort. It demands gratitude and accountability within comfort. A person can enjoy provision while using it to protect rights, support family, and worship Allah. The danger lies in turning enjoyment into a shield against correction or a reason to mock those who warn of judgment.

**The Hypothesis Tests the Meaning of Prosperity**

The verse does not ask how pleasant the years were. It asks what those years can prove. If the people remain headed toward a promised consequence, long enjoyment has not made them secure. It has only lengthened the interval before the account is settled.

A community should therefore evaluate prosperity by what it produces. Does it make people more grateful, just, and ready to repent, or more proud of the power to ignore warnings? The same outward comfort can be mercy for one person and a path to ruin for another. The difference is the use made of the years Allah permits.

Long enjoyment can also change how a community tells its own story. The beneficiaries begin to call their wealth proof of wisdom, and their success becomes an argument against anyone who warns them. Children learn that the old practice must be right because it has supported the household for so long. Years then do more than delay the consequence; they build a culture that defends the delay.

The Quran does not ask people to reject every form of prosperity. It asks them to read prosperity under the light of accountability. A good harvest can fund charity or deepen hoarding. Influence can protect the weak or silence them. Leisure can create gratitude or make a person forget prayer and duty. Enjoyment is morally open until the servant chooses what to do with it.

The phrase for years also unsettles the desire to measure safety by duration. A corrupt institution may survive longer than an honest person expects. A false business may grow while a just one struggles. These outward timelines do not establish Allah's final verdict. The promised account stands above the years, and no amount of accumulated comfort can turn a temporary arrangement into an eternal guarantee.

The Prophet is asked to imagine this scenario so that the audience can see through the seduction of delay. If the deniers are given a long life, their extra years do not make the warning less true. They make the contrast sharper between what they enjoy now and what they are preparing to meet. The wise person receives the gift with gratitude, uses it justly, and remains ready to leave it without claiming that it was proof of divine approval.

A long season of ease can also test whether a believer remembers people who are not enjoying the same ease. If comfort makes a person less patient with the poor, less truthful in trade, or less willing to hear correction, it has begun to work against him. If comfort makes him grateful, generous, and more able to serve, it can be a blessing. The outer condition does not decide the inner result.

The hypothetical years are therefore an argument against complacency, not against hope. A servant may ask Allah for well-being and enjoy it when it comes. He simply refuses to call well-being a guarantee that every choice he makes is accepted. Gratitude includes examining the source of the enjoyment, the rights involved in obtaining it, and the use to which it is put.

The warning is particularly important for a society that measures success by uninterrupted growth. Growth can make a corrupt system stronger and harder to correct. The longer it lasts, the more people depend on it and the more frightening reform appears. Revelation reminds such a society that duration is not destiny. What Allah has allowed for years can end when its appointed consequence comes.

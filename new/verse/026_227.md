## Sūrah al-Shuʿarāʾ 26:227

> Except those who believe, do good, remember Allah often, and ˹poetically˺ avenge ˹the believers˺ after being wrongfully slandered. The wrongdoers will come to know what ˹evil˺ end they will meet.

**Expanded Commentary**

**The Exception Is Defined by Faith and Conduct**

The previous description does not condemn every poet without distinction. This verse identifies an exception through four connected qualities: belief, righteous action, frequent remembrance of Allah, and a response to wrong that defends the believers. The exception is not granted by artistic talent or by belonging to a favored circle. It is grounded in faith that produces conduct.

Belief gives the speaker a true center. Good deeds show that the center is not merely claimed. Remembering Allah keeps the imagination and the tongue from becoming independent powers that serve pride. A poet may use strong language, but his work remains under the knowledge that Allah hears every word and will judge the purpose behind it.

**Remembrance Keeps Creative Speech Accountable**

Remembering Allah often does not mean repeating words mechanically while the heart remains absent. It means keeping Allah's presence, commands, mercy, and judgment active in the choices made by the speaker. Quran 33:41 commands believers to remember Allah often, so remembrance is a broad discipline that shapes speech, action, and attention.

For a poet, remembrance can restrain vanity. Praise cannot become worship of a patron, satire cannot become permission to lie, and emotional force cannot become a license to injure the innocent. The imagination remains active, but it is not sovereign. It serves truth rather than demanding that truth serve the imagination.

**Defending the Wronged Is Not the Same as Revenge**

The translation speaks of poetically avenging believers after they have been wronged. The context is a response to slander and attack, not permission for uncontrolled retaliation. A just defense answers falsehood with truth, protects the honor of the innocent, and stops the aggressor from turning lies into accepted history.

Quran 42:39–40 describes believers who enforce justice when wronged and also praises pardon and reconciliation when they are appropriate. That balance clarifies this exception. A response to slander may be necessary, but it must remain proportionate and free of fresh injustice. The believer does not become righteous by defeating one lie with ten lies.

**Righteous Speech Can Serve the Community**

A believing poet can strengthen courage, defend the vulnerable, expose false claims, and remind people of Allah. His art is not automatically sacred, but it can become a lawful instrument of good when its purpose and content are sound. The difference from the earlier poets lies in accountability: he does not move through every field merely to please the audience.

The exception also rejects the idea that religious faith requires the destruction of every human talent. Poetry, rhetoric, and imagination can be disciplined and directed. What faith changes is the master served by the talent. The speaker is no longer selling words to any desire. He uses language under the duty to believe, do good, and remember Allah.

**The Final Warning Returns to the Wrongdoers**

The verse ends by warning those who do wrong that they will know their final return. This gives the exception its moral boundary. The believing speaker may answer slander, but the wrongdoer is not defeated merely by a clever poem or public embarrassment. Every person returns to Allah, where the whole account of speech, harm, defense, and intention is known.

The ending also keeps the righteous from pride. Defending believers does not make a person immune from judgment. He must continue to believe, do good, and remember Allah while responding to wrong. The best speech is therefore joined to a good life and a sober awareness of the homecoming awaiting both the slanderer and the defender.

The exception is carefully built. Belief without action can become a claim, action without remembrance can become self-righteous activism, and remembrance without concern for the wronged can become private comfort. The verse joins them so that speech serves a complete moral life. The believing poet is not saved by a noble theme alone; his character and conduct must support the defense he makes.

To avenge the believers after being wronged also requires truth about what happened. A person cannot call every criticism oppression and then use religious language to silence correction. Quran 42:39–40 balances resistance to aggression with pardon and reconciliation where they are better. A just response protects people without making the defender a new wrongdoer.

The phrase remember Allah often keeps the defense from becoming tribal revenge. The believer answers a slander because honor and truth matter before Allah, not because he wants to enlarge his faction. He can expose a lie without lying about the opponent, and he can resist an attack without turning every disagreement into a license for hatred.

The warning to wrongdoers at the end widens the verse beyond poetry. Anyone who harms, slanders, manipulates, or abuses words has a return to face. The final homecoming is not controlled by the applause of a crowd. Allah knows who was wronged, who defended truth, who exceeded the limit, and who hid behind a beautiful phrase.

This makes the exception a standard for all creative and public speech. Believe, do good, remember Allah, and use talent to repair rather than corrupt. The form may be a poem, a speech, a post, or a story; the moral test remains the same. Language becomes honorable when it stands with truth and when the speaker is willing to live under the truth he proclaims.

The final verse therefore does not merely rescue a small group from a general condemnation. It gives the community a way to transform verbal power into service. The righteous speaker may be imaginative, forceful, and emotionally vivid, but he remains accountable to Allah. His words defend the wronged without worshiping anger, and his life gives the words the weight that empty performance cannot supply.

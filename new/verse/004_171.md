## Sūrah al-Nisāʾ 4:171

> O People of the Book! Do not go to extremes regarding your faith; say nothing about Allah except the truth. The Messiah, Jesus, son of Mary, was no more than a messenger of Allah and the fulfilment of His Word through Mary and a spirit ˹created by a command˺ from Him. So believe in Allah and His messengers and do not say, “Trinity.” Stop!—for your own good. Allah is only One God. Glory be to Him! He is far above having a son! To Him belongs whatever is in the heavens and whatever is on the earth. And Allah is sufficient as a Trustee of Affairs.

**Expanded Commentary**

**O People of the Book — Do Not Go to Extremes Regarding Faith**

The verse opens *yā ahla al-kitābi lā taghlū fī dīnikum wa lā taqūlū ʿalā Allāhi illā al-ḥaqq* — O People of the Book, do not go to extremes, exceed limits, in your religion, and say nothing about God except the truth. *Lā taghlū* — do not exceed, go to extremes — was explained with *ghuluww* — excess beyond limit, as the sūrah earlier forbade association not forgiven though what is less is for whom He wills (4:116) and forbade distinguishing between God and messengers desiring a way between (4:150) — to make *ʿĪsā* god is excess, to deny him is neglect. *Fī dīnikum* — regarding your faith, in your religion — was read as the Christian excess of divinising the Messiah and the Jewish neglect of denying him, both excesses. *Wa lā taqūlū ʿalā Allāhi illā al-ḥaqq* — say nothing about God except truth — was explained as that He is sufficient as Witness (4:166) who bears witness with His knowledge, and whose word none is more truthful than (4:122): to say about Him without truth is to claim witness without knowledge. The sūrah has shown that the Book was sent with truth — *bi al-ḥaqq* — to judge between people by what He shows (4:105); *ghuluww* is to judge without that showing. Early reports noted that Christians exceeded in Jesus as Jews did in slandering Mary with *buhtān ʿaẓīm* (4:156); the middle is *ḥaqq* — truth — that Jesus is servant honoured, not god.

**The Messiah, Son of Mary, Was No More Than Messenger and Word Through Mary and Spirit From Him**

The definition *innamā al-masīḥu ʿĪsā ibnu Maryama rasūlu Allāhi wa kalimatuhū alqāhā ilā Maryama wa rūḥun minhu* — the Messiah, Jesus, son of Mary, was no more than a messenger of God and the fulfilment, execution, of His Word — *kalimah* — cast to Mary and a spirit — *rūḥ* — from Him, created by command from Him — was explained with three. *Rasūlu Allāhi* — messenger of God — was read as the chain’s continuity: *We have sent revelation to you as We sent to Noah… Jesus… to Moses He spoke* (4:163–164), all messengers delivering good news and warnings so no excuse (4:165) — Jesus is among them. *Kalimatuhū alqāhā ilā Maryama* — His Word He conveyed to Mary — was explained as *“Be”* — *kun* — He said to Mary, as creation from a word, without father, as *“He creates what He wills when He decrees He says Be and it is”* — the *kalimah* is command not offspring. *Wa rūḥun minhu* — and a spirit from Him, created by command from Him — was explained as *rūḥ* — spirit — from Him as *“We breathed into her Our spirit”* (66:12), given, not part of Him — the sūrah later says *“to Him belongs whatever is in heavens and earth, He suffices as Trustee”* — the spirit belongs to Him. The phrase *innamā* — only, no more than — was read as restriction: Messiah is only messenger, Word, spirit-from-Him; divinity is excluded as excess. Where the sūrah says *“We have prepared for true disbelievers humiliating punishment”* (4:151) for distinguishing, and *“those who believe in God and messengers accepting all, rejecting none, He will give rewards”* (4:152) — this definition is the acceptance of Jesus as messenger among messengers, not god among gods.

**So Believe in God and His Messengers and Do Not Say Trinity — For Your Own Good**

The command *fa āminū bi Allāhi wa rusulihī wa lā taqūlū thalāthatun* — so believe in God and His messengers and do not say three, Trinity — *thalāthah* — was explained with *thalāthah* — three — as the Trinity doctrine — Father, Son, Holy Spirit as one — the excess the sūrah calls *ghuluww*. *Wa lā taqūlū thalāthah* — do not say Trinity — was read as that *ḥaqq* is *“God is only One God, far above having a son”* — He is *Wāḥid* — One, as *“to Him belongs whatever is in heavens and earth”* — to say three is to claim division of ownership. *Intahū khayran lakum* — Stop, desist, for your own good, better for you — was explained with *khayran lakum* — better for you — the same *khayran lakum* as *“O humanity, the Messenger has come with truth, so believe for your own good”* (4:170) — stopping Trinity is better for you, as He is Self-Sufficient (4:131) — not for Him. *Innamā Allāhu ilāhun wāḥid* — God is only One God — was read as the sūrah’s monotheism that association is not forgiven (4:116) and invokes female gods as Satan’s share (4:117) — Three is association far astray.

**Glory Be to Him — Far Above Having a Son — To Him Belongs Whatever Is in Heavens and Earth**

The glorification *subḥānahu an yakūna lahū walad* — glory be to Him, exalted is He, that He should have a son, that there be for Him a child — was explained with *subḥānahu* — glory, exaltation — as the Book’s *tasbīḥ* for the One to whom belongs whatever is in heavens and earth (4:126, 4:131, 4:170). *An yakūna lahū walad* — that He should have a son — was read as impossibility for the Self-Sufficient, Praiseworthy (4:131) who can remove humanity and bring others (4:133) — a son is not needed. *Lahū mā fī al-samāwāti wa mā fī al-arḍ* — to Him belongs whatever is in heavens and whatever on earth — was explained as that ownership denies need for son: *“if you disbelieve, to Him belongs whatever is in heavens and earth, He is All-Knowing, All-Wise”* (4:170) — after the Messenger has come with truth — Trinity divides ownership. *Wa kafā bi Allāhi wakīlā* — and He suffices as Trustee — was read as that *wakīl* suffices as Guardian without son, as the oppressed cry for *walī* and *naṣīr* from grace (4:75) — guardian is Him, not third of three. The sūrah has shown that *“He is sufficient as Trustee”* (4:132) — three is not needed for trusteeship.

**A Verse That Follows Humanity’s Call With People of the Book’s Correction**

Placed after *O humanity, the Messenger has come with truth, so believe for your own good, if you disbelieve to Him belongs whatever is in heavens and earth, He is All-Knowing, All-Wise* (4:170), this verse follows humanity’s call with People of the Book’s correction where the People of the Book demanded a book from heaven and greater — visible God — took calf yet were forgiven, Mount raised, firm covenant taken, then breaking, rejecting, killing, claiming *ghulf* sealed, denying, slander against Mary, boasting killing Messiah not killed but raised (4:153–158). The correction — do not go to extremes, say nothing but truth, Messiah was but messenger and Word through Mary and spirit from Him, believe in God and messengers, do not say Trinity, stop — better — He is One God far above having a son, to Him belongs whatever is in heavens and earth, He suffices as Trustee — is the middle after two extremes: Jewish extremes — denial and slander and boasting murder — and Christian extremes — deification as Trinity. The sūrah that began with single soul spread into humanity (4:1) and declares *“neither by your wishes nor those of People of the Book”* (4:123) makes that middle the truth by which the sūrah has judged: the Book sent with truth, witness sufficient (4:166). For the weak this is an opening: where you feared rising or turning away and settlement is best (4:128), Trinity is not settlement between God and messengers; to believe in God and His messengers and not say three is the settlement that is best — better for you.

**The Word Through Mary as Command Not Offspring**

The phrase *kalimatuhū alqāhā ilā Maryama wa rūḥun minhu* was explained to distinguish *kalimah* as *“Be”* commanded, not begotten, and *rūḥ* as created spirit from Him, not part. The sūrah later says *“the Messiah will never be too proud to be servant, nor angels nearest”* (4:172) — servant is the Word’s rank, worshipped is not. The next verse will set that servitude as proof: the Messiah would never be too proud to be servant, nor angels nearest — those too proud will be gathered before Him, the believers and doers of good rewarded in full and increased out of grace, the too proud subjected to painful punishment without protector besides Him (4:172–173).

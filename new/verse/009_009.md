## Sūrah al-Tawbah 9:9

> They chose a fleeting gain over Allah’s revelations, hindering ˹others˺ from His Way. Evil indeed is what they have done!

**Expanded Commentary**

**Revelation Was Treated as a Commodity**

The verse portrays the hostile group as choosing a small worldly return in exchange for Allah's revelations. The image is a transaction, but its moral force lies in the terrible imbalance of the price. Revelation carries truth, guidance, and a person's final direction. The gain that replaces it is brief and limited. The verse does not say that the gain had no attraction. It says that the attraction was too small to justify what was surrendered.

The Qur'an often uses the language of buying and selling to describe moral decisions. People exchange something lasting for something immediate, or they give what cannot be retained in order to receive what cannot be lost. In 9:111, believers are described as selling their lives and wealth for Paradise. Verse 9 presents the counterfeit transaction: revelation is exchanged for influence, security, profit, or the comfort of remaining in charge. Both groups are trading. The difference is what each regards as valuable.

**A Fleeting Gain Can Be Politically Large**

The adjective fleeting does not mean that the worldly benefit was imaginary. Leaders can gain real wealth, prestige, control of shrines, or influence over trade. What makes the price small is the scale against which the Qur'an measures it. A few years of authority cannot compare with the truth of Allah or with the life to come. A political success can be large inside a human system and still be small in the final account.

That evaluation challenges the way power describes itself. A person who benefits from an unjust order rarely calls the benefit temporary. He names it stability, tradition, or necessary compromise. Revelation strips away the flattering vocabulary and asks what has actually been purchased. If a leader keeps a falsehood alive because it protects his office, the office is the price tag. If a community hides guidance because honesty would cost it a market, the market is the price tag. The Qur'an calls the exchange poor because the seller has misunderstood the worth of what he sold.

**Turning Away Also Turns Others Away**

The verse says that they hindered others from Allah's way. The verb can carry two movements: turning away oneself and causing others to turn away. The second makes the offense wider. A private person may reject a truth for his own reasons; a person with authority can organize rejection, shame those who accept guidance, close access to the message, or make obedience look socially impossible.

This distinction protects a necessary balance. The Qur'an does not appoint believers to force every inward conviction. It does hold accountable those who use their influence to prevent others from hearing or following the truth. Verse 6 illustrates the opposite duty: the seeker must be allowed to hear the Word and be escorted safely. Verse 9 describes a system that does the reverse, using power to keep people from the road. The wrongdoing is therefore not only disbelief but obstruction.

**The Market Metaphor Includes a Better Trade**

The same Qur'an that criticizes the bad sale invites a better one. It asks, Who will lend Allah a goodly loan? He will multiply it for him, and he will have a generous reward (57:11). It also announces a commerce that saves from painful punishment: faith in Allah and His Messenger and striving in His way (61:10–11). These passages are not separate decorations. They clarify verse 9 by showing that human life always involves preference and exchange. The question is whether the person trades truth for a short gain or trades temporary possessions for lasting nearness.

The good trade is not a bargain in which Allah needs what the believer owns. It is a merciful way of describing obedience in terms human beings understand. The bad trade is equally compassionate in its warning: the one who sells revelation for advantage is not a shrewd merchant. He is the seller who gives away the only asset that could have saved him.

**The Price Is Paid by the Whole Community**

When leaders trade away revelation, the loss does not remain private. Their followers inherit a distorted map. A population taught that betrayal is normal begins to regard trust as weakness. A child raised to mock faith may never be given a fair chance to understand it. A society that rewards obstruction eventually loses the ability to recognize truth even when it appears plainly. This is why the verse's second clause matters as much as the first. The sale becomes destructive when the seller uses his position to make others enter the same market.

The believer's duty is not merely to avoid personally selling divine guidance. He must also refuse to profit from a system that blocks it. That refusal can take the form of honest speech, protection for a vulnerable person, accurate teaching, or declining a reward tied to deception. Not every application is dramatic. The essence is the same: do not make another person's access to truth the collateral for your comfort.

**A Daily Audit of What Is Being Sold**

The verse invites a practical inventory. What benefit would make me soften a clear principle? Which approval would persuade me to call falsehood wisdom? What do I ask other people to surrender so that I can keep my position? These questions expose the small prices that govern large decisions. The answer may be money, but it may also be reputation, belonging, or the avoidance of an uncomfortable conversation.

The final judgment, Evil indeed is what they have done, is brief because the transaction has already explained itself. No ornate condemnation is needed. They knew what they were exchanging, and they chose a temporary gain over revelation and then invited others to lose the road as well. The believer who remembers this verse learns to distrust any bargain that requires truth to be treated as expendable. Some gains are not gains. They are receipts for what was sold.

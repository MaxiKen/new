## Sūrah al-Tawbah 9:77

> So He caused hypocrisy to plague their hearts until the Day they will meet Him, for breaking their promise to Allah and for their lies.

**Expanded Commentary**

**A Broken Promise Can Become a Heart Disease**

The verse describes hypocrisy as a plague that remains in the heart until the meeting with Allah. The image is strong because hypocrisy spreads through the moral system. One broken promise is not merely an isolated failure; when it is defended by lies and followed by indifference, it trains the heart to live in contradiction.

The servant should not confuse a sin followed by repentance with a permanent disease. The danger lies in refusing to admit the sin, repeating it, and allowing the falsehood to become part of one's identity. A person may begin by hiding one failure and end by needing an entire public story to protect the hiding. The heart then spends more effort preserving the image than seeking Allah's forgiveness.

**Divine Requital and Human Choice Meet**

Many exegetes understand the subject of the punishment to be Allah, while another reading emphasizes that their own miserliness produced the hypocrisy. These readings are not enemies. Allah judges and allows the consequence, while the people choose the conduct that makes the consequence fitting. Divine judgment does not cancel human responsibility.

This balance protects the believer from two errors. He cannot say that his hypocrisy was forced on him and therefore blame no one. He also cannot imagine that his self-repair alone controls the final result apart from Allah's mercy. He must repent, tell the truth, and ask Allah to heal the heart. Guidance is sought through action and received as grace.

**The Promise and the Lie Stand Together**

The verse names two causes: breaking the promise to Allah and lying. The breach is not only financial. It is a refusal to let one's word bind one's conduct. The lie then protects that refusal. Together they create a person whose statements point one way while his choices travel another.

Truthfulness is therefore more than accurate information. It is agreement between speech and intention. A servant may change a plan for a valid reason, but he explains the change honestly and accepts the consequence. He does not make a promise in order to gain a benefit and then treat the promise as disposable once the benefit arrives. Allah hears the words at both ends of the bargain.

**A Plague Grows Through Repetition**

A physical plague spreads by repeated contact; a moral plague spreads by repeated habits. Each lie makes another lie easier. Each withheld obligation teaches the hand to close more quickly. Each uncorrected promise makes future promises less meaningful. The person may still speak the language of faith, but the language has become a cover rather than a guide.

The cure must therefore interrupt repetition. Stop making claims that are not intended, fulfill a neglected obligation, admit what has been concealed, and accept a smaller reputation for a greater truth. The servant may need trustworthy help because hidden habits are difficult to see alone. Humility is part of treatment; the person who insists that no disease exists cannot seek healing.

**Until the Meeting With Allah Means Time Is Not Endless**

The phrase until the Day they meet Him gives the warning a final horizon. The disease may remain throughout life if the person refuses repentance, but life itself is the period in which repair is still possible. The servant should not postpone return because the heart still feels divided. Delay gives the plague more time to become normal.

Meeting Allah means that the hidden state will no longer be hidden. The public story will have no power to edit the account. This should produce both fear and hope. Fear moves the servant to correct the lie now; hope tells him that correction is still meaningful before death closes the record.

**Repentance Restores Agreement Between Word and Deed**

Repentance begins when the servant stops bargaining with the truth. He names the broken promise, returns what he withheld, apologizes where necessary, and makes future commitments carefully. He does not demand immediate trust from people he deceived. He earns trust through consistent action while asking Allah for forgiveness.

The heart can change direction. A plague is not a permanent name for every patient. But healing requires obedience that contradicts the old habit. The servant must let his hand open, his speech become accurate, and his private worship become more real than his public claim. Allah knows the unseen, and His mercy welcomes the person who returns before the meeting arrives.

The image of a plague also teaches that hypocrisy harms relationships. People stop trusting promises, then stop offering honest counsel, and finally protect themselves through suspicion. The liar has damaged not only his own heart but the social fabric around him. Repair therefore includes patient consistency. He must keep small promises, accept verification, and avoid demanding instant confidence from those he deceived. Allah can heal what repeated falsehood damaged, but healing follows truth. The servant should not despair at the seriousness of the disease; he should fear the greater danger of becoming proud of the disease and calling its symptoms wisdom.

A promise should therefore be made only after the servant has considered the cost and the ability to fulfill it. He should say less and do more. If circumstances change, he reports the change rather than disappearing behind silence. This habit protects the heart from the first stages of hypocrisy. It also protects other people from building plans on words that were never secure. Allah's knowledge of the hidden makes every promise weighty, even when no document records it. Truthful conduct keeps the heart flexible; lies harden it until the person experiences hypocrisy as normal.

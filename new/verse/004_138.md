## Sūrah al-Nisāʾ 4:138

> Give good news of a painful punishment to hypocrites,

**Expanded Commentary**

**Give Good News of Painful Punishment to Hypocrites**

The verse states *bashshiri al-munāfiqīna bi anna lahum ʿadhāban alīmā* — give good news, announce, to hypocrites that for them is a painful punishment. *Bashshir* — to give good news, to announce joy — was explained as irony: to announce painful punishment as if good news, because the hypocrite’s own announcement is deceit — they seek to deceive God while He outwits them (4:142), they show prayer to be seen (4:142), they wait to see what happens to you and say to whichever side wins *were we not with you?* (4:141). *Al-munāfiqīn* — hypocrites — was read as those who believed then disbelieved then believed again then increased (4:137) and who are torn between neither believers nor disbelievers (4:143) and who will be in lowest depth of Fire without helper (4:145). *Bi anna lahum ʿadhāban alīman* — that for them is painful punishment — was explained as *alīm* — painful, aching — the same *alīm* prepared for those who consume orphans’ wealth after warning (4:10’s Fire) and for those who knowingly persist then say now I repent (4:18). The sūrah has traced hypocrisy from the community divided into two groups about them (4:88) where some wished you alike in disbelief (4:89) to the double-faced who plunge into *fitnah* (4:91), to the lagger who stays and envies (4:72–73), to the plotters by night (4:108); here the announce is not warning but recompense — *painful punishment* — for the territory between belief and disbelief where they tried to keep both.

**A Verse That Follows Oscillation With Irony**

Placed after *those who believed then disbelieved then increased, He will neither forgive nor guide* (4:137), this verse follows oscillation with irony: the good news for the one who increased in disbelief after believing is painful punishment. The sūrah has shown that honour and power belongs to God (4:139) where hypocrites seek honour from disbelievers as allies, and that sitting with those who deny or ridicule revelations makes you like them and gathered together in Hell (4:140); the painful punishment is the announcement for that alliance and that sitting. The sūrah has also shown that the deceitful’s night speech displeases Him (4:108) and that advocacy for self-wrongers in this life will find no advocate on the Day (4:109); painful punishment is the Day’s answer to the night’s delight. Where the sūrah elsewhere says *“do not consume wealth unjustly, God is ever Merciful to you”* (4:29) yet *“whoever does this unjustly We will burn in Fire, easy for God”* (4:30) — easy where threats are — this painful punishment is the Fire easy for the One who can remove humanity and bring others (4:133).

**The Good News That Is Not Good**

The verb *bashshir* — give good news — was explained to show that hypocrisy inverts news: what they took as good — advantage with both sides, honour through company with disbelievers (4:139), safety from you and from their people while plunging into hostility (4:91) — is announced as painful. The sūrah has said that the Devil promises only delusion (4:120) — his good news is delusion; God’s *bashshir* here is true news — painful. The contrast was linked to *“those who believe and do good, We will admit into Gardens … whose word more truthful?”* (4:122) — their good news is Gardens true; hypocrites’ good news is *alīm* true. Classical notes added that *bashshir* for punishment appears where mockery preceded — the hypocrites mock revelations with those who deny or ridicule (4:140) — so the reply mocks the mocker with true phrase.

**A Short Verse That Opens the Hypocrite Portrait**

Though only *“give good news of painful punishment to hypocrites,”* the verse opens the hypocrite portrait that follows. Immediately: *who choose disbelievers as allies instead of believers, do they seek honour through that company? Surely all honour belongs to God* (4:139) — portraits of their alliance. Then: *He has already revealed that when you hear revelations denied or ridiculed do not sit in that company else you are like them, He will gather hypocrites and disbelievers together in Hell* (4:140) — portraits of their sitting. Then: *those who wait to see what happens to you, saying to each winner were we not on your side?* (4:141) — portraits of their waiting. All are branches of the oscillation that neither forgives nor guides (4:137). For the weak this is a clearing: where you were divided into two groups about hypocrites (4:88) and told not to take them as allies unless they emigrate (4:89), the painful punishment is the news that the division is not equal — to ally with disbelievers for honour is not the community’s way, as *millata Ibrāhīma ḥanīfā* (4:125) was not alliance with many.

**Painful Punishment as Measure of Deception’s Pain**

The attribute *alīm* — painful — was explained as measure where deception sought to avoid pain: the lagger sought to avoid disaster by lagging (4:72), the emigrant sought many havens to avoid oppression (4:100), the self-wronger sought advocacy to avoid blame (4:107); hypocrisy seeks to avoid pain of standing — *qawwāmīn bi al-qisṭ* (4:135) — by belonging to neither side (4:143). The sūrah says pain is shared — *if you suffer they suffer* (4:104) — yet the hypocrite who belongs nowhere suffers twice: belonging without hope and pain without reward. The painful punishment is therefore not arbitrary but the hypocrite’s own avoided pain returned as lasting pain, as Hell without escape (4:121) was the delusion’s end. The next verses will name the seeking that makes it painful — honour sought through wrong company — and the sitting that makes company like them.

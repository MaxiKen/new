## Sūrah al-Nisāʾ 4:61

> When it is said to them, “Come to Allah’s revelations and to the Messenger,” you see the hypocrites turn away from you stubbornly.

**Expanded Commentary**

**Come to the Revelations and the Messenger**

The verse pictures a call and a response: *wa idhā qīla lahum taʿālaw ilā mā anzala Allāhu wa ilā al-rasūl* — when it is said to them, come to Allah’s revelations and to the Messenger — referring to those who claim belief yet seek judgment of false judges (4:60). The invitation *taʿālaw* — come, come up — carries elevation, as though the Book and the Messenger are higher ground. The revelations — *mā anzala Allāhu* — include the shares after debts, the dowry protection, trade by mutual consent, and the command to return trusts and judge with fairness (4:58), while the Messenger is the one who makes those matters clear, guides to noble ways, and prays for forgiveness where the wrongdoer returns (4:64). To come to both is to refer disputes where the sūrah said referral secures the best and fairest outcome (4:59). The call is plural — *qīla lahum* — it is said to them — showing that the community itself calls, not only the Prophet ﷺ alone. The believers who were told to obey and refer are the ones who say come, because the sūrah’s ethic is communal: return trusts, appoint mediators, speak kindly. The invitation therefore tests whether the claim of belief is willing to be judged by what it claims to believe.

**You See the Hypocrites Turn Away Stubbornly**

The response is *raʾayta al-munāfiqīna yaṣuddūna ʿanka ṣudūdā* — you see the hypocrites turn away from you stubbornly, barring, holding back. *Ṣudūd* doubled as verb and verbal noun — *yaṣuddūna ṣudūdā* — stresses stubborn barring, not a polite excuse. The hypocrites were explained as those who display belief with the tongue while preferring another judge with the heart, as in the report of a Jew and a hypocrite who disputed and the Jew accepted the Prophet ﷺ while the hypocrite sought a soothsayer. The turning is *ʿanka* — from you — personal to the Messenger ﷺ, showing that referral to the Book is inseparable from referral to him. The sūrah has said that God is sufficient as Guardian and Helper (4:45) and that whoever takes Satan as associate has an evil associate (4:38); to turn from the Messenger to *ṭāghūt* is to choose the second companionship. The doubling *ṣudūdā* suggests both that they turn themselves away and that they bar others, as earlier they wished you to deviate greatly (4:27) and promoted stinginess (4:37). To see them turn in that way is to see the earlier diagnosis made visible: those given a portion who trade it for misguidance (4:44) and who believe in *jibt* do not merely hold an opinion; they move.

**Why the Call Exposes the Hypocrite**

The call to *“come to what Allah revealed and to the Messenger”* exposes what seeking *ṭāghūt* had concealed. A person can claim *“we believe in what was revealed to you and before you”* (4:60) while no dispute is present; the claim is cheap where it costs nothing. When a real dispute over property, marriage, or alliance requires a judgment that will give a wife her quarter or an orphan his share, the claim is tested. The hypocrite turns because the Book’s judgment will not favour the desire that sent him to the false judge. The sūrah has prepared this test since it defined trade by mutual consent (4:29) and said deliberate devouring leads to Fire that is easy for God (4:30); the hypocrite prefers the judge whose consent is to power, not to truth. The verse therefore links to 4:59’s alternative for true believers: *if you disagree, refer it to Allah and the Messenger, if you truly believe in God and the Last Day*. The hypocrite’s turning shows the condition was not met; belief in the Last Day where a witness is brought and nothing hidden (4:41–42) would have made him come, but he turns, because he trusts the nearer gain of *ṭāghūt* more than the distant witness.

**Turning as Barring**

The emphatic *yaṣuddūna ʿanka ṣudūdā* was read as both intransitive and transitive: they turn away and they bar. The same root *ṣadda* described those who turned away from him (4:55) — *man ṣadda ʿanhu* — where Hell sufficed as blaze. Here the hypocrites are *yaṣuddūna* — barring — implying they not only refuse to come but keep others from coming by example, excuse, or alliance with those who distort words — *we hear and disobey* (4:46). The sūrah has shown that barring is the method of those who follow desires and wish you to deviate (4:27) and of those who reassure disbelievers they are better guided (4:51). The hypocrite’s stubborn turning is therefore not a private withdrawal; it is a public barricade. The Prophet ﷺ is told *you see them* — *raʾayta* — you see it plainly, implying the turning is not hidden behind courtesy. The same community that was told to give advice that will shake souls (4:63) is first told to see the turning for what it is, so that advice is not mistaken for mere quarrel. To see is the first step of the sūrah’s handling of hypocrisy, which will add turning away from them, cautioning, and admonishing within (4:63) before any public exposure.

**A Picture That Precedes the Next Disaster**

Placed after the exposure of seeking false judges (4:60) and before the scene where disaster strikes because of what hands have done and the same people come swearing they intended only goodwill (4:62), this verse sets the picture of stubborn turning before its consequence. The sūrah has said that God is All-Hearing, All-Seeing over trusts and judgments (4:58) and All-Knowing, All-Aware over mediations where harmony is desired (4:35); here seeing — *raʾayta* — is given to the Prophet ﷺ as human witness to the same. The believers who were taught to appoint mediators from both families where a split is feared will now apply the same realism to the community’s split: when the call to the Book and the Messenger is met with barring, they know whom they see. The verse does not yet command what to do with the hypocrites; it says look. That looking prepares the next steps — disaster that brings them back swearing, and the counsel to turn away, caution, and speak to their souls — without rushing to force. The stubborn turning is first named, then its fruit will be tasted, and then the way of dealing with those who turn will be shown as both firm and inward — advice that reaches the soul rather than only the tribunal.

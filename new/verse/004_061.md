## Sūrah al-Nisāʾ 4:61

> When it is said to them, “Come to Allah’s revelations and to the Messenger,” you see the hypocrites turn away from you stubbornly.

### Expanded Commentary

**The revealed standard exposes hypocrisy**

When the hypocrites are invited to the Qur’an and the Messenger, they turn away stubbornly. Their problem is not simply intellectual disagreement; they refuse the authority that would judge their motives and restrain their interests. A person may attend religious gatherings, repeat the vocabulary of belief, and still reveal his allegiance when obedience becomes inconvenient.

**Turning away as a deliberate act**

The verse depicts movement: a summons comes, and the hypocrite withdraws. This does not condemn every person who needs time to understand a difficult ruling. It addresses the stubborn refusal to let revelation adjudicate. The believer can ask questions and seek clarification while remaining open to correction; hypocrisy closes the door before the answer is heard.

The warning is communal. A society cannot preserve justice if its members invoke scripture for identity but reject it whenever property, power, or reputation is at stake. The remedy is not to spy on hearts, which belong to Allah, but to judge visible conduct fairly and to keep inviting people toward honest submission.

**Honest disagreement is better than false assent**

A community can survive a sincere question or a principled objection more easily than a promise designed to conceal sabotage. Leaders should make room for consultation so that obedience is not confused with silence. Members should speak before a decision, then honour the decision once they have accepted the process.

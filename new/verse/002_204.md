## Sūrah al-Baqarah 2:204

> There are some ˹hypocrites˺ who impress you with their views regarding worldly affairs and openly call upon Allah to witness what is in their hearts, yet they are your worst adversaries.

**Expanded Commentary**

**The Portrait Introduced**

The verse describes a person whose speech about the present life impresses those who hear him and who calls God to witness what is in his heart, and it states that he is the community's most determined adversary. The sentence opens a short passage about the conduct of the hypocrites, and the portrait it draws is the first of several.

The Book's method here is to describe a type rather than an individual, and the commentators noted that the passage concerns the nature of hypocrisy wherever it appears. What the reader receives is a description that combines an attractive exterior with an attitude that the sentence names at its close.

**The Impression Made by Speech**

The sentence states that the person's discourse about the affairs of the present life impresses the hearer, and the commentators explained the verb as covering the pleasure and the wonder that the speech produces. The description concerns the impression rather than the truth of what is said.

The Book's attention to the effect of speech on its audience gives the passage its subject, and the community's teachers read the sentence as a warning about eloquence that is not matched by conduct. What the reader receives is the account of a person whose words are persuasive and whose intentions are otherwise, and the sentence shows that the Book measures the two separately.

**The Invocation of God as Witness**

The sentence states that the person calls God to witness what is in his heart, and the commentators explained the phrase as a claim to sincerity that the person makes on his own behalf. The appeal to God's knowledge is used as a form of assurance rather than as an admission.

The Book's account of the hypocrites elsewhere describes their oaths and their professions, and the community's scholars connected the passages. The reader carries away the picture of a person who enlists the most solemn testimony available in support of a claim, and the sentence leaves the truth of the claim to God.

**The Adversary Named**

The sentence closes by describing the person as the most determined adversary, and the community's scholars explained the phrase as a description of the obstinacy with which the person opposes what the community stands for. The wording concerns a contest of positions rather than a private fault.

The Book's use of the vocabulary of conflict in this sentence gives the passage its weight, and the community's teachers read it as a statement that the harm done by the hypocrite is not less than the harm done by an open enemy. The sentence leaves him with the account of an opposition that is concealed, and the verses that follow describe what it produces.

**The Occasions Reported**

The commentators preserved reports about particular individuals to whom the sentence may have referred, and they mention a man who professed interest in the religion while saying that God knew what was in his heart. Other reports connect the passage with a delegation that the Prophet ﷺ sent to teach a group of inquirers, and the delegation was waylaid and killed on the road.

The reports give the passage a setting, and the community's teachers noted that the instruction is not confined to the occasions. The passage supplies him with the account of a text whose circumstances are described and whose warning is general, and the sentence's portrait applies to anyone whose outward profession conceals a different purpose.

**The Passage's Method**

The sentence introduces a type rather than a story, and the verses that follow describe the person's conduct when he leaves the company of those he has impressed. The Book's method of presenting a character in a few strokes and then showing what he does is used throughout the sūrah, and the reader is given the portrait before the conduct.

**The Verse's Warning**

The sentence warns the community against being impressed by the fluency of those who speak about the world, and the community's teachers used the verse when they spoke about the danger of judging a person by his manner.

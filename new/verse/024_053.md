## Sūrah an-Nūr 24:53

> They swear by Allah their most solemn oaths that if you ˹O Prophet˺ were to command them, they would certainly march forth ˹in Allah’s cause˺. Say, “˹You˺ do not ˹have to˺ swear; your obedience is well known!” Surely Allah is All-Aware of what you do.”

**Expanded Commentary**

**The Rhetoric of Exaggerated Oaths: wa-aqsamū bi-Allāh**

The divine text exposes another psychological defense mechanism employed by the hypocrites: *wa-aqsamū bi-Allāhi jahda aymānihim la-in amartahum la-yakhrujunna*. The expression *jahda aymānihim* (their most solemn, strenuous oaths) denotes the swearing of aggressive, hyperbolic oaths invoking the supreme majesty and holiness of God.

Whenever their lack of commitment was unmasked by their refusal to submit to prophetic arbitration (vv. 48–50), the hypocrites felt an urgent need to restore their social credibility within the Madinan community. Rather than offering humble repentance or rectifying their daily ethical failures, they resorted to bombastic, theatrical oaths to impress onlookers. They swore that if the Prophet ﷺ were to issue a supreme military mobilization, they would immediately abandon homes, families, and fortunes to march forth into the most dangerous battlefronts. They used the sacred Name of God as an emotional shield, hoping that the sheer volume and solemnity of their oaths would silence suspicion, deceive the innocent, and intimidate doubters into accepting their loyalty.

**Feigned Zeal for Military Mobilization: la-yakhrujunna**

The hypocrites' promise to march forth (*la-yakhrujunna*) represents the classic tactic of offering extravagant hypothetical loyalty while evading concrete, present duties. It is astonishingly easy to swear that one will perform heroic, life-threatening sacrifices in some distant, hypothetical future conflict that may never materialize.

By pledging their lives in hypothetical wars, the hypocrites attempted to distract the community from their refusal to perform humble, immediate duties: telling the truth in business, surrendering disputed land, lowering their gaze, and paying zakāh. They calculated that a dramatic declaration of military bravery would earn them the prestige of warriors while costing them absolutely nothing in the present. This performative zeal was entirely fraudulent; history repeatedly demonstrated that whenever actual battle arrived, these very individuals invented domestic emergencies, fabricated medical excuses, and sowed defeatist panic to stay behind in safety, abandoning their brothers in the hour of peril.

**The Divine Rebuff: lā taqsimū**

The divine response cuts through their melodramatic theatrics with icy, dismissive authority: *qul lā taqsimū*, say: do not swear! The imperative prohibition strips them of their verbal weaponry, rendering their protestations meaningless.

The Prophet ﷺ is commanded not to engage their arguments, debate their promises, or acknowledge their oaths. God silences their grandstanding. In the divine economy of truth, cheap oaths cannot compensate for bankrupt deeds. A community grounded in authentic moral integrity does not require citizens to swear hyperbolic oaths before they can be trusted; an honest person's simple word is sufficient, while a liar's solemn oath is worthless dust, disregarded by righteous believers. By commanding *lā taqsimū*, the revelation teaches believers to disregard theatrical protestations of loyalty and look exclusively at observable, consistent behavior across daily challenges.

**Deconstructing "Recognized Obedience": ṭāʿatun maʿrūfah**

The short sentence *ṭāʿatun maʿrūfah* carries dual layers of brilliant exegesis among classical commentators. First, as rendered by al-Ṭabarī, al-Qurṭubī, and Ibn Kathīr, it can be read as a devastating piece of divine sarcasm: Say: do not swear, for the reality of your obedience is well known!

Their track record was already thoroughly documented across earlier campaigns: they fled during crises, spread rumors during trials, and evaded court when the law disfavored them. Their "obedience" was universally recognized as hollow cowardice, cynical self-interest, and calculated deceit. Second, as understood by al-Zamakhsharī and al-Rāzī, it can be interpreted as an ethical imperative: What is demanded of you is normal, recognized, customary obedience (*ṭāʿah maʿrūfah*), not theatrical oaths! God does not demand impossible, cinematic heroics; He demands ordinary, reliable integrity—praying on time, honoring contracts, speaking truth, avoiding slander, and submitting humbly to the law in daily life, proving faith through consistent character.

**The Psychological Function of Overcompensation**

Modern behavioral psychology sheds profound light on the phenomenon diagnosed in this verse. Individuals who harbor deep, unresolved guilt regarding their character flaws routinely engage in aggressive overcompensation to mask their shame.

Aware of their inner cowardice and moral duplicity, the hypocrite experiences acute anxiety that their peers see through their disguise. To suppress this anxiety and project confidence, they adopt an exaggerated persona of hyper-loyalty. They speak louder than everyone else, propose extreme measures, and swear terrifying oaths to prove their devotion. This overcompensation is a transparent confession of internal insecurity. Sūrah an-Nūr exposes this psychological mechanism, instructing the believers not to be swayed by loud zealots whose public noise is merely an attempt to drown out the whispering condemnation of their own guilty consciences and conceal their spiritual emptiness.

**Divine Surveillance: inna Allāha khabīrun bimā taʿmalūn**

The verse seals its verdict with a sobering reminder of divine omniscience: *inna Allāha khabīrun bimā taʿmalūn*, surely Allah is All-Aware of what you do. The attribute *al-Khabīr* denotes intimate, comprehensive knowledge of inner realities, hidden motives, and subtle nuances of human conduct.

Human observers may be temporarily deceived by eloquent speeches, impressive military pledges, or solemn oaths sworn by the Kaʿbah. But the Lord of the worlds cannot be deceived by public relations campaigns or rhetorical posturing. He sees the secret cynicism festering in their chests, notes the discrepancy between their private mockery and public declarations, and records every deceitful evasion in an unalterable cosmic ledger. For the sincere believer, *al-Khabīr* is a source of serene comfort, knowing that their silent sacrifices are recognized; for the hypocrite, it is a terrifying warning that their masks will be violently ripped away on the Day when all secrets stand naked before the supreme Judge, where truth alone prevails.

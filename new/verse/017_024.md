## Sūrah al-Isrāʾ 17:24

> **And be humble with them out of mercy, and pray, “My Lord! Be merciful to them as they raised me when I was young.”**

**Expanded Commentary**

**Be Humble With Them Out Of Mercy**

Wa-ikhfiḍ lahumā janāḥa al-dhulli mina al-raḥmati — and lower for them wing of humility out of mercy. Wa-ikhfiḍ from kh-f-ḍ, to lower, humble, from khafaḍa, to lower, to be humble. Lahumā, for them, to them, dual parents. Janāḥa from j-n-ḥ, wing, side, from janāḥ, wing, as bird lowers wing. Al-dhulli from dh-l-l, humility, lowness, from dhalla, to be humble, lowly, from dhull, humility, not humiliation. Mina al-raḥmati, out of mercy, from raḥmah, mercy, from r-ḥ-m, mercy, womb. Al-Ṭabarī explains that lower for them wing of humility out of mercy, meaning be humble with them, lower yourself, as bird lowers wing for its chicks, out of mercy, compassion. Al-Qurṭubī says janāḥ al-dhull metaphor for humility, lowering wing indicates softness, humility, not arrogance, as bird lowers wing to protect and show humility. Mina al-raḥmati indicates motivation: out of mercy, compassion, not out of fear or show.

**Pray My Lord Be Merciful To Them As They Raised Me When Young**

Wa-qul rabbi irḥamhumā kamā rabbayānī ṣaghīran — and say My Lord be merciful to them as they raised me small, young. Wa-qul from q-w-l, to say, pray. Rabbi from r-b-b, My Lord. Irḥamhumā from r-ḥ-m, to be merciful, mercy, dual parents, from raḥima, to have mercy. Kamā, as, like, as. Rabbayānī from r-b-b, to raise, nurture, educate, from rabbā, to raise, same root as rabb, Lord who raises. Ṣaghīran from ṣ-gh-r, small, young, from ṣaghura, to be small. Al-Ṭabarī says pray My Lord be merciful to them as they raised me when young, meaning ask Allah to have mercy on parents as they had mercy raising you small, young, weak, needing care. Al-Rāzī says this supplication teaches gratitude and reciprocity: as they raised you with mercy when young, ask Allah to be merciful to them when old, in your care.

**Why Wing Of Humility**

Why wing? Because wing is used for protection, softness, humility. Bird lowers wing for chicks out of mercy, to protect, to show humility. Lowering wing of humility for parents indicates being humble, soft, protective, not arrogant, not raising self above them. The phrase janāḥa al-dhulli mina al-raḥmati, wing of humility out of mercy, combines humility and mercy: humility is attitude, mercy is motivation. The believer should be humble with parents out of mercy, compassion, remembering they raised him when young with mercy.

**Connection To Previous Verse About Ugh And Noble Word**

Verse 23 said if parents reach old age in your care, never say even ugh nor yell, but address respectfully. Verse 24 says be humble with them out of mercy and pray My Lord be merciful to them as they raised me when young. The connection is that previous verse prohibited disrespectful speech and commanded respectful speech, this verse commands humble attitude and supplication. Together they cover speech and attitude and prayer: do not say ugh, do not yell, say noble word, lower wing of humility out of mercy, pray for mercy for them as they raised you young. Comprehensive honouring parents.

**Link To Gratitude And Raising**

Rabbayānī ṣaghīran — they raised me when young — indicates parents' raising, nurturing, with mercy, when child small, weak, needy. This is reason for mercy prayer: as they raised with mercy when young, be merciful to them when old. The root r-b-b, to raise, same as rabb, Lord, indicates parents' raising is like Lord's raising, nurturing. Gratitude to parents for raising is part of gratitude to Lord who decreed worship none but Him and honour parents. The sūrah's theme of gratitude appears again.

**Linguistic Note On Ikhfid And Janah And Rabbayani**

Ikhfiḍ from kh-f-ḍ, to lower, humble, from khafaḍa. Lahumā, to them dual. Janāḥ from j-n-ḥ, wing, side. Dhull from dh-l-l, humility, lowness, from dhalla. Raḥmah from r-ḥ-m, mercy, from raḥima. Qul from q-w-l, to say. Rabb from r-b-b, Lord, My Lord. Irḥam from r-ḥ-m, be merciful. Kama from k-m, as. Rabbayānī from r-b-b, to raise, nurture, from rabbā. Ṣaghīr from ṣ-gh-r, small, young. The verse's structure — lower for them wing of humility out of mercy and say My Lord be merciful to them as they raised me young — with attitude command and supplication, shows that honouring parents includes humility and prayer for mercy, not only respectful speech.

**How This Verse Teaches Humility And Supplication For Parents**

This verse teaches humility with parents out of mercy and supplication for them: be humble, lower wing of humility, out of mercy, compassion, not arrogance, and pray My Lord be merciful to them as they raised me when young. Al-Qurṭubī says believer should be humble with parents, lower self, be soft, out of mercy, remembering they raised him when young with mercy, and should pray for them for mercy, both in life and after death. The practical lesson is to show humility, not arrogance, with old parents, to be merciful, and to pray for them regularly: My Lord be merciful to them as they raised me when young, as gratitude and reciprocity.

**A Reflection On Mercy As They Raised**

Kamā rabbayānī ṣaghīran — as they raised me when young — is powerful reminder that parents raised you when you were small, weak, needy, with mercy, patience, care. Now when they are old in your care, you should be merciful as they were merciful. The supplication My Lord be merciful to them as they raised me young asks Allah to be merciful to them with same mercy they showed raising you. This teaches gratitude, humility, mercy, and supplication. The believer who reflects will lower wing of humility out of mercy, not be arrogant, not say ugh, not yell, say noble word, and pray for parents' mercy as they raised him young, as part of most upright guidance that leads to mighty reward and appreciated striving.

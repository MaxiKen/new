## Sūrah al-Baqarah 2:46

> those who are certain that they will meet their Lord and to Him they will return.

**Expanded Commentary**

**Who Are the Humble? Certainty of Meeting**

“those who are certain that they will meet their Lord and to Him they will return” (2:46). Alladhīna yaẓunnūna annahum mulāqū Rabbihim wa annahum ilayhi rājiʿūn. The verse defines al-khāshiʿīn — the humble of 2:45 — not by temperament but by conviction. Yaẓunnūna here does not mean to conjecture; in this idiom it means to be certain, to hold with firm expectation. The source notes the same polyvalence for ẓanna elsewhere — to conjecture (6:116), to think (48:6), to believe — and for rajāʾ, usually hope, yet also expectation (2:75; 71:13; 18:110). Here ẓann is yaqīn’s synonym: settled certainty that softens the heart rather than merely informing it.

The definition gathers two certainties: liqāʾ — meeting — and rujūʿ — return. Both echo earlier stations: “to Him you will be returned” (2:28) after the four stages, and “then to Him you will return” as the sealing of the Adam narrative. What was stated as universal itinerary becomes here a lived conviction that makes patience and prayer light.

**Meeting the Lord — What Liqāʾ Means**

Liqāʾ al-Rabb is not spatial encounter. It is the face-to-face accountability and nearness that the Qur’an elsewhere names as seeing, meeting, and being presented: “whoever hopes for the meeting with his Lord” (18:110). To be certain of meeting is to live now as one who will be seen. That certainty reshapes present choices: the paltry price (2:41) loses appeal, mixing (2:42) becomes intolerable, and the counsel to seek help in patience and prayer (2:45) becomes practicable because help is sought from the One who will be met.

The meeting is also promise, not only reckoning. The same One to be met is al-Tawwāb al-Raḥīm (2:37), the Accepter of Repentance, Most Merciful, who taught words of return. Certainty of meeting therefore does not crush; it steadies. Humble hearts do not presume escape; they count on mercy yet prepare for hearing.

**“And to Him They Will Return” — Return as Home**

“and to Him they will return” (2:46) — wa annahum ilayhi rājiʿūn — repeats what 2:28 and the innā lillāhi formula (2:156) will soon make a communal utterance. Return is not drift but destination. The phrase gathers “you will be returned” (2:28) into a present conviction: the return is not rumor but expectation that structures days. Every patience exercised and every prayer established is already a small return before the great Return.

The Qur’an multiplies return language — “you shall be returned to Him” (29:57), “Hearts that tremble because they will return” (23:60) — to make return a habit of thought. The humble keep two times in mind at once: the appointed term of residence on earth (2:36) and the unappointed term of presentation.

**Why Certainty Lightens Burden**

2:45 said patience and prayer are “a burden except for the humble.” 2:46 explains the exception: those certain they will meet their Lord and return find the burden lightened, not because the duty shrank but because the horizon enlarged. To know with whom one has to do changes what one can bear. A duty done for a present pay of status or for avoidance of blame is heavy; the same duty done for the One to be met becomes light, even beloved.

This also answers why the sūrah pairs fear and grief curatively: “no fear… nor will they grieve” (2:38) for followers of guidance; here the humble carry that promise forward as expectation. Certainty of meeting quiets fear of the future, and certainty of return quiets grief for the past.

**From General Address to Formed Character**

2:40–46 moves from address to character formation:

- Remember favour and fulfil covenant (2:40)
- Believe the confirming revelation, refuse sale and first denial (2:41)
- Do not mix or hide while knowing (2:42)
- Establish prayer, give alms, bow together (2:43)
- Close the say-do gap while reciting (2:44)
- Seek help in patience and prayer (2:45)
- Be of those certain of meeting and return (2:46)

The seventh step is not an extra; it is the interior that makes the prior six sustainable. Without meeting-certainty, the counsels remain injunctions; with it, they become helps.

**Living the Certainty**

- Make meeting concrete. Begin prayer with the sentence “whoever hopes to meet his Lord, let him do righteous deeds” (18:110) in mind; let that hope measure the prayer just prayed.
- Practice return as daily phrase. The soon-coming “Indeed we belong to Allah, and indeed to Him we will return” (2:156) is not only for calamity; it is for every day’s small losses and gains, training the heart to refer all things back.
- Keep the polyvalence in view: ẓann as certainty reminds that faith is not mere conjecture nor cold proof but a firm, lived expectation that humbles and steadies at once.

**What Not to Overread**

The verse does not define the modality of meeting; it defines the attitude of those who are certain of it. Theological speculation about vision should not displace the character point: khushūʿ grows where meeting is counted as true, and return is counted as home.

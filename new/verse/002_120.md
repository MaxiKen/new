## Sūrah al-Baqarah 2:120

> Never will the Jews or Christians be pleased with you, until you follow their faith. Say, “Allah’s guidance is the only ˹true˺ guidance.” And if you were to follow their desires after ˹all˺ the knowledge that has come to you, there would be none to protect or help you against Allah.

**Expanded Commentary**

**A Satisfaction With One Condition**

The verse states a categorical impossibility in the grammar of never: *never will the Jews or Christians be pleased with you, until you follow their faith*. The particle combination — *lan* with the *until*-clause — produces the verse's precise claim: the displeasure is permanent, and its terminus is a single condition, the Messenger's adoption of the objectors' religion. The exegetes confirm that the verse ratifies the reading of the passage before it: the demands of verse 118 were taunt rather than inquiry (al-Qushayrī's note), because a demand whose satisfaction is defined as the demanders' conversion of the answerer was never a demand for evidence. Al-Thaʿlabī supplies the historical texture — the qibla's turn from Jerusalem to the Kaʿbah had generated resentment among both communities, and truce-proposals functioned as delaying tactics aimed at turning the Prophet ﷺ from his course — which shows the condition operating in live negotiation: concessions offered, alliances dangled, satisfaction promised, all indexed to one non-negotiable surrender. The verse's insight into negotiation deserves its full statement. Ordinary disputes have settlement points — interests traded, positions adjusted, partial satisfactions built. The dispute this verse describes has no settlement point short of total conversion, because the displeasure is not about terms but about the Messenger's existence as Messenger: a truth received from Allah is precisely what the condition demands he cease to hold. Against such a condition, every concession is waste and every partial agreement is purchase of time for the other side — which is why the verse's answer is not a counter-proposal but a definition.

**Millah: The Word for a Community's Way**

The condition's object — *their faith* — translates *millah*, and the word's range shapes the verse's claim. Millah is not bare belief but the religious law, the way that defines a community: the term the Qur'an uses across a dozen stations for the total pattern of a tradition — the millah of Abraham, upright and unaffiliated with the later communities (2:135's context, 3:95, 4:125, 16:123, and the prophetic confessions from Noah's ship to Joseph's prison, 7:88, 12:37, 14:13, 18:20, 22:78, 38:7). To follow a millah is to enter a community's whole order — its law, its direction of prayer, its definition of the righteous past. The verse's condition is therefore maximal in its wording: not adjustment of some rulings, not adoption of some practices, but entry into the way as way. The maximality exposes the condition's function. A demand that could be met partially would be a negotiating position; a demand that can only be met totally is a statement that no negotiation is intended. And the two named communities' mills, in the recital's account, share the feature that makes the condition impossible for a Messenger: each is defined in part by the rejection of what comes after its own scripture — the exclusivism the recital quoted at verse 111, the *none will enter except us* that verse 121's following-logic dismantles from within. The Messenger asked to follow either millah is asked to follow a way whose own logic denies his standing — the condition contains its own refusal, which is the verse's first proof that the plea for satisfaction was never sincere.

**“Allah's Guidance Is the Guidance”**

The answer comes in a sentence of exclusive definition: *say: Allah's guidance is the only true guidance*. The sentence's form — the definite subject, the predicate with the article of restriction — yields the reading the tradition gives it: guidance, in the unqualified sense, is Allah's guidance; whatever else bears the name bears it by courtesy or by claim. The tautology is the point. The verse does not argue that Allah's guidance is better than the alternatives — an argument would concede the alternatives' standing as comparable guidance — but defines the term: hudā is what Allah gives, and the giving has occurred, in the Book carried by the Messenger the condition demands surrender. The definition answers the negotiation from above it: the displeasure of parties who condition satisfaction on the surrender of guidance is not a cost to be weighed against guidance's benefit, because there is no second guidance to weigh it against. The recital's earlier stations supply the definition's evidence: the recognized Book rejected (89), the proof demanded and never produced (111), the Scripture cast behind backs (101) — each a case where human preference called itself guidance and the record exposed the call. The verse's *say* places the definition in the Messenger's mouth as his standing reply: not a polemical production for this negotiation only but the sentence the office repeats to every satisfaction-conditioned demand in every generation — Allah's guidance is the guidance, and the sentence ends the bargaining by ending the premise that anything else is on the table.

**The Impossible Conditional**

The verse's third sentence takes the form of a hypothetical addressed to the one person for whom it is impossible: *if you were to follow their desires after all the knowledge that has come to you*. The conditional's addressee is the Messenger ﷺ, whose following of desire after knowledge is excluded by the office the recital has defined — and the impossibility is the sentence's method. The Qur'an's rhetoric repeatedly legislates to the community through address to its head: the case stated at its maximum clarity, in the person least able to fulfill it, so that the law's severity lands undiminished on everyone for whom fulfillment is possible. The clause's terms carry the sūrah's standing aggravation. *Their desires* — ahwāʾ, the whims and appetites, the same word-family the recital contrasted with knowledge since verse 75: following desire after understanding is the named structure of informed corruption. *After the knowledge that has come to you* — the aggravator in its exact form: not ignorance but post-knowledge reversal, the sin the whole passage has been prosecuting. And the consequence clause states the reversal's total cost: *there would be none to protect or help you against Allah* — the walī and naṣīr vocabulary of verse 107, the patronage-monopoly declared there, now applied as the defector's sentence. Sūrah al-Māʾidah legislates the same prohibition to the office in the same terms: judge by what Allah has revealed, *and do not follow their desires over the truth that has come to you* (5:48). The two verses together make the conditional a law rather than a warning: for every holder of knowledge, the following of another's desire over received truth forfeits the divine protection that knowledge was held under — and the forfeiture is total, because the protection had no second source.

**The Law of Pleasing**

The verse's transfer is a standing law for every party under pressure to purchase peace with principle. The mechanism it exposes runs in three moves, and the recital has documented each. The counterparty defines satisfaction as your surrender — not adjustment, not concession, but the abandonment of what makes you a distinct holder of truth. The counterparty offers, in exchange, the pleasure the verse names: acceptance, alliance, the end of hostility, the truce presented as delayed friendship. And the exchange rate is fixed against the buyer, because the pleasure on offer is contingent on a condition that, once met, dissolves the reason for the relationship — a Messenger who follows another's millah is no longer a Messenger, and the parties who demanded the following demanded it precisely so the demand could never be met or the office survive its meeting. The verse's answer installs the believer's diplomacy on its actual foundation: kindness, justice, and coexistence with those who do not war against the faith remain commanded elsewhere in the Book; what is never available is the trade this verse describes — guidance for pleasure, knowledge for desire, protection for applause. The conditional's tail is the trade's true invoice: none to protect or help against Allah. The purchaser of human satisfaction at revelation's price finds, at the settlement date, that the satisfaction was provisional, the purchasers were insatiable, and the only protection that ever existed has been signed away — after the knowledge came, which is the clause that makes the signing unforgivable by its own terms.

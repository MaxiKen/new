## Sūrah al-Baqarah 2:120

> Never will the Jews or Christians be pleased with you, until you follow their faith. Say, “Allah’s guidance is the only ˹true˺ guidance.” And if you were to follow their desires after ˹all˺ the knowledge that has come to you, there would be none to protect or help you against Allah.

**Expanded Commentary**

**A Statement About Expectations**

The verse tells the Prophet ﷺ what to expect from the two communities that possessed scriptures, and it does so without qualification. Their satisfaction is not attainable by a change of policy on his part; it would require the abandonment of the revelation he was sent with.

The sentence is therefore an instruction about hope rather than a judgment about individuals, and it follows the sūrah's pattern of preparing its community for the shape of the opposition it will meet. The Book has already stated that a portion of the People of the Book would like the believers to return to disbelief, and this verse names the condition on which the pressure would cease.

**The Word Behind Faith**

The term translated as faith or creed is the Arabic word for a community, a way, and a religious law, and the Book uses it for the tradition of Abraham as well as for the paths of other communities. The sentence therefore concerns the whole religious identity of the two groups rather than a single doctrine.

That breadth is important for understanding the demand reported here. What was being asked was not agreement on a point of theology but conformity to another community's revealed path, which would have replaced the revelation the Prophet ﷺ was given. The verse's answer accordingly concerns the direction of guidance rather than the details of a dispute.

**"Allah's Guidance Is the Only True Guidance"**

The instruction to the Prophet ﷺ supplies the sentence he is to say, and the wording is a statement about guidance itself rather than about the two communities. Guidance comes from God, and whatever instruction is not from Him has no claim to the name.

The Book uses the same move elsewhere when it answers claims to exclusive possession of truth, and in each case it returns the reader to the source of guidance. The verse does not deny that the earlier scriptures were given by God; it denies that their later guardians own the guidance that those scriptures conveyed. The sentence also supplies the community with the answer it would need when pressed to conform. What is divine guidance is known by its source, and the Book's instruction is to name the source rather than to argue about the preferences of the two communities. That formulation leaves the discussion open to evidence rather than to pressure.

**Following Desires After Knowledge**

The warning in the second half of the sentence concerns the Prophet ﷺ himself, and the Book uses the same warning in several passages addressed to him. The phrase speaks of desires rather than of arguments, and it identifies the pressure as a pull toward convenience rather than a case to be answered.

The Book states in the following verses that even if every proof were brought, the two communities would not accept the direction of prayer of the believers, and it adds the same conditional warning. The repetition indicates that the sūrah is describing a standing situation rather than a single incident, and that the caution applies to every community that holds a revelation.

**The Condition Attached**

The warning is conditioned on knowledge having already come, and the phrase recurs in the Book where a person or community is warned against departing from what has been made clear. Knowledge of this kind settles the responsibility of the one who receives it, so that no further argument can be pleaded.

The sūrah's earlier verses used the same formula when they told the community that they had received proofs and signs. The Book's treatment of knowledge is consistent: what has been made clear is a trust, and the One who made it clear does not accept the appeal that the case was hard. The clause also marks out the boundary of the Prophet's flexibility. His dealings with the two communities were to be generous, as the surrounding verses show, but they were not to extend to accepting a standard of truth derived from resentment. The Book distinguishes between courtesy toward people and agreement with positions.

**No Protector and No Helper**

The sentence ends with a statement that the one who departs from guidance has no protector or helper against God, and the wording repeats the closing of an earlier verse in the sūrah. The repetition is deliberate, since the passage has already established that God alone owns the heavens and the earth and that no one besides Him can guard or assist.

The Book's argument is complete at this point: guidance belongs to God, the messenger delivers it, and no community can supply protection from the consequences of abandoning it. The verse therefore addresses the Prophet ﷺ in the second person while instructing every reader of the Book afterwards. The wording also echoes the sūrah's earlier statement that God alone owns the heavens and the earth and that no one besides Him can guard or assist, so that the two passages frame the section with the same claim about where protection is found.

**The Occasion Reported**

Some commentators connected the sentence with the change of the direction of prayer, reporting that the two communities resented the shift from the earlier direction to the Kaʿbah and used the dispute to press the Muslims toward their own tradition. The reports show the kind of pressure the verse anticipates, and the sentence's wording shows that the pressure was expected to continue regardless of how the community conducted itself.

The Book does not ask the believers to react to that pressure with hostility. The following passage commands the community in similar circumstances to pardon and bear with those who oppose them, and the present verse supplies the reason such an attitude is possible: the outcome of the disagreement rests with God, and no amount of accommodation on the believers' part would settle it on terms other than those He has set.

**Guidance as a Matter of Ownership**

The verse treats guidance as something God gives rather than something a community possesses, and the distinction governs the whole passage. The two communities had inherited scriptures and had come to regard the guidance they contained as their own property, so that agreement with them became the condition of their approval. The Book answers by locating guidance in its source, which no community administers. That position has practical consequences for the believers, since it frees them from the need to obtain approval and obliges them to state what has been revealed. The sūrah has already told them that even proofs would not change the attitude described here, and it will shortly command them to pardon and bear with those who hold it. What the sentence establishes is the ground on which a community can be both unyielding about the message and generous toward the people who dispute it.

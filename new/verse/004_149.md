## Sūrah al-Nisāʾ 4:149

> Whether you reveal or conceal a good or pardon an evil—surely Allah is Ever-Pardoning, Most Capable.

**Expanded Commentary**

**Whether You Reveal or Conceal a Good or Pardon Evil**

The verse states *in tubdū khayran aw tukhfūhu aw taʿfū ʿan sūʾin* — whether you reveal, display, a good, make it apparent, or conceal it, hide it, or pardon, efface, an evil, bad deed. *Tubdū khayran* — to reveal a good — was explained as doing good openly: charity, kindness, reconciliation seeking His pleasure (4:114) where secret talk except those three has no good, and standing for justice as witnesses for Him even against yourselves (4:135) where testimony is revealing good. *Aw tukhfūhu* — or conceal it, hide it — was explained as doing good secretly: the charity waived charitably as blood-money (4:92) or dowry graciously waived (4:4), the good hidden where showing would be *riyāʾ* — showing off (4:38, 4:142). Both — revealing and concealing — were read as equal where intention is His pleasure, as *“whether you reveal or conceal a good”* the sūrah says both are known. *Aw taʿfū ʿan sūʾin* — or pardon an evil, overlook, efface a bad from another — was explained as pardoning the forgivable wrong done to you, after the permission for the wronged to voice loudly (4:148) was given; pardoning after voicing is the higher. *ʿAfā* — to pardon, to efface — the same root as *ʿAfuww* — Ever-Pardoning — the Name that seals. The sūrah has shown that settlement is best where she fears *nushūz* (4:128) and that settlement involves mutual graciousness; pardoning evil is that graciousness where grievance was voiced.

**Surely God Is Ever-Pardoning, Most Capable**

The seal *fa inna Allāha kāna ʿAfuwwan Qadīrā* — surely God is, was ever, Ever-Pardoning, effacing, Most Capable, Powerful — was explained with *ʿAfuww* — much effacing, wiping away trace — the name that seals the helpless hoped to be pardoned (4:99) and the reminder that He is Ever-Pardoning before prayer where purity is sought (4:43). *Qadīr* — Most Capable, Powerful over all — was explained as that He pardons though capable to punish, as He can remove humanity and bring others (4:133) yet appreciates gratitude (4:147), as He owns heavens and earth (4:126) yet forgives what is less than association (4:116). To be *ʿAfuww Qadīr* was read as that pardoning evil after being wronged is divine imitation: you pardon though you are capable to voice and retaliate, as He pardons though He is Most Capable to punish. The pairing was linked to *“He does not like loud negative speech except by wronged, He is All-Hearing, All-Knowing”* (4:148) — the wronged is allowed to voice, yet the higher is to pardon, because the All-Hearing hears the wronged’s voicing and the *ʿAfuww Qadīr* shows the pardon that surpasses it. The sūrah has shown that the emigrant’s reward is secured though He could withhold (4:100), and that both rewards are with Him though He could give none; *ʿAfuww Qadīr* is that generosity made character for the believer.

**A Verse That Follows Permission to Voice With Invitation to Pardon**

Placed after *He does not like loud negative speech except by wronged, He is All-Hearing, All-Knowing* (4:148), this verse follows permission to voice with invitation to pardon that is higher than permission. The structure mirrors the household law: *settlement best* (4:128) after *fear of nushūz* is invitation beyond permission; *do not incline entirely* (4:129) is permission bounded; here *whether you reveal or conceal good or pardon evil* is invitation beyond the permission that the wronged may voice loudly. The sūrah has shown that most secret talks have no good except charity, kindness, reconciliation (4:114) — revealing or concealing good is that charity; and that standing for justice though against yourselves (4:135) is the good voiced where needed — pardoning evil is the good that forgives where justice’s voice could have condemned. Where the deceitful hide from people but He is with them displeased (4:108) and advocates dispute for self-wrongers (4:107), pardoning evil is the believer’s opposite: he effaces despite being wronged.

**Revealing or Concealing — Both Known, Both Rewarded**

The equalisation — *in tubdū … aw tukhfūhu* — was explained as removing show: whether good is displayed or hidden, it is good before Him who owns heavens and earth (4:126) and who is Fully Aware (4:126) and All-Hearing, All-Seeing (4:134). To reveal is not automatically *riyāʾ*, to conceal not automatically sincerity; both are *khayr* where sought for His pleasure (4:114). The verse was linked to *“everyone will find whatever good they did present”* (?) and to *“God does not wrong even an atom”* (4:40): the revealed atom and the concealed atom are both present before the *ʿAlīm*. The addition *aw taʿfū ʿan sūʾ* was read as the highest *khayr*: pardoning the evil done to you is good concealed as forbearance, yet its effacement is most like His *ʿafw*. The sūrah has said *“why should He punish if you are grateful and faithful? He is ever Appreciative, All-Knowing”* (4:147) — gratitude for good is shown by doing good whether revealed or hidden, and pardoning evil is gratitude that He is *ʿAfuww*.

**A Short Verse That Anchors Forbearance in Capability**

Though only *“whether you reveal or conceal a good or pardon an evil, surely He is Ever-Pardoning, Most Capable,”* the verse anchors forbearance in capability. The chapter has commanded to refer disputes to God and Messenger (4:59), to verify reports, to be sure before fighting, to judge by what He shows, to command charity, to stand for justice; all involve power to reveal good or to voice evil; the verse says the higher use of power is to pardon, as the Higher is Pardoning though Capable. For the weak this is the lightest of strengths: where you were wronged — the orphan whose wealth was devoured, the wife suspended, the party whose testimony was twisted — you may voice as wronged is allowed, yet if you pardon while capable, you meet the Name *ʿAfuww Qadīr* where He meets you. The next passage will turn from speech and pardon to creed: those who deny Him and His messengers making distinction between Him and messengers desiring compromise — they are true disbelievers, humiliating punishment prepared (4:150–151).

## Sūrah al-Nisāʾ 4:46

> Some Jews take words out of context and say, “We listen and we disobey,” “Hear! May you never hear,” and “Râ’ina!” [Herd us!]—playing with words and discrediting the faith. Had they said ˹courteously˺, “We hear and obey,” “Listen to us,” and “Unẓurna,” [Tend to us!] it would have been better for them and more proper. Allah has condemned them for their disbelief, so they do not believe except for a few.

### Expanded Commentary

**Words can be used as weapons**

The verse describes people who distort words, use ambiguous expressions as insults, and speak in ways designed to discredit the faith. The examples concern phrases that could sound like obedience while carrying mockery or malediction. Language is not morally neutral when a speaker deliberately chooses a form that humiliates, confuses, or undermines another person while preserving deniability.

**Respectful speech and honest disagreement**

The Qur’an contrasts the manipulative expressions with plain speech: “we hear and obey,” “listen to us,” and “attend to us.” The correction is not a ban on questions or criticism. It is a demand that disagreement be expressed without hidden contempt. Believers too can fail this standard when they use technical religious terms to insult opponents or hide a refusal to engage honestly.

**A warning against generalization**

The verse refers to some among the Jews, not to every Jew. It is part of a sustained Qur’anic method that distinguishes people by conduct: some distort, some believe, some act justly. The passage should therefore never be recruited to justify ethnic hatred or collective punishment. Its target is the abuse of language and rejection of truth.

The final description of disbelief shows that deliberate verbal sabotage is spiritually serious when it expresses settled opposition. Yet the gate of belief is not closed to anyone who abandons that conduct. The believer should guard the tongue, ask whether a phrase is truthful and fair, and prefer clarity to clever cruelty.

**Language and dignity**

A community’s verbal habits reveal whether it values truth. Sarcasm that humiliates, ambiguous insults, and religious mockery can damage trust even when a speaker later claims he meant no harm. The verse recommends plain courtesy because honest language makes repentance, correction, and coexistence possible.

## Sūrah al-Nisāʾ 4:17

> Allah only accepts the repentance of those who commit evil ignorantly ˹or recklessly˺ then repent soon after—Allah will pardon them. And Allah is All-Knowing, All-Wise.

### Expanded Commentary

**Repentance is received while the moral capacity remains**

Allah’s acceptance of repentance is described as applying to those who commit evil in ignorance and then turn soon afterward. Ignorance here is not limited to having no information. It can include recklessness, impulsiveness, the clouding of judgment by passion, or acting without considering the divine consequence. It does not make the sin permissible; it explains the condition of a person who has not made defiance into a settled identity.

“Soon” does not set a stopwatch that humans can use to declare another person hopeless. Rather, it contrasts living responsiveness with deliberate postponement. A sinner who recognizes the wrong, stops it, regrets it, and begins repair should not be told that mercy is closed merely because days or years have passed. The urgency lies in not making delay a strategy.

**The elements of genuine return**

The jurists generally describe repentance as regret, cessation of the sin, resolve not to return, and restoration of other people’s rights when the offence involved them. A person who stole must return or compensate; a person who defamed must correct the harm in a wise manner; a person who abandoned an obligation must resume it according to its rules. Mere verbal regret while the benefit of the wrongdoing is retained does not match the verse’s moral movement.

**Knowledge and wisdom**

The divine names at the end are precise. Allah knows the inward truth of the turning person—the fear, motives, and resolve that human observers cannot fully measure—and He is wise in judging both the sin and its remedy. Human beings may need safeguards and consequences even after repentance, but they must not claim access to the final judgment of hearts.

The verse keeps hope and urgency together. No one should despair because the past is ugly; no one should feel secure because repentance can be pronounced later. The door is open now, and the wise person walks through it while choice, strength, and the possibility of repair are still present.

**No despair, no delay**

A person who has sinned should begin with the next available act of obedience rather than waiting to feel worthy. At the same time, “I will repent later” is not a harmless plan. The heart becomes accustomed to the sin it repeatedly postpones. The verse’s mercy is an invitation to turn today, when the turning can still shape the rest of a life.

## Sūrah al-Naḥl 16:48

> **Have they not considered how the shadows of everything Allah has created incline to the right and the left ˹as the sun moves˺, totally submitting to Allah in all humility?**

**Expanded Commentary**

**Shadows Inclining Right And Left**

A-wa-lam yaraw ilā mā khalaqa Allāhu min shay'in yaẓillu ẓilāluhu ʿan al-yamīni wa-l-shamā'ili sujjadan li-Allāhi — have they not considered how shadows of everything Allah created incline to right and left, prostrating to Allah? Yaraw from r-ʾ-y means to see, consider, reflect. Mā khalaqa Allāhu min shay'in, whatever Allah created of things, general, includes everything with shadow: humans, animals, trees, mountains, buildings. Yaẓillu ẓilāluhu, its shadows incline, from ẓ-l-l, to shade, shadow, incline. ʿAn al-yamīni wa-l-shamā'ili, to right and left, indicating movement of shadows as sun moves from east to west, shadows move from west to east, inclining. Sujjadan li-Allāhi, prostrating to Allah, in submission. Al-Ṭabarī explains that shadows prostrate to Allah by inclining, submitting to His command, following sun's course that Allah decreed. The prostration is not like human prostration with intention, but subjection to divine command, as 13:15 says *And to Allah prostrates whoever is in heavens and earth willingly or unwillingly, and their shadows in mornings and evenings* (13:15).

**Shadows As Submitting Creatures**

Shadows are described as sujjadan li-Allāhi wa-hum dākhirūn — prostrating to Allah while they are humble, submissive. Dākhirūn from d-kh-r means to be humble, submissive, lowly, from same root as dakhara, to enter humbly. Shadows are humble because they follow light, cannot stand alone, cannot resist sun's movement. Al-Rāzī notes that shadows prostrating is sign of Allah's power: even shadows, which are non-material, intangible, obey Allah's command to incline. If shadows obey, how can humans who have intellect and will refuse? The verse uses shadows as example of universal submission: everything, even shadows, submits to Allah, while some humans refuse out of arrogance. The contrast is stark: shadows, which have no choice, submit humbly; humans, who have choice, often refuse arrogantly.

**Why Shadows Are Mentioned After Punishment Warnings**

Verses 45-47 warned plotters who feel secure from earth swallowing, torment from unexpected, seizure while going about, gradual destruction, but Lord is gracious, merciful. Verse 48 shifts to shadows prostrating humbly to Allah, showing that everything submits, even shadows, so plotters' feeling of security and arrogance is out of sync with creation. All creation submits, even shadows; why do plotters not submit? Al-Qurṭubī says shadows prostrating is visible sign that can be seen daily: look at shadows morning and evening, they incline right and left, submitting. If you see that, you should submit too. The verse therefore calls to reflection via observable phenomenon that is constant and undeniable.

**The Science Of Shadows And Sun Movement**

Shadows inclining right and left as sun moves is due to sun's apparent movement from east to west, causing shadows to move opposite. In morning, shadows long to west, at noon short, in evening long to east, inclining. The Qur'an describes this as sujjadan, prostrating, indicating that shadows' movement is not random but commanded, following divine law. Al-Rāzī discusses that shadows' prostration is by subjection to natural law that Allah created, and that law is itself command. Modern understanding of light and shadow confirms regularity, but Qur'an points to meaning behind regularity: submission to Creator. The verse invites to see shadows not as mere absence of light but as creatures submitting.

**A Sign For Those Who Reflect**

Though seal here is not explicitly for those who reflect, the question a-wa-lam yaraw, have they not considered, have they not seen, invites reflection. The same root r-ʾ-y appears in many calls to reflection. Shadows prostrating is sign that everything submits, so human should submit too. The verse also prepares for next verses about everything in heavens and earth bowing to Allah, including living creatures and angels who are not too proud. Shadows are non-living, yet they submit; living creatures and angels also submit, as next verses say. The progression is from shadows to living creatures to angels, showing universal submission, with humans who refuse being exception.

**Linguistic Note On Ẓill And Sujūd**

Ẓill from ẓ-l-l means shadow, shade, from same root as ẓullah, shade, and ẓalīl, shady. Ẓilāl plural of ẓill. Yaẓillu from ẓ-l-l means to incline, to be shady. Yamīni, right, and shamā'il, lefts, plural of shimāl, left. Sujjadan, prostrating, from s-j-d, to prostrate, submit. Dākhirūn, humble, from d-kh-r. The verse's structure — have they not considered how shadows of everything Allah created incline right and left prostrating to Allah while humble — with question and description, invites observation of daily phenomenon and interpretation as submission, guiding listener to see that even shadows submit, so he should too.

**How Shadows Teach Humility**

Shadows are humble because they depend on light and object; without light, no shadow; without object, no shadow. They cannot exist independently. Their humility, dākhirūn, teaches that all creation depends on Allah and is humble before Him. A human who sees his own shadow inclining should remember that he too depends on Allah and should be humble, not arrogant. The verse therefore uses shadow as teacher of humility, opposite of arrogance that leads to Hell as evil home for arrogant. The shadow that prostrates humbly will be in Paradise where shadows are permanent and pleasant, as 13:35 says shade permanent, while arrogant who refused to prostrate will be in Hell where no shade.


**A Lesson That Connects To Gratitude And Reflection**

This verse, like all verses in Sūrat al-Niʿam, ultimately calls to gratitude and reflection. Whether it describes emigration, patience, trust, human messengers, clear proofs, earth swallowing, or angels fearing, the purpose is to make reader recognize blessings and respond with thanks and reflection. Al-Qurṭubī notes that every warning in this sūrah is also a blessing because it warns before punishment, giving chance to change. Every promise is also a blessing because it encourages good. The sūrah weaves warning and promise, blessing and accountability, to cultivate heart that is mindful, humble, grateful. The reader who reflects on this verse with that mindset will find both knowledge and motivation to be among those who say goodness when asked what Lord revealed, and who are greeted with peace at death and enter Gardens.

**How This Verse Fits The Sūrah's Argument**

The sūrah moves from blessings to argument against shirk to warnings to examples to guidance. This verse fits in that flow by showing either reward for patience and trust, or principle of human messengers, or modes of punishment, or universal submission. Each verse adds layer to argument that Allah alone creates, sustains, knows, and deserves worship. The verse's placement after previous warnings and before next commands is deliberate: it shows consequence of previous attitude and prepares for next command. The reader is invited to see connection and to respond with gratitude, patience, trust, and asking knowledgeable, rather than with denial, arrogance, and plotting.

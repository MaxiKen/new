## Sūrah an-Nūr 24:39

> As for the disbelievers, their deeds are like a mirage in a desert, which the thirsty perceive as water, but when they approach it, they find it to be nothing. Instead, they find Allah there ˹in the Hereafter, ready˺ to settle their account. And Allah is swift in reckoning.

**Expanded Commentary**

**The Dramatic Shift: From Luminous Reality to Desert Delusion**

The revelation executes a dramatic pedagogical contrast: having illuminated the radiant pathway of believers whose hearts are niches of divine light (vv. 35–38), the text turns abruptly to the tragic reality of disbelief: *wa-alladhīna kafarū aʿmāluhum ka-sarābin bi-qīʿah*. Disbelief is not merely a lack of intellectual assent; it is an ontological dislocation from truth that invalidates human exertion.

The verse addresses the ostensible "good deeds" performed by disbelievers—their philanthropic donations, scientific discoveries, civic building projects, and acts of social compassion. While these actions appear noble, impressive, and beneficial on the surface of temporal history, they lack the foundational anchor of divine faith. Without connection to the transcendent Creator, human deeds become weightless phantoms, devoid of eternal substance and doomed to evaporate like desert mist when confronted with ultimate reality, leaving the soul impoverished and spiritually barren.

**Anatomy of the Desert Mirage: ka-sarābin bi-qīʿatin**

The Qur'an selects an exquisite natural phenomenon to illustrate the tragic illusion of unanchored deeds: *ka-sarābin bi-qīʿatin*. The *sarāb* (mirage) occurs on flat, sun-baked desert plains (*qīʿah*), where intense thermal gradients refract light rays upward, creating the deceptive optical appearance of shimmering lakes and expansive bodies of cool water.

The physics of the mirage mirrors the moral psychology of the disbeliever. The mirage exists purely as a subjective optical illusion in the observer's mind, possessing zero objective physical reality. Similarly, the disbeliever flatters himself that his charitable works, cultural achievements, and worldly legacy will secure him salvation, redemption, or eternal remembrance. He gazes across the parched expanse of his mortal life and imagines that his self-congratulatory deeds form a reservoir of refreshing merit that will quench his spiritual thirst in the Hereafter, mistaking atmospheric tricks and optical illusions for enduring substance, grounding his hopes in baseless vanity.

**The Shattered Expectation of Thirst: yaḥsabuhu al-ẓamʾānu māʾā**

The human pathos of the parable is heightened by the traveler's desperation: *yaḥsabuhu al-ẓamʾānu māʾan ḥattā idhā jāʾahū lam yajidhu shayʾā*. The traveler is *ẓamʾān*—dying of agonizing, unquenchable thirst in a barren wilderness where survival depends entirely on reaching water.

Driven by desperate hope, the exhausted traveler expends his remaining physical energy stumbling toward the shimmering lake. Yet, as he draws closer, the optical illusion dissolves before his eyes, leaving only burning sand and mocking heat: *lam yajidhu shayʾan*—he finds it to be utterly nothing! This captures the horrific moment of eschatological awakening for those who rejected God. At the moment of death and resurrection, when the soul experiences the ultimate, existential thirst for mercy, forgiveness, and deliverance, they will reach for their accumulated worldly deeds only to find them hollow, worthless dust blown away by cosmic reality, leaving them in utter ruin, bankruptcy, and inconsolable despair.

**The Inescapable Divine Reckoning: wa-wajada Allāha ʿindah**

The text delivers a terrifying theological reversal: *wa-wajada Allāha ʿindahū fa-waffāhu ḥisābah*. Expecting life-giving water, the disbelieving soul discovers that instead of finding their deeds, they find God Himself waiting to execute judgment!

The expression *wa-wajada Allāha ʿindah* (and he found Allah there) conveys absolute inescapability. Throughout his worldly existence, the disbeliever ignored God, denied His messengers, and acted as though the universe possessed no sovereign Lord. Yet at the end of the desert mirage, God is present as the supreme Judge. God pays him his reckoning in full (*fa-waffāhu ḥisābah*). Any temporal reward deserved for worldly good was already granted during mortal life in the form of health, fame, or wealth; in the Hereafter, stripped of faith, the soul faces the crushing weight of its unrepented rebellion and finds itself completely destitute before divine majesty, with nowhere left to flee.

**Cosmic Irony of Disbelieving Righteousness**

This parable exposes the profound absurdity of attempting to construct moral goodness independent of God. When an individual performs philanthropic deeds while openly denying or ignoring the Creator who provided them with hands to work, breath to live, and wealth to spend, their actions are poisoned by ultimate ingratitude and pride.

It is as if an impostor stole a king's royal treasury, distributed a few coins to beggars in the street, and then demanded the king bestow a crown upon him for his philanthropy! True righteousness begins with acknowledging the Source of all existence. Good deeds detached from faith are severed branches that wither inevitably. They may look green for a brief moment in the temporal marketplace, but they have no spiritual root system capable of sustaining them into eternity, exposing the hollow nature of godless morality and moral pretense that dissolves upon encountering absolute reality.

**Divine Velocity in Cosmic Accounting: sarīʿu al-ḥisāb**

The verse concludes with an ominous warning: *wa-Allāhu sarīʿu al-ḥisāb*, and Allah is swift in reckoning. The divine reckoning is not delayed by bureaucratic hesitation, investigative delays, or difficult calculations.

Classical commentators explained that God reckons the deeds of all creatures simultaneously in a single instant, just as He sustains and provides for all creatures at once without fatigue. The swiftness of reckoning also signifies that the transition from mortal life to divine judgment is shockingly rapid. The lifespan of a human being in this world is but a fleeting moment, and before the heedless traveler can even comprehend the tragedy of their choices, the desert road terminates, the mirage evaporates, and they stand naked before the swift, unerring justice of the Lord of the worlds, where no excuses remain, no appeals exist, and truth reigns supreme forever.

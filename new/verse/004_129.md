## Sūrah al-Nisāʾ 4:129

> You will never be able to maintain ˹emotional˺ justice between your wives—no matter how keen you are. So do not totally incline towards one leaving the other in suspense. And if you do what is right and are mindful ˹of Allah˺, surely Allah is All-Forgiving, Most Merciful.

**Expanded Commentary**

**You Will Never Be Able to Be Just Between Wives Even If Keen**

The verse states *wa lan tastaṭīʿū an taʿdilū bayna al-nisāʾi wa law ḥaraṣtum* — and you will never be able, you will not be capable, to be just, equitable, even, between women — wives — even if you are keen, eager, desirous. *Lan tastaṭīʿū* — never be able — was explained as incapacity in emotional justice — *ḥubb*, inclination of heart, inclination that cannot be weighed — even where legal justice — maintenance, night, kindness — is possible. *An taʿdilū* — to be just, to equalise — was read as to make equal in love, in inclination, in the heart’s turning, which the sūrah elsewhere shows even the Prophet’s heart inclined where he was taught to say *“O God, this is my division where I have control; do not blame me where I do not”* (Abū Dāwūd 2134, graded ḥasan). *Wa law ḥaraṣtum* — even if you are keen, even with eagerness — was explained as that keenness does not create capacity where God created inclination unequal, as humanity was created weak (4:28) and *shuḥḥ* — selfishness — is ever present with souls (4:128). The sūrah has shown that marriage limits — two, three, four where justice is feared else one (4:3) — assume justice as condition; here the condition is refined: legal justice remains commanded, emotional equality is declared impossible, so that the one who cannot equalise love is not told the faith is divorce, but is taught how to incline.

**So Do Not Incline Entirely, Leaving the Other Suspended**

The command *fa lā tamīlū kulla al-mayli fa tadharūhā ka al-muʿallaqah* — so do not incline, lean, entirely, with total inclining, so that you leave her as the suspended, hanging — was explained with *mayl* — inclination, leaning, bias — the same *mayl* the hostile wished you deviate — *mayl ʿaẓīmā* — greatly (4:27) and where night plotting wished you neglect for a sweep — *maylatan wāḥidah* (4:102). *Kulla al-mayli* — entirely of inclination — was read as total turning to one and away from another: not sharing nights, not providing, not speaking, so that the wife remains *muʿallaqah* — suspended, hanging — neither married in full nor divorced free, as if tied to a rope without ground. *Fa tadharūhā ka al-muʿallaqah* — so you leave her as the hanging one — was explained as the sūrah’s protection of the wife who earlier feared *nushūz* or *iʿrāḍ* (4:128): the remedy there was settlement where both sought it, which is best; here the remedy is prohibition of total inclining that creates the hanging. The sūrah elsewhere says *“do not incline towards one leaving the other in suspense”* is the counterpart to *“be mindful of God”* where settlement was taught with *shuḥḥ* present (4:128); not to incline entirely is to practice *taqwā* where love cannot be equal.

**And If You Do What Is Right and Are Mindful — He Is All-Forgiving, Most Merciful**

The encouragement *wa in tuṣliḥū wa tattaqū fa inna Allāha kāna Ghafūran Raḥīmā* — and if you set right, reconcile, do good, and are mindful, then indeed God is All-Forgiving, Most Merciful, was ever — was explained with *tuṣliḥū* — to set right, to reconcile, to do good — the same *iṣlāḥ* as reconciliation between people (4:114) and mediators desiring reconciliation (4:35) and settlement best (4:128). *Wa tattaqū* — and are mindful, God-wary — was read as the sūrah’s *taqwā* that began with mindfulness of the Lord who created from single soul (4:1) and that seals every marriage rule: where emotional justice is impossible yet legal justice is commanded, mindfulness and setting right cover the shortfall. *Fa inna Allāha kāna Ghafūran Raḥīmā* — He is All-Forgiving, Most Merciful — was explained as the Seal that excuses what you cannot equalise while holding you to what you can — not to incline entirely. Where the intentional killer was sealed with anger and curse (4:93) and the self-wronger with not loved (4:107), the husband who cannot equalise love yet does not incline totally and is gracious and mindful is sealed with forgiveness and mercy. The same pair sealed the fear that settlement requires graciousness where *shuḥḥ* is present (4:128) with awareness — *Khabīr* — here with mercy, matching the earlier limit of justice where the sūrah says *“do not crave what God gave some over others; ask Him for bounty”* (4:32): what you cannot equalise, seek from His forgiveness.

**A Verse That Places Limit Beside Permission**

Placed after settlement is best where she fears *nushūz* or turning away and selves were made present with *shuḥḥ* (4:128), this verse places limit beside permission. The sūrah permitted settlement waiving part of right to keep marriage; here it acknowledges that perfect equalising of hearts after settlement remains impossible. The two verses together teach the household ethic after the public ethic of judging by Book (4:105) and most secret talks (4:114): private settlement is best, yet private hearts remain unequal, so do not make inequality total. The sūrah has shown that the owner of heavens and earth is Fully Aware of everything (4:126) and Whatever good you do is well known to Him (4:127); He knows the inclination you cannot weigh and He is All-Forgiving for it where you do not incline entirely and you set right and are mindful. For the weak this is protective: the wife suspended — *muʿallaqah* — is named as a wrong to be avoided, as orphan women deprived were protected from being married for profit (4:3, 4:127); protection is by prohibition of total inclining.

**The Incline That the Sūrah Has Named Two Kinds**

The word *mayl* was traced: the hostile wish you incline greatly from God’s way (4:27) is one *mayl* — away from Him; the husband’s incline totally to one wife leaving the other suspended is the other *mayl* — within what He permitted — between wives. The first *mayl* is prohibited absolutely — do not follow desires to deviate — the second is limited — do not incline entirely. The sūrah that warns not to deviate by desire and not to follow other than believers’ path (4:115) here warns that even lawful love can become unlawful neglect where it is total. The medicine is the sūrah’s general medicine after exposing night plotting and throwing sin — *whoever wrongs themselves then seeks finds Him All-Forgiving* (4:110) — here expressed as *if you set right and are mindful He is All-Forgiving, Most Merciful*. The next verse will set the other face: if they separate, He will enrich both from His bounties, and He is Ever-Bountiful, All-Wise (4:130), so that staying with settlement and leaving with enrichment are both under mercy.

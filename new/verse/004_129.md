## Sūrah al-Nisāʾ 4:129

> You will never be able to maintain ˹emotional˺ justice between your wives—no matter how keen you are. So do not totally incline towards one leaving the other in suspense. And if you do what is right and are mindful ˹of Allah˺, surely Allah is All-Forgiving, Most Merciful.

### Expanded Commentary

**Outward equality and inward feeling**

The verse states that complete emotional justice between multiple wives is beyond human capacity, even for one who desires it. Feelings cannot be divided with mathematical precision. The admission is realistic, but it is not permission to abandon outward justice.

The warning not to incline entirely toward one wife so that another is left suspended demands equal provision, time, housing, and recognized marital rights. A husband cannot defend deliberate neglect by saying that love is involuntary. The law controls what can be controlled and asks mercy regarding what cannot.

The verse is also a reason for caution before entering a plural marriage. If the person cannot maintain the practical balance, he must not use the impossibility of perfect feeling as an excuse. The closing promise of forgiveness and mercy encourages those who notice their failures to correct them rather than become defensive.

**The practical limit of plurality**

The admission that emotional equality is impossible should make a person more cautious, not more confident. Control the outward harms that feeling can produce, seek counsel before neglect becomes habitual, and do not leave a wife suspended. The verse’s mercy is available to the person who notices bias and works to correct it.

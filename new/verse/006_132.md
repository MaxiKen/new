## Sūrah al-Anʿām 6:132

> They will each be assigned ranks according to their deeds. And your Lord is not unaware of what they do.

**Expanded Commentary**

**The Assignment of Ranks**

They will each be assigned ranks according to their deeds. The verse states the outcome of the reckoning for the assembled people, and it does so in the vocabulary of ranks or degrees. The Book uses the same term in its other accounts of the Hereafter: the sūrah of the Sand Dunes says that each of the two groups will be ranked according to what they have done so that God may fully reward all of them and that none will be wronged (46:19); the sūrah of the Family of Imran states that they each have varying degrees in the sight of God and that God sees what they do (3:163); and the sūrah of the Repentance places those who believed, emigrated, and strove with their wealth and their lives in greater rank before God (9:20).

The Book's teaching on ranks appears in its treatment of the present life as well. The last verse of this sūrah states that God has placed human beings as successors on earth and elevated some of them in rank above others as a test (6:165), and the sūrah of the Ornament says that God has distributed among people their livelihood in the present life and raised some above others in degrees. What the verse in front of us states is the arrangement of the Hereafter, where the ranks correspond to deeds rather than to the circumstances a person was given.

**The Correspondence Between Deeds and Ranks**

The sentence states that the assignment follows what each person did, and the Book's accounts of the reward and the punishment consistently describe the outcome as measured to the conduct. The sūrah of the Sand Dunes adds the declaration that none will be wronged, and the sūrah of the Cattle states elsewhere that God does not wrong anyone by the weight of an atom.

The Book's emphasis on that correspondence serves its argument about the justice of the reckoning. The verses that surround this one describe the warning given to the communities and the confession of the people who received it, and the sentence in front of us states that the response to the warning is what the ranks measure. Nothing in the arrangement depends on the standing a person held in the world or on the family into which he was born.

**The Knowledge Attached to the Sentence**

And your Lord is not unaware of what they do. The clause states the ground on which the ranks are assigned, and the Book uses the same formula at the end of many of its passages about the deeds of the people it addresses. The sūrah of the Cow uses it after describing the hardness of hearts and the corruption of the Scripture (2:74; 2:85), and the sūrah of the Family of Imran attaches it to its statement about the varying degrees.

The formula is the Book's way of closing an account of human conduct without leaving the impression that the conduct goes unobserved. The sūrah has described God's knowledge of the leaf that falls and the grain in the darkness (6:59), and the sentence in this verse applies that knowledge to what people do, which is the point on which the assignment of ranks depends.

**What the Verse Says About the Reckoning**

The verse presents the judgement as a settlement in which every person's situation is placed where it belongs. The Book does not describe the Hereafter as an undifferentiated reward or punishment; it describes degrees and orders, and it attaches them to the deeds of the people who receive them. The sūrah's audience, which measured standing by descent and wealth, is being told that the ranks of the Hereafter are produced by a different measure.

The Book's descriptions of the day of judgement include the same feature in other sūrahs, where people are said to be shown their records and to be sorted according to what they did. The sūrah of the Emissaries and the sūrah of the Inevitable describe the people of the right hand and the people of the left as distinct groups, and the sūrah of the Cave states that God has prepared for the believers the highest place in the garden.

**The Verse's Place in the Passage**

The verse follows the address to the assembled jinn and humans and their confession, and it precedes the statement that the Lord is the Self-Sufficient, Full of Mercy, able to replace a people with another (6:133). The three verses give the reckoning its context: the warning that makes it just, the ranks that make it exact, and the sufficiency of God that makes it independent of the people who are judged.

The passage therefore completes the section that began with the gathering of the jinn and their human associates. The partnership, the term, the fire, the alliance, the address, the confession, the ranks, and the sufficiency of God follow one another as a single account, and the verse in front of us states the measure by which the account's outcome is set.

**The Transition to the Next Verse**

The next verse tells the Prophet that his Lord is the Self-Sufficient, Full of Mercy, and that if He wills He can do away with the audience and replace them with whoever He wills, just as He produced them from the offspring of other people (6:133). The statement of God's self-sufficiency follows directly from the assignment of ranks, since the judgement described in this verse is not a transaction that benefits God or diminishes Him.

The sūrah's later verses continue with the promise that what the audience has been warned about will come to pass and that they will have no escape (6:134), and then with the instruction to the Prophet to tell his people to persist in their ways while he persists in his (6:135). The verse in front of us supplies the measure for the reckoning those passages announce.

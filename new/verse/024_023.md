## Sūrah an-Nūr 24:23

> Surely those who accuse chaste, unsuspecting, believing women are cursed in this life and the Hereafter. And they will suffer a tremendous punishment

**Expanded Commentary**

**The Threefold Description of the Inviolable Believer: al-muḥṣanāt, al-ghāfilāt, al-muʾmināt**

The verse establishes the absolute sanctity of innocent human dignity through three profound descriptive attributes: *al-muḥṣanāt*, *al-ghāfilāt*, and *al-muʾmināt*. Each term illuminates a specific dimension of moral inviolability. *Al-muḥṣanāt* signifies women who reside within the fortified castle of recognized chastity, free status, and legal protection. *Al-muʾmināt* anchors their standing within the sanctified covenant of faith, making them autonomous citizens of the divine commonwealth whose honor is protected by divine law and communal solidarity across generations.

The central descriptive term *al-ghāfilāt* carries an exquisite spiritual luminosity. In ordinary discourse, *ghaflah* denotes heedlessness or negligence. In this context, however, classical commentators such as al-Zamakhsharī and al-Qurṭubī explained that it denotes complete innocence, purity of heart, and total unacquaintance with deception, guile, or shameless conduct. An unsuspecting woman is so profoundly rooted in virtue that the very concept of illicit intimacy never crosses her conscious mind. To target such a guileless soul with fabricated accusations is an act of sheer demonic malice, calculated deceit, and unprovoked cruelty.

**The Sentence of Cosmic Banishment: luʿinū fī al-dunyā wa-al-ākhirah**

The divine decree against those who defame such innocent souls is uncompromising: *luʿinū fī al-dunyā wa-al-ākhirah*, they are cursed in this world and the Hereafter. The pronunciation of *laʿnah* signifies absolute expulsion, banishment, and total abandonment by divine mercy. Unlike the general legislation of slander in verse 4—which prescribed eighty lashes and testamentary disqualification while explicitly holding open the door of civil and spiritual redemption through repentance—verse 23 issues a categorical condemnation.

This cosmic curse strips the malicious slanderer of spiritual shelter across both realms of existence. In this world, they are pursued by public disgrace, legal humiliation, and divine exposure. In the Hereafter, they are cast into the outer darkness of eternal alienation from the Creator. The absolute phrasing emphasizes that when slander is deployed with calculated malice against the purest members of society, it touches upon an atrocity that shakes the very throne of divine justice, bringing ruin upon the slanderer in both realms without relief.

**The Tremendous Punishment Awaiting the Unrepentant**

The verse culminates in a terrifying eschatological decree: *wa-lahum ʿadhābun ʿaẓīm*, and for them is a tremendous, overwhelming punishment. The use of the indefinite adjective *ʿaẓīm* conveys a magnitude of agony that defies human comprehension. When a person uses their tongue to demolish the social existence and emotional peace of an innocent soul, they commit an act of spiritual murder.

The penalty decreed by divine justice matches the profound devastation of the crime. Those who sought to plunge innocent believers into earthly despair, tearing their domestic sanctuaries apart and subjecting them to the mockery of the public square, will find themselves submerged in an ocean of unbearable divine retribution in the Hellfire. The verse forces every human being to recognize that the casual entertainment of defamatory speech carries an eternal price that no soul can endure, warning society against moral recklessness and cruel gossip.

**The Special Sanctity of the Mothers of the Believers**

Classical exegetes, including Ibn ʿAbbās, al-Ḍaḥḥāk, and ʿIkrimah, maintained that this specific verse descended as a unique, non-abrogable ruling regarding the wives of the Prophet ﷺ. They argued that an attack on the honor of the Prophet's ﷺ wives is not an ordinary act of civil defamation; it is a direct assault on the Messenger ﷺ himself, which constitutes theological rebellion and disbelief (*kufr*).

Under this interpretive framework, whoever accuses the Mother of the Believers ʿĀʾishah—after God Himself has established her innocence in the eternal text of the Qur'an—is expelled from the fold of Islam, earning the eternal curse of God that cannot be erased by ordinary civil repentance. This position highlights the extraordinary theological sanctity surrounding the prophetic household, demonstrating that the Mothers of the Believers stand in a unique category of inviolability that permanently shields the integrity of the prophetic mission from domestic subversion and malicious intrigue.

**The Universal Application to Unsuspecting Innocence**

In contrast to the particularist interpretation, the majority of classical authorities, including Imām al-Ṭabarī and Fakhr al-Dīn al-Rāzī, held that while the verse was occasioned by the slander against ʿĀʾishah, its legal and moral force encompasses all chaste, unsuspecting believing women until the Day of Resurrection. The grammatical structure employs a general plural that establishes a universal moral canopy.

Under this universal reading, the verse redefines the ethical expectations of society. A woman of pure character is not required to live in a state of chronic paranoia, constantly formulating legal defenses or structuring her movements to satisfy cynical busybodies. Her simplicity, modesty, and devotion to God should be her sufficient defense. Anyone who takes advantage of a righteous woman's guileless nature to manufacture scandalous narratives incurs the full, terrifying weight of the divine curse, regardless of the victim's social rank or worldly influence.

**The Divine Protection of Guileless Souls**

Reflecting upon the perennial human condition reveals the profound spiritual mercy embedded within this statute. In a world dominated by political intrigue, cynical opportunism, and manipulative rhetoric, innocent and guileless people are uniquely vulnerable. They lack the ruthless rhetorical skills, tribal alliances, or media power needed to defend themselves against coordinated campaigns of character assassination.

Verse 23 establishes that God Himself acts as the personal advocate and cosmic guardian of the unsuspecting. When human society fails to shield the pure-hearted from predatory tongues, heaven intervenes with an unyielding curse. The slanderer may believe they have scored an easy victory over a defenseless victim, but they will discover that they have declared war against the Almighty Creator, who guarantees that absolute justice will inevitably overtake the wicked, granting everlasting victory to the pure and vindicating their honor.

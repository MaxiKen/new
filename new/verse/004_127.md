## Sūrah al-Nisāʾ 4:127

> They ask you ˹O Prophet˺ regarding women. Say, “It is Allah Who instructs you regarding them. Instruction has ˹already˺ been revealed in the Book concerning the orphan women you deprive of their due rights but still wish to marry, also helpless children, as well as standing up for orphans’ rights. And whatever good you do is certainly well known to Allah.”

### Expanded Commentary

**The Prophet is asked again about women**

The answer returns to the orphan women and vulnerable children at the centre of the opening legislation. People ask about women, and revelation says that Allah gives the instruction. The repetition shows that the earlier rules did not exhaust the problem: guardians continued to need correction about dowry, marriage, inheritance, and the protection of those unable to defend themselves.

The verse specifically mentions orphan girls whose due rights are withheld while guardians still desire to marry them. This exposes the conflict of interest. A guardian may want the girl as a wife precisely because control of her property makes the arrangement profitable. Revelation separates care from desire and demands that her legal rights be honoured.

The command to stand up for orphans’ rights broadens the duty beyond avoiding theft. It calls for advocacy, fair valuation, education, and protection from coercion. Whatever good people do is known to Allah, including work that receives no public thanks. The verse makes the treatment of vulnerable women a revealed test of goodness.

**Advocacy for orphan girls**

The chapter returns to the orphan because legal reform must be defended after the first command is heard. A girl’s guardian may know the rule and still seek a profitable marriage. Advocacy requires independent voices, access to information, and people willing to challenge a respected guardian when the girl’s consent or property is threatened.

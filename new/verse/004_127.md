## Sūrah al-Nisāʾ 4:127

> They ask you ˹O Prophet˺ regarding women. Say, “It is Allah Who instructs you regarding them. Instruction has ˹already˺ been revealed in the Book concerning the orphan women you deprive of their due rights but still wish to marry, also helpless children, as well as standing up for orphans’ rights. And whatever good you do is certainly well known to Allah.”

**Expanded Commentary**

**They Ask You Regarding Women — God Instructs**

The verse states *wa yastaftūnaka fī al-nisāʾi quli Allāhu yuftīkum fīhinna* — they ask you, they seek your ruling, regarding women — say, God instructs you, gives ruling, regarding them. *Yastaftūnaka* — to seek *fatwā*, ruling — was explained as the community asking the Prophet ﷺ about women’s rights, as earlier at the chapter’s start they were taught to marry orphan women with justice or marry others two, three, four where justice is feared (4:3), to give dowries graciously (4:4), and to return orphans’ property with witnesses (4:6). *Quli Allāhu yuftīkum fīhinna* — say, God gives ruling regarding them — was read as referral to the Owner to whom belongs whatever is in heavens and earth and who is Fully Aware of everything (4:126); instruction is not by wishes (4:123) nor by Satan’s ordering to alter creation (4:119), but by the One who created from a single soul and spread men and women (4:1). The sūrah has shown that the Book was sent in truth to judge between people by what He shows (4:105) and that wisdom was taught (4:113); here the Book judges between men and women as between peoples. Early reports linked the asking to questions about orphan girls under guardianship where the guardian desired to marry them without full dowry; the verse ties past instruction to present asking, showing a single instruction extended.

**Instruction Already Revealed — Orphan Women Deprived Yet Wished to Marry**

The reference *wa mā yutlā ʿalaykum fī al-kitābi fī yatāmā al-nisāʾi allātī lā tuʾtūnahunna mā kutiba lahunna wa targhabūna an tankiḥūhunna* — and what is recited to you in the Book concerning the orphan women you deprive — do not give — of what is prescribed, due, for them yet desire to marry them — was explained as the earlier ruling in 4:3 — *if you fear not to be just to orphan women, marry other women* — and in 4:19–21 — *not to inherit women against will and firm commitment*. *Mā kutiba lahunna* — what is prescribed, written for them — was read as dowry, inheritance, kind maintenance, the obligatory shares for women whether little or much (4:7). *Targhabūna an tankiḥūhunna* — you desire to marry them — was explained with two inclinations: *targhabūna fīhinna* — desire to marry the beautiful or wealthy orphan for her property while depriving her right — and *targhabūna ʿanhunna* — desire against them, to leave them unmarried to keep their wealth — both of which the guardian might do; the sūrah pairs both as injustice. The instruction already recited — *mā yutlā* — was read as showing the Qur’an as ongoing recital: what was revealed at the start is re-cited as answer to new asking, so that the instruction about women is not new but the same justice extended.

**Also Helpless Children and Standing Up for Orphans’ Rights**

The continuation *wa al-mustaḍʿafīna mina al-wildāni wa an taqūmū li al-yatāmā bi al-qisṭ* — also helpless children, also that you stand up, establish, for orphans with justice — was explained as the same inclusive justice seen where men, women, children were named for pardon and haven (4:98, 4:75). *Mustaḍʿafīn mina al-wildān* — helpless among children, deemed weak children — were those without guardian, whose shares hypocrites might diminish. *Wa an taqūmū li al-yatāmā bi al-qisṭ* — and that you stand up for orphans with equity, with justice, with the balance — was read as the command to *return trusts, judge fairly* (4:58) applied to orphans as the prime trust: the sūrah that warns against devouring orphans’ wealth as fire in bellies (4:10) and that commands to test orphans until judicious then return property with witnesses (4:6) here generalizes: stand up — *qūmū* — as you stand for prayer at appointed time (4:103) and for justice though against yourselves (4:135). The sūrah has said that God is sufficient as Witness and Trustee (4:132, 4:79); to stand for orphans *bi al-qisṭ* is to stand with Him as witness.

**And Whatever Good You Do Is Well Known to God**

The seal *wa mā tafʿalū min khayrin fa inna Allāha kāna bihī ʿalīmā* — and whatever good you do, of good, then indeed God is, was ever, All-Knowing of it — was explained as the encouragement matching the warning. *Mā tafʿalū min khayr* — whatever good you do — was read as the good commanded for secret talks — charity, kindness, reconciliation seeking His pleasure (4:114) — and the good of submitting face to Him doing good as Abraham (4:125) and the good male or female with faith entering Paradise without wrong of speck (4:124). *Fa inna Allāha kāna bihī ʿalīmā* — He is All-Knowing of it — was explained as that He who owns heavens and earth (4:126) and is Fully Aware of everything knows the speck of good as He knows the speck not wronged (*naqīrā* — 4:124) and does not wrong even an atom but multiplies (4:40). The sūrah has said *“you do not know who is more beneficial”* among parents and children (4:11); here *He* knows the good done for orphan women deprived yet desired, for helpless children, for orphans stood for. For the weak this is the promise: good for the weakest — the orphan girl, the helpless child — is the most known.

**A Verse That Follows Ownership With Instruction for the Weakest**

Placed after *to Him belongs whatever is in heavens and earth and He is Fully Aware* (4:126), this verse follows ownership with instruction for the weakest owned: women asked about. The sūrah that opened by creation from a single soul and honouring ties (4:1) and that will say humans are ever inclined to selfishness yet gracious mindfulness is All-Aware (4:128) here says: the One to whom all belongs instructs you regarding them, and instruction already recited about orphan women and helpless children is that you give what is prescribed or marry others with justice, and stand for orphans with equity. The circle closes where the sūrah began with orphans’ wealth and marriage justice (4:2–4); the question reopens it, and the answer cites the earlier verses as still recited, still ruling, so that the chapter’s ethic — return trusts, judge fairly, verify reports, be sure before fighting, do not advocate for deceit — is again applied to the household. The next verses will apply that ethic within marriage: if a woman fears indifference or neglect, no blame to seek settlement which is best, though humans incline to selfishness, yet gracious mindfulness is All-Aware (4:128).

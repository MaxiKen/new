## Sūrah al-Aʿrāf 7:6

> We will surely question those who received messengers and We will question the messengers ˹themselves˺.

**Expanded Commentary**

**The Recipients Are Questioned**

The verse states that God will question those to whom the message was sent, and the questioning is placed after the destruction of the communities in the preceding verses. The Book mentions the interrogation of the recipients of revelation in several places: the sūrah of the Bee says that they will be questioned about what they used to do, and repeats the statement later in the same sūrah (16:56; 16:93); the sūrah of the Night Journey states that every person will be questioned about what he heard and saw (17:36); the sūrah of the Spider says that those who were given the message will be asked (29:13); and the sūrah of the Ornament says that the recipients of the reminder will be questioned (43:44).

The questioning is not the gathering of information, and the verse that follows states the reason plainly when it says that God will recount the deeds with knowledge and that He was never absent (7:7). The interrogation serves the reckoning that the sūrah describes in its following verses, where the scales are set and the deeds are weighed.

**The Messengers Are Questioned**

The verse adds that the messengers themselves will be questioned, and the tradition of the commentary explains that their questioning concerns the delivery of the message rather than the response of the people. The Book states in several passages that the duty of the Prophet is only to convey: the sūrah of the Family of Imran says the messenger's duty is to deliver (3:20), the sūrah of the Table repeats it twice (5:92; 5:99), the sūrah of the Thunder says the messenger's duty is only to deliver the message (13:40), and the sūrah of the Bee makes the same statement twice in a single passage (16:35; 16:82).

The distinction is important for the sūrah's argument. The messenger is not answerable for the refusal of his people, and the people are not relieved of responsibility by the messenger's diligence. The verse in front of us places both parties in the same hearing, and the reckoning of each is conducted on the terms of what each was given.

**The Questioning in the Qur'an and the Question It Raises**

The Book's statements about the judgment include passages that appear to deny any questioning at all. The sūrah of the Merciful says that on that day neither human nor jinn will be questioned about his sin (55:39), and the Book elsewhere describes the interrogation as a confrontation with a record that leaves nothing out. The tradition of commentary addresses the apparent conflict by distinguishing between the interrogation that establishes facts and the questioning that serves as rebuke.

The resolution rests on the Book's own description of the day. The deeds of every person are recorded in a book that he will be given in the Hereafter, and the sūrah of the Night Journey states that every person will be called with his record (17:71); the sūrah of the Inevitable describes the one who is given his record in his right hand and the one who is given it behind his back (69:19). What the questioning adds is the confrontation with the record, and what it produces is the acknowledgment that the condemned themselves supply.

**The Rebuke That the Questioning Serves**

The tradition of commentary understands the questioning of the Hereafter as a means of rebuke, and the Book's accounts of the day support that reading. The condemned admit their guilt, the followers blame their leaders, and the leaders blame the followers, in a sequence the sūrah itself describes later in the chapter (7:38–39). The questioning that opens the reckoning sets that exchange in motion.

The Book's description of the final address to the condemned includes the reminder of what they were given, and the sūrah of the Cave reports the words spoken to those who are brought before their Lord after their deeds have been presented. The verse in front of us states the principle behind those scenes: the recipients of revelation are asked, and the messengers are asked, and the record decides between them.

**What the Verse Says About the Community**

The verse speaks to the sūrah's audience about the position it occupies in the history of revelation. The community in Makkah has received a messenger, and the verse states that the receipt of a message is a matter that will be examined. The Book's warnings about the earlier communities therefore apply to the present audience on the terms the verse states, since the question the earlier communities answered late is the question this audience is asked now.

The Book's treatment of the community of the Prophet's followers in the same terms appears in the passages that describe the covenant taken from the children of Israel and the covenant taken from the believers. The verse in front of us states the common feature of every generation that has received a message: each is questioned about what it did with what it heard.

**Where the Verse Sits**

The verse stands sixth in the chapter's opening section, and it introduces the theme of the final accounting that the two following verses develop. The verses before it have described the destruction of the communities and their confession; the verses after it describe the knowledge with which God recounts the deeds and the justice with which the scales are set.

The reader of the sūrah's opening is thus given the full arc of the warning in six verses: the Book is sent down, the community is commanded to follow it, the communities that refused were destroyed, they confessed too late, and the reckoning of both the warned and the warners follows.

**The Transition to the Next Verse**

The next verse states that God will recount to them their deeds with knowledge and that He was never absent (7:7). It supplies the ground on which the questioning of the previous verse proceeds, and it names the attribute of knowledge that makes the account complete.

The two verses together place the day of judgment in the hands of a Lord who knows. The question is asked and the record is exact, because nothing that the recipients of the message did was hidden from the One who sent it.

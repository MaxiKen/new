## Sūrah al-Anʿām 6:85

> Likewise, ˹We guided˺ Zachariah, John, Jesus, and Elias, who were all of the righteous.

**Expanded Commentary**

**The Second Group in the Catalogue**

Likewise, We guided Zachariah, John, Jesus, and Elias, who were all of the righteous. The sentence continues the list from the previous verse without introducing a new subject, and the connecting word makes the catalogues one continuous act of guidance. The four names belong to the later part of the Israelite prophetic tradition, and they sit in the same list as David and Moses with no distinction drawn between them.

The arrangement is deliberate. The sūrah's audience argued with the Prophet about the standing of particular prophets, and the Christians of the region had raised Jesus to a status the Book rejects. The verse places him in a line with Zachariah and John and gives the whole group one description: they were all of the righteous. The Book makes the same point in the sūrah of the Family of Imran, where the believers are told that they make no distinction between any of the prophets (3:84).

**The Pair of Zachariah and John**

Zachariah and John are named together because their stories are joined, and the Book tells that story in detail in the sūrah of Maryam. Zachariah prayed for an heir in his old age, the prayer was answered with John, and the Book describes the household as one that used to race in doing good, calling upon God with hope and fear, and humbling themselves before Him (21:90). The father and the son stand at the head of the group in this verse as an example of what the guidance produces.

John holds a particular place in the Book's account, described as chaste and as one who honoured his parents, and the sūrah of the Family of Imran names him among those who confirmed the word from God. The two men are therefore being presented to the audience as recipients of the same guidance the sūrah has been describing, given in answer to a prayer and rewarded with offspring who carried the message further.

**Jesus in the Line of the Righteous**

The mention of Jesus in this list is one of the sūrah's clearest statements about his standing, and it is placed in a passage aimed at the claims the region's Christians were making about him. He is named among the guided and described as one of the righteous, which is the Book's ordinary designation for the servants of God. The sūrah of the Table records his own declaration that he is a servant of God, and the sūrah of Maryam preserves his words from the cradle in the same terms.

The Book's treatment of Jesus throughout is consistent on this point: he is honoured, inspired, supported with the holy spirit, and given clear proofs, and he is not described anywhere as sharing the divine nature. The sūrah of the Women states that the Messiah would never disdain to be a servant of God, and the sūrah of the Family of Imran tells the Christians not to exceed the limits in their religion. This verse's placement of him inside a list of guided men is part of the same teaching.

**Elias and the Prophetic Tradition**

Elias appears in the Book in the sūrah of the Ranged, where he calls his people to be mindful of God and asks them whether they invoke Baal and abandon the best of creators. The sūrah records that his people rejected him and that he was among those brought near, and it closes the account by saying that peace be upon the family of Elias. His inclusion in this list connects him with the same line of messengers and with the same refusal of the worship of created things.

What Elias preached is therefore identical to what the sūrah has been presenting. He asked his people why they called upon an idol while abandoning the One who made them and their ancestors, and the account in the sūrah of the Ranged ends with the notice that the men who rejected him will be brought before their Lord. The Book's catalogue in this verse draws men from different centuries into a single description because they carried a single message.

**"All of the Righteous"**

The description that closes the verse is one word in the Arabic, and the Book uses it for those whose conduct and belief are sound together. The sūrah will use the same description in the next verse for the third group, and the Book uses it of the believers generally in passages about reward. The uniformity of the description is the point: whatever their rank and whatever the miracles attached to their stories, the prophets are grouped under the same moral category.

That category is also the one the sūrah has been recommending to its audience. The statement in the previous verse that God rewards the good-doers is followed by a list of men who were described as righteous, and the reader is being shown what the reward looked like in their lives. The Book's habit in these passages is to describe the prophets not by their extraordinary gifts but by the conduct that made them worthy of the guidance they received.

**The Function of the Two Verses Together**

The two catalogues in verses 84 and 85 serve the sūrah's argument in a way that a single name could not. They show the audience a continuous tradition of guided men spanning centuries and lineages, and they do it in a passage whose immediate subject is Abraham's argument for the oneness of God. The implication is that the teaching the Prophet was bringing had been the content of every mission in the list.

The sūrah then adds the third group of names in the next verse and follows the whole catalogue with a statement about what the guidance was and what would have destroyed it. That statement is the conclusion the list was assembled to support, and it appears in the very next sentence.

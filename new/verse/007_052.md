## Sūrah al-Aʿrāf 7:52

> We have certainly brought them a Book which We explained with knowledge—a guide and mercy for those who believe.

**Expanded Commentary**

**The Book Explained With Knowledge**

The verse returns from the account of the two companies to the revelation, and it states that a Book has been brought to the audience and explained with knowledge. The Book's own descriptions of its clarity appear throughout, and the sūrah of the Cow opens with the statement that the Book contains no doubt and is a guide for the mindful (2:2), the sūrah of the Rocky Tract describes the sūrah as verses of the clear Book (15:1), and the sūrah of Fussilat states that its verses have been detailed and made clear. The verse in front of us attaches the explanation to knowledge, which states that the clarification is exact rather than approximate.

The Book's account of its own arrangement includes the statement that God has made the signs clear for people who know, and the sūrah of the Cattle uses the phrase in its descriptions of the revelations (6:97). The verse in front of us states that the Book has been explained by the One whose knowledge covers what it explains, and the sūrah's reader is given the revelation as a text that has been clarified rather than left to guesswork.

**Knowledge and Guidance Together**

The verse names knowledge and guidance in the same sentence, and the combination describes the two services the Book performs. The explanation provides the knowledge of what is required, and the guidance directs the reader to the conduct that the knowledge describes. The Bee's account states that the Book has been sent down to make clear to people what has been sent to them so that they may reflect (16:44), and the sūrah of the Family of Imran states that God has sent down the Book with the truth.

The Book's teaching about knowledge and action as a pair appears in its criticisms of those who know and do not act as well as its praise of those who combine the two. The account of the Ranks criticizes those who say what they do not do, and the sūrah of the Cow describes the people of the earlier books who conceal what they know. The verse in front of us presents the Book as the remedy for both errors, since it explains and it guides.

**A Guide and a Mercy**

The verse describes the Book as a guide and a mercy for the believers, and the Book uses the pair in several passages. The sūrah of the Ant states that the Book is a guide and a mercy for the believers (27:77), the sūrah of the Night Journey states that the Quran is sent down as a healing and a mercy for the believers while it increases the wrongdoers in loss (17:82), and the sūrah of Fussilat states that the Book is a guide and a healing for the believers (41:44). This verse states the two benefits in the order the Book repeats.

The mercy in front of us is attached to the guidance, and the Book presents the guidance as the mercy itself. The sūrah of the Cattle says that God has brought the community a Book that explains everything and a guidance and a mercy (16:89), and the sūrah of the Cow states that the guidance is what the Book exists to provide. The verse here places the two names side by side, and the passage that follows states the response of those who ignore the Book.

**For Those Who Believe**

The verse attaches the benefit of the Book to the believers, and the Book's descriptions of its audience include the same qualification. The sūrah of the Cow states that the Book is a guide for the mindful (2:2), and it states later in its account of the parables that God misleads many by them and guides many, and that He misleads none but the iniquitous (2:26). The present verse states the condition, and the sūrah's reader is given the Book as a gift that produces its fruit in those who receive it.

The Book's teaching about the reception of the revelation uses the image of the land that receives rain, and the sūrah of the Heights itself has already stated that the good land brings forth its produce by the permission of its Lord while the poor land brings forth little (7:58). The two passages belong to the same argument. The Book is brought to all of its hearers, and its guidance and mercy take effect in those who believe.

**The Book That the Audience Has Received**

The verse addresses the audience of the sūrah directly, since the Book in question is the one being recited to them. The sūrah's opening verses named the Book that had been sent down to the Prophet, and the passage in front of us restates that fact after the long account of the two companies. The arrangement reminds the hearer that the warning he has just read comes through a text that is in his hands.

The Book's descriptions of its audience include the charge that they were given the message and turned away from it, and the sūrah of the Cattle reports the same accusation against the communities that received messengers before them. The verse under discussion places the audience in the company of those who have received a clarified Book, and the following verses state what awaits those who ignore it.

**Where the Verse Sits**

The verse stands fifty-second in the chapter, and it begins the passage about the revelation that runs to the description of creation in the following verses. It follows the scene of the two companies and precedes the warning about the fulfillment of the Book's promise (7:53). Here the verse supplies the ground of that warning, since the Book that explains with knowledge is the Book whose promise cannot fail.

The reader who has been shown the Hereafter is returned to the text he is reading. The Book that has been explained and given as a guide and a mercy is presented to the people of the sūrah, and the verses that follow state what happens when its warning arrives.

**The Transition to the Next Verse**

The next verse asks whether the people are waiting for anything but the fulfillment of the Book's warning, and it reports the words of those who ignored it when the fulfillment arrives, together with their request for intercessors or for a return to the world (7:53). The Book that has been explained in front of us is the same Book whose warning will arrive, and the verse that follows states the regret of those who put off their response to it.

The two verses together place the revelation and its outcome in sequence. The Book has been brought and explained, the guidance and the mercy are available to those who believe, and the day on which its promise is fulfilled will produce the confession of those who ignored it; and the sūrah then turns to the signs of the created order.

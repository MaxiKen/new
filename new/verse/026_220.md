## Sūrah al-Shuʿarāʾ 26:220

> He ˹alone˺ is indeed the All-Hearing, AllKnowing.

**Expanded Commentary**

**Hearing and Knowing Complete the Reassurance**

The passage closes with two divine attributes. Allah is All-Hearing and All-Knowing. He hears the Prophet's recitation, prayer, warning, and private appeal, and He knows the intention, effort, and response connected with each act. Nothing is lost because it was spoken softly, performed at night, or ignored by the audience.

The word alone in the translation reinforces exclusivity. The Prophet trusts the One who hears and knows, not a rival power and not the approval of people. This completes the command to trust the Almighty, Most Merciful. His power is joined to perfect perception, so His care is never blind and His judgment is never based on incomplete information.

**Allah Hears More Than Public Speech**

The All-Hearing hears the warning made in public and the prayer made in solitude. He hears the believer who can explain his faith and the person whose only response is a quiet cry. This attribute gives hope to people whose truth is not recognized by society. A person's voice may be dismissed by the crowd without being lost before Allah.

It also gives weight to every word. A person cannot hide a false promise by speaking it where no witness stands. A leader cannot erase a cruel command because the victims were too afraid to repeat it. Allah hears the words that create justice and the words that create harm, including the tone and purpose humans may fail to perceive.

**Allah Knows the Movement Behind the Act**

Knowledge reaches the inward reason for worship. Two people may make the same movement in prayer while one seeks Allah and the other seeks reputation. Human observers can see the form, but Allah knows the heart. The pairing of hearing and knowing prevents the believer from reducing accountability to visible performance.

Quran 6:19 presents Allah as the witness between the Prophet and his people and the Quran as a warning that reaches whoever it reaches. The same principle appears here at the close of prayer and mission. Allah knows who conveyed faithfully, who listened honestly, and who turned the reminder into an excuse for denial.

**The Ending Returns the Servant to Reliance**

The Prophet has been told to warn relatives, be gracious to believers, separate from disobedience, and trust Allah. The final attributes make that trust reasonable. The messenger does not need to force recognition because Allah hears the call and knows the full account. He does not need to answer every insult with anger because the One who knows all motives will judge rightly.

For the believer, the conclusion joins worship to conduct. Stand before Allah with sincerity, move among worshippers with humility, speak truth without cruelty, and trust the Lord with what people do afterward. The All-Hearing receives the prayer, and the All-Knowing understands the servant who made it. That knowledge is a warning to the dishonest and a refuge for the faithful.

The two attributes also explain why prayer cannot be separated from speech and conduct. Allah hears the words of recitation and knows whether the heart accepts their claim. He hears the warning given to relatives and knows whether the speaker seeks their good or his own importance. He hears the apology of the person who returns from wrong and knows whether the return is sincere.

The All-Hearing does not mean that every request receives the exact outcome desired by the one who makes it. Hearing includes complete reception, while the All-Knowing chooses with wisdom. This protects the believer from despair when a prayer seems unanswered and from arrogance when a request is granted. The servant asks, acts, and trusts the Lord's knowledge of what the servant cannot see.

The name All-Knowing also makes hidden leadership accountable. A person can appear gentle before the congregation while carrying contempt for the weak. He can move correctly in prayer while planning to cheat afterward. Allah knows the inward movement behind the outward movement. That knowledge is not a reason to abandon outward law; it is a reason to make outward law honest.

The closing attributes return the whole passage to worship. Exclusive invocation, warning relatives, gentleness with believers, freedom from disobedience, trust, and prayer all belong to one order. The servant speaks to Allah, serves people under Allah, and leaves the result to Allah. The Lord hears the words, sees the rising, knows the movements, and judges the response.

For the faithful, this is a deep reassurance. A prayer whispered in the night, a warning rejected by a relative, or a small act of humility in a congregation is fully known. For the dishonest, it is a serious boundary: no hidden motive remains outside knowledge. The All-Hearing and All-Knowing Lord gives the servant reason to be sincere when people are absent and patient when people do not understand.

The final names also protect the believer from the fear that no one noticed a hidden act of good. Allah hears a quiet supplication and knows the intention behind a small sacrifice. He knows when a servant lowers his voice to avoid humiliating another person, refuses unlawful profit, or continues a prayer while no one praises the effort. Hidden faith is not hidden from Him.

They also prevent the dishonest from treating silence as safety. A false promise may deceive the other party, and a secret plan may escape public attention, but neither is outside divine hearing and knowledge. The believer does not need to expose every hidden wrong himself in order to trust that Allah's account is complete. He acts justly and leaves the unseen judgment to the One who knows it.

The passage ends where worship and mission meet. The Prophet warns, shows mercy, separates from disobedience, trusts Allah, rises in prayer, and moves with worshippers. Each act is observed by the Lord who hears and knows. The sequence teaches that public guidance is sustained by private worship and that private worship is proven by public truthfulness.

A faithful response to the verse is therefore a life of honest reliance. Speak to Allah, listen to His guidance, and remember that every motive is known. The One who hears does not overlook the servant's prayer, and the One who knows does not confuse the messenger with the denier or the sincere act with its outward imitation. His knowledge makes worship meaningful and accountability certain.

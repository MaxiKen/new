## Sūrah al-Nisāʾ 4:85

> Whoever intercedes for a good cause will have a share in the reward, and whoever intercedes for an evil cause will have a share in the burden. And Allah is Watchful over all things.

### Expanded Commentary

**Intercession participates in the consequence**

Whoever intercedes for a good cause receives a share of its reward, while whoever intercedes for an evil cause shares its burden. Intercession here includes advocacy, mediation, recommendation, and using influence to open or close an opportunity. The person who says “I only passed along the request” cannot pretend to be morally absent when the request produces harm.

**Influence is a trust**

A recommendation for a qualified worker, a mediation that prevents violence, or an appeal for a prisoner’s lawful rights can be good intercession. Lobbying for a bribe, suppressing a victim’s complaint, or securing an undeserved appointment is evil intercession. The same social power can serve justice or corruption.

Allah is Watchful over all things, including motives and outcomes. The verse does not demand that people refuse all advocacy; it makes advocacy accountable. Before intervening, ask whether the cause is true, whether the process remains fair, and whether the person helped will use the opportunity responsibly.

**Recommendation is moral participation**

Before lending a name or influence, ask whether the person or cause deserves support. The same recommendation can open a job to a qualified applicant or help an unqualified favourite displace one. Allah’s Watchfulness makes social influence part of the accounting of deeds.

## Sūrah al-Nisāʾ 4:85

> Whoever intercedes for a good cause will have a share in the reward, and whoever intercedes for an evil cause will have a share in the burden. And Allah is Watchful over all things.

**Expanded Commentary**

**Intercession for Good and for Evil**

The verse states *man yashfaʿ shafāʿatan ḥasanatan yakun lahu naṣībun minhā wa man yashfaʿ shafāʿatan sayyiʾatan yakun lahu kiflun minhā* — whoever intercedes for a good cause will have a share in the reward, and whoever intercedes for an evil cause will have a share in the burden. *Shafāʿah* — intercession, mediation, joining — was explained as adding one’s effort to another’s so that a matter is accomplished: recommending, urging, or making a cause possible. *Ḥasanah* — good — was read as the causes the sūrah has commended — motivating believers to fight where fighting is in God’s cause for the oppressed (4:74–75, 4:84), appointing mediators who desire harmony where God brings it (4:35), returning trusts to rightful owners and judging with fairness (4:58), and referring news of security or fear to those with sound judgment (4:83). *Sayyiʾah* — evil — was read as the opposite — interceding with *ṭāghūt* (4:60), promoting stinginess (4:37), encouraging lagging (4:72), or publicising fear (4:83). *Naṣīb* — share in reward — and *kifl* — share in burden, portion to bear — were explained as the intercessor’s recorded portion of the outcome, because intercession makes the outcome possible. The sūrah has said that the atom’s weight is not wronged but multiplied (4:40) and that whoever obeys the Messenger is with the blessed (4:69); intercession for good is the social form of that obedience, where the urger shares the reward of the doer.

**A Share in Reward and a Share in Burden**

The pairing *naṣīb* and *kifl* was stressed as exact. *Naṣīb* — share — echoes *naṣīban mafrūḍan* — obligatory share — for heirs (4:7) and *naṣībun mimmā iktasabū* — share of what they earned for men and women (4:32), showing that intercession earns as deeds earn. *Kifl* — portion, like the load on a camel’s side — was explained as the burden of evil intercession, the weight the intercessor bears of the sin he helped make possible, as the sūrah elsewhere warns that deliberate devouring leads to Fire easy for God (4:30) and that trading a portion of the Book for misguidance bears humiliating punishment (4:37). Early authorities gave examples: interceding to free an oppressed person, to reconcile spouses, or to motivate to fight in God’s cause brings a share of reward; interceding to protect an oppressor, to take a widow as inheritance (4:19), or to seek a false judge (4:60) brings a share of burden. The sūrah’s ethic is therefore collective: no one acts alone, and no one’s speech — *we hear and obey* or *we hear and disobey* (4:46) — is without share.

**Why Intercession Follows Motivating to Fight**

Placed after *motivate the believers to fight, perhaps God will curb the might* (4:84), this verse generalizes that urging. *Taḥrīḍ* — to motivate — is a form of *shafāʿah* — intercession — where the motivator joins the fighter’s cause and shares its reward, the *ajr ʿaẓīm* promised whether martyred or victorious (4:74). The verse was linked to the Prophet ﷺ urging the believers while he himself fights though accountable only for himself (4:84); his urging is intercession for a good cause whose share is great. The opposite — interceding for evil — was linked to those who intercede with *ṭāghūt*, who reassure disbelievers they are better guided (4:51), and who lag and wish for gain (4:73). The sūrah has shown that Satan’s schemes are weak (4:76); to intercede for the Devil’s cause is to join weakness and to bear its burden. The verse therefore tells the community that urging matters; the one who says *“do not fight, delay us”* (4:77) will have a share in the burden of those who stay from fighting for the oppressed, while the one who says *take precautions and go forth* (4:71) will have a share in the reward of those who go.

**And Allah Is Watchful Over All Things**

The closing *wa kāna Allāhu ʿalā kulli shayʾin ḥasībā* — and Allah is Watchful over all things, sufficient as Reckoner — was read as the recorder of shares. *Ḥasīb* — reckoner, guardian, sufficient — was explained as counting intercession as deeds are counted, not missing the atom’s weight (4:40) and not wronging by a thread (4:49, 4:77). To say He is Watchful over all things is to say that intercession for good done quietly — a word that moves a hesitant fighter, a mediation that seeks harmony (4:35), a referral of news to sound judgment (4:83) — is known and weighted, and that intercession for evil done by night — *yubayyitūna* what contradicts *we obey* (4:81) — is also known and weighted. The sūrah has used *ḥasīb* for the orphan’s handover — *sufficient is God as Reckoner* (4:6) — and *shahīd* for witnessing the Messenger’s mission (4:79), and *wakīl* for trusting affairs (4:81); here *ḥasīb* gathers them as the watch over every joining. The one who intercedes for a good cause, even if the cause does not fully succeed — *perhaps God will curb* (4:84) — still has his *naṣīb*; the one who intercedes for evil, even if the evil is not fully realized, still bears *kifl*. Watchfulness ensures both, which is why the verse is both invitation and warning.

**A Community Where Urging Earns**

Between the command to fight and the greeting that answers with better (4:86), this verse holds the community’s social economy together. The economy was stated as: do not crave what God gave some over others; ask Him for bounty (4:32) — because shares are from Him. Here it adds: whoever joins a good cause will have a share in it; whoever joins an evil, a burden. The community that was told *“if you avoid major sins We will cover lesser ones”* (4:31) and *“good from God, evil from yourself”* (4:79) is here told that joining amplifies both: joining good multiplies good, joining evil burdens. For the weak for whom burdens were lightened (4:28), the verse is the lightest encouragement to speak: a word of intercession for the oppressed who cry for a helper (4:75), for the couple seeking mediators, for the fighter needing urging, earns a *naṣīb* as though the speaker had done the deed. The sūrah therefore invites the tongue that once lagged or publicised fear to become a tongue that intercedes for good, whose share is reward and whose watcher is the One who is Watchful over all things.

## Sūrah al-Naḥl 16:46

> **Or that He will not seize them while they go about ˹their day˺, for then they will have no escape?**

**Expanded Commentary**

**Seizure While Going About**

Aw ya'khudhahum fī taqallubihim — or that He will not seize them while they are going about, turning, moving. Taqallub from q-l-b means to turn, to go about, to move in daily affairs, travel, trade, seeking provision. Al-Ṭabarī explains that taqallubihim means while they are going about in land for trade, travel, work, feeling secure, not expecting punishment. The question is continuation of previous: do those who plot evil feel secure that Allah will not seize them while going about? The answer is no, they should not feel secure. Seizure while going about means punishment can come during normal activity, not only during plotting. A person may plot at night and feel secure by day while trading, but punishment can come while trading. Al-Rāzī notes that taqallub includes turning in beds, turning in markets, turning in journeys, indicating all daily movements. No moment is safe from Allah's seizure if one persists in evil.

**No Escape When Seized**

Fa-mā hum bi-muʿjizīn — for then they will have no escape, will not be able to escape, frustrate, evade. Muʿjizīn from ʿ-j-z means to be unable, to escape, to make helpless, from same root as iʿjāz, inimitability. Al-Ṭabarī says fa-mā hum bi-muʿjizīn means they will not be able to escape Allah's punishment, to frustrate His plan, to make Him unable. When Allah seizes, there is no escape. The phrase appears in other warnings, as in 29:22: *You will not be able to escape on earth nor in heaven* (29:22). The verse emphasizes that feeling secure while going about is delusion because when seizure comes, escape is impossible. The time to seek security is before seizure, by abandoning evil plotting and turning to gratitude and tawḥīd.

**Why Seizure While Active Is Mentioned**

Previous verse mentioned earth swallowing from below and torment from unexpected direction. This verse adds seizure while active, covering another mode: punishment while person is engaged in worldly activity, feeling secure in routine. Al-Qurṭubī says this includes being seized while traveling, trading, farming, etc., when person thinks he is safe because he is busy with lawful activity. The warning is that evil plotting taints all activity; even while doing apparently lawful daily going about, punishment can come because plotting continues in heart. The verse also shows that Allah's punishment is not limited to moments of sin; it can come at any time, so one should not feel secure at any time while persisting in evil.

**The Connection To Security And Gratitude**

Sūrat al-Niʿam emphasizes that security is from Allah, not from plotting or wealth. Mountains prevent earth from shaking with you as mercy, but if you plot evil, that security can be removed. The verse asks do they feel secure that He will not seize them while going about, implying that true security is only for those who are grateful and mindful, not for plotters. The righteous who emigrated after being wronged and were patient and trusted Lord were given good home in world as security, but that security came after patience and trust, not while plotting evil. The contrast is clear: plotters feel false security while plotting, righteous receive true security after patience and trust.

**A Lesson On Constant Awareness**

The verse teaches constant awareness that Allah can seize at any moment, so one should not feel secure in disobedience at any moment. Al-Rāzī notes that taqallubihim, while going about, includes all states: awake, asleep, traveling, staying, working, resting. No state is safe from seizure if one persists in evil. Therefore one should be mindful in all states, not only during worship. This is meaning of taqwa, mindfulness, being conscious of Allah in all going about. The sūrah's call to reflect, understand, be mindful applies to all moments, not only moments of ritual.

**Linguistic Note On Taqallub And Muʿjizīn**

Taqallub from q-l-b means to turn, flip, go about, from same root as qalb, heart, which turns, and taqlīb, turning. In this context, fī taqallubihim means in their going about, turning in affairs. Ya'khudhahum from a-kh-dh means to seize, take. Fa-mā hum bi-muʿjizīn, they will not be able to escape, from ʿ-j-z, to be unable, to escape. The construction mā hum bi-muʿjizīn with bi- emphasizes negation: they are not at all able to escape. The verse's brevity — two clauses, question and answer — delivers warning that seizure can come during daily activity and then no escape, urging listener not to feel secure while persisting in evil plotting.


**A Lesson That Connects To Gratitude And Reflection**

This verse, like all verses in Sūrat al-Niʿam, ultimately calls to gratitude and reflection. Whether it describes emigration, patience, trust, human messengers, clear proofs, earth swallowing, or angels fearing, the purpose is to make reader recognize blessings and respond with thanks and reflection. Al-Qurṭubī notes that every warning in this sūrah is also a blessing because it warns before punishment, giving chance to change. Every promise is also a blessing because it encourages good. The sūrah weaves warning and promise, blessing and accountability, to cultivate heart that is mindful, humble, grateful. The reader who reflects on this verse with that mindset will find both knowledge and motivation to be among those who say goodness when asked what Lord revealed, and who are greeted with peace at death and enter Gardens.

**How This Verse Fits The Sūrah's Argument**

The sūrah moves from blessings to argument against shirk to warnings to examples to guidance. This verse fits in that flow by showing either reward for patience and trust, or principle of human messengers, or modes of punishment, or universal submission. Each verse adds layer to argument that Allah alone creates, sustains, knows, and deserves worship. The verse's placement after previous warnings and before next commands is deliberate: it shows consequence of previous attitude and prepares for next command. The reader is invited to see connection and to respond with gratitude, patience, trust, and asking knowledgeable, rather than with denial, arrogance, and plotting.

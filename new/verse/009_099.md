## Sūrah al-Tawbah 9:99

> However, among the nomadic Arabs are those who believe in Allah and the Last Day, and consider what they donate as a means of coming closer to Allah and ˹receiving˺ the prayers of the Messenger. It will certainly bring them closer. Allah will admit them into His mercy. Surely Allah is AllForgiving, Most Merciful.

**Expanded Commentary**

**The Same Setting Contains Sincere Believers**

After criticizing some nomadic Arabs, the verse identifies others who believe in Allah and the Last Day. This correction is essential. A social setting may create certain pressures, but it does not determine every person's heart. The Qur'an refuses to turn a contextual warning into a universal condemnation.

The sincere nomads understand their donations differently from those who call them loss. They see spending as a means of nearness to Allah. The money still leaves their hands, but the act gains a spiritual direction. Their geography does not prevent faith because faith is not owned by a city. It is shown through belief, intention, and obedience wherever a person lives.

**Giving Can Bring the Servant Near**

The verse describes charity as a means of approaching Allah. Nearness is not physical distance. It is a change in the servant's relation to the Lord: gratitude deepens, attachment weakens, and the heart becomes more willing to obey. A donation can therefore be an act of inner travel even when the giver never leaves his home.

This nearness is not purchased by an amount. A small gift can be spiritually significant when it is given with sincerity and lawful intention. A large gift can be empty if it seeks status. The believer gives what he can, asks Allah to accept it, and remembers that Allah's mercy is the true source of nearness.

**The Messenger's Prayer Is a Human Channel of Mercy**

The verse mentions receiving the prayers of the Messenger. This points to the blessing of being connected to a prophetic community whose leader asks Allah's forgiveness and goodness for the believers. The Messenger is not an independent owner of mercy. His prayer is a means through which Allah's mercy is sought and received.

The principle also teaches leaders to pray for those they serve. A teacher who corrects a student should want the student's good, and an administrator who receives a donation should ask Allah to bless the donor rather than treating the gift as a mere transaction. Human relationships become spiritual when each person directs benefit toward Allah.

**Intention Transforms a Material Act**

The same physical act can have different meanings. One person gives because he fears loss of reputation. Another gives because he recognizes Allah's bounty and wants to serve. The amount may be identical, but the inner movement differs. Intention does not make an unlawful donation lawful, yet it gives a lawful donation its devotional direction.

The sincere believer should renew intention during and after giving. Pride can enter after the gift, especially if people praise it. He remembers that he was able to give only because Allah gave him something first. He avoids counting the act as a personal achievement. Nearness grows when the servant sees the gift as a response to grace.

**Faith in the Last Day Enlarges the Account**

Belief in the Last Day makes spending intelligible when no worldly return is visible. The servant may not receive thanks, status, or even evidence that the money reached its goal. He still believes that Allah knows the act and will reveal its value. The future account makes present generosity rational.

This faith also protects against wasteful giving. The believer does not throw resources away to feel generous. He seeks a genuine need, sound administration, and a use that Allah permits. Hope in the Hereafter joins careful means to sincere intention. The gift is not lost because its final return is with Allah.

**Mercy Completes the Act**

The verse ends with Allah's mercy and forgiveness. The sincere donor is not admitted because his charity forced the door of Heaven. He is admitted through Allah's grace, with the donation serving as evidence of faith and a means of nearness. This keeps the servant grateful and humble.

The community should therefore honor sincere giving without idolizing donors. It should teach people to spend, to seek Allah, and to hope for the Messenger's prayers and Allah's mercy. A person far from the city can be near to Allah through faith. A person near a religious center can be distant through hypocrisy. The measure is not location but the direction of the heart.

This verse also protects sincere donors from being judged by their distance, clothing, or economic class. A person from a remote place may understand the spiritual purpose of giving more clearly than a person surrounded by institutions. The measure is the heart's direction and the lawful use of the gift. The donor should still seek competent administration, because sincerity does not make a careless channel wise. When intention, sound means, and gratitude meet, spending becomes nearness. Allah's mercy then gives the act a value larger than its visible amount and admits the servant without allowing him to boast of having purchased proximity.

The verse also shows that sincere faith can live alongside a simple or remote life. The believer does not need a public platform to seek nearness. He needs a sound intention, lawful spending, and trust that Allah sees the effort. The prayers of the Messenger are a blessing, but they do not make the donor passive. He gives, learns, and acts while hoping for mercy. The result is nearness because the material act has been joined to belief in Allah and the Last Day. Every community should make this path open to the overlooked, not only to the influential.

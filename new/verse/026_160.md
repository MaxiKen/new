## Sūrah al-Shuʿarāʾ 26:160

> The people of Lot rejected the messengers

**Expanded Commentary**

**A New People Enters the Repeated Pattern**

The account turns from Thamûd to the people of Lot with another opening verdict: they rejected the messengers. The change of community does not change the central test. A people hears a warning and decides whether revelation will govern its desires, customs, and treatment of others.

The repeated wording teaches the reader to compare rather than isolate the stories. Thamûd's setting involved land, cultivation, skill, and a sign. The next account will have its own circumstances, but its first moral fact is the same. Rejection is the community's response to the messenger, and that response places every later detail under accountability.


Lot is the messenger associated with his people, yet the verse uses the plural messengers. As in the accounts of Noah, ʿĀd, and Thamûd, rejecting one true messenger is treated as rejection of the shared prophetic call. The messengers do not invite different forms of worship based on tribal preference. They call people to the Lord who owns every community.

The plural also prevents a people from reducing revelation to a personal dispute. Lot is not merely an individual whose lifestyle or temperament the community dislikes. He stands within the line of warning that has already been heard by earlier nations. To reject him is to resist the authority of the message he carries.

**The People Are Named Before Their Practice**

The verse calls them the people of Lot before the account develops their behavior. Identity is introduced through relation to the messenger. This keeps the reader from approaching the community as a collection of strange acts without moral history. They are people who receive guidance and then choose how to answer it.

That order also protects against reducing an entire people to a single description. God knows individuals, motives, and degrees of responsibility. The collective verdict concerns the community's response to the messenger, while divine judgment remains more precise than human stereotypes. A later reader must learn the moral lesson without pretending to possess God's complete knowledge of every person.


To reject a messenger is not merely to disagree with one sentence. It is to protect a pattern that the messenger's warning threatens. A community may prefer its inherited pleasures, social power, or idea of freedom to the limits of revelation. The opening verdict identifies that preference before the narrative describes its public consequences.

This is why the same wording can appear across very different stories. The surface temptations differ, but rejection has a common structure. The people want to keep authority over worship and conduct for themselves. The messenger says that God has the final word, and the community responds by treating the messenger as the problem.

**Lot's Message Is Not a Private Innovation**

The plural messengers places Lot within continuity. His call must be heard alongside the prophetic summons already described in this sūrah: worship God, fear Him, obey the messenger, and do not let appetite rule the community. The setting changes, but divine oneness and moral accountability remain central.

Quran 7:80 introduces Lot's rebuke to his people within a broader sequence of prophetic warnings. That parallel confirms that the narrative is not inviting curiosity about an isolated scandal. It is exposing what happens when a society is warned about wrongdoing and chooses to defend the wrongdoing instead.


A reader may become accustomed to the refrain and stop hearing it. Repetition can dull attention, but it can also reveal that human beings repeatedly need the same correction. The verse insists that a new society is not exempt merely because another society has already failed.

Each community has its own reasons for believing it is different. It may point to its culture, its intelligence, or its freedoms. The repeated verdict asks a more serious question: when a truthful messenger challenges the community, will it submit to God or make its own preference the measure of truth?

**Collective Identity Cannot Carry a Person to Safety**

Being associated with a people does not determine a person's final standing. The community may reject while an individual believes, and a person may belong outwardly to a group without sharing every intention within it. The verse names the collective because collective choices matter, but God's judgment does not erase individual accountability.

This distinction prevents both contempt and complacency. Do not despise every person because of a group's rejection, and do not assume that belonging to a group protects you from its moral direction. Each hearer must decide whether to support falsehood, remain silent before it, or follow the guidance God sends.


The verse is a threshold. It does not yet detail every claim made by Lot or every response of his people; it establishes the fact that rejection governs the story. The reader should carry forward the earlier lessons: prosperity, custom, and majority opinion cannot replace submission to God.

A messenger's presence is mercy because it gives the community an opportunity to change before judgment. If the people reject that mercy, the outcome will not be a mystery of fate. Their own response will reveal what they chose to protect. The opening sentence asks the reader to listen before the next exchange begins.


The phrase “the people of Lot” can tempt later readers to look at another society with distance. The Qur'an places the verse here to prevent that escape. Every community has desires it protects and boundaries it resents. The question is whether it will accept correction when revelation confronts those desires.

The safest response is humility. Hear the messenger, test claims by God's guidance, and do not treat a familiar social order as an authority above Him. The people of Lot rejected the messengers; the account is a warning to anyone who believes that a community's insistence can make its chosen way immune to judgment.

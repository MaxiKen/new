## Sūrah al-Naḥl 16:41

> **As for those who emigrated in ˹the cause of˺ Allah after being persecuted, We will surely bless them with a good home in this world. But the reward of the Hereafter is far better, if only they knew.**

**Expanded Commentary**

**Emigration After Persecution**

Wa-al-ladhīna hājarū fī Allāhi min baʿdi mā ẓulimū — as for those who emigrated in cause of Allah after being persecuted, wronged. Hājarū from h-j-r means to emigrate, to leave home, to abandon. Fī Allāhi means in cause of Allah, for sake of Allah, because of faith. Min baʿdi mā ẓulimū, after being wronged, oppressed, persecuted. Al-Ṭabarī explains that this refers to Muslims who were persecuted in Makkah and emigrated, first to Abyssinia, then to Madinah. Some say verse 41 refers specifically to Abyssinian emigrants, while verse 110 refers to Madinan emigrants. Others say it is general for all who emigrate after being wronged for faith. The persecution included torture, boycott, and social pressure, as experienced by Bilāl, ʿAmmār, and others. The verse acknowledges their wronging and promises recompense.

**Good Home In This World**

La-nubawwi'annahum fī al-dunyā ḥasanatan — We will surely bless them, settle them, with good home, good place in this world. Nubawwi' from b-w-ʾ means to settle, to give abode, to lodge. Ḥasanatan means good, beautiful, goodly. Al-Ṭabarī says this means We will give them good abode in world, either in Madinah after emigration, where they found security and provision, or good mention and honor. Al-Rāzī adds that good home in world includes both physical settlement and spiritual contentment, honor among people, and opening of provision. The oath la- indicates certainty: We will surely. The promise is for those who left homes for Allah after being wronged; Allah will give them better homes. This was fulfilled for emigrants to Madinah who became leaders, and for emigrants to Abyssinia who found protection under Najāshī.

**Reward Of Hereafter Far Better**

Wa-la-ajru al-ākhirati akbaru — but reward of Hereafter is greater, far better. Ajr means reward, wage. Ākhirah, Hereafter, is greater than worldly settlement. Al-Qurṭubī explains that even if worldly good home is given, Hereafter reward is greater in quality, quantity, duration. The comparison akbaru, greater, bigger, indicates that worldly settlement is good but Hereafter is far better, so believer should not be satisfied with worldly settlement alone but should seek Hereafter. The verse ends with law kānū yaʿlamūn — if only they knew, if they knew, indicating that if people knew how great Hereafter reward is, they would emigrate and endure persecution more willingly. Most people do not know, as verse 38 said.

**Why Emigration Is Praised Here**

Emigration after persecution is praised because it shows preference for faith over home, for Allah over comfort. In Makkan context, leaving home meant leaving family, tribe, wealth, status, and facing unknown. Those who did so demonstrated sincerity. Al-Zamakhsharī notes that hājarū fī Allāhi indicates motive: in cause of Allah, not for worldly gain. Min baʿdi mā ẓulimū indicates timing: after being wronged, not before, showing they endured as long as possible then left when staying would mean compromising faith. The verse therefore honors patience and sacrifice. It also consoles those who were wronged and considering emigration: you will have good home in world and far better in Hereafter.

**Connection To Patience And Trust**

Verse 42 will say they are those who patiently endured and in Lord put trust. Emigration requires both patience, ṣabr, and trust, tawakkul. Patience to endure persecution and hardship of leaving, trust that Allah will provide good home. The sūrah's theme of gratitude and patience appears here: those who emigrated after being wronged show gratitude by preserving faith despite persecution, and patience by enduring. Their reward is both worldly and otherworldly, showing that Allah does not let sacrifice for Him go unrewarded.

**Linguistic Note On Hājarū And Nubawwi'**

Hājarū from h-j-r means to emigrate, to cut off, to leave, from same root as hijrah, emigration, and hajr, abandonment. Fī Allāhi, in Allah, meaning in cause of Allah, for Allah. Ẓulimū from ẓ-l-m, to be wronged. La-nubawwi'annahum from b-w-ʾ, to settle, lodge, give abode, with la- for oath, surely. Ḥasanatan, good, from ḥ-s-n. Ajru al-ākhirati akbaru, reward of Hereafter greater, from a-j-r, reward, and k-b-r, great. Law kānū yaʿlamūn, if they knew, from ʿ-l-m, to know. The verse's structure — those who emigrated after being wronged, We will settle them with good in world, but Hereafter reward greater, if they knew — moves from deed to worldly reward to otherworldly reward to awareness, encouraging emigration and patience by showing both immediate and ultimate recompense.

**A Lesson For All Who Are Wronged**

Though verse refers to specific historical emigration, its principle is general: those who are wronged for sake of Allah and who leave wrongdoing environment for Allah will be blessed with good home in world and greater in Hereafter. Al-Qurṭubī extends it to anyone who leaves sinful environment, abandons bad company, or migrates from place where he cannot practice faith. The promise of good home in world is not necessarily physical house but good life, as 16:97 says We will give them good life. The verse therefore offers hope to anyone feeling persecuted for faith: your sacrifice will be rewarded with good now and far better later, if you knew how great reward is, you would not hesitate.


**A Lesson That Connects To Gratitude And Reflection**

This verse, like all verses in Sūrat al-Niʿam, ultimately calls to gratitude and reflection. Whether it describes emigration, patience, trust, human messengers, clear proofs, earth swallowing, or angels fearing, the purpose is to make reader recognize blessings and respond with thanks and reflection. Al-Qurṭubī notes that every warning in this sūrah is also a blessing because it warns before punishment, giving chance to change. Every promise is also a blessing because it encourages good. The sūrah weaves warning and promise, blessing and accountability, to cultivate heart that is mindful, humble, grateful. The reader who reflects on this verse with that mindset will find both knowledge and motivation to be among those who say goodness when asked what Lord revealed, and who are greeted with peace at death and enter Gardens.

**How This Verse Fits The Sūrah's Argument**

The sūrah moves from blessings to argument against shirk to warnings to examples to guidance. This verse fits in that flow by showing either reward for patience and trust, or principle of human messengers, or modes of punishment, or universal submission. Each verse adds layer to argument that Allah alone creates, sustains, knows, and deserves worship. The verse's placement after previous warnings and before next commands is deliberate: it shows consequence of previous attitude and prepares for next command. The reader is invited to see connection and to respond with gratitude, patience, trust, and asking knowledgeable, rather than with denial, arrogance, and plotting.

## Sūrah an-Nūr 24:10

> ˹You would have suffered,˺ had it not been for Allah’s grace and mercy upon you, and had Allah not been Accepting of Repentance, All-Wise.

**Expanded Commentary**

**The Dramatic Ellipsis of Divine Grace**

The verse pauses the detailed legislative discourse with a profound rhetorical exclamation: *wa-lawlā faḍlu Allāhi ʿalaykum wa-raḥmatuhū wa-anna Allāha tawwābun ḥakīm*. Grammatically, the conditional particle *lawlā* (were it not for) is left without an explicit result clause (*ḥadhf al-jawāb*). In classical Arabic rhetoric (*balāghah*), omitting the consequence is an intentional literary device designed to convey an outcome too immense and terrifying for words to encompass.

The omission forces the mind to imagine what would have occurred had divine grace not intervened. Without divine guidance and restraint, the community would have descended into violent chaos, husbands and wives would have destroyed one another through uncontrolled vengeance, false swearers would have been struck dead by immediate heavenly wrath, and social trust would have disintegrated completely. The rhetorical silence underscores the vast gulf between divine mercy and human ruin, reminding humanity that every moment of social peace is an unearned gift from the Almighty. Without divine forbearance, no human community could long survive its own moral failures, rash accusations, and internal strife.

**Grace (faḍl) and Mercy (raḥmah) as Social Bulwarks**

The pairing of divine grace (*faḍl*) and divine mercy (*raḥmah*) identifies the theological foundation upon which all communal stability rests. Grace represents unearned divine generosity, while mercy represents active protection against destruction and suffering. In the immediate context of marital disputes and false accusations, God's grace manifested in the revelation of clear, humane legal procedures.

Before this legislation descended, people were trapped between the rigid demand for four external witnesses and the agony of silent domestic betrayal. By revealing the precise statutes of *qadhf* and *liʿān*, God provided an equitable judicial exit that preserves human honor while containing private grief. Divine law is not a punitive burden imposed upon humanity; it is a manifestation of pure divine benevolence that rescues human civilization from its own destructive impulses, providing order where human passions would otherwise reign supreme and produce endless cycles of bloodshed and vendetta across generations.

**The Attribute of Tawwāb: Welcoming the Errant Soul**

The verse highlights the divine name *al-Tawwāb*—the One who constantly turns toward His servants with forgiveness and acceptance of repentance. Human beings are fragile, emotional creatures who routinely succumb to anger, jealousy, suspicion, and moral weakness. In the heat of domestic discord or communal panic, individuals often utter words they later regret or make accusations born of hasty judgment.

By invoking *al-Tawwāb*, the revelation reminds the community that legal conflict does not represent the end of spiritual hope. Even when a marriage ends in the acrimony of mutual imprecation, or when a person stumbles into false speech, the door of repentance remains wide open before the Creator. God welcomes the contrite servant who returns with genuine remorse, wiping away past transgressions and offering a pathway toward spiritual renewal and reconciliation with the divine reality. Sincere repentance can heal the deepest wounds of the soul, transforming personal ruin into spiritual elevation and enduring peace with God.

**Divine Wisdom (ḥakīm) in Legal Delineation**

The verse concludes with the attribute *al-Ḥakīm*—the All-Wise. Every statute, procedural condition, and evidentiary standard established in the preceding verses reflects flawless divine wisdom. Human legislation frequently oscillates between destructive extremes: either indulging in permissive laxity that trivializes marital sanctity, or enforcing draconian measures that violate human dignity and destroy privacy.

Divine wisdom alone strikes the perfect equilibrium. It prescribes severe corporal deterrence for public licentiousness while demanding an almost insurmountable four-witness threshold; it establishes eighty lashes for unproven slander while creating a specialized oath-based exit for spouses. No human assembly could have calibrated legal mechanisms that simultaneously defend chastity, safeguard domestic sanctuary, protect lineage, and shield reputations from gossip with such sublime balance. Divine legislation remains peerless in its harmony and justice, answering human needs across every generation with absolute equity.

**A Transition Between Private Slander and Public Conspiracy**

Structurally, verse 10 functions as a monumental pivot within the architecture of Sūrah an-Nūr. It brings the intimate legislation concerning marital accusation and *liʿān* (vv. 6–9) to a reverent close, while simultaneously preparing the audience for the national crisis of the Grand Slander (*al-Ifk*) detailed in verses 11 through 26.

The reminder of divine grace applies in both directions. It looks backward to the salvation of fragile households from murderous jealousy, and it looks forward to the miraculous preservation of the Prophet's ﷺ household and the young Muslim state from a coordinated campaign of political and moral sabotage. The community is reminded that its survival through every trial—whether domestic or civic—is owed entirely to transcendent divine intervention. The mercy that saves the individual family is the same mercy that guards the entire civilization against subversive collapse and communal hysteria.

**The Restraint of Immediate Retribution**

A deeper theological lesson lies in the divine restraint that characterizes God's governance. Had God acted with swift, uncompromising retribution, every individual who entertained a wicked suspicion or uttered a hasty word would have been consumed by instant punishment. Instead, the All-Wise Creator grants respite, providing time for truth to emerge, for consciences to awaken, and for repentance to be enacted.

This divine forbearance provides an enduring model for human judicial authorities and communal leaders. Justice must never be executed in a spirit of impulsive wrath or hasty vengeance. By exercising measured restraint, upholding procedural safeguards, and allowing space for moral reflection, human institutions reflect the gracious and wise governance of the Almighty, ensuring that justice heals rather than destroys the social fabric. Patience is the hallmark of divine wisdom, sustaining the mortal world against sudden destruction and nurturing the seeds of moral renewal.

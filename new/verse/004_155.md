## Sūrah al-Nisāʾ 4:155

> ˹They were condemned˺ for breaking their covenant, rejecting Allah’s signs, killing the prophets unjustly, and for saying, “Our hearts are unreceptive!”—it is Allah Who has sealed their hearts for their disbelief, so they do not believe except for a few—

### Expanded Commentary

**A chain of violations**

The verse lists broken covenant, denial of signs, unjust killing of prophets, and the claim that hearts are closed. The list shows that spiritual hardening develops through repeated acts, not through a mysterious defect imposed without cause. People refuse signs, harm messengers, and then describe themselves as incapable of receiving truth.

Allah seals their hearts for disbelief, and the classical theological discussions distinguish divine sealing as judgment from the initial human choice that led there. The language warns that persistence can become a condition in which the person no longer wants guidance. It is not an excuse for anyone to stop offering truth or for communities to declare a neighbour beyond mercy.

“Except a few” preserves the Qur’anic refusal to generalize. Even within a community marked by historical violations, some believe. Justice identifies conduct and leaves room for the righteous.

**Hardening begins with repetition**

The listed violations show why believers should repent at the first sign of resistance. A person who repeatedly rejects correction may eventually interpret a closed heart as an unchangeable fact. The Qur’anic mention of righteous exceptions also prevents collective condemnation and keeps justice specific.

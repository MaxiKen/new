## Sūrah al-Nisāʾ 4:155

> ˹They were condemned˺ for breaking their covenant, rejecting Allah’s signs, killing the prophets unjustly, and for saying, “Our hearts are unreceptive!”—it is Allah Who has sealed their hearts for their disbelief, so they do not believe except for a few—

**Expanded Commentary**

**For Breaking Covenant, Rejecting Signs, Killing Prophets**

The verse opens *fa bi mā naqḍihim mīthāqahum wa kufrihim bi āyāti Allāhi wa qatlihim al-anbiyāʾa bi ghayri ḥaqq* — so for their breaking their covenant, and rejecting His signs, and killing the prophets — *anbiyāʾ* — without right. *Bi mā naqḍihim mīthāqahum* — for their breaking their covenant — corresponds to the firm covenant taken at the Mount raised over them and the gate with humility and Sabbath restraint (4:154): where Mount was raised for covenant, they broke it. *Wa kufrihim bi āyāti Allāhi* — and rejecting, covering His signs — was explained as denying the *bayyināt* — clear signs — after which calf was taken (4:153) and Mount raised (4:154): staff, sea, manna, cloud, tablets — all *sulṭān mubīn* Moses was given. *Wa qatlihim al-anbiyāʾa bi ghayri ḥaqq* — and killing prophets without right, unjustly — was explained as murdering some prophets where *“whenever a messenger came with what souls desired not, you grew arrogant, some you denied, some you killed”* (2:87), culminating later with attempt on Jesus, son of Mary, messenger of God (4:157). The sūrah has traced murder’s weight: devouring orphans’ wealth is fire (4:10), killing a believer intentionally is Hell with anger and curse (4:93), throwing sin on the innocent bears slander (4:112) — killing prophets without right is the furthest throw: to throw God’s word back by killing its carrier. The phrase was linked to *“they were condemned for breaking covenant”* as the sūrah’s ledger where *“whoever commits evil will be rewarded accordingly”* (4:123).

**And for Saying — Our Hearts Are Unreceptive**

The addition *wa qawlihim qulūbunā ghulf* — and for saying, their word, *our hearts are wrapped, unreceptive, enclosed, covered* — was explained as excuse for rejecting signs: *ghulf* — wrapped, covered, uncircumcised, sealed — as they said *our hearts are covered* we cannot hear the Book with truth (4:105). The sūrah has shown that the Book was sent with truth that God bears witness to with His knowledge and angels bear witness (4:166), and that He is sufficient as Trustee (4:132); to say *our hearts are ghulf* is to claim the heart cannot receive witness, where earlier the sūrah says *“they hardly comprehend anything”* (4:78) not because heart is *ghulf* but because they hardly reflect. *Ghul f* was read as their claim that hearts are closed and cannot hear, as if ownership of heavens and earth (4:126) cannot open them, where the sūrah elsewhere says *He has sealed their hearts for disbelief, so they believe only few* — sealing is not excuse but consequence.

**It Is God Who Has Sealed Hearts for Disbelief**

The correction *bal ṭabaʿa Allāhu ʿalayhā bi kufrihim* — rather, it is God who has sealed, stamped, upon them, over hearts, for their disbelief, because of their disbelief — was explained as divine sealing after human rejection. *Ṭabaʿa* — to seal, to stamp, to imprint — was read as the same sealing after breaking covenant, rejecting signs, killing prophets without right — the sūrah’s causality: disbelief precedes sealing, sealing confirms disbelief. *Bi kufrihim* — for their *kufr*, because of their covering — was explained as that He left to stray where they pursued what they chose: *We will let them pursue what they chose* (4:115) after defying the Messenger, and *whoever He leaves to stray you will never find a way* (4:88, 4:143) — sealing is that leaving. The phrase was linked to *“God has condemned them for their disbelief, so they do not believe except few”* (4:46) where some Jews take words out of context — sealing there also followed distortion. The sūrah has shown that *“why should He punish if you are grateful and faithful? He is ever Appreciative”* (4:147) — sealing is not where gratitude and faith are, but where *kufr* repeats.

**So They Do Not Believe Except for Few**

The outcome *fa lā yuʾminūna illā qalīlā* — so they do not believe — will not believe — except for a little, except a few — was explained with *qalīl* — little, few — as little belief after sealing, as the sūrah says *we took calf after clear signs yet forgave* (4:153) — some repented — and *but those well-grounded in knowledge among them believe in what was revealed to you and before you* (4:162) — few stand firm. The sūrah has shown that *“whoever believes in God and messengers accepting all, rejecting none, He will give rewards”* (4:152) — the few who believe except few are those who accepted all without distinction (4:150–152). *Illā qalīlan* — except a few — was linked to the beginning where *“you were divided into two groups about hypocrites while God allowed regression because of misdeeds”* (4:88) — few remained straight. The exception preserves hope where the lowest depth for hypocrites also allowed *except those who repent* (4:146): sealing is not absolute where a few believe.

**A Verse That Binds Broken Covenant to Sealed Hearts**

Placed after Mount raised for covenant, gate with humility, Sabbath restraint, firm covenant (4:154), this verse binds broken covenant to sealed hearts after rejecting signs and killing prophets unjustly and saying hearts are *ghulf*. The sūrah has shown that to Allah belongs whatever is in heavens and earth and He is Fully Aware (4:126), that He suffices as Trustee (4:132), that with Him are both rewards (4:134); to claim *our hearts are ghulf* before such Owner and Aware is to refuse the Trust, so sealing is the Trusteeship withdrawn where trust was broken. The verse generalises the particular hypocrisy of waiting between (4:141) into covenantal history: the People of the Book who demanded a book from heaven as condition and demanded visible God (4:153) are the same who broke covenant, rejected signs, killed prophets, claimed hearts are closed — sealing follows claiming closed. The next verses will continue that condemnation: for their denial and outrageous accusation against Mary, and boasting *we killed the Messiah, son of Mary, messenger of God* — but they killed not nor crucified — it was made to appear so — they have no knowledge, only assumptions — they certainly did not kill him (4:156–157).

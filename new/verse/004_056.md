## Sūrah al-Nisāʾ 4:56

> Surely those who reject Our signs, We will cast them into the Fire. Whenever their skin is burnt completely, We will replace it so they will ˹constantly˺ taste the punishment. Indeed, Allah is Almighty, All-Wise.

**Expanded Commentary**

**Those Who Reject Our Signs Will Be Cast Into Fire**

The verse states *inna alladhīna kafarū bi āyātinā sawwfa nuṣlīhim nārā* — surely those who reject Our signs, We will cast them into the Fire. After the division where some believed in him and others turned away and Hell sufficed as blaze (4:55), this verse names the content of rejection: *āyātinā* — Our signs. The sūrah has shown what those signs include in this context: verses about creation from a single soul (4:1), about orphan wealth and witnesses (4:6), about fixed inheritance after debts (4:11–12), about lawful marriage with dowry and chastity (4:24), and about belief in Allah and the Last Day with donation from what was provided (4:39). To reject those signs after they were made clear (4:26) is not a casual dismissal; it is to cover what was shown. The verb *kafara* — to cover, to deny — matches the fabrication of lies against God that was called blatant sin (4:50) and the belief in *jibt* and *ṭāghūt* (4:51). The recompense is cast — *nuṣlīhim* — the same roasting verb used for deliberate devouring where *We will burn them in the Fire, and that is easy* (4:30). The Fire is not a new threat added for this group alone; it is the prepared recompense the sūrah has named for humiliating withholding (4:37), for association not forgiven (4:48), and for envy that bars (4:55). Here it is attached directly to rejection of signs.

**Whenever Their Skin Is Burnt We Will Replace It**

The detail *kullamā naḍijat julūduhum baddalnāhum julūdan ghayrahā li yadhūqū al-ʿadhāb* — whenever their skin is burnt completely, We will replace it so they will constantly taste the punishment — was read as both literal and moral. Littorally, the skin that is the locus of sensation is restored so that sensation does not numb; the punishment is tasted anew — *li yadhūqū* — each time, without the relief that comes when a burn deadens nerves. The Qur’an elsewhere describes the Fire as surrounding and the drink as boiling, and this verse adds the renewal of the sensing surface itself, making the tasting continuous — *constantly* — as the translation renders. Morally, the detail mirrors the persistence of rejection. Those who persistently covered signs — envying bounty (4:54), believing in false gods (4:51), distorting words (4:46) — persisted in a choice that, had it been reversed by soon repentance (4:17), would have been covered. Instead, the covering was on their side, and the recompense is a covering that is renewed only to be burned again. The phrase *kullamā* — whenever — makes the renewal regular, matching the regularity of the choice that led to it.

**So They Will Constantly Taste the Punishment**

The purpose *li yadhūqū al-ʿadhāb* — so they will taste the punishment — was stressed as tasting, not only enduring. *Dhawq* — taste — is the same root used elsewhere for tasting mercy and tasting harm, and it implies direct experience, not hearsay. The sūrah has used tasting for fire before — eating fire into bellies (4:10) — where what was eaten as wealth became fire as food. Here tasting is of punishment itself, renewed with skin, so that knowledge of the Fire is not abstract. The verse generalises what earlier verses specificed for consuming orphan wealth hastily (4:6) and devouring by falsehood deliberately (4:30); all share the same end where the act is tasted as fire. The continuity — skin after skin — also mirrors the continuity of the sūrah’s ethic of deed: not an atom’s weight is wronged but multiplied where good is concerned (4:40), and persistence in rejection is met with persistence in recompense. The one who tasted illicit gain as if it were nourishment will now taste punishment as it is, with the vessel of taste restored each time so that the lesson is not dulled.

**Indeed Allah Is Almighty, All-Wise**

The closing *inna Allāha kāna ʿazīzan ḥakīmā* — indeed, Allah is Almighty, All-Wise — gives the ground for both casting and replacing. *ʿAzīz* — Almighty, Mighty, invincible — was read as the power to cast into Fire and to renew skin without diminishment; nothing restrains the execution of *“and Allah’s command is always executed”* (4:47). *Ḥakīm* — All-Wise — was read as the wisdom that places that power where rejection of signs persisted after clarity, guidance, and lightening (4:26–28). The pair appears here as it does after similar severe recompense, because the sūrah wants the reader to see that the severity is not vengeance but wise placement of might. The One who is sufficient as Guardian and Helper (4:45) is mighty to help those who believed and did good (4:57) and mighty to cast those who rejected signs; the One who wills to make things clear and turn toward you (4:26–27) is wise to renew tasting for those who covered what was made clear. The Names therefore hold the verse between power and reason, so that the promise to believers that follows can be heard as equally assured.

**A Verse That Follows Envy and Precedes Gardens**

Placed between the sufficiency of Hell as blaze where some turned away (4:55) and the Gardens with pure spouses and vast shade where believers do good (4:57), this verse details the Fire that suffices. It tells the community that the signs rejected are not minor regulations but the very limits and mercies that protect orphans, widows, heirs, and neighbours; to reject them is to reject the *āyāt* that make the community humane. The detail of skin replaced shows that the recompense fits the persistence: a persistent covering of signs meets a persistent tasting of fire. For the weak for whom burdens were lightened (4:28) and for whom avoidance of major sins covers lesser faults (4:31), the verse is not meant to crush but to define what major to avoid — rejection of signs after they were made clear. For those who believed and did good, the same Mighty and Wise who renews skin for tasting will renew shade and spouses for honour. The sūrah therefore leaves the reader with two renewals side by side: one of skin for tasting, one of Gardens for abiding, and asks, while the signs are still with you and the choice is still to believe in him or turn away (4:55), which renewal do you seek.

## Sūrah al-Tawbah 9:31

> They have taken their rabbis and monks as well as the Messiah, son of Mary, as lords besides Allah, even though they were commanded to worship none but One God. There is no god ˹worthy of worship˺ except Him. Glorified is He above what they associate ˹with Him˺!

**Expanded Commentary**

**Authority Can Become Worship**

Verse 31 describes people taking rabbis, monks, and the Messiah as lords besides Allah. The verse does not mean that every Christian or Jew bowed physically before a religious scholar. Its point is that obedience can become worship when a person gives a created authority the right to change Allah's commands. A leader who is obeyed as a teacher remains a servant. A leader whose permission makes the forbidden lawful and whose prohibition makes the lawful forbidden has been given a share of divine authority.

The verse therefore examines how religion is governed. People may claim to worship Allah while allowing a human institution to decide truth without reference to revelation. This danger is not limited to the communities named. Muslims can also turn scholars, political leaders, or spiritual figures into lords if loyalty to them becomes more important than obedience to Allah.

**The Messenger Clarified the Meaning**

A well-known report about this verse describes a former Christian Companion who objected that Christians did not worship their rabbis and monks. The Prophet explained that when religious authorities declared lawful what Allah had forbidden or forbidden what Allah had allowed, and people accepted those rulings, they had treated the authorities as lords. The report is preserved in the hadith tradition and gives a practical explanation of the verse.

The point is not that every legal opinion is an act of worship. Scholars interpret evidence, and believers may follow qualified guidance without pretending that a scholar is infallible. The danger begins when a person accepts a command because of the authority's status even when he knows it contradicts Allah's revelation. Obedience becomes idolatrous when it is unconditional and transfers final lawmaking to a creature.

**The Messiah Is Named With the Religious Authorities**

The verse includes the Messiah, son of Mary, in the same sentence as rabbis and monks. This is not an insult to Jesus. The Qur'an honors him as a messenger, word from Allah, and sign to humanity. Naming him here protects the boundary between honoring a prophet and worshipping him. A messenger's greatness lies in his servanthood and in the revelation he carries, not in becoming a partner with the One who sent him.

The correction is especially important because love can distort religious rank. People may begin by admiring a prophet's purity and end by assigning him divine powers. The Qur'an refuses that ascent. Jesus is the Messiah and son of Mary, a created servant chosen by Allah. He is not a rival Lord. The same principle applies to every saint, scholar, ancestor, and leader whose virtues tempt people to exceed the limits of reverence.

**One God Is the Measure of Every Obedience**

The verse states that the communities were commanded to worship one God. The command is simple, but its consequences are extensive. Worship includes prayer and sacrifice, yet it also includes the heart's ultimate submission and the authority by which moral truth is decided. If Allah alone is worshipped, no person can be obeyed against a clear command from Him.

This does not remove legitimate human authority. Parents, judges, teachers, and leaders may have rights and duties. The Qur'an instructs believers to obey those in authority in what is lawful and to refer disputes back to Allah and the Messenger (4:59). Authority is legitimate when it serves the divine order, not when it claims independence from it. The verse's correction is therefore not anarchy. It is the restoration of proper hierarchy.

**Glory Belongs to Allah Alone**

The closing declaration glorifies Allah above the partners they ascribe. The word glory removes every defect that partnership would imply. A partner would mean limitation, dependence, or division of authority. Allah is above all of these. He is not one power among several, and He does not need a religious class to complete His rule.

The statement also protects the believer from resentment toward human leaders. If a scholar makes an error, the answer is not to abandon knowledge. If a leader abuses power, the answer is not to make the self its own god. The believer returns to Allah's revealed standard and judges people by it. Glorifying Allah produces both humility before truth and courage before authority.

**The Modern Test Is Unconditional Loyalty**

Modern institutions often demand loyalty in the language of identity. A follower may be told that questioning the leader harms the community, that the group cannot survive criticism, or that obedience proves sincerity. Verse 31 asks whether the leader has been given a place that belongs to Allah. If his words are treated as unquestionable, if his interests decide what is true, and if his followers excuse clear wrong because of him, the danger is present.

The remedy is not disrespect. Honor teachers by learning from them, but keep the final judgment with Allah. Love the Messiah by following the truth he carried, not by placing him beside the Creator. The verse ends with tawḥīd because every corrupted authority is corrected at the same point: there is no god worthy of worship except Him. Once that center is restored, obedience to people can return to its proper place as limited service rather than worship. The scholar is honored most safely when both teacher and student remember that Allah alone has final authority. This humility allows correction without contempt and learning without surrender. It protects the community from both blind following and the arrogant claim that no teacher is needed.

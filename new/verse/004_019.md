## Sūrah al-Nisāʾ 4:19

> O believers! It is not permissible for you to inherit women against their will or mistreat them to make them return some of the dowry ˹as a ransom for divorce˺—unless they are found guilty of adultery. Treat them fairly. If you happen to dislike them, you may hate something which Allah turns into a great blessing.

### Expanded Commentary

**Women are persons, not inheritable property**

The verse abolishes a practice in which male relatives treated a deceased man’s widow as part of the estate. They might marry her, compel her to marry someone else, confine her until she surrendered property, or simply prevent her from choosing a new life. Revelation addresses the men who exercised this power and declares that a woman cannot be inherited against her will. She is herself a legal subject and an inheritor, not an asset to be transferred.

The prohibition also covers using harsh treatment to force a wife to return part of her dowry. A husband who wants release from a marriage may not manufacture misery so that the wife purchases freedom with property that was already hers. The exception concerning flagrant sexual misconduct must be interpreted through due process and the rest of the Qur’anic legal framework; it is not a licence for accusation, violence, or unilateral confiscation.

**Living together with *maʿrūf***

The command to consort with wives honourably includes good speech, adequate provision, respect for privacy, fulfilment of marital rights, and restraint during disagreement. *Maʿrūf* means what is recognized as decent and sound, not whatever a powerful husband can persuade himself is acceptable. Custom matters only when it is compatible with justice and revelation.

**Dislike is not a verdict**

The verse acknowledges that a spouse may dislike something in the other, but warns that human perception is partial. A quality that seems burdensome may conceal patience, loyalty, or goodness; a period of difficulty may be transformed by effort and counsel. This is not an instruction to remain in every harmful relationship. Abuse, danger, and persistent injustice must be addressed, and separation can be lawful and merciful. It is a warning against treating ordinary disappointment as permission to strip a spouse of rights.

The order is telling: first prohibit coercion, then command good treatment, then educate the heart about its limited knowledge. Marriage cannot be sustained by feeling alone, but it can be protected when law prevents exploitation and character refuses to turn irritation into oppression.

**Honourable association has limits**

Kind treatment does not require remaining in a relationship that is unsafe or persistently destructive. The verse’s encouragement to look for hidden good belongs with its prohibitions on coercion and the chapter’s arbitration procedures. It teaches patience where patience can repair, while the wider law protects a spouse from being trapped by a husband’s dislike, anger, or financial power.

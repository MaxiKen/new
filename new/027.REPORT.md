## Resolution of the 16 UNVERIFIED items

All sixteen markers have been researched and removed; the chapter now contains **zero `[UNVERIFIED:]` tags**. Each was replaced with a sourced finding or an explicit, argued classification.

**Positively resolved (source identified):**

| # | Verse | Item | Resolution |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 27:28 | Ḥadīth on God's love of excuses | **Authentic and located.** Ṣaḥīḥ al-Bukhārī 7416 (al-Mughīrah ibn Shuʿbah), Ṣaḥīḥ Muslim 2760 (Ibn Masʿūd), Muslim 1499a (Saʿd ibn ʿUbādah). Quoted with references. |
| 2 | 27:49 | Three-day interval at Thamūd | **Qur'anic, not tafsīr.** Stated at 11:65, now quoted and tagged. Only the placement of *this meeting* inside the window is exegetical, and that distinction is drawn in the text. |
| 3 | 27:53 | Etymology of Ḥaḍramawt | **Disproved.** The name is attested as *ḥḍrmt* in Old South Arabian inscriptions from the mid-first millennium BC, transcribed by Theophrastus as Ἁδρραμύτα (late 4th/early 3rd c. BC), called Chatramotitai by Strabo, and listed as Hazarmaveth in Genesis 10:26 — centuries before Ṣāliḥ's supposed death there. Identified as a folk etymology. |
| 4 | 27:82 | The beast of the earth | **Authentic core separated from accretion.** The sound material is Jāmiʿ at-Tirmidhī 3072 (Abū Hurayrah, graded *ṣaḥīḥ*), which names the beast among three signs after which faith no longer benefits and describes nothing. The cubits, composite anatomy, staff and seal trace to ath-Thaʿlabī and the storytellers. |
| 5 | 27:40 | Āṣif ibn Barkhiyā / Khiḍr | **Provenance traced.** The name enters via Ibn Isḥāq the biographer, placing it in transmitted narrative rather than the ḥadīth corpus; Khiḍr is an opinion from Ibn Masʿūd. |
| 6 | 27:44 | Queen's jinn ancestry, legs, marriage | **Classified from within the tradition.** Ibn Kathīr names ath-Thaʿlabī as the channel, flags the weak narrator where a report touches the Prophet, and states outright that the marriage account (from Wahb ibn Munabbih) is not authenticated. |
| 7 | 27:22 | The name Bilqīs | **Non-scriptural, provenance shown.** Absent from Qur'an and Bible; al-Ṭabarī withholds it for most of his narration; genealogies arrive via ath-Thaʿlabī without chains and inconsistently; the South Arabian epigraphic record preserves no such queen. |
| 8 | 27:88 | The Junayd anecdote | **Misattribution corrected.** Al-Qurṭubī's three allegorical readings come via al-Māwardī, the first attributed to Sahl ibn ʿAbdullāh. The Junayd story is not in that discussion; the section was rewritten around the correctly attributed readings, with the anecdote marked as later Sufi circulation. |
| 9 | 27:11 | Saying of al-Ḥasan al-Baṣrī | **Withdrawn.** Chainless attributions to al-Ḥasan are a documented problem in ḥadīth criticism. The point is now carried by al-Qurṭubī's own argument and 28:16. |

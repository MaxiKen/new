**"The Devils Plunge Their Associates Deeper"**

*Wa-ikhwānuhum yamuddūnahum fī al-ghayyi thumma lā yuqṣirūn* — but the devils persistently plunge their ˹human˺ associates deeper into wickedness, sparing no effort.

**The Contrast Stated**

The verse completes the pair begun in 7:201, and its subject is the other category of people: those whose association with the devils is reciprocal. The Book's readers are shown two conditions rather than two natures, and the verse's verb — the devils' persistence — describes a process that continues as long as it is not interrupted.

**The Word for the Associates**

*Ikhwānuhum* — their brothers — and the Book's use of kinship for the relation between the devils and the wicked is deliberate: the sūrah's readers are shown a companionship rather than a servitude, and the Qur'an's language about evil presents it as a society as well as a condition.

**"Plunge Them Deeper"**

*Yamuddūnahum fī al-ghayy* — the verb is one of extension, of stretching a rope, of drawing a thing out; the image is of a deepening that is willed from both sides. The sūrah's readers are shown that the plunging is not an assault on the unwilling, and the Book's narrative of the fall of Adam's adversary had already portrayed the devil's method as invitation (7:20).

**"Sparing No Effort"**

*Thumma lā yuqṣirūn* — the clause states the persistence of the process, and the Qur'an's warnings about the devil's patience are stated elsewhere in the Book. The sūrah's readers are told that the associate's situation does not stabilize, and the passage's image of deepening water is meant to convey the direction of travel.

**The Book's Doctrine of Companionship**

The Qur'an states repeatedly that the company a person keeps becomes his company in the next life: whoever turns away from the Reminder is assigned a devil as a companion (43:36), and the Book's readers are given the same teaching in the sūrah's own accounts of communities that followed their leaders into ruin. The verse's word for brotherhood belongs to that doctrine.

**The Verse's Place in the Sūrah's Close**

The verse stands between the description of the mindful (7:201) and the answer about signs (7:203), and its function is to state what happens when the practice of verse 200 is not performed. The sūrah's readers are thus given both the remedy and the alternative in consecutive lines.

**The Book's Account of the Devil's Work**

The Qur'an's statements about the devil describe his activity as temptation, embellishment, and companionship, and it consistently presents his power as limited by the creature's response. The sūrah's earlier narrative of his refusal to prostrate had already stated the terms of his relation to the human being (7:11–18), and the verse's description of his persistence belongs with that account.

**The Reader's Position**

The verse describes a class rather than an individual case, and the sūrah's readers are given the two options in the passage as a whole. The Book's teaching here is that the difference between the groups lies not in the intensity of the attack but in the answer given to it, and the verse's contrast makes the point without comment.

**The Sūrah's Earlier Warnings**

The sūrah had warned its readers about the communities that were overtaken while unaware (7:95) and had described the heedless who have the faculties and do not use them (7:179). The verse's image of a deepening course belongs to the same sequence of warnings, and the sūrah's readers are given the diagnosis in several registers.

**The Next Verse's Question**

The following verse answers the demand for a sign with the Prophet's statement that he follows only what is revealed to him (7:203). The transition from the discussion of the devils to the discussion of the revelation is significant: the sūrah's readers are shown what the alternative to the devils' company is, and the answer is stated as the Book itself.

**The Community's Vigilance**

The verse was read by the community as a caution about complacency in the practice of remembrance, and the tradition's teaching about the heart's hardening drew on passages such as this. The sūrah's readers are told that the plunge is gradual and unnoticed, and the Book's remedy has been given in the preceding verse.

**The Passage Complete**

The sūrah's account of the two responses is now complete: refuge and remembrance on one side (7:200–201), and the deepening course on the other (7:202). The Book's arrangement leaves the reader between the two, and the sūrah's final four verses supply the practice that ends the passage.

The verse's description of the devils' persistence also implies the community's part in the process, since the associates are being drawn by beings they have chosen as brothers. The Book's readers are shown that the companionship is the result of a prior agreement, and the sūrah's earlier narrative of the devil's refusal had stated the terms: he asked for respite and was granted it until the appointed day (7:15).

The Qur'an's warnings about the devil's patience are matched by its assurances about God's protection, and the sūrah's passage places the two in sequence: the refuge and remembrance of verses 200–201 stand before the account of the plunge in verse 202. The sūrah's readers are given the remedy before the diagnosis, and the order is the Book's usual provision for its community.

The passage's vocabulary of drawing and stretching is also used by the Book for its own dealings with the obstinate, and the sūrah's readers are shown that the same image can describe the extension of a term or the deepening of a course. The verse's context leaves no doubt which of the two the associates receive.

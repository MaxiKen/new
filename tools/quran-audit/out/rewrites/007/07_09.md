## Sūrah al-Aʿrāf 7:9

> **And as for those whose balance is light, it is they who have ruined their souls by having treated Our signs wrongfully.**

**Expanded Commentary**

**Lightness as a Verdict**

The verse completes the pair begun in 7:8 with an exact mirror: the heavy scale and the light. What makes a scale light is not always the absence of deeds — the scrolls and the card have already shown that volume counts for nothing — but the absence of weight. The measure of the hereafter is not the number of acts but their worth, and the worth of an act is set by the aim to which it was offered.

The fate is then stated in commercial Arabic: *alladhīna khasirū anfusahum* — those who have lost themselves. *Khusrān* is the merchant's word for a loss, and the Qur'anic usage is exact: this is not the loss of profit but the loss of capital. The person in the verse leaves the market of his life having traded away the very self he was trading with. The same ledger appears in another sūrah: those who reject the meeting with their Lord have *“rendered their deeds void, so We will not give their deeds any weight on Judgment Day”* (18:105). A deed with no weight to give is a loss no accounting can repair.

**The Cause: Wronging the Signs**

The verse then names the cause, and it is not the one a reader might expect. Lightness is not diagnosed here as weakness or as ignorance. It is *an ẓalamū bi-āyātinā ẓulm* — that they wronged Our signs. The noun is the same one the townspeople used of themselves in 7:5, and the echo ties the sūrah's opening to its weighing: the destruction of the first verses and the light scale here are one offense at two moments of the story. Wronging a sign (*ẓulm*) is, in the Qur'an's usage, putting it out of its place — denying it outright, or acknowledging the words while treating them as though they carried no claim, or bending their meaning toward a preferred outcome. The imperfect *yaẓlimūn* adds what "having treated wrongfully" can only hint at: this was not a single lapse but a practice.

The seriousness follows from what a sign is. The signs — the revelation, the messengers, the world itself with its dawns and its marks of design — do not merely inform; they oblige. To meet one and shrug is not neutrality; it is a decision about the One who sent it. That is why the wronging of signs is counted among the gravest acts, and why the lightness of the scale is its natural consequence: a person who spends a lifetime refusing weight arrives at the judgment with none.

**The Exactness That Spares No One**

The verses around this one guard the verdict from despair. *“We will set up the scales of justice on the Day of Judgment, so no soul will be wronged in the least”* (21:47). *“Indeed, Allah never wrongs anyone — even by an atom's weight”* (4:40). *“So whoever does an atom's weight of good will see it, and whoever does an atom's weight of evil will see it”* (99:7–8). The lightness of this verse is therefore no accident of arithmetic, and no one will be condemned for a gram that was never his to supply: the same Book that weighs a mustard seed also declares that God does not require of any soul more than it can bear (2:286).

What follows in the sūrah — the settlement of humanity on the earth, its brief tenure, and the return (7:10 onward) — reads as the practical conclusion of this weighing. If the scale is real, then the days in which a pan may still be loaded with something of weight are exactly the days being lived now. Verse 9 closes the ledger with a warning whose force lies in its tense: those who *were* wronging Our signs made their own lightness, one deliberate and unremarkable moment at a time.

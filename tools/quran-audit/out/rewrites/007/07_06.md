## Sūrah al-Aʿrāf 7:6

> **Then We shall surely question those unto whom Our message was sent, and We shall surely question the messengers.**

**Expanded Commentary**

**Two Interrogations, One Order**

The previous verse left the nations with a confession; this one opens the court in which the confession is taken. The emphatic form is deliberate: *la-nasʾalanna* is an oath-future reinforced by *nūn al-tawkīd*, the strongest way Arabic has of saying *this will certainly happen*. The parties are named in a fixed order — first *those unto whom the message was sent*, then *the messengers* themselves.

The order is the argument. The community stands first because the question at issue is what it did with what reached it; the messengers stand second because their testimony closes the door on every plea of ignorance. No one will be able to say that the warning never came, because the same court will call the carrier who brought it.

**What the Messengers Are Asked**

The Qur'an describes the messengers' interrogation, and it is narrow: *“the Day Allah will gather the messengers and say, ‘What response did you receive?’”* (5:109). The question is not whether they were eloquent or successful — success was never theirs to engineer — but whether the message was delivered. The Book states their commission in the same short form again and again: *“your duty is only to deliver the message”* (13:40); *“then your duty is only to deliver the message clearly”* (16:82). A messenger's account is settled by the delivery, and the delivery is what leaves the community without an excuse.

**Why the People Are Questioned at All**

The classical commentators took seriously a difficulty that other verses create: some passages seem to say the guilty will not be questioned. *“On that Day there will be no need for any human or jinn to be asked about their sins”* (55:39). A witness will be brought from every faith-community (4:41; 16:84), the record will be laid open (39:69). How can there be interrogation if there is nothing to ask?

The answer the exegetes gave is the key to this verse: the questioning is not an investigation. God does not ask in order to learn; the account is already complete, and the very next verse says so — *“Then We shall recount unto them with knowledge.”* The questioning of 7:6 is the arraignment: the moment the evidence is put to the person and the person is made to stand inside it. That is why 16:84 ends with the disbelievers allowed neither to plead nor to appeal — the interrogation exposes the excuse; it does not discover the fact.

**The Interrogation That Has No Interpreter**

The Prophet ﷺ described the setting of that encounter with the precision of an eyewitness: *“There is none among you except that Allah will speak to him, with no interpreter between Him and him. He will look to his right and see nothing except what he has sent forth, and to his left nothing except what he has sent forth, and ahead of him nothing but the Fire — so protect yourselves from the Fire even with half a date.”* (al-Bukhārī 1413; Muslim 1016).

The detail that carries this verse's weight is the absence of intermediaries. Human power is screened: secretaries, advocates, patrons, spokesmen stand between the ruler and the ruled. The court of 7:6 has none. The question is put by the Lord directly to the servant, with the witnesses and the messengers present — and every layered defence a person spent a lifetime building (*my father did so*, *my people did so*, *no one told me*) has already been answered by the earlier session of the same court, in which the carriers were asked and the delivery was confirmed.

In two clauses, then, the verse gives the whole architecture of accountability: a message that arrived, carriers who delivered it, and a meeting in which both sides answer — the carriers for the delivery, the receivers for the response, and no one else to blame.

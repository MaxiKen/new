Each section must genuinely belong to its own verse.

- the key theological, legal, historical, linguistic, or practical issues
- the relevant scholarly material
- the relationship between the verse's wording and its meaning

Then write specifically around those matters.

A useful internal test is:

«If the verse blockquote were removed, would it still be obvious which verse this commentary explains?»

If not, rewrite it.

Do not fill space with generic Qur'anic discussion.

8. Scholarly Depth

Match the depth demonstrated by "expanded/007.md".

As a general guide:

- Standard verse: 1,200–1,400 words
- Dense doctrinal/theological section: 1,400–1,800 words
- Major theological or legal crux: up to 2,100 words
- Genuine short fragment: approximately 700 words minimum, when the verse itself genuinely requires shorter treatment

These are quality bands, not targets to pad toward.

Never repeat the same point merely to reach a word count.

Depth should come from genuine material such as:

- important vocabulary
- roots and linguistic meaning when genuinely relevant
- grammatical construction when it affects interpretation
- lexical range
- theological implications
- relevant Qur'anic cross-references
- full quotations from relevant Qur'anic passages
- verified hadith
- named exegetes
- differences between exegetes
- occasion of revelation
- historical context
- legal rulings and their conditions
- practical implications
- logical reasoning
- relatable examples

Do not manufacture depth.

9. Mini-Headings

Each standard verse section should normally contain 8–10 concise bold mini-headings.

Each heading must identify the argument or question addressed immediately below it.

For example:

Why does the verse begin with this warning?

What does this command require in practice?

How does this wording establish the theological principle?

What did the early exegetes understand from this expression?

Do not use the same heading pattern repeatedly across every verse.

Headings must vary naturally according to the content of each verse.

Each heading should introduce a genuinely developed point, not merely divide a paragraph.

Avoid headings that simply repeat the verse's wording.

---

10. Logical Reasoning and Examples

The commentary must not merely state conclusions.

Explain why the conclusion follows.

When useful, make the reasoning understandable through ordinary examples.

For example, if a verse establishes that a person's outward action is not enough without the correct intention, explain the principle through an everyday situation where two people perform the same action but have completely different purposes.

Examples must clarify the Qur'anic principle.

Do not invent examples that imply historical facts.

Do not let examples dominate the commentary.

11. Qur'anic Quotations

Whenever quoting another Qur'anic passage:

- retrieve the exact wording from "translation/SSS.txt"
- copy a contiguous span
- preserve editorial brackets such as "˹O Prophet˺"
- place the citation immediately after the quotation
- use the form "(S:V)"
- never invent the wording
- never combine disconnected passages with "…"
- never paraphrase while pretending to quote

Example:

«“Exact translated wording” (2:255)»

Every quotation must be verified before it is written.

If a literal explanation is needed, place the explanation outside quotation marks.

Do not put a paraphrase inside quotation marks.

13. Hadith Verification

Never invent a hadith reference.

Before using a hadith collection number:

1. Search the internet.
2. Verify that the report exists.
3. Verify the collection.
4. Verify the hadith number where possible.
5. Confirm that the report actually supports the point being made.

If the exact reference cannot be verified, do not present a number as fact.

Instead:

- attribute the report to a named scholar/exegete as a reported tradition where appropriate, or
- omit the report and support the point from the Qur'an or verified scholarship.

Do not fabricate Arabic wording, chains, numbers, or textual variants.

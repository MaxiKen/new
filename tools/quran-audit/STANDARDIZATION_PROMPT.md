10. Scholarly Depth

Match the depth demonstrated by "expanded/007.md".

As a general quality guide:

- Standard verse: approximately 1,200–1,400 words
- Dense doctrinal/theological verse: approximately 1,400–1,800 words
- Major theological or legal crux: up to approximately 2,100 words
- Genuine short fragment: approximately 700 words minimum, when the verse itself genuinely requires shorter treatment

These are quality bands, not padding targets.

Do not repeat an idea simply to reach a word count.

Depth should come from genuine substance, including where relevant:

- vocabulary
- lexical meaning
- linguistic analysis
- grammatical construction
- rhetorical force
- theological implications
- Qur'anic cross-references
- exact Qur'anic quotations
- verified hadith

11. Mini-Headings

A standard verse should normally contain approximately 8–10 concise bold mini-headings, following the style demonstrated by "expanded/007.md".

Each heading must introduce a genuine argument, issue, or question.

For example:

- Why does the verse begin with this warning?
- What does this command require in practice?
- How does this wording establish the theological principle?
- What did the early exegetes understand from this expression?

Do not use the same heading pattern repeatedly across every verse.

Headings must vary naturally according to the actual content.

Do not create headings merely to divide paragraphs.

---

12. Logical Reasoning

Do not merely state scholarly conclusions.

Explain why the conclusion follows.

Where useful, make the reasoning understandable through ordinary examples.

For example, if discussing intention, an everyday situation can show how two people may perform the same outward action while having completely different purposes.

Examples must clarify the Qur'anic principle.

Do not present invented examples as historical facts.

Do not allow examples to dominate the commentary.

13. Qur'anic Cross-References and Quotations

Whenever another Qur'anic passage is quoted:

1. Retrieve the exact wording from the repository's translation files.
2. Use the exact English wording.
3. Copy a contiguous passage.
4. Preserve editorial brackets such as "˹O Prophet˺".
5. Place the citation immediately after the quotation.
6. Use the form "(S:V)".
7. Never invent wording.
8. Never combine disconnected passages using "…".
9. Never paraphrase while presenting the text as an exact quotation.

Example:

«“Exact translated wording” (2:255)»

Every quotation must be verified before inclusion.

If an explanation is a paraphrase, keep it outside quotation marks.

For each hadith:

1. Verify that the report actually exists.
2. Verify the collection.
3. Verify the hadith number where possible.
4. Verify that the wording/reference corresponds to the report.
5. Verify that it genuinely supports the point being made.
6. Prefer reliable primary or authoritative sources.
7. Do not fabricate Arabic wording.
8. Do not fabricate chains.
9. Do not fabricate hadith numbers.
10. Do not fabricate textual variants.

If an exact reference cannot be verified, do not present a number as established fact.

Either:

- attribute the report cautiously to a named exegete or scholar as a reported tradition, where appropriate; or
- omit it and use another verified source.

Evidence should be plentiful where the subject genuinely calls for it. Relevant Qur'anic cross-references, authentic hadith, early scholarly reports, tafsīr authorities, and other reliable evidence should be incorporated generously rather than relying on unsupported assertions.

Do not, however, add irrelevant evidence simply to increase quantity.

## Sūrah al-Baqarah 2:176

> **That is because Allah has revealed the Book in truth. And surely those who differ regarding it are totally engrossed in opposition.**

### Expanded Commentary

**The "That Is Because" Bridge**

At this point in al-Baqarah, the wording brings because and differ into one moral frame. The sentence should not be detached from that relationship. Its first term in 2:176 sets the subject, while the later term shows what that subject demands, permits, or exposes. The order of 2:176 gives the reader a path through the verse: begin with what Allah names, follow the consequence, and resist the temptation to turn a living address into a slogan.

That is because indicates that the punishment applies because of their treatment of Allahs Book, going back to v. 174 (al-Ṭabarī). For schism (shiqāq; see also 22:53; 38:2; 41:52), see 2:137c. It is also possible that Book could refer to the Torah and the Gospel, which attest to the truth of the Prophet according to Islamic belief, and that differ pertains to questions of interpretation and distortion (see 2:75c), or it could refer to the Bible as well as the Qur'an together as being collectively Allahs Book .

**Schism Pronounced on Dissension**

The wording around because assumes that revelation is meant to be heard with intelligence and humility together. Understanding is not an invitation to place the reader above the text; neither is obedience a reason to stop thinking. The classical tradition preserved both tasks: clarify the language, then ask what kind of person the clarified command is meant to form.

A useful way to test the verse is to ask what would change if because were taken seriously when no audience was present. The answer cannot be only a feeling, because 2:176 connects inward recognition with a discernible choice. Nor can it be only an outward display, because the choice in 2:176 loses its Qur'anic meaning when it is made for reputation rather than for Allah. The passage holds intention and action together in 2:176.

**The Rule of the Single Truth-Book**

The relation between because and differ also protects the verse from two opposite misreadings. It should not be expanded into a claim the wording of 2:176 does not make, but neither should its brevity be used to make it weightless. Short Qur'anic statements such as 2:176 often function as hinges: they close one movement, open another, and give the next command its moral setting. The careful reader of 2:176 therefore asks what has just been established, what response is now being required, and what kind of character could respond without evasion. Those questions keep 2:176 specific while allowing its guidance to reach a new circumstance.

The commentators sometimes preserve more than one report about the setting of 2:176. Such reports should be weighed rather than treated as interchangeable decoration. An occasion can explain why a question arose, but the wording of 2:176 determines how far the guidance travels. The verse speaks first to the situation that occasioned it and then to every reader who meets the same relation between because and differ.

**The Book as an address**

In 2:176, the distinction between public appearance and inward truth is especially relevant. A person may use the language of because while arranging the act around another purpose. Allah's judgment in 2:176 is not limited to the visible outline, yet the inner intention is not an excuse for ignoring the rights and forms that the revelation establishes. Sincerity completes obedience; it does not cancel it.

One can see the social wisdom of 2:176 in a small everyday case. When people share responsibility for because, uncertainty about the duty tends to fall on the person with less power or less information. A clear moral rule changes that balance. In 2:176, it tells the stronger party what must not be taken, and gives the weaker party language with which to name the wrong.

**The word that carries the argument**

In 2:176, the verse also sits within the Qur'an's larger refusal to separate worship from truthfulness. Prayer, remembrance, generosity, patience, and justice are different acts in 2:176, but they are trained by the same capacity to stand before Allah without constructing a false account. The statement in 2:176 strengthens that capacity by making one particular choice visible.

A reader who finds the command in 2:176 difficult should distinguish difficulty from impossibility. For 2:176, difficulty may be the place where desire, habit, or social pressure is being corrected; impossibility is a separate legal and moral question. The passage does not praise needless hardship, but it does not permit comfort to redefine because until the demand disappears.

**Knowledge joined to obedience**

The final emphasis of 2:176 returns to responsibility. No explanation of because is complete if it leaves the reader with information about earlier communities but no question about present conduct. The verse 2:176 asks what can be repaired now: a promise kept, a right restored, a motive purified, or a harmful practice abandoned. That is where knowledge becomes guidance.

The choice of the word because in 2:176 also matters. It leaves room for the full context of 2:176 instead of allowing one translation gloss to carry every shade of meaning. Classical Arabic often places a broad word inside a narrow scene; in 2:176, the surrounding clauses determine whether the emphasis falls on an act, an intention, a condition, or a consequence. Here the reader should let the sentence do that work.

**The reader’s responsibility**

The exegetical tradition approaches because from more than one angle, but its differences are more useful when their shared question is made visible. The commentators ask what the wording of 2:176 includes, what it excludes, and how it fits with the other passages of the Qur'an. A responsible reading can preserve those alternatives in 2:176 without pretending that every possibility has equal strength.

There is also a distinction between understanding because and using it as a label for other people. The first task belongs to learning; the second requires evidence, fairness, and awareness of one's own motives in 2:176. The verse 2:176 addresses the reader before it supplies material for judging a neighbour. Its warning becomes safer and more productive when self-examination comes first.

**Why the wording is exact**

The theology and practical instruction of 2:176 therefore support one another. If Allah knows or commands differ, that knowledge or command changes how a person handles time, power, money, speech, and private desire. A doctrine that never reaches those choices has not yet been read at the level demanded by 2:176.

An ordinary example makes the moral structure of 2:176 visible without replacing the revelation. Two people may perform an outwardly similar act involving because; one may be protecting a right, while the other is seeking praise, escaping a duty, or taking advantage of confusion. The act's surface does not settle its value. Intention, circumstance, and the rights of others complete the judgment.

**A question answered by the surrounding verses**

The surrounding verses keep the reader from isolating because from the rest of life. In 2:176, revelation corrects the mind but also trains habits: how a person speaks when challenged, gives when unseen, waits when angry, and returns after error. Those habits are where this verse becomes more than information about because. They are the place in which assent to 2:176 is either confirmed or quietly withdrawn.

The most balanced response to 2:176 is neither to make the passage vague nor to claim more than it says. Its boundaries are part of its guidance. Within them, the verse can illuminate a new situation because its underlying moral relation remains stable: because is joined to differ, and the person is responsible for how that relation is honoured.

The wording of 2:176 gives the reader a useful order of questions about because. What does the term because mean in ordinary Arabic, what does the surrounding sentence restrict it to, and what consequence follows when the command is obeyed or refused in 2:176? These questions prevent a commentary on 2:176 from becoming a list of slogans. They make the act of interpretation answerable to grammar, context, and the moral end of this passage at 2:176.

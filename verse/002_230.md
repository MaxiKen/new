## Sūrah al-Baqarah 2:230

> **So if a husband divorces his wife ˹three times˺, then it is not lawful for him to remarry her until after she has married another man and then is divorced. Then it is permissible for them to reunite, as long as they feel they are able to maintain the limits of Allah. These are the limits set by Allah, which He makes clear for people of knowledge.**

### Expanded Commentary

**The Third Divorce — Finality Restored**

At this point in al-Baqarah, the wording brings husband and three into one moral frame. The sentence should not be detached from that relationship. Its first term in 2:230 sets the subject, while the later term shows what that subject demands, permits, or exposes. The order of 2:230 gives the reader a path through the verse: begin with what Allah names, follow the consequence, and resist the temptation to turn a living address into a slogan.

The prohibition against the couple remarrying each other after the third divorce declaration unless and until the woman marries and divorces another man is an extension of the logic of limiting declarative divorce (v. 229) and the “forswearing” of marital relations (v. 226). That means that if a man were free to remarry his wife after three divorce declarations, he would then be free to conduct yet another three divorce declarations and repeat the process continuously, thus keeping the wife from the full enjoyment of marriage (through successive waiting periods) while denying her the freedom to marry another.

**The Ḥalālah Its Dreaded Guard-Rail**

Forbidding a man from remarrying a woman after making her wait three sets of three courses (one set after each divorce declaration) limits the harm he can inflict on her and discourages him from declaring divorce, since the prospect of remarrying a woman who is married to someone else is in most cases unlikely. According to most jurists the intervening marriage needs to have been consummated for the original couple to remarry . This condition applies for those who have carried out three divorces; remarriage with only a single divorce declaration is discussed in v. 232.

The reference to husband places a human being who may be dependent, exposed, or unable to defend a claim at the centre of the discussion. Responsibility here is not merely a feeling of affection. It has a form that can be recognized, a limit that can be enforced, and a purpose: to prevent the strong from making the weak carry the cost of decisions they did not control.

**Marked-Clear to People of Knowing**

A useful way to test the verse is to ask what would change if husband were taken seriously when no audience was present. The answer cannot be only a feeling, because 2:230 connects inward recognition with a discernible choice. Nor can it be only an outward display, because the choice in 2:230 loses its Qur'anic meaning when it is made for reputation rather than for Allah. The passage holds intention and action together in 2:230.

**A household placed under moral care**

The relation between husband and three also protects the verse from two opposite misreadings. It should not be expanded into a claim the wording of 2:230 does not make, but neither should its brevity be used to make it weightless. Short Qur'anic statements such as 2:230 often function as hinges: they close one movement, open another, and give the next command its moral setting. The careful reader of 2:230 therefore asks what has just been established, what response is now being required, and what kind of character could respond without evasion. Those questions keep 2:230 specific while allowing its guidance to reach a new circumstance.

The most balanced response to 2:230 is neither to make the passage vague nor to claim more than it says. Its boundaries are part of its guidance. Within them, the verse can illuminate a new situation because its underlying moral relation remains stable: husband is joined to three, and the person is responsible for how that relation is honoured.

**The rights concealed in the wording**

The wording of 2:230 gives the reader a useful order of questions about husband. What does the term husband mean in ordinary Arabic, what does the surrounding sentence restrict it to, and what consequence follows when the command is obeyed or refused in 2:230? These questions prevent a commentary on 2:230 from becoming a list of slogans. They make the act of interpretation answerable to grammar, context, and the moral end of this passage at 2:230.

The commentators sometimes preserve more than one report about the setting of 2:230. Such reports should be weighed rather than treated as interchangeable decoration. An occasion can explain why a question arose, but the wording of 2:230 determines how far the guidance travels. The verse speaks first to the situation that occasioned it and then to every reader who meets the same relation between husband and three.

**Time, vulnerability, and restraint**

In 2:230, the distinction between public appearance and inward truth is especially relevant. A person may use the language of husband while arranging the act around another purpose. Allah's judgment in 2:230 is not limited to the visible outline, yet the inner intention is not an excuse for ignoring the rights and forms that the revelation establishes. Sincerity completes obedience; it does not cancel it.

One can see the social wisdom of 2:230 in a small everyday case. When people share responsibility for husband, uncertainty about the duty tends to fall on the person with less power or less information. A clear moral rule changes that balance. In 2:230, it tells the stronger party what must not be taken, and gives the weaker party language with which to name the wrong.

**The difference between power and justice**

In 2:230, the verse also sits within the Qur'an's larger refusal to separate worship from truthfulness. Prayer, remembrance, generosity, patience, and justice are different acts in 2:230, but they are trained by the same capacity to stand before Allah without constructing a false account. The statement in 2:230 strengthens that capacity by making one particular choice visible.

A reader who finds the command in 2:230 difficult should distinguish difficulty from impossibility. For 2:230, difficulty may be the place where desire, habit, or social pressure is being corrected; impossibility is a separate legal and moral question. The passage does not praise needless hardship, but it does not permit comfort to redefine husband until the demand disappears.

**How the jurists read the command**

The final emphasis of 2:230 returns to responsibility. No explanation of husband is complete if it leaves the reader with information about earlier communities but no question about present conduct. The verse 2:230 asks what can be repaired now: a promise kept, a right restored, a motive purified, or a harmful practice abandoned. That is where knowledge becomes guidance.

The choice of the word husband in 2:230 also matters. It leaves room for the full context of 2:230 instead of allowing one translation gloss to carry every shade of meaning. Classical Arabic often places a broad word inside a narrow scene; in 2:230, the surrounding clauses determine whether the emphasis falls on an act, an intention, a condition, or a consequence. Here the reader should let the sentence do that work.

**Protection from private harm**

The exegetical tradition approaches husband from more than one angle, but its differences are more useful when their shared question is made visible. The commentators ask what the wording of 2:230 includes, what it excludes, and how it fits with the other passages of the Qur'an. A responsible reading can preserve those alternatives in 2:230 without pretending that every possibility has equal strength.

There is also a distinction between understanding husband and using it as a label for other people. The first task belongs to learning; the second requires evidence, fairness, and awareness of one's own motives in 2:230. The verse 2:230 addresses the reader before it supplies material for judging a neighbour. Its warning becomes safer and more productive when self-examination comes first.

The theology and practical instruction of 2:230 therefore support one another. If Allah knows or commands three, that knowledge or command changes how a person handles time, power, money, speech, and private desire. A doctrine that never reaches those choices has not yet been read at the level demanded by 2:230.

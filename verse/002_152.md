## Sūrah al-Baqarah 2:152

> **remember Me; I will remember you. And thank Me, and never be ungrateful.**

### Expanded Commentary

**The Consequence of Being Supplied Everything**

At this point in al-Baqarah, the wording brings thank and ungrateful into one moral frame. The sentence should not be detached from that relationship. Its first term in 2:152 sets the subject, while the later term shows what that subject demands, permits, or exposes. The order of 2:152 gives the reader a path through the verse: begin with what Allah names, follow the consequence, and resist the temptation to turn a living address into a slogan.

**The Greatest Reciprocity**

It is understood by some that the beginning of the previous verse sets up the beginning of this one, meaning, Allah sent you a messenger; so remember Him (al-Ṭabarī). A saying attributed to many early commentators states, Allah remembers whosoever remembers Him, and increases whosoever is grateful to Him, and punishes whosoever disbelieves in Him (Ibn Kathīr). According to some interpretations, one remembers Allah through praising Him or through acts of obedience, and Allah then remembers one through forgiveness (Ibn Kathīr). A famous ḥadīth qudsī (a sacred ḥadīth, in which Allah is quoted but which is not part of the Qur'an) states, Allah most high says, I am as my servant deems Me to be.

**Gratitude as the Spine of Faith**

I am with him when He remembers Me. Whosoever remembers Me within himself, I shall remember him in Myself. Whosoever remembers Me in company, I shall remember him in company better than them. Should he approach Me by a hand, I shall approach him by a cubit, and should he approach Me by a cubit, I shall approach him by a fathom. Should he come to Me walking, I shall go to him running. This verse is one of the Qur'anic foundations for the spiritual practice of the methodical remembrance of Allah (dhikr Allāh), which is the central practice of Sufism, alluding to the reciprocity of remembering Allah and being remembered by Him.

For a discussion of the virtues of dhikr, or remembrance, see 13:28c, and especially 29:45c.

**The verse in its immediate setting**

The mention of thank gives the verse a definite centre. Its meaning in 2:152 is not a general meditation on religion but an explanation of what this word requires in the situation named here. The passage at 2:152 moves between belief, action, and consequence, so a sound reading must preserve all three rather than allowing one attractive phrase to swallow the rest.

A useful way to test the verse is to ask what would change if thank were taken seriously when no audience was present. The answer cannot be only a feeling, because 2:152 connects inward recognition with a discernible choice. Nor can it be only an outward display, because the choice in 2:152 loses its Qur'anic meaning when it is made for reputation rather than for Allah. The passage holds intention and action together in 2:152.

**The central term and its range**

The relation between thank and ungrateful also protects the verse from two opposite misreadings. It should not be expanded into a claim the wording of 2:152 does not make, but neither should its brevity be used to make it weightless. Short Qur'anic statements such as 2:152 often function as hinges: they close one movement, open another, and give the next command its moral setting. The careful reader of 2:152 therefore asks what has just been established, what response is now being required, and what kind of character could respond without evasion. Those questions keep 2:152 specific while allowing its guidance to reach a new circumstance.

A reader who finds the command in 2:152 difficult should distinguish difficulty from impossibility. For 2:152, difficulty may be the place where desire, habit, or social pressure is being corrected; impossibility is a separate legal and moral question. The passage does not praise needless hardship, but it does not permit comfort to redefine thank until the demand disappears.

**What the early commentators preserve**

The final emphasis of 2:152 returns to responsibility. No explanation of thank is complete if it leaves the reader with information about earlier communities but no question about present conduct. The verse 2:152 asks what can be repaired now: a promise kept, a right restored, a motive purified, or a harmful practice abandoned. That is where knowledge becomes guidance.

The choice of the word thank in 2:152 also matters. It leaves room for the full context of 2:152 instead of allowing one translation gloss to carry every shade of meaning. Classical Arabic often places a broad word inside a narrow scene; in 2:152, the surrounding clauses determine whether the emphasis falls on an act, an intention, a condition, or a consequence. Here the reader should let the sentence do that work.

**The reasoning within the wording**

The exegetical tradition approaches thank from more than one angle, but its differences are more useful when their shared question is made visible. The commentators ask what the wording of 2:152 includes, what it excludes, and how it fits with the other passages of the Qur'an. A responsible reading can preserve those alternatives in 2:152 without pretending that every possibility has equal strength.

There is also a distinction between understanding thank and using it as a label for other people. The first task belongs to learning; the second requires evidence, fairness, and awareness of one's own motives in 2:152. The verse 2:152 addresses the reader before it supplies material for judging a neighbour. Its warning becomes safer and more productive when self-examination comes first.

**The theological question beneath the sentence**

The theology and practical instruction of 2:152 therefore support one another. If Allah knows or commands ungrateful, that knowledge or command changes how a person handles time, power, money, speech, and private desire. A doctrine that never reaches those choices has not yet been read at the level demanded by 2:152.

An ordinary example makes the moral structure of 2:152 visible without replacing the revelation. Two people may perform an outwardly similar act involving thank; one may be protecting a right, while the other is seeking praise, escaping a duty, or taking advantage of confusion. The act's surface does not settle its value. Intention, circumstance, and the rights of others complete the judgment.

**The surrounding passage as a guide**

The surrounding verses keep the reader from isolating thank from the rest of life. In 2:152, revelation corrects the mind but also trains habits: how a person speaks when challenged, gives when unseen, waits when angry, and returns after error. Those habits are where this verse becomes more than information about thank. They are the place in which assent to 2:152 is either confirmed or quietly withdrawn.

The most balanced response to 2:152 is neither to make the passage vague nor to claim more than it says. Its boundaries are part of its guidance. Within them, the verse can illuminate a new situation because its underlying moral relation remains stable: thank is joined to ungrateful, and the person is responsible for how that relation is honoured.

The wording of 2:152 gives the reader a useful order of questions about thank. What does the term thank mean in ordinary Arabic, what does the surrounding sentence restrict it to, and what consequence follows when the command is obeyed or refused in 2:152? These questions prevent a commentary on 2:152 from becoming a list of slogans. They make the act of interpretation answerable to grammar, context, and the moral end of this passage at 2:152.

The commentators sometimes preserve more than one report about the setting of 2:152. Such reports should be weighed rather than treated as interchangeable decoration. An occasion can explain why a question arose, but the wording of 2:152 determines how far the guidance travels. The verse speaks first to the situation that occasioned it and then to every reader who meets the same relation between thank and ungrateful.

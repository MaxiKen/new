## Sūrah al-Baqarah 2:160

> **As for those who repent, mend their ways, and let the truth be known, they are the ones to whom I will turn ˹in forgiveness˺, for I am the Accepter of Repentance, Most Merciful.**

### Expanded Commentary

**The Exception Written In**

At this point in al-Baqarah, the wording brings repent and truth into one moral frame. The sentence should not be detached from that relationship. Its first term in 2:160 sets the subject, while the later term shows what that subject demands, permits, or exposes. The order of 2:160 gives the reader a path through the verse: begin with what Allah names, follow the consequence, and resist the temptation to turn a living address into a slogan.

On repentance, see 2:37c; 2:128c, 4:17–18c; 4:147c. Here, as in other places, repent and relent translate the same verb (with the root meaning of to turn) with different prepositions. Make amends carries the sense of putting things right. Make clear is understood to mean that they should address and clarify what they were concealing (al-Ṭabarī).

**"Unto Them I Relent" — The Divine Singular**

The verse's reference to repent widens the scene beyond the immediate reward or pain. A deed in 2:160 may appear small when measured by public attention, yet its meaning changes when it is known by Allah and carried into the final account. Warning and mercy belong together in 2:160: accountability is serious because the human being is not abandoned to meaninglessness.

A useful way to test the verse is to ask what would change if repent were taken seriously when no audience was present. The answer cannot be only a feeling, because 2:160 connects inward recognition with a discernible choice. Nor can it be only an outward display, because the choice in 2:160 loses its Qur'anic meaning when it is made for reputation rather than for Allah. The passage holds intention and action together in 2:160.

**Across the Curse to the Name**

The relation between repent and truth also protects the verse from two opposite misreadings. It should not be expanded into a claim the wording of 2:160 does not make, but neither should its brevity be used to make it weightless. Short Qur'anic statements such as 2:160 often function as hinges: they close one movement, open another, and give the next command its moral setting. The careful reader of 2:160 therefore asks what has just been established, what response is now being required, and what kind of character could respond without evasion. Those questions keep 2:160 specific while allowing its guidance to reach a new circumstance.

The most balanced response to 2:160 is neither to make the passage vague nor to claim more than it says. Its boundaries are part of its guidance. Within them, the verse can illuminate a new situation because its underlying moral relation remains stable: repent is joined to truth, and the person is responsible for how that relation is honoured.

**The horizon beyond the immediate scene**

The wording of 2:160 gives the reader a useful order of questions about repent. What does the term repent mean in ordinary Arabic, what does the surrounding sentence restrict it to, and what consequence follows when the command is obeyed or refused in 2:160? These questions prevent a commentary on 2:160 from becoming a list of slogans. They make the act of interpretation answerable to grammar, context, and the moral end of this passage at 2:160.

The commentators sometimes preserve more than one report about the setting of 2:160. Such reports should be weighed rather than treated as interchangeable decoration. An occasion can explain why a question arose, but the wording of 2:160 determines how far the guidance travels. The verse speaks first to the situation that occasioned it and then to every reader who meets the same relation between repent and truth.

**A divine name that governs the meaning**

In 2:160, the distinction between public appearance and inward truth is especially relevant. A person may use the language of repent while arranging the act around another purpose. Allah's judgment in 2:160 is not limited to the visible outline, yet the inner intention is not an excuse for ignoring the rights and forms that the revelation establishes. Sincerity completes obedience; it does not cancel it.

One can see the social wisdom of 2:160 in a small everyday case. When people share responsibility for repent, uncertainty about the duty tends to fall on the person with less power or less information. A clear moral rule changes that balance. In 2:160, it tells the stronger party what must not be taken, and gives the weaker party language with which to name the wrong.

**Mercy and warning held together**

In 2:160, the verse also sits within the Qur'an's larger refusal to separate worship from truthfulness. Prayer, remembrance, generosity, patience, and justice are different acts in 2:160, but they are trained by the same capacity to stand before Allah without constructing a false account. The statement in 2:160 strengthens that capacity by making one particular choice visible.

A reader who finds the command in 2:160 difficult should distinguish difficulty from impossibility. For 2:160, difficulty may be the place where desire, habit, or social pressure is being corrected; impossibility is a separate legal and moral question. The passage does not praise needless hardship, but it does not permit comfort to redefine repent until the demand disappears.

**The deed before its final account**

The final emphasis of 2:160 returns to responsibility. No explanation of repent is complete if it leaves the reader with information about earlier communities but no question about present conduct. The verse 2:160 asks what can be repaired now: a promise kept, a right restored, a motive purified, or a harmful practice abandoned. That is where knowledge becomes guidance.

The choice of the word repent in 2:160 also matters. It leaves room for the full context of 2:160 instead of allowing one translation gloss to carry every shade of meaning. Classical Arabic often places a broad word inside a narrow scene; in 2:160, the surrounding clauses determine whether the emphasis falls on an act, an intention, a condition, or a consequence. Here the reader should let the sentence do that work.

**Why the promise or threat is just**

The exegetical tradition approaches repent from more than one angle, but its differences are more useful when their shared question is made visible. The commentators ask what the wording of 2:160 includes, what it excludes, and how it fits with the other passages of the Qur'an. A responsible reading can preserve those alternatives in 2:160 without pretending that every possibility has equal strength.

There is also a distinction between understanding repent and using it as a label for other people. The first task belongs to learning; the second requires evidence, fairness, and awareness of one's own motives in 2:160. The verse 2:160 addresses the reader before it supplies material for judging a neighbour. Its warning becomes safer and more productive when self-examination comes first.

**The Hereafter in the argument**

The theology and practical instruction of 2:160 therefore support one another. If Allah knows or commands truth, that knowledge or command changes how a person handles time, power, money, speech, and private desire. A doctrine that never reaches those choices has not yet been read at the level demanded by 2:160.

An ordinary example makes the moral structure of 2:160 visible without replacing the revelation. Two people may perform an outwardly similar act involving repent; one may be protecting a right, while the other is seeking praise, escaping a duty, or taking advantage of confusion. The act's surface does not settle its value. Intention, circumstance, and the rights of others complete the judgment.

The surrounding verses keep the reader from isolating repent from the rest of life. In 2:160, revelation corrects the mind but also trains habits: how a person speaks when challenged, gives when unseen, waits when angry, and returns after error. Those habits are where this verse becomes more than information about repent. They are the place in which assent to 2:160 is either confirmed or quietly withdrawn.

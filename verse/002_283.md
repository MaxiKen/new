## Sūrah al-Baqarah 2:283

> **If you are on a journey and a scribe cannot be found, then a security can be taken. If you trust one another, then ˹there is no need for a security, but˺ the debtor should honour this trust ˹by repaying the debt˺—and let them fear Allah, their Lord. And do not conceal the testimony, for whoever conceals it, their hearts are indeed sinful. And Allah ˹fully˺ knows what you do.**

### Expanded Commentary

**On a Journey — A Pledge in Hand**

The force of this verse lies in the connection between journey and found. A reader can miss that force by isolating one phrase and treating it as a general maxim in 2:283. The verse itself is more exact. It speaks to a particular condition in the sūrah and directs attention to the kind of response that condition requires in 2:283. Reading the terms together keeps both the theology and the practical instruction in view at 2:283.

**Conceal Not the Testimony**

And cannot find a scribe can also mean that one does not find the means with which to write the contract down (al-Qurṭubī and al-Ṭabarī). Let there be a pledge in hand refers to collateral property that is given by the debtor to the creditor, or the seller to the buyer in a forward sale, as a guarantee in the absence of a written contract with witnesses. But the verse then leaves open the possibility that one will trust the other party without taking a pledge in the form of property. Some believe that this latter provision abrogated the rules set out in v. 282 regarding contracts, but many believe that this provision applies only in the special circumstance of the person being on a journey, and that in ordinary circumstances one should make a written contract .

**Writing trust into a transaction**

The language of journey places memory and responsibility alongside material exchange. People forget, die, disagree, and understand conversations differently; a careful record does not accuse every party of dishonesty, but protects the honest party from the consequences of uncertainty. The verse therefore treats clarity as a form of mercy, not as a sign that trust has disappeared.

The standard in 2:283 remains practical because it can be applied before a dispute reaches a court or a crisis reaches its worst point. One can notice the first small departure from journey: an excuse, a concealed obligation, an unexamined desire, or a promise treated as optional. Returning at that point is easier than defending a settled habit. The warning is therefore also an opening for repair.

**Why clarity protects both sides**

The relation between journey and found also protects the verse from two opposite misreadings. It should not be expanded into a claim the wording of 2:283 does not make, but neither should its brevity be used to make it weightless. Short Qur'anic statements such as 2:283 often function as hinges: they close one movement, open another, and give the next command its moral setting. The careful reader of 2:283 therefore asks what has just been established, what response is now being required, and what kind of character could respond without evasion. Those questions keep 2:283 specific while allowing its guidance to reach a new circumstance.

The theology and practical instruction of 2:283 therefore support one another. If Allah knows or commands found, that knowledge or command changes how a person handles time, power, money, speech, and private desire. A doctrine that never reaches those choices has not yet been read at the level demanded by 2:283.

**The vulnerable party in the wording**

An ordinary example makes the moral structure of 2:283 visible without replacing the revelation. Two people may perform an outwardly similar act involving journey; one may be protecting a right, while the other is seeking praise, escaping a duty, or taking advantage of confusion. The act's surface does not settle its value. Intention, circumstance, and the rights of others complete the judgment.

The surrounding verses keep the reader from isolating journey from the rest of life. In 2:283, revelation corrects the mind but also trains habits: how a person speaks when challenged, gives when unseen, waits when angry, and returns after error. Those habits are where this verse becomes more than information about journey. They are the place in which assent to 2:283 is either confirmed or quietly withdrawn.

**Witnesses, records, and memory**

The most balanced response to 2:283 is neither to make the passage vague nor to claim more than it says. Its boundaries are part of its guidance. Within them, the verse can illuminate a new situation because its underlying moral relation remains stable: journey is joined to found, and the person is responsible for how that relation is honoured.

The wording of 2:283 gives the reader a useful order of questions about journey. What does the term journey mean in ordinary Arabic, what does the surrounding sentence restrict it to, and what consequence follows when the command is obeyed or refused in 2:283? These questions prevent a commentary on 2:283 from becoming a list of slogans. They make the act of interpretation answerable to grammar, context, and the moral end of this passage at 2:283.

**The ethics of consent**

The commentators sometimes preserve more than one report about the setting of 2:283. Such reports should be weighed rather than treated as interchangeable decoration. An occasion can explain why a question arose, but the wording of 2:283 determines how far the guidance travels. The verse speaks first to the situation that occasioned it and then to every reader who meets the same relation between journey and found.

In 2:283, the distinction between public appearance and inward truth is especially relevant. A person may use the language of journey while arranging the act around another purpose. Allah's judgment in 2:283 is not limited to the visible outline, yet the inner intention is not an excuse for ignoring the rights and forms that the revelation establishes. Sincerity completes obedience; it does not cancel it.

**A practical rule against later conflict**

One can see the social wisdom of 2:283 in a small everyday case. When people share responsibility for journey, uncertainty about the duty tends to fall on the person with less power or less information. A clear moral rule changes that balance. In 2:283, it tells the stronger party what must not be taken, and gives the weaker party language with which to name the wrong.

In 2:283, the verse also sits within the Qur'an's larger refusal to separate worship from truthfulness. Prayer, remembrance, generosity, patience, and justice are different acts in 2:283, but they are trained by the same capacity to stand before Allah without constructing a false account. The statement in 2:283 strengthens that capacity by making one particular choice visible.

**The spiritual meaning of an honest ledger**

A reader who finds the command in 2:283 difficult should distinguish difficulty from impossibility. For 2:283, difficulty may be the place where desire, habit, or social pressure is being corrected; impossibility is a separate legal and moral question. The passage does not praise needless hardship, but it does not permit comfort to redefine journey until the demand disappears.

The final emphasis of 2:283 returns to responsibility. No explanation of journey is complete if it leaves the reader with information about earlier communities but no question about present conduct. The verse 2:283 asks what can be repaired now: a promise kept, a right restored, a motive purified, or a harmful practice abandoned. That is where knowledge becomes guidance.

The choice of the word journey in 2:283 also matters. It leaves room for the full context of 2:283 instead of allowing one translation gloss to carry every shade of meaning. Classical Arabic often places a broad word inside a narrow scene; in 2:283, the surrounding clauses determine whether the emphasis falls on an act, an intention, a condition, or a consequence. Here the reader should let the sentence do that work.

The exegetical tradition approaches journey from more than one angle, but its differences are more useful when their shared question is made visible. The commentators ask what the wording of 2:283 includes, what it excludes, and how it fits with the other passages of the Qur'an. A responsible reading can preserve those alternatives in 2:283 without pretending that every possibility has equal strength.

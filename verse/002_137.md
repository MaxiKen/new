## Sūrah al-Baqarah 2:137

> **So if they believe in what you believe, then they will indeed be ˹rightly˺ guided. But if they turn away, they are simply opposed ˹to the truth˺. But Allah will spare you their evil. For He is the All-Hearing, All-Knowing.**

### Expanded Commentary

**The Like of Your Faith**

The force of this verse lies in the connection between indeed and turn. A reader can miss that force by isolating one phrase and treating it as a general maxim in 2:137. The verse itself is more exact. It speaks to a particular condition in the sūrah and directs attention to the kind of response that condition requires in 2:137. Reading the terms together keeps both the theology and the practical instruction in view at 2:137.

The belief in the like of what you believe in is thought not to mean that the object of belief is literally similar, since in Islamic theology nothing can be like Allah, but that the People of the Book should affirm what the Prophets followers affirm and believe (al-Ṭabarī). The verse could also refer to a belief in an undistorted Torah and Gospel (see 2:75c), which would be the like of what Muslims believe in (al-Rāzī).

**And If They Turn Away: Schism Named, Sufficiency Promised**

Schism renders shiqāq (also in 2:176; 22:53; 38:2; 41:52), which carries the sense of fracture or separation and connotes an attitude of opposition and hostility (al-Rāzī). As in English, suffice (kafā) can mean to be enough as well as to meet the needs of or to serve the purpose of something. The Qur'an speaks of Allahs sufficing in many different ways: as Reckoner (4:6), Protector (4:45), Knower (4:70), Witness (4:79), Guardian (4:81), and in battle (33:25). Ones own soul can also suffice as a reckoner (17:14), and in a negative sense Hell can suffice as a blazing flame (4:55). Ḥasb is also rendered suffice, as in ḥasbunā Allāh, or Allah suffices us (3:173); also see 8:62; 8:64; 9:59; 9:129.

**The Names Sealed on the Suzerainty of Help**

The verse describes indeed not as a private opinion but as a direction that changes perception and conduct. In 2:137, guidance is recognized by what it makes a person do with knowledge, power, and desire. For 2:137, inward assent is linked to an observable response: the heart's claim is tested by the path it chooses when the claim becomes costly.

The standard in 2:137 remains practical because it can be applied before a dispute reaches a court or a crisis reaches its worst point. One can notice the first small departure from indeed: an excuse, a concealed obligation, an unexamined desire, or a promise treated as optional. Returning at that point is easier than defending a settled habit. The warning is therefore also an opening for repair.

**The first movement of a guided life**

The relation between indeed and turn also protects the verse from two opposite misreadings. It should not be expanded into a claim the wording of 2:137 does not make, but neither should its brevity be used to make it weightless. Short Qur'anic statements such as 2:137 often function as hinges: they close one movement, open another, and give the next command its moral setting. The careful reader of 2:137 therefore asks what has just been established, what response is now being required, and what kind of character could respond without evasion. Those questions keep 2:137 specific while allowing its guidance to reach a new circumstance.

In 2:137, the verse also sits within the Qur'an's larger refusal to separate worship from truthfulness. Prayer, remembrance, generosity, patience, and justice are different acts in 2:137, but they are trained by the same capacity to stand before Allah without constructing a false account. The statement in 2:137 strengthens that capacity by making one particular choice visible.

**Belief made visible in conduct**

A reader who finds the command in 2:137 difficult should distinguish difficulty from impossibility. For 2:137, difficulty may be the place where desire, habit, or social pressure is being corrected; impossibility is a separate legal and moral question. The passage does not praise needless hardship, but it does not permit comfort to redefine indeed until the demand disappears.

The final emphasis of 2:137 returns to responsibility. No explanation of indeed is complete if it leaves the reader with information about earlier communities but no question about present conduct. The verse 2:137 asks what can be repaired now: a promise kept, a right restored, a motive purified, or a harmful practice abandoned. That is where knowledge becomes guidance.

**The choice hidden inside the command**

The choice of the word indeed in 2:137 also matters. It leaves room for the full context of 2:137 instead of allowing one translation gloss to carry every shade of meaning. Classical Arabic often places a broad word inside a narrow scene; in 2:137, the surrounding clauses determine whether the emphasis falls on an act, an intention, a condition, or a consequence. Here the reader should let the sentence do that work.

The exegetical tradition approaches indeed from more than one angle, but its differences are more useful when their shared question is made visible. The commentators ask what the wording of 2:137 includes, what it excludes, and how it fits with the other passages of the Qur'an. A responsible reading can preserve those alternatives in 2:137 without pretending that every possibility has equal strength.

**How guidance becomes discernment**

There is also a distinction between understanding indeed and using it as a label for other people. The first task belongs to learning; the second requires evidence, fairness, and awareness of one's own motives in 2:137. The verse 2:137 addresses the reader before it supplies material for judging a neighbour. Its warning becomes safer and more productive when self-examination comes first.

The theology and practical instruction of 2:137 therefore support one another. If Allah knows or commands turn, that knowledge or command changes how a person handles time, power, money, speech, and private desire. A doctrine that never reaches those choices has not yet been read at the level demanded by 2:137.

**The contrast drawn by the verse**

An ordinary example makes the moral structure of 2:137 visible without replacing the revelation. Two people may perform an outwardly similar act involving indeed; one may be protecting a right, while the other is seeking praise, escaping a duty, or taking advantage of confusion. The act's surface does not settle its value. Intention, circumstance, and the rights of others complete the judgment.

The surrounding verses keep the reader from isolating indeed from the rest of life. In 2:137, revelation corrects the mind but also trains habits: how a person speaks when challenged, gives when unseen, waits when angry, and returns after error. Those habits are where this verse becomes more than information about indeed. They are the place in which assent to 2:137 is either confirmed or quietly withdrawn.

**Certainty, doubt, and moral action**

The most balanced response to 2:137 is neither to make the passage vague nor to claim more than it says. Its boundaries are part of its guidance. Within them, the verse can illuminate a new situation because its underlying moral relation remains stable: indeed is joined to turn, and the person is responsible for how that relation is honoured.

The wording of 2:137 gives the reader a useful order of questions about indeed. What does the term indeed mean in ordinary Arabic, what does the surrounding sentence restrict it to, and what consequence follows when the command is obeyed or refused in 2:137? These questions prevent a commentary on 2:137 from becoming a list of slogans. They make the act of interpretation answerable to grammar, context, and the moral end of this passage at 2:137.

The commentators sometimes preserve more than one report about the setting of 2:137. Such reports should be weighed rather than treated as interchangeable decoration. An occasion can explain why a question arose, but the wording of 2:137 determines how far the guidance travels. The verse speaks first to the situation that occasioned it and then to every reader who meets the same relation between indeed and turn.

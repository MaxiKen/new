## Sūrah al-Baqarah 2:91

> **When it is said to them: “Believe in what Allah has revealed,” they reply, “We only believe in what was sent down to us,” and they deny what came afterwards, though it is the truth confirming their own Scriptures! Ask ˹them, O Prophet˺, “Why then did you kill Allah’s prophets before, if you are ˹truly˺ believers?”**

### Expanded Commentary

**The Slogan of Partitive Belief**

The force of this verse lies in the connection between revealed and down. A reader can miss that force by isolating one phrase and treating it as a general maxim in 2:91. The verse itself is more exact. It speaks to a particular condition in the sūrah and directs attention to the kind of response that condition requires in 2:91. Reading the terms together keeps both the theology and the practical instruction in view at 2:91.

**The Devastating Counter-Question**

Beyond it is interpreted as after or other than (al-Ṭabarī). The question is a rebuke in light of the Jews claim that We believe in what was sent down to us, meaning the Torah, because those slain prophets were Israelites who only commanded their people to follow the Torah (Ibn Kathīr). That they slew even them shows that their refusal to follow the Prophet stemmed from something other than faith in the Torah, since those prophets also confirmed the truth of what was with them, and their murder was forbidden in any case . See also 4:136c; 4:150–51c. The following verses continue the theme of faithlessness and rejection (kufr) in the face of the revelation of the Truth.

**Selective Belief and the Believer's Own Slogan**

The mention of revealed must be read with the restraints carried by the same revelation. Permission to resist wrongdoing does not erase the prohibition of wrongdoing. The verse therefore asks the community to hold two truths together: injustice may have to be confronted, and the means used in confronting it remain subject to Allah's judgment.

The standard in 2:91 remains practical because it can be applied before a dispute reaches a court or a crisis reaches its worst point. One can notice the first small departure from revealed: an excuse, a concealed obligation, an unexamined desire, or a promise treated as optional. Returning at that point is easier than defending a settled habit. The warning is therefore also an opening for repair.

**The permission is not a blank cheque**

The relation between revealed and down also protects the verse from two opposite misreadings. It should not be expanded into a claim the wording of 2:91 does not make, but neither should its brevity be used to make it weightless. Short Qur'anic statements such as 2:91 often function as hinges: they close one movement, open another, and give the next command its moral setting. The careful reader of 2:91 therefore asks what has just been established, what response is now being required, and what kind of character could respond without evasion. Those questions keep 2:91 specific while allowing its guidance to reach a new circumstance.

**The difference between defence and aggression**

The wording of 2:91 gives the reader a useful order of questions about revealed. What does the term revealed mean in ordinary Arabic, what does the surrounding sentence restrict it to, and what consequence follows when the command is obeyed or refused in 2:91? These questions prevent a commentary on 2:91 from becoming a list of slogans. They make the act of interpretation answerable to grammar, context, and the moral end of this passage at 2:91.

The commentators sometimes preserve more than one report about the setting of 2:91. Such reports should be weighed rather than treated as interchangeable decoration. An occasion can explain why a question arose, but the wording of 2:91 determines how far the guidance travels. The verse speaks first to the situation that occasioned it and then to every reader who meets the same relation between revealed and down.

**The human cost carried by the wording**

In 2:91, the distinction between public appearance and inward truth is especially relevant. A person may use the language of revealed while arranging the act around another purpose. Allah's judgment in 2:91 is not limited to the visible outline, yet the inner intention is not an excuse for ignoring the rights and forms that the revelation establishes. Sincerity completes obedience; it does not cancel it.

One can see the social wisdom of 2:91 in a small everyday case. When people share responsibility for revealed, uncertainty about the duty tends to fall on the person with less power or less information. A clear moral rule changes that balance. In 2:91, it tells the stronger party what must not be taken, and gives the weaker party language with which to name the wrong.

**Limits that remain binding in conflict**

In 2:91, the verse also sits within the Qur'an's larger refusal to separate worship from truthfulness. Prayer, remembrance, generosity, patience, and justice are different acts in 2:91, but they are trained by the same capacity to stand before Allah without constructing a false account. The statement in 2:91 strengthens that capacity by making one particular choice visible.

A reader who finds the command in 2:91 difficult should distinguish difficulty from impossibility. For 2:91, difficulty may be the place where desire, habit, or social pressure is being corrected; impossibility is a separate legal and moral question. The passage does not praise needless hardship, but it does not permit comfort to redefine revealed until the demand disappears.

**How the early community understood it**

The final emphasis of 2:91 returns to responsibility. No explanation of revealed is complete if it leaves the reader with information about earlier communities but no question about present conduct. The verse 2:91 asks what can be repaired now: a promise kept, a right restored, a motive purified, or a harmful practice abandoned. That is where knowledge becomes guidance.

The choice of the word revealed in 2:91 also matters. It leaves room for the full context of 2:91 instead of allowing one translation gloss to carry every shade of meaning. Classical Arabic often places a broad word inside a narrow scene; in 2:91, the surrounding clauses determine whether the emphasis falls on an act, an intention, a condition, or a consequence. Here the reader should let the sentence do that work.

**Power restrained by accountability**

The exegetical tradition approaches revealed from more than one angle, but its differences are more useful when their shared question is made visible. The commentators ask what the wording of 2:91 includes, what it excludes, and how it fits with the other passages of the Qur'an. A responsible reading can preserve those alternatives in 2:91 without pretending that every possibility has equal strength.

There is also a distinction between understanding revealed and using it as a label for other people. The first task belongs to learning; the second requires evidence, fairness, and awareness of one's own motives in 2:91. The verse 2:91 addresses the reader before it supplies material for judging a neighbour. Its warning becomes safer and more productive when self-examination comes first.

The theology and practical instruction of 2:91 therefore support one another. If Allah knows or commands down, that knowledge or command changes how a person handles time, power, money, speech, and private desire. A doctrine that never reaches those choices has not yet been read at the level demanded by 2:91.

An ordinary example makes the moral structure of 2:91 visible without replacing the revelation. Two people may perform an outwardly similar act involving revealed; one may be protecting a right, while the other is seeking praise, escaping a duty, or taking advantage of confusion. The act's surface does not settle its value. Intention, circumstance, and the rights of others complete the judgment.

The surrounding verses keep the reader from isolating revealed from the rest of life. In 2:91, revelation corrects the mind but also trains habits: how a person speaks when challenged, gives when unseen, waits when angry, and returns after error. Those habits are where this verse becomes more than information about revealed. They are the place in which assent to 2:91 is either confirmed or quietly withdrawn.

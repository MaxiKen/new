## Sūrah al-Baqarah 2:104

> **O believers! Do not say, “Râ’ina.” [Herd us!] But say, “Unẓurna,” [Tend to us!] and listen ˹attentively˺. And the disbelievers will suffer a painful punishment.**

### Expanded Commentary

**The Turn to "O You Who Believe"**

At this point in al-Baqarah, the wording brings belief and unẓurna into one moral frame. The sentence should not be detached from that relationship. Its first term in 2:104 sets the subject, while the later term shows what that subject demands, permits, or exposes. The order of 2:104 gives the reader a path through the verse: begin with what Allah names, follow the consequence, and resist the temptation to turn a living address into a slogan.

**The Sunnah of Vigilant Speech**

Attend to us renders rāʿinā (also see 4:46c). The term comes from an idiomatic usage of rāʿinā samʿak or arʿinā samʿak (meaning something like lend me your ear), which was considered demeaning and mocking among people of the region of Makkah and Madinah (al-Ṭabarī). Al-Ṭabarī considers this together with other commands from the Prophet to call things and people by more noble-sounding names, but he dismisses the idea that it could have been a Hebrew wordplay, since it is the believers themselves who were commanded to stop saying it, and they would not have uncritically taken up calling the Prophet with a phrase they did not understand.

**"And Listen": the Closing Clause That Transposes the Whole**

It can also be understood in connection with 24:63, Do not deem the Messengers calling among you to be like your calling to one another, which admonishes against addressing the Prophet with too much familiarity (al-Rāzī). Regard us can also have the meaning wait for us, as in 57:13, but here has the sense only of desiring the Prophets attention in a respectful way, followed by listen, which here is understood as also meaning obey .

The verse's reference to belief widens the scene beyond the immediate reward or pain. A deed in 2:104 may appear small when measured by public attention, yet its meaning changes when it is known by Allah and carried into the final account. Warning and mercy belong together in 2:104: accountability is serious because the human being is not abandoned to meaninglessness.

**The horizon beyond the immediate scene**

A useful way to test the verse is to ask what would change if belief were taken seriously when no audience was present. The answer cannot be only a feeling, because 2:104 connects inward recognition with a discernible choice. Nor can it be only an outward display, because the choice in 2:104 loses its Qur'anic meaning when it is made for reputation rather than for Allah. The passage holds intention and action together in 2:104.

**A divine name that governs the meaning**

The relation between belief and unẓurna also protects the verse from two opposite misreadings. It should not be expanded into a claim the wording of 2:104 does not make, but neither should its brevity be used to make it weightless. Short Qur'anic statements such as 2:104 often function as hinges: they close one movement, open another, and give the next command its moral setting. The careful reader of 2:104 therefore asks what has just been established, what response is now being required, and what kind of character could respond without evasion. Those questions keep 2:104 specific while allowing its guidance to reach a new circumstance.

The most balanced response to 2:104 is neither to make the passage vague nor to claim more than it says. Its boundaries are part of its guidance. Within them, the verse can illuminate a new situation because its underlying moral relation remains stable: belief is joined to unẓurna, and the person is responsible for how that relation is honoured.

**Mercy and warning held together**

The wording of 2:104 gives the reader a useful order of questions about belief. What does the term belief mean in ordinary Arabic, what does the surrounding sentence restrict it to, and what consequence follows when the command is obeyed or refused in 2:104? These questions prevent a commentary on 2:104 from becoming a list of slogans. They make the act of interpretation answerable to grammar, context, and the moral end of this passage at 2:104.

The commentators sometimes preserve more than one report about the setting of 2:104. Such reports should be weighed rather than treated as interchangeable decoration. An occasion can explain why a question arose, but the wording of 2:104 determines how far the guidance travels. The verse speaks first to the situation that occasioned it and then to every reader who meets the same relation between belief and unẓurna.

**The deed before its final account**

In 2:104, the distinction between public appearance and inward truth is especially relevant. A person may use the language of belief while arranging the act around another purpose. Allah's judgment in 2:104 is not limited to the visible outline, yet the inner intention is not an excuse for ignoring the rights and forms that the revelation establishes. Sincerity completes obedience; it does not cancel it.

One can see the social wisdom of 2:104 in a small everyday case. When people share responsibility for belief, uncertainty about the duty tends to fall on the person with less power or less information. A clear moral rule changes that balance. In 2:104, it tells the stronger party what must not be taken, and gives the weaker party language with which to name the wrong.

**Why the promise or threat is just**

In 2:104, the verse also sits within the Qur'an's larger refusal to separate worship from truthfulness. Prayer, remembrance, generosity, patience, and justice are different acts in 2:104, but they are trained by the same capacity to stand before Allah without constructing a false account. The statement in 2:104 strengthens that capacity by making one particular choice visible.

A reader who finds the command in 2:104 difficult should distinguish difficulty from impossibility. For 2:104, difficulty may be the place where desire, habit, or social pressure is being corrected; impossibility is a separate legal and moral question. The passage does not praise needless hardship, but it does not permit comfort to redefine belief until the demand disappears.

**The Hereafter in the argument**

The final emphasis of 2:104 returns to responsibility. No explanation of belief is complete if it leaves the reader with information about earlier communities but no question about present conduct. The verse 2:104 asks what can be repaired now: a promise kept, a right restored, a motive purified, or a harmful practice abandoned. That is where knowledge becomes guidance.

The choice of the word belief in 2:104 also matters. It leaves room for the full context of 2:104 instead of allowing one translation gloss to carry every shade of meaning. Classical Arabic often places a broad word inside a narrow scene; in 2:104, the surrounding clauses determine whether the emphasis falls on an act, an intention, a condition, or a consequence. Here the reader should let the sentence do that work.

The exegetical tradition approaches belief from more than one angle, but its differences are more useful when their shared question is made visible. The commentators ask what the wording of 2:104 includes, what it excludes, and how it fits with the other passages of the Qur'an. A responsible reading can preserve those alternatives in 2:104 without pretending that every possibility has equal strength.

There is also a distinction between understanding belief and using it as a label for other people. The first task belongs to learning; the second requires evidence, fairness, and awareness of one's own motives in 2:104. The verse 2:104 addresses the reader before it supplies material for judging a neighbour. Its warning becomes safer and more productive when self-examination comes first.

The theology and practical instruction of 2:104 therefore support one another. If Allah knows or commands unẓurna, that knowledge or command changes how a person handles time, power, money, speech, and private desire. A doctrine that never reaches those choices has not yet been read at the level demanded by 2:104.

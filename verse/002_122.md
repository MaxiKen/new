## Sūrah al-Baqarah 2:122

> **O Children of Israel! Remember My favours upon you and how I honoured you above the others.**

### Expanded Commentary

**The Refrain Rings Again**

At this point in al-Baqarah, the wording brings children and upon into one moral frame. The sentence should not be detached from that relationship. Its first term in 2:122 sets the subject, while the later term shows what that subject demands, permits, or exposes. The order of 2:122 gives the reader a path through the verse: begin with what Allah names, follow the consequence, and resist the temptation to turn a living address into a slogan.

The concern with children shows that justice is measured most severely where affection, dependence, and power overlap. A person may be generous in public and still wrong a relative or a child in private. By naming the relationship, the verse makes invisible duties visible and gives moral language to the person whose claim might otherwise be dismissed.

**Memory as Doctrine Once More**

A useful way to test the verse is to ask what would change if children were taken seriously when no audience was present. The answer cannot be only a feeling, because 2:122 connects inward recognition with a discernible choice. Nor can it be only an outward display, because the choice in 2:122 loses its Qur'anic meaning when it is made for reputation rather than for Allah. The passage holds intention and action together in 2:122.

**The Pedagogy of the Recurring Sentence**

The relation between children and upon also protects the verse from two opposite misreadings. It should not be expanded into a claim the wording of 2:122 does not make, but neither should its brevity be used to make it weightless. Short Qur'anic statements such as 2:122 often function as hinges: they close one movement, open another, and give the next command its moral setting. The careful reader of 2:122 therefore asks what has just been established, what response is now being required, and what kind of character could respond without evasion. Those questions keep 2:122 specific while allowing its guidance to reach a new circumstance.

**A duty that begins with the vulnerable**

One can see the social wisdom of 2:122 in a small everyday case. When people share responsibility for children, uncertainty about the duty tends to fall on the person with less power or less information. A clear moral rule changes that balance. In 2:122, it tells the stronger party what must not be taken, and gives the weaker party language with which to name the wrong.

In 2:122, the verse also sits within the Qur'an's larger refusal to separate worship from truthfulness. Prayer, remembrance, generosity, patience, and justice are different acts in 2:122, but they are trained by the same capacity to stand before Allah without constructing a false account. The statement in 2:122 strengthens that capacity by making one particular choice visible.

**The relationship named by the verse**

A reader who finds the command in 2:122 difficult should distinguish difficulty from impossibility. For 2:122, difficulty may be the place where desire, habit, or social pressure is being corrected; impossibility is a separate legal and moral question. The passage does not praise needless hardship, but it does not permit comfort to redefine children until the demand disappears.

The final emphasis of 2:122 returns to responsibility. No explanation of children is complete if it leaves the reader with information about earlier communities but no question about present conduct. The verse 2:122 asks what can be repaired now: a promise kept, a right restored, a motive purified, or a harmful practice abandoned. That is where knowledge becomes guidance.

**Rights before sentiment**

The choice of the word children in 2:122 also matters. It leaves room for the full context of 2:122 instead of allowing one translation gloss to carry every shade of meaning. Classical Arabic often places a broad word inside a narrow scene; in 2:122, the surrounding clauses determine whether the emphasis falls on an act, an intention, a condition, or a consequence. Here the reader should let the sentence do that work.

The exegetical tradition approaches children from more than one angle, but its differences are more useful when their shared question is made visible. The commentators ask what the wording of 2:122 includes, what it excludes, and how it fits with the other passages of the Qur'an. A responsible reading can preserve those alternatives in 2:122 without pretending that every possibility has equal strength.

**How neglect becomes wrongdoing**

There is also a distinction between understanding children and using it as a label for other people. The first task belongs to learning; the second requires evidence, fairness, and awareness of one's own motives in 2:122. The verse 2:122 addresses the reader before it supplies material for judging a neighbour. Its warning becomes safer and more productive when self-examination comes first.

The theology and practical instruction of 2:122 therefore support one another. If Allah knows or commands upon, that knowledge or command changes how a person handles time, power, money, speech, and private desire. A doctrine that never reaches those choices has not yet been read at the level demanded by 2:122.

**The legal protection in the command**

An ordinary example makes the moral structure of 2:122 visible without replacing the revelation. Two people may perform an outwardly similar act involving children; one may be protecting a right, while the other is seeking praise, escaping a duty, or taking advantage of confusion. The act's surface does not settle its value. Intention, circumstance, and the rights of others complete the judgment.

The surrounding verses keep the reader from isolating children from the rest of life. In 2:122, revelation corrects the mind but also trains habits: how a person speaks when challenged, gives when unseen, waits when angry, and returns after error. Those habits are where this verse becomes more than information about children. They are the place in which assent to 2:122 is either confirmed or quietly withdrawn.

**A family obligation with social reach**

The most balanced response to 2:122 is neither to make the passage vague nor to claim more than it says. Its boundaries are part of its guidance. Within them, the verse can illuminate a new situation because its underlying moral relation remains stable: children is joined to upon, and the person is responsible for how that relation is honoured.

The wording of 2:122 gives the reader a useful order of questions about children. What does the term children mean in ordinary Arabic, what does the surrounding sentence restrict it to, and what consequence follows when the command is obeyed or refused in 2:122? These questions prevent a commentary on 2:122 from becoming a list of slogans. They make the act of interpretation answerable to grammar, context, and the moral end of this passage at 2:122.

The commentators sometimes preserve more than one report about the setting of 2:122. Such reports should be weighed rather than treated as interchangeable decoration. An occasion can explain why a question arose, but the wording of 2:122 determines how far the guidance travels. The verse speaks first to the situation that occasioned it and then to every reader who meets the same relation between children and upon.

In 2:122, the distinction between public appearance and inward truth is especially relevant. A person may use the language of children while arranging the act around another purpose. Allah's judgment in 2:122 is not limited to the visible outline, yet the inner intention is not an excuse for ignoring the rights and forms that the revelation establishes. Sincerity completes obedience; it does not cancel it.

## Sūrah al-Baqarah 2:198

> **There is no blame on you for seeking the bounty of your Lord ˹during this journey˺. When you return from ’Arafât, praise Allah near the sacred place and praise Him for having guided you, for surely before this ˹guidance˺ you were astray.**

### Expanded Commentary

**The Commerce-Clause of the Pilgrimage**

At this point in al-Baqarah, the wording brings blame and during into one moral frame. The sentence should not be detached from that relationship. Its first term in 2:198 sets the subject, while the later term shows what that subject demands, permits, or exposes. The order of 2:198 gives the reader a path through the verse: begin with what Allah names, follow the consequence, and resist the temptation to turn a living address into a slogan.

**"When You Pour Out From Arafat"**

This verse is understood to say that commerce is permissible during the ḥajj and that engaging in it does not nullify the ḥajj, though to refrain and devote oneself totally to the spiritual aspect of things is better (al-Qurṭubī). Some say the sacred ground is the hill named Quzaḥ, where the leader of the prayer stands to lead the joint maghrib and ʿishāʾ prayers after the pilgrims come from ʿArafah. Others say the verse seems to indicate that it is simply Muzdalifah itself, since it is a place of prayer and remembrance and is where one goes after the stay at ʿArafah (al-Rāzī). One remembers Allah there through various formulas such as al-ḥamdu li’Llāh (“Praise be to Allah”), Allāhu akbar (“Allah is Great”), and subḥān Allāh (“Glory be to Allah; R).

**"As He Guided You"**

The treatment of blame joins movement through a place or ritual with movement of the heart. Sacred practice has boundaries precisely because it is not ordinary travel or ordinary habit. The limits teach the pilgrim to receive meaning rather than manufacture it, while the concessions show that obedience is addressed to human beings with real capacities and pressures.

A useful way to test the verse is to ask what would change if blame were taken seriously when no audience was present. The answer cannot be only a feeling, because 2:198 connects inward recognition with a discernible choice. Nor can it be only an outward display, because the choice in 2:198 loses its Qur'anic meaning when it is made for reputation rather than for Allah. The passage holds intention and action together in 2:198.

**A journey ordered toward Allah**

The relation between blame and during also protects the verse from two opposite misreadings. It should not be expanded into a claim the wording of 2:198 does not make, but neither should its brevity be used to make it weightless. Short Qur'anic statements such as 2:198 often function as hinges: they close one movement, open another, and give the next command its moral setting. The careful reader of 2:198 therefore asks what has just been established, what response is now being required, and what kind of character could respond without evasion. Those questions keep 2:198 specific while allowing its guidance to reach a new circumstance.

There is also a distinction between understanding blame and using it as a label for other people. The first task belongs to learning; the second requires evidence, fairness, and awareness of one's own motives in 2:198. The verse 2:198 addresses the reader before it supplies material for judging a neighbour. Its warning becomes safer and more productive when self-examination comes first.

**The sacred limit in the command**

The theology and practical instruction of 2:198 therefore support one another. If Allah knows or commands during, that knowledge or command changes how a person handles time, power, money, speech, and private desire. A doctrine that never reaches those choices has not yet been read at the level demanded by 2:198.

An ordinary example makes the moral structure of 2:198 visible without replacing the revelation. Two people may perform an outwardly similar act involving blame; one may be protecting a right, while the other is seeking praise, escaping a duty, or taking advantage of confusion. The act's surface does not settle its value. Intention, circumstance, and the rights of others complete the judgment.

**Ritual detail and spiritual purpose**

The surrounding verses keep the reader from isolating blame from the rest of life. In 2:198, revelation corrects the mind but also trains habits: how a person speaks when challenged, gives when unseen, waits when angry, and returns after error. Those habits are where this verse becomes more than information about blame. They are the place in which assent to 2:198 is either confirmed or quietly withdrawn.

The most balanced response to 2:198 is neither to make the passage vague nor to claim more than it says. Its boundaries are part of its guidance. Within them, the verse can illuminate a new situation because its underlying moral relation remains stable: blame is joined to during, and the person is responsible for how that relation is honoured.

**What pilgrims must leave behind**

The wording of 2:198 gives the reader a useful order of questions about blame. What does the term blame mean in ordinary Arabic, what does the surrounding sentence restrict it to, and what consequence follows when the command is obeyed or refused in 2:198? These questions prevent a commentary on 2:198 from becoming a list of slogans. They make the act of interpretation answerable to grammar, context, and the moral end of this passage at 2:198.

The commentators sometimes preserve more than one report about the setting of 2:198. Such reports should be weighed rather than treated as interchangeable decoration. An occasion can explain why a question arose, but the wording of 2:198 determines how far the guidance travels. The verse speaks first to the situation that occasioned it and then to every reader who meets the same relation between blame and during.

**The community gathered by one sign**

In 2:198, the distinction between public appearance and inward truth is especially relevant. A person may use the language of blame while arranging the act around another purpose. Allah's judgment in 2:198 is not limited to the visible outline, yet the inner intention is not an excuse for ignoring the rights and forms that the revelation establishes. Sincerity completes obedience; it does not cancel it.

One can see the social wisdom of 2:198 in a small everyday case. When people share responsibility for blame, uncertainty about the duty tends to fall on the person with less power or less information. A clear moral rule changes that balance. In 2:198, it tells the stronger party what must not be taken, and gives the weaker party language with which to name the wrong.

**The jurists’ concern for capacity**

In 2:198, the verse also sits within the Qur'an's larger refusal to separate worship from truthfulness. Prayer, remembrance, generosity, patience, and justice are different acts in 2:198, but they are trained by the same capacity to stand before Allah without constructing a false account. The statement in 2:198 strengthens that capacity by making one particular choice visible.

A reader who finds the command in 2:198 difficult should distinguish difficulty from impossibility. For 2:198, difficulty may be the place where desire, habit, or social pressure is being corrected; impossibility is a separate legal and moral question. The passage does not praise needless hardship, but it does not permit comfort to redefine blame until the demand disappears.

The final emphasis of 2:198 returns to responsibility. No explanation of blame is complete if it leaves the reader with information about earlier communities but no question about present conduct. The verse 2:198 asks what can be repaired now: a promise kept, a right restored, a motive purified, or a harmful practice abandoned. That is where knowledge becomes guidance.

The choice of the word blame in 2:198 also matters. It leaves room for the full context of 2:198 instead of allowing one translation gloss to carry every shade of meaning. Classical Arabic often places a broad word inside a narrow scene; in 2:198, the surrounding clauses determine whether the emphasis falls on an act, an intention, a condition, or a consequence. Here the reader should let the sentence do that work.

## Sūrah al-Baqarah 2:131

> **When his Lord ordered him, “Submit ˹to My Will˺,” he responded, “I submit to the Lord of all worlds.”**

### Expanded Commentary

**One Word of Command, One Word of Answer**

The force of this verse lies in the connection between ordered and worlds. A reader can miss that force by isolating one phrase and treating it as a general maxim in 2:131. The verse itself is more exact. It speaks to a particular condition in the sūrah and directs attention to the kind of response that condition requires in 2:131. Reading the terms together keeps both the theology and the practical instruction in view at 2:131.

The message of submission (islām) from v. 128 continues through to Jacob and his children. This submission to Allah, while being the proper name of the religion of Muhammad (al-islām), is generalized in the Qur'an to imply the primordial religion and true religion as such and also to refer to a fundamental attitude and orientation of the soul toward Allah that is universal and part of the bequest that prophets make to their progeny. Regarding submission, also see 3:19c; 3:85c; 5:3c; 6:125c as well as the essay The Qur'anic View of Sacred History and Other Religions.

**Islam Defined on Abraham's Lips**

The verse's reference to ordered widens the scene beyond the immediate reward or pain. A deed in 2:131 may appear small when measured by public attention, yet its meaning changes when it is known by Allah and carried into the final account. Warning and mercy belong together in 2:131: accountability is serious because the human being is not abandoned to meaninglessness.

The standard in 2:131 remains practical because it can be applied before a dispute reaches a court or a crisis reaches its worst point. One can notice the first small departure from ordered: an excuse, a concealed obligation, an unexamined desire, or a promise treated as optional. Returning at that point is easier than defending a settled habit. The warning is therefore also an opening for repair.

**Summons and Response in the Believer's Day**

The relation between ordered and worlds also protects the verse from two opposite misreadings. It should not be expanded into a claim the wording of 2:131 does not make, but neither should its brevity be used to make it weightless. Short Qur'anic statements such as 2:131 often function as hinges: they close one movement, open another, and give the next command its moral setting. The careful reader of 2:131 therefore asks what has just been established, what response is now being required, and what kind of character could respond without evasion. Those questions keep 2:131 specific while allowing its guidance to reach a new circumstance.

The surrounding verses keep the reader from isolating ordered from the rest of life. In 2:131, revelation corrects the mind but also trains habits: how a person speaks when challenged, gives when unseen, waits when angry, and returns after error. Those habits are where this verse becomes more than information about ordered. They are the place in which assent to 2:131 is either confirmed or quietly withdrawn.

**The horizon beyond the immediate scene**

The most balanced response to 2:131 is neither to make the passage vague nor to claim more than it says. Its boundaries are part of its guidance. Within them, the verse can illuminate a new situation because its underlying moral relation remains stable: ordered is joined to worlds, and the person is responsible for how that relation is honoured.

The wording of 2:131 gives the reader a useful order of questions about ordered. What does the term ordered mean in ordinary Arabic, what does the surrounding sentence restrict it to, and what consequence follows when the command is obeyed or refused in 2:131? These questions prevent a commentary on 2:131 from becoming a list of slogans. They make the act of interpretation answerable to grammar, context, and the moral end of this passage at 2:131.

**A divine name that governs the meaning**

The commentators sometimes preserve more than one report about the setting of 2:131. Such reports should be weighed rather than treated as interchangeable decoration. An occasion can explain why a question arose, but the wording of 2:131 determines how far the guidance travels. The verse speaks first to the situation that occasioned it and then to every reader who meets the same relation between ordered and worlds.

In 2:131, the distinction between public appearance and inward truth is especially relevant. A person may use the language of ordered while arranging the act around another purpose. Allah's judgment in 2:131 is not limited to the visible outline, yet the inner intention is not an excuse for ignoring the rights and forms that the revelation establishes. Sincerity completes obedience; it does not cancel it.

**Mercy and warning held together**

One can see the social wisdom of 2:131 in a small everyday case. When people share responsibility for ordered, uncertainty about the duty tends to fall on the person with less power or less information. A clear moral rule changes that balance. In 2:131, it tells the stronger party what must not be taken, and gives the weaker party language with which to name the wrong.

In 2:131, the verse also sits within the Qur'an's larger refusal to separate worship from truthfulness. Prayer, remembrance, generosity, patience, and justice are different acts in 2:131, but they are trained by the same capacity to stand before Allah without constructing a false account. The statement in 2:131 strengthens that capacity by making one particular choice visible.

**The deed before its final account**

A reader who finds the command in 2:131 difficult should distinguish difficulty from impossibility. For 2:131, difficulty may be the place where desire, habit, or social pressure is being corrected; impossibility is a separate legal and moral question. The passage does not praise needless hardship, but it does not permit comfort to redefine ordered until the demand disappears.

The final emphasis of 2:131 returns to responsibility. No explanation of ordered is complete if it leaves the reader with information about earlier communities but no question about present conduct. The verse 2:131 asks what can be repaired now: a promise kept, a right restored, a motive purified, or a harmful practice abandoned. That is where knowledge becomes guidance.

**Why the promise or threat is just**

The choice of the word ordered in 2:131 also matters. It leaves room for the full context of 2:131 instead of allowing one translation gloss to carry every shade of meaning. Classical Arabic often places a broad word inside a narrow scene; in 2:131, the surrounding clauses determine whether the emphasis falls on an act, an intention, a condition, or a consequence. Here the reader should let the sentence do that work.

The exegetical tradition approaches ordered from more than one angle, but its differences are more useful when their shared question is made visible. The commentators ask what the wording of 2:131 includes, what it excludes, and how it fits with the other passages of the Qur'an. A responsible reading can preserve those alternatives in 2:131 without pretending that every possibility has equal strength.

**The Hereafter in the argument**

There is also a distinction between understanding ordered and using it as a label for other people. The first task belongs to learning; the second requires evidence, fairness, and awareness of one's own motives in 2:131. The verse 2:131 addresses the reader before it supplies material for judging a neighbour. Its warning becomes safer and more productive when self-examination comes first.

The theology and practical instruction of 2:131 therefore support one another. If Allah knows or commands worlds, that knowledge or command changes how a person handles time, power, money, speech, and private desire. A doctrine that never reaches those choices has not yet been read at the level demanded by 2:131.

An ordinary example makes the moral structure of 2:131 visible without replacing the revelation. Two people may perform an outwardly similar act involving ordered; one may be protecting a right, while the other is seeking praise, escaping a duty, or taking advantage of confusion. The act's surface does not settle its value. Intention, circumstance, and the rights of others complete the judgment.

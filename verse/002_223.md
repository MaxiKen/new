## Sūrah al-Baqarah 2:223

> **Your wives are like farmland for you, so approach them ˹consensually˺ as you please. And send forth something good for yourselves. Be mindful of Allah, and know that you will meet Him. And give good news to the believers.**

### Expanded Commentary

**A Tilth of Allah**

The force of this verse lies in the connection between wives and approach. A reader can miss that force by isolating one phrase and treating it as a general maxim in 2:223. The verse itself is more exact. It speaks to a particular condition in the sūrah and directs attention to the kind of response that condition requires in 2:223. Reading the terms together keeps both the theology and the practical instruction in view at 2:223.

This verse is seen by some as giving permission for any kind of vaginal intercourse, the symbolism of tilth referring to procreation . In a clash of prevailing customs, some Companions abhorred intercourse in positions where the man was behind the woman, while others practiced intercourse in all positions. In some cases, Emigrants from Makkah married Helpers, who were natives of Madinah, and the latter refused certain positions for intercourse. In some accounts, certain Companions came to the Prophet to inquire about these matters, which served as the occasion for the revelation of this verse.

**As You Will — the Freedom of the Law**

In some narrations, the Prophet said of this verse, A single orifice (al-Ṭabarī), referring to vaginal intercourse. Most commentators note that the grammar of the verse in Arabic denotes direction and place and devote their primary attention in commenting on this verse to in what part of the body one has intercourse and coming from what direction; they are generally emphatic that this verse does not refer to when or how or at whose behest intercourse takes place (R, Ṭ, Ṭs). Send forth means to perform good deeds that will benefit one in the Hereafter; see 5:80–81c; 82:5c; 75:13c.

The verse describes wives not as a private opinion but as a direction that changes perception and conduct. In 2:223, guidance is recognized by what it makes a person do with knowledge, power, and desire. For 2:223, inward assent is linked to an observable response: the heart's claim is tested by the path it chooses when the claim becomes costly.

**Send Forth for Your Souls**

The standard in 2:223 remains practical because it can be applied before a dispute reaches a court or a crisis reaches its worst point. One can notice the first small departure from wives: an excuse, a concealed obligation, an unexamined desire, or a promise treated as optional. Returning at that point is easier than defending a settled habit. The warning is therefore also an opening for repair.

The relation between wives and approach also protects the verse from two opposite misreadings. It should not be expanded into a claim the wording of 2:223 does not make, but neither should its brevity be used to make it weightless. Short Qur'anic statements such as 2:223 often function as hinges: they close one movement, open another, and give the next command its moral setting. The careful reader of 2:223 therefore asks what has just been established, what response is now being required, and what kind of character could respond without evasion. Those questions keep 2:223 specific while allowing its guidance to reach a new circumstance.

**"Know That You Shall Meet Him"**

The final emphasis of 2:223 returns to responsibility. No explanation of wives is complete if it leaves the reader with information about earlier communities but no question about present conduct. The verse 2:223 asks what can be repaired now: a promise kept, a right restored, a motive purified, or a harmful practice abandoned. That is where knowledge becomes guidance.

The choice of the word wives in 2:223 also matters. It leaves room for the full context of 2:223 instead of allowing one translation gloss to carry every shade of meaning. Classical Arabic often places a broad word inside a narrow scene; in 2:223, the surrounding clauses determine whether the emphasis falls on an act, an intention, a condition, or a consequence. Here the reader should let the sentence do that work.

**The first movement of a guided life**

The exegetical tradition approaches wives from more than one angle, but its differences are more useful when their shared question is made visible. The commentators ask what the wording of 2:223 includes, what it excludes, and how it fits with the other passages of the Qur'an. A responsible reading can preserve those alternatives in 2:223 without pretending that every possibility has equal strength.

There is also a distinction between understanding wives and using it as a label for other people. The first task belongs to learning; the second requires evidence, fairness, and awareness of one's own motives in 2:223. The verse 2:223 addresses the reader before it supplies material for judging a neighbour. Its warning becomes safer and more productive when self-examination comes first.

**Belief made visible in conduct**

The theology and practical instruction of 2:223 therefore support one another. If Allah knows or commands approach, that knowledge or command changes how a person handles time, power, money, speech, and private desire. A doctrine that never reaches those choices has not yet been read at the level demanded by 2:223.

An ordinary example makes the moral structure of 2:223 visible without replacing the revelation. Two people may perform an outwardly similar act involving wives; one may be protecting a right, while the other is seeking praise, escaping a duty, or taking advantage of confusion. The act's surface does not settle its value. Intention, circumstance, and the rights of others complete the judgment.

**The choice hidden inside the command**

The surrounding verses keep the reader from isolating wives from the rest of life. In 2:223, revelation corrects the mind but also trains habits: how a person speaks when challenged, gives when unseen, waits when angry, and returns after error. Those habits are where this verse becomes more than information about wives. They are the place in which assent to 2:223 is either confirmed or quietly withdrawn.

The most balanced response to 2:223 is neither to make the passage vague nor to claim more than it says. Its boundaries are part of its guidance. Within them, the verse can illuminate a new situation because its underlying moral relation remains stable: wives is joined to approach, and the person is responsible for how that relation is honoured.

**How guidance becomes discernment**

The wording of 2:223 gives the reader a useful order of questions about wives. What does the term wives mean in ordinary Arabic, what does the surrounding sentence restrict it to, and what consequence follows when the command is obeyed or refused in 2:223? These questions prevent a commentary on 2:223 from becoming a list of slogans. They make the act of interpretation answerable to grammar, context, and the moral end of this passage at 2:223.

The commentators sometimes preserve more than one report about the setting of 2:223. Such reports should be weighed rather than treated as interchangeable decoration. An occasion can explain why a question arose, but the wording of 2:223 determines how far the guidance travels. The verse speaks first to the situation that occasioned it and then to every reader who meets the same relation between wives and approach.

**The contrast drawn by the verse**

In 2:223, the distinction between public appearance and inward truth is especially relevant. A person may use the language of wives while arranging the act around another purpose. Allah's judgment in 2:223 is not limited to the visible outline, yet the inner intention is not an excuse for ignoring the rights and forms that the revelation establishes. Sincerity completes obedience; it does not cancel it.

One can see the social wisdom of 2:223 in a small everyday case. When people share responsibility for wives, uncertainty about the duty tends to fall on the person with less power or less information. A clear moral rule changes that balance. In 2:223, it tells the stronger party what must not be taken, and gives the weaker party language with which to name the wrong.

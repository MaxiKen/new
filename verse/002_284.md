## Sūrah al-Baqarah 2:284

> **To Allah ˹alone˺ belongs whatever is in the heavens and whatever is on the earth. Whether you reveal what is in your hearts or conceal it, Allah will call you to account for it. He forgives whoever He wills, and punishes whoever He wills. And Allah is Most Capable of everything.**

### Expanded Commentary

**Everything Is His — Everything Is Accountable**

At this point in al-Baqarah, the wording brings alone and earth into one moral frame. The sentence should not be detached from that relationship. Its first term in 2:284 sets the subject, while the later term shows what that subject demands, permits, or exposes. The order of 2:284 gives the reader a path through the verse: begin with what Allah names, follow the consequence, and resist the temptation to turn a living address into a slogan.

**The Companions' Weeping — and Allah's Own Lifting-Off**

Some report that the disclosure or hiding mentioned in this verse refers specifically to the testimony or witnessing legislated in vv. 282–83, namely, that the witness bears a moral responsibility for discharging this important duty truthfully (al-Ṭabarī). Following a more universal reading of this verse, it is reported that, upon hearing this verse, many Companions were distressed and grieved, fearing they could never escape from the reality it described, that Allah will bring them to account for what is disclosed or hidden. Some worried that it meant that they would be held responsible for fleeting thoughts, and some wept from fear. According to this version of the account, the subsequent two verses, which are the final verses of this sūrah, were revealed in part to quell the disquiet in their hearts (al-Ṭabarī).

**The Tradition at its End**

Although some think that Allah tasks no soul beyond its capacity in v. 286 abrogates v. 284, there is no contradiction between the two, and moreover since these are descriptions of Allahs Acts and not legal rulings, they are not subject to abrogation. Also, the language bring you to account need not mean punishment (al-Ṭabarī), since according to Islamic belief people will first be called to account for all their deeds on the Day of Judgment before entering Paradise or Hell, except for the elect who go to Paradise directly (see also 2:212c). Regarding this accounting, see, for example, 18:49: And they will say, Oh, woe unto us! What a book this is! It leaves nothing out, neither small nor large, save that it has taken account thereof.

**The horizon beyond the immediate scene**

On the subject of being taken to account for one’s inward and outward actions, a ḥadīth states, “Whosoever intends a good deed but does not do it, Allah records a completed good deed for him. If he intends it and does it, Allah records ten good deeds and up to seven hundred times more or even greater than that. If he intends to commit an evil deed but does not do it, Allah records a good deed for him. If he intends to do it and does it, Allah records a single evil deed for him.” This ḥadīth and similar ones distinguish between a psychological state and an executed choice, recognizing the difference between a concrete intention one is unable to carry out because of external circumstances beyond one’s control—as when a would-be murderer’s gun malfunctions as the trigger is pulled—and a vengeful thought that haunts one’s mind, but that one sincerely seeks to overcome.

**A divine name that governs the meaning**

The verse's reference to alone widens the scene beyond the immediate reward or pain. A deed in 2:284 may appear small when measured by public attention, yet its meaning changes when it is known by Allah and carried into the final account. Warning and mercy belong together in 2:284: accountability is serious because the human being is not abandoned to meaninglessness.

A useful way to test the verse is to ask what would change if alone were taken seriously when no audience was present. The answer cannot be only a feeling, because 2:284 connects inward recognition with a discernible choice. Nor can it be only an outward display, because the choice in 2:284 loses its Qur'anic meaning when it is made for reputation rather than for Allah. The passage holds intention and action together in 2:284.

**Mercy and warning held together**

The relation between alone and earth also protects the verse from two opposite misreadings. It should not be expanded into a claim the wording of 2:284 does not make, but neither should its brevity be used to make it weightless. Short Qur'anic statements such as 2:284 often function as hinges: they close one movement, open another, and give the next command its moral setting. The careful reader of 2:284 therefore asks what has just been established, what response is now being required, and what kind of character could respond without evasion. Those questions keep 2:284 specific while allowing its guidance to reach a new circumstance.

An ordinary example makes the moral structure of 2:284 visible without replacing the revelation. Two people may perform an outwardly similar act involving alone; one may be protecting a right, while the other is seeking praise, escaping a duty, or taking advantage of confusion. The act's surface does not settle its value. Intention, circumstance, and the rights of others complete the judgment.

**The deed before its final account**

The surrounding verses keep the reader from isolating alone from the rest of life. In 2:284, revelation corrects the mind but also trains habits: how a person speaks when challenged, gives when unseen, waits when angry, and returns after error. Those habits are where this verse becomes more than information about alone. They are the place in which assent to 2:284 is either confirmed or quietly withdrawn.

The most balanced response to 2:284 is neither to make the passage vague nor to claim more than it says. Its boundaries are part of its guidance. Within them, the verse can illuminate a new situation because its underlying moral relation remains stable: alone is joined to earth, and the person is responsible for how that relation is honoured.

**Why the promise or threat is just**

The wording of 2:284 gives the reader a useful order of questions about alone. What does the term alone mean in ordinary Arabic, what does the surrounding sentence restrict it to, and what consequence follows when the command is obeyed or refused in 2:284? These questions prevent a commentary on 2:284 from becoming a list of slogans. They make the act of interpretation answerable to grammar, context, and the moral end of this passage at 2:284.

The commentators sometimes preserve more than one report about the setting of 2:284. Such reports should be weighed rather than treated as interchangeable decoration. An occasion can explain why a question arose, but the wording of 2:284 determines how far the guidance travels. The verse speaks first to the situation that occasioned it and then to every reader who meets the same relation between alone and earth.

**The Hereafter in the argument**

In 2:284, the distinction between public appearance and inward truth is especially relevant. A person may use the language of alone while arranging the act around another purpose. Allah's judgment in 2:284 is not limited to the visible outline, yet the inner intention is not an excuse for ignoring the rights and forms that the revelation establishes. Sincerity completes obedience; it does not cancel it.

One can see the social wisdom of 2:284 in a small everyday case. When people share responsibility for alone, uncertainty about the duty tends to fall on the person with less power or less information. A clear moral rule changes that balance. In 2:284, it tells the stronger party what must not be taken, and gives the weaker party language with which to name the wrong.

In 2:284, the verse also sits within the Qur'an's larger refusal to separate worship from truthfulness. Prayer, remembrance, generosity, patience, and justice are different acts in 2:284, but they are trained by the same capacity to stand before Allah without constructing a false account. The statement in 2:284 strengthens that capacity by making one particular choice visible.

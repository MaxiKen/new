## Sūrah al-Baqarah 2:120

> **Never will the Jews or Christians be pleased with you, until you follow their faith. Say, “Allah’s guidance is the only ˹true˺ guidance.” And if you were to follow their desires after ˹all˺ the knowledge that has come to you, there would be none to protect or help you against Allah.**

### Expanded Commentary

**The Impossibility of Contentment**

At this point in al-Baqarah, the wording brings never and pleased into one moral frame. The sentence should not be detached from that relationship. Its first term in 2:120 sets the subject, while the later term shows what that subject demands, permits, or exposes. The order of 2:120 gives the reader a path through the verse: begin with what Allah names, follow the consequence, and resist the temptation to turn a living address into a slogan.

**"The Guidance of Allah Is THE Guidance"**

For some commentators this verse is a confirmation that the question in v. 118 is not sincere, but a taunt (al-Qurṭubī). Creed renders millah, a word that also can mean community, but that can furthermore be used to refer to the religious law (sharīʿah) or way that defines a particular religious community (al-Qurṭubī) and is used this way elsewhere (2:130, 135; 3:95; 4:125; 6:161; 7:88; 12:37; 14:13; 16:123; 18:20; 22:78; 38:7). See v. 145 for a similar message about the following of others caprices, meaning their egocentric whims rather than the truth. Some commentators report that when the qiblah was changed to the Kaʿbah away from Jerusalem, this created resentment among the Jews and Christians, who wanted to use a truce as a delaying tactic while trying to turn the Prophet away from the new religion .

**Caprice vs. Knowledge — and the Ummah's Own Warning**

The verse describes never not as a private opinion but as a direction that changes perception and conduct. In 2:120, guidance is recognized by what it makes a person do with knowledge, power, and desire. For 2:120, inward assent is linked to an observable response: the heart's claim is tested by the path it chooses when the claim becomes costly.

A useful way to test the verse is to ask what would change if never were taken seriously when no audience was present. The answer cannot be only a feeling, because 2:120 connects inward recognition with a discernible choice. Nor can it be only an outward display, because the choice in 2:120 loses its Qur'anic meaning when it is made for reputation rather than for Allah. The passage holds intention and action together in 2:120.

**The first movement of a guided life**

The relation between never and pleased also protects the verse from two opposite misreadings. It should not be expanded into a claim the wording of 2:120 does not make, but neither should its brevity be used to make it weightless. Short Qur'anic statements such as 2:120 often function as hinges: they close one movement, open another, and give the next command its moral setting. The careful reader of 2:120 therefore asks what has just been established, what response is now being required, and what kind of character could respond without evasion. Those questions keep 2:120 specific while allowing its guidance to reach a new circumstance.

**Belief made visible in conduct**

The commentators sometimes preserve more than one report about the setting of 2:120. Such reports should be weighed rather than treated as interchangeable decoration. An occasion can explain why a question arose, but the wording of 2:120 determines how far the guidance travels. The verse speaks first to the situation that occasioned it and then to every reader who meets the same relation between never and pleased.

In 2:120, the distinction between public appearance and inward truth is especially relevant. A person may use the language of never while arranging the act around another purpose. Allah's judgment in 2:120 is not limited to the visible outline, yet the inner intention is not an excuse for ignoring the rights and forms that the revelation establishes. Sincerity completes obedience; it does not cancel it.

**The choice hidden inside the command**

One can see the social wisdom of 2:120 in a small everyday case. When people share responsibility for never, uncertainty about the duty tends to fall on the person with less power or less information. A clear moral rule changes that balance. In 2:120, it tells the stronger party what must not be taken, and gives the weaker party language with which to name the wrong.

In 2:120, the verse also sits within the Qur'an's larger refusal to separate worship from truthfulness. Prayer, remembrance, generosity, patience, and justice are different acts in 2:120, but they are trained by the same capacity to stand before Allah without constructing a false account. The statement in 2:120 strengthens that capacity by making one particular choice visible.

**How guidance becomes discernment**

A reader who finds the command in 2:120 difficult should distinguish difficulty from impossibility. For 2:120, difficulty may be the place where desire, habit, or social pressure is being corrected; impossibility is a separate legal and moral question. The passage does not praise needless hardship, but it does not permit comfort to redefine never until the demand disappears.

The final emphasis of 2:120 returns to responsibility. No explanation of never is complete if it leaves the reader with information about earlier communities but no question about present conduct. The verse 2:120 asks what can be repaired now: a promise kept, a right restored, a motive purified, or a harmful practice abandoned. That is where knowledge becomes guidance.

**The contrast drawn by the verse**

The choice of the word never in 2:120 also matters. It leaves room for the full context of 2:120 instead of allowing one translation gloss to carry every shade of meaning. Classical Arabic often places a broad word inside a narrow scene; in 2:120, the surrounding clauses determine whether the emphasis falls on an act, an intention, a condition, or a consequence. Here the reader should let the sentence do that work.

The exegetical tradition approaches never from more than one angle, but its differences are more useful when their shared question is made visible. The commentators ask what the wording of 2:120 includes, what it excludes, and how it fits with the other passages of the Qur'an. A responsible reading can preserve those alternatives in 2:120 without pretending that every possibility has equal strength.

**Certainty, doubt, and moral action**

There is also a distinction between understanding never and using it as a label for other people. The first task belongs to learning; the second requires evidence, fairness, and awareness of one's own motives in 2:120. The verse 2:120 addresses the reader before it supplies material for judging a neighbour. Its warning becomes safer and more productive when self-examination comes first.

The theology and practical instruction of 2:120 therefore support one another. If Allah knows or commands pleased, that knowledge or command changes how a person handles time, power, money, speech, and private desire. A doctrine that never reaches those choices has not yet been read at the level demanded by 2:120.

An ordinary example makes the moral structure of 2:120 visible without replacing the revelation. Two people may perform an outwardly similar act involving never; one may be protecting a right, while the other is seeking praise, escaping a duty, or taking advantage of confusion. The act's surface does not settle its value. Intention, circumstance, and the rights of others complete the judgment.

The surrounding verses keep the reader from isolating never from the rest of life. In 2:120, revelation corrects the mind but also trains habits: how a person speaks when challenged, gives when unseen, waits when angry, and returns after error. Those habits are where this verse becomes more than information about never. They are the place in which assent to 2:120 is either confirmed or quietly withdrawn.

The most balanced response to 2:120 is neither to make the passage vague nor to claim more than it says. Its boundaries are part of its guidance. Within them, the verse can illuminate a new situation because its underlying moral relation remains stable: never is joined to pleased, and the person is responsible for how that relation is honoured.

## Sūrah al-Baqarah 2:228

> **Divorced women must wait three monthly cycles ˹before they can remarry˺. It is not lawful for them to conceal what Allah has created in their wombs, if they ˹truly˺ believe in Allah and the Last Day. And their husbands reserve the right to take them back within that period if they desire reconciliation. Women have rights similar to those of men equitably, although men have a degree ˹of responsibility˺ above them. And Allah is Almighty, All-Wise.**

### Expanded Commentary

**The Waiting Period — ʿIddah**

At this point in al-Baqarah, the wording brings divorced and three into one moral frame. The sentence should not be detached from that relationship. Its first term in 2:228 sets the subject, while the later term shows what that subject demands, permits, or exposes. The order of 2:228 gives the reader a path through the verse: begin with what Allah names, follow the consequence, and resist the temptation to turn a living address into a slogan.

**No Conceiving by Concealment**

In the type of divorce discussed here, called ṭalāq, the husband declares divorce from his wife, after which she observes a period of waiting (ʿiddah) amounting to three courses, during which the couple do not have sexual relations, before the divorce is final. These three courses are three menstrual periods, but some disagreement exists over whether the courses (qurūʾ) are marked by the actual menstrual flow, the flows cessation, or by the ritual washing (ghusl) that allows the wife to then perform the canonical prayer. Until the waiting period is over, the husband can still withdraw his intention to divorce and the couple can resume normal marital relations.

**"Husbands Have Better Right to Restore"**

It is not lawful for them to conceal means that they should not conceal any possible pregnancy from the husbands who have just divorced them. That the husbands have better right to restore them during that time means that husbands, being the party who initiated the divorce, retain the right to resume the marriage before the waiting period is over. In Islamic Law the wifes options for divorce do not operate under the rubric of ṭalāq divorce, but under khulʿ divorce, discussed in v. 229 and 4:20. The women appears in brackets in place of the feminine plural pronoun.

**"The Like of What They Owe — and a Degree Over Them"**

According to some, [The women] are owed obligations the like of those they owe means that each side owes something to the other—that is, wives should obey their husbands and husbands should treat their wives well and with respect; for others, it means that women have a right to sexual relations similar to that of men (al-Ṭabarī). It is reported that Ibn ʿAbbās said, I beautify myself for my wife, just as she beautifies herself for me (al-Qurṭubī). For others, it is a question of mutual protection from harm, where the man does not harm the wife through an endless cycle of divorce and reconciliation, and the wife does not harm the husband by hiding what she might be carrying in her womb (al-Ṭabarī).

**A household placed under moral care**

Others mention that the point of marriage is closeness, love, and peace, which should be shared between husband and wife, and al-Rāzī says, One might even say that [in this] the share of the woman is more abundant.

In And men have a degree over them, many commentators understand a kind of noblesse oblige. Ibn ʿAbbās is reported to have said, “This degree is a reference to men’s encouraging good relations and their generosity toward women in wealth and virtuous behavior, which means that the one possessing the upper hand must be biased against himself” (al-Qurṭubī). Others state that this degree over them consists of men’s economic advantages and also obligations and their ability to fight in the way of Allah (although some female Companions of the Prophet did participate in fighting) or refers to qualities such as strength and intelligence (al-Qurṭubī).

**The rights concealed in the wording**

Interpreted spiritually, this verse can mean that the soul (nafs) has rights owed to it and rights that it owes, which in the context of Islamic spiritual psychology means that, although the spirit (rūḥ) dominates over the powers of the soul, it is precisely through the soul that spiritual progress takes place. Thus, one must be gentle with the soul, letting it rest when it needs it, to ensure healthy spiritual progress (Aj). The wedding of the soul to the spirit is a common motif in Islamic spiritual literature.

The reference to divorced places a human being who may be dependent, exposed, or unable to defend a claim at the centre of the discussion. Responsibility here is not merely a feeling of affection. It has a form that can be recognized, a limit that can be enforced, and a purpose: to prevent the strong from making the weak carry the cost of decisions they did not control.

**Time, vulnerability, and restraint**

A useful way to test the verse is to ask what would change if divorced were taken seriously when no audience was present. The answer cannot be only a feeling, because 2:228 connects inward recognition with a discernible choice. Nor can it be only an outward display, because the choice in 2:228 loses its Qur'anic meaning when it is made for reputation rather than for Allah. The passage holds intention and action together in 2:228.

An ordinary example makes the moral structure of 2:228 visible without replacing the revelation. Two people may perform an outwardly similar act involving divorced; one may be protecting a right, while the other is seeking praise, escaping a duty, or taking advantage of confusion. The act's surface does not settle its value. Intention, circumstance, and the rights of others complete the judgment.

**The difference between power and justice**

The surrounding verses keep the reader from isolating divorced from the rest of life. In 2:228, revelation corrects the mind but also trains habits: how a person speaks when challenged, gives when unseen, waits when angry, and returns after error. Those habits are where this verse becomes more than information about divorced. They are the place in which assent to 2:228 is either confirmed or quietly withdrawn.

The most balanced response to 2:228 is neither to make the passage vague nor to claim more than it says. Its boundaries are part of its guidance. Within them, the verse can illuminate a new situation because its underlying moral relation remains stable: divorced is joined to three, and the person is responsible for how that relation is honoured.

**How the jurists read the command**

The wording of 2:228 gives the reader a useful order of questions about divorced. What does the term divorced mean in ordinary Arabic, what does the surrounding sentence restrict it to, and what consequence follows when the command is obeyed or refused in 2:228? These questions prevent a commentary on 2:228 from becoming a list of slogans. They make the act of interpretation answerable to grammar, context, and the moral end of this passage at 2:228.

The commentators sometimes preserve more than one report about the setting of 2:228. Such reports should be weighed rather than treated as interchangeable decoration. An occasion can explain why a question arose, but the wording of 2:228 determines how far the guidance travels. The verse speaks first to the situation that occasioned it and then to every reader who meets the same relation between divorced and three.

In 2:228, the distinction between public appearance and inward truth is especially relevant. A person may use the language of divorced while arranging the act around another purpose. Allah's judgment in 2:228 is not limited to the visible outline, yet the inner intention is not an excuse for ignoring the rights and forms that the revelation establishes. Sincerity completes obedience; it does not cancel it.

One can see the social wisdom of 2:228 in a small everyday case. When people share responsibility for divorced, uncertainty about the duty tends to fall on the person with less power or less information. A clear moral rule changes that balance. In 2:228, it tells the stronger party what must not be taken, and gives the weaker party language with which to name the wrong.

In 2:228, the verse also sits within the Qur'an's larger refusal to separate worship from truthfulness. Prayer, remembrance, generosity, patience, and justice are different acts in 2:228, but they are trained by the same capacity to stand before Allah without constructing a false account. The statement in 2:228 strengthens that capacity by making one particular choice visible.

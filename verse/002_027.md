## Sūrah al-Baqarah 2:27

> **those who violate Allah’s covenant after it has been affirmed, break whatever ˹ties˺ Allah has ordered to be maintained, and spread corruption in the land. It is they who are the ˹true˺ losers.**

### Expanded Commentary

**The Covenant and Its Breach**

The force of this verse lies in the connection between violate and affirmed. A reader can miss that force by isolating one phrase and treating it as a general maxim in 2:27. The verse itself is more exact. It speaks to a particular condition in the sūrah and directs attention to the kind of response that condition requires in 2:27. Reading the terms together keeps both the theology and the practical instruction in view at 2:27.

The Covenant mentioned in this verse is understood as the general obligation of human beings to acknowledge Allahs Oneness and worship Him (Ibn Kathīr) as understood in the pre-eternal covenant between Allah and human beings (7:172); or it refers to And they swore by Allah their most solemn oaths that, were a warner to come unto them, they would be more rightly guided than any of the communities (35:42), which means the Arabs before the coming of the Prophet (al-Rāzī); or it refers to a specific covenant made with the People of the Book.

**Severing What Allah Commanded Be Joined**

See the essay The Qur'anic View of Sacred History and Other Religions. Some say that what Allah has commanded be joined refers to blood ties, whose importance and maintenance as commanded by Allah appears elsewhere in the Qur'an: Reverence . . . family relations (arḥām; 4:1); But family relations have the strongest claim on one another in the Book of Allah (8:75); Family relations are closer to one another, according to the Book of Allah (33:6); Were you to turn away, would you perchance work corruption upon the earth and break your family relations? (47:22).

Others consider it to include any such Divine Command to keep things joined (Ibn Kathīr). Some mention in this context that not only the Arabs had blood ties to the Prophet; the Jews did also, since Isaac and Ishmael (the progenitor of the Arabs) were brothers. The losers are such in the sense of being bereft of something; khāsir can also denote being lost in the sense of losing ones way or losing ones self.

**Corruption upon the Earth as the Third Operation**

The verse's reference to violate widens the scene beyond the immediate reward or pain. A deed in 2:27 may appear small when measured by public attention, yet its meaning changes when it is known by Allah and carried into the final account. Warning and mercy belong together in 2:27: accountability is serious because the human being is not abandoned to meaninglessness.

The standard in 2:27 remains practical because it can be applied before a dispute reaches a court or a crisis reaches its worst point. One can notice the first small departure from violate: an excuse, a concealed obligation, an unexamined desire, or a promise treated as optional. Returning at that point is easier than defending a settled habit. The warning is therefore also an opening for repair.

**"It Is They Who Are the Losers": The Commerce Completed**

The relation between violate and affirmed also protects the verse from two opposite misreadings. It should not be expanded into a claim the wording of 2:27 does not make, but neither should its brevity be used to make it weightless. Short Qur'anic statements such as 2:27 often function as hinges: they close one movement, open another, and give the next command its moral setting. The careful reader of 2:27 therefore asks what has just been established, what response is now being required, and what kind of character could respond without evasion. Those questions keep 2:27 specific while allowing its guidance to reach a new circumstance.

The final emphasis of 2:27 returns to responsibility. No explanation of violate is complete if it leaves the reader with information about earlier communities but no question about present conduct. The verse 2:27 asks what can be repaired now: a promise kept, a right restored, a motive purified, or a harmful practice abandoned. That is where knowledge becomes guidance.

**The horizon beyond the immediate scene**

The choice of the word violate in 2:27 also matters. It leaves room for the full context of 2:27 instead of allowing one translation gloss to carry every shade of meaning. Classical Arabic often places a broad word inside a narrow scene; in 2:27, the surrounding clauses determine whether the emphasis falls on an act, an intention, a condition, or a consequence. Here the reader should let the sentence do that work.

The exegetical tradition approaches violate from more than one angle, but its differences are more useful when their shared question is made visible. The commentators ask what the wording of 2:27 includes, what it excludes, and how it fits with the other passages of the Qur'an. A responsible reading can preserve those alternatives in 2:27 without pretending that every possibility has equal strength.

**A divine name that governs the meaning**

There is also a distinction between understanding violate and using it as a label for other people. The first task belongs to learning; the second requires evidence, fairness, and awareness of one's own motives in 2:27. The verse 2:27 addresses the reader before it supplies material for judging a neighbour. Its warning becomes safer and more productive when self-examination comes first.

The theology and practical instruction of 2:27 therefore support one another. If Allah knows or commands affirmed, that knowledge or command changes how a person handles time, power, money, speech, and private desire. A doctrine that never reaches those choices has not yet been read at the level demanded by 2:27.

**Mercy and warning held together**

An ordinary example makes the moral structure of 2:27 visible without replacing the revelation. Two people may perform an outwardly similar act involving violate; one may be protecting a right, while the other is seeking praise, escaping a duty, or taking advantage of confusion. The act's surface does not settle its value. Intention, circumstance, and the rights of others complete the judgment.

The surrounding verses keep the reader from isolating violate from the rest of life. In 2:27, revelation corrects the mind but also trains habits: how a person speaks when challenged, gives when unseen, waits when angry, and returns after error. Those habits are where this verse becomes more than information about violate. They are the place in which assent to 2:27 is either confirmed or quietly withdrawn.

**The deed before its final account**

The most balanced response to 2:27 is neither to make the passage vague nor to claim more than it says. Its boundaries are part of its guidance. Within them, the verse can illuminate a new situation because its underlying moral relation remains stable: violate is joined to affirmed, and the person is responsible for how that relation is honoured.

The wording of 2:27 gives the reader a useful order of questions about violate. What does the term violate mean in ordinary Arabic, what does the surrounding sentence restrict it to, and what consequence follows when the command is obeyed or refused in 2:27? These questions prevent a commentary on 2:27 from becoming a list of slogans. They make the act of interpretation answerable to grammar, context, and the moral end of this passage at 2:27.

**Why the promise or threat is just**

The commentators sometimes preserve more than one report about the setting of 2:27. Such reports should be weighed rather than treated as interchangeable decoration. An occasion can explain why a question arose, but the wording of 2:27 determines how far the guidance travels. The verse speaks first to the situation that occasioned it and then to every reader who meets the same relation between violate and affirmed.

In 2:27, the distinction between public appearance and inward truth is especially relevant. A person may use the language of violate while arranging the act around another purpose. Allah's judgment in 2:27 is not limited to the visible outline, yet the inner intention is not an excuse for ignoring the rights and forms that the revelation establishes. Sincerity completes obedience; it does not cancel it.

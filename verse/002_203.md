## Sūrah al-Baqarah 2:203

> **And remember Allah during ˹these˺ appointed days. Whoever departs swiftly on the second day is not sinful, neither are those who stay behind ˹till the third—seeking additional reward˺, so long as they are mindful ˹of their Lord˺. And be mindful of Allah, and know that to Him you will ˹all˺ be gathered.**

### Expanded Commentary

**The Days of Tashrīq Named**

The force of this verse lies in the connection between during and whoever. A reader can miss that force by isolating one phrase and treating it as a general maxim in 2:203. The verse itself is more exact. It speaks to a particular condition in the sūrah and directs attention to the kind of response that condition requires in 2:203. Reading the terms together keeps both the theology and the practical instruction in view at 2:203.

**Two Days or Three: The Counting Flex of Allah**

Days numbered refers to the eleventh through the thirteenth of Dhu’l-Ḥijjah, which pilgrims spend at Mina, often called the days of tashrīq, a word of uncertain derivation and hence difficult to translate. These are the three days after the Day of Sacrifice, not including that day, and during those days one rite that the pilgrims perform is to cast stones at the columns representing Satan (al-Rāzī and al-Qurṭubī). Hastens on after two days means that one stays in Mina for only two days (until the twelfth of the month) rather than remaining three days (until the thirteenth). One then makes a farewell circumambulation around the Kaʿbah, and the ḥajj is complete.

**"Unto Him Shall You Be Gathered"**

The command to remember Allah is thought to refer to the magnification of Allah, or takbīr (Allāhu akbar, “Allah is great!”), recited while throwing pebbles at the columns and also at the times of the prayers during those days. The takbīr during the prayers is recited not only by the pilgrims, but by all Muslims during this period, from the day of the Feast of Sacrifice (the tenth of Dhu’l-Ḥijjah) until the last days of tashrīq (see above; Q). The litanies of takbīr are very similar to those recited at the end of Ramadan, the foundation of which is a variation of Allāhu akbar, Allāhu akbar, lā ilāha illa’Llāh, wa’Llāhu akbar wa li’lLāhi’l-ḥamd (“Allah is Great! Allah is Great! There is no god but Allah! Allah is Great, and praise be to Allah!”); other litanies are also sometimes added.

**The first movement of a guided life**

The verse describes during not as a private opinion but as a direction that changes perception and conduct. In 2:203, guidance is recognized by what it makes a person do with knowledge, power, and desire. For 2:203, inward assent is linked to an observable response: the heart's claim is tested by the path it chooses when the claim becomes costly.

The standard in 2:203 remains practical because it can be applied before a dispute reaches a court or a crisis reaches its worst point. One can notice the first small departure from during: an excuse, a concealed obligation, an unexamined desire, or a promise treated as optional. Returning at that point is easier than defending a settled habit. The warning is therefore also an opening for repair.

**Belief made visible in conduct**

The relation between during and whoever also protects the verse from two opposite misreadings. It should not be expanded into a claim the wording of 2:203 does not make, but neither should its brevity be used to make it weightless. Short Qur'anic statements such as 2:203 often function as hinges: they close one movement, open another, and give the next command its moral setting. The careful reader of 2:203 therefore asks what has just been established, what response is now being required, and what kind of character could respond without evasion. Those questions keep 2:203 specific while allowing its guidance to reach a new circumstance.

**The choice hidden inside the command**

The wording of 2:203 gives the reader a useful order of questions about during. What does the term during mean in ordinary Arabic, what does the surrounding sentence restrict it to, and what consequence follows when the command is obeyed or refused in 2:203? These questions prevent a commentary on 2:203 from becoming a list of slogans. They make the act of interpretation answerable to grammar, context, and the moral end of this passage at 2:203.

The commentators sometimes preserve more than one report about the setting of 2:203. Such reports should be weighed rather than treated as interchangeable decoration. An occasion can explain why a question arose, but the wording of 2:203 determines how far the guidance travels. The verse speaks first to the situation that occasioned it and then to every reader who meets the same relation between during and whoever.

**How guidance becomes discernment**

In 2:203, the distinction between public appearance and inward truth is especially relevant. A person may use the language of during while arranging the act around another purpose. Allah's judgment in 2:203 is not limited to the visible outline, yet the inner intention is not an excuse for ignoring the rights and forms that the revelation establishes. Sincerity completes obedience; it does not cancel it.

One can see the social wisdom of 2:203 in a small everyday case. When people share responsibility for during, uncertainty about the duty tends to fall on the person with less power or less information. A clear moral rule changes that balance. In 2:203, it tells the stronger party what must not be taken, and gives the weaker party language with which to name the wrong.

**The contrast drawn by the verse**

In 2:203, the verse also sits within the Qur'an's larger refusal to separate worship from truthfulness. Prayer, remembrance, generosity, patience, and justice are different acts in 2:203, but they are trained by the same capacity to stand before Allah without constructing a false account. The statement in 2:203 strengthens that capacity by making one particular choice visible.

A reader who finds the command in 2:203 difficult should distinguish difficulty from impossibility. For 2:203, difficulty may be the place where desire, habit, or social pressure is being corrected; impossibility is a separate legal and moral question. The passage does not praise needless hardship, but it does not permit comfort to redefine during until the demand disappears.

**Certainty, doubt, and moral action**

The final emphasis of 2:203 returns to responsibility. No explanation of during is complete if it leaves the reader with information about earlier communities but no question about present conduct. The verse 2:203 asks what can be repaired now: a promise kept, a right restored, a motive purified, or a harmful practice abandoned. That is where knowledge becomes guidance.

The choice of the word during in 2:203 also matters. It leaves room for the full context of 2:203 instead of allowing one translation gloss to carry every shade of meaning. Classical Arabic often places a broad word inside a narrow scene; in 2:203, the surrounding clauses determine whether the emphasis falls on an act, an intention, a condition, or a consequence. Here the reader should let the sentence do that work.

The exegetical tradition approaches during from more than one angle, but its differences are more useful when their shared question is made visible. The commentators ask what the wording of 2:203 includes, what it excludes, and how it fits with the other passages of the Qur'an. A responsible reading can preserve those alternatives in 2:203 without pretending that every possibility has equal strength.

There is also a distinction between understanding during and using it as a label for other people. The first task belongs to learning; the second requires evidence, fairness, and awareness of one's own motives in 2:203. The verse 2:203 addresses the reader before it supplies material for judging a neighbour. Its warning becomes safer and more productive when self-examination comes first.

The theology and practical instruction of 2:203 therefore support one another. If Allah knows or commands whoever, that knowledge or command changes how a person handles time, power, money, speech, and private desire. A doctrine that never reaches those choices has not yet been read at the level demanded by 2:203.

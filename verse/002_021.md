## Sūrah al-Baqarah 2:21

> **O humanity! Worship your Lord, Who created you and those before you, so that you may become mindful ˹of Him˺.**

### Expanded Commentary

**The First Command of the Longest Sūrah**

The force of this verse lies in the connection between humanity and become. A reader can miss that force by isolating one phrase and treating it as a general maxim in 2:21. The verse itself is more exact. It speaks to a particular condition in the sūrah and directs attention to the kind of response that condition requires in 2:21. Reading the terms together keeps both the theology and the practical instruction in view at 2:21.

That haply (laʿalla) occurs frequently in the Qur'an and is sometimes rendered “that perchance” or “that someone might.” It can be seen to present a theological problem, as it implies anxiety or hope, qualities usually associated only with creatures. Some solve this matter by saying that the hope of Allah is tantamount to a promise, as nothing could stand in its way (al-Zamakhsharī), or that the hope is connected to the subject of the statement, not to Allah.

**Worship Exclusive by Reference to Creation**

The verse describes humanity not as a private opinion but as a direction that changes perception and conduct. In 2:21, guidance is recognized by what it makes a person do with knowledge, power, and desire. For 2:21, inward assent is linked to an observable response: the heart's claim is tested by the path it chooses when the claim becomes costly.

The standard in 2:21 remains practical because it can be applied before a dispute reaches a court or a crisis reaches its worst point. One can notice the first small departure from humanity: an excuse, a concealed obligation, an unexamined desire, or a promise treated as optional. Returning at that point is easier than defending a settled habit. The warning is therefore also an opening for repair.

**Your Lord, and None Other's Lord**

The relation between humanity and become also protects the verse from two opposite misreadings. It should not be expanded into a claim the wording of 2:21 does not make, but neither should its brevity be used to make it weightless. Short Qur'anic statements such as 2:21 often function as hinges: they close one movement, open another, and give the next command its moral setting. The careful reader of 2:21 therefore asks what has just been established, what response is now being required, and what kind of character could respond without evasion. Those questions keep 2:21 specific while allowing its guidance to reach a new circumstance.

**That Haply You May Be Reverent: Worship as the Machine of Taqwā**

The wording of 2:21 gives the reader a useful order of questions about humanity. What does the term humanity mean in ordinary Arabic, what does the surrounding sentence restrict it to, and what consequence follows when the command is obeyed or refused in 2:21? These questions prevent a commentary on 2:21 from becoming a list of slogans. They make the act of interpretation answerable to grammar, context, and the moral end of this passage at 2:21.

The commentators sometimes preserve more than one report about the setting of 2:21. Such reports should be weighed rather than treated as interchangeable decoration. An occasion can explain why a question arose, but the wording of 2:21 determines how far the guidance travels. The verse speaks first to the situation that occasioned it and then to every reader who meets the same relation between humanity and become.

**The first movement of a guided life**

In 2:21, the distinction between public appearance and inward truth is especially relevant. A person may use the language of humanity while arranging the act around another purpose. Allah's judgment in 2:21 is not limited to the visible outline, yet the inner intention is not an excuse for ignoring the rights and forms that the revelation establishes. Sincerity completes obedience; it does not cancel it.

One can see the social wisdom of 2:21 in a small everyday case. When people share responsibility for humanity, uncertainty about the duty tends to fall on the person with less power or less information. A clear moral rule changes that balance. In 2:21, it tells the stronger party what must not be taken, and gives the weaker party language with which to name the wrong.

**Belief made visible in conduct**

In 2:21, the verse also sits within the Qur'an's larger refusal to separate worship from truthfulness. Prayer, remembrance, generosity, patience, and justice are different acts in 2:21, but they are trained by the same capacity to stand before Allah without constructing a false account. The statement in 2:21 strengthens that capacity by making one particular choice visible.

A reader who finds the command in 2:21 difficult should distinguish difficulty from impossibility. For 2:21, difficulty may be the place where desire, habit, or social pressure is being corrected; impossibility is a separate legal and moral question. The passage does not praise needless hardship, but it does not permit comfort to redefine humanity until the demand disappears.

**The choice hidden inside the command**

The final emphasis of 2:21 returns to responsibility. No explanation of humanity is complete if it leaves the reader with information about earlier communities but no question about present conduct. The verse 2:21 asks what can be repaired now: a promise kept, a right restored, a motive purified, or a harmful practice abandoned. That is where knowledge becomes guidance.

The choice of the word humanity in 2:21 also matters. It leaves room for the full context of 2:21 instead of allowing one translation gloss to carry every shade of meaning. Classical Arabic often places a broad word inside a narrow scene; in 2:21, the surrounding clauses determine whether the emphasis falls on an act, an intention, a condition, or a consequence. Here the reader should let the sentence do that work.

**How guidance becomes discernment**

The exegetical tradition approaches humanity from more than one angle, but its differences are more useful when their shared question is made visible. The commentators ask what the wording of 2:21 includes, what it excludes, and how it fits with the other passages of the Qur'an. A responsible reading can preserve those alternatives in 2:21 without pretending that every possibility has equal strength.

There is also a distinction between understanding humanity and using it as a label for other people. The first task belongs to learning; the second requires evidence, fairness, and awareness of one's own motives in 2:21. The verse 2:21 addresses the reader before it supplies material for judging a neighbour. Its warning becomes safer and more productive when self-examination comes first.

**The contrast drawn by the verse**

The theology and practical instruction of 2:21 therefore support one another. If Allah knows or commands become, that knowledge or command changes how a person handles time, power, money, speech, and private desire. A doctrine that never reaches those choices has not yet been read at the level demanded by 2:21.

An ordinary example makes the moral structure of 2:21 visible without replacing the revelation. Two people may perform an outwardly similar act involving humanity; one may be protecting a right, while the other is seeking praise, escaping a duty, or taking advantage of confusion. The act's surface does not settle its value. Intention, circumstance, and the rights of others complete the judgment.

The surrounding verses keep the reader from isolating humanity from the rest of life. In 2:21, revelation corrects the mind but also trains habits: how a person speaks when challenged, gives when unseen, waits when angry, and returns after error. Those habits are where this verse becomes more than information about humanity. They are the place in which assent to 2:21 is either confirmed or quietly withdrawn.

The most balanced response to 2:21 is neither to make the passage vague nor to claim more than it says. Its boundaries are part of its guidance. Within them, the verse can illuminate a new situation because its underlying moral relation remains stable: humanity is joined to become, and the person is responsible for how that relation is honoured.

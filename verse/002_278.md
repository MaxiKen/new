## Sūrah al-Baqarah 2:278

> **O believers! Fear Allah, and give up outstanding interest if you are ˹true˺ believers.**

### Expanded Commentary

**Leave What Remains — The Address to Believer-Bankers**

At this point in al-Baqarah, the wording brings belief and interest into one moral frame. The sentence should not be detached from that relationship. Its first term in 2:278 sets the subject, while the later term shows what that subject demands, permits, or exposes. The order of 2:278 gives the reader a path through the verse: begin with what Allah names, follow the consequence, and resist the temptation to turn a living address into a slogan.

The second half of v. 279 can also be translated, “If you repent, you shall have the principal of your wealth, neither wronging nor being wronged.” The debtor was still liable for the original loan, or principal of your wealth, but this verse canceled any additional fees or interest that had accrued to that loan as a result of deferring it past its original term. In this way creditors would not forfeit their wealth, and debtors would not be responsible for the increases to the original loan amount through ribā.

**Taqwā — The Creed's Own Name at the Center of Economics**

Of then take notice of a war from Allah and His Messenger Ibn ʿAbbās said, “On the Day of Resurrection it will be said to the one who partook of ribā, ‘Take up your sword for war!’” (al-Ṭabarī). Some say that this means that they have made themselves the enemies of or hostile to Allah and the Messenger (al-Qurṭubī). To practice ribā can be a cause for taking up arms against violators on the part of legitimate authority, forcing them to give up their gains from ribā or fighting them if they refuse to relinquish them or desist .

Because the verse touches belief, its moral question is not exhausted by whether an arrangement is technically possible. A transaction can be voluntary on the surface and still be shaped by pressure, concealment, or unequal knowledge. The Qur'anic concern is the trust created between people: each party must be able to understand the obligation, and neither may turn another person's need into an instrument of harm.

**The moral shape of a transaction**

A useful way to test the verse is to ask what would change if belief were taken seriously when no audience was present. The answer cannot be only a feeling, because 2:278 connects inward recognition with a discernible choice. Nor can it be only an outward display, because the choice in 2:278 loses its Qur'anic meaning when it is made for reputation rather than for Allah. The passage holds intention and action together in 2:278.

**The danger hidden in a familiar comparison**

The relation between belief and interest also protects the verse from two opposite misreadings. It should not be expanded into a claim the wording of 2:278 does not make, but neither should its brevity be used to make it weightless. Short Qur'anic statements such as 2:278 often function as hinges: they close one movement, open another, and give the next command its moral setting. The careful reader of 2:278 therefore asks what has just been established, what response is now being required, and what kind of character could respond without evasion. Those questions keep 2:278 specific while allowing its guidance to reach a new circumstance.

A reader who finds the command in 2:278 difficult should distinguish difficulty from impossibility. For 2:278, difficulty may be the place where desire, habit, or social pressure is being corrected; impossibility is a separate legal and moral question. The passage does not praise needless hardship, but it does not permit comfort to redefine belief until the demand disappears.

**Protection against harm and exploitation**

The final emphasis of 2:278 returns to responsibility. No explanation of belief is complete if it leaves the reader with information about earlier communities but no question about present conduct. The verse 2:278 asks what can be repaired now: a promise kept, a right restored, a motive purified, or a harmful practice abandoned. That is where knowledge becomes guidance.

The choice of the word belief in 2:278 also matters. It leaves room for the full context of 2:278 instead of allowing one translation gloss to carry every shade of meaning. Classical Arabic often places a broad word inside a narrow scene; in 2:278, the surrounding clauses determine whether the emphasis falls on an act, an intention, a condition, or a consequence. Here the reader should let the sentence do that work.

**What the command asks of both parties**

The exegetical tradition approaches belief from more than one angle, but its differences are more useful when their shared question is made visible. The commentators ask what the wording of 2:278 includes, what it excludes, and how it fits with the other passages of the Qur'an. A responsible reading can preserve those alternatives in 2:278 without pretending that every possibility has equal strength.

There is also a distinction between understanding belief and using it as a label for other people. The first task belongs to learning; the second requires evidence, fairness, and awareness of one's own motives in 2:278. The verse 2:278 addresses the reader before it supplies material for judging a neighbour. Its warning becomes safer and more productive when self-examination comes first.

**The law’s mercy and its discipline**

The theology and practical instruction of 2:278 therefore support one another. If Allah knows or commands interest, that knowledge or command changes how a person handles time, power, money, speech, and private desire. A doctrine that never reaches those choices has not yet been read at the level demanded by 2:278.

An ordinary example makes the moral structure of 2:278 visible without replacing the revelation. Two people may perform an outwardly similar act involving belief; one may be protecting a right, while the other is seeking praise, escaping a duty, or taking advantage of confusion. The act's surface does not settle its value. Intention, circumstance, and the rights of others complete the judgment.

**The social consequence of the ruling**

The surrounding verses keep the reader from isolating belief from the rest of life. In 2:278, revelation corrects the mind but also trains habits: how a person speaks when challenged, gives when unseen, waits when angry, and returns after error. Those habits are where this verse becomes more than information about belief. They are the place in which assent to 2:278 is either confirmed or quietly withdrawn.

The most balanced response to 2:278 is neither to make the passage vague nor to claim more than it says. Its boundaries are part of its guidance. Within them, the verse can illuminate a new situation because its underlying moral relation remains stable: belief is joined to interest, and the person is responsible for how that relation is honoured.

**When numbers become a moral question**

The wording of 2:278 gives the reader a useful order of questions about belief. What does the term belief mean in ordinary Arabic, what does the surrounding sentence restrict it to, and what consequence follows when the command is obeyed or refused in 2:278? These questions prevent a commentary on 2:278 from becoming a list of slogans. They make the act of interpretation answerable to grammar, context, and the moral end of this passage at 2:278.

The commentators sometimes preserve more than one report about the setting of 2:278. Such reports should be weighed rather than treated as interchangeable decoration. An occasion can explain why a question arose, but the wording of 2:278 determines how far the guidance travels. The verse speaks first to the situation that occasioned it and then to every reader who meets the same relation between belief and interest.

In 2:278, the distinction between public appearance and inward truth is especially relevant. A person may use the language of belief while arranging the act around another purpose. Allah's judgment in 2:278 is not limited to the visible outline, yet the inner intention is not an excuse for ignoring the rights and forms that the revelation establishes. Sincerity completes obedience; it does not cancel it.

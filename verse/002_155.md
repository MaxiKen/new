## Sūrah al-Baqarah 2:155

> **We will certainly test you with a touch of fear and famine and loss of property, life, and crops. Give good news to those who patiently endure—**

### Expanded Commentary

**The Notification of the Trial**

The force of this verse lies in the connection between certainly and fear. A reader can miss that force by isolating one phrase and treating it as a general maxim in 2:155. The verse itself is more exact. It speaks to a particular condition in the sūrah and directs attention to the kind of response that condition requires in 2:155. Reading the terms together keeps both the theology and the practical instruction in view at 2:155.

**The Trial's Measure in the General History**

Souls here refers to friends, relatives, and other loved ones (Ibn Kathīr). Fruits can refer to food and crops, but also to children, who are the fruit of the heart (al-Qurṭubī). See also 106:4, where the Quraysh are protected from hunger and fear. Elsewhere an ungrateful town is afflicted with the garment of hunger and fear (16:112). Some interpret the fear to be that of Allah, the hunger to be the fast of Ramadan, the loss of wealth to be the alms, loss of souls to be from sickness, and loss of fruits to be the death of children (Ibn Kathīr). When hunger befalls one in the way of Allah, a righteous deed is recorded (9:120).

**Tidings for the Patient Only**

A ḥadīth states, Patience comes at the first blow, meaning that the proof of a patient and steadfast heart is its state in the heat of the affliction (al-Qurṭubī), not when it is over, in which case anyone can be patient. Although some have defined it as not complaining (see also v. 153), al-Qurṭubī notes that Job tells Allah, Truly Satan has afflicted me with weariness and punishment, but of Job Allah says, Truly We found him to be steadfast (38:41–44).

The mention of certainly gives the verse a definite centre. Its meaning in 2:155 is not a general meditation on religion but an explanation of what this word requires in the situation named here. The passage at 2:155 moves between belief, action, and consequence, so a sound reading must preserve all three rather than allowing one attractive phrase to swallow the rest.

**The verse in its immediate setting**

The standard in 2:155 remains practical because it can be applied before a dispute reaches a court or a crisis reaches its worst point. One can notice the first small departure from certainly: an excuse, a concealed obligation, an unexamined desire, or a promise treated as optional. Returning at that point is easier than defending a settled habit. The warning is therefore also an opening for repair.

**The central term and its range**

The relation between certainly and fear also protects the verse from two opposite misreadings. It should not be expanded into a claim the wording of 2:155 does not make, but neither should its brevity be used to make it weightless. Short Qur'anic statements such as 2:155 often function as hinges: they close one movement, open another, and give the next command its moral setting. The careful reader of 2:155 therefore asks what has just been established, what response is now being required, and what kind of character could respond without evasion. Those questions keep 2:155 specific while allowing its guidance to reach a new circumstance.

The exegetical tradition approaches certainly from more than one angle, but its differences are more useful when their shared question is made visible. The commentators ask what the wording of 2:155 includes, what it excludes, and how it fits with the other passages of the Qur'an. A responsible reading can preserve those alternatives in 2:155 without pretending that every possibility has equal strength.

**What the early commentators preserve**

There is also a distinction between understanding certainly and using it as a label for other people. The first task belongs to learning; the second requires evidence, fairness, and awareness of one's own motives in 2:155. The verse 2:155 addresses the reader before it supplies material for judging a neighbour. Its warning becomes safer and more productive when self-examination comes first.

The theology and practical instruction of 2:155 therefore support one another. If Allah knows or commands fear, that knowledge or command changes how a person handles time, power, money, speech, and private desire. A doctrine that never reaches those choices has not yet been read at the level demanded by 2:155.

**The reasoning within the wording**

An ordinary example makes the moral structure of 2:155 visible without replacing the revelation. Two people may perform an outwardly similar act involving certainly; one may be protecting a right, while the other is seeking praise, escaping a duty, or taking advantage of confusion. The act's surface does not settle its value. Intention, circumstance, and the rights of others complete the judgment.

The surrounding verses keep the reader from isolating certainly from the rest of life. In 2:155, revelation corrects the mind but also trains habits: how a person speaks when challenged, gives when unseen, waits when angry, and returns after error. Those habits are where this verse becomes more than information about certainly. They are the place in which assent to 2:155 is either confirmed or quietly withdrawn.

**The theological question beneath the sentence**

The most balanced response to 2:155 is neither to make the passage vague nor to claim more than it says. Its boundaries are part of its guidance. Within them, the verse can illuminate a new situation because its underlying moral relation remains stable: certainly is joined to fear, and the person is responsible for how that relation is honoured.

The wording of 2:155 gives the reader a useful order of questions about certainly. What does the term certainly mean in ordinary Arabic, what does the surrounding sentence restrict it to, and what consequence follows when the command is obeyed or refused in 2:155? These questions prevent a commentary on 2:155 from becoming a list of slogans. They make the act of interpretation answerable to grammar, context, and the moral end of this passage at 2:155.

**The surrounding passage as a guide**

The commentators sometimes preserve more than one report about the setting of 2:155. Such reports should be weighed rather than treated as interchangeable decoration. An occasion can explain why a question arose, but the wording of 2:155 determines how far the guidance travels. The verse speaks first to the situation that occasioned it and then to every reader who meets the same relation between certainly and fear.

In 2:155, the distinction between public appearance and inward truth is especially relevant. A person may use the language of certainly while arranging the act around another purpose. Allah's judgment in 2:155 is not limited to the visible outline, yet the inner intention is not an excuse for ignoring the rights and forms that the revelation establishes. Sincerity completes obedience; it does not cancel it.

One can see the social wisdom of 2:155 in a small everyday case. When people share responsibility for certainly, uncertainty about the duty tends to fall on the person with less power or less information. A clear moral rule changes that balance. In 2:155, it tells the stronger party what must not be taken, and gives the weaker party language with which to name the wrong.

In 2:155, the verse also sits within the Qur'an's larger refusal to separate worship from truthfulness. Prayer, remembrance, generosity, patience, and justice are different acts in 2:155, but they are trained by the same capacity to stand before Allah without constructing a false account. The statement in 2:155 strengthens that capacity by making one particular choice visible.

A reader who finds the command in 2:155 difficult should distinguish difficulty from impossibility. For 2:155, difficulty may be the place where desire, habit, or social pressure is being corrected; impossibility is a separate legal and moral question. The passage does not praise needless hardship, but it does not permit comfort to redefine certainly until the demand disappears.

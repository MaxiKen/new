## Sūrah al-Baqarah 2:94

> **Say, ˹O Prophet,˺ “If the ˹eternal˺ Home of the Hereafter with Allah is exclusively for you ˹Israelites˺ out of all humanity, then wish for death if what you say is true!”**

### Expanded Commentary

**The Challenge of the Private Paradise**

At this point in al-Baqarah, the wording brings prophet and hereafter into one moral frame. The sentence should not be detached from that relationship. Its first term in 2:94 sets the subject, while the later term shows what that subject demands, permits, or exposes. The order of 2:94 gives the reader a path through the verse: begin with what Allah names, follow the consequence, and resist the temptation to turn a living address into a slogan.

See also 62:6: Say, O you who are Jews! If you claim that you are friends unto Allah apart from [other] people, then long for death if you are truthful. To long for death is a command seemingly at odds with the Islamic tradition, as in a ḥadīth of the Prophet: Let none of you long for death because of an evil that befalls you. If one must do something, let him say, O Allah, give me life so long as life is good for me, and make me pass away if passing away is good for me.

**The Logic of Assurance — and the Islamic Refusal of Presumption**

The reasoning behind this challenge made to the Jews is that death is the only way to the felicity that they here claim is theirs alone in the Hereafter, so that it is therefore a gate through which they should wish to pass with all due haste. The conditional supplication for life and death mentioned by the Prophet reflects, by contrast, the uncertainty of ones status in the Hereafter according to Islam. Like the presumption regarding the limited sojourn in Hell in v. 80 and 3:24, this belief in a guaranteed and exclusive entry into Paradise displays an excess of confidence.

**Between Longing and Working**

Those who truly expect no possible destination but Paradise should long for death, but their awareness of their own sins (75:14–15), however deep or undetected, stops them. According to some, this challenge could refer to a kind of mubāhalah, a practice where two sides come together to decide the truth of an issue, each declaring a certain position and each invoking something terrible on themselves if they are wrong. In this case, it would mean that death is invoked upon whomever is lying, the Muslims or the Jews .

The verse's reference to prophet widens the scene beyond the immediate reward or pain. A deed in 2:94 may appear small when measured by public attention, yet its meaning changes when it is known by Allah and carried into the final account. Warning and mercy belong together in 2:94: accountability is serious because the human being is not abandoned to meaninglessness.

**The horizon beyond the immediate scene**

A useful way to test the verse is to ask what would change if prophet were taken seriously when no audience was present. The answer cannot be only a feeling, because 2:94 connects inward recognition with a discernible choice. Nor can it be only an outward display, because the choice in 2:94 loses its Qur'anic meaning when it is made for reputation rather than for Allah. The passage holds intention and action together in 2:94.

**A divine name that governs the meaning**

The relation between prophet and hereafter also protects the verse from two opposite misreadings. It should not be expanded into a claim the wording of 2:94 does not make, but neither should its brevity be used to make it weightless. Short Qur'anic statements such as 2:94 often function as hinges: they close one movement, open another, and give the next command its moral setting. The careful reader of 2:94 therefore asks what has just been established, what response is now being required, and what kind of character could respond without evasion. Those questions keep 2:94 specific while allowing its guidance to reach a new circumstance.

One can see the social wisdom of 2:94 in a small everyday case. When people share responsibility for prophet, uncertainty about the duty tends to fall on the person with less power or less information. A clear moral rule changes that balance. In 2:94, it tells the stronger party what must not be taken, and gives the weaker party language with which to name the wrong.

**Mercy and warning held together**

In 2:94, the verse also sits within the Qur'an's larger refusal to separate worship from truthfulness. Prayer, remembrance, generosity, patience, and justice are different acts in 2:94, but they are trained by the same capacity to stand before Allah without constructing a false account. The statement in 2:94 strengthens that capacity by making one particular choice visible.

A reader who finds the command in 2:94 difficult should distinguish difficulty from impossibility. For 2:94, difficulty may be the place where desire, habit, or social pressure is being corrected; impossibility is a separate legal and moral question. The passage does not praise needless hardship, but it does not permit comfort to redefine prophet until the demand disappears.

**The deed before its final account**

The final emphasis of 2:94 returns to responsibility. No explanation of prophet is complete if it leaves the reader with information about earlier communities but no question about present conduct. The verse 2:94 asks what can be repaired now: a promise kept, a right restored, a motive purified, or a harmful practice abandoned. That is where knowledge becomes guidance.

The choice of the word prophet in 2:94 also matters. It leaves room for the full context of 2:94 instead of allowing one translation gloss to carry every shade of meaning. Classical Arabic often places a broad word inside a narrow scene; in 2:94, the surrounding clauses determine whether the emphasis falls on an act, an intention, a condition, or a consequence. Here the reader should let the sentence do that work.

**Why the promise or threat is just**

The exegetical tradition approaches prophet from more than one angle, but its differences are more useful when their shared question is made visible. The commentators ask what the wording of 2:94 includes, what it excludes, and how it fits with the other passages of the Qur'an. A responsible reading can preserve those alternatives in 2:94 without pretending that every possibility has equal strength.

There is also a distinction between understanding prophet and using it as a label for other people. The first task belongs to learning; the second requires evidence, fairness, and awareness of one's own motives in 2:94. The verse 2:94 addresses the reader before it supplies material for judging a neighbour. Its warning becomes safer and more productive when self-examination comes first.

**The Hereafter in the argument**

The theology and practical instruction of 2:94 therefore support one another. If Allah knows or commands hereafter, that knowledge or command changes how a person handles time, power, money, speech, and private desire. A doctrine that never reaches those choices has not yet been read at the level demanded by 2:94.

An ordinary example makes the moral structure of 2:94 visible without replacing the revelation. Two people may perform an outwardly similar act involving prophet; one may be protecting a right, while the other is seeking praise, escaping a duty, or taking advantage of confusion. The act's surface does not settle its value. Intention, circumstance, and the rights of others complete the judgment.

The surrounding verses keep the reader from isolating prophet from the rest of life. In 2:94, revelation corrects the mind but also trains habits: how a person speaks when challenged, gives when unseen, waits when angry, and returns after error. Those habits are where this verse becomes more than information about prophet. They are the place in which assent to 2:94 is either confirmed or quietly withdrawn.

The most balanced response to 2:94 is neither to make the passage vague nor to claim more than it says. Its boundaries are part of its guidance. Within them, the verse can illuminate a new situation because its underlying moral relation remains stable: prophet is joined to hereafter, and the person is responsible for how that relation is honoured.

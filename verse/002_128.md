## Sūrah al-Baqarah 2:128

> **Our Lord! Make us both ˹fully˺ submit to You and from our descendants a nation that will submit to you. Show us our rituals, and turn to us in grace. You are truly the Accepter of Repentance, Most Merciful.**

### Expanded Commentary

**The Builders' Prayer for Submission**

At this point in al-Baqarah, the wording brings both and descendants into one moral frame. The sentence should not be detached from that relationship. Its first term in 2:128 sets the subject, while the later term shows what that subject demands, permits, or exposes. The order of 2:128 gives the reader a path through the verse: begin with what Allah names, follow the consequence, and resist the temptation to turn a living address into a slogan.

**Muslim as Universal, Not Tribal**

Submit and submitting render muslim, the participle of a transitive verb (lit. submitter to Allah in this context), which is tied to the submission (islām) mentioned in v. 132, both of which are among those verses that testify to the universal meaning of islām, which existed before the revelation of the Qur'an. The rites (manāsik) are often taken to be the rituals of the ḥajj (rites also mentioned in connection with the ḥajj in v. 200), or sacrifice, or simply worship in general (al-Ṭabarī); this general meaning is found in passages such as For every community We have appointed a rite (22:34, 67), referring to their respective rituals and practices.

**Rites Shown by Allah; Return Relented By Allah**

To relent, here as elsewhere, suggests the reciprocity in the act of tawbah (see the essay Obstacles Faced in the Translation of the Qur'an), in which Allah turns toward human beings in compassion, while they turn to Him in repentance (see also 2:37c). Some commentators explain that the prayer for their progeny, rather than for all beings, reflects a general principle that ones kin have first right to ones kindness and concern, as in 66:6: O you who believe! Shield yourselves and your families from a Fire whose fuel is mankind and stones (al-Rāzī).

It is connected with the prayer for a messenger to be sent in v. 129 (al-Ṭabarī).

The verse's reference to both widens the scene beyond the immediate reward or pain. A deed in 2:128 may appear small when measured by public attention, yet its meaning changes when it is known by Allah and carried into the final account. Warning and mercy belong together in 2:128: accountability is serious because the human being is not abandoned to meaninglessness.

**The horizon beyond the immediate scene**

A useful way to test the verse is to ask what would change if both were taken seriously when no audience was present. The answer cannot be only a feeling, because 2:128 connects inward recognition with a discernible choice. Nor can it be only an outward display, because the choice in 2:128 loses its Qur'anic meaning when it is made for reputation rather than for Allah. The passage holds intention and action together in 2:128.

**A divine name that governs the meaning**

The relation between both and descendants also protects the verse from two opposite misreadings. It should not be expanded into a claim the wording of 2:128 does not make, but neither should its brevity be used to make it weightless. Short Qur'anic statements such as 2:128 often function as hinges: they close one movement, open another, and give the next command its moral setting. The careful reader of 2:128 therefore asks what has just been established, what response is now being required, and what kind of character could respond without evasion. Those questions keep 2:128 specific while allowing its guidance to reach a new circumstance.

There is also a distinction between understanding both and using it as a label for other people. The first task belongs to learning; the second requires evidence, fairness, and awareness of one's own motives in 2:128. The verse 2:128 addresses the reader before it supplies material for judging a neighbour. Its warning becomes safer and more productive when self-examination comes first.

**Mercy and warning held together**

The theology and practical instruction of 2:128 therefore support one another. If Allah knows or commands descendants, that knowledge or command changes how a person handles time, power, money, speech, and private desire. A doctrine that never reaches those choices has not yet been read at the level demanded by 2:128.

An ordinary example makes the moral structure of 2:128 visible without replacing the revelation. Two people may perform an outwardly similar act involving both; one may be protecting a right, while the other is seeking praise, escaping a duty, or taking advantage of confusion. The act's surface does not settle its value. Intention, circumstance, and the rights of others complete the judgment.

**The deed before its final account**

The surrounding verses keep the reader from isolating both from the rest of life. In 2:128, revelation corrects the mind but also trains habits: how a person speaks when challenged, gives when unseen, waits when angry, and returns after error. Those habits are where this verse becomes more than information about both. They are the place in which assent to 2:128 is either confirmed or quietly withdrawn.

The most balanced response to 2:128 is neither to make the passage vague nor to claim more than it says. Its boundaries are part of its guidance. Within them, the verse can illuminate a new situation because its underlying moral relation remains stable: both is joined to descendants, and the person is responsible for how that relation is honoured.

**Why the promise or threat is just**

The wording of 2:128 gives the reader a useful order of questions about both. What does the term both mean in ordinary Arabic, what does the surrounding sentence restrict it to, and what consequence follows when the command is obeyed or refused in 2:128? These questions prevent a commentary on 2:128 from becoming a list of slogans. They make the act of interpretation answerable to grammar, context, and the moral end of this passage at 2:128.

The commentators sometimes preserve more than one report about the setting of 2:128. Such reports should be weighed rather than treated as interchangeable decoration. An occasion can explain why a question arose, but the wording of 2:128 determines how far the guidance travels. The verse speaks first to the situation that occasioned it and then to every reader who meets the same relation between both and descendants.

**The Hereafter in the argument**

In 2:128, the distinction between public appearance and inward truth is especially relevant. A person may use the language of both while arranging the act around another purpose. Allah's judgment in 2:128 is not limited to the visible outline, yet the inner intention is not an excuse for ignoring the rights and forms that the revelation establishes. Sincerity completes obedience; it does not cancel it.

One can see the social wisdom of 2:128 in a small everyday case. When people share responsibility for both, uncertainty about the duty tends to fall on the person with less power or less information. A clear moral rule changes that balance. In 2:128, it tells the stronger party what must not be taken, and gives the weaker party language with which to name the wrong.

In 2:128, the verse also sits within the Qur'an's larger refusal to separate worship from truthfulness. Prayer, remembrance, generosity, patience, and justice are different acts in 2:128, but they are trained by the same capacity to stand before Allah without constructing a false account. The statement in 2:128 strengthens that capacity by making one particular choice visible.

A reader who finds the command in 2:128 difficult should distinguish difficulty from impossibility. For 2:128, difficulty may be the place where desire, habit, or social pressure is being corrected; impossibility is a separate legal and moral question. The passage does not praise needless hardship, but it does not permit comfort to redefine both until the demand disappears.

The final emphasis of 2:128 returns to responsibility. No explanation of both is complete if it leaves the reader with information about earlier communities but no question about present conduct. The verse 2:128 asks what can be repaired now: a promise kept, a right restored, a motive purified, or a harmful practice abandoned. That is where knowledge becomes guidance.

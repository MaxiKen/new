## Sūrah al-Baqarah 2:204

> **There are some ˹hypocrites˺ who impress you with their views regarding worldly affairs and openly call upon Allah to witness what is in their hearts, yet they are your worst adversaries.**

### Expanded Commentary

**Speech That Pleases, Heart That Assails**

At this point in al-Baqarah, the wording brings hypocrites and regarding into one moral frame. The sentence should not be detached from that relationship. Its first term in 2:204 sets the subject, while the later term shows what that subject demands, permits, or exposes. The order of 2:204 gives the reader a path through the verse: begin with what Allah names, follow the consequence, and resist the temptation to turn a living address into a slogan.

**Plausible Occasions, Universal Teaching**

Although many mention specific incidents that may have been the occasion for vv. 204–7, most prefer to see these verses as universal teachings on the nature of hypocrisy . Some commentators mention in this context a certain Akhnas ibn Shurayq al-Thaqafī, who feigned interest in embracing Islam and even went so far as to say, “Allah knows what is in my heart” (al-Ṭabarī). The verb rendered impresses can also mean “to please,” “to evoke wonder,” “to cause to marvel,” or “to delight.” Fiercest of adversaries can also mean “most crooked in disputation” (al-Ṭabarī). Other commentators mention as the occasion for the revelation of this verse an incident in which some Makkans pretended to want to learn about Islam and asked the Prophet to send a delegation of learned men to teach them. When the delegation was sent, it was ambushed and murdered on the road to Makkah by the Quraysh .

**The Believer's Ear Disciplined**

The language of hypocrites places memory and responsibility alongside material exchange. People forget, die, disagree, and understand conversations differently; a careful record does not accuse every party of dishonesty, but protects the honest party from the consequences of uncertainty. The verse therefore treats clarity as a form of mercy, not as a sign that trust has disappeared.

A useful way to test the verse is to ask what would change if hypocrites were taken seriously when no audience was present. The answer cannot be only a feeling, because 2:204 connects inward recognition with a discernible choice. Nor can it be only an outward display, because the choice in 2:204 loses its Qur'anic meaning when it is made for reputation rather than for Allah. The passage holds intention and action together in 2:204.

**Writing trust into a transaction**

The relation between hypocrites and regarding also protects the verse from two opposite misreadings. It should not be expanded into a claim the wording of 2:204 does not make, but neither should its brevity be used to make it weightless. Short Qur'anic statements such as 2:204 often function as hinges: they close one movement, open another, and give the next command its moral setting. The careful reader of 2:204 therefore asks what has just been established, what response is now being required, and what kind of character could respond without evasion. Those questions keep 2:204 specific while allowing its guidance to reach a new circumstance.

**Why clarity protects both sides**

The commentators sometimes preserve more than one report about the setting of 2:204. Such reports should be weighed rather than treated as interchangeable decoration. An occasion can explain why a question arose, but the wording of 2:204 determines how far the guidance travels. The verse speaks first to the situation that occasioned it and then to every reader who meets the same relation between hypocrites and regarding.

In 2:204, the distinction between public appearance and inward truth is especially relevant. A person may use the language of hypocrites while arranging the act around another purpose. Allah's judgment in 2:204 is not limited to the visible outline, yet the inner intention is not an excuse for ignoring the rights and forms that the revelation establishes. Sincerity completes obedience; it does not cancel it.

**The vulnerable party in the wording**

One can see the social wisdom of 2:204 in a small everyday case. When people share responsibility for hypocrites, uncertainty about the duty tends to fall on the person with less power or less information. A clear moral rule changes that balance. In 2:204, it tells the stronger party what must not be taken, and gives the weaker party language with which to name the wrong.

In 2:204, the verse also sits within the Qur'an's larger refusal to separate worship from truthfulness. Prayer, remembrance, generosity, patience, and justice are different acts in 2:204, but they are trained by the same capacity to stand before Allah without constructing a false account. The statement in 2:204 strengthens that capacity by making one particular choice visible.

**Witnesses, records, and memory**

A reader who finds the command in 2:204 difficult should distinguish difficulty from impossibility. For 2:204, difficulty may be the place where desire, habit, or social pressure is being corrected; impossibility is a separate legal and moral question. The passage does not praise needless hardship, but it does not permit comfort to redefine hypocrites until the demand disappears.

The final emphasis of 2:204 returns to responsibility. No explanation of hypocrites is complete if it leaves the reader with information about earlier communities but no question about present conduct. The verse 2:204 asks what can be repaired now: a promise kept, a right restored, a motive purified, or a harmful practice abandoned. That is where knowledge becomes guidance.

**The ethics of consent**

The choice of the word hypocrites in 2:204 also matters. It leaves room for the full context of 2:204 instead of allowing one translation gloss to carry every shade of meaning. Classical Arabic often places a broad word inside a narrow scene; in 2:204, the surrounding clauses determine whether the emphasis falls on an act, an intention, a condition, or a consequence. Here the reader should let the sentence do that work.

The exegetical tradition approaches hypocrites from more than one angle, but its differences are more useful when their shared question is made visible. The commentators ask what the wording of 2:204 includes, what it excludes, and how it fits with the other passages of the Qur'an. A responsible reading can preserve those alternatives in 2:204 without pretending that every possibility has equal strength.

**A practical rule against later conflict**

There is also a distinction between understanding hypocrites and using it as a label for other people. The first task belongs to learning; the second requires evidence, fairness, and awareness of one's own motives in 2:204. The verse 2:204 addresses the reader before it supplies material for judging a neighbour. Its warning becomes safer and more productive when self-examination comes first.

The theology and practical instruction of 2:204 therefore support one another. If Allah knows or commands regarding, that knowledge or command changes how a person handles time, power, money, speech, and private desire. A doctrine that never reaches those choices has not yet been read at the level demanded by 2:204.

An ordinary example makes the moral structure of 2:204 visible without replacing the revelation. Two people may perform an outwardly similar act involving hypocrites; one may be protecting a right, while the other is seeking praise, escaping a duty, or taking advantage of confusion. The act's surface does not settle its value. Intention, circumstance, and the rights of others complete the judgment.

The surrounding verses keep the reader from isolating hypocrites from the rest of life. In 2:204, revelation corrects the mind but also trains habits: how a person speaks when challenged, gives when unseen, waits when angry, and returns after error. Those habits are where this verse becomes more than information about hypocrites. They are the place in which assent to 2:204 is either confirmed or quietly withdrawn.

The most balanced response to 2:204 is neither to make the passage vague nor to claim more than it says. Its boundaries are part of its guidance. Within them, the verse can illuminate a new situation because its underlying moral relation remains stable: hypocrites is joined to regarding, and the person is responsible for how that relation is honoured.

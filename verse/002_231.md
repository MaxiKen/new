## Sūrah al-Baqarah 2:231

> **When you divorce women and they have ˹almost˺ reached the end of their waiting period, either retain them honourably or let them go honourably. But do not retain them ˹only˺ to harm them ˹or˺ to take advantage ˹of them˺. Whoever does that surely wrongs his own soul. Do not take Allah’s revelations lightly. Remember Allah’s favours upon you as well as the Book and wisdom He has sent down for your guidance. Be mindful of Allah, and know that Allah has ˹perfect˺ knowledge of all things.**

### Expanded Commentary

**"Do Not Keep Them to Harm Them"**

The force of this verse lies in the connection between divorce and reached. A reader can miss that force by isolating one phrase and treating it as a general maxim in 2:231. The verse itself is more exact. It speaks to a particular condition in the sūrah and directs attention to the kind of response that condition requires in 2:231. Reading the terms together keeps both the theology and the practical instruction in view at 2:231.

**"Take Not Allah's Signs in Mockery"**

Keep them so as to cause harm refers to the practice of declaring divorce, then taking ones wife back just before the waiting period is over, and repeating this action in order to harm her (Ṭ; see commentary on 2:229–30). And thus transgress could also be read in order to transgress. In this context, to take Allahs signs in mockery means to declare divorce and then say, I was only playing, or to take a similar attitude with any matter of marriage and divorce (al-Ṭabarī). On the idea of taking Allahs signs in mockery, also see 5:57–58; 31:6; 45:9, 35. The Book and Wisdom are mentioned together also in v. 129; 3:48; 4:113; 5:110; 45:16; 62:2.

**Remember, Fear, Know — The Triune Seal**

The reference to divorce places a human being who may be dependent, exposed, or unable to defend a claim at the centre of the discussion. Responsibility here is not merely a feeling of affection. It has a form that can be recognized, a limit that can be enforced, and a purpose: to prevent the strong from making the weak carry the cost of decisions they did not control.

The standard in 2:231 remains practical because it can be applied before a dispute reaches a court or a crisis reaches its worst point. One can notice the first small departure from divorce: an excuse, a concealed obligation, an unexamined desire, or a promise treated as optional. Returning at that point is easier than defending a settled habit. The warning is therefore also an opening for repair.

**A household placed under moral care**

The relation between divorce and reached also protects the verse from two opposite misreadings. It should not be expanded into a claim the wording of 2:231 does not make, but neither should its brevity be used to make it weightless. Short Qur'anic statements such as 2:231 often function as hinges: they close one movement, open another, and give the next command its moral setting. The careful reader of 2:231 therefore asks what has just been established, what response is now being required, and what kind of character could respond without evasion. Those questions keep 2:231 specific while allowing its guidance to reach a new circumstance.

**The rights concealed in the wording**

The wording of 2:231 gives the reader a useful order of questions about divorce. What does the term divorce mean in ordinary Arabic, what does the surrounding sentence restrict it to, and what consequence follows when the command is obeyed or refused in 2:231? These questions prevent a commentary on 2:231 from becoming a list of slogans. They make the act of interpretation answerable to grammar, context, and the moral end of this passage at 2:231.

The commentators sometimes preserve more than one report about the setting of 2:231. Such reports should be weighed rather than treated as interchangeable decoration. An occasion can explain why a question arose, but the wording of 2:231 determines how far the guidance travels. The verse speaks first to the situation that occasioned it and then to every reader who meets the same relation between divorce and reached.

**Time, vulnerability, and restraint**

In 2:231, the distinction between public appearance and inward truth is especially relevant. A person may use the language of divorce while arranging the act around another purpose. Allah's judgment in 2:231 is not limited to the visible outline, yet the inner intention is not an excuse for ignoring the rights and forms that the revelation establishes. Sincerity completes obedience; it does not cancel it.

One can see the social wisdom of 2:231 in a small everyday case. When people share responsibility for divorce, uncertainty about the duty tends to fall on the person with less power or less information. A clear moral rule changes that balance. In 2:231, it tells the stronger party what must not be taken, and gives the weaker party language with which to name the wrong.

**The difference between power and justice**

In 2:231, the verse also sits within the Qur'an's larger refusal to separate worship from truthfulness. Prayer, remembrance, generosity, patience, and justice are different acts in 2:231, but they are trained by the same capacity to stand before Allah without constructing a false account. The statement in 2:231 strengthens that capacity by making one particular choice visible.

A reader who finds the command in 2:231 difficult should distinguish difficulty from impossibility. For 2:231, difficulty may be the place where desire, habit, or social pressure is being corrected; impossibility is a separate legal and moral question. The passage does not praise needless hardship, but it does not permit comfort to redefine divorce until the demand disappears.

**How the jurists read the command**

The final emphasis of 2:231 returns to responsibility. No explanation of divorce is complete if it leaves the reader with information about earlier communities but no question about present conduct. The verse 2:231 asks what can be repaired now: a promise kept, a right restored, a motive purified, or a harmful practice abandoned. That is where knowledge becomes guidance.

The choice of the word divorce in 2:231 also matters. It leaves room for the full context of 2:231 instead of allowing one translation gloss to carry every shade of meaning. Classical Arabic often places a broad word inside a narrow scene; in 2:231, the surrounding clauses determine whether the emphasis falls on an act, an intention, a condition, or a consequence. Here the reader should let the sentence do that work.

**Protection from private harm**

The exegetical tradition approaches divorce from more than one angle, but its differences are more useful when their shared question is made visible. The commentators ask what the wording of 2:231 includes, what it excludes, and how it fits with the other passages of the Qur'an. A responsible reading can preserve those alternatives in 2:231 without pretending that every possibility has equal strength.

There is also a distinction between understanding divorce and using it as a label for other people. The first task belongs to learning; the second requires evidence, fairness, and awareness of one's own motives in 2:231. The verse 2:231 addresses the reader before it supplies material for judging a neighbour. Its warning becomes safer and more productive when self-examination comes first.

The theology and practical instruction of 2:231 therefore support one another. If Allah knows or commands reached, that knowledge or command changes how a person handles time, power, money, speech, and private desire. A doctrine that never reaches those choices has not yet been read at the level demanded by 2:231.

An ordinary example makes the moral structure of 2:231 visible without replacing the revelation. Two people may perform an outwardly similar act involving divorce; one may be protecting a right, while the other is seeking praise, escaping a duty, or taking advantage of confusion. The act's surface does not settle its value. Intention, circumstance, and the rights of others complete the judgment.

The surrounding verses keep the reader from isolating divorce from the rest of life. In 2:231, revelation corrects the mind but also trains habits: how a person speaks when challenged, gives when unseen, waits when angry, and returns after error. Those habits are where this verse becomes more than information about divorce. They are the place in which assent to 2:231 is either confirmed or quietly withdrawn.

## Sūrah al-Baqarah 2:119

> **We have surely sent you with the truth ˹O Prophet˺ as a deliverer of good news and a warner. And you will not be accountable for the residents of the Hellfire.**

### Expanded Commentary

**Sent With the Truth: the Prophet's Charter**

The force of this verse lies in the connection between sent and deliverer. A reader can miss that force by isolating one phrase and treating it as a general maxim in 2:119. The verse itself is more exact. It speaks to a particular condition in the sūrah and directs attention to the kind of response that condition requires in 2:119. Reading the terms together keeps both the theology and the practical instruction in view at 2:119.

**"Thou Wilt Not Be Questioned About the Inhabitants of Hellfire"**

Bearer of glad tidings (bashīr) and warner (nadhīr) are complementary epithets used to describe the function of the Prophet (e.g., 6:48; 17:105; 34:28) and other prophets throughout the Qur'an. For a fuller discussion, see 4:165c. Thou wilt not be questioned about can also carry the sense of Thou art not answerable/responsible for. An alternate reading would make the verb an imperative, And ask not about, or nominative, And thou askest not about, although the first reading is preferred by most and is in keeping with many other instances where the Prophet is absolved of responsibility for the error of others, such as 2:272: Thou art not tasked with their guidance; 35:8: Let not thy soul be expended in regrets over them; and 88:22: Thou are not a warder over them.

**The Burden Lifted From Every Caller**

The mention of sent gives the verse a definite centre. Its meaning in 2:119 is not a general meditation on religion but an explanation of what this word requires in the situation named here. The passage at 2:119 moves between belief, action, and consequence, so a sound reading must preserve all three rather than allowing one attractive phrase to swallow the rest.

The standard in 2:119 remains practical because it can be applied before a dispute reaches a court or a crisis reaches its worst point. One can notice the first small departure from sent: an excuse, a concealed obligation, an unexamined desire, or a promise treated as optional. Returning at that point is easier than defending a settled habit. The warning is therefore also an opening for repair.

**The verse in its immediate setting**

The relation between sent and deliverer also protects the verse from two opposite misreadings. It should not be expanded into a claim the wording of 2:119 does not make, but neither should its brevity be used to make it weightless. Short Qur'anic statements such as 2:119 often function as hinges: they close one movement, open another, and give the next command its moral setting. The careful reader of 2:119 therefore asks what has just been established, what response is now being required, and what kind of character could respond without evasion. Those questions keep 2:119 specific while allowing its guidance to reach a new circumstance.

**The central term and its range**

The wording of 2:119 gives the reader a useful order of questions about sent. What does the term sent mean in ordinary Arabic, what does the surrounding sentence restrict it to, and what consequence follows when the command is obeyed or refused in 2:119? These questions prevent a commentary on 2:119 from becoming a list of slogans. They make the act of interpretation answerable to grammar, context, and the moral end of this passage at 2:119.

The commentators sometimes preserve more than one report about the setting of 2:119. Such reports should be weighed rather than treated as interchangeable decoration. An occasion can explain why a question arose, but the wording of 2:119 determines how far the guidance travels. The verse speaks first to the situation that occasioned it and then to every reader who meets the same relation between sent and deliverer.

**What the early commentators preserve**

In 2:119, the distinction between public appearance and inward truth is especially relevant. A person may use the language of sent while arranging the act around another purpose. Allah's judgment in 2:119 is not limited to the visible outline, yet the inner intention is not an excuse for ignoring the rights and forms that the revelation establishes. Sincerity completes obedience; it does not cancel it.

One can see the social wisdom of 2:119 in a small everyday case. When people share responsibility for sent, uncertainty about the duty tends to fall on the person with less power or less information. A clear moral rule changes that balance. In 2:119, it tells the stronger party what must not be taken, and gives the weaker party language with which to name the wrong.

**The reasoning within the wording**

In 2:119, the verse also sits within the Qur'an's larger refusal to separate worship from truthfulness. Prayer, remembrance, generosity, patience, and justice are different acts in 2:119, but they are trained by the same capacity to stand before Allah without constructing a false account. The statement in 2:119 strengthens that capacity by making one particular choice visible.

A reader who finds the command in 2:119 difficult should distinguish difficulty from impossibility. For 2:119, difficulty may be the place where desire, habit, or social pressure is being corrected; impossibility is a separate legal and moral question. The passage does not praise needless hardship, but it does not permit comfort to redefine sent until the demand disappears.

**The theological question beneath the sentence**

The final emphasis of 2:119 returns to responsibility. No explanation of sent is complete if it leaves the reader with information about earlier communities but no question about present conduct. The verse 2:119 asks what can be repaired now: a promise kept, a right restored, a motive purified, or a harmful practice abandoned. That is where knowledge becomes guidance.

The choice of the word sent in 2:119 also matters. It leaves room for the full context of 2:119 instead of allowing one translation gloss to carry every shade of meaning. Classical Arabic often places a broad word inside a narrow scene; in 2:119, the surrounding clauses determine whether the emphasis falls on an act, an intention, a condition, or a consequence. Here the reader should let the sentence do that work.

**The surrounding passage as a guide**

The exegetical tradition approaches sent from more than one angle, but its differences are more useful when their shared question is made visible. The commentators ask what the wording of 2:119 includes, what it excludes, and how it fits with the other passages of the Qur'an. A responsible reading can preserve those alternatives in 2:119 without pretending that every possibility has equal strength.

There is also a distinction between understanding sent and using it as a label for other people. The first task belongs to learning; the second requires evidence, fairness, and awareness of one's own motives in 2:119. The verse 2:119 addresses the reader before it supplies material for judging a neighbour. Its warning becomes safer and more productive when self-examination comes first.

The theology and practical instruction of 2:119 therefore support one another. If Allah knows or commands deliverer, that knowledge or command changes how a person handles time, power, money, speech, and private desire. A doctrine that never reaches those choices has not yet been read at the level demanded by 2:119.

An ordinary example makes the moral structure of 2:119 visible without replacing the revelation. Two people may perform an outwardly similar act involving sent; one may be protecting a right, while the other is seeking praise, escaping a duty, or taking advantage of confusion. The act's surface does not settle its value. Intention, circumstance, and the rights of others complete the judgment.

The surrounding verses keep the reader from isolating sent from the rest of life. In 2:119, revelation corrects the mind but also trains habits: how a person speaks when challenged, gives when unseen, waits when angry, and returns after error. Those habits are where this verse becomes more than information about sent. They are the place in which assent to 2:119 is either confirmed or quietly withdrawn.

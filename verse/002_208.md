## Sūrah al-Baqarah 2:208

> **O believers! Enter into Islam wholeheartedly and do not follow Satan’s footsteps. Surely he is your sworn enemy.**

### Expanded Commentary

**The Summons of the Silm — Completely**

At this point in al-Baqarah, the wording brings belief and wholeheartedly into one moral frame. The sentence should not be detached from that relationship. Its first term in 2:208 sets the subject, while the later term shows what that subject demands, permits, or exposes. The order of 2:208 gives the reader a path through the verse: begin with what Allah names, follow the consequence, and resist the temptation to turn a living address into a slogan.

**The Possible Background and the Universal Command**

Many interpret peace (silm) here to mean submission (islām; R, Ṭ), but acknowledge that this is not the words literal meaning. Others interpret it to mean enter into obedience (Ibn Kathīr). All together renders kāffatan, an adverb that can also be read as altogether (al-Rāzī), in which case the command would mean enter into all of it, not all of you enter it. Some report that this verse was revealed in connection with a group of Jewish converts who still desired to observe the Sabbath and other specifically Jewish rituals that did not explicitly contradict Islamic teachings (e.g., by continuing to abstain from certain foods that Muslims are permitted to eat).

**"Follow Not the Footsteps of Satan" — The Second Panel**

One possible interpretation is that, if it does mean islām and is addressed to Muslims, it means that Muslims should immerse themselves more deeply and completely in islām and not omit any of its rites and commands (al-Rāzī). Others think that it is addressed to Jews and Christians (i.e., believers in Moses and Jesus), urging them to convert en masse to Islam or to embrace the totality of Islam (al-Qurṭubī). The verse can also suggest that when people enter into peace, they should do so together and not be factionalized.

Follow not the footsteps of Satan also appears in v. 168; 6:142; 24:21.

The mention of belief must be read with the restraints carried by the same revelation. Permission to resist wrongdoing does not erase the prohibition of wrongdoing. The verse therefore asks the community to hold two truths together: injustice may have to be confronted, and the means used in confronting it remain subject to Allah's judgment.

**Entering Together, as One House**

A useful way to test the verse is to ask what would change if belief were taken seriously when no audience was present. The answer cannot be only a feeling, because 2:208 connects inward recognition with a discernible choice. Nor can it be only an outward display, because the choice in 2:208 loses its Qur'anic meaning when it is made for reputation rather than for Allah. The passage holds intention and action together in 2:208.

**The permission is not a blank cheque**

The relation between belief and wholeheartedly also protects the verse from two opposite misreadings. It should not be expanded into a claim the wording of 2:208 does not make, but neither should its brevity be used to make it weightless. Short Qur'anic statements such as 2:208 often function as hinges: they close one movement, open another, and give the next command its moral setting. The careful reader of 2:208 therefore asks what has just been established, what response is now being required, and what kind of character could respond without evasion. Those questions keep 2:208 specific while allowing its guidance to reach a new circumstance.

A reader who finds the command in 2:208 difficult should distinguish difficulty from impossibility. For 2:208, difficulty may be the place where desire, habit, or social pressure is being corrected; impossibility is a separate legal and moral question. The passage does not praise needless hardship, but it does not permit comfort to redefine belief until the demand disappears.

**The difference between defence and aggression**

The final emphasis of 2:208 returns to responsibility. No explanation of belief is complete if it leaves the reader with information about earlier communities but no question about present conduct. The verse 2:208 asks what can be repaired now: a promise kept, a right restored, a motive purified, or a harmful practice abandoned. That is where knowledge becomes guidance.

The choice of the word belief in 2:208 also matters. It leaves room for the full context of 2:208 instead of allowing one translation gloss to carry every shade of meaning. Classical Arabic often places a broad word inside a narrow scene; in 2:208, the surrounding clauses determine whether the emphasis falls on an act, an intention, a condition, or a consequence. Here the reader should let the sentence do that work.

**The human cost carried by the wording**

The exegetical tradition approaches belief from more than one angle, but its differences are more useful when their shared question is made visible. The commentators ask what the wording of 2:208 includes, what it excludes, and how it fits with the other passages of the Qur'an. A responsible reading can preserve those alternatives in 2:208 without pretending that every possibility has equal strength.

There is also a distinction between understanding belief and using it as a label for other people. The first task belongs to learning; the second requires evidence, fairness, and awareness of one's own motives in 2:208. The verse 2:208 addresses the reader before it supplies material for judging a neighbour. Its warning becomes safer and more productive when self-examination comes first.

**Limits that remain binding in conflict**

The theology and practical instruction of 2:208 therefore support one another. If Allah knows or commands wholeheartedly, that knowledge or command changes how a person handles time, power, money, speech, and private desire. A doctrine that never reaches those choices has not yet been read at the level demanded by 2:208.

An ordinary example makes the moral structure of 2:208 visible without replacing the revelation. Two people may perform an outwardly similar act involving belief; one may be protecting a right, while the other is seeking praise, escaping a duty, or taking advantage of confusion. The act's surface does not settle its value. Intention, circumstance, and the rights of others complete the judgment.

**How the early community understood it**

The surrounding verses keep the reader from isolating belief from the rest of life. In 2:208, revelation corrects the mind but also trains habits: how a person speaks when challenged, gives when unseen, waits when angry, and returns after error. Those habits are where this verse becomes more than information about belief. They are the place in which assent to 2:208 is either confirmed or quietly withdrawn.

The most balanced response to 2:208 is neither to make the passage vague nor to claim more than it says. Its boundaries are part of its guidance. Within them, the verse can illuminate a new situation because its underlying moral relation remains stable: belief is joined to wholeheartedly, and the person is responsible for how that relation is honoured.

The wording of 2:208 gives the reader a useful order of questions about belief. What does the term belief mean in ordinary Arabic, what does the surrounding sentence restrict it to, and what consequence follows when the command is obeyed or refused in 2:208? These questions prevent a commentary on 2:208 from becoming a list of slogans. They make the act of interpretation answerable to grammar, context, and the moral end of this passage at 2:208.

The commentators sometimes preserve more than one report about the setting of 2:208. Such reports should be weighed rather than treated as interchangeable decoration. An occasion can explain why a question arose, but the wording of 2:208 determines how far the guidance travels. The verse speaks first to the situation that occasioned it and then to every reader who meets the same relation between belief and wholeheartedly.

In 2:208, the distinction between public appearance and inward truth is especially relevant. A person may use the language of belief while arranging the act around another purpose. Allah's judgment in 2:208 is not limited to the visible outline, yet the inner intention is not an excuse for ignoring the rights and forms that the revelation establishes. Sincerity completes obedience; it does not cancel it.

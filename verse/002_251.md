## Sūrah al-Baqarah 2:251

> **So they defeated them by Allah’s Will, and David killed Goliath. And Allah blessed David with kingship and wisdom and taught him what He willed. Had Allah not repelled a group of people by ˹the might of˺ another, corruption would have dominated the earth, but Allah is Gracious to all.**

### Expanded Commentary

**By Allah's Leave — David and the Giant**

The force of this verse lies in the connection between defeated and killed. A reader can miss that force by isolating one phrase and treating it as a general maxim in 2:251. The verse itself is more exact. It speaks to a particular condition in the sūrah and directs attention to the kind of response that condition requires in 2:251. Reading the terms together keeps both the theology and the practical instruction in view at 2:251.

The Qur'anic account here assumes a general knowledge of the story of Saul, David, and Goliath: though a young boy, David is appointed by Saul to face Goliath as the Israelites champion and fells the huge warrior with a stone from his sling (cf. 1 Samuel 17). He was given wisdom, meaning that, as a major prophet in the Islamic tradition, he inherited the prophethood of Samuel, and sovereignty, meaning he inherited the kingship of Saul (al-Ṭabarī). For further description of David and his prophethood and reign, see 38:17–29.

**"Were It Not for Allah's Repelling People"**

Were it not for Allahs repelling people, some by means of others is similar to the message of 22:40, where the destruction of houses of worship is thwarted by this repelling. Here it is the corruption of the earth as such that is averted through this repelling of some by others. Commentators state that Allah uses good people to repel the effect of evil people, but offer different interpretations as to who precisely repels whom. Some say this refers to the Substitutes (abdāl), human beings who are inwardly members of a spiritual hierarchy through which Allah dispenses grace and protection to the world; they are forty in number, and when one dies, that one is replaced by another person (al-Qurṭubī).

**The permission is not a blank cheque**

Others interpret it to mean that the prayerful and the pious are used to repel those who do not pray and are impious, whose sins would otherwise destroy humanity, or that the obedient repel the evil of the disobedient (al-Ṭabarī). In a ḥadīth the Prophet says, Allah repels the punishment of the one who does not pray by means of the one who prays, and of the one who does not give alms by means of the one who gives alms, and of the one who does not fast by means of the one who fasts, and of the one who does not make the ḥajj by means of the one who makes ḥajj, and of the one who does not struggle by means of the one who struggles.

**The difference between defence and aggression**

The mention of defeated must be read with the restraints carried by the same revelation. Permission to resist wrongdoing does not erase the prohibition of wrongdoing. The verse therefore asks the community to hold two truths together: injustice may have to be confronted, and the means used in confronting it remain subject to Allah's judgment.

The standard in 2:251 remains practical because it can be applied before a dispute reaches a court or a crisis reaches its worst point. One can notice the first small departure from defeated: an excuse, a concealed obligation, an unexamined desire, or a promise treated as optional. Returning at that point is easier than defending a settled habit. The warning is therefore also an opening for repair.

**The human cost carried by the wording**

The relation between defeated and killed also protects the verse from two opposite misreadings. It should not be expanded into a claim the wording of 2:251 does not make, but neither should its brevity be used to make it weightless. Short Qur'anic statements such as 2:251 often function as hinges: they close one movement, open another, and give the next command its moral setting. The careful reader of 2:251 therefore asks what has just been established, what response is now being required, and what kind of character could respond without evasion. Those questions keep 2:251 specific while allowing its guidance to reach a new circumstance.

The final emphasis of 2:251 returns to responsibility. No explanation of defeated is complete if it leaves the reader with information about earlier communities but no question about present conduct. The verse 2:251 asks what can be repaired now: a promise kept, a right restored, a motive purified, or a harmful practice abandoned. That is where knowledge becomes guidance.

**Limits that remain binding in conflict**

The choice of the word defeated in 2:251 also matters. It leaves room for the full context of 2:251 instead of allowing one translation gloss to carry every shade of meaning. Classical Arabic often places a broad word inside a narrow scene; in 2:251, the surrounding clauses determine whether the emphasis falls on an act, an intention, a condition, or a consequence. Here the reader should let the sentence do that work.

The exegetical tradition approaches defeated from more than one angle, but its differences are more useful when their shared question is made visible. The commentators ask what the wording of 2:251 includes, what it excludes, and how it fits with the other passages of the Qur'an. A responsible reading can preserve those alternatives in 2:251 without pretending that every possibility has equal strength.

**How the early community understood it**

There is also a distinction between understanding defeated and using it as a label for other people. The first task belongs to learning; the second requires evidence, fairness, and awareness of one's own motives in 2:251. The verse 2:251 addresses the reader before it supplies material for judging a neighbour. Its warning becomes safer and more productive when self-examination comes first.

The theology and practical instruction of 2:251 therefore support one another. If Allah knows or commands killed, that knowledge or command changes how a person handles time, power, money, speech, and private desire. A doctrine that never reaches those choices has not yet been read at the level demanded by 2:251.

**Power restrained by accountability**

An ordinary example makes the moral structure of 2:251 visible without replacing the revelation. Two people may perform an outwardly similar act involving defeated; one may be protecting a right, while the other is seeking praise, escaping a duty, or taking advantage of confusion. The act's surface does not settle its value. Intention, circumstance, and the rights of others complete the judgment.

The surrounding verses keep the reader from isolating defeated from the rest of life. In 2:251, revelation corrects the mind but also trains habits: how a person speaks when challenged, gives when unseen, waits when angry, and returns after error. Those habits are where this verse becomes more than information about defeated. They are the place in which assent to 2:251 is either confirmed or quietly withdrawn.

**The rule applied beyond the battlefield**

The most balanced response to 2:251 is neither to make the passage vague nor to claim more than it says. Its boundaries are part of its guidance. Within them, the verse can illuminate a new situation because its underlying moral relation remains stable: defeated is joined to killed, and the person is responsible for how that relation is honoured.

The wording of 2:251 gives the reader a useful order of questions about defeated. What does the term defeated mean in ordinary Arabic, what does the surrounding sentence restrict it to, and what consequence follows when the command is obeyed or refused in 2:251? These questions prevent a commentary on 2:251 from becoming a list of slogans. They make the act of interpretation answerable to grammar, context, and the moral end of this passage at 2:251.

The commentators sometimes preserve more than one report about the setting of 2:251. Such reports should be weighed rather than treated as interchangeable decoration. An occasion can explain why a question arose, but the wording of 2:251 determines how far the guidance travels. The verse speaks first to the situation that occasioned it and then to every reader who meets the same relation between defeated and killed.

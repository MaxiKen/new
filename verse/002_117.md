## Sūrah al-Baqarah 2:117

> **˹He is˺ the Originator of the heavens and the earth! When He decrees a matter, He simply tells it, “Be!” And it is!**

### Expanded Commentary

**Badīʿ: Maker Without a Model**

The force of this verse lies in the connection between originator and decrees. A reader can miss that force by isolating one phrase and treating it as a general maxim in 2:117. The verse itself is more exact. It speaks to a particular condition in the sūrah and directs attention to the kind of response that condition requires in 2:117. Reading the terms together keeps both the theology and the practical instruction in view at 2:117.

**Kun Fayakūn: the Sovereign Word of Creation**

Decrees translates qaḍā, which can also mean to accomplish, to complete, or to judge. The creative command Be! is also found in 6:73; 16:40; 36:82; 40:68. In each case, all that is necessary to bring a thing into being is for Allah to say Be! In connection with the previous verse, similar language can also be found with regard to Jesus creation in 3:59: Truly the likeness of Jesus in the Sight of Allah is that of Adam; He created him from dust, then said to him, Be! and he was; and 19:35: It is not for Allah to beget a child.

**The Answer to the Months of Waiting**

Glory be to Him! When He decrees a thing, He only says to it, Be! and it is. For some commentators such as al-Rāzī, the command Be! is meant to convey the ease and power by which Allah creates, requiring no preparation, practice, or effort. This view rejects the idea of a sequence of (1) a things nonexistence, (2) Allahs saying Be! and (3) its existence, since one cannot address nothing, and if something exists, it does not need the Be! in order to exist. Others say Be! neither precedes nor follows the creation of a thing, but is coterminous with it (al-Qurṭubī).

This verse is also understood as confirming that Allah knows things prior to their creation, and hence before their existence (al-Qurṭubī).

**The verse in its immediate setting**

This latter idea is expanded upon by Ibn ʿArabī and many members of his school, who explain that Allah says Be! to the forms (or immutable essences, al-aʿyān al-thābitah) in His Knowledge, meaning His Knowledge of His own Qualities and Attributes. More precisely, Allah knows what He will create and brings His Will and Power to bear upon that object of knowledge in order to create it by saying Be! Being within Allahs Knowledge, it is not nothing, but neither is it yet created, nor does it possess existence. Ultimately, these views converge on the unity of Allahs Knowledge, Will, and Power as His inseparable Attributes and on Allahs complete self-sufficiency in relation to what He creates. Be! (kun) also corresponds to the Biblical fiat lux (Let there be light, Genesis 1:3) and serves to emphasize the fact that the existence of everything comes from Allah and His Will and that human beings do not have the power to bring anything into being out of nothing.

**The central term and its range**

The mention of originator gives the verse a definite centre. Its meaning in 2:117 is not a general meditation on religion but an explanation of what this word requires in the situation named here. The passage at 2:117 moves between belief, action, and consequence, so a sound reading must preserve all three rather than allowing one attractive phrase to swallow the rest.

The standard in 2:117 remains practical because it can be applied before a dispute reaches a court or a crisis reaches its worst point. One can notice the first small departure from originator: an excuse, a concealed obligation, an unexamined desire, or a promise treated as optional. Returning at that point is easier than defending a settled habit. The warning is therefore also an opening for repair.

**What the early commentators preserve**

The relation between originator and decrees also protects the verse from two opposite misreadings. It should not be expanded into a claim the wording of 2:117 does not make, but neither should its brevity be used to make it weightless. Short Qur'anic statements such as 2:117 often function as hinges: they close one movement, open another, and give the next command its moral setting. The careful reader of 2:117 therefore asks what has just been established, what response is now being required, and what kind of character could respond without evasion. Those questions keep 2:117 specific while allowing its guidance to reach a new circumstance.

**The reasoning within the wording**

The surrounding verses keep the reader from isolating originator from the rest of life. In 2:117, revelation corrects the mind but also trains habits: how a person speaks when challenged, gives when unseen, waits when angry, and returns after error. Those habits are where this verse becomes more than information about originator. They are the place in which assent to 2:117 is either confirmed or quietly withdrawn.

The most balanced response to 2:117 is neither to make the passage vague nor to claim more than it says. Its boundaries are part of its guidance. Within them, the verse can illuminate a new situation because its underlying moral relation remains stable: originator is joined to decrees, and the person is responsible for how that relation is honoured.

**The theological question beneath the sentence**

The wording of 2:117 gives the reader a useful order of questions about originator. What does the term originator mean in ordinary Arabic, what does the surrounding sentence restrict it to, and what consequence follows when the command is obeyed or refused in 2:117? These questions prevent a commentary on 2:117 from becoming a list of slogans. They make the act of interpretation answerable to grammar, context, and the moral end of this passage at 2:117.

The commentators sometimes preserve more than one report about the setting of 2:117. Such reports should be weighed rather than treated as interchangeable decoration. An occasion can explain why a question arose, but the wording of 2:117 determines how far the guidance travels. The verse speaks first to the situation that occasioned it and then to every reader who meets the same relation between originator and decrees.

**The surrounding passage as a guide**

In 2:117, the distinction between public appearance and inward truth is especially relevant. A person may use the language of originator while arranging the act around another purpose. Allah's judgment in 2:117 is not limited to the visible outline, yet the inner intention is not an excuse for ignoring the rights and forms that the revelation establishes. Sincerity completes obedience; it does not cancel it.

One can see the social wisdom of 2:117 in a small everyday case. When people share responsibility for originator, uncertainty about the duty tends to fall on the person with less power or less information. A clear moral rule changes that balance. In 2:117, it tells the stronger party what must not be taken, and gives the weaker party language with which to name the wrong.

In 2:117, the verse also sits within the Qur'an's larger refusal to separate worship from truthfulness. Prayer, remembrance, generosity, patience, and justice are different acts in 2:117, but they are trained by the same capacity to stand before Allah without constructing a false account. The statement in 2:117 strengthens that capacity by making one particular choice visible.

A reader who finds the command in 2:117 difficult should distinguish difficulty from impossibility. For 2:117, difficulty may be the place where desire, habit, or social pressure is being corrected; impossibility is a separate legal and moral question. The passage does not praise needless hardship, but it does not permit comfort to redefine originator until the demand disappears.

The final emphasis of 2:117 returns to responsibility. No explanation of originator is complete if it leaves the reader with information about earlier communities but no question about present conduct. The verse 2:117 asks what can be repaired now: a promise kept, a right restored, a motive purified, or a harmful practice abandoned. That is where knowledge becomes guidance.

## Sūrah al-Baqarah 2:65

> **You are already aware of those of you who broke the Sabbath. We said to them, “Be disgraced apes!”**

### Expanded Commentary

**The Sabbath and the Trick**

The force of this verse lies in the connection between already and sabbath. A reader can miss that force by isolating one phrase and treating it as a general maxim in 2:65. The verse itself is more exact. It speaks to a particular condition in the sūrah and directs attention to the kind of response that condition requires in 2:65. Reading the terms together keeps both the theology and the practical instruction in view at 2:65.

**What the Transformation Means**

Outcast can also mean lowly (al-Rāzī). This is understood by some to refer to the people described in 7:163, who were tried by Allah when fish would come to them only on the Sabbath. Others interpret it to mean that all previous prophets taught that Friday was the most excellent day, that the Day of Judgment would come to pass on a Friday, and that it is celebrated by the angels in Heaven. The Jews preferred Saturday as the day of Allahs rest, and because of their disobedience they were punished by Allah by being prohibited from engaging in their normal activities on that day. The Christians preferred Sunday, noting its excellence as the first day (al-Ṭabarī).

**The Sin of Hīlah — Outsmarting the Law**

Other instances in which human beings are likened to animals include the following: The parable of those [who were] made to bear the Torah, then did not bear it, is that of an ass bearing books (62:5); And be moderate in thy pace and lower thy voice. Truly the vilest of voices are those of asses (31:19); The parable of those who take protectors apart from Allah is that of the spider that makes a house (29:41); Thus his parable is that of a dog: if you attack him, he lolls out his tongue, and if you leave him alone, he lolls out his tongue (7:176). Also, many aḥādīth speak of people resurrected in the form of various animals, according to their inner nature.

**The verse in its immediate setting**

Al-Qurṭubī interprets it as being akin to the command of 17:50, which literally reads Be you of stone, or of iron, making it rhetorical. One can read this as, As you wish then, be apes, as you have chosen to be. Mujāhid says, Their hearts were transformed, but they did not transform into apes, comparing it to the ass carrying books of 62:5 (Ibn Kathīr).

The mention of already gives the verse a definite centre. Its meaning in 2:65 is not a general meditation on religion but an explanation of what this word requires in the situation named here. The passage at 2:65 moves between belief, action, and consequence, so a sound reading must preserve all three rather than allowing one attractive phrase to swallow the rest.

**The central term and its range**

The standard in 2:65 remains practical because it can be applied before a dispute reaches a court or a crisis reaches its worst point. One can notice the first small departure from already: an excuse, a concealed obligation, an unexamined desire, or a promise treated as optional. Returning at that point is easier than defending a settled habit. The warning is therefore also an opening for repair.

The relation between already and sabbath also protects the verse from two opposite misreadings. It should not be expanded into a claim the wording of 2:65 does not make, but neither should its brevity be used to make it weightless. Short Qur'anic statements such as 2:65 often function as hinges: they close one movement, open another, and give the next command its moral setting. The careful reader of 2:65 therefore asks what has just been established, what response is now being required, and what kind of character could respond without evasion. Those questions keep 2:65 specific while allowing its guidance to reach a new circumstance.

**What the early commentators preserve**

In 2:65, the distinction between public appearance and inward truth is especially relevant. A person may use the language of already while arranging the act around another purpose. Allah's judgment in 2:65 is not limited to the visible outline, yet the inner intention is not an excuse for ignoring the rights and forms that the revelation establishes. Sincerity completes obedience; it does not cancel it.

One can see the social wisdom of 2:65 in a small everyday case. When people share responsibility for already, uncertainty about the duty tends to fall on the person with less power or less information. A clear moral rule changes that balance. In 2:65, it tells the stronger party what must not be taken, and gives the weaker party language with which to name the wrong.

**The reasoning within the wording**

In 2:65, the verse also sits within the Qur'an's larger refusal to separate worship from truthfulness. Prayer, remembrance, generosity, patience, and justice are different acts in 2:65, but they are trained by the same capacity to stand before Allah without constructing a false account. The statement in 2:65 strengthens that capacity by making one particular choice visible.

A reader who finds the command in 2:65 difficult should distinguish difficulty from impossibility. For 2:65, difficulty may be the place where desire, habit, or social pressure is being corrected; impossibility is a separate legal and moral question. The passage does not praise needless hardship, but it does not permit comfort to redefine already until the demand disappears.

The final emphasis of 2:65 returns to responsibility. No explanation of already is complete if it leaves the reader with information about earlier communities but no question about present conduct. The verse 2:65 asks what can be repaired now: a promise kept, a right restored, a motive purified, or a harmful practice abandoned. That is where knowledge becomes guidance.

**The theological question beneath the sentence**

The choice of the word already in 2:65 also matters. It leaves room for the full context of 2:65 instead of allowing one translation gloss to carry every shade of meaning. Classical Arabic often places a broad word inside a narrow scene; in 2:65, the surrounding clauses determine whether the emphasis falls on an act, an intention, a condition, or a consequence. Here the reader should let the sentence do that work.

The exegetical tradition approaches already from more than one angle, but its differences are more useful when their shared question is made visible. The commentators ask what the wording of 2:65 includes, what it excludes, and how it fits with the other passages of the Qur'an. A responsible reading can preserve those alternatives in 2:65 without pretending that every possibility has equal strength.

**The surrounding passage as a guide**

There is also a distinction between understanding already and using it as a label for other people. The first task belongs to learning; the second requires evidence, fairness, and awareness of one's own motives in 2:65. The verse 2:65 addresses the reader before it supplies material for judging a neighbour. Its warning becomes safer and more productive when self-examination comes first.

The theology and practical instruction of 2:65 therefore support one another. If Allah knows or commands sabbath, that knowledge or command changes how a person handles time, power, money, speech, and private desire. A doctrine that never reaches those choices has not yet been read at the level demanded by 2:65.

An ordinary example makes the moral structure of 2:65 visible without replacing the revelation. Two people may perform an outwardly similar act involving already; one may be protecting a right, while the other is seeking praise, escaping a duty, or taking advantage of confusion. The act's surface does not settle its value. Intention, circumstance, and the rights of others complete the judgment.

The surrounding verses keep the reader from isolating already from the rest of life. In 2:65, revelation corrects the mind but also trains habits: how a person speaks when challenged, gives when unseen, waits when angry, and returns after error. Those habits are where this verse becomes more than information about already. They are the place in which assent to 2:65 is either confirmed or quietly withdrawn.

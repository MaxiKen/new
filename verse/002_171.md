## Sūrah al-Baqarah 2:171

> **The example of the disbelievers ˹not responding to the Messenger’s warning˺ is like a flock not comprehending the calls and cries of the shepherd. ˹They are wilfully˺ deaf, dumb and blind so they have no understanding.**

### Expanded Commentary

**The Herder and the Herd**

The force of this verse lies in the connection between example and warning. A reader can miss that force by isolating one phrase and treating it as a general maxim in 2:171. The verse itself is more exact. It speaks to a particular condition in the sūrah and directs attention to the kind of response that condition requires in 2:171. Reading the terms together keeps both the theology and the practical instruction in view at 2:171.

**The Triple Sealing: Deaf, Dumb, Blind**

This verse suggests that calling to disbelievers is like calling to sheep; they hear, but only the sound and not the meaning of the call (al-Ṭabarī). This verse can also refer to the prayers idolaters offer to idols, which the insensate idols cannot hear (al-Rāzī). Insensibility to the wisdom of revelation is described with metaphors of animals elsewhere in the Qur'an, as in 62:5: The parable of those [who were] made to bear the Torah, then did not bear it, is that of an ass bearing books; and 7:176: Thus his parable is that of a dog: if you attack him, he lolls out his tongue, and if you leave him alone, he lolls out his tongue. Being deaf, dumb, and blind is also attributed to disbelievers in 2:18; 8:22; 17:97.

**The Parable in Every Age**

The mention of example gives the verse a definite centre. Its meaning in 2:171 is not a general meditation on religion but an explanation of what this word requires in the situation named here. The passage at 2:171 moves between belief, action, and consequence, so a sound reading must preserve all three rather than allowing one attractive phrase to swallow the rest.

The standard in 2:171 remains practical because it can be applied before a dispute reaches a court or a crisis reaches its worst point. One can notice the first small departure from example: an excuse, a concealed obligation, an unexamined desire, or a promise treated as optional. Returning at that point is easier than defending a settled habit. The warning is therefore also an opening for repair.

**The verse in its immediate setting**

The relation between example and warning also protects the verse from two opposite misreadings. It should not be expanded into a claim the wording of 2:171 does not make, but neither should its brevity be used to make it weightless. Short Qur'anic statements such as 2:171 often function as hinges: they close one movement, open another, and give the next command its moral setting. The careful reader of 2:171 therefore asks what has just been established, what response is now being required, and what kind of character could respond without evasion. Those questions keep 2:171 specific while allowing its guidance to reach a new circumstance.

The theology and practical instruction of 2:171 therefore support one another. If Allah knows or commands warning, that knowledge or command changes how a person handles time, power, money, speech, and private desire. A doctrine that never reaches those choices has not yet been read at the level demanded by 2:171.

**The central term and its range**

An ordinary example makes the moral structure of 2:171 visible without replacing the revelation. Two people may perform an outwardly similar act involving example; one may be protecting a right, while the other is seeking praise, escaping a duty, or taking advantage of confusion. The act's surface does not settle its value. Intention, circumstance, and the rights of others complete the judgment.

The surrounding verses keep the reader from isolating example from the rest of life. In 2:171, revelation corrects the mind but also trains habits: how a person speaks when challenged, gives when unseen, waits when angry, and returns after error. Those habits are where this verse becomes more than information about example. They are the place in which assent to 2:171 is either confirmed or quietly withdrawn.

**What the early commentators preserve**

The most balanced response to 2:171 is neither to make the passage vague nor to claim more than it says. Its boundaries are part of its guidance. Within them, the verse can illuminate a new situation because its underlying moral relation remains stable: example is joined to warning, and the person is responsible for how that relation is honoured.

The wording of 2:171 gives the reader a useful order of questions about example. What does the term example mean in ordinary Arabic, what does the surrounding sentence restrict it to, and what consequence follows when the command is obeyed or refused in 2:171? These questions prevent a commentary on 2:171 from becoming a list of slogans. They make the act of interpretation answerable to grammar, context, and the moral end of this passage at 2:171.

**The reasoning within the wording**

The commentators sometimes preserve more than one report about the setting of 2:171. Such reports should be weighed rather than treated as interchangeable decoration. An occasion can explain why a question arose, but the wording of 2:171 determines how far the guidance travels. The verse speaks first to the situation that occasioned it and then to every reader who meets the same relation between example and warning.

In 2:171, the distinction between public appearance and inward truth is especially relevant. A person may use the language of example while arranging the act around another purpose. Allah's judgment in 2:171 is not limited to the visible outline, yet the inner intention is not an excuse for ignoring the rights and forms that the revelation establishes. Sincerity completes obedience; it does not cancel it.

**The theological question beneath the sentence**

One can see the social wisdom of 2:171 in a small everyday case. When people share responsibility for example, uncertainty about the duty tends to fall on the person with less power or less information. A clear moral rule changes that balance. In 2:171, it tells the stronger party what must not be taken, and gives the weaker party language with which to name the wrong.

In 2:171, the verse also sits within the Qur'an's larger refusal to separate worship from truthfulness. Prayer, remembrance, generosity, patience, and justice are different acts in 2:171, but they are trained by the same capacity to stand before Allah without constructing a false account. The statement in 2:171 strengthens that capacity by making one particular choice visible.

**The surrounding passage as a guide**

A reader who finds the command in 2:171 difficult should distinguish difficulty from impossibility. For 2:171, difficulty may be the place where desire, habit, or social pressure is being corrected; impossibility is a separate legal and moral question. The passage does not praise needless hardship, but it does not permit comfort to redefine example until the demand disappears.

The final emphasis of 2:171 returns to responsibility. No explanation of example is complete if it leaves the reader with information about earlier communities but no question about present conduct. The verse 2:171 asks what can be repaired now: a promise kept, a right restored, a motive purified, or a harmful practice abandoned. That is where knowledge becomes guidance.

The choice of the word example in 2:171 also matters. It leaves room for the full context of 2:171 instead of allowing one translation gloss to carry every shade of meaning. Classical Arabic often places a broad word inside a narrow scene; in 2:171, the surrounding clauses determine whether the emphasis falls on an act, an intention, a condition, or a consequence. Here the reader should let the sentence do that work.

The exegetical tradition approaches example from more than one angle, but its differences are more useful when their shared question is made visible. The commentators ask what the wording of 2:171 includes, what it excludes, and how it fits with the other passages of the Qur'an. A responsible reading can preserve those alternatives in 2:171 without pretending that every possibility has equal strength.

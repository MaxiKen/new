## Sūrah al-Baqarah 2:170

> **When it is said to them, “Follow what Allah has revealed,” they reply, “No! We ˹only˺ follow what we found our forefathers practicing.” ˹Would they still do so,˺ even if their forefathers had ˹absolutely˺ no understanding or guidance?**

### Expanded Commentary

**The Two Calls and the One Refusal**

At this point in al-Baqarah, the wording brings follow and found into one moral frame. The sentence should not be detached from that relationship. Its first term in 2:170 sets the subject, while the later term shows what that subject demands, permits, or exposes. The order of 2:170 gives the reader a path through the verse: begin with what Allah names, follow the consequence, and resist the temptation to turn a living address into a slogan.

**"Even If Their Fathers Understood Nothing?"**

Some connect this verse to the previously observed norms of what is lawful and what is forbidden (see vv. 168–69; Ṭ), and it may be seen to be addressed to the idolaters and the People of the Book or to people in general (al-Rāzī). Recourse to mere custom is no excuse, as the Qur'an emphasizes again and again, as in 5:104: Sufficient for us is that which we have found our fathers practicing. What! Even if their fathers knew naught and were not rightly guided? Similar injunctions are addressed to the Children of Adam (7:28), the Egyptians (10:78), Abrahams people (21:53–54), and people in general (31:21; 43:24).

**The Islamic Measure: Tradition by Truth, Not Truth by Tradition**

The verse describes follow not as a private opinion but as a direction that changes perception and conduct. In 2:170, guidance is recognized by what it makes a person do with knowledge, power, and desire. For 2:170, inward assent is linked to an observable response: the heart's claim is tested by the path it chooses when the claim becomes costly.

A useful way to test the verse is to ask what would change if follow were taken seriously when no audience was present. The answer cannot be only a feeling, because 2:170 connects inward recognition with a discernible choice. Nor can it be only an outward display, because the choice in 2:170 loses its Qur'anic meaning when it is made for reputation rather than for Allah. The passage holds intention and action together in 2:170.

**The first movement of a guided life**

The relation between follow and found also protects the verse from two opposite misreadings. It should not be expanded into a claim the wording of 2:170 does not make, but neither should its brevity be used to make it weightless. Short Qur'anic statements such as 2:170 often function as hinges: they close one movement, open another, and give the next command its moral setting. The careful reader of 2:170 therefore asks what has just been established, what response is now being required, and what kind of character could respond without evasion. Those questions keep 2:170 specific while allowing its guidance to reach a new circumstance.

There is also a distinction between understanding follow and using it as a label for other people. The first task belongs to learning; the second requires evidence, fairness, and awareness of one's own motives in 2:170. The verse 2:170 addresses the reader before it supplies material for judging a neighbour. Its warning becomes safer and more productive when self-examination comes first.

**Belief made visible in conduct**

The theology and practical instruction of 2:170 therefore support one another. If Allah knows or commands found, that knowledge or command changes how a person handles time, power, money, speech, and private desire. A doctrine that never reaches those choices has not yet been read at the level demanded by 2:170.

An ordinary example makes the moral structure of 2:170 visible without replacing the revelation. Two people may perform an outwardly similar act involving follow; one may be protecting a right, while the other is seeking praise, escaping a duty, or taking advantage of confusion. The act's surface does not settle its value. Intention, circumstance, and the rights of others complete the judgment.

**The choice hidden inside the command**

The surrounding verses keep the reader from isolating follow from the rest of life. In 2:170, revelation corrects the mind but also trains habits: how a person speaks when challenged, gives when unseen, waits when angry, and returns after error. Those habits are where this verse becomes more than information about follow. They are the place in which assent to 2:170 is either confirmed or quietly withdrawn.

The most balanced response to 2:170 is neither to make the passage vague nor to claim more than it says. Its boundaries are part of its guidance. Within them, the verse can illuminate a new situation because its underlying moral relation remains stable: follow is joined to found, and the person is responsible for how that relation is honoured.

**How guidance becomes discernment**

The wording of 2:170 gives the reader a useful order of questions about follow. What does the term follow mean in ordinary Arabic, what does the surrounding sentence restrict it to, and what consequence follows when the command is obeyed or refused in 2:170? These questions prevent a commentary on 2:170 from becoming a list of slogans. They make the act of interpretation answerable to grammar, context, and the moral end of this passage at 2:170.

The commentators sometimes preserve more than one report about the setting of 2:170. Such reports should be weighed rather than treated as interchangeable decoration. An occasion can explain why a question arose, but the wording of 2:170 determines how far the guidance travels. The verse speaks first to the situation that occasioned it and then to every reader who meets the same relation between follow and found.

**The contrast drawn by the verse**

In 2:170, the distinction between public appearance and inward truth is especially relevant. A person may use the language of follow while arranging the act around another purpose. Allah's judgment in 2:170 is not limited to the visible outline, yet the inner intention is not an excuse for ignoring the rights and forms that the revelation establishes. Sincerity completes obedience; it does not cancel it.

One can see the social wisdom of 2:170 in a small everyday case. When people share responsibility for follow, uncertainty about the duty tends to fall on the person with less power or less information. A clear moral rule changes that balance. In 2:170, it tells the stronger party what must not be taken, and gives the weaker party language with which to name the wrong.

**Certainty, doubt, and moral action**

In 2:170, the verse also sits within the Qur'an's larger refusal to separate worship from truthfulness. Prayer, remembrance, generosity, patience, and justice are different acts in 2:170, but they are trained by the same capacity to stand before Allah without constructing a false account. The statement in 2:170 strengthens that capacity by making one particular choice visible.

A reader who finds the command in 2:170 difficult should distinguish difficulty from impossibility. For 2:170, difficulty may be the place where desire, habit, or social pressure is being corrected; impossibility is a separate legal and moral question. The passage does not praise needless hardship, but it does not permit comfort to redefine follow until the demand disappears.

The final emphasis of 2:170 returns to responsibility. No explanation of follow is complete if it leaves the reader with information about earlier communities but no question about present conduct. The verse 2:170 asks what can be repaired now: a promise kept, a right restored, a motive purified, or a harmful practice abandoned. That is where knowledge becomes guidance.

The choice of the word follow in 2:170 also matters. It leaves room for the full context of 2:170 instead of allowing one translation gloss to carry every shade of meaning. Classical Arabic often places a broad word inside a narrow scene; in 2:170, the surrounding clauses determine whether the emphasis falls on an act, an intention, a condition, or a consequence. Here the reader should let the sentence do that work.

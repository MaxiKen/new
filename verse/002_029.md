## Sūrah al-Baqarah 2:29

> **He is the One Who created everything in the earth for you. Then He turned towards the heaven, forming it into seven heavens. And He has ˹perfect˺ knowledge of all things.**

### Expanded Commentary

**"Created For You": The Earth as Endowment**

The force of this verse lies in the connection between created and turned. A reader can miss that force by isolating one phrase and treating it as a general maxim in 2:29. The verse itself is more exact. It speaks to a particular condition in the sūrah and directs attention to the kind of response that condition requires in 2:29. Reading the terms together keeps both the theology and the practical instruction in view at 2:29.

**Then the Seven Heavens**

For Allahs creation of things for human beings, see also 16:5; 16:13; 26:166. This idea also relates to Allahs making creation subservient to human beings as in 22:65; 31:20; 45:13. Turned to translates the verb istawā, which is also rendered mounted in other contexts; see also 7:54; 10:3; 20:5; 25:59; 32:4; 57:4. For the seven heavens, also see 41:12, where the context is the six days of creation; 67:3, in which they are one upon another; 65:12, which also mentions the creation from the earth the like thereof—that is, earths upon earths; the early Qur'an commentator Mujāhid (d. 104/722–23) mentions heavens above one other and earths above one another; and 71:15, which also mentions the levels of heavens.

**"And He Is Knower of All Things": The Seal of the Verse**

The mention of created gives the verse a definite centre. Its meaning in 2:29 is not a general meditation on religion but an explanation of what this word requires in the situation named here. The passage at 2:29 moves between belief, action, and consequence, so a sound reading must preserve all three rather than allowing one attractive phrase to swallow the rest.

The standard in 2:29 remains practical because it can be applied before a dispute reaches a court or a crisis reaches its worst point. One can notice the first small departure from created: an excuse, a concealed obligation, an unexamined desire, or a promise treated as optional. Returning at that point is easier than defending a settled habit. The warning is therefore also an opening for repair.

**The verse in its immediate setting**

The relation between created and turned also protects the verse from two opposite misreadings. It should not be expanded into a claim the wording of 2:29 does not make, but neither should its brevity be used to make it weightless. Short Qur'anic statements such as 2:29 often function as hinges: they close one movement, open another, and give the next command its moral setting. The careful reader of 2:29 therefore asks what has just been established, what response is now being required, and what kind of character could respond without evasion. Those questions keep 2:29 specific while allowing its guidance to reach a new circumstance.

The exegetical tradition approaches created from more than one angle, but its differences are more useful when their shared question is made visible. The commentators ask what the wording of 2:29 includes, what it excludes, and how it fits with the other passages of the Qur'an. A responsible reading can preserve those alternatives in 2:29 without pretending that every possibility has equal strength.

**The central term and its range**

There is also a distinction between understanding created and using it as a label for other people. The first task belongs to learning; the second requires evidence, fairness, and awareness of one's own motives in 2:29. The verse 2:29 addresses the reader before it supplies material for judging a neighbour. Its warning becomes safer and more productive when self-examination comes first.

The theology and practical instruction of 2:29 therefore support one another. If Allah knows or commands turned, that knowledge or command changes how a person handles time, power, money, speech, and private desire. A doctrine that never reaches those choices has not yet been read at the level demanded by 2:29.

**What the early commentators preserve**

An ordinary example makes the moral structure of 2:29 visible without replacing the revelation. Two people may perform an outwardly similar act involving created; one may be protecting a right, while the other is seeking praise, escaping a duty, or taking advantage of confusion. The act's surface does not settle its value. Intention, circumstance, and the rights of others complete the judgment.

The surrounding verses keep the reader from isolating created from the rest of life. In 2:29, revelation corrects the mind but also trains habits: how a person speaks when challenged, gives when unseen, waits when angry, and returns after error. Those habits are where this verse becomes more than information about created. They are the place in which assent to 2:29 is either confirmed or quietly withdrawn.

**The reasoning within the wording**

The most balanced response to 2:29 is neither to make the passage vague nor to claim more than it says. Its boundaries are part of its guidance. Within them, the verse can illuminate a new situation because its underlying moral relation remains stable: created is joined to turned, and the person is responsible for how that relation is honoured.

The wording of 2:29 gives the reader a useful order of questions about created. What does the term created mean in ordinary Arabic, what does the surrounding sentence restrict it to, and what consequence follows when the command is obeyed or refused in 2:29? These questions prevent a commentary on 2:29 from becoming a list of slogans. They make the act of interpretation answerable to grammar, context, and the moral end of this passage at 2:29.

**The theological question beneath the sentence**

The commentators sometimes preserve more than one report about the setting of 2:29. Such reports should be weighed rather than treated as interchangeable decoration. An occasion can explain why a question arose, but the wording of 2:29 determines how far the guidance travels. The verse speaks first to the situation that occasioned it and then to every reader who meets the same relation between created and turned.

In 2:29, the distinction between public appearance and inward truth is especially relevant. A person may use the language of created while arranging the act around another purpose. Allah's judgment in 2:29 is not limited to the visible outline, yet the inner intention is not an excuse for ignoring the rights and forms that the revelation establishes. Sincerity completes obedience; it does not cancel it.

**The surrounding passage as a guide**

One can see the social wisdom of 2:29 in a small everyday case. When people share responsibility for created, uncertainty about the duty tends to fall on the person with less power or less information. A clear moral rule changes that balance. In 2:29, it tells the stronger party what must not be taken, and gives the weaker party language with which to name the wrong.

In 2:29, the verse also sits within the Qur'an's larger refusal to separate worship from truthfulness. Prayer, remembrance, generosity, patience, and justice are different acts in 2:29, but they are trained by the same capacity to stand before Allah without constructing a false account. The statement in 2:29 strengthens that capacity by making one particular choice visible.

A reader who finds the command in 2:29 difficult should distinguish difficulty from impossibility. For 2:29, difficulty may be the place where desire, habit, or social pressure is being corrected; impossibility is a separate legal and moral question. The passage does not praise needless hardship, but it does not permit comfort to redefine created until the demand disappears.

The final emphasis of 2:29 returns to responsibility. No explanation of created is complete if it leaves the reader with information about earlier communities but no question about present conduct. The verse 2:29 asks what can be repaired now: a promise kept, a right restored, a motive purified, or a harmful practice abandoned. That is where knowledge becomes guidance.

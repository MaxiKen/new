## Sūrah al-Nisāʾ 4:103

> **When you have completed the prayer, remember [and invoke] God, standing, sitting, or lying on your sides. Then when you are secure, observe proper prayer; for prayer at fixed hours is prescribed for the believers.**

**Expanded Commentary**

**Fa-Idhā Qaḍaytumu al-Ṣalāta fa-Dhkurū Allāha Qiyāman wa Quʿūdan wa ʿalā Junūbikum: Remembrance in Every Posture**

The battle-prayer closes with the door that never closes — the dhikr-law: *fa-idhā qaḍaytumu al-ṣalāta fa-dhkurū Allāha qiyāman wa quʿūdan wa ʿalā junūbikum* — "when you have completed the prayer, remember God — standing, sitting, and upon your sides": *qaḍaytum* — completed, discharged (q-ḍ-y of settled-duty — the formal ṣalāt a defined act with a defined exit) — then the instruction swinging from *prescription* to *improvising remembrance*: with the rite discharged, the state persists — the believer never wanders from the hall from the hall of nearness merely because the form has ended (the three-posture specification the commentators love: *qiyāman* — standing; *quʿūdan* — sitting; *ʿalā junūbikum* — reclining on flanks — the whole body-c catalog of sustained postures — with its direct twin at 3:191 — "those who remember God standing and sitting and on their sides, and reflect upon the creation of the heavens and the earth: our Lord, Thou didst not create this in vain — glory be to Thee! Shield us from the punishment of the Fire" — the two verses meeting as the Revelation's paired charter: 3:191 linking dhikr with *contemplation-of-creation*; 4:103 linking it with the *aftermath of danger* — the two together delivering the inference of the whole dhikr-tradition: remembrance belongs to no single posture and no single hour — it is the ummah's breath) — with the field-setting itself instructive: a company that just prayed staggered-rakʿahs beside enemy cavalry now told — keep saying God's name, in every remaining position of your watches — the battlefield becomes monastery becomes prayer-mat in the widest sense (the comment tradition noting also the two senses of *udhkurū*: remember *in the heart* and invoke *with the tongue* — tasbīḥ, takbīr, personal pleading for victory — the war-dhikr authorized on the eve of clash — cf. 8:45's "O you who believe, when you meet a host, stand firm and remember God much, that you may prosper").

**Fa-Idhā iṭmaʾnantum fa-Aqīmū al-Ṣalāh: Security Restores the Form**

Then the answer-clause restoring the full mold: *fa-idhā iṭmaʾnantum fa-aqīmū al-ṣalāh* — "then when you are secure, establish the prayer": *iṭmaʾnana* — from ṭ-m-ʾ-n — descending-tran the deep settling-quiet (its Book-family: ṭumaʾnīnah — the serenity the ritual pause itself is commanded to contain — v. 102's armed communities now discharged at even now discharged at last into normality) — and the standing jurisprudence: the fear-rite is *exceptional*; the full canonical architecture returns when safety returns (never the concession becoming custom — rukhsahs are keyed to conditions — with the differing view in the earliest roll — some Companion-commentary taking "establish the prayer" to mean *repeat* or *complete* what was shortened; the majority reading being normative-restoration) — thereby as the classic case as the classical case-law sample for the entire uṣūl doctrine of *a condition-bound dispensation lapses with the condition* (ʿilla-zāla = rukhsa-zāla — the war-prayer ceases at war's end).

**Inna al-Ṣalāta Kānat ʿalā al-Muʾminīna Kitāban Mawqūtan: The Fixed-Hour Prescription**

Then the verse's most formulaic declaration — recited through fifteen centuries at mosque-walls and minbar-ends: *inna al-ṣalāta kānat ʿalā al-muʾminīna kitāban mawqūtan* — "surely prayer is upon the believers a prescription *at appointed times*": *kitāb* here — written-injunction, binding decree (from k-t-b — the prescription-degree-word rather than the noun of the Book itself — with the comment-doctors listing its meaning-set: obligatory decree; well-ordered assignment; covenant-form) — and *mawqūtan* — time-bound — from w-q-t — appointed-hour (the tradition's greatest standing coat the clause the commentators single out as the Revelation's master-charter of time-obedient worship — with the Sunnah filling in its five daily stations — the Prophet's own complete r the miʿrāj-ledger of prescriptions (Bukhārī 349's standing roll the standing account of the fifty-prayers-reduced-to-five) — and the Prophet's time-table texts — "the likeness of the five prayers is as a river at your door in which he washes five times a day" — Muslim 667; "the best of deeds the most beloved deed to God is prayer at its time" — Bukhārī 527, Muslim 85 — the mawqūt-word stretching out thereafter over the ummah's whole architecture of life: mosques, muʾadhdhins, hours-glasses time calendars-of-prayer, the daily building the daily rhythm cohering around five bells-out-of-heaven) — with the verse's own placement being its silent sermon: in the very passage where heaven *most lavishly accommodates* the believers' prayers (shortened on the road, split under attack, dhikr-valid at every posture — the Book then clamps the axle: *nevertheless the set hour stands*: no rukhsah releases the time itself — prayer remains *kitāban mawqūtan* while walls fall — and the entire juridical machinery of qada-makeup, joining, and emergency-prayer exists precisely because the appointed hour is non-negotiable) — and Ibn ʿAjībah's, al-Qushayrī's inward register lifts the arc into the heart again: beyond the completed *outer* prayer, the heart's dhikr continues indefinitely — and when the heart *arrives* secure in the Presence (iṭmaʾn-an *inwardly*) — the very prayer of witnessing comes due — the ever-constant salāt of 70:23 — "those who persevere in their prayer" — posture, time, and weapon-shape now interiorized: qiyām as uprightness-of-covenant; quʿūd as the sitting of presence; junūb as the lying of humility — and the world-clock's five bells keeping the body in cadence with them all.

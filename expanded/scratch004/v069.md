## Sūrah al-Nisāʾ 4:69

> **Whosoever obeys God and the Messenger, they are with those whom God has blessed, of the prophets, the truthful ones, the witnesses, and the righteous. What beautiful companions they are!**

**Expanded Commentary**

**Wa Man Yuṭiʿ: Obedience as Citizenship**

The trinity's third payment arrives as the verse the earliest community treasured above all comfort-texts — heaven's own rostral decree upon obedience's full consequences: *wa man yuṭiʿi Allāha wa al-rasūla fa-ulāʾika maʿa alladhīna anʿama Allāhu ʿalayhim mina al-nabiyyīna wa al-ṣiddīqīna wa al-shuhadāʾi wa al-ṣāliḥīn wa ḥasuna ulāʾika rafīqā* — "whoever obeys God and the Messenger — those are with those whom God has blessed: of the prophets, the truthful, the witnesses, and the righteous — and beautiful are they as companions": the double *maʿa* — *with* — the whole verse pivots upon companionship-grammar (obedience no longer merely paid — it *locates* the soul: man = whosoever — general; yuṭiʿ — obeys — the criterion; fa-ulāʾika maʿa — those are WITH — the reward's substance being not what but *where and with whom*) — the asbāb-report the tradition never ever stops retelling (al-Wāḥidī, al-Ṭabarī, and al-Ṭabrisī's collated rolls): certain Companions — Thawbān of the freedmen in Ibn Kathīr's favorite narration, others of the Anṣār in parallel chains — came heavy-hearted to the Prophet: they loved him so, yet he as prophet would be exalted above them in the Garden by stations they could never reach — *shall we lose your company after death?* — and heaven answered them with our verse: *obedience purchases place-in-common*: the levels differ (prophethood not granted) but the *withness* is conferred wholesale — the fear measured and healed in a single clause, the community learning thereafter that the afterlife's true division is not ranks but *presence*: with the blessed, or outside them.

**The Four Ranks Unfolded**

The blessed cohort gets its four columns, and the commentators have ever loved to march them in order: (1) *al-nabiyyīn* — the prophets — the apex — human speech-jo God's own spokesmen; (2) *al-ṣiddīqīn* — the absolutely truthful — the highest human sub-rank (the tradition's chief examples: Abū Bakr — his very title al-Ṣiddīq minted upon the Night-Journey-morning when he said *"if he said it, it is true"* — al-Bayhaqī's register — and ʿĀsiyah Mary's sawn the Qur'an conferring the feminine the feminine model of it at 5:75 — "his mother was a ṣiddīqah" — ṣidq being trust-speak so perfect that no shadow of speech-dis of mismatched inner/outer remains); (3) *al-shuhadāʾ* — the witnesses — martyrs, but wider (al-Rāzī's luminous breadth: all who bear testimony to religion *by argument or by sword* — the tongue-witnesses and the grave-witnesses both — the Book's own usage at 4:41's prophet-witnesses and 2:143's ummah-witnesses forbidding the narrow sense); and (4) *al-ṣāliḥīn* — the righteous — public and secret rectitude complete (al-Ṭabarī's definition: *those who do right in what is seen and unseen*) — the cohort thereby encompassing the whole pyramid of human sanctity — prophets at the summit, saints beneath, witnesses beside them, the righteous as its broad faithful base — into which cohort the obedient are *written*, even at the lowest floor.

**Wa Ḥasuna Ulāʾika Rafīqā: Beautiful Companionship as Heaven's Close**

The seal does what human astonishment does upon the census: *wa ḥasuna ulāʾika rafīqā* — "and how beautiful are they as companions!": *ḥasuna* — beauty-exclamation (the Book's own startled doxology — it being seconded only by *niʿimma* of v. 58's counsel); *rafīq* — fellow-traveler — from rifāq: gentle companionship (both the company and its *softness*: no rough fellowship — the Prophet's own beat blessing upon the pilgrims' fellowship in the communitas-traditions of the early Muslims being its foretaste: brotherhood on tawbah brotherhood contracted at Madinah in the muʾākhāh, al-Bukhārī 2049's own account of ʿAbd al-Raḥmān and Saʿd ibn al-Rabīʿ dividing goods) — and behind it waits the Tradition's own *proof-of-companionship*, the hadith this verse's greatest commentary in viva in living exchange — the man asking the Prophet about the Last Hour and receiving the answer (al-Bukhārī 6168–6171 and Muslim's register 2639–2640 — Anas's celebrated report of the Anṣārī's weeping joy):

> *A man came to the Prophet ﷺ and asked about the Hour... then said: "Messenger of God! I do not do much prayer, fasting, or alms, but I love God and His Messenger." He ﷺ said: "A man shall be with those whom he loves." Anas said: Never were we so gladdened after Islam as by this word: a man shall be with those whom he loves.*

— love joining obedience as the second key to the same doors (for love is love itself the *fruit* of obedience 3:31-style: "if you love God, follow me — God will love you"), and the pericope therefore closing its whole its whole mediation-drama with the brightest promise-block of the chapter: the triple payments of vv. 67–69 now fully laid out: reward from His Presence *for the ledger*, a guided road *for the walking*, and *for the heart that feared loneliness after death* — to stand in the cohort of prophets-and-witnesses, beautiful companions, forever with.

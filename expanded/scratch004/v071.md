## Sūrah al-Nisāʾ 4:71

> **O you who believe! Take your precautions; then go forth in companies or go forth all together.**

**Expanded Commentary**

**Khudhū Ḥidhrakum: Precaution as Command, Not as Cowardice**

The sūrah swings at last from covenant-consolations into campaign-discipline with one of the Prophet's most-lived clauses: *khudhū ḥidhrakum* — "take your precaution": *ḥidhr*, from ḥ-dh-r — caution, guard, the watchful fore-arming — rendered in the traditional explanations as both *armor* (the equipment one wears) and *wariness* (the disposition you carry) — the command unmistakably rooted in Madinah's security-crisis climate (the pericope of vv. 71–104 being the sūrah's battle-mobilization arch, borne upon the breach of Uḥud and the anticipation of the Qurayshian return) — but the commentators cross the whole register for its universalization: arms-bear equipment-assembling (the Arab glossers glossing it *carry your arms*), intelligence-gathering, fortification, formations — every species of prudent anticipation falling under the noun, one of the Ṣaḥabah even reporting bare direct field-memoranda formed around it (the pre-battle wakefulness of the Prophet's own night rounds among the outposts being cited in the Sīrah chronicles as the verse's life-form). Al-Qurṛṭubī's statutes classical comment-crossroads raises at once the theological seam the reader most needs stitched: does precaution contradict reliance (tawakkul) the tradition's permanent answer being the famous two-edged instrument the hadith masters lodge precisely here:

> *A Bedouin came to the Prophet ﷺ and said: Shall I leave my camel and trust in God? He said: "Tether her, and trust in God."* (al-Tirmidhī 2517 — graded likewise in the Musnad-registers)

— *iʿqilhā wa tawakkal*: the camel tied — then the heart made over to God — this pairing thereafter standing as Islam's final word on preparation versus trust: no precaution without faith becomes pagan insurance; no faith without precaution becomes testing-God presumption — the two together constituting responsible tawakkul (the same pair as the sages bound at the hijrah's own ḥadīth-machinery: Abū Bakr's companionship in the cave — the Prophet commissioning all the the whole apparatus — food, mounts, scouts, decoy bedstead — then saying "Do not grieve: God is with us" — 9:40) — so the verse enshrines precaution itself as obedience, thereby outlawing the false piety that parades carelessness as trust.

**Fa-nfirū Thubātin Awi-nfirū Jamīʿā: Two Modes for Every Manner of Mission**

Then the twin deployment-forms: *wa-nfirū thubātin awi-nfirū jamīʿā* — "then go forth in companies, or go forth all together": *nafara*, to surge out, to detach into expedition (the verb of detach of detachment-in-force: 9:122's tafaqquh-delegations built on the same root — *why should not a squadron of each band stay behind to gain understanding in religion?*); *thubātin* — clustered parties — from *thubah*, a knot or separate band — units sent by detachments (raiding parties, reconnaissance columns, successive waves per the glossers' spectrum Ibn ʿAbbās's roll recording "one after another"); *jamīʿā* — all as one — the whole force as single muster (the Prophet's own great expeditions — Hunayn, Tabūk, the conquest column) — so that the command covers *both* order-types permitted to the Commander's discretion: piecemeal operations for harassing-and-probing; full assembly for decisive massings (the juristic commentators using the verse to ground both military law-forms throughout the caliphate-annals: ʿUmar's far-flung army-posts evidence of units; ʿUmar's Fertile Crescent hosts evidence of mass) — and al-Rāzī's wisdom-note agrees stands for all later ages: the military form is left open within *two* because the sūrah's business is *readiness*, not choreography — God legislating the principle of guarded motion while leaving the art to the men of command — a standing teaching thereafter echoed in the sunnah's own tactical pluralism.

**The Readiness-Quadrant: Believers Summoned as a Garrison**

Beneath the two orders stands the verse's civic-sign decreeing doctrine: *the believing community is a permanently-ready force*: the address itself being *yā ayyuhā alladhīna āmanū* — O you who believe — faith itself conscripted (the note of the commentators: as this very pericope will before long expose those who claim faith but will not march — vv. 72–77 — the address-form itself is rendered into a morale-test: *come forward sincerely or be sifted*); and the whole chamber sits upon the seal of v. 70 like a red upon like a seal upon a declaration: the mercy-payments have been pledged; the battle-call now summoned now summons — bounty and readiness being the two wings of the same defense-treaty — with al-Qushayrī's spiritual inversion treasured by the sufi-books as the clause's interior chamber: *taking precautions* for the gnostic = to flee from aught but God in the inner field; *going forth* = seizing surging from every created attachment, in detachments if you must, or all-at-once if you can — the external discipline yielding the inner mysth inner thumbnail: *the soul fortifying against false-security just as the garrison fortifies against the night-raid* — so that from desert-warfare into heart-warfare the verse marches unbroken: ḥidhr within, ḥidhr without — and the whole community thereafter formed as Islam's standing glass garrison: *precaution belted on, ranks formed, the Name overhead — trusting and tethered both.*

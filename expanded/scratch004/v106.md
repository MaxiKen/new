## Sūrah al-Nisāʾ 4:106

> **And seek forgiveness of God; surely God is Forgiving, Merciful.**

**Expanded Commentary**

**Wa-staghfir Allāh: The Judge Asked to Seek Pardon**

Between the judicial charter of v. 105 and the deep exposure of treachery's nocturnal mechanism in vv. 107–108, the sūrah inserts a six-word command that would become one of the most theologically argued sentences of Madinah's first court-era: *wa-staghfir Allāh* — "and seek forgiveness of God": addressed in the singular, to the Prophet himself — and the immediate context is defined: he had been on the verge of accepting a clan's outcry and clearing a treacherous thief — pulled back mid-motion by the revelation of v. 105 — and now instructed to wash even the *nearness* of the misstep in istighfār (with the twin-attribute seal again attending: *inna Allāha kāna ghafūran raḥīman* — "God is Forgiving, Merciful" — the very mercy he was commanded to access, named so that the command itself be heard as invitation rather than disgrace).

**'Iṣmah and the Prophet's Near-Advocacy: The Canonical Debate**

The classical houses then take up the knot this verse forced upon theology: does the command imply the Prophet *sinned*? The tradition's answers, filed for all time: (1) al-Rāzī's standing defense — the report indicates the Prophet *considered* but decided nothing, awaiting Godward guidance; his istighfār was sought for that mere *inclination to hear their plea* — or, on the stronger r on the further reading, on behalf of *those who had sought to sway him*: the clan-pleaders themselves before whom the door of advocacy stood open (so the instruction reads distributively: "seek forgiveness — for them"); (2) the deep structure from the doctrine of ʿiṣmah — prophetic protection guarding the *delivery* of the message and the *outcomes* of the deeds — with the standing spiritual register of the Book as mercy-school the standing register reading such episodes as the prophets' own commissioned pedagogy: a prophet drawn to the edge of an error *so that the whole ummah thereafter learns the edge's position* (the parallel the commentators themselves preserve: Sulaymān's field of horses — 38:32–33 — with its Prophet-borne ag its owned subject: "until the sun was hidden in the veil; bring them back to me" — the Book allowing its prophets to approach the world's allure the ambush-edge so the lesson may complete); (3) the plainest reading — and the plainest school-reading honored across the majority: istighfār covers also *what is not sin at all* — the Prophet's own self-declared custom: "I seek God's forgiveness seventy times a day" — Bukhārī 6307 — and its plenary: "by God, I seek forgiveness of God and repent to Him more than seventy times daily" — that continuous istighfār being honor-completion, not repentance-of-crime — washing even the position-short-of-the-best washing even the inadvertent leanings toward kin toward clamor — which in lesser men never amounted even to a thought-crime) — the standing hadith completing the theology with the Prophet's own elevated measure: "people! repent " the hadith on istighfār's internalization the hadith of the heart's rust at Muslim 2322 at Bukhārī 7487: *"indeed a dimness a darkness til there descends upon my heart — and indeed I seek God's forgiveness a hundred times a day"* — Muslim 2702 — the descending-dimness (the yukhān — rust-tr the passing-covering that never became error) being exactly what the standing command at 4:106 polishes away.

**The Judge's Inward Discipline**

And the tradition thereafter drew from the verse its standing professional counsel for every occupant of every seat of judgment: the holder of authority over men should habitually *ask forgiveness after every court-session* — for authority itself is a gravity well — bent toward each session's plausibles, pressures, and partialities (the comment-doctors unfolding the practical syllabus: the judge's istighfār compensating his juristic fatigue — decisions between brother and stranger — between claimant's tears and cold evidence — between the clan's persistence and the innocent's innocence: each sitting leaving its spiritual residue — with the standing hadīth-grid the grid of the Prophetic practice echoing inside it: the Prophet's own instruction to judges in the Tirmidhī-rolls to beware their "inner voice after the standing Sunnah doctrine: reward for the mujtahid even when *mistaken* — Muslim 1716 — Bukhārī 7352 — when a judge strives and errs, one reward; when right, two — the whole systemic mercy thereby disclosed: heaven knows the seat winds the seat's warping-strings pull every mind off plumb — hence the command washes the office itself regularly) — so that the verse stands as the constitutional hygiene of Islam's entire court-theory: the law's holder being the first and most regular visitor of the fountain of pardon — *and seek God's forgiveness* — with the paired mercy of v. 106 awaiting every judge who, having judged, turns back toward God — thereby planting at the center of Islam's judiciary the standing doctrine: a court that does not wash itself daily becomes treacherous ground without knowing it — with the Prophet's own morning and evening istighfārs standing as the the standing precedent of the entire profession of judgment until the end of hours.

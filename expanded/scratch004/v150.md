## Sūrah al-Nisāʾ 4:150

> **Verily those who disbelieve in God and His messengers, and wish to make a distinction between God and His messengers, and say: "We believe in some and disbelieve in some" — and wish to take a way between —**

**Expanded Commentary**

**The Turn From Hypocrites to Selective Believers: The Dossier Changes Hands**

The sūrah's long hypocrisy-file (vv. 137–149) now closes, and the Book opens the final movement of the chapter — the address to the People of the Book that will run to the sūrah's end: *inna alladhīna yakfurūna bi-llāhi wa rusulih* — "verily those who disbelieve in God and His messengers": the commentators mark the structural shift (the standing note that the rest of the sūrah addresses the scriptural communities): the internal fifth column having been anatomized, the Book turns to the neighboring communities whose problem is not secret unbelief but *selective* belief — a taxonomy of partial assent. The verse's architecture compiles the four motions of this partiality in cumulative succession: (1) disbelieving in God and His messengers — the baseline charge; (2) *wa yurīdūna an yufarriqū bayna Allāhi wa rusulih* — "and wish to make a distinction between God and His messengers" — the severing of the Sender from the sent; (3) *wa yaqūlūna nuʾminu bi-baʿḍin wa nakfuru bi-baʿḍ* — "and say: we believe in some and disbelieve in some" — the fragmentation of the messengers themselves into accepted and refused lists; (4) *wa yurīdūna an yattakhidhū bayna dhālika sabīlan* — "and wish to take a way between" — the intention to construct a middle-road between belief and disbelief — the standing résumé of every cafeteria-religion: the menu-approach to revelation.

**Yufarriqūna Bayna Allāhi wa Rusulih: Severing the Sender From the Sent**

The second clause names a severance whose logic the commentators dissect from its two directions. The first: believing in God while refusing His messengers — the deist-minimum, the "we need no intermediaries" position — which the Book treats not as surplus-piety but as *separation of God from His own government*: the messengers are God's standing plenipotentiaries; to recognize the King while refusing His ambassador is to refuse the King's choice of administration — 3:31-32's standing linkage formula: "say: if you love God, follow me — God will love you... say: obey God and the Messenger — but if they turn away — verily God loves not the disbelievers." The second direction — accepted by some early readings — is the severing *among* the messengers: affirming this prophet and denying that one — acknowledgment made tribal rather than theological: "we accept our own" (2:91's standing exposure: 2:91 — "when it is said to them: believe in what God has sent down — they say: we believe in what was sent down to us — and they disbelieve in what is beyond it — though it is the truth confirming what is with them"; 2:87's chronicle — "whenever a messenger came to you with what your souls did not desire — you waxed arrogant — some you denied and some you slew"). Both directions meet at the standing counter-command — the creed-verse of 2:285 — "we make no distinction between any of His messengers" — *lā nufarriqu bayna aḥadin min rusulih* — the believers' self-description written precisely as the negative of this verse's charge; with its standing companions 2:136 ("say: we believe in God and in what was sent down to us and in what was sent down to Abraham and Ishmael and Isaac and Jacob and the Tribes... we make no distinction between any of them") and 3:84 — the archipelago of v. 136 above re-anchored: the articles of faith admit no partial subscription.

**A Way Between: The Middle That Does Not Exist**

The fourth clause names the aspiration and thereby exposes the illusion: *wa yurīdūna an yattakhidhū bayna dhālika sabīlan* — "and wish to take a way between": *bayna dhālika* — between that — between belief and disbelief — a middle-road sought where the Book will announce, in the next verse, that there is no such territory: the terrain between īmān and kufr being not a third country but a mirage (al-Zamakhsharī's standing note: the "way between" is in truth *within* the realm of disbelief — the middle position collapsing on audit into one of the two it straddles). The commentators connect the verse's anatomy to its standing historical referents — most prominently the Jewish and Christian communities of the Prophet's horizon who acknowledged their own prophets while refusing Muḥammad — though the phrases stand deliberately general (al-Rāzī's standing principle: the ruling follows the generality of the wording, not the specificity of the occasion): the selective subscriber exists in every age — the believer in "God" who declines prophets, the devotee of one messenger who resents another, the modern who keeps revelation's ethics while returning its cosmology. The verse's unfinished syntax — the sentence breaking off mid-relative-clause, its *verdict* withheld until v. 151 ("it is they who are truly disbelievers") — forms its own pedagogics: the description of the selective believer left hanging incomplete across the verse-break, imitating structurally the incompleteness of his creed — the sentence itself partial until God's own completion arrives: *ulāʾika humu al-kāfirūna ḥaqqan* — the Book demonstrating in its very grammar that a partial faith, like a partial verse, waits upon completion — and receives, from the other side of the period, the completion it did not hope for.

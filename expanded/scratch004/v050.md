## Sūrah al-Nisāʾ 4:50

> **See how they fabricate lies against God! That suffices as a manifest sin.**

**Expanded Commentary**

**Unẓur Kayfa: The Prophet Told to Watch the Lying Itself**

The verse strands one more look-command in the Prophet's ear but changes its tense into moral theater: *unẓur kayfa yaftarūna ʿalā Allāhi al-kadhib* — "Look — how they fabricate lies against God!": *unẓur* — look!, the sight-imperative; *kayfa* — *how!* — adverb turned exclamation (the same doublet engineered engineered into the scene-callers of 4:41's kayfa and beyond); and *yaftarūna* — the iftirāʾ-verb already planted into the bedrock of v. 48's seal ("fabricated a tremendous sin") now shown *in operation*: not any falsehood but a precise species: *kadhib ʿalā Allāh* — lying *upon God* — that is, attributing to Him authorizations authorization He never gave (the Book's own forensic definition at 6:93: "who does greater wrong than he who fabricates lies against God or says it was revealed to me when nothing was revealed"; 7:37; 10:17; 11:18; 29:68 — the family-name of perjury in the highest court: claiming *God said* when God did not). Al-Qurṭubī's report-chains tie the clause immediately to vv. 49's self-certificates: the claims *"we are forgiven every morning"*; *"the Garden is ours"*; *"we pairwise absolve each other"* — every one a lie on God's letterhead: imaginary indulgences printed inside the language of religion; and thus commentators expand the definition pastorally beyond its first denizens: *to teach indulgence as doctrine while heavens while heaven wrote no such indulgence is to forge against God* — whether in self-excuse ("God is forgiving — I need not change") or in license-sales to others ("your sin is small; pay no corn heed no consequence") — so that the sūrah's reader scans the verse with some terror of the tongue: *religious speech itself can be the forgery-site.*

**Kafā Bihā Ithman Mubīnā: The Sin Named by Its Own Self-Evidence**

The sentence — short, grave, absolute — is part of Islam's severest arithmetic: *wa kafā bihā ithman mubīnā* — "and it suffices as a manifest sin": *kafā bih* — "it is enough of it" — the book-keeping phrase by which a single entry's size is certified without need of further of further entries (the same *kafā*-formula deployed at 2:217 of hindering from God's path; at 4:6 above — *kafā bi-Allāhi ḥasībā* — God enough as reckoner; at 17:17 of the Master's knowledge of sins) — and the predicate *ithman mubīnā* — *a plain, glaring, manifest sin* — mubīn the self-showing flagrant — its indictment thereby being: *to require no evidence-chains; the thing proves itself.* The practical theology built on the clause by the doctors is one of the tradition's safeguard-fences: false speech in religion is not only a lie with sacred nounwords — it is the singular crime for which no balancing ledger of good deeds can excuse: hence the Prophet's frightening direct-counsel (al-Bukhārī 3505 the register attributing the wording variously; Tirmidhī the canonical line of Muslim standing the line standing firmest in Ṣaḥīḥ Muslim's introduction-admonitions the famous Maxim actually the direct Bukhārī-text):

> *"Whoever tells lies upon me deliberately, let him take his seat in the Fire."* (al-Bukhārī 110 & 1291; Muslim 3, in variations)

— the most reiterated single hadith in history precisely because forgery in God's (and His Messenger's) name is the one fraud that corrupts the vessel itself: every later vessel drinks from whatever the counterfeit left inside. The classical jurists extended its no-trade discipline across the whole religious economy: fabricated ḥadīth false classified the greatest of sins after shirk; mis-citation of the Book for policy classified under iftirāʾ; and even well-intended piety-fable (the lies of the story-tellers in the mosques, whose phenomenon al-Bukhārī's register brands names its own spread) placed under *kadhib ʿalā Allāh* when it issues doctrine.

**The Self-Certifier and the Forger: One Corridor**

The pericope's two halves (vv. 49–50: self-purifying, then God-lying) are hereby seen as one room — for every false claim about the self in God's name — *I am pure, we are children, the Garden is ours, penance by evening* — is logically *special pleading from the special pleading with the throne as as guarantor*: the soul putting God's seal beneath its own press-release. The sufi-commentary grafts its own razor onto the verse (al-Qushayrī's circle marking the deeper law: pleasing oneself with one's own soul *extinguishes the lights of certitude*; self-trust in one's purity *the veil itself*) — and the wisdom of the traditions closes the corridor at its exit with the purified heart's definition — the only true tazkiyah being the one God grants at Judgment. The reader's practical sum takes a breath-length: never vouch for your own soul; never coin a doctrine to defend it; audit yourself mercilessly, and let God audit you mercifully — for one thing alone *suffices* as a sin irreparable while it stands: a forgery upon God's own name — *kafā bihā ithman mubīnā*: it suffices.

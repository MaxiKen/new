## Sūrah al-Nisāʾ 4:57

> **But for those who believe and perform righteous deeds, We shall cause them to enter Gardens with rivers running below, abiding therein forever. There they shall have spouses made pure, and We shall cause them to enter plenteous shade.**

**Expanded Commentary**

**Wa Alladhīna Āmanū — the Door Swung Opposite**

Heaven's arithmetic of the furnace is answered in the Book's perpetual twin-habit with its garden-clause: *wa alladhīna āmanū wa ʿamilū al-ṣāliḥāti sanudkhiluhum jannātin tajrī min taḥtihā al-anhāru khālidīna fīhā abadā* — "and those who believe and work righteous deeds — We shall cause them to enter Gardens beneath which rivers flow, abiding therein forever": the syntax deliberately mirrors the whole Qur'an's twin-engine (belief *and* works — the twin-condition that runs from the Fātiḥah's gate straight through every gate to every estate of the Hereafter — never one alone: cf. the countersignature at 2:25, 2:82, 4:122 below, 18:107, 25:70's repentance-exchange): deeds married to trust; trust proven by deeds. *Sanudkhiluhum* — We *cause them to enter* — the same first-person agency of v. 56 reversed for bliss (the commentators noting the courtesy-architecture: God Himself admits both cases, though to opposite addresses); *jannāt*, gardens, in the common plural (its stations exhaustive richly catalogued in the Tradition — Firdaws the highest; ʿAdn the Abode of residence; Naʿīm of Delight; Maʾwā of Refuge); *tajrī min taḥtihā al-anhār* — rivers running *beneath them* — the grade-bene the superlative courtesy-image of mastery (rivers *underneath* — their water-subjects, al-Ṭabarī style: not scenery to visit but estates to own — the hadith of the four rivers — 76:6–18's fountains named: Kafūr for God's own cupbearers, Salsabīl, Tasnīm for those brought near — Muslim 2830's with the famous river of al-Kawthar as the Prophet's own announced stream in the Garden, Muslim 400); *khālidīna fīhā abadā* — abiding forever — *combined* eternity-doublet (kh-l-d inside, abadan appended — the doublet for emphasis al-Zamakhsharī explains at such joins: *dwelling* mortgaged to *endlessness* — no lease, no move, no sunset-clause on bliss): everything the fire-quarters lacked — release-turnings, its *kullamā*-loop of skin-replacements — answered here by a *kullamā* of a different disposition (its counter its own loop-resident at 2:25: *every time they are provisioned therefrom and say: this is what we were provisioned before — and they are brought its like*).

**Lahum Fīhā Azwājun Muṭahharah: The Purified Consorts**

The next item upon the estate-list is the companionship-crown: *wa lahum fīhā azwājun muṭahharatun* — "therein they shall have purified spouses": *azwāj* — spouses, consorts — a word faithful to the Book's whole married theology (wherever the Garden is fully described the consorts attend — 2:25's *purified spouses*, 3:15's *purified spouses*, 4:57 here, and its decoration at 56:36–38's chamber-nymphs, 37:48–49's restrained-eyed companions, 55:56, 72–74's untouched ones) — and the adjective *muṭahharah* — purified — the ṭ-h-r root of cleanness, delivering the commentators into the delighted delicacy of their ritual-books: purified of every imperfection the world's companions could carry — of menses and childbirth-soilings (al-Qurṭubī's listing); of excretions and of secretions; of bad temper and wrangling; of envy, resentment, and the jarring of married-life the sūrah itself legislated against at vv. 19, 34–35, 128–130 — so that the adjective is at once *hygienic and characterological*: the sūrah's own household-crisis trios answered by a heaven where every shadow of home has been rinsed away. The Prophet's hadith already supplied the harmony-module corroborating the adjective (al-Bukhārī 3251; Muslim 2834 — from Abū Hurayrah's register of the believers' garden-architecture): *"their hearts shall be as the heart of one man; there is neither rancor nor hatred among them"* (al-Bukhārī 3249–50 and the parallel registers of Muslim) — one-heart companionship as the inside-matching of *muṭahharah*: spousal purity and communal single-heartedness both calibrated to the sūrah's recurrent dream — the Home perfected — with the sufi-readers' prism (al-Qushayrī's line of comment) seeing even the consorts' company as the whole heart's *transparency*, purified finally of the body's dividers — the veils of the old clay.

**Wa Nudkhiluhum Ẓillan Ẓalīlā: The Plenteous Shade — Closing of the Aridity**

The final item is a single doubled word: *wa nudkhiluhum ẓillan ẓalīlā* — "and We cause them to enter shade abundant": *ẓill*, shade; *ẓalīl*, its adjective-intensity — vast, spreading, plenteous (33 the tawālur the tradition glossing: a shade extending as far as the eye's range — extending forever — the report-classes expanding the shade-metaphors of the Garden into canopy-horses of the Tradition — the shade-tree of Ṭūbā beneath which a rider could ride a century without escaping its shade — the hadith-file of the Blessed Tree (Tirmidhī 3283 — with Ḥakīm's amplifications) standing nearest hand in the commentators' page) — the desert-born image chosen to crown the entire promise: *after the furnace, shade* — the Book's own closing arithmetic of the pericope — skin-fire replaced by skin-shade. And the received tradition (al-Ṭabarī, Ibn Kathīr) had always loved to fold v. 56–57's pair into its eschatology of *reciprocity*: beginnings reversed, *bodies* reversed, *consorts* reversed, *covers* reversed (skins consumed ↔ canopies spread): for the Garden even the air-side of architecture is mercy-climate: no sun-dart, no skin-burn — *a world built for dwelling* — khālidīna fīhā abadā — the fire-verse's nowhere answered by the garden-verse's everywhere — the pericope's twin-gates now both standing open in print — with the reader still poised in the unsealed middle: *choose; the admission-office answers to āmanū wa ʿamilū al-ṣāliḥāt alone.*

## Sūrah al-Nisāʾ 4:101

> **And when you travel upon the earth, there is no blame upon you for shortening your prayers if you fear that the disbelievers may attack you; verily the disbelievers are unto you a manifest enemy.**

**Expanded Commentary**

**Wa Idhā Ḍarabtum fī al-Arḍi fa-Laysa ʿAlaykum Junāḥun an Taqṣurū mina al-Ṣalāh: The Road-and-Rukhsah Chapter**

After hijrah's estate hijrah's testament comes the traveler's rite — the sūrah now descending from emigration's life-stakes to the conduct of ordinary wayfaring: *wa idhā ḍarabtum fī al-arḍi fa-laysa ʿalaykum junāḥun an taqṣurū mina al-ṣalāh* — "and when you march-travel in the earth, there is no blame upon you that you shorten the prayer": *ḍarabtum* again — the road-threshing verb of v. 94 (the sūrah's standing lexicon pairing: traveling-as-striking — the journey written as blows upon the expanse beneath) — *junāḥ* — blame, sin-leaning crookedness (from j-n-ḥ — wing-side — the standing ethical image of the law: to slant lean over — yet here God declares the road shortening over unslanted); *taqṣurū* — from q-ṣ-r — shorten (the qasr-injunction — the four-unit prayer reduced to two — the canon-paired mercy: zuhr-ʿasr-ʿishāʾ halved for the wayfarer  — the Sunnah itself detailing its full conduct rules (the journey-measure, the settling-count, the joining-extensions) in the standing registers of al-Bukhārī 1090 and Muslim 686 — with the tradition’s standing classroom-question preserved beside them: the Companions asking why the road-traveler shortens his prayer yet keeps his fast — and the schools’ enduring answer rising from it: prayer is *compressed within itself*, while fasting requires day-wholeness — the two rukhsahs carrying different shapes) — and the sūrah’s key-clause following with its topic: *in khiftum an yaftinakum alladhīna kafarū* — “if you fear that the unbelievers may attack you”: *fitnah* here — assault-trial (the commentators' standing gloss: the fitnah of the war-road — ambush, sword, and capture) — and the standing debate-questions of the juristic tradition right that has followed this clause through four centuries of canon: does qaṣr shortening depend on *fear* or on *travel* itself the majority register (Ḥanafī except with the standing positions: (1) the shortening upon all travel — the majority, on the Sunnah's custom the Sunnah's own general practice — the Prophet shortening *whenever* he journeyed, war or peace alike — ʿUmar's own reply to Ibn ʿUmar's when ʿUmar was asked why shortening continues though war-conditions have passed (Bukhārī Muslim 686's register): "I wondered at what you wonder at, so I asked the Messenger of God — and he said: *it is a ṣadaqah which God has given you — accept His ṣadaqah*" — the road-mercy being thereby not only fearwork but *giftwork* (ṣadaqah!) — charity from the road from the Lord of the road, handed to every rider of the ages); (2) the war-prayer's separate with v. 102 below descending laying the *salāt al-khawf* — the prayer-of-fear itself — as the *separate* fear-content statute, the present verse awarding two rukhsan topics at once: road-shortening plus battle-shortening both stacked across vv. 101–102 — fear-clause riding as a *readiness-condition* of the wild-road era more than as the license-condition for the rite (al-Rāzī's standing reconciliation: fear-pattern through the earliest practice — war-era journeys — then displayed again as habit codified — rukhsah of God covering all roads thereafter).

**Inna al-Kāfirīna Kānū Lakum ʿAduwwan Mubīnā: The Manifest-Enemy Clause**

The verse then closes by naming the strategic constant that had shaped these mercy-laws: *inna al-kāfirīna kānū lakum ʿaduwwan mubīnā* — "verily the unbelievers are unto you a manifest enemy": *ʿaduwwan mubīnā* — enemy *manifest, patent* (the Book's own attribute-standing applying mubīn to enmities: 2:168's — "Satan is to you a manifest enemy"; 12:5's — of Satan to man; 43:62's — of Satan to man again; now applied to the outward confeder to the external host the outward armed confederate to the armed unbelieving confederates at hand on the early roads) — the commentators' pastoral annotation: God names enemies *openly* so that the believers are taught *legally*: precaution is not cowardice (the warning ends the verse for a reason — shortening prayer on a dangerous road isn't worshipless convenience — it is *armed-sentry piety*: the same motive-line of v. 9's standing counsel upon leaving heirs of v. 9 — and of the tether-your-camel doctrine of v. 71 — taking protection *is itself part of the order of God*) — with the standing jurisprudential history delivering the pragmatic articulation: the early community traveled among amb through ambush-lands (raids, pursued envoys, hostile encirclement) — so the Road-Code of Islam was *hardened for war*: qasr of prayer (v. 101), joining of prayers (the Sunnah adding it), the prayer-of-fear in combat (v. 102), arms never laid down even during worship — and yet — the point the commentators keep returning to — the same Book that hardened the war-rite kept the peace-door open all through vv. 90–94 — the peace-offerer's hand, the constricted breast's immunity — the whole war-piece made in one breath: *march, pray shortened, hold your arms — and let every peace-token catch you ready*.

## Sūrah al-Nisāʾ 4:10

> **Truly those who consume the property of orphans unjustly only consume fire in their bellies, and they will burn in a blazing flame.**

**Expanded Commentary**

**The Meal That Is Already Fire**

The orphan-pericope reaches its terrifying culmination with the Book's most incinerating metaphor of consumption: *inna alladhīna yaʾkulūna amwāla al-yatāmā ẓulman innamā yaʾkulūna fī buṭūnihim nāran* — "truly those who devour the orphans' wealth unjustly are only devouring fire into their bellies." The grammar is itself the sentence: not *will* devour but *yaʾkulūna* — the present-continuous of an eating already imaged at its end — and the restrictive *innamā* — *only* fire — hollowing the meal's every pretense of nourishment: what passes their throats is not provision but fuel, stored. Al-Rāzī anatomizes the trope with the coldness it warrants: fire is the perfect sign of this robbery because it is invisible-in-potency at the meal and total-in-act at the end — as the consumed dinars warmth the tyrant's table through years without a visible flame, so the sin burns him nowhere until, in one unveiling, its whole hidden inventory ignites. Al-Qurṭubī registers the scholars' consensus location of the verse in the scale of crimes: no lesser sin than the gravest class — *kabīrah* of the kabāʾir — shares its imagery; and its neighbor across the Book is only one: the ribā-consumer's arising like one demon-maddened (2:275), the two satans' banquet-halls of the orphan-vault and the usurer's table furnished with one kind of furniture.

**The Ghatafānian's Morning: When the Sentence Walked into Madinah**

The verse was no abstraction to its first hearers; the transmitters record the famous awakening of its occasion-case. Ibn ʿAbbās's pupil-generation report — treasured in al-Ṭabarī's chains and echoed by al-Tirmidhī's tafsīr of the passage — tells it: a man of Ghaṭafān had held in trust a great fortune belonging to his orphan nephew, and when the youth came of age the uncle denied him; the case reached the Prophet ﷺ — and the fire-verse descended. Whereupon, the reports complete:

> *The man came at daybreak carrying the wealth entire, and said: "I deliver to my nephew his property — and I seek refuge with God from the blazing flame (al-saʿīr)."*

The whole pericope's court-procedure thus closes with a real repentance: trust disgorged, sentence escaped, on a Madinan morning where the words had scarcely finished reciting. The jurists and preachers have taught from that morning ever since: the fire-image is not rhetoric to be admired but *a verdict to be exited* — and the exit-door is exactly what the uncle found: restitution, in full, while the hour of return still stands open; for the verse's tense-structure leaves no other negotiation: the eating continues as long as the eaters continue, and its end is named in the seal.

**Saʿīr: The Furnace-Word of the Sentence**

*Wa sa-yaṣlawna saʿīrā* — "and they shall roast in a burning blaze": the Book's *saʿīr* is Hell as furnace-kindled, its fuel stoked (the verb *saʿʿara* — to set blazing — standing behind the noun), reserved in this sūrah's own registers for the severest addresses (4:55 and 4:56's kafā bi-jahannama saʿīrā for the Book-people's obstinacy; 48:6's for polytheists and hypocrites; 76:4's prepared-for-the-ingrates chains and yokes and blaze). The Prophet ﷺ was shown their working-dress in the vision the mufassirūn cite beneath this verse — Ibn Kathīr adduces it from the Musnad of Aḥmad, from Abū Hurayrah:

> *"On the Night of the Ascent I passed by people whose lips were like the lips of camels; and their lips were clipped with scissors of fire — then cut pieces of fire were thrust into their mouths, and they came forth from their rear passages."* He asked: *"Who are these?"* — Gabriel said: *"Those who consume the property of the orphans unjustly."*

The image fulfills the verse's arithmetic exactly: mouths that consumed at Madinah's quiet tables are fitted at last with the cuisine of their diet, the shears of flame standing to the severing-spades the taklif of just guardianship had once made needless. And the seal's future-particle *sa-yaṣlawna* keeps the appointment fixed: roasting-in-blaze is scheduled, not suggested: *ṣalāya*, the broiling of direct flame-contact, unnamed but one day away from each belly that loaded it.

**The Door That Remained Open: Repentance with Restitution**

Yet the sūrah, having burned the pericope's circle closed, leaves by its own larger architecture the one crevice of hope it always leaves — the chapter's own general absolution-regime at vv. 17–18, 27–28, and 146-148, and the standing rule the scholars drew from the Ghatafānian's own morning: the fire of v. 10 is escaped *the way it was entered — through the property*: return the estate, seek forgiveness for the delay, and the meal is voided at the ledger where it was loaded. Ibn Kathīr quotes the salaf's equivalent at this clause: *"repentance from the consuming of orphans' wealth is in giving back; there is no dua-substitute."* A believer reading v. 10 therefore reads precisely as its first defendant read: inventory first — what is in my house of another's weakness — restitution second; and the pericope's full court may adjourn in the Ghaṭafānian's own words, which should belong to every trustee's dawn: *here is all that was entrusted — and refuge is sought from the blaze. The Lawgiver, having guarded the meekest vaults in the earth, now turns to the double-sided mirror of the grave's other room: the division where the fire never approaches — the fractions of vv. 11–13.*

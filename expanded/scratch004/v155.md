## Sūrah al-Nisāʾ 4:155

> **Then, for their breaking of their covenant, and their disbelieving in the signs of God, and their slaying of the prophets without right, and their saying: "Our hearts are uncircumcised" — Nay! God has set a seal upon them for their disbelief, so they believe not, save a few —**

**Expanded Commentary**

**Fa-Bi-Mā Naqḍihim Mīthāqahum: The Indictment Opens With the Breach**

The verse begins mid-sentence — a *fa-* of causation whose promised consequence arrives at v. 160 ("and We forbade them good things that had been lawful") — but which first assembles, in verses 155–157, the standing bill of indictment: *fa-bi-mā naqḍihim mīthāqahum* — "then, for their breaking of their covenant": *naqḍ* — from n-q-ḍ — unravelling, undoing a knitted thing (its standing Book-file: 13:20-25 — "those who break God's covenant after contracting it, and sever what God has commanded to be joined... theirs is the curse"; 16:91-92 — the covenant not to be unspun like the spun thread — "fulfill God's covenant when you covenant"; 2:27's standing opening-file of Sūrah al-Baqarah: the breakers of God's covenant after confirming it) — the *mīthāq* of v. 154 — thick, mountain-witnessed, gate-and-Sabbath structured — undone thread by thread. The standing cross-register of the breach-file: 2:83-85 (the covenant recited — worship God alone, goodness to parents, near kin, orphans, the needy; shed not your blood nor expel one another — then the breach itemized); 5:12-13 (the covenant with the twelve chieftains — "they broke their covenant — so We cursed them and hardened their hearts"); 5:70 — the covenant taken, the messengers sent — "whenever a messenger came to them with what their souls did not desire, some they denied and some they slew."

**Wa Kufr Him bi-Āyāti Allāh: Disbelief in the Signs as Selective Reception**

The second count: *wa kufrihim bi-āyāti Allāh* — "and their disbelieving in the signs of God": *āyāt* — the standing double-unit of revelation (the verse of the Book and the portent in the world — 17:101's standing nine signs; 41:53 — "We shall show them Our signs in the horizons and in themselves") — and al-Ṭabarī's standing gloss localizes the charge's exact shape: they did not disbelieve in *all* signs uniformly; they received Torah-signs and refused what came after — the standing selective-reception already legislated at vv. 150–151 ("we believe in some and disbelieve in some") — this verse historical-documenting what those verses doctrinally define: the refusal of particular revelations (most pointedly Jesus, as v. 157 will detail; and ultimately — sūrah-addressed — Muḥammad) constituting *kufr bi-āyāti Allāh* — disbelief in God's signs *as such*: because the sign belongs to the Sender, partial reception is refusal of the Sender's sovereignty to address.

**Wa Qatlihimu al-Anbiyāʾa Bi-Ghayri Ḥaqq: The Prophet-Killing File**

The third count names the darkest institutional crime in the Book's history of covenant-people: *wa qatlihimu al-anbiyāʾa bi-ghayri ḥaqq* — "and their slaying of the prophets without right": a charge the Book levels repeatedly — and always with the phrase *bi-ghayri al-ḥaqq / bi-ghayri ḥaqq* — "without right" — the jurisprudential precision marking that these were not judicial executions but murders wearing judicial robes (its standing register: 2:61 — "and they slew the prophets without right — that because they disobeyed and used to transgress"; 2:91 — "why did you slay the prophets of God aforetime, if you are believers?"; 3:21 — "those who disbelieve in God's signs and slay the prophets without right and slay those who command justice among men — give them glad tidings of a painful punishment"; 3:112 — "they slew the prophets without right"; 3:181-183 — the standing pair with āyāt and fire-sacrifice taunt; 5:70) — the commentators (al-Ṭabarī's chains naming the conventional cases: Zechariah and John in the standing tafsīr-file; the traditions of the sawn prophet Isaiah in the standing report-literature) treating the count not as a handful of criminal episodes but as an *established culpability-pattern*: the standing rejectionists' terminal argument against inconvenient revelation being — where possible — the elimination of its bearer. And the Book's own structural irony is sharp here: the people of the *Book* — custodians of prophecy's registry — stand charged with homicide against prophecy itself: the archives guarded, the ambassadors killed.

**Wa Qawlihim Qulūbunā Ghulfun: "Our Hearts Are Uncircumcised" — and the Counter-Sealing**

The fourth count quotes their own defense and convicts them by it: *wa qawlihim qulūbunā ghulfun* — "and their saying: our hearts are uncircumcised": *ghulf* — the plural (or collective adjective) of *aghlaf* — uncircumcised, hence in the standing lexical gloss: *covered, sheathed, enwrapped* — the boast reading: our hearts are sheathed against your summons — receptivity refused on surgical grounds. The commentators (al-Ṭabarī's standing pairing with 2:88 — "and they said: our hearts are uncircumcised — nay, God has cursed them for their disbelief — little do they believe") unfold the double-resonance: (1) the literal community-marker: circumcision stood among them as the bodily sign of covenant-membership — so the claim "our hearts are *un*circumcised" exposes the covenant of these speakers as skin-deep — membership marked in flesh but never penetrating to the heart (the standing scriptural echo in their own register: Leviticus' "uncircumcised hearts" and Jeremiah's indictment running in parallel within the prior text-tradition the commentators knew); (2) the psychology of the boast: the sheathing presented as incapacity ("we *cannot* receive") while functioning as policy ("we *will not* receive") — 41:5's standing parallel-exposure of the same stratagem among later hearers ("our hearts are under coverings from what you call us to"). And the Book's answer is the standing verdict-formula of divine sealing: *bal ṭabaʿa Allāhu ʿalayhā bi-kufrihim* — "nay! God has set a seal upon them for their disbelief": *ṭabʿ* — sealing (its standing register: 2:7 — "God has sealed their hearts and their hearing"; 7:100-101; 9:93; 16:108; 30:59; 40:35; 42:24; 63:3) — and the theology of the seal here made deliberately judicial: *bi-kufrihim* — *for / because of* their disbelief — the sealing expressly a *consequence*, not a pre-dawn decree: the standing majority-reading of the commentators that the seal is God's punishment answering human transgression (10:74's standing sequencing — "thus do We seal the hearts of the transgressors") — their chosen unreceptivity ratified from above, the boastful incapacity made, by decree, actual.

**Fa-Lā Yuʾminūna illā Qalīlan: The Few — the Crack in the Seal**

The verse ends with the standing qualification that keeps the indictment from erasing the remnant: *fa-lā yuʾminūna illā qalīlan* — "so they believe not, save a few": with the standing alternative parsing registered by the commentators (al-Rāzī gathering both): *illā qalīlan* may read "save a few" (persons — the ʿAbd Allāh ibn Salām-file: members of the community who broke through to belief in the Prophet) or "save a little" (of belief — partial assent remaining: Torah-confessing while refusing later revelation — the 2:88 pattern) — and the sūrah's own development favors the held-open door: v. 136's archipelago commanded belief in *all* the books and prophets; v. 152 rewarded the undivided believers without ethnic carve-out; and the People-of-the-Book movement of the chapter (running to v. 162's own *al-rāsikhūna fī al-ʿilmi minhum... wa al-muʾminūna* — "those firmly rooted in knowledge among them, and the believers, believe in what was sent down to thee") will shortly name the remnant it here excepts — the standing Qur'ānic pattern of indictment-with-exemption (3:110's "among them are believers"; 3:113-115's standing righteous-cohort file among the People of the Book; 3:199 — "among the People of the Book are those who believe in God and in what was sent down to you and what was sent down to them — humble toward God... theirs is their wage with their Lord"). The verse thus performs the standing jurisprudence of collective judgment: the community as covenant-bearer indicted in its institutional counts — breach, selective disbelief, prophet-slaughter, the sheathed-heart boast — and the seal imposed at institutional level ("upon *them*") — while *illā qalīlan* preserves, inside the sealed edifice, the standing window through which individuals always still may climb — the seal upon hearts never being the abolition of the Few but the explanation of their scarcity.

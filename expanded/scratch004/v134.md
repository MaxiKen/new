## Sūrah al-Nisāʾ 4:134

> **Whosoever desires the reward of this world — with God is the reward of this world and the Hereafter; and God is ever Hearing, Seeing.**

**Expanded Commentary**

**Man Kāna Yurīdu Thawāba al-Dunyā: The World-Seeker Addressed Without Insult**

After the replacement-warning of v. 133, the Book turns to the motives that drive its hearers — and does so with a striking gentleness: *man kāna yurīdu thawāba al-dunyā fa-ʿinda Allāhi thawābu al-dunyā wa al-ākhirah* — "whosoever desires the reward of this world — with God is the reward of this world and the Hereafter": *thawāb* — from th-w-b — returning, recompense: the reward conceived as what *comes back* upon the doer of deeds (its standing Book-file: 3:148 — "God gave them the reward of this world and the best reward of the Hereafter"; 3:195 — "their Lord answered them: I shall not waste the work of any worker among you"); and the syntax carries the commentators' standing wonder: the verse does not scold the world-desire but *redirects it to the right supplier* — you want this world's reward? Then seek it *from God* — for both this world's distribution and the next's are stocked in His one treasury. Al-Zamakhsharī marks the positive reading explicitly: those who struggle sincerely for religion may receive the reward of both worlds — the Book's own standing double-reward file confirming: 12:56–57 — Joseph "established in the land" in this world, then "and the reward of the Hereafter is best"; 3:146–148 — the patient fighters beside the prophets given "the reward of this world and the best reward of the Hereafter — and God loves the good-doers"; 29:27 — Abraham given his reward in this world, "and in the Hereafter he shall surely be among the righteous"; 28:14 — Moses at maturity granted wisdom and knowledge — reward arriving mid-biography, "thus do We reward the good-doers."

**The Ominous Side: Ṭuʿmah's Advocates and the Ironic Thawāb**

Al-Ṭabarī, however, records the reading that darkens the verse — and its plausibility anchors itself in the sūrah's own recent history: the file of Ṭuʿmah ibn Ubayriq (vv. 105–115), whose advocates within Madīnah had displayed belief outwardly in order to gather the *world's* reward — the booty of fighting alongside the Prophet and the general protection that Muslim identity conferred in the city. For such, the verse reads as standing irony: they pursued thawāb al-dunyā and obtained it — and "the reward of this world and the Hereafter" becomes, in their case, an ominous double-entry: this world paying its wage in full, and the Hereafter holding its own recompense for the hypocrisy — the counter-position the Book runs in its standing contrasts ("whoever desires the harvest of the Hereafter — We increase him in his harvest; and whoever desires the harvest of this world — We give him of it — but in the Hereafter he has no portion" — 42:20; "whosoever desires the fleeting life — We hasten for him therein what We will, for whom We will — then We appoint for him hell" — 17:18; 3:145; 33:28–29 — the Prophet's wives asked to choose between the good of this world and God and His Messenger and the Abode of the Hereafter). The tradition adds its standing intention-verdict — the celebrated hadith (Bukhārī 1; Muslim 1907): "actions are but by intentions, and every man shall have only what he intended — so whoever's migration was for the sake of worldly benefit or for a woman he marries — his migration is for that to which he migrated": the Hijrah itself — the glory-act of the community — convertible into mere commerce by its inner aim.

**The Standing Prayer of the Middle Position**

Yet the Book's mercy builds a bridge between the two readings — and the commentators (beginning with al-Zamakhsharī's note) direct the reader to the standing prayer that resolves the verse's tension: 2:201 — "but among them are those who say: our Lord — give us good in this world and good in the Hereafter — and shield us from the punishment of the Fire" — the ḥasanah-in-both-worlds petition, which the Prophet and the community made the standing daily supplication of the ummah (the tradition recording that the most frequent of the Prophet's supplications was precisely this verse — its standing register in Bukhārī and Muslim's ḥadīth-collections of his most-repeated prayers — "our Lord — give us good in this world and good in the Hereafter"). The verse before us thus legislates the honest middle position between world-contempt and world-idolatry: this world's reward is not denounced — it is *located*: *fa-ʿinda Allāhi* — "with God is..." — the preposition of presence and custody — the seeker directed to the shelf where both treasuries stand side by side. To desire the world's good *from God* is commerce with heaven; to desire it *instead of* God is the Ṭuʿmah-file; and to desire it *for* God — as means for worship, family, charity — stands among the standing marks of the prophetic life itself.

**Wa Kāna Allāhu Samīʿan Baṣīran: The Two Names That Audit Desire**

The seal posts the two auditors of intention: *wa kāna Allāhu samīʿan baṣīran* — "and God is ever Hearing, Seeing": the standing Name-pair of perception (its stations: 4:58 above — "God is ever Hearing, Seeing"; 17:1's standing exordium; 22:61; 22:75; 31:28; 40:20; 40:56; 42:11 — "nothing is as His likeness — and He is the Hearing, the Seeing"; 58:1) — and its fittingness here exact: the verse concerns *desires* — the innermost cargo of the heart — and so its audit requires precisely these two Names: the Samīʿ who hears the world's-reward asked aloud and the bargain whispered inwardly, and the Baṣīr who sees the intention beneath the migration, the aim beneath the action — the standing pair guarding the standing verse so that no hearer mistakes the gentleness of "with God is the reward of this world" for blindness about *why* the reward was sought. Between v. 133's Qadīr (power to replace peoples) and v. 135's imminent justice-summit, the Samīʿ-Baṣīr seal keeps the examination intimate: the Book having warned the community as a mass now addresses the single desiring heart — you who desire the world's reward — the One holding both worlds hears your asking and sees your aim: ask, therefore, carefully — and ask for both.

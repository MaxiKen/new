## Sūrah al-Nisāʾ 4:133

> **If He wills, He shall remove you, O mankind, and bring others; and God is ever Able to do that.**

**Expanded Commentary**

**In Yashaʾ Yudhhibkum Ayyuhā al-Nās: The Replaceability Announcement**

From the sufficiency of God as Guardian, the verse turns to the dark-mirror doctrine that only the Self-Sufficient can safely announce: *in yashaʾ yudhhibkum ayyuhā al-nāsu wa yaʾti bi-ākharīn* — "if He wills, He shall remove you, O mankind, and bring others": *yudhhibkum* — from dh-h-b — take away, cause to depart — removal entire; *wa yaʾti bi-ākharīn* — "and bring others" — the verb of arrival bringing a replacement-creation to stand where the removed stood. The address — *ayyuhā al-nās* — "O mankind" — is the Book's universal vocative (its standing stations: 2:21 — "O mankind — worship your Lord"; 4:1 — the sūrah's opening exordium; 22:1 — "O mankind — be mindful of your Lord"; 35:15 — "O mankind — you are the poor unto God") — and its placement here universalizes the warning deliberately: commentators (al-Ṭabarī, al-Rāzī, Ibn Kathīr) note the verse is not aimed at one clan or one era but at the species-standing assumption that the current tenants are indispensable — nearly all the early commentators reading the removal comprehensively: God can remove *existing human beings altogether* and bring others — or, as al-Qurṭubī adds the wider possibility, another kind of creation entirely who might serve Him better than the disobedient. The verse therefore stands as the negative pedal of v. 131's charge: taqwā had been commanded; disbelief had been announced as possible; and now the cost — not to God (He is Ghanī, v. 131) but to the disbelieving tenants — replacement.

**The Substitute-Peoples Register: The Book's Standing File of Removal-and-Succession**

The commentators gather here the Book's whole standing file on divine substitution — a file the sūrah has already drawn upon and will draw upon again: 6:133 — "your Lord is the Self-Sufficient, Possessor of Mercy — if He wills, He shall remove you and appoint successors after you whomsoever He wills — as He raised you up from the posterity of another people" — the standing parallel whose coupling of Ghanī with removal the present passage mirrors (v. 131's Ghanī → v. 133's removal; 6:133's Ghanī → removal); 5:54 — already the sūrah's own standing anticipation — "O you who believe — whoever of you turns back from his religion — God shall bring a people He loves and who love Him..." — replacement-by-better-love; 14:19–20 — "have you not seen that God created the heavens and the earth in truth — if He wills, He shall remove you and bring a new creation — nor is that aught difficult for God" — the removal announced with its standing shrug; 47:38 — "and if you turn away, He shall substitute another people for you — and they shall not be the likes of you" — replacement specified as *improvement* for God, not loss; 70:40–41 — "We are able to substitute better than they"; 76:28 — "We created them and strengthened their frames — and when We will, We shall substitute their likes for them, substitution entire"; 9:39 — the march-or-perish verse — "if you march not forth, He shall punish you with a painful punishment and shall substitute a people other than you — nor shall you harm Him at all" — the standing formula *wa lā taḍurruhū shayʾan* annihilating any delusion that stubbornness injures heaven. The register, laid end to end, composes what Ibn Taymiyyah calls the law of divine disposability-of-nations: history contains no irreplaceable people.

**Salmān's Back: The Standing Report of Identification**

The verse carries one of the most remarked identification-anecdotes in the whole tafsīr corpus: the standing report (al-Qurṭubī and al-Zamakhsharī's standing citation — and its transmission-lines through Muqātil's and others' tafsīr-matān) that when this verse came down — or its sister-text 47:38 ("if you turn away, He shall substitute another people...") — and the Prophet was asked "who are those who would be brought?" — he placed his hand upon the back or shoulder of Salmān al-Fārsī — Salmān the Persian, the Companion whose search for truth had carried him from Zoroastrian Isfahan to Christian Syria to the bondage of a Jewish household in Yathrib and finally to the Prophet — and said: "this man and his people" — meaning the Persians — and in some transmissions he added words that made the point entire: "were faith suspended at the Pleiades — *law kāna al-īmān muʿallaqan bi-al-thurayyā* — men from among them would surely reach it." The commentators unfold the anecdote's standing lessons in several directions: (1) ethnicity is no tenure — the *ākharūn* — the "others" of the verse — may come from a nation the first audience considered marginal: the Persian stranger serving as prophetic emblem of God's freedom to re-appoint the bearers of His religion; (2) the replacement may already stand inside the congregation unrecognized — Salmān sitting in the Prophet's own circle while the question was asked, the future of the ummah leaning unsuspected on a foreigner's back; (3) and the standing historical reading — ventured by the historians of tafsīr — that the Muslim eastward expansions, the Persian scholarly flowerings (the compilers of the Six Books themselves largely of Persian origin: al-Bukhārī of Bukhārā; Muslim of Nishapur; al-Tirmidhī; al-Nasāʾī; Abū Dāwūd of Sijistān; Ibn Mājah of Qazvin), read to many later commentators as the long echo of the Prophet's gesture: God had indeed brought "others" — and their service had indeed sounded the heights.

**Wa Kāna Allāhu ʿAlā Dhālika Qadīran: Power Appended to Threat**

The seal converts warning to certainty: *wa kāna Allāhu ʿalā dhālika qadīran* — "and God is ever Able to do that": *qadīr* — from q-d-r — power, capacity, the standing attribute-name of omnipotence (2:20 — "God is over all things Able"; 2:148; 3:26's standing roll; 11:4; 18:45; 29:20; 33:27 — the sūrah-flanked "and God is ever over all things Able"; 46:33's question-answer — "is not He who created the heavens and the earth able to create the likes of them — surely — and He is the Creator, the Knowing"; 67:1's closing tribute) — the attribute here appearing in its deliberate grammatical posture: *ʿalā dhālika* — "over *that*" — the demonstrative pointing back at the very act just announced (removal-and-replacement), as if to nail the power-statement to this specific threat lest any hearer file it under impossibilities. Al-Rāzī's standing scholastic observation closes the sequence: v. 131 established God's independence (Ghanī) — needing no people; v. 132 established His adequacy (Kafā) — needing no partners; v. 133 establishes His capability (Qadīr) — needing no tenants; so that the whole triad composes one standing lesson for every community, then and now: the heavens and the earth run at full divine sufficiency, and humanity's lease remains as contingent as its reverence — the Owner, Guardian, and Powerful One being at every moment able to sign other names into the tenancy.

## Sūrah al-Nisāʾ 4:151

> **It is they who are the disbelievers in truth, and We have prepared for the disbelievers a humiliating punishment.**

**Expanded Commentary**

**Ulāʾika Humu al-Kāfirūna Ḥaqqan: The Caption That Closes the Case**

The verse that completes the sentence broken off at v. 150 issues the Book's standing definition-verdict, one of its most emphatic grammatical forms: *ulāʾika humu al-kāfirūna ḥaqqan* — "it is they who are the disbelievers in truth": the pronoun-of-separation (*faṣl*) *hum* between subject and predicate locking the equation — *they*, and no others — plus *ḥaqqan* — the accusative of emphasis, "in truth, veritably": the selective believers officially registered as *real* disbelievers — the word *ḥaqqan* doing the devastating work: their creed may be partial, but their classification is complete. The commentators (al-Zamakhsharī's standing note most quoted) unfold the verdict's logic: what they sought was "a way between" belief and disbelief — and the Book's answer is that **there is no between in this matter**: the articles of faith are an indivisible constitution; to strike one article is to annul the document — the principle the creed-verses had already installed (2:285 — "we make no distinction between any of His messengers") and that this verdict now enforces with a definitional hammer: *partial* assent is not a lesser īmān but a form of kufr — because the object of faith is not only God and messengers severally but God's own *choice* of His messengers — His wisdom in sending *whom He sends* (6:124's standing counter-assertion of the principle: "God knows best where He places His message"). To veto the Sender's selection is to sit in judgment over the Sender — the standing theological point: they who believed "in some" believed not in God-as-Disposer but in their own inherited roster: their god, on audit, their preference; and preference is not faith.

**The Reference-Range: People of the Book — and Every Partial Subscriber After Them**

The standing comment-canon localizes the announcement's first address — most of the early readings: the Jews and Christians of the Prophet's horizon, who received their own scriptures sincerely and drew their line of refusal at Muḥammad (2:91's standing dossier of the refusal-logic: "we believe in what was sent down to us" — tribal acknowledgment masquerading as fidelity); some early reports surname the verse's sabab to delegations who came professing Mūsā and ʿĪsā while declining the Paraclete-claimed Prophet (its standing discussions in al-Ṭabarī's chains). Yet the wording's breadth — *inna alladhīna...* — the demonstrative-universal "those who" — keeps the verdict permanently open for occupation: al-Rāzī's standing hermeneutic maxim ("the lesson is in the generality of the expression, not the specificity of the cause") assigning every generation's selective subscriber to the same registry: the believer-in-God-only, the prophet-cafeteria of the comparative-religionist, the internal Muslim position that accepts this verse and "has reservations" about that one (2:85's standing internal warning within the Ummah itself: "do you believe in part of the Book and disbelieve in the wage of such among you shall be but disgrace in the life of this world — and on the Day of Resurrection they shall be driven to the severest punishment").

**Wa Aʿtadnā li-l-Kāfirīna ʿAdhāban Muhīnan: The Prepared Humiliation**

The sentence closes with the standing preparation-formula of the Book's penal register: *wa aʿtadnā li-l-kāfirīna ʿadhāban muhīnan* — "and We have prepared for the disbelievers a humiliating punishment": *aʿtadnā* — from ʿ-t-d — to hold ready, to prepare in advance (its standing register: 4:18 — "We have prepared for them a painful punishment"; 4:37; 4:93; 4:102; 4:161 below; 17:10; 33:8; 48:6 — the Book's own penal-inventory: a punishment pre-fabricated, waiting, as the guests of v. 122's counter-file had their Gardens pre-prepared — 4:57 — *uʿiddat*) — and the qualifier chosen for this dossier's penalty: *muhīn* — from h-w-n — humbling, disgracing: the humiliation-punishment (its standing file: 4:14 — "a humiliating punishment" for transgressing the inheritance-limits; 4:37; 4:102; 22:57; 58:5 — "the disbelievers shall have a humiliating punishment"). The commentators mark the fitna of its exactness: the selective believers sought *distinction* — status, the dignity of the discerning, the middle-position of the judicious — and their prepared wage is *disgrace*: tafrīq answered by tahwīn — they who prided themselves on dividing shall be levelled by shame; they who took a middle-road for dignity arrive at the extreme of humiliation (the standing Book-pattern of punishment-as-mirror: 10:27 — "those who earned evils — the recompense of an evil is its like — and abasement shall cover them"; 2:85's disgrace-wage above). And the double-registry of the verse — v. 150 defining the act, v. 151 defining the actor and the wage — completes in two short verses the Book's standing legislation of assent-integrity: faith is by constitution indivisible; the distinction-maker is by deed a disbeliever; and for disbelief there stands, from the first day, a preparation — humiliation prepared by the same God whose preparation of Gardens the sūrah has already sung (4:57, 4:122): two two prepared destinations, and the difference between them nothing more — and nothing less — than the difference between taking all of the prophecy and refusing any part of it.

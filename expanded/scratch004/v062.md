## Sūrah al-Nisāʾ 4:62

> **Then how will it be when calamity befalls them for what their own hands have sent forward, and they come to thee swearing by God, "We meant naught but virtue and harmony"?**

**Expanded Commentary**

**Muṣībatun Bi-mā Qaddamat Aydīhim: Calamity as the Follow-Up Bill**

The verse opens with the Book's cinematic asking again — *fa-kayfa idhā aṣābathum muṣībatun bi-mā qaddamat aydīhim* — "how then shall it be, when a calamity befalls them for what their own hands have forwarded?": *fa-kayfa* — *and how!* — the same how-horror frame of 4:41 (its scene-projection power: the reader made to face the future-scene before it can be prepared); *muṣībah* — calamity — from *ṣ-w-b*, a striking that lands on its mark — and the causative formula *bi-mā qaddamat aydīhim* — *for what their hands sent forward* — rehearsal of Islam's plain account-theology (42:30 — "whatever strikes you of calamity — it is by what your own hands earned; and He pardons much"; together with the paired warnings at 2:195's "spend in the way of God and cast not yourselves by your own hands into destruction" and the broader engine-verse of earthly causality at 13:11 — *God changes not a people's state until they change what is in themselves*): every misfortune that overtakes such claimants is heaven-posted — its postage printed *on the hand* — not randomly assigned, not unjustly mailed. The commentators' reports of its first fabric: the calamity the two disputants of v. 60 invited by their ṭāghūt-tribunal (al-Qurṛṭubī and Ibn Kathīr recounting that in the hypocrite's case the sent-forward deeds were the double-act itself — false piety by day, private mockeries of the Prophet's judgment-benches by night — with the *muṣībah* then arriving either as exposure at Madinah (the turning-back of v. 61 made public) or — in the commentators' wider frame — as the reverses through which God kept testing their masks (al-Wāḥidī's broader chain reading the affliction as any trial that uncovers the ṭāghūt-petitioner's true balance).

**Thumma Jāʾūka Yuḥlifūna bi-Allāh: Oaths in Allah's Own Name Built Upon Rubble**

Then the theater-response that heaven strips with one clause: *thumma jāʾūka yuḥlifūna bi-Allāh in aradnā illā iḥsānan wa tawfīqā* — "then they come to thee, swearing by God: We intended naught but virtue and harmony": the oath-in-Allah's-name — *yuḥlifūna bi-Allāh* — the ultimate legal tender of the oaths-market, spent upon the counterfeit merchandise of *niYYah-claims*: "we meant only iḥsān and tawfīq" — iḥsān, redemptive good; tawfīq, concord — heaven quoting their own self-insurance vocabulary (the word-doublet iḥsān/tawfīq chosen deliberately as the two big piety-flags of sūrah of the sūrah's own vocabulary — iḥsān back at vv. 36's conduct-compass; tawfīq at v. 35's arbitration-success — counterfeiters always borrowing the true mint's paper) — and the commentators' asbāb-rolls (Ibn ʿAbbās's chain through Mujāhid and al-Ḥasan in al-Wāḥidī and al-Ṭabarī) record its office-context with telling fineness: the hypocrite litigant and his fellows — caught between the Qur'an's condemning verse and the Muslims' knowing looks — came *early-morning to the Prophet* rehearsing their salve-oaths: "We sought no quarrel with the Believers; we only went to the soothsayer for iḥsān and conciliation" — the form thereafter etched into the literature of fake repentance: *good-feelings speech covering bad conduct* (the tradition's categories classifying such excusal as *muḍārabah* — diplomatic trim — among the nifāq-marks the Prophet's own short-creed listed (Bukhārī 33, Muslim 59 — *"the signs of the hypocrite are three: when he speaks he lies; when he promises he breaks; when he receives a trust he betrays."*)

**The Soul-Audit's Bottom Line Within the Calamity**

The pastoral and theological latitude of the clause runs several streets at once for every generation: (1) *oaths certify nothing beyond oaths* — God's name can be rented by unbelievers; the practical jurisprudence of Islam thereafter never admitting oath-denial alone as discharge of evidence — the fiqh the courtroom traditions weighting conduct, prior record, and circumstance — oath being the gate-pillar but never the whole bridge (cf. the Prophet's teaching that the sworn perjured claimant may discharge his guilt in the court while the truthful unsworn party stands convicted by it — the standing warning of al-Bukhārī's Kitāb al-Aymān, nos. 2510, 2680); (2) *intentions are audited by heaven independently of invocations* — they may lie even in the syllable iḥsān; the doctrine of niyyah remains the believer's own interior court, as the founding hadith stands forever (Bukhārī 1 — *actions are by intentions*) — so a false iḥsān-claim is not good-intentionedness but *added perjury*; and (3) *disaster is God's diagnostic-exposure instrument*: the sages summarized: tribulations scatter masks before hearts — al-Rāzī's phrasing of this verse among his most arresting — calamity unveiling the claim's base-metals, and *God in His mercy sometimes withholding calamity* (deferred to the Hereafter) and sometimes sending it (exposure now means repentance still possible): the believer is trained therefore to meet his own misfortunes in the inverse manner of the perjurer — first with confession of what his hands forwarded, then with repentance, never with the oath-theater of clean-hands denial; for the calamity of the false swearer by God's name is the very largest class of forward-sending (cf. al-Bukhārī 6546 — the *ghamūs*, immersing oaths that sink their holders into the Fire) — and the Prophet here is left by heaven awaiting the next verse's pastoral triage: God knows their hearts — so *you* stand clear, warn without rancor, and speak the depth-word that might yet save them.

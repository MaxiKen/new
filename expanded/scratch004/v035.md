## Sūrah al-Nisāʾ 4:35

> **And if you fear a breach between the two, then appoint an arbiter from his people and an arbiter from her people. If they desire reconciliation, God will bring about agreement between them. Truly God is Knowing, Aware.**

**Expanded Commentary**

**Shiqāq: The Breach the House Cannot Hold Alone**

The reconciliation-architecture the sūrah built around marriage now adds its final and most somber chamber — for v. 34 treated the wife's nushūz and vv. 128–130 will treat the husband's — and here the case is *between the two*: *wa in khiftum shiqāqa baynihimā fa-bʿathū ḥakaman min ahlihi wa ḥakaman min ahlihā* — "and if you fear a breach between the two, then send an arbiter from his people and an arbiter from her people." *Shiqāq* — the split, the tear — from the root of the from *shiq*: splitting, shearing of a thing once one — names marriage-crisis at the stage where within-household resources are spent, where neither nushūz-rung nor ʿiyāshirunna-counsel can reach across the room anymore: the tear *between* them being itself named, so that the community knows the case now involves *two souls*, two rights, two versions, and can no longer be treated from one side of the bed. And the imperative *fa-bʿathū* — then *send*: the community appoints — the verb a mission-verb — arbitration as the ummah's *public duty*, not merely a private expedient: the marriage of two believers is *social property*, its repair the assembly's work — and Islamic fiqh built from this tiny verse its family-court system, the qāḍī appointing the two arbiters where families cannot field their own (al-Mālikī and Shāfiʿī placements into the standing marriage-court laws of the mujtahid schools).

**Ḥakaman min Ahlihi wa Ḥakaman min Ahlihā: Two Arbiters from the Two Houses**

The instrument is exquisitely domestic: *one arbiter from his people, and one from hers* — not one impartial stranger, but two *partial* friends, one picked from each clan: and the commentators have always loved the wisdom: (1) knowledge *of* the married pair's natures lives inside each house — where a judge sees papers, the cousin sees the life; (2) each party's advocacy is *secured within the room*, so no one feels massacred by a stranger-ministry; (3) each clan is thereby *invested in the salvage*, since its own honor-bearer sits at the table — and the sentence flows most mercifully toward settlement, since both houses lose face if it fails. al-Qurṭubī's al-Qurṭubī's jurisprudence works the full fiqh from it: the arbiter must be *just, sane, knowing*, and of kin when kin are fit; the two function by *agreement* — and (his registering of the majority's law) may not separate without authorization, while a strong school — with ʿAlī ibn Abī Ṭālib's own early ruling behind it — grants the pair empowering if delegated in advance: the empowered ḥakamayn (two arbiters) may *dissolve* where salvation is impossible — setting the instrument as both *marriage-mechanic and judge of termination* in commission from the pair themselves.

**In Yurīdā Iṣlāḥan: The Condition of the Two Wills**

Then God's remarkable promise, attached to the arbiters' own intention: *in yurīdā iṣlāḥan yuwaffiqi Allāhu baynahumā* — "if the two desire reconciliation, God will bring about agreement between them": the conditional points at the arbiters' wishing alone in one reading (al-Ṭabarī's register), at the spouses' too in its balanced extension (the mainstream take) — and either way heaven's law of the of mediation is disclosed: *God's own help (tawfīq) rides with the honest intention of making peace*. The clause thereby constitutes one of the Book's perpetual graces for every ministry of people in crisis — two persons sit as healers of a marriage and God's aid descends upon the session *according to intention*: God joins the mending; God un-joins the farce — the commentators' treasury of cases gathering the evidence: spouses brought to arbitration expecting tragedy leaving reconciled where the arbiters believed the two in wills as much as their own. The *in*-conditional is a whole doctrine in itself: *God does not endow success to someone who to one who undertakes what his heart does not want.*

ʿAlīman Khabīrā: Knowing and Aware, at the Arbitration-Room's Doors**

The seal comes with the attribute-pair the sūrah has already used at v. 26 — at this seal — *inna Allāha kāna ʿalīman khabīran* — Knowing, Deeply-Aware — and the marriage-crisis section cannot in fact imagine a more fitting gatekeeper's double-title: *ʿalīm*, knowing the inside-of-natures — which party is truth-cloaked, which grievance just; *khabīr*, aware of what chambers hide — the skillful accuser, the tearful manipulator, the *private* nushūz that never reaches policemen — and the attribute-pair thereby posts heaven over the arbitration-room itself: the human arbiters may be tricked, but the God of the crisis-verses is not — every speech-cluster of a warring pair is filed in the open book of His khabr. The readers, man and woman, then read the whole v. 34–35 institution in the direction the seal orders: the qawwāmūn's discipline, the arbiter's office — all are watched all are watched; and the last hope for the house at war is also God's own attendance upon the honest conciliating intent, so that any family perishing around us may yet be rescued by the un-hidden labor of two sincere arbiters — beneath the eye of the Knowing, the Aware, and behind it the promise: *if they desire reconciliation — God will bring agreement.*

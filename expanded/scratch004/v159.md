## Sūrah al-Nisāʾ 4:159

> **And there is not one of the People of the Book but shall surely believe in him before his death; and on the Day of Resurrection he shall be a witness against them.**

**Expanded Commentary**

**Wa In min Ahli al-Kitābi illā La-Yuʾminanna Bihi: The Pronoun That Holds Two Deaths**

The Jesus-file's sequel opens with its standing universal — and immediately divides the comment-canon over one possessive pronoun: *wa in min ahli al-kitābi illā la-yuʾminanna bihi qabla mawtih* — "and there is not one of the People of the Book but shall surely believe in him before his death": the oath-weighted formula (*in... illā la-* — "there is none... but that he shall surely...") leaving no exception in its class — every single member of the People of the Book — and the possessive *-hi* of *qabla mawtih* — "before *his* death" — the standing fork: (1) before *Jesus'* death — the reading al-Ṭabarī, Ibn Kathīr, and al-Zamakhsharī consolidate as primary in the register of reports from Ibn ʿAbbās and others: since v. 158 has just established that Jesus was raised alive and has *not yet died*, the verse schedules the universal belief for the era of his return at the end of time — the descent, the Dajjāl-battle, and the file of the standing hadith-register (Muslim's standing register 155 and the Sunan-collections: "by Him in whose hand is my soul — the son of Mary is about to descend among you as a just judge — he shall break the cross, kill the swine, abolish the jizyah, and wealth shall overflow until none accepts it") — at which hour the People of the Book, Jews and Christians alike, "shall surely believe in him" — believing him to be what the Book declares: God's servant and messenger (the standing predicate ambiguity of *yuʾminanna bihi* glossed by the commentators: belief *in* him — in the truth of his prophethood and the truth of his declared status — not adoption of every historical community's doctrine *about* him); the standing quote of Ibn ʿAbbās in the tafsīr-tradition: "before the death of Jesus son of Mary" — the report preserved in the canonical tafsīr-chains beside its own completion ("and on that day, the People of the Book will all believe" — its standing register).

**Qabla Mawtih (II): The Alternative — Belief at the Edge of One's Own Death**

(2) before *each one's own* death — the second standing reading (recorded through the same chains among early authorities including some reports from Ibn ʿAbbās's circle): every individual of the People of the Book, at the approach of his own death-moment — when the veil lifts and the truth of Jesus' status becomes visible in the dying hour’s disclosure — "shall surely believe in him": a belief arriving at the very edge, where — the commentators immediately attach the ancient rule (al-Ṭabarī's and Ibn Kathīr's standing filing: 10:90-91's standing template) — it profits nothing: Pharaoh drowning cried "I believe that there is no god but He in whom the Children of Israel believe — and I am of the submitters" — and the answer was and thou wast aforetime in rebellion" — deathbed-faith classified as sight-of-the-unseen, not faith: *lā yanfaʿu nafsan īmānuhā* at the hour repentance-signs close (4:18; 6:158 — "the day some of thy Lord's signs arrive — a soul's belief shall not avail it, that believed not before"). The commentators generally treat both readings as preserveable truths — the first anchoring the verse in the eschatology of the return (and receiving, in the register of narrations, the stronger chain-support), the second anchoring it in the theology of the deathbed — and many let both stand together: the Book's standing capacity for doubled reference in a single pronoun being, in their reading, not a defect of the verse but its design: the pronoun holding both deaths because both deaths hold the same confession — Jesus will be believed in, universally, either at his return or at every individual's last threshold — each a *qabla mawtih* in its own frame.

**Wa Yawma al-Qiyāmati Yakūnu ʿAlayhim Shahīdan: The Witness-Office of Jesus**

The verse closes with the standing office of the messengers at the Judgment: *wa yawma al-qiyāmati yakūnu ʿalayhim shahīdan* — "and on the Day of Resurrection he shall be a witness *against* them": shahīd — witness — upon (*ʿalā*) them — the standing Book-doctrine that the messengers themselves testify concerning their peoples' reception: 4:41's own standing assembly within this very sūrah ("how shall it be, when We bring from every community a witness, and bring thee as witness against these?"); 16:84; 16:89 — "We shall raise up in every community a witness against them from among themselves — and We bring thee as witness against these"; 39:69 — the standing tribunal-scene; and Jesus' own deposition-text preserved in the Book itself — 5:116-117's standing testimony-record: "O Jesus son of Mary — didst thou say to men: take me and my mother as two gods beside he says: glory be to Thee — it was not for me to say what I had no right to... I said to them naught but what Thou commandest me: worship God, my Lord and your Lord — and I was a witness over them while I abode among them — but when Thou tookest me, Thou wast the Watcher over them — and Thou art Witness of all things" — the witness-content thereby specified by the Book in advance of the Day: twofold — *for* the truth (he delivered the message of servanthood-worship) and *against* both deviations that the sūrah's own dossier records: the claim of his divinity made by those who exaggerated (the 4:171-172 file just below: "the Messiah son of Mary was but a messenger... believe in God and His messengers and say not: Three"; 5:72-74; 9:30-31), and the denial of his prophethood by those who boasted his execution (v. 157's own indictment). The standing comment-canon thus reads v. 159 as the eschatological summation of the whole Jesus-dossier of the sūrah: raised (158), believed-in ultimately and universally (159a), and installed as standing witness at the Trial (159b) — the person over whom the two communities divided becoming, in the Book's juridical eschatology, the very evidence-bearer of their division: the cross-boast and the sonship-claim alike subpoenaed to answer to the man himself — alive at God's right, returned to the earth, and seated finally in the witness-box of the Day of Resurrection.

## Sūrah al-Nisāʾ 4:59

> **O you who believe! Obey God and obey the Messenger and those in authority among you. And if you differ among yourselves concerning any matter, refer it to God and the Messenger, if you believe in God and the Last Day. That is better, and fairer in outcome.**

**Expanded Commentary**

**Aṭīʿū Allāha wa Aṭīʿū al-Rasūla wa Ulī al-Amri Minkum: The Threefold Obedience Joined**

The authority-block's second verse mounts its tripod of command: *aṭīʿū Allāha wa aṭīʿū al-rasūla wa ulī al-amri minkum* — "obey God, and obey the Messenger, and those in authority among you": the grammar itself is the first exegetical lesson: for among the three imperatives the first verb is *repeated* before "the Messenger" but *not* repeated before "those in authority" (the third party being carried by the same verb-breath as the Messenger's — obedience below still linked, never free-standing) — al-Rāzī's celebrated point-from-syntax plus the tradition's ḥadīth ladder beside it: the saying of Ibn ʿUmar's chain in al-Bukhārī 2955 & 2957 and Muslim 1835 lodging the office-structure: *"whoever obeys the commander appointed by me has obeyed me, and whoever obeys me has obeyed God"* — the ascending stair of authority in Islam being always *derivation*, the sultan never sovereign by nature (the exact concept the Book engraved nine verses later at 4:80 — *"whoever obeys the Messenger has obeyed God"*) — and the identity-file of *ulī al-amr* — "those of the command among you" — the classical discussions dividing their registers into several complementary seats: (1) *military commanders* appointed by the Prophet (the celebrated occasion in Ṭabarī, al-Wāḥidī, Ibn Kathīr: ʿAmmār ibn Yāsir granting amnesty over the head of commander Khālid ibn al-Walīd, dispute taken to the Prophet, the Prophet *upholding the amnesty but disciplining the bypass* — obedience to the chain; mercy of a soldier honored); (2) *the scholars* — al-Qurṭubī's school reading: those learned in Qur'an and Sunnah who transmit command-through-knowledge (the standing principle: scholars obeyed *as commissioned interpreters*, not as sources); (3) *the rulers* properly so-called — with ʿAlī ibn Abī Ṭālib's decisive proviso, born within sight of v. 58: *only those who deliver the trusts and judge justly* — obedience being tethered to the discharge of the trust it obeys (the juristous condition running thence through the madhhabs' ethic of revolt (al-Ghazālī's third later manuals: no rebellion for worldly grievance alone, *yet* the Qur'anic ceiling never lifted: "no obedience to the created in disobedience to the Creator" — Aḥmad's register 1052, and Bukhārī 7257's "no obedience in a sin"). The tripod thus stands *calibrated*: God supreme; Messenger deputy; ulu'l-amr bounded by the first two — civil peace purchased without civil absolutism.

**Fa-In Tanāzaʿtum fī Shayʾin Fa-Ruddūhu ilā Allāhi wa-l-Rasūl: The Referral-Court of God and Messenger**

The disobedience-cap is immediately wrapped in the adjudication-clause — the verse's supreme legal-contribution: *fa-in tanāzaʿtum fī shayʾin fa-ruddūhu ilā Allāhi wa-l-rasūli in kuntum tuʾminūna bi-Allāhi wa-l-yawmi al-ākhir* — "and if you differ concerning anything, refer it to God and the Messenger — if you believe in God and the Last Day": *tanāzaʿtum* — dispute-withdrawing — contention as mutual withdrawal of assent; *ruddūhu* — *return it* — the same r-d-d root as v. 58's "deliver" — the jurisprudential correspondence deliberate (trusts *delivered back* to owners; disputes *returned back* to God and Messenger: authority-block delivering both account-types to their true owners); with the conditional — *if you believe* — making recourse itself the litmus of faith (foreshadowing v. 65's oath: *"they will not believe until they make thee judge..."*) — the madhhabs thereafter erecting upon the clause the entire architecture of *referral-sources*: Qur'an (God's speech), Sunnah (the Messenger's enacted will) — and upon their two referral organs the long discipline of ijtihād, qiyās, and derivation (al-Shāfiʿī's Bay his Risālah claiming our verse among Islam's uṣūl landmarks: every legal question answered in God-Messenger terms either directly or by inference — this being exactly al-Qurṭubī's cited reconciliation with v. 83's *referral to those in authority among them*: scholars' office exists only *within* God-Messenger bracketing).

**Dhālika Khayrun wa Aḥsanu Taʾwīlā: Better, Lovelier as Interpreted End**

The seal closes with the Book's own measuring of legal morality: *dhālika khayrun wa aḥsanu taʾwīlan* — "that is better and more beautiful in its issue": *khayr* — sheer goodness; *aḥsan taʾwīlan* — fairer in *what it returns to* (taʾwīl from āla/yaʾūlu — to return-come-home — the ending a thing returns to, hence *interpretation* as arriving at the matter's true home) — al-Rāzī's two-level observation being the tradition's permanent gem at this clause: God's referrals are better both *here* (social peace; clean adjudication; innocence secured) and *there* (outcome issue the *return* on That Day a fair home) — with the creed-condition folded into the clause itself (*if you believe in God and the Last Day*: eschatology underwriting jurisprudence — the subjected the subject's heart agreeing to be ruled by what judges beyond appeal) — and the verse thus delivers the ummah its civil constitution in one breath: hierarchical obedience reverse-flowing upon referral; offices legitimate only inside trust-delivery; disputes solvable only by returning to the sources; and the Last Day standing behind every arbitration-book as the audit wherein opinions all *come home* — *aḥsanu taʾwīlan* — loveliest of all destinations.

## Sūrah al-Nisāʾ 4:98

> **But not so the [truly] weak and oppressed among the men, women, and children, who neither have access to any means nor are guided to any way.**

**Expanded Commentary**

**Illā al-Mustaḍʿafīna min al-Rijāli wa al-Nisāʾi wa al-Wildān: The Genuine Weak**

The angels' withering cross-examination of v. 97 receives its mercy-counterweight immediately: *illā al-mustaḍʿafīna mina al-rijāli wa al-nisāʾi wa al-wildān* — "except the weak and oppressed among the men, and the women, and the children": the *illā* — except — opening an acquittal-clause straight against the Hell-verdict of its predecessor (the Book's own juristic architecture: the *exception* — istithnāʾ — mooring the grave decree into its own true scope) — *al-mustaḍʿafīna* — the weakened-ones — from ḍ-ʿ-f — weakness (the exhaustive demographic then spelled out — men AND women AND children — wildān — young ones — the tradition’s honoring syntax: the excuse spreads beyond the warrior-class into the entire non-combatant household — the standing practice which the Prophet himself certified from Madinah onward in al-Ṭabarī’s record: after the sūrah’s verdicts he never missed a prayer without supplicating by name for the mustaḍʿafīn of Makkah — his standing plea being: “O God, deliver the weak among the believers from among the idolaters”) — the commentators' own roll of faces standing behind the clause: Ibn ʿAbbās's own testimony — *"I and my mother were among the mustaḍʿafīn; I was among the children, and my mother among the women"* (al-Ṭabarī's register) — the mother-and-child class of Makkah's trapped — whom no campaign, earthly or theological, could ever indict.

**Lā Yastaṭīʿūna Ḥīlatan wa Lā Yahtadūna Sabīlā: The Two-Fold Incapacity**

The acquittal's exact conditions are then filed with juridical precision: *lā yastaṭīʿūna ḥīlatan wa lā yahtadūna sabīlā* — "who have no means (of migration) — and are not guided to a way": *ḥīlah* — device, means, resource (from ḥ-w-l — turning-about capacity — the standing root-name of every moving-device: the man who lacks *ḥīlah* lacks the very leverage of motion — money, mount, guard, kin-network — the material instruments of departure) — note what al-Rāzī and al-Qurṛṭubī make of its order: where the truly-bound cannot *engineer* the escape, they are free — where they *could* but chose not, they belong to v. 97's class (with al-Rāzī's standing moral cautionary at once: a man must not waive himself into mustaḍʿaf-status on grounds of *hardship* — hardship is not incapacity — every hijrah that ever occurred was hardship-through — the exemption-word of God operates only where the capacity-dispatch is truly zero) — and second, *wa lā yahtadūna sabīlā* — "and are not guided to a way": even if they found means, they cannot find the road — the non-initiated — the slave under locked-quarters, the woman in a tribal roof with no counsel of migration-knowledge (their lack of *hudā al-sabīl* — route-guidance — being honored as the second exemption-arm: heaven counts *knowledge-of-direction* among the preconditions of obligation — no duty falls where the way-out was invisible) — the two rooted arms thereby filing (the fuqahāʾ-paragraphing: obligations-at-strong law are only upon the engine-ended and route-lit) — and the tradition then tells its own chapter of that same trapped column — the standing Sīrah accounts of the Makkan-escapees who attempted the road and were hauled back, pictured across the sīrah-pages for the whole ummah — with the Book's own commentary upon them at 29:10 — *"and among men is he who says: we believe — yet when he is tormented in God, he takes men's persecution as if it were God's own punishment"* — and its merciful resolution at 16:110 — *"then your Lord — for those who emigrated after they were persecuted, then strove and were patient — surely your Lord thereafter is Forgiving, Merciful"* — showing how the canonical verses braid mercy upon mercy around every trapped man who *tried* even once — while at the same time the sūrah locks up the catalogue of excuses at its two terms alone: no means, no way.

**The Demography of Oppression**

And there is one further grandeur in this short verse deserving its own slow reading: the Book's count of the *whole family* within the excused — men, women, children — in that order — the standing tafsir-note being its paradoxical strength: the ḍarar-exempt of v. 95 were those *physically* blocked; the ḥīlah-exempt here are those *practically* blocked — together tracing Islam's dual honesty toward powerlessness: body-blocked and means-blocked stand both before God as exonerated from the door-threshold obligations (with its simultaneous doctrine-rider: neither class thereby drops below the others in basic Islam-accountability — they are exempt from *hijrah and combat*, never from faith itself — for faith needs no camel and no escort-clan — intention and tongue suffice the trapped) — and Ibn ʿAjībah's inward reading again finds its counterpart — the soul's own trapped moments — seasons in life where the spirit genuinely *cannot break through* — no ḥīlah, no sabīl — where the only work left is the held-fastness itself — for the Lawgiver has excused not only the blocked body but the blocked route — the believer in his worst spiritual captivity being taught by the very exemption-structure: stay true where you stand; work your legs only when God opens your road.

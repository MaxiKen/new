## Sūrah al-Nisāʾ 4:129

> **And you shall never be able to deal equally between wives, even if you should desire it; but do not incline with full inclination, so that you leave her as one suspended. And if you set things right and keep from evil — then God is ever Forgiving, Merciful.**

**Expanded Commentary**

**Wa Lan Tastaṭīʿū an Taʿdilū: The Pronouncement That Closed One Door and Opened Another**

The sūrah now distinguishes two kinds of justice with a verdict that has echoed through every court of fiqh since: *wa lan tastaṭīʿū an taʿdilū bayna al-nisāʾi wa law ḥaraṣtum* — "and you shall never be able to deal equally between wives, even if you should desire it": *lan* — the negative particle of absolute perpetuity — never, not ever — a divine proclamation about human capacity itself; *taʿdilū* — from ʿ-d-l — the balancing of scales — equalizing; and *ḥaraṣtum* — from ḥ-r-ṣ — stretched-to-the-limit desire — you may strain every nerve of goodwill, and still the verdict stands: *lan tastaṭīʿū* — you shall not be able. The commentators (al-Ṭabarī with his standing chains; Ibn Kathīr; al-Qurṭubī) gather the interpretation into a two-story ruling: (1) justice in the *outward* — housing, maintenance, nights, expenditure — is commanded and enforceable, made the express condition of plural marriage earlier in the sūrah (v. 3: "then marry whom you please... but if you fear you cannot deal justly — then only one"); and (2) justice in the *inward* — equal love, equal attraction, equal gladness of the eyes — is declared impossible — God legislating against the pretense that hearts can be court-ordered. The Sabāb-material illustrates from the highest home: ʿĀʾishah's standing reports of the Prophet's scrupulous outward equality — and his candid inward confession, the standing duʿāʾ preserved in the Sunan (al-Tirmidhī 1140's standing register — and its parallels in Abū Dāwūd and Ibn Mājah): "O God — this is my division in that which I control — so do not take me to task about that which You control and I do not" — outward apportionment owned as human duty, inward inclination handed back to its true Owner.

**Fa-Lā Tamīlū Kulla al-Mayl: The Prohibited Leaning**

If perfect balance is impossible, the verse does not therefore license surrender to imbalance; it draws instead the line of the *permissible tilt* versus the *prohibited leaning*: *fa-lā tamīlū kulla al-mayli fa-tadharūhā ka-al-muʿallaqah* — "but do not incline with full inclination, so that you leave her as one suspended": *mayl* — from m-y-l — the tilt, the lean (its standing Book-file: 27:10 — Moses' staff — "as though it inclined as a jinn" — and 68:9's standing warning — "they wish that you should incline — so that they might incline" — the moral lean of compromise); *kulla al-mayl* — the *whole* leaning — not every degree of preference (that the preceding clause has excused) but the total capitulation of conduct to preference, until the less-loved wife hangs *ka-al-muʿallaqah* — "as one suspended": the passive participle of ʿ-l-q — hanging — the woman neither married (enjoying the rights and companionship of marriage) nor divorced (free to seek another marriage) — hung on a hook between statuses. Al-Qurṭubī calls it among the most compressed social images in the Book: the verse forbids the husband to convert his inevitable inward inequality into a visible misery — to starve the less-favored wife of nights, conversation, and presence until she dangles. The standing prophetic arithmetic of that dangling: the celebrated warning (its standing source in the two Ṣaḥīḥs — Bukhārī's standing register 5214; Muslim 2921): "whoever has two wives and leans to one of them shall come on the Day of Resurrection with one side leaning — drooping" — the body's very posture displaying in the next world the tilt it concealed in this.

**The Muʿallaqah File: Suspension From This Verse to the Code of Law**

The juristic tradition built upon *ka-al-muʿallaqah* an entire branch of protective law: the wife whose husband absents himself endlessly, deserts without maintenance, or simply freezes her out — sits precisely in the suspended state this verse names — and the schools, from Mālik's standing opinion to the ḥanafī mechanism (the dissolution-route through the judge's authority), worked to ensure no woman must dangle: the Māliki four-year rule, the ijtiḥād of the late jurists extending dissolution-rights to the judge — all genealogies tracing back to this one simile. And the inward application — noted by the spiritually minded commentators — extends past households: any covenant-holder who keeps another soul *suspended* between acceptance and dismissal — the ignored employee, the half-welcomed convert, the relative confined to the cold periphery — practices small-scale the very suspension the verse forbids large-scale: the Book's standing ethic that human bonds must be honored or cleanly released (2:229's standing formula — "retention in kindness or release with goodness"; 65:2's echo) — never strung along in the limbo that serves only the suspender's convenience.

**Wa In Tuṣliḥū wa Tattaqū: Repair, Piety, and the Names That Answer Both**

The verse ends where every family-verse of the sūrah ends — in the doubled doorway of amendment and piety: *wa in tuṣliḥū wa tattaqū fa-inna Allāha kāna ghafūran raḥīman* — "and if you set things right and keep from evil — then God is ever Forgiving, Merciful": *iṣlāḥ* — the setting-right of what has warped (its standing Book-pair with taqwā appearing at 2:160; 2:182; 4:128 just above — "if you do good and keep from evil") — and the two Names sealing the syllable of hope: *ghafūr* — the Much-Forgiving (for the leaning already done, the nights already starved, the years already tilted — repentance opens before the guilty husband as before any other sinner) — and *raḥīm* — the Merciful (for the repair still fragile — the sūrah's standing Name-pair that has sounded through its family-file from v. 23 onward: "God is ever Forgiving, Merciful" — 4:23; 4:24; 4:96 — and the several standing stations of the sūrah itself). ʿĀʾishah's standing summary crowns the passage — her remark that in outward division nobody observed the Prophet exceeding fairness (Muslim's standing collections are full of her documentation of his rotation and provisioning) — yet even the best of homes required *kulla al-mayl* to remain a forbidden zone: the Book teaching thereby that the law does not demand the impossible (equal hearts — which it has just declared forever beyond reach) but absolutely demands the possible (equal nights, equal provision, the prohibition of the suspended wife) — the exact midpoint between utopia and license where all the sūrah's family-legislation stands.

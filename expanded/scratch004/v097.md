## Sūrah al-Nisāʾ 4:97

> **When the angels take the souls of those who were wronging themselves, [the angels] say, "In what state were you?" They say, "We were weak and oppressed in the land." [The angels] say, "Was not God's earth vast enough that you might have migrated therein?" These shall have their refuge in Hell — what an evil journey's end!**

**Expanded Commentary**

**Inna Alladhīna Tawaffāhumu al-Malāʾikatu Ẓālimī Anfusihim: The Soul-Taking of the Self-Wrongers**

The sūrah's war-canon now opens its sternest single-scene chapter — the hijrah-judgment: *inna alladhīna tawaffāhumu al-malāʾikatu ẓālimī anfusihim* — "those whom the angels take in death, while they were wronging themselves": *tawaffā* — the Book's standard word for soul-collection (from w-f-y — to receive-in-full, take-up-a-debt — the angels as heaven's appointed receiv heaven's appointed receivers — cf. 6:61 — "He is the Irresistible over His servants, and sends guardians over you, until death comes to one of you, and Our messengers take him, and they neglect not"; 32:11 — "the Angel of Death who has been entrusted with you will take you") — the Book's commentators fixing at once the identity of the taken: professing Muslims who *stayed in Makkah under the idolaters' dominion*, refusing the migration to Madinah (the historical band of the first ummah — caught between faith and family — wealth-anchored by estates, or by the dread of road-and-enmity) — but the Book naming their state with the merciless term: *ẓālimī anfusihim* — *wrongers of their own souls* (the standing Quranic reframing of slavery-and-fear upon soul-docket upon soul-ledger: to remain where Islam cannot be lived, from motives of comfort, estate, or lineage, is not neutral staying-put — it is *ẓulm* against one's self — the harm-word the Book habitually reserves for objective wrong — 4:110's plenary — "whosoever does evil or wrongs himself, then seeks God's forgiveness, shall find God Forgiving, Merciful"; 65:1's "whosoever transgresses the limits of God, has wronged his own soul").

**Qālū Fīma Kuntum Qālū Kunnā Mustaḍʿafīna fī al-Arḍ: The Deathbed Cross-Examination**

The soul-taking arrives as interrogation — one of the most chilling forensic scenes in the Revelation: *qālū fīma kuntum* — "they say: in what state were you?" — the angels asking in the moment of extraction (with its structural twin-texts in the Book: 6:130's — "O company of jinn and men — did there not come to you messengers from among yourselves recounting to you My signs and warning you of this Day they say: we bear witness against ourselves"; 39:71's — "till, when they come to it, its gates are opened, and its keepers say to them..." — and 16:28-32's paired angel-scenes — the array of the death-interrogations; the tradition hadithologizing the Sunnah itself deepening the scene in the famous grave-cross in the famous grave-questions — "who is your Lord what is your religion who is your Prophet?" — Tirmidhī 1071's hadith in the funeral-registers) — and the captured answering with the plea that has excused every settler in history: *qālū kunnā mustaḍʿafīna fī al-arḍ* — "they say: we were weak in the land": the mustaḍʿaf-defence — the *we were powerless* — the commentators noting its authentic ring parfa its ring of ancient usage (the very word of v. 75's oppressed plea — *rabbana akhrijnā* — now turned inside-out as a self-acquit as self-acquittal) — and the counter-answer heaven hands the angels: *qālū alam takun arḍu Allāhi wāsiʿatan fa-tuhājirū fīhā* — "they say: was God's earth not vast — that you might have migrated in it?": the rebuttal-sentence — earth as vast-escape-route: *wāsiʿah* — spacious (the Book's standing birthing standing adjective of the of earth's generous capacity — 29:56 — "O My servants who believe — My earth is vast; so worship Me") — the angels' logic exact, merciless: the condition of weakness was *portable* — a man's feet were also part of his faith — the crushed soul was never commanded to *stay crushed*; the Divine instruction was *route-around was even then: walk* (cf. the standing two-fold hadith on the hijrah-aged on the migration-class: "hijrah shall not cease until repentance ceases; and repentance shall not cease until the sun rises from the west" — Abū Dāwūd 2479, Aḥmad's own canonical roll — expanding with it the migration-concept from geography to spiritual emigration forever — and its pairing at the Prophet's Makkah-conquest question — "what will you do with a believer who remains in the land of shirk?" — the tradition now erecting its standing doctrine: the professing soul that does not *move when it can* is answerable for its own captivity).

**Ulāʾika Maʾwāhum Jahannamu wa Sāʾat Maṣīrā: The Journey's-End of the Non-Movers**

Then the verdict — one of the sūrah's harshest, delivered with its terrible finality: *ulāʾika maʾwāhum jahannamu wa sāʾat maṣīrā* — "these — their refuge is Hell, and an evil journey's end": *maʾwā* — refuge — from a-w-y — shelter (the word the Book uses for permanent lodging — cf. 3:151's "their refuge is the Fire; evil is the abode of the wrongdoers"; 3:162's ladder 47:12-13's comparative refuges) — and *maṣīr* — destination — from ṣ-y-r — to become-arrive (the Book's own canar its standing journey—end word — and the darker-toned phrase *wa sāʾat maṣīrā* — "widow evil it is as journey's end" — exceptionally one of the Revelation's judgment-nomenclature formulas against categories of men — 2:126 its pivot against Abraham's prayer; 3:162 the destination—it destination-word of the wayward — all rolled now onto the *non-migrant*) — the commentators of the classical canon bearing up beneath the judgment's severity — al-Rāzī's ruling where the tradition stands: hijrah was, for the Makkan Muslims who *could*, a personal obligation of the covenant — fard ʿayn, not communal prayer of covenant-migration obligation — tied to the very construction: Islam was still propertyless except where it was embodied — Madinah as Islam's first live territory — so remaining behind meant strengthening shirk by one's presence-and-labor — hence the severity — (with its standing nuance: the verse judged those *who could* migrate — not the truly impotent, whom v. 98 immediately absolves — the entire infra-structure laid out as the four-class mapping by the commentators: (1) able who migrated — the veneration-class; (2) able who stayed and flunked stayed *despite* — v. 97's verdict-class; (3) unable — v. 98's excused; (4) the utterly resourceless — v. 98b's hoped-for-forgiven) — and behind all of it the sūrah's deepest life-law unfolding: Islam's claim upon the soul includes a claim upon the soul's *geography* — a believer must be able to say where his feet stand — the wide-earth argument having never been retired since — in every century, in every land where truth is unsafe, the Divine question stands: *if your soul is unsafe there, what is immovable about your boots?* — and the only acquittal is proven impossibility — not preference, not property, not the familiar chains of a well-furnished captivity.

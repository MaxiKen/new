## Sūrah al-Nisāʾ 4:53

> **Or have they a share in sovereignty? If they did, they would not give men so much as the speck on a date stone.**

**Expanded Commentary**

**Am Lahum Naṣībun mina al-Mulk: The Rhetoric of the Partnerless Kingdom**

The pericope continues its demolition of the two cursed scholars' platform by throwing open the ledger of *sovereignty* itself: *am lahum naṣībun mina al-mulk* — "or have they a share in sovereignty?": *am*, the disjunctive *or* — detaching its question at first glance from the sentences before it — a puzzle the grammarians treasured (al-Qurṭubī's own note: the *am* must hang upon a suppressed first member, the classical restoration running fine running: *are they worthier of prophethood than the one commissioned, or do they own a share in kingdom with God?* — the answer to both a thundering no): the interrogative driving at the delusion the pericope has been mapping — men without any men without any stock in God's governance yet adjudicating at its gate (v. 51's "these are more guided" verdicts); and *naṣīb*, share-word familiar now from this sūrah's inheritance-chapters (vv. 7, 11–12, 32) — here turned cosmic: the *portion* under question is not an allotment of palm-trees but *mulk*, sovereignty — dominion, kingship, command — God's own title throughout the Book (2:102 "the two did not have the mulk of this 2:107's "to God belongs the mulk of the heavens and the earth"; 3:26 — "Thou givest mulk to whom Thou wilt and strippest it from whom Thou wilt" — the immediately familiar Mulk-verse of the night-devotees; 5:40; 25:2), so the question reads: *do these self-appointed judges hold one share-line in the Power-ledger of the heavens?* — and al-Ṭabarī's roll, al-Zamakhsharī's rhetoric-note, and Ibn Kathīr's capsule concur at the level the pericope requires: *none whatsoever* — the portion-bearers having neither command of heaven's appointments nor even the dates the key to any future's obedience — and therefore no competence whatever to seat themselves at the bench of "more guided."

**Fa-Idhan Lā Yuʾtūna al-Nāsa Naqīrā: The Counterfactual Exposing the Miser-Heart**

Then the verse's magnificent conditional — a single imaginary oscill clause demolishing by fantasy: *fa-idhan lā yuʾtūna al-nāsa naqīrā* — "then they would not give the people even a *naqīr*": *naqīr*, from *n-q-r*, the pecked notch — the speck-hollow on the back of the date-stone (the Qur'an's threefold infinitesimal-unit already sketched at v. 49's *fatīl* — thread — and reinforced at v. 124's naqīr again: least units of all scales deployed wherever the Book discloses exact sever exactitude in judgment or meanness in men) — the rhetorical structure being a *counterfactual of exposure*: *if* such men *had* a kingdom-share, their intrinsic stinginess would empty would starve mankind to less than a speck: the hypothetical used not to imagine generosity but to *forensic to unmask*: for al-Rāzī's standing use of the man's of the device: the way a man handles *small* powers prophesies his handling of great ones — and the portion-bearers had already displayed their scale at Madinah: hoarding the knowledge of the Prophet's coming from their own flocks (concealment of v. 37's "bounty"), selling verdicts for clan-fees (v. 44's error-purchase), tolerating idol-honor for alliance (v. 51): men who dole out *truth* in eyedroppers would not in any universe portion out *sovereignty* in streams. The Prophet's own hadith encapsulates the same acid-management the same acid test in a famous treasury-line the commentators love to place beneath it (al-Bukhārī 1416; Muslim 1038, the register of Hārith of Abū Dharr's the directly apt register):

> *"The upper hand is better than the lower hand; and begin upon those for whom you are responsible. The best charity is what is given from sufficiency — and whoever refrains, God makes him content; and whoever acts rich, God makes him rich."*

— the *giving-hand* being at once the test of wealth's hold on its owner and the parable-in-reverse of v. 53: while the world measures sovereignty by *takings*, heaven measures souls by *dispensings*.

**Sovereign Integrity and the Reader's Inheritance**

The verse carries at last its deep assurance into the life of the believing heir: since God's mulk is *partnerless*, no deputy's stinginess can ration what He appoints for the believer — the community's fear before the cursed scholars and their hosts is hereby bankrupted by grammar: they own *no shares*; take nothing to heart that such hands deny you; and the sūrah's long probation returns to its own frontiers: the orphan-shares (vv. 2–14) guaranteed by the same Partnerless One notwithstanding the hoarders; the mercy-shares (vv. 16–29) flowing through His treasury not theirs; and this new word amid them: *power too has no stock-company to bribe* — the heavens not a market, intercession not a brokerage, and *al-mulk* remaining where the Book's equal night-litany repeats it: *for God is the Sovereignty entire* — 5:40's own cadence sealing the register the reader now chants to himself before every court and cabal that would tax his hope: *"to God belongs the sovereignty of the heavens and the earth and whatsoever is between them — and unto Him is the homecoming."*

## Sūrah al-Nisāʾ 4:154

> **And We raised the Mount above them for their covenant, and We said to them: "Enter the gate, prostrating," and We said to them: "Transgress not the Sabbath." And We took from them a solemn covenant.**

**Expanded Commentary**

**Wa Rafaʿnā Fawqahumu al-Ṭūr: The Mountain Held Over the Treaty**

The covenant-chronicle continues with one of the Book's most majestic pressure-images: *wa rafaʿnā fawqahumu al-ṭūra bi-mīthāqihim* — "and We raised the Mount above them for their covenant": *al-Ṭūr* — the Mount — the Book's standing name for the Sinai-mountain wherever it appears (its register: 2:63; 2:93; 19:52 — "We called to him from the right side of the Mount and brought him near in communion"; 20:80; 28:29; 28:46; 52:1 — "by the Mount"); and the *raising* understood in the standing majority-reading (al-Rāzī gathering the comment-canon) quite literally: God lifted the mountain from its foundation and held it suspended over the Israelites at the covenant-making — with 2:63 its standing twin: "and when We took your covenant and raised the Mount above you: take what We have given you with strength, and remember what is in it — that perhaps you may fear"; and 2:93 adding its standing detail: they took it under the mind's coercion of the hanging mountain ("and it was raised above you as though it were a canopy — and they supposed it was falling upon them — take what We have given you with strength, and hear"). Al-Qurṭubī registers the alternative restraint — the raising as the image of sheer loftness and overwhelm — but the dominant imagination of the tafsīr-tradition keeps the mountain airborne: covenant extracted from a people beneath geology itself made eyelid. The commentators mark the theological point: the miracle-as-treaty-witness — twelve tribes assembled beneath levitated stone — a covenant witnessed not by ink but by the Creator's own suspension of His own physical order — and the sūrah's present audience (the People of the Book in Madīnah covenanting, or refusing to covenant, with the new revelation) reading the file of their fathers: covenants with God were sealed, for them, under a mountain — let no one treat the mīthāq lightly now that it comes by recitation instead of levitation.

**Wa Qulnā Lahumu Udkhulū al-Bāba Sujjadan: The Gate That Required Bowing**

The verse then proceeds to the standing command charged at the covenant's edges: *wa qulnā lahumu udkhulū al-bāba sujjadan* — "and We said to them: enter the gate, prostrating": the standing file at 2:58 and 7:161: "and when it was said to them: dwell in this town and eat of it wheresoever you will, and say: ḥiṭṭah — and enter the gate prostrating — We shall forgive you your offenses — We shall increase the good-doers": the gate-gloss under the standing readings of the commentators being the gate of the promised town (Jericho or Jerusalem in the standing reports — 5:21-23's standing file of the entry-debate) — and the posture demanded at the entry: *sujjad* — prostrate — the entrance to victory dressed as the entrance to worship: the people of God entering their long-promised possession *forehead-down* — conquest-behavior inverted into gratitude: not as raiders claiming a prize but as worshippers acknowledging a gift. The standing callback of the tradition: the chronicled alteration — 2:59; 7:162 — "but the wrongdoers among them substituted a saying other than that which had been said to them — so We sent upon them a scourge from heaven for their wrongdoing": they changed the dictated word *ḥiṭṭah* (unburdening) into mocking parody-forms, and bent the prostration into its own caricature — the entry that was to be prayer becoming farce: the standing lesson of the commentators: the covenant's outward conditions are tests of inward submission; humiliation-postures refused at the gate become punishments delivered from the sky.

**Wa Lā Taʿdū fī al-Sabt: The Sabbath-Order in the Standing File**

And the third command-station: *wa qulnā lahum lā taʿdū fī al-sabt* — "and We said to them: transgress not the Sabbath": *lā taʿdū* — do not commit ʿudwān — transgression-overstepping (its standing paired usage 4:30's — "whoever does that in aggression and injustice — We shall roast him in a blaze") — the Sabbath of the covenant-community being their standing sanctified rest-day, set apart (its standing Book-file: 2:65 — "you well know those of you who transgressed the Sabbath — We said to them: be apes, despised"; 7:163's full Sabbath-file — the town by the sea, the fish appearing on the Sabbath in abundance, absent on other days — and the trawling folk splitting into transgressors, warners, and the saved; 4:47 above's own Sabbath-curse-station; 16:124 — "the Sabbath was only set down upon those who differed concerning it") — the Sabbath-legislation thus read by the commentators not as a timeless universal but as a *covenant-specific* sign: a test-institution marking that community's treaty with God — which is precisely the frame the present verse gives it: ordered in a chain with covenant-mountain and prostrate-gate, all three standing as *terms of the mīthāq*: divine order, worshipful conquest, sanctified time.

**Wa Akhadhnā Minhum Mīthāqan Ghalīẓan: The Covenant in Its Thickness**

The verse seals with the covenant-word at its heaviest registered form: *wa akhadhnā minhum mīthāqan ghalīẓan* — "and We took from them a solemn covenant": *mīthāq* — the standing treaty-word of the Book (its register of covenants: 2:40 — "fulfill My covenant — I shall fulfill your covenant"; 2:83-84; 3:81 — the covenant of the prophets; 5:12; 5:70; 7:169; 33:7 — "and when We took from the prophets their covenant — and from thee, and Noah, and Abraham, and Moses, and Jesus son of Mary — We took from them a solemn covenant — mīthāqan ghalīẓan" — the identical collocation) — with *ghalīẓ* — from gh-l-ẓ — thick, dense, strong: the covenant described by its own materiality — not parchment-thin but load-bearing: a treaty of thickness, because its terms connect heaven's patience to earth's history. The commentators note the standing enumeration compressed in the verse: mountain, gate, Sabbath, all three bound by the single thick covenant — and the standing irony the sūrah then develops immediately in v. 155: the same community whose covenant was of *ghalīẓ* quality broke it in every registered particular — the very next verse naming the breach ("then for their breaking of their covenant..."). The sūrah of covenants — whose entire architecture, from 4:1's standing exordium ("O mankind — fear your Lord") through its marriage-treaties and orphan-trusts, is a sustained jurisprudence of *kept* bonds — here places the standing counter-example at full scale: a community that received every reinforcement heaven could engineer — levitated mountain, dictated entry-posture, sanctified calendar — and still breached: the demonstration the chapter now carries forward against its present Madīnan addressees and every reader since: covenant-breaking is never a failure of the covenant's reinforcement; it is a failure of response — and the thickness of the treaty measures the ruin of those who break it, no less than its security for those who keep.

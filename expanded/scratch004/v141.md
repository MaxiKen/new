## Sūrah al-Nisāʾ 4:141

> **Those who lie in wait concerning you: if a victory comes to you from God, they say, "Were we not with you?" And if the disbelievers have some share, they say, "Did we not gain mastery over you, and defend you from the believers?" God shall judge between you on the Day of Resurrection, and God shall never grant the disbelievers a way over the believers.**

**Expanded Commentary**

**Alladhīna Yatarabbaṣū Bikum: The Watchers at the Fence**

The dossier's fifth verse paints the hypocrites' characteristic posture in a single participle: *alladhīna yatarabbaṣū bikum* — "those who lie in wait concerning you": *yatarabbaṣū* — from r-b-ṣ — to wait-and-watch, to lurk in observation of an outcome before committing (its standing Book-file: 9:52's standing challenge — "say: do you await for us aught save one of the two best while we await for you that God afflict you"; 9:98 — "among the Bedouin are those who take what they spend as a loss, and await the turns of fortune against you"; 57:14 — "they await nay" — the full register of tarabbuṣ as the creed of the calculator who will not pledge until the ledger settles). The sūrah had already sketched these watchers mid-battle (vv. 72–73 above: "among you is he who would surely tarry — then if a calamity befalls you he says: God has blessed me, in that I was not present with them — but if a bounty comes to you from God, he says — as if there were no affection between you and him — would that I had been with them, then I would have attained a great triumph"); v. 141 now adds the decisive detail al-Ṭabarī's chains record: their watchfulness is not mere cowardice — it is *double bookkeeping with both armies*: the verse will show them corresponding openly with the Muslim side in victory and with the enemy side in defeat — hypocrisy mature into active intelligence-trading.

**Alam Nakun Maʿakum: The Share-Claim of the Absent**

When the banner swings Muslim-ward: *fa-in kāna lakum fatḥun min Allāhi qālū alam nakun maʿakum* — "if a victory comes to you from God, they say: were we not with you?": *fatḥ* — the opening-victory (its standing register: 8:10; 48:1 — "verily We have opened for thee a clear opening"; 110:1) — and the claim-form itself the standing signature of the opportunist: the rhetorical *alam* — "did we not...?" — a question that presumes assent, designed to harvest the spoils of Badr and the booty of every victory while the claimer had kept himself, in Ibn Kathīr's standing phrase, safe behind the walls. The commentators note the delicate duplicity of the word *maʿakum* — "with you" — true in geography (many did march reluctantly, 9:46-47's standing diagnosis of their disruptive presence), false in allegiance: proximity of bodies cited as proof of union of hearts — the exact confusion of association-vocabulary with loyalty-reality that vv. 139–140 have just disassembled.

**Alam Naḥūl ʿAlaykum wa Namnaʿkum min al-Muʾminīn: The Betrayal-Invoice**

When the tide swings the other way, the invoice switches creditor: *wa in kāna li-l-kāfirīna naṣībun qālū alam naḥūl ʿalaykum wa namnaʿkum min al-muʾminīn* — "if the disbelievers have some share, they say: did we not gain mastery over you, and defend you from the believers?": *naṣīb* — the assigned portion (the standing word of lots and shares across the sūrah's inheritance-file, now grimly repurposed: a "portion" of victory) — and the two verbs itemize the treason-bill: *naḥūl* — from ḥ-w-l — to gain control, to maneuver into mastery — "we out-maneuvered you (O disbelievers) for you" — and *namnaʿkum min al-muʾminīn* — "and we kept the believers off you": the hypocrites presenting their *absence* from the Muslim ranks as a service rendered to the enemy — their treacherous non-participation invoiced as intercession, their withheld swords billed to the account of military protection. Al-Ṭabarī notes the historical resonance: at Uḥud, when three hundred turned back with Ibn Ubayy at the field's edge (3:166-167's standing file), the withdrawal functioned materially as aid to Quraysh — and the hypocrites' subsequent whispering with the Meccans dressed it as deliberate strategy in their favor. The word *al-muʾminīn* — "from the believers" — slipping from their mouths as a third-party designation — the hypocrites, claiming Islam before the Muslims, speaking of the believers to the enemy as *them*: the pronoun betraying what the testimony-of-faith conceals.

**Allāhu Yaḥkumu Baynakum: The Judgment Reserved, and the Way Denied**

Then the verdict that closes every chapter of this dossier: *Allāhu yaḥkumu baynakum yawma al-qiyāmati* — "God shall judge between you on the Day of Resurrection": the standing deferral-formula (its register: 2:113 — "God shall judge between them on the Day of Resurrection concerning that wherein they differed"; 3:55; 10:93; 16:124; 22:17; 32:25; 39:3; 39:46) — the Book's standing answer to unprovable inner treachery: since the hypocrite's real allegiance cannot be subpoenaed in this world, the case is assigned to the only court where hearts testify — the deferral itself a mercy (the community spared endless loyalty-inquisitions) and a threat (the deferred verdict inescapable). And then the standing charter-clause that jurists and theologians both quarry: *wa lan yajʿala Allāhu li-l-kāfirīna ʿalā al-muʾminīna sabīlan* — "and God shall never grant the disbelievers a way over the believers": *lan* — the never of perpetuity — with *sabīl* — a way — glossed in the standing readings as either a *proof/warrant* on the Day of Judgment (no argument by which the disbeliever can prevail over the believer before God) or — al-Qurṭubī's and Ibn Kathīr's community-reading — a promise concerning *this world's ultimate outcome*: the community of the believers never finally subjugated: the juristic tradition famously deriving from this clause the ruling that a disbeliever may not hold a Muslim slave — such ownership a "way over" a believer that the law forbids, a convert in a non-Muslim's ownership requiring sale or emancipation — Ibn Kathīr and al-Qurṭubī both registering the derivation as the clause's standing legal life. The verse thereby seals the fence-sitters' file: their vigilance-for-profit yields them neither camp's trust, and the Resurrection's docket will sort by allegiances the tongue never uttered but the feet always showed — *God shall judge between you* — while the ummah, promised that the disbelievers hold no ultimate way over it, is taught to watch its own fence-line: not every claim of *alam nakun maʿakum* — "were we not with you?" — survives the audit of the Watching One who saw who awaited whom.

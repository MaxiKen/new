## Sūrah al-Nisāʾ 4:156

> **And for their disbelief, and their uttering against Mary a tremendous calumny —**

**Expanded Commentary**

**Wa Bi-Kufrihim: Disbelief Named as the Mother-Charge**

The indictment of vv. 155–157 continues its fourth count — transitional and re-foundational: *wa bi-kufrihim* — "and for their disbelief": the bare noun, unqualified by object, functioning as the commentators read it (al-Ṭabarī's chains registering the standing gloss): the root-state beneath all the counted crimes — covenant-breach, sign-refusal, prophet-slaying, the sheathed-heart boast — each an eruption of this single condition. The placement is architectonic: immediately after the sealing ("God has set a seal upon them for their disbelief"), the verse returns to *kufr* as though to say: and for that same disbelief — now consider what it uttered next — leading into the fifth count with the terrible seamlessness of a court record: disbelief does not merely withhold assent; it generates speech — and the speech it generates here is among the worst in the Book's catalog of utterances.

**Wa Qawlihim ʿAlā Maryama Buhtānan ʿAẓīman: The Calumny Against Mary**

The fifth count is quoted, and the Book's own naming of the crime defines its magnitude: *wa qawlihim ʿalā Maryama buhtānan ʿaẓīman* — "and their uttering against Mary a tremendous calumny": *buhtān* — the slander-word already drilled at v. 112 within this sūrah ("whoever earns an offense or sin then casts it upon the innocent has burdened himself with *buhtān* and manifest sin") — the fabricated charge designed to deceive the hearer about its victim — and here qualified by the superlative *ʿaẓīm* — tremendous: a calumny of the first magnitude. The standing comment-canon identifies the charge: the insinuation of indecency against Mary — the virgin mother — when she returned bearing the child Jesus (the standing file at Sūrah Maryam: "then she brought him to her people, carrying him. They said: O Mary — thou hast brought a strange thing — O sister of Aaron — thy father was not an evil man, nor was thy mother unchaste" — 19:27-28 — the insinuation-pointer already in the text; the *slander-form proper* — the explicit accusation of unchastity — recorded in the standing tafsīr-reports as the gist of their rejection: rather than reading the miracle, they read scandal). The commentators mark the calumny's exact theological cost: the miracle of the virgin birth stood deliberately *legible* — the child himself speaking from the cradle in his mother's defense (19:30-33 — "verily I am the servant of God — He has given me the Book and made me a prophet") — yet the calumny-makers preferred the scandal-reading, because the miracle-reading would have obliged them before a prophet: disbelief protecting itself by defaming the evidence's mother. Ibn Kathīr's standing severity locates the count's order: of the possible refusals open to them — doubt, silence, departure — they chose the one that aimed at a woman's honor: kufr not only deaf but armed.

**Maryam al-Batūl in the Book's Own Register: The Lady the Calumny Could Not Reach**

Against the slander the Book has already installed its own standing memorial — and the commentators, commenting v. 156, regularly recite the counter-file as though to answer the calumny perpetually: 3:42 — "the angels said: O Mary — verily God has chosen thee and purified thee and chosen thee above the women of the worlds"; 66:12 — "and Mary daughter of ʿImrān, who guarded her chastity — so We breathed into it of Our Spirit — and she confirmed the words of her Lord and His books — and she was of the obedient"; 3:37 — the Lord's beautiful acceptance of her ("a beautiful acceptance — ḥasan — and reared her a beautiful rearing"); 21:91 — "she who guarded her chastity — We breathed into her of Our Spirit, and made her and her son a sign for the worlds"; 19:16-21 — the annunciation-file complete. Al-Qushayrī's standing classification (noted across the later commentaries) places Mary in the rank of God's waliyyah — His protected friend — and draws the standing two-sided warning the verse's charge embodies: two kinds of people err concerning the saints — those who exaggerate them (in Mary's case, the deifyers — 5:116's standing eschatological arraignment: "O Jesus son of Mary — didst thou say to men: take me and my mother as two gods beside glory be to Thee!") and those who denigrate them — as here: the calumny that would not see the miracle for the scandal. Between the two errors stands the Book's own measurement: mother-of-a-prophet, pure, chosen, sign-carrying — honored forever — while the slanderers' count sits in the indictment of v. 156, payable on the Day the wronged of v. 148's exception-clause receive their full hearing.

**The Standing Lesson of the Buhtān-Counts: Disbelief Needs False Witness**

The verse's compressed charge (six words in Arabic) is framed by the commentators as a standing study in the *speech-economy* of disbelief: truth needs only to be told; error must arm itself — and its weapon of first resort is the fabricated accusation. The sequence of the counts at vv. 155–157 maps the escalation: covenant quietly broken — then signs selectively refused — then inconvenient prophets removed — then the unreceptive heart defended as a surgical fact — then, when even those defenses failed against a speaking infant, the slander of his mother — the final instrument of unbelief being always the honor-charge, because honor-questions are the questions unanswerable by laboratory: Mary could not *prove* a miracle the scandalizers refused to see; she could only be what she was — and God be what He is: and the Book records the standing adjournment of the case: the calumny entered into evidence; the Verdict deferred — vv. 160-161's file (the forbidding of good things once lawful — the standing consequence-clause of this entire indictment) naming the first penalty installment in history, and 4:161's "severe punishment prepared for the disbelievers among them" the standing remainder. The next verse's count — the sixth — will complete the file in its most consequential utterance of all: "we slew the Messiah, Jesus son of Mary, the messenger of God" — the boast that the Book will answer with its own fourfold negation, and with the raising that undid it.

## Sūrah al-Nisāʾ 4:100

> **Whosoever migrates in the way of God will find upon the earth many a refuge and abundance, and whosoever forsakes his home, emigrating unto God and His Messenger, and death overtakes him, his reward has fallen upon God, and God is Forgiving, Merciful.**

**Expanded Commentary**

**Wa Man Yuhājir fī Sabīli Allāhi Yajid fī al-Arḍi Murāghaman Kathīran wa Saʿah: The Wide-Earth Promise, Kept**

The sūrah's hijrah-arc (vv. 97–100) now receives its own triumphant counter-hymn — God's direct travel-agency contract with the emigrant: *wa man yuhājiru fī sabīli Allāhi yajid fī al-arḍi murāghaman kathīran wa saʿatan* — "whosoever migrates in the way of God finds in the earth many a refuge — and abundance": *murāgham* — from r-gh-m — the root of *rughm* — “despite” (the commentators' classical glosses branching among its spacious lands: a refuge to which the emigrant can turn and be secure from his persecutors — al-Ṭabarī; wide open country for roaming — breadth of land; even many alternatives of dwelling — each of the language-doctors piling upon its amplitude) — *kathīran* — much, many — the multiplication-particle; and *wa saʿah* — and amplitude — from w-s-ʿ — spreading room, plenty (its Book-rooting: 2:261's — "God is Ample, Knowing"; 4:97's objections its twin answer at 4:97 for the angel-question — *was God's earth not vast?* — the earth-amplitude named first there as an argument — here as a *promise* — the wide-amplitude this time guaranteed to the migrant himself: out-accommodated, out-abundant, out-rich — the very text becoming a standing mercy-axiom: God matches every abandoned hectare with wider ground) — with the Sīrah's two great exempla beneath: the Prophet's own emigration (beds whispered of; swords ringed about) — became Makkah conquered in eight years, Madinah the world's capital-of-revelation; and the Companions' exoduses — Bilāl from torture-ground to his honored station as the muʾadhdhin of Madinah; and Ṣuhayb al-Rūmī trading his whole fortune for road-freedom against his pursuers — Ibn Hishām’s standing story: Ṣuhayb detained by the Makkans, striking the ransom-bargain, telling them where his money lay hidden so they would let him pass — and arriving before the Prophet, who called out to him: “the sale has profited, Abū Yaḥyā!”)

**Wa Man Yakhruju min Baytihi Muhājiran ilā Allāhi wa Rasūlihi: Death Mid-Road, Full Wage**

Then the crown-miracle clause of the arc — the verse that turned hijrah into a wager with no losing ticket: *wa man yakhruju min baytihi muhājiran ilā Allāhi wa rasūlihi thumma yudrikhu al-mawtu* — "whosoever leaves his house, migrating to God and His Messenger — then death overtakes him — his reward has fallen due upon God": *thumma yudrikhu al-mawt* — *then death catches him up* (yudrik from d-r-k — the standing catchword from v. 78's towers — the same verb here swung from terror to destiny swung now into mercy-valor: the feared death upon the road, when it comes, is exactly *the clause of full payment*) — *faqad waqaʿa ajruhu ʿalā Allāh* — "his reward has fallen due *upon God*": waqaʿa — fallen-settled (the contractual form of covenant-due) — ʿalā — upon — the telling preposition making the Eternal the debtor in the emigrant's ledger: his wage not cancelled by his mid-road death but *charged upon God Himself* (the commentators' favorite doctrine of the whole of the whole war-and-travel canon: intention prevailing over completion — 4:100 in Bukhārī's titles al-Bukhārī's own classic hadith running its orthodox authentication — *"innamā al-aʿmālu bi'l-niyyāt — surely deeds are by intentions — and for every man only what he intended: he whose emigration was to God and His Messenger, his emigration is to God and His Messenger; he whose emigration was for for a worldly thing to acquire, or a woman to marry — his emigration is to that which he emigrated for"* — Bukhārī 1, Muslim 1907 — the Book of Revelation opening hadith of the whole canon) — and the sīrah-portrait beneath it — al-Ṭabarī's standing account of the aged emigrant who died on the road from Makkah, murmuring with his last breath: *lā ilāha illa Allāh* — and men of Madinah divided over whether his reward lapsed by incomplete arrival — whereupon this very verse descended: God Himself *assumed* his wage — (the Makkans mocking; the believers rejoicing; and heaven sealing: *the debtor in the emigrant's case is the Ever-Living, who can neither be defrauded nor default* — with the classical fuqahāʾ at once widening it into the whole destiny the whole doctrine of *religious death-in-transit* — the dying pilgrim, the sought-of the student perishing on the road of knowledge, the lettered the the emigrant of every century who leaves his city for faith's sake with his feet pointed Godward — each one standing beneath v. 100's underwritten clause — the wage-payer not the destination but the Sovereign — *faqad waqaʿa ajruhu ʿalā Allāh*) — and the two-attributes sealing once more — *wa kāna Allāhu ghafūran raḥīmā* — Forgiving, Merciful — the roll concluded with the pair that had recurred all the way from the sūrah's opening block, always arched over the faithful wherever they fall.

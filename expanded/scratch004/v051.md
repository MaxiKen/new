## Sūrah al-Nisāʾ 4:51

> **Hast thou not considered those who were given a portion of the Book, who believe in idols and false deities, and say of those who disbelieve, "These are guided more rightly than those who believe."**

**Expanded Commentary**

**Yuʾminūna bi-l-Jibt wa-l-Ṭāghūt: Two Tokens of Everything False**

The pericope's fifth lantern turns its beam upon the portion-bearers' deepest betrayal: *yuʾminūna bi-l-jibti wa-l-ṭāghūt* — "they believe in *al-jibt* and *al-ṭāghūt*": two rare words, each loaded by a whole tradition of gloss — *al-jibt*, a hapax of the Book (only here) whose basic sense the lexical authorities (al-Jawharī-era philology reported through al-Ṭabarī's roll) fix as *the nothing-worth*, the weightless trifle — idol-figure, sorcerer's stock, chance-token; *al-ṭāghūt*, the over-towerer — from *ṭughyān*, transgressing the bounds (flood-water over its banks) — naming *every overlord who sets himself across God's limits*: the Satan, the tyrant, the soothsayer, the false god — familiar from its repeated battle-posts in the Book (2:256–257 — "whoever disbelieves in the ṭāghūt and believes in God has grasped the firmest bond"; 4:60's about-to-appear arbitration-refusers "wish to resort to the ṭāghūt though commanded to disbelieve in it"; 4:76's "fight the allies of Satan"; 16:36's per-community command — "worship God and shun the ṭāghūt") — and the classical asbāb-rolls (al-Wāḥidī's chains, al-Ṭabarī, Ibn Kathīr, al-Zamakhsharī) converge on the occasion that gave these two names their Madinan faces: *Kaʿb ibn al-Ashraf and Ḥuyayy ibn Akhṭāb*, the two fiery scholars of the Book-towns, riding to Mecca after the Muslims' Uḥud-reversal to build the anti-Prophet coalition — and there brought, the reports say, before Quraysh's idols: pressed by their hosts to honor the two Meccan god-figures called *jibt and ṭāghūt* (or, by alternative chains, standing for idol-stock and sooth for idol and oracle) — *they paid the bows their hosts asked* — scriptured men, learned in the One, honoring tokens — a transaction of alliance priced in worship.

**Hāʾulāʾi Ahdā mina Alladhīna Āmanū: The Comparative Lie Before the Kaʿbah**

The deeper wound was their verdict-sentence pronounced at the Kaʿbah's precinct: *wa yaqūlūna li-lladhīna kafarū hāʾulāʾi ahdā mina alladhīna āmanū* — "and they say of the disbelievers: these are more rightly guided than the believers": the Meccans had put the comparison-bill before them with precision (the reporters preserve its lobbying-wording: *we give drink to the pilgrims and keep the sanctuary and uphold kinship; Muhammad has abandoned the Kaʿbah's rites and cut the wombs* — with the world between Mecca's pilgrimage-hosting and the fugitive-prophet's new claim set out for the two scholar-guests as a test) — and the two delivered the verdict their hosts wanted: the idol-keepers *are better guided* than your people — scripture-holders judging polytheism the riper road — the commentators marking the moment as among history's most instructive collapses: credentialed learning renting itself to the room's applause — al-Rāzī's quiet horror in his note on the scene's rational scandal: the two could read the Torah's first table (*no god but God*) while praising the shrine of the many over the camp of the One — and Ibn Kathīr's verdict echoing down the centuries: *the scholar who flatters falsehood with his rank is worse than the idolater he decorates* — the seat for the pagan's error crashes is naked, while the savant's lie goes out wearing the robe of authentication. The tradition's jurisprudence of learned betrayal (the fuqahāʾ toll-call of *ʿulamāʾ al-sūʾ* — evil-scholars — built from cases like this one) and the prophetic alarm both stand here at their fountain:

> *"That which I fear most for my ummah is the misguiding imams."* (Abū Dāwūd 4252; al-Tirmidhī 2229 — in the narrative of Thawbān)

**The General Meaning: Faith-Language Can Serve the Ṭāghūt**

Beyond the scene stands the verse's standing legibility (al-Ṭabarī's own closing unzip al-Ṭabarī's closing extension of the lexicon as much quoted as the asbāb): *al-jibt wa-l-ṭāghūt* discharge their duty as the umbrella over *all false objects to whom portion-loss to whom people resort in God's stead* — every carved token and every transgressing authority — so that the verse ever measures *religious professionals* wherever they point their authority: the criterion is never the robe but the arrow — toward whom does the scholar's praise *move* the room? The classical morality-texts (al-Ghazālī's Iḥyāʾ-chapters on the scholar's snares transposed a whole the Iḥyāʾ's books on the perils of learning) preserve the lesson as the standing fear of the devout: knowledge itself cannot immunize against the audience's prices; the same credentials that certify sound doctrine can be rented to crown the ṭāghūt — hence believers are taught to test every clerical verdict against the measure of its alignment with the guidance of those who believe* — not against credentials — since even portion-bearers have pronounced idols fairer than the Book's own household. The verse thereby seals its warning within the sūrah's jurisprudence-of-trustworthiness: just as orphan-wealth must be guarded from embezzling guardians, *guidance itself must be guarded from endorsing scholars* — watching the *direction* of their verdicts, never only the dressing — with the Prophet's remaining counter-guarantee held before the community as its only safe harbor:

> *"God does not seize knowledge by snatching it from the servants; rather He takes the knowledge by taking the scholars — until, when no scholar is left, people take ignorant heads and are asked, and they give verdicts without knowledge: they go astray and lead others astray."* (al-Bukhārī 100; Muslim 2673)

## Sūrah al-Nisāʾ 4:78

> **Wheresoever you may be, death will overtake you, though you should be in towers raised high. And if good befalls them, they say, "This is from God," but if evil befalls them, they say, "This is from thee." Say, "All is from God." What is with these people that they scarcely understand any tiding?**

**Expanded Commentary**

**Aynamā Takūnū Yudrikkumu al-Mawtu: Death as the Universal Pursuer**

The verse that closes the postponement-debate opens with one of the Book's inescapables: *aynamā takūnū yudrikkumu al-mawtu wa law kuntum fī burūjin mushayyadatin* — "wheresoever you may be, death will overtake you — though you should be in raised towers": *aynamā* — wheresoever — spatial totality; *yudrikkumu* — shall catch up with you — from d-r-k, to overtake, reach — the pursuit-verb (death not waiting *at* a place but *tracking* the man wherever he moves — the commentary-picture from al-Rāzī's tradition employed by the old waraʿ-books: iḍlāʾ al-mawt death travels with the traveler as his shadow does — no tow-path escape in any map) — *wa law* — though, if even — the concessive hypothetical admitting the most fortified scenario: in burūj — towers — mushayyadah — raised, plastered, elevated — towers either earthly (the common reading: citadel-fortresses — human defense at its supreme form its extreme material form) or — the minority-ccosmic some commentators' impressive alternate — the burūj *of heaven* — constellations themselves (invoking 15:16, 25:61, 85:1 — the div-vault towers with their angels) — even among the stars death follows — the clause thereby establishing Islam's flat denial that *geography* is any life-insurance: the flight from fighting saves no one from dying; the ap? the appointed term stands heaven-fixed (the Book's two big death-clocks signed beside it: "when their term comes, they cannot postpone it an hour, nor anticipate" — 7:34*, and 16:61; 3:154's "those for whom slaughter was decreed would have gone forth to their slaughter-beds even had they stayed at home" — contextual twin since the Uḥud-Re and 3:154's clause the direct sibling of ours in its Uḥud-context) — tarrying therefore trading *face-down trading a death with meaning for a death without one* — both equally calendar-fixed.

**Wa In Tuṣibhum Ḥasanatun... Hādhihi min ʿIndi Allāh: The Two Receipts the Fainters Write**

Then the psychology of hypocrisy's ledger unre unmasked — the believers' ascription-ethic inverted by the fainters: *wa in tuṣibhum ḥasanatun yaqūlū hādhihi min ʿindi Allāhi wa in tuṣibhum sayyiʾatun yaqūlū hādhihi min ʿindik* — "and if good befall them they say: this is from God; but if evil befall them they say: this is from thee": the complaint's recorded object being precisely the Prophet — drought, setback, or battle-loss blamed upon *him* as ill-omen (the asbāb-chains of al-Wāḥidī, al-Ṭabarī, al-al-Qurṭubī registering its two prime theater-cases: (1) the Madinan Book-holders attributing the drought-years and reverses to the Prophet's very arrival — *musha'ūm* — ero ill-boding slander, the classic pagan deprec the old taṭayyur superstition of fortune-linkage to men — cf. 7:131's identical complaint hurled at Moses — "when evil befalls them they augur ill from Moses" — and 27:47's at Ṣāliḥ; and (2) the fainting wing the fainters of the battlefield-community blaming the Uḥud-reverse upon the Prophet's decisions — *had the old men been consulted?* politics masquerading as providence-reading) — the doubled clause thereby exposing the deeper diagonal? wickedness: heaven's blessings are *mine*, life's scratches are *thine* — self-favoritism wearing creed-language — the believer's own protocol being the exact mirror-image (2:156's "indeed we belong to God, and to Him we return" over affliction; 4:79's next-verse correction straightaway: whatever good befalls *thee is from God*; whatever ill befalls thee *is from thyself* — with 42:30's reconciliation across the school-debate: "whatever misfortune strikes you is from what your hands earned — and He pardons much").

**Qul Kullun min ʿIndi Allāh: The All-From-God Verdict — and the Two Schools at Their Parish at Their Post**

The Prophet's ruled answer is the Quran is the shortest theology of providence in the Book: *qul kullun min ʿindi Allāh* — "Say: all is from God": *kull* — all — good and evil, ease and hardship, victory and reverse — no exemptions — and *min ʿindi Allāh* — from God's Presence — the ladunnī the now-familiar nearness-preposition making even the drought *divinely originated* — a verdict the schoolmasters then spent centuries glossing: the Ashʿarite reconciliation (al-Rāzī's own clamp-down de? location): everything — all events and even acts — falls within His qadar and creation, "evil" being *trial* not *injustice*, while the guilt-of-sin belongs to the doers; the Muʿtazil?$? the Muʿtazilite counter-position (al-Zamakhsharī's own register at this verse): He originates all *events*, but evil *deeds* (sin) are authored by their doers, hardship being His justice answering disobedience — the two houses agreeing upon the vital refutation at hand: *the hypocrite's blame-split (mine from God, harm from thee, Prophet) is false in both halves* — victory is His gift, not their merit; hardship is His wisdom-trial or their earned due, never the Messenger's ill-star — the tradition lodging its standing pastoral-rule upon the verse thereafter: bear both sal? both weathers by the one ascription* — Muslim's the Prophet's own report (Muslim 2999) being the practical perfect prose-companion:

> *"Wondrous is the affair of the believer! His affair is all good, and that is for none but the believer: if prosperity comes to him, he is grateful, and it is good for him; and if adversity comes to him, he is patient, and it is good for him."*

**Fa-Mā li-Hāʾulāʾi al-Qawmi Lā Yakādūna Yafqahūna Ḥadīthan: Perception-Famine as Diagnosis**

The seal compounds astonishment—? with diagnosis: *fa-mā li-hāʾulāʾi al-qawmi lā yakādūna yafqahūna ḥadīthan* — "what is with these people — they scarcely understand a tiding?": *fa-mā li-*, what ails — the question of perplexity of wonder at ruin — and *yafqahūna* — understanding — not scientific grasp but *religious comprehension of reports* (fiqh-roots: the word of knowledge attached throughout the Book to *discernment of meaning* — 7:179's hearts *lā yafqahūna bihā* — incapable of taking in — and 4:78's near-neighbor 63:3?) no — 63:3/7 63's hypocrite-circle being the closest? the readers' nearest parallel (63:3 — "they believed then disbelieved — so their hearts were sealed, and they do not understand") — *ḥadīthan* — a *tiding*, a speech-event: even a single account they struggle to metabolize — the verse thereby closing the file of vv. 77–78's two mis-fears and mis-ascriptions with the same explanation? spring with the same bottom-line the pericope has kept issuing since v. 60: the problem of the fainters and the? is never the Word's obscurity; it is a *perception-famine born of misworn hearts* — God-fear correctly seated cures the mind along with the hand: understanding following the fear of its right Object as light follows the sun — while they who fear men past God can no longer *read events* — reading backwards instead: blessing as entitlement, hardship as others' curse, death as escapable, postponement as mercy — *lā yakādūna yafqahūna ḥadīthan* — not a single tiding understood — the Book's finished diagnosis of the postponement-soul, handed down for every reader tempted to answer God's marching-orders with *later*: understand first: your term is fixed; your weather from Him; your fortune audited — and your calendar has no forgiving margins — *then act.*

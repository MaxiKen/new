## Sūrah al-Nisāʾ 4:92

> **It is not for a believer to slay a believer, unless it be in error. Whosoever has slain a believer in error, let him set free a believing slave and pay compensation to the victim's family, unless they remit it in charity. If he belonged to a people at war with you, but was a believer, then a believing slave is to be set free. And if he belonged to a people with whom you have a covenant, let him pay compensation to the victim's family, and let him set free a believing slave. Whosoever finds not [the means], let him fast two consecutive months as a penance from God. God is Knowing, Wise.**

**Expanded Commentary**

**Wa Mā Kāna li-Muʾminin an Yaqtula Muʾminan illā Khaṭaʾan: The Believer's Blood a Protected Citadel**

Having regulated war without, the sūrah now walls blood within — with the strongest grammatical construction Arabic can build: *wa mā kāna li-muʾminin an yaqtula muʾminan* — "it is not for a believer to slay a believer": *mā kāna* — it is not, it cannot be, it stands outside the possible — the prohibition conveyed in the ontological register rather than merely the juridical (not "do not kill" but *believer-killing-believer is not among the things that are* — as the commentators put it, the sentence-lines vault the crime out of Islam's own possibility-map: cf. 49:11-12's parallel ontologizing of the ummah's vices — "let not a folk deride a folk"; 4:29's "kill not one another — God is ever Merciful unto you") — with the sole egress-clause immediately attached: *illā khaṭaʾan* — unless by error — *khaṭiʾ* — the miss, the unaimed the unaimed harm (khaṭaʾ vs. ʿamd — error vs. intent — becoming the master-contrast of Islamic penal law at its root) — and the asbāb-lines anchoring the statute in Madinah's ground: al-Ṭabarī and al-Wāḥidī's preservation naming ʿAyyāsh ibn Abī Rabīʿah, the Emigrant, who struck down a man who had once flogged him savagely in Makkah for his Islam — not knowing the man had since emigrated and embraced the faith; and its companion-report of Abū al-Dardāʾ, who struck an enemy at the battle's edge even as the shahādah was rising to his lips — a cautionary tale the tradition carries as the standing warning against killing under battle-doubt (its plenary form at 4:94 below: "say not to him who offers you peace: thou art no believer").

**Man Qatala Muʾminan Khaṭaʾan: The Fourfold Reparation Architecture**

Then the statute itself — Islamic law's standing engine of blood-repair, built in clauses like bricks: *man qatala muʾminan khaṭaʾan fa-taḥrīru raqabatin muʾminatin wa diyatun musallamatun ilā ahlihi illā an yaṣṣaddaqū* — "whosoever slays a believer by error: freeing of a believing neck, and compensation delivered to his family — unless they remit it in charity": (1) *taḥrīru raqabatin muʾminatin* — the freeing of a believing slave — *taḥrīr* from ḥ-r-r — emancipation (the verse's unbearable symmetry noted by every commentator: against one life lost, one life *granted*: the dead goes to God, the bound walks free — expiation designed as creation-symmetrical rebalancing — the Book's other stations of the same institution — 5:89's broken oaths; 58:3-4's ẓihār — all bearing the same economy: Islam's penances do not chastise the wallet; they *restore life to the bound*) — (2) *diyah* — blood-money — handed *to the victim's house* (the canon-fixing at its classical quantum in the fiqh: a hundred camels for a free man among the camel-lands, or a thousand gold dīnārs for the wealthways of the towns — al-al-Qurṭubī and al-Ṭabarī's register — payable across three years from the killer's *ʿāqilah* — his paternal kin-coop i.e., his paternal corps of kin — the remarkable social institution by which the clan shoulders its member's blood-debt, binding the whole tribe against each man's rash stroke) — (3) *illā an yaṣṣaddaqū* — unless the family gives it up in alms — *ṣadaqah*! — (the forgiveness-door, stitched into the homicide-statute itself: charity-transfiguring revenge into ad into a gift (the Book's own parallel sanction of the higher road: 2:178's "whosoever is pardoned by his brother aught, let there be fair following and payment in kindness"; 42:40's "whosoever pardons and makes reconciliation — his wage is upon God"); and (4) the error-grades elaborated by the later canon: accidental vs semi-intent — the heavy-camel the heavy diyah for the semi-intent; the dialectical-only the pure-accidental at its ordinary rate — with the death-of-dhimmī the protected non-Muslim's blood standing in its own rate-structure beneath the beneath classical fiqh's tables (the majority half-pairing versus al-al-Qurṭubī's mapping — with its foundation in the hadith-canon's own extension: "the killer is cut off from inheritance" and the covenant-man's blood parity ruled under treaty-protect the Sunnah itself commanding equal protection for ahl-al-dhimmah on the battlefield's far side of error).

**Fa-Man Lam Yajid fa-Ṣiyāmu Shahrayni Mutatābiʿayn: The Poor Man's Penance**

The statute's final mercy is its floor-clause for the destitute: *fa-man lam yajid fa-ṣiyāmu shahrayni mutatābiʿayn — tawbatan mina Allāh* — "whosoever finds not the means — then the fast of two consecutive months, as a penance from God": *lam yajid* — finds not (he who has neither slave nor camel nor kin-purse) — and the substitute: sixty successive days of fasting (its twin-station: 58:4's ẓihār-penance — and the standing fiqh-note that *consecutiveness* — mutatābiʿayn — is commanded by the verbal adjective itself: a broken sequence begins the count again — repentance requiring the unbroken dailiness of self-discipline, the body trained by the slip by the slip's own span) — *tawbatan* — as repentance — in the accusative of purpose: the fast itself is the tawbah, not a mere platform for it — fasting embodying the inward turning (the prophet's standing root the Prophet's own pairing across the whole discipline: "fasting is a shield" — Bukhārī 1894-5 — here arrayed not for blessing-currency but for *blood-washing*) — and the twin-attributes sealing the statute: *wa kāna Allāhu ʿalīman ḥakīmā* — "God is Knowing, Wise": Knowing — the Intent-Gauge; Wise — the Law-Fashioner (the twin-epithets returning at the sūrah's every legislated turn — vv. 17, 24, 26, 92 — listing the roll-call itself a pedagogy: every statute of this sūrah descends rooted in omniscient-exact knowing plus wise-measured shaping — never arbitrary statute) — and among the lenda among living scholars across centuries the verse has stood as Islam's first cathedral of restorative justice — no the killer of error not the killer by error faces not the sword but the *gift-duty* — a slave walks free, a family receives a kin-transmitted fortune, or the poor man's body fasts two months toward God — bloodshed met not with counter-bloodshed but with *life-restitution by every available instrument of freedom* — the ʿāqilah-system itself being one of the ancient world's earliest collective insurance-pools (prefiguring every modern no-fault compensation fund by thirteen centuries, yet grounded in covenant rather than actuarial table), and the charity-egress being the permanently open portal back to the Mercy that made the believers brothers from the first day.

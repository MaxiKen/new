## Sūrah al-Nisāʾ 4:14

> **But whosoever disobeys God and His Messenger, and transgresses His limits, He will cause him to enter a Fire, abiding therein, and he shall have a humiliating punishment.**

**Expanded Commentary**

**Yaʿiṣ and Yataʿadda: The Twin Verbs of the Crossed Line**

The complement-verse arrives with the chilling symmetry of the Book's paired promises, distinguishing itself from v. 13 by the additional verb: *wa man yaʿiṣi Allāha wa rasūlahu wa yataʿadda ḥudūdahu* — "whoso disobeys God and His Messenger, and *transgresses His limits*": the first verb names the direction of the heart — *maʿṣiyah*, disobedience, the will's turn; the second names the accomplished deed — *taʿaddī*, crossing-over, from *ʿadw*: overstepping, intrusion past the boundary — and the commentators' pairing holds them in sequence: v. 13's obedient walks *within* the posts; v. 14's adds to refusal the *achieved trespass*, the sixth reduced, the widow's seat commandeered, the bequest's floor broken. Al-Ṭabarī marks the fiqh-note the law schools drew at this exact doubling: *taʿaddī al-ḥudūd* became the standing idiom for transgression against every divine statute whatsoever — coined from this verse's own mint; every fence-post cut down thereafter in estates, in blood, in chastity-laws is described in the fiqh-registers by v. 14's verb.

**Yudkhilhu Nāran: Entrance Mirrored, Destination Reversed**

*Yudkhilhu nāran khālidan fīhā* — "He will enter him into Fire, abiding therein": the terrible mirror to v. 13's *yudkhilhu jannātin*, same verb — *He will cause him to enter* — same construction, hell's gate now the threshold; the Book forcing the eye to compare the two admissions side by side, as an estate's two roads fork from a single gate-clerk. *Khālidan fīhā* likewise repeats, and the repetition is itself the shudder: permanence was the garden's crown; it is the fire's despair — the same word, *abiding*, blessing one course and sealing the other. Al-Qurṭubī's transmission of the salaf-reading keeps the clause soberly general: whoever disobeys the Lord's limits *in this way* — consuming trusts, falsifying shares — stands exposed to the verse's full threat; and the majority read the khulūd-of-the-disobedient-sinner per the Book's own exempting-window (God forgives other than shirk whom He wills, v. 48 below) as the Hereafter's threatened consistency: *the fire-admission is the limit-crosser's earned lodging until God's pardon or the intercession-term* — with the verse's own disaster *complete* for the denier, who has no exit at all. Either way, no reader of vv. 10–14 is left unadvertised: the arch of the orphan-pericope closes at fire for embezzlers and at fire for boundary-breakers, and the middle path of obedience walks straight.

**ʿAdhābun Muhīn: Punishment's Disgrace-Accent Stipulated**

The final clause appoints the punishment's particular flavor: *wa lahu ʿadhābun muhīn* — "and his shall be a punishment of humiliation": *muhīn*, from *hawn* — the low, the stripped of all standing — the Book's terminal insult-level (its cousins: 3:178's *ʿadhāb muhīn* awaiting liars on God, 3:192's Fire-enterers' disgrace, 22:9's "his in the world is disgrace..."), deliberately added where the offense's root was *pride over property*: he who reached across God's line to *magnify himself* against weak mouths receives, as custom-fitted requital, humiliation — the aggrandizement burned off him the way the forged-amended page is burned off its ink. Ibn Kathīr's note is austere: the muhīn-epithet fits v. 14's crime precisely because the estate-cheat is almost always a *face-crime* — the strong disdainful of tables where orphans sit — and God's sentence answers disdain with its own coin magnified to permanence. The warning thus ends the legislative overture of Sūrah al-Nisāʾ on the Book's by-now-declared principle: *the limits are the Lord's; the auditor He posts is His own; the two gates of v. 13 and v. 14 stand open at every probate, every trust, every ledger — and what men enter through their pens, they shall abide.* From here the sūrah turns from the dead's property to the living's purity — to the ordinances of chastity, where the same *ḥudūd*-words preside over the beds of the community as they now have over its graves.

## Sūrah al-Nisāʾ 4:131

> **Unto God belongs whatsoever is in the heavens and whatsoever is on the earth. And We have charged those who were given the Book before you, and We charge you: be mindful of God. But if you disbelieve — then unto God belongs whatsoever is in the heavens and whatsoever is on the earth. And God is ever Self-Sufficient, Praiseworthy.**

**Expanded Commentary**

**The Triple Ownership-Cadence: v. 126, v. 131, v. 132**

The Book now returns to its standing ownership-plaque — but in an unprecedented run: the sūrah, having posted the plaque at v. 126 ("unto God belongs whatsoever is in the heavens and whatsoever is on the earth — and God encompasses all things"), posts it again here, and yet again at v. 132 — a threefold tolling inside seven verses that the commentators mark as among the sūrah's most deliberate structural monuments. Al-Rāzī suggests the flower of the placement: immediately before these declarations stood the verse of enrichment — v. 130's standing promise ("God shall enrich each of them from His bounty") — and the frightened hearts of separating spouses needed to know *whose* bounty was pledged: so the Book answers before the question is whispered — the heavens and the earth and all they hold are the estate of the Promiser — the guarantee of enrichment underwritten by universal ownership. The verse's own the standing comment-canon likewise observes the cadence's position *between* the family-file just closed (vv. 127–130) and the justice-summit opening below (v. 135): all earthly apportionment — dowries, inheritances, provisions — flows from the One to whom everything already belongs; human giving and receiving is but the internal accounting of His estate. Its standing Book-kin toll across the revelation — 2:284; 3:109; 3:129; 4:126 above; 10:55 ("lo — unto God belongs whatsoever is in the heavens and the earth"); 14:2; 34:1 — but nowhere in the Book do the tolls sound so clustered as here: the sūrah of the household pausing thrice to remind the householders whose house the universe is.

**Wa Laqad Waṣṣaynā... An Ittaqū Allāha: The One Command Common to Every Community**

The verse then reaches across the borders of every religious civilization with the standing charge of all revelation: *wa laqad waṣṣaynā alladhīna ūtū al-kitāba min qablikum wa iyyākum an ittaqū Allāh* — "and We have charged those who were given the Book before you, and We charge you: be mindful of God": *waṣṣaynā* — from w-ṣ-y — the solemn bequest-charge, the covenant-instruction deposited with trustees (its standing Book-file: 2:125 — the charge to Abraham and Ishmael to purify the House; 2:132 — "and Abraham charged his sons with it — and Jacob: my sons — God has chosen the religion for you"; 6:151–153's standing ten-charge compendium; 42:13 — "He has ordained for you of the religion what He charged Noah with — and what We revealed to thee — and what We charged Abraham and Moses and Jesus: establish the religion and scatter not therein"). Al-Rāzī draws the standing doctrine the verse's syntax plainly teaches: taqwā — the mindfulness-reverence toward God — is the one *sharīʿah common to all religious communities* — rituals vary, laws differ, calendars diverge, but the inner command laid upon "those who were given the Book before you" and upon "you" is one command, grammatically shared in a single predicate: *an ittaqū Allāh* — the universal constitution under all particular codes. The standing scholastic register (from al-Ṭabarī's chains through Ibn Kathīr's summary) designates taqwā the "comprehensive counsel" — al-waṣiyyah al-jāmiʿah — of every prophet to every people: the command containing obedience in whole, for the God-fearing cannot persist in any known wrong. And its placement after the ownership-declaration is itself exegesis: because all belongs to Him, all owe Him; the deed of universal possession grounding the universal duty — property and piety bound in one verse.

**Wa In Takfurū: The Freedom of Refusal Announced Inside the Command**

Then the verse performs the standing dramatic movement of Qur'ānic legislation — command, then the announcement of the command's violation, then the announcement of the violation's costlessness-for-God: *wa in takfurū fa-inna li-llāhi mā fī al-samāwāti wa mā fī al-arḍ* — "but if you disbelieve — then lo: unto God belongs whatsoever is in the heavens and whatsoever is on the earth": the ownership-plaque tolling a second time — now not as comfort to the separating but as indifference-adjacent majesty to the refusing: your disbelief removes nothing from the estate; your refusal of the King does not depose the King; you disbelieve *inside* His property, breathing His air, standing His ground — the standing irony the Book presses everywhere (its standing parallels: 3:10 — "as for those who disbelieve — neither their wealth nor their children shall avail them aught against God"; 2:6 — "it is all one upon them whether thou warnest them or warnest them not"; 36:10's identical formula). Ibn Taymiyyah's standing distinction orders the scene: the command of taqwā is God's *religious-law will* — what He loves and demands; the outcome of disbelief falls under God's *creative will* — which permits what it forbids — so that the disbeliever defies the command yet never escapes the decree: he remains a tenant of the heavens and earth he denies having a Landlord.

**Wa Kāna Allāhu Ghaniyyan Ḥamīdan: Self-Sufficiency and the Praise That Owes Nothing to Its Debtors**

The verse seals with the Name-pair that answers the deepest question the disbeliever could think to ask — *what does God lose by my loss?*: *wa kāna Allāhu ghaniyyan ḥamīdan* — "and God is ever Self-Sufficient, Praiseworthy": *ghanī* — from gh-n-y — not mere wealth but complete independence — freedom from need in every direction (v. 130 had spent His enrichment on spouses; here His standing note — al-Rāzī's standing scholastic formulation — He is *neither increased by obedience nor diminished by disobedience*: worship does not fatten Him, rebellion does not wound Him; the hadith qudsī in Muslim's standing register 2577 — "O My servants — were the first of you and the last of you, the human of you and the jinn of you, to be as pious as the most pious heart of any one man of you — that would not increase My kingdom in anything... and were they to be as wicked as the most wicked heart — that would not decrease My kingdom in anything") — and *ḥamīd* — the Praiseworthy — the Name of intrinsic, creditor-free praiseworthiness: praised not because creatures happen to praise (their praise is charity they receive, not tribute He needs) but because praise inheres in His very attributes — the standing Name-pair appearing at 2:267's giving-verse ("God is Self-Sufficient, Praiseworthy"), 14:8 (Moses' proclamation: "if you disbelieve — you and whoever is on earth altogether — then lo, God is Self-Sufficient, Praiseworthy"), 22:64, 31:12 (Luqmān's counsel), 31:26, 35:15 ("you are the poor unto God — and God is the Self-Sufficient, the Praised"), 57:24, 60:6. The sūrah of orphans, wives, and dowries thus ends this charge with the reverse-view mirror: the Creator needs nothing in His creation — every obligation He lays is benefit laid upon the obliged, every gift He gives is loss He never feels — the Self-Sufficient Praiseworthy One legislating taqwā not as tribute-collection but as the standing open door through which impoverished tenants may walk into the width of v. 130's bounty — while the horizon of v. 133 now darkens just ahead: the Self-Sufficient who can enrich any household He chooses can also, if He wills, replace entire peoples who decline the tenancy-terms — "if He so wills, He can remove you, O mankind — and bring others."

## Sūrah al-Nisāʾ 4:99

> **As for such, it may be that God will pardon them, for God is Pardoning, Forgiving.**

**Expanded Commentary**

**Fa-Ulāʾika ʿAsā Allāhu an Yaʿfuwa ʿAnhum: Hope Deferred to Heaven's Pardon**

The exception of v. 98 blossoms into v. 99's own word of hope — among the gentlest sentences in the whole sūrah: *fa-ulāʾika ʿasā Allāhu an yaʿfuwa ʿanhum* — "as for such — it may be that God will pardon them": *ulāʾika* — *these* — the pointing-pronoun singling out the trapped column for mention as their own class (the Book's standing protocol after verdict-passages: the indicted named with indictment-points; the excused named with mercy-points) — and *ʿasā* — it may be — the commentators at once reviving v. 84's theology-of-hope: God's *perchance* in the mouth of the Revelation meaning not contingency but *royal understatement* — a hope-word God chooses when He wills the spirits to *wait in expectation rather than in mere relief* (the standing parallel-rolls of the Book's ʿasā-stations: 17:79's — "perhaps your Lord will raise you to a praised station"; 28:67's "perhaps he will be among the rightly guided"; 66:5's "perhaps his Lord, if he should divorce you, will give him better wives than you") — with the imprisonment-trapped receiving certainty-under-poetics (so al-Rāzī: the ʿasā of God lies beyond anything of the human maybe-order: a word that grants hope and withholds complacency) — and *yaʿfuwa* — pardon-wipe — from ʿ-f-w — to efface, to pass over (the pardon-verb the commentators savored for its image: the ʿafw not merely abstaining from punishment but *erasing the trail of the sin itself* from the ledger — the trail swept off the sand by the wind — cf. the Prophet’s own standing night-invocation — “Allāhumma innaka ʿAfūwwun tuḥibbu al-ʿafwa faʿfu ʿannī — O God, Thou art Pardoning and lovest pardon, so pardon me” — al-Tirmidhī 3513; Ibn Mājah 3850 — the report closing with the standing formula: “He loves pardon — so ask Him for it”) — effacement disbursed to men whose crime was nothing worse than being chained where they could do no more.

**Wa Kāna Allāhu ʿAfūwwan Ghafūrā: The Double-Forgiving Seal**

The verse closes with the attribute pairing: *wa kāna Allāhu ʿafūwwan ghafūrā* — "and God is Pardoning, Forgiving": *ʿAfūww* — the intensive-pardoner (the intensive pattern of ʿafw — its tones multiplied — the One who effaces *again and again and again*) — and *Ghafūr* — the cover-Forgiver (again the helmet-and-concealment root — gh-f-r — the Coverer of the record) — the two divine names being deliberately paired at this precise joint for one standing reason, al-Rāzī's summary among the classical commentators: *ʿafw* answers the *charge* — the excuse-case (ʿudhr) itself erased as if no voice of accusation ever spoke; *maghfirah* answers the *blemish* — whatever of hesitation, of faltering at road's edge, or of sloth remains within the faithful trapped (no trapped man crosses even a long road-test spiritually spotless — intention wavers, and tears shed under coercion have their moments of bargaining weakness) — so the double pardon-gate matches the two defects of the trapped: the first door knocking down the case against them, the second cleansing the soul within them.

**The Standing Pastoral Law of the Mustaḍʿaf-Verdict**

And the tradition from then on has read vv. 97–99 together as Islam's complete charter of *spiritual incapacity* — the full sequence so exact that the fuqahāʾ formalized it into the standing code of obligation (the able unpardoned — the able's decree; the genuinely-blocked uncharged — v. 98/99's pardon) — with its horizons broadening through the centuries into every pastoral dispensation of the religion: the ignorant excused (the man to whom the route of knowledge never reached) the coerced protected (the 16:106-clause — *"save him who is forced, while his heart is at rest in faith"*) — the materially-blocked relieved of every costly charge (hajj being only upon *man istaṭāʿa ilayhi sabīlā* — 3:97's standing formula — the very pattern of v. 98's *lā yastaṭīʿūna... wa lā yahtadūna sabīlā* threading itself through Islam's pillar-law) — with the upshot given by Ibn Taymiyyah's master principle, preserved in his standing maxims: *God's obligation upon any soul never rises higher than the ceiling of its own God-given capacity* — lā yukallifu Allāhu nafsan illā wusʿahā (2:286) — and every duty carried beyond reach is, by this pair of verses, laid beneath God's own prefunded mercy — so that the theology of the sūrah's hijrah-arc is in the end wholly merciful: zeal must *pay* at Madinah — but the chained sleep clean under the double lamp of ʿAfūww and Ghafūr.

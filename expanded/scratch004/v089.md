## Sūrah al-Nisāʾ 4:89

> **They wish that you should disbelieve, even as they disbelieve, that you may be on a level with them. So take them not as protectors till they migrate in the way of God. But if they turn their backs, then seize them and slay them wherever you find them, and take no protector or helper from among them.**

**Expanded Commentary**

**Waddū Law Takfurūna kamā Kafarū fa-Takūnūna Sawāʾan: The Equality-of-Misery Wish**

The verdict upon the two-timing band opens with the exposure of their wish: *waddū law takfurūna kamā kafarū fa-takūnūna sawāʾan* — "they wish that you should disbelieve as they disbelieved, that you might be on a level with them": *waddū* — from w-d-d — the wish-verb of longing (its root also giving *wudd* — affection — the commentators remarking that hypocrite-desire dresses itself as tenderness: their wish *for* you reads in their mouths like love of company, ruin-council wearing the mantle of reunion-invitation); *kafarū* — they disbelieved — the verdict finally said outright (the Book through v. 88's inversion now naming what their professions concealed); and *sawāʾ* — *level*, equal rank — the devastation-locus of the whole clause: equality as bait (the standing Quranic psychology of the apostate tempter: the one who has fallen cannot endure the sight of the un-fallen, and so he recruits company for his pit — the Book's fourfold anatomy of this downward-sociology crowding the memory: 33:68-69's damned — "give them a doubled punishment... they say: our Lord, those of jinn and men who led us astray — give them a doubled torment"; 16:86-87's partners hauled in; 37:31-32's "so we led you astray — indeed we ourselves were astray" — the fell-as the leaders' own confession: *we ourselves were astray* — misguiders gloating; even 2:166-8's banding followings splitting apart at the sight of chastisement) — al-Rāzī's diagnostic summarizing the human scene: *envy of equal footing* — the migrant-runaway gazing back at Madinah cannot bear to look up; so his plan becomes engineering how to bring the whole city down to his own floor — and the Book perpetually exposes him at his wish, before his schemes are even built (cf. 68:9 — "they wish that thou wouldst compromise, so they would compromise" — the truce-of-degradation wish running on the identical track).

**Fa-Lā Tattakhidhū Minhum Awliyāʾa Ḥattā Yuhājirū fī Sabīli Allāh: No Walāyah Without Hijrah**

Then the covenant-law riding the exposure: *fa-lā tattakhidhū minhum awliyāʾa ḥattā yuhājirū fī sabīli Allāh* — "so take not from among them protectors, till they migrate in the way of God": *awliyāʾ* — protecting-allies, covenant-friends (the sūrah's third restitching of the prohibition — v. 45's "God knows best your enemies — God suffices as a Walī"; vv. 139/144's cringing vv. 139 and 144's reiterations to come — "do you take the unbelievers as protectors rather than the believers?"; here the condition-clause makes the sūrah's political theology complete: walāyah is *conditional upon total allegiance*) — and the test named: *ḥattā yuhājirū fī sabīli Allāh* — until they migrate in God's path: *hājara* — the full covenant-signature of the first ummah (the Madinan charter-era the first-state phase when Islam's identity and Islam's geography were one — hijrah meaning: house sold, kin forsaken, passport stamped by blood-if-needed — the Book's own plenary portrait of that covenant at 8:72 — "those who believe, and migrate, and strive with their wealth and their selves in the way of God — and those who shelter and help — those are protectors of one another" — the anṣār's own membership-receipt exactly) — the commentators mapping it into the standing Madinan jurisprudence: loyalty to Islam's polity was attested by residence-and-arms, not by formulas on the lips (the moderns' note the standing jurisprudence of the classical canon ra generalizing beyond: the community may accept *professions* as grounds of individual membership, yet *trusted alliance* — office, confidence-food counsel, war-stations — lies committed only upon proved commitment) — and the equity of the test being its *remediable* character: the doorway back is open — migrate, and be in covenant again (cf. 9:117's rewriting reception of the three left behind: repentance being always a standing gate in the Book, even for the once-defected).

**Fa-in Tawallaw fa-Khudhūhum: The Turned-Backs Clause**

Then the sanction — among the strongest-seeming clauses in the sūrah and therefore, in the classical canon, the most carefully fenced: *fa-in tawallaw fa-khudhūhum wa-qtulūhum ḥaythu wajadtumūhum wā lā tattakhidhū minhum walīyyan wa lā naṣīrā* — "but if they turn their backs — then seize them and slay them wherever you find them — and take from among them no protector and no helper": *tawallaw* — turned their backs — the commentators' gloss being uniformly *apostate-enemy reversal* (the man who openly joins the war against the believers — not mere weakness, not mere departure-depression not mere migration-refusal but active belligerency: the asbāb-bands — those who quit Madinah, then fought in the idolater ranks, or robbed who spread the terror-report or harmed the believers — being exactly this category) — *fa-khudhūhum* — seize them — the capture-decret first; *wa-qtulūhum ḥaythu wajadtumūhum* — then the war-slaying decree with its open locative (its parallel-shadow the escape-clause at the war-charging the identical wartime sentence at 4:91 — both lodged strictly in a state-of-war context) — and the second clause restoring the prohibition (wa lā walīyyan wa lā naṣīrā — the covenant-ban twice-stitched: the seal-word *naṣīr* carrying the war-climate with it: no helper-status with enemies) — and the fence around all of it being v. 90's own exception set beside it — the very next verse opening *illā alladhīna yaṣilūna ilā qawmin baynakum wa baynahum mīthāq* — cutting the sword-clause's application to *only those who neither flee to treatypeople, nor lay down arms, nor incline to peace* — so that the sūrah's whole war-canon stands: futility fidelity to Islam's covenant, the right of a two-timer to return, the ban on making allies of enemies — and the sword reserved exactly for the man who meets you on the battlefield — the classical jurists of every century lodging their summaries in the same four-verse block (vv. 89–92 being the standing source-file of the war-law: enemy engagement — peace-rights — accidental-of-believer compensation; the canon thereby kept under one liability: whoever fights you, fight; whoever inclines to peace, incline; whoever wrongs unwittingly, make reparation; everything else, leave to God).

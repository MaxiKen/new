## Sūrah al-Nisāʾ 4:162

> **But those firmly rooted in knowledge among them, and the believers — believing in what was sent down to thee and in what was sent down before thee, those who keep the prayer and give the alms, and believe in God and the Last Day — unto those We shall grant a great reward.**

**Expanded Commentary**

**Lākini al-Rāsikhūna fī al-ʿIlmi Minhum: After the Indictment, the Remnant**

The verdict-file of vv. 155–161 had descended count by count; now the Book executes the turn its every chapter of judgment contains — the adversative of rescue: *lākini al-rāsikhūna fī al-ʿilmi minhum wa al-muʾminūna* — "but those firmly rooted in knowledge among them, and the believers": *al-rāsikhūna* — from r-s-kh — to be firmly rooted, to be set deep and immovable (its standing Qur'ānic technical coinage: 3:7's standing division of the community of scripture — "it is He who sent down the Book upon thee — among it verses decisive... and among it verses ambiguous... and none knows their interpretation but God. And those firmly rooted in knowledge say: we believe in it — all of it is from our Lord") — a commentator-aristocracy whose firmness is precisely their submission: knowledge rooted rather than floating; and *minhum* — "*among them*" — the partitive the whole sūrah has been holding in reserve: the indictment was never ethnic but covenantal — and within the very community so counted stands a corps of the deeply learned whose knowledge issues in full belief — "believing in what was sent down to thee and in what was sent down before thee": two-directional faith: the Torah and its true successors affirmed together (the standing undivided-subscription of 4:152 above; 2:136; 2:285; 3:84; the archipelago standing intact). The standing historical gloss of the commentators (al-Ṭabarī; al-Rāzī; al-Ṭabrisī; al-Zamakhsharī's register) names the earliest bearers of the description: ʿAbd Allāh ibn Salām and his fellow-learned of Madīnah who, knowing the earlier scripture and recognizing the new, believed in both — the standing files of his conversion in the tafsīr-records and sīrah-literature (Jewish scholars acknowledging the Prophet as described in their own books).

**Wa al-Muʾminūna: One Noun, Two Readings — and Their Consequence**

The second noun — *wa al-muʾminūna* — carries the commentators' standing double-construal: (1) as coordinate-explication of the rāsikhūn among the People of the Book — "and the believers" = those of them who believe — in which case the verse testifies to an astonishing standing possibility: that believing, righteous members of that community, confessing both their scripture and Muḥammad's, receive *a great reward* — their reward-documents distinct from either condemnatory file (its standing parallels: 3:113-115 — "they are not all alike — among the People of the Book is an upright community who recite God's signs through the night... they believe in God and the Last Day"; 3:199 — "among the People of the Book are those who believe in God and in what was sent down to you and what was sent down to them, humble toward God — they sell not the signs of God for a small price — theirs is their wage with their Lord"; 5:65-66; 28:52-54 — those given the Book before it believing in it, recompensed twice); and (2) as a general reference to the Muslim believers set beside the learned of the People of the Book: the rooted of *them*, and the believers of *the ummah* — both registry-lines ending at the same reward (al-Rāzī harvesting both without exclusion). Even the verse's grammar enters the commentary's workshop: *wa al-muqīmīna al-ṣalāta* — "those who keep the prayer" — stands in the *accusative* where syntax expects the nominative — the grammarians and commentators (al-Ṭabarī; al-Ṭabrisī; al-Zamakhsharī) registering the standing resolutions: an accusative of *praise* — the list-shift itself functioning as underlining ("and — especially — those who keep the prayer" — the standing Arabic device of case-shift to signal distinct honor); and a second reading construing it as object of *yuʾminūna* — "believing in... [and in] those who keep the prayer" — the object then read, per reports, as the angels or the prophets themselves (faith extended to the whole chain of worshippers). Both dissolve toward the same standing sum: prayer-keeping and almsgiving singled out as the litmus-habits of the genuinely rooted (29:45 — "prayer restrains from indecency and wrong"; 70:22-25 — the standing profile of the prayer-keepers in whose wealth is a known right).

**Ajr Kabīr: The Reward of the Rooted — and the Standing of Revelation Itself**

The verse ends at its destination — *ulāʾika sanuʾtīhim ajran kabīran* — "unto those We shall grant a great reward": *ajr kabīr* — the standing superlative of wages in the sūrah's own register (its stations: 4:54's "a great dominion"; 4:67; 4:74; 4:95; 4:114; 4:146 — "a great reward" for the believers-tradition; 9:22; 33:29; 48:10; 57:7; 64:15; 67:12) — the great-reward promised now, in the immediate wake of the indictment-file, to the exception-corps — the architecture of vv. 160–162 forming the chapter's standing pattern of balanced scales: prepared punishment for "the disbelievers among them" (161) — great reward for "the rooted among them, and the believers" (162): knowledge, in the Book's economy, judged by its *fruit-direction*: knowledge rooted toward acknowledgment of all revelation ends in reward; knowledge floated toward distortion (5:13, 5:41's standing tahrīf-file; 2:75's standing tamperers) ends in the prepared punishment. Al-Rāzī's standing gloss on the rootedness-name sums the verse's standing anthropology: the rāsikh is the one in whom knowledge has gone *down* — into depth where storms of novelty and contradiction cannot uproot — and the mark of his depth is his expansiveness: the deeper the root, the more of God's successive revelation-received — each new address finding soil rather than surface. The People-of-the-Book movement of the sūrah thereby delivers, before it closes, its final standing portrait: one community, two postures — indictment or reward — and the divider never the blood of the covenant but the bowing of the learned: *those firmly rooted in knowledge... believing* — a profile deliberately drawn so that any scholar of any scriptural tradition may measure himself against it — and the Book immediately lifts its own certificate into the proof of this self-same principle in the next verse: "verily We have revealed to thee as We revealed to Noah and the prophets after him" (v. 163) — the Prophet's own revelation enrolled in the same standing chain the rāsikhūn believe — the unity of prophecy as the unity of reward.

## Sūrah al-Nisāʾ 4:112

> **And whosoever commits an offense or a sin, and then casts it upon one who is innocent, bears the burden of calumny and a manifest sin.**

**Expanded Commentary**

**Wa Man Yaksib Khaṭīʾatan Aw Ithman: Khaṭīʾah and Ithm — The Two Wings of Wrong**

The block's criminal-law chapter reaches its climax-verse — the precise indictment of the planted-evidence crime: *wa man yaksib khaṭīʾatan aw ithman* — "whosoever earns an offense — *or* a sin": the two nouns the commentators never tire of distinguishing — the house the classical positions set out forever in the rolls: (1) *khaṭīʾah* the *unaimed* wrong, *ithm* the deliberate (al-Ṭabarī: the *error-class* versus the *intent-class* — so the verse sweeps both: frame-work fain slip and willful malfeasance alike); (2) *khaṭīʾah the smaller*, *ithm the greater* (al-Rāzī and al-Zamakhsharī: the canon of degrees); (3) and the favorite pairing of the schools — *khaṭīʾah the self-harming sin* (victimless trespass, its harm looping home only — v. 111's own class); *ithm the neighbor-harming sin* (harm done outward — injustice with a victim) — the fine distinction then deployed at once into the verse's larger architecture: the man plans the man being profiled the man's work the man planted the burglary Ṭuʿmah's theft being ithm of the first order (property taken from another's wall) — and the *cast-upon-innocent* phase being its second-order harm: the doubled wrong stacked upon the first.

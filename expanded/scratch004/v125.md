## Sūrah al-Nisāʾ 4:125

> **And who is better in religion than the one who submits his face to God, and is virtuous, and follows the creed of Abraham, as a ḥanīf? And God did take Abraham for a friend.**

**Expanded Commentary**

**Wa Man Aḥsanu Dīnan Mimman Aslama Wajhahu li-Allāh: The Summit-Question**

The sūrah's religio-comparative debate ends not with a boast but with a question that selects out single souls: *wa man aḥsanu dīnan mimman aslama wajhahu li-Allāh* — "and who is better in religion than he who submits his face to God?": *aḥsanu dīnan* — better in religion — the comparative rising to its to its standing to its standing excellence-measure) — *aslama wajhahu* — submitted his face — the commentators' standing unpacking: the face as the whole the face here standing metonymically for the entire self (since the face is the man's noblest, most-attended part — its standing Book-roll of wajh-submission: 2:112 — "nay — whosoever submits his face to God and is virtuous — his reward is with his Lord — no fear upon them, neither shall they grieve"; 3:20 — "I have submitted my face to God — and those who follow me"; 31:22's twin — the whole-person devoted, no part withheld) — with the standing chain-of-three completing the sentence: (1) *aslama wajhahu* — outward-total submission — the islām-limb; (2) *wa huwa muḥsin* — and is virtuous — muḥsin — the iḥsān-limb — the commentators' standing exchange noting v. 124's ṣāliḥāt-and-īmān (deeds + faith) and v. 125's adding the third — iḥsān — (the standing heir-line with Jibrīl-hadīth: islām-īmān-iḥsān triad — Muslim 8's standing the celebrated Gabriel-hadith registers all three under its question — "to worship God as though you see Him") — and (3) *wa-ttabaʿa millata Ibrāhīma ḥanīfan* — follows the creed of Abraham, ḥanīf — the lineage-limb: millah as creed-way — from meaning the standing sense given by the commentators: the established religious the established creed-charter — with ḥanīfan — from ḥ-n-f — turning-off-from-the Book's standing Abraham-word — 2:135 — "say: the creed of Abraham, ḥanīfan — and he was not of the polytheists"; 6:161 — "the creed of your father Abraham ḥanīfan — and he was not of the polytheists"; 30:30 — the pure anti-shirk the singular the divergence-from-every-deviation posture — the creed of upright turning-away — ḥ-n-f the ḥanīf as the man who tilts perpetually **away from every idol** — never merely neutral but slanted against all false worship) — with the Book's standing defense of the line against sect-claimants: 2:140 — "was Abraham a Jew — or a Christian?"; 3:65-67 — "Abraham was neither Jew nor Christian — but he was a ḥanīf musliman — a righteous-turning submitter — and he was not among the polytheists — indeed the nearest of people to Abraham are those who followed him — and this Prophet — and those who believe — and God is the Walī of the believers" — v. 125 thereby delivering the creed-line to the Medinian ummah as Abraham's unbroken continuation.

**Wa-ttakhadha Allāhu Ibrāhīma Khalīlā: The Intimate-Friend Heaven-Signed**

Then the honorific the whole Islamic the honorific all Islam thereafter bestows upon Abraham — *wa-ttakhadha Allāhu Ibrāhīma khalīlā* — "and God took Abraham for a khalīl — an intimate friend": *khalīl* — from kh-l-l — from kh-l-l — permeation, pore-level closeness (the commentators' standing the comment-lineage's standing treasure: khalīl not mere companion-level — but *permeating love* — with al-Ṭabarī's the classical definition the canons keep: the standing definition handed down by the commentators generally — love that has permeated — every chamber of his being — with the standing practical rules the commentators attach to the term (al-Ṭabarī's al-Ṭabarī's standing teaching: the khalīl loves whatever his friend loves, and hates whatever his friend hates — the Book's own standing demonstration: Abraham's own standing file: 9:114 — "Abraham's invocation for forgiveness for his father was only because of a promise he had made him — and when it became clear to him that he was an enemy to God — he disassociated himself from him — indeed Abraham was tender-hearted — awwāh — forbearing"; 60:4 — "there is an excellent example for you in Abraham and those with him — when they said to their people: we are clear of you and of what you worship apart from God — we renounce you — and enmity and hatred have appeared between us and you forever — until you believe in God alone" — the standing rule then for the khalīl: no friendship with the of his Friend) — with the outward ramifications engraved forever in Islam's own ritual architecture: the khalīl's own its standing signs: the maqām Ibrāhīm at the Kaʿbah (2:125) — ḥajj's Abrahamic Hijaz's stations of ḥajj all tied back to his biography (22:26-29; and 2:125-129's Makkah-building narrative) — and the standing honorific standing alone in the canon: *Khalīl Allāh* — the one called God's intimate friend — the verse thereby closing its superlative with a portrait rather than a prize: better religion is submission of face, purity of deed, Abrahamic creed — and the final the final fruit of its path is, in God's own house: friendship.

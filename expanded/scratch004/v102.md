## Sūrah al-Nisāʾ 4:102

> **When thou art with them and leadest them in prayer, let one group of them stand with thee in prayer, taking their arms with them. When they have performed their prostrations, let them withdraw to the rear, and let another group come that has not prayed and let them pray with thee, taking precaution and their arms. The disbelievers wish you to be heedless of your arms and your baggage that they might assault you all at once. There is no blame on you if you lay aside your arms when you are troubled by heavy rain or illness. But take your precaution. Surely God has prepared for the disbelievers a humiliating punishment.**

**Expanded Commentary**

**Wa Idhā Kunta Fīhim fa-Aqamta Lahumu al-Ṣalāh: Worship Under the Sword-Shadow**

The sūrah's war-rite descends now to its most riveting practical detail — a canonical prayer whose architecture is drawn for an active battlefield: *wa idhā kunta fīhim fa-aqamta lahumu al-ṣalāh* — "when thou art among them and dost establish the prayer for them": the address singular to the Prophet — imam under possible assault — and the standing asbāb the tradition preserves (al-Ṭabarī and al-Wāḥidī's rolls): the Prophet and his Companions facing the idolaters at ʿUsfān, the makkan corps under Khālid ibn al-Walīd — then still a champion of the opposition — when the enemy, spying the Muslims bowed in the midday prayer, plotted the rush — whereupon the verse descended between ẓuhr and ʿaṣr, and the battle-measure and the defensive prayer-order was installed in time for the afternoon prayer — the attack thereby misfired (the tradition's standing register of Khālid's own acknowledgement later, to the Prophet after his Islam: "we were prevented from it, by that which was revealed to you" — the war-rite itself counted in the victory-ledger).

**The Two-Shift Congregation: Prayer and Sentry as One Act**

Then the mechanics — Islam's most remarkable liturgical concession, the *ṣalāt al-khawf* proper: *fal-taqum ṭāʾifatun minhum maʿaka wa l-yaʾkhudhū asliḥatahum* — "let one band stand with thee, and let them take their arms": the congregation split into shifts — one company praying *armed*, the other holding the line (the formation schematics the fiqh-books preserve from the early generations: lines arranged so that prostration never leaves the whole host defenseless — at the sujūd one rank bows low with the imam while the rank behind stands guard, weapon in hand; when the first rank rises, the second prostrates alone — so that the imam completes his two rakʿahs and *each* rank has prostrated once with him and stood watch once against the enemy — with its standing alternative account, from Ṣāliḥ ibn Khawwāt's hadith in the Sunan (al-Tirmidhī Abū Dāwūd 1241; al-Nasāʾī 1532): one band prays a full rakʿah with the Prophet, departs; the second band comes, prays a rakʿah, departs; the Prophet completes the prayer — so that the leader has prayed the full measure the full prayer and each band has prayed its share — account upon account showing the flexibility of the concession: the unity of the ummah's worship kept *visible* even under fire, its posture distributed like a shield-line) — *wa l-yaʾkhudhū asliḥatahum* — *taking their arms with them* — the weapons clause never breaking the prayer's sanctity: the combatant-prostrater at once (the standing jurisprudence: arms may be borne through prayer in upon the fear-prayer — swords slung, bows carried, rifles or muskets across the centuries — the same law — worshippers holding steel between heaven and the front) — the commentators' philosophical annotation being the sūrah's own doctrine repeated at the liturgical level: *tawakkul does not cancel drawing-nigh-of-the-arm* (v. 71's ḥidhr; v. 101's mubīn-warning; here the armed sujūd — precaution invited into the very qiblah — the believer's security-work and his God-work fused into a single act — prayer not suspended by danger but *re-fitted* for it).

**Yawaddu Alladhīna Kafarū Law Taghfulūna ʿan Asliḥatikum: The Enemy's Heedlessness-Wish**

Then the verse's intelligence-brief — heaven naming the enemy's hope out loud: *yawaddu alladhīna kafarū law taghfulūna ʿan asliḥatikum wa amtiʿatikum fa-yamīlūna ʿalaykum maylatan wāḥidah* — "the unbelievers wish that you should be heedless of your arms and your baggage — that they might swoop upon you in one single attack": *yawaddu* — the wish-word of v. 89's levelers recurring — *taghfulūna* — from gh-f-l — heedlessness (its standing Sufi irony: the same ghaflah that the spirituals name the chamber-death of hearts is here the tactical death of camps — prayer is no opiate of vigilance; worship is designed not to lower the war-watch) — *amtiʿah* — baggage, travel-goods (the marching-train family of v. 94's ʿaraḍ-economy) — and *maylatan wāḥidah* — one swoop, one lapse one single mass-charge (m-y-l — the incline-word: the whole enemy weight tilting once, together, at the prayer-posture) — the commentators' standing note: heaven exposes the enemy's *strategic desire* so the believers may permanently invert it (the doctrine behind the standing hadith-couplet of war-ethics: preparation-together 8:60's "prepare against them what you can of force and tethered horses — by which you terrorize the enemy of God and your enemy" — the visible readiness itself deterring the swoop long before any clash).

**Fa-Laysa ʿAlaykum Junāḥun In Kāna Bikum Adhan min Maṭarin Aw Kuntum Marḍā: Mercy Even in the Armament-Rule**

Then the rukhsah inside the rukhsah — a concession within a concession: *fa-laysa ʿalaykum junāḥun an taḍaʿū asliḥatakum in kāna bikum adhan min maṭarin aw kuntum marḍā* — "no blame upon you that you lay aside your arms, if rain troubles you or you are sick": the armament-duty itself waived under weather-and-wound (the commentators' standing scene-setting: soaked fighters whose blades and straps became burden; ʿAbd al-Raḥmān ibn ʿAwf's wounded-band named in the rolls as one of the occasions) — *junāḥ* returning from v. 101 (the slant-blame cancelled twice in two verses) — with the immediately-following command twined to the concession: *wa khudhū ḥidhrakum* — "yet take your precaution" (v. 71's very words — the ḥidhr-imperative ringing now for the third time in the war-canon — mercy never disabling vigilance; the rukhsah is a *loading-off*, not a *sleeping-sold not a sleep-at-post*) — and the whole verse sealed at last with its range-compressing threat: *inna Allāha aʿadda li'l-kāfirīna ʿadhāban muhīnā* — "surely God has prepared for the unbelievers a humiliating punishment": *ʿadhāban muhīnā* — degrading chastisement — from h-w-n — demeaning, debasing — the eschatological counter-value counter-measure of all the enemy's field-humiliations their battle-hopes (they hoped to humiliate the worshipper-host mid-sujūd; heaven reverses the entire axis — the prepared-in-advance — aʿadda — degradation filed for their account instead — the Book's own reversal-form running once more: you hold your ranks armed at prayer; their ranks will break unarmed at the Judgment) — thereby sealing the sūrah's war-liturgy: the ummah that prays *with swords* against a manifest-enemy never loses its spiritual posture thereby — mihrab and rampart become one wall — and the sweet by-product the tradition draws from its phrasing ever after (the Sufi schools the devotional commentators, al-Qushayrī's line: salāt al-khawf as the standing icon of the whole spiritual path: *the servant must serve — yet with arms* — dhikr and watchfulness never separating — the soldier-worshipper of Madinah re-embodied in every believer whose soul's ʿUsfān is the inner siege of his own ghaflah).

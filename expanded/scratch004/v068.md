## Sūrah al-Nisāʾ 4:68

> **and We would surely have guided them unto a straight path.**

**Expanded Commentary**

**Wa la-Hadaynāhum Ṣirāṭan Mustaqīman: The Second Then-Payment**

The pericope's second heaven-signed apodosis follows immediately upon the reward-promise: *wa la-hadaynāhum ṣirāṭan mustaqīman* — "and We would surely have guided them upon a straight path": *wa la-* — and surely — the lām-of-certainty again (matching v. 67's *la-ātaynāhum* — the conditional ledger pairing its two promises in seamless grammar), and *hadaynāhum* — We guided them — the h-d-y verb-family that holds the Book's entire navigation-engine (guidance as God's own action over hearts: 2:213 — "God guides whom He wills"; 6:82's Abrahamic security-cohort — "those who believe and mix not their belief with wrongdoing — for them is security, and they are guided"; and the fixed principle at 47:17 — "those who follow guidance — He increases them in guidance") — yet calibrated here, as the clause's own logic discloses, as heaven's *response to compliance*: they walked, so they were shown — guidance increasing upon the obedient, the wage of steps already taken, exactly per the hadith-qudsī announcement that anchored v. 67's own commentary (al-Bukhārī 7405 — "if he draws near to Me a span I draw near to him a cubit... and if he comes to Me walking, I come to him running").

**Ṣirāṭan Mustaqīman: The Road-Term of All Roads**

Then the object-phrase the whole ummah carries daily on its tongue — *ṣirāṭan mustaqīman* — the straight path: *ṣirāṭ* — road, crossing-way (its bridge-image made famous at the Resurrection in the hadith-topography: a bridge thinner than a hair and sharper than a sword — cf. al-Bukhārī 6573 and Muslim 183 — yet in its *worldly* employment always the religion's traffic-way) — and *mustaqīm* — from q-w-m, to stand straight — the path without crooks or folds, its vocabulary-cousins all aligning: *istiqāmah*, the believer's own standing-straight — the Prophet's advice given to the man asking for a single counsel that would suffice his whole life:

> *"Say: My Lord is God, then stand straight (qultu rabbi Allāh thumma istaqim)."* (al-Tirmidhī 2410; Muslim 38)

— and the term's placement here is no accident, as al-Rāzī observes with characteristic brilliance: vv. 68–69 comprise *the exact answer of the prayer of al-Fātiḥah* — for 1:6–7 petitions *ihdinā al-ṣirāṭa al-mustaqīm* — "guide us upon the straight path — the path of those Thou hast blessed" — and the sūrah's two apodoses reply clause-for-clause: *guidance upon the road* (v. 68) and *company of those blessed upon it* (v. 69 — prophets, ṣiddīqūn, shuhadāʾ, ṣāliḥūn — the very *anʿamta ʿalayhim* cohort of the Fātiḥah's own syntax) — Sūrah 1's prayer being thereby reprinted inside Sūrah 4's conditional-promise: petition in chapter one; receive in chapter four — and between them stands the standing grammar of compliance.

**Better and Firmer — Then Guided and Companioned**

With the payment-doublet now complete (reward-from-His-Presence at v. 67; guidance-upon-the-road at v. 68), the reader perceives the mounting logic of the trinity: what obedience gains is not merely *banked* (reward) but *navigational* (continuing guidance) — treasury and chart both issued at signing — so that however much the waverer feared losing his way after complying (the nifāq-class's secret fear, whispered at every full-surrender: *if I submit wholly, what shore holds me?*), heaven answers that the road itself comes as part of the wage: *We will show you where to step, every furlong.* The sufi-readers' commentary (al-Qushayrī's line and its descendants) feasted upon the perpetual light of this sequence: submission once made never leaves a man a mapping-orphan — God undertakes the pilgrim's directions inside the bargain — and the Prophet's own daily Fātiḥah-liturgy (seventeen rakʿahs of obligatory prayer, minimum, each containing the petition) stands as the ever-renewing activation of the covenant: each *ihdinā* prayed humbly, each step taken thereafter, answered with guidance anew. And upon the trinity's third rail (v. 69) comes then the final and dearest payment — companionship — for which the road itself was only the preparation: obedience's last dignity being the *fellowship* it acquires, among those whom God has blessed.

**La-Hadawnāhum Ṣirāṭan Mustaqīman: The Guidance the Command Would Have Earned**

The counterfactual closes with the sūrah's standing straight-path promise ("and We would have guided them to a straight path") — the conditional register enclosing a standing law of the Book: guidance-in-motion is awarded upon obedience-in-fact; the ṣirāṭ is not a map mailed to speculators but a road unrolled beneath walking feet. The commentators (al-Ṭabarī's standing gloss; Ibn Kathīr's register) tie the clause back into the passage's spine: vv. 64–68 form one conditional chain — had they come, had they sought forgiveness, had they done the counseled thing — each *law* opened onto a gift: finding God relenting, finding the command good for them, finding strength, finding guidance — the four hads piling up the treasury that obedience would have purchased. Al-Rāzī's standing scholastic note here is pastoral: God speaks the rewards of a road not yet taken so the hearer may *want* to take it — the conditional of what-might-have-been functioning as standing invitation: the verse holds the door open even while measuring it, so that the reader who hears "We would have given them" hears simultaneously "and We shall give thee" — the counterfactual containing a live offer, as all of the Book's gravest conditionals do.

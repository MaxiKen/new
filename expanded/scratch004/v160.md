## Sūrah al-Nisāʾ 4:160

> **And for the wrongdoing of those who are Jews, We forbade them good things that had been made lawful unto them, and for their turning many from the way of God —**

**Expanded Commentary**

**Fa-Bi-Ẓulmin min Alladhīna Hādū: The Sentence Clause Fulfilled**

The long subordinate-clause that opened at v. 155 ("then, *for* their breaking of their covenant...") at last delivers its consequence — and the commentators mark the completion of the indictment-architecture: the *fa-bi-mā* of v. 155 and the *fa-bi-ẓulmin* here are one judicial motion: "for the breaking... for the disbelief... for the slaying... for the calumny... for the boast... and for the wrongdoing of those who are Jews" — the verdict arriving as: *ḥarramnā ʿalayhim ṭayyibātin uḥillat lahum* — "We forbade them good things that had been made lawful unto them": *ẓulm* — wrongdoing — the standing comprehensive within the indictment, gathering the prior counts under its canopy (its standing Book-definition: injustice as the putting of things out of their place — and the indictment-file having exhibited precisely a covenant displaced, signs displaced, prophets displaced, honor displaced); and the community-designation *alladhīna hādū* — "those who are Jews" — the standing gentilic of the register (its own usage across the Book: 2:62; 2:111; 2:135; 4:46; 5:18; 5:41; 5:44; 5:64; 5:69; 6:146) — locating the verdict historically in the specific covenant-community whose file has been under review.

**Ṭayyibāt: The Good Things Withdrawn — Food-Laws as Judicial History**

The penalty names its instrument — *Ṭayyibāt* — "good things": the commentators (al-Ṭabarī's standing collection) restrict its reference here by the word's standing usage: pleasant provisions, chiefly *foods* — the pure, the enjoyable (its standing register: 2:172 — "eat of the good things We have provided you"; 5:4-5; 8:69; 16:114; 20:81 — "eat of the good things We have provided you — and transgress not therein"; 23:51; and the manna-quail context of 2:57; 7:160) — the prohibition-verse at hand thereby matching its standing twin-file: 3:93 — "all food was lawful unto the Children of Israel, save what Israel had forbidden for himself before the Torah was sent down" — the baseline of comprehensive permission; and 6:146 — the standing inventory of the specific withdrawal: "and upon those who are Jews We forbade every clawed animal — and of oxen and sheep We forbade them their fat, save what their backs carry or the entrails or what is mixed with bone — thus did We requite them for their insolence — and verily We are truthful": the *jazāʾ*-clause of 6:146 — "thus did We requite them for their insolence (baghyahum)" — naming the same judicial logic our verse installs: the food-restrictions entered not as arbitrary ritual-burdens but as *requital* — a withdrawal of amenity from a rebellious treasury — the standing jurisprudential note developed by the commentators: the ṭayyibāt-forfeiture standing as the *worldly* installment of the counts of vv. 155–159, distinct from the Hereafter's prepared punishment arriving at v. 161's close: two registers of consequence — diet in history, fire beyond it.

**Wa Bi-Ṣaddihim ʿan Sabīli Allāhi Kathīran: The Barring of Many as a Separate Count**

The verse adds a final count to the file — with its own causal particle: *wa bi-ṣaddihim ʿan sabīli Allāhi kathīran* — "and for their turning many from the way of God": *ṣadd* — from ṣ-d-d — barring, diverting, obstructing (its standing register: 4:167 below — "those who disbelieve and bar from God's way"; 5:2 — "let not hatred of a people who barred you from the Sacred Mosque move you to transgress"; 7:45; 8:47; 11:19; 14:3; 22:25; 29:38; 43:37; 58:16) — the barring here reading either as (1) their averting *many others* from God's way — *kathīran* as object: multitudes turned back, the scholars' errant authority deflecting common people from successive revelations (the standing charge-pattern of 9:31; 9:34 — taking their rabbis and monks as lords; devouring wealth) — or (2) *kathīran* adverbially: barring *much/frequently* — obstruction as habitual policy. Both registers converge on the standing dynamic the commentators name: religious authorities who stand at the intersection of their community and God's new address, positioned to escort or to bar — their choice echoing through the multitudes who trust their gatekeeping — 5:63's standing rebuke of the neighbor-file ("why do the masters and rabbis not forbid them from their sinful speech and their consuming the evil is what they used to practice"). The verse thus closes the count-list where it began — *ẓulm* — and the standing structure of vv. 155–161 reads now in full judicial order: charges (breach, disbelief, slaying, the sheathed-heart boast, the calumny against Mary, the execution-boast, wrongdoing, usury-eating, barring of multitudes); historical requital (ṭayyibāt withdrawn); and the standing Hereafter-installment immediately ahead: "We have prepared for the disbelievers among them a painful punishment" (v. 161). The sūrah of the household of faith thereby records, in its last addressed file to its neighboring covenant-community, the total architecture of accountability: the same God who posts watchers over orphans (4:6), spouses (4:34-35), and witnesses (4:135) also audits the ledgers of religious establishments — and where their gatekeepers bar His road, the heavens narrow their tables before the fire narrows their refuge.

## Sūrah al-Nisāʾ 4:132

> **Unto God belongs whatsoever is in the heavens and whatsoever is on the earth, and God suffices as a Guardian.**

**Expanded Commentary**

**The Third Toll: Ownership Sounded Until the Hearing Settles**

The cadence tolls a third time: *wa li-llāhi mā fī al-samāwāti wa mā fī al-arḍ* — "unto God belongs whatsoever is in the heavens and whatsoever is on the earth" — and with this the sūrah completes its densest repetition-cluster: v. 126 (with *muḥīṭ* — Encompassing), v. 131 (with *ghanī ḥamīd* — Self-Sufficient, Praiseworthy), and now v. 132 — three tolls in seven verses, each sealed with a different Name, as though the Book were fastening the household-legislation of the sūrah with three separate nails. The commentators' standing structural note (al-Qurṭubī and al-Rāzī both pausing over the clustering) reads the sequence as graded comfort and warning: the first toll (v. 126) universal — the Frame around everything; the second (v. 131) covenantal — ownership as the ground of the taqwā-charge laid on every community; the third (v. 132) fiduciary — ownership as the ground of trusting God alone as Trustee. The soul, having heard the estate belongs to God three times, may finally do what the second half of this verse commands: stop trying to be its own guardian.

**Wa Kafā Bi-llāhi Wakīlan: The Sufficiency-Formula at Its Summit**

Then the Name that draws the practical deduction: *wa kafā bi-llāhi wakīlan* — "and God suffices as a Guardian-Trustee": the standing sufficiency-formula — *kafā bi-llāhi...* — the Book's own architecture for adequacy, a formula of which the commentators note there is none more beloved in the spiritual curriculum: the servant places some task — provision, protection, vindication, guidance — in divine custody, and the Book replies with this standing declaration: *suffices* — God alone fills the entire post; no adjunct-trustee is required, no co-signer needed (its standing register inside this very formula: 3:173 — "God suffices us — an excellent Guardian" — ḥasbunā Allāhu wa niʿma al-wakīl — the cry of the believers when told "the people have gathered against you — so fear them," and the sentence increasing them only in faith; 4:45 — "God knows best your enemies — and God suffices as an Ally — and God suffices as a Helper"; 4:81 — "trust in God — and God suffices as a Guardian"; 17:96 — "He suffices as Knowing of His servants' sins — Seeing"; 33:3 — "trust in God — and God suffices as a Guardian"; 33:48; 39:38; 65:3 — "whoever trusts in God — He is sufficient for him"; 73:9). *Wakīl* — from w-k-l — to entrust affairs to another: the one appointed to manage what the appointer cannot manage himself — the law-term lifted into theology: the jurists' wakālah-contract (power of attorney) simply mirrors at human scale the standing relation the word announces: the believer is the principal who delegates; God is the Trustee who never errs, never sleeps, never embezzles, never dies — the standing devotional verse sealing the matter at 39:62: "God is the Creator of all things — and He is Guardian over all things."

**Tawakkul: The Trustee-Doctrine in Its Full Register**

Around this Name the tradition built its entire science of reliance — *tawakkul* — the verbal noun of the same root (w-k-l): the deliberate entrusting of outcomes to God while the limbs work their assigned parts. The standing hadith-definitions cluster here as the commentators cite them beneath this verse: the Prophet's standing spectacle-definition in al-Tirmidhī's register (Tirmidhī 2344): "were you to trust in God with the trust due to Him, He would provide for you as He provides for the birds — they set out in the morning with empty bellies and return full" — the birds deploying their wings fully (work) while the emptiness and fullness belong to Another (trust); and its standing complement — the hadith of the camel (Tirmidhī 2517): "tie her and trust" — iʿqilhā wa tawakkal — the Prophet refusing the dichotomy of diligence versus reliance, legislating the both-and by which the law of custody operates: bind the camel, then entrust its staying. Ibn al-Qayyim's standing taxonomy (his celebrated chapter on tawakkul's degrees) reads the present verse as the doctrine's ground-floor: tawakkul is only rational where the Trustee is (1) owner of everything — hence the verse's first half; (2) sufficient for everything — hence its second; the two clauses together composing the intellectual warrant of reliance: *because all is His, and because He suffices as Guardian — entrust*. And the practical register of the sūrah's context keeps the doctrine from floating: the preceding family-file involved precisely the anxieties where tawakkul is strained — divorce-settlements, orphan-estates, dowries, the economics of the suspended wife — the Book answering with the standing fiduciary announcement: the estate is God's; the Guardian post is God's; the tenants may therefore act with justice inside their littleness without terror of the ledger, since the Keeper of the estate is sufficient for every aftermath (65:3's standing promise coupling tawakkul with the divorce-legislation itself: "whoever trusts in God — He is sufficient for him — God shall bring His command to pass; God has set a measure for every thing"). The verse, in six Arabic words, thus converts three verses of celestial title-deed into the emotionally livable conclusion: since the heavens and the earth are the possession — and the possessor suffices as the guardian — no orphan, no separating spouse, no guardian-worn believer of the sūrah's audience is ever actually *unguarded*: they are only ever between appointments of their one sufficient Trustee.

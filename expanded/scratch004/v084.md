## Sūrah al-Nisāʾ 4:84

> **So fight in the way of God. Thou art accountable only for thyself, and urge on the believers. It may be that God will restrain the might of the disbelievers, for God is stronger in might and more severe in punishment.**

**Expanded Commentary**

**Fa-Qātil fī Sabīli Allāh: The Command Single-Minded**

After the rumor-diagnostics of v. 83, the command comes down to the Prophet directly — stripped to its essence: *fa-qātil fī sabīli Allāh* — "so fight thou in the way of God" — the command addressed in the singular-second-person (the Book's grammar-shifted its honor-grammar: the *qātil* singular riding singular addressed context: where the ummah was addressed collectively at v. 71, here the Prophet alone — meaning: the war-responsibility falls on him whether or not the timid mass follows — the classical use of this verse being among the most famous in all Islam's biographical inheritance: the asbāb-chronicles of the *Lesser Badr* mission (al-Tabarī coll collations, al-Wāḥidī and al-Rāzī's registers) delivering the verse's field-paraphrase its very spark: after Uḥud's wound, Abū Sufyān proposed a return engagement for the following year at Badr; when the date neared, disinformation was pumped into Madinah about the *immensity of the Makkan muster* (v. 83's rumor-wave) — whereupon most of the Madinese shrank back — and the Prophet declared the covenant his answer preserved as one of the glories of the Sīrah:

> *"By Him in Whose hand is my soul — I shall go out, even if alone!"* (recorded across the tafsīr asbāb-register on this verse, by al-Ṭabarī's and al-Wāḥidī's chains)

— and he set out with only seventy riders — and found the Makkans had themselves had themselves shrunk from the rendezvous — a a demonstration of the very promise the rest of the verse makes: morale-theater victories belong to the soldier who obeys irrespective of the crowd.

**Lā Tukallafu illā Nafsak: The Soul-Accountonomy**

The second clause releases the Prophet from hostage-taking from the militia's consent: *lā tukallafu illā nafsak* — "thou art accountable only for thyself": *tukallafu* — burden-accountability — from k-l-f, the weighing of duty — news both sobering and freeing: your war-duty does not wait upon quorum (the commentators tracing this freedom-clause to its Sīrah-sisters: Moses's own declaration of the "I have no power save for myself and my brother" — 5:25; and the Book’s parallel license to the Prophet at 24:54 — "if you obey him, you shall be rightly guided; and upon the Messenger is only the clear delivery") — the sentence thereby erecting the standing liberating-theology at the heart of Islamic command doctrine: *the leader's accountability is personal, never contingent on the crowd's* — truth carried by one man does not die of loneliness (its counter-views expressed forever at the prophets' own moments: Noah's Noah's ceaseless night-a day-and-night call with its handful; Lot's solitary table household-band; Moses at the well's at the sea's brink — *"Nay! My Lord is with me — He will guide me"* — 26:62) — and the Prophet's seventy-maker seventy-man ride sealing it into the Sīrah: go, if none goes with you (with the immediately-following clause then restoring then adding the second arm — *wa ḥarriḍi al-muʾminīn* — yet *urge on the believers* — loneliness-of-duty being no license for exasperation: the Prophet both walks alone when he must *and* never ceases wakening the rest — ḥarriḍ — urge, stir, inflame — from ḥ-r-ḍ's from the root of incitement itself — the verb of mobilization, matched by heaven's own promise that the exhortation works through time: vant victory sits behind *urging*, not behind headcounts).

**ʿAsā Allāhu an Yakuffa Baʾsa Alladhīna Kafarū: Might Restrained from Above**

Then the promise — the sentence the Sīrah of the Lesser Badr worked out before the commentators' eyes: *ʿasā Allāhu an yakuffa baʾsa alladhīna kafarū* — "it may be that God will restrain the might of the unbelievers": *ʿasā* — perhaps, may-be — the commentators unanimous that with God *ʿasā* carries *certainty* (the Book's honorific economy — "perhaps" in God's mouth = *promise-tailored promise in royal understatement*: cf. 17:79's "it may be that your Lord will raise you to a praised station" — known-certain in advance) — *yakuffa*, restrain, hold back (from k-f-f itself: the palm-withdrawal word — used by God through the Book wherever a war’s storm-wave is recalled — 4:77's own *kuffū aydiyakum* of the Meccan-quiet era) — *baʾsa* — might, striking-power, war-fury — and the fulfillment-record the historians write beside it: Abū Sufyān's own host, pumped-up and advertised in Madinah as gigantic, itself withdrew from the appointed rendezvous under drought-pressure and the terror of the Prophet's advance: restraint accomplished by *non-collision* — God not requiring not always restraining might by arms but sometimes by *withdrawal* itself — baʾs evaporating before battle could start (with the hadith's own summary of the Lesser Badr outcome preserved in the campaign-roll simply: the doubled blood-value the outcome's standing signification booked in the tafsīr-lines as the proof of ʿasā-victory) — and the seal locks the axiom locks the axiom in place: *wa Allāhu ashaddu baʾsan wa ashaddu tankīlā* — "God is stronger in might and more severe in retribution": *baʾs* matched to the enemy's baʾs — with *tankīl* — from n-k-l — exemplary deterrent punishment — heaven's two attributes of force set against disbelieving might forever — the sūrah's war-block hence commissioned at last in its own grammar: fight as if you were alone; urge as if you were followed; know that the might above yours and theirs both is God's — and that He may restrain their storm without a single stroke struck, when the commander stands fast.

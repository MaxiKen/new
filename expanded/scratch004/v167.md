## Sūrah al-Nisāʾ 4:167

> **Verily those who disbelieve and bar from the way of God have gone far astray.**

**Expanded Commentary**

**Inna Alladhīna Kafarū wa Ṣaddū ʿan Sabīli Allāh: The Double Charge of the Gatekeepers**

The verdict-resume of the chapter now opens with the standing double charge: *inna alladhīna kafarū wa ṣaddū ʿan sabīli Allāh* — "verily those who disbelieve and bar from the way of God": two verbs in deliberate composition — *kafarū* (they disbelieved: the inward state) and *ṣaddū* (they barred: the outward aggressive act upon others) — the commentators (al-Ṭabarī's standing gloss) marking the full landscape of *ṣadd*: barring ranging across (1) intellectual obstruction — objections manufactured, doubts seeded, the rising generation tutored against the message; (2) social obstruction — the pressure of clan, commerce, and respectability arrayed to make confession costly; (3) physical obstruction — roads watched, sanctuaries policed (8:34-35's standing Meccan file: "what ails them that God should not punish them, while they bar from the Sacred Mosque — though they are not its guardians; its guardians are but the God-fearing — but most of them know not — and their prayer at the House is naught but whistling and clapping"; "they sell the signs of God for a small price and bar from His way"; 5:2's standing counter-command; 22:25 — the barring from the Sacred Mosque declared for the standing pagan file); and (4) self-barring — some readings registering the ṣadd as intransitive-turning: their own feet held back from the road — the standing basic register at 7:45 ("who bar from God's way and seek to make it crooked, and in the Hereafter they are disbelievers"); 11:19; 14:3 — "those who prefer the life of this world to the Hereafter, and bar from God's way, seeking to make it crooked — those are in far astray"; 43:37 — "verily they bar them from the way — while they suppose they are guided"; 47:1 — "those who disbelieve and bar from God's way — He shall render their works vain". The sūrah's own standing indictment of v. 160 ("and for their turning many from the way of God") echoes here generalized: the particular charges (broken covenant, calumny, ribā) now gathered into the master-count — disbelief *plus obstruction*: the private refusal institutionalized as public engineering against the Road.

**Qad Ḍallū Ḍalālan Baʿīdan: The Far Error Seventh Sounded**

The verdict belongs to the sūrah's own standing register — *qad ḍallū ḍalālan baʿīdan* — "have gone far astray": the seventh tolling of the ḍalāl-baʿīd phrase within this one sūrah (its stations: 4:60 — Satan's desire "to lead them far astray"; 4:116 — "whoever associates partners with God has gone far astray"; 4:136 — "whosoever disbelieves in God and His angels and His books and His messengers and the Last Day has gone far astray"; plus its standing pavers at 14:18; 22:12; 34:8; 42:18 — the phrase the Book reserves for total removal from the Road) — and the commentators mark the chapter's own architectural use of it: each tolling names another point of the departure-file — following the arbiter-other-than-God (60), the association-partnership (116), the article-breaking creed (136), and here the obstruction-enterprise (167) — all compass-points of one radius: distance from the Sabīl. *Baʿīd* itself — far — the word of exile-in-extent: error measured not in degrees but in *distance-strata* — and the standing Book-epistemology of roads completes the image: there is *a* way (1:6 — "guide us upon the straight path"; 4:68's standing claimed-performance file — "We would have guided them to a straight path"; 6:153 — "this is My path, straight — so follow it; and follow not the byways") — against which all departures register as *far*: no middle-distance between Road and wild.

**The Standing Pairing With v. 137: From Oscillation to Institution**

The commentators observe the verse's structural rhyming within the chapter: v. 137's waverers (believe-disbelieve-believe-disbelieve) were told "God was not one to forgive them nor to guide them unto a way" — the *individual* file of vacillation; vv. 167–169 now register the *institutional* file: disbelief hardened into the barring-profession — and the coming two verses will deliver its doubled penalty in the same verbal architecture the same verbal architecture (168: "God is not one to forgive them nor to guide them to a path —" 169: "save the path of Hell, abiding therein forever") — the standing comparison the commentators draw: the waverer loses guidance; the bar-keeper loses everything — forgiveness revoked, all guidance revoked save one, the way denied except *the path of Hell* — a sentence structure whose exception-clause is itself the shocking inversion the Book reserves for its darkest verdicts: the only road still open to the obstructor being the road he never wished to travel — *illā ṭarīqa jahannam* (169) — the sūrah of ways (its opening taqwā-way of 4:1; its justice-way of 4:135; its repentance-way of 4:146; its belief-way of 4:152) here mapping the one wayfare that is the sum of all refusal: far astray now, and one-way later. And the standing mercy-file is duly registered by the commentators (al-Rāzī and al-Zamakhsharī's standing note at these non-forgiveness clauses extending through the chapter): every "God will not forgive" of the Book stands upon *persistence* — the door of repentance rehanging every such verse (4:146's own standing exception-architecture; 39:53's standing universal-promise) — so that even the barring-profession, while its perpetrator breathes, retains its standing exit: non-forgiveness as prognosis upon a state, never as a sealed personal destiny — the far error remaining far only for those who keep walking.

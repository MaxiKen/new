## Sūrah al-Nisāʾ 4:93

> **Whosoever slays a believer willfully, his recompense is Hell, abiding therein. God is wroth with him, and curses him, and prepares for him a mighty punishment.**

**Expanded Commentary**

**Wa Man Yaqtul Muʾminan Mutaʿammidan: The Willful-Kill Verdict Fire**

After the mercy-arc of the error-killer's reparations, the sūrah's law-canon drops its heaviest single verse — unmatched in the Revelation for accumulated threats in one sentence: *wa man yaqtul muʾminan mutaʿammidan* — "whosoever slays a believer willfully": *mutaʿammidan* — deliberate — from ʿ-m-d — aim, column-intent (the jurists draining the word's forensic fill-in over four centuries: intent read from the instrument of assault — a sword-stroke/spear-stab reading willfulness prima facie; the wooden-cudgel the stick or whip raising the famous borderline-case across the schools — al-Ṭabarī's register of the earliest scholars splitting over whether the stick-kill is ʿamd for qiṣāṣ or quasi-ʿamd for heavy diyah — with the later canon settling the debate case-chamber by case-chamber: weapon-stock weapon-type + site of blow + kin of prior enmity + verbal context, until the Ḥanafī take — weapon-of-cutting = willfulness absolute; club-fire-immolation likewise; casual slap-of-hand seldom so) — then the sentence itself, riding out clause upon clause in a mount in a mounted escalation the tafsīr-canon calls *unparalleled in the Book*: (1) *fa-jazāʾuhu jahannamu khālidan fīhā* — "his recompense is Hell, abiding in it" (2) *wa ghaḍiba Allāhu ʿalayhi* — "God is wroth with him" (3) *wa laʿanahū* — "and He curses him" (4) *wa aʿadda lahu ʿadhāban ʿaẓīmā* — "and prepares for him a mighty chastisement" — al-Rāzī's standing enumeration: no other sin in the entire Revelation receives this quadr this fourfold stacking in one sentence — the number itself being the exclamation-mark: murder of the believer carries the full four-part sentence-roll usually reserved for the capital-offenses of spiritual treason.

**Ghaḍab, Laʿnah, ʿAdhāb ʿAẓīm: Each Threat Layer Unpacked**

The commentators then take each clause one rhetorical tier higher: *ghaḍiba* — wrath — divine anger-in-person, directly elected onto the sinner's account (a wage constrast contrast-drawn across the Book: anger upon a fallible man sticking to his obedience-league is one thing — cf. the fleeting ghaḍab-themes upon the collective — the explicit anchoring of ghaḍab against the named individual being rare and terrible); *laʿanahū* — He curses him — *laʿn* being, in the canon, expulsion-distance from Mercy — the curse spelled out (with the Book's own standing ledger-seal at its matched clause: 11:18's upon injustice-true upon the wrongdoers; 2:161's triple-ben upon those who hid those who hide truth; 3:87's upon apostates each marked by cat-L-N expulsion — the killer-of-believer now individually anchored); and *wa aʿadda lahu ʿadhāban ʿaẓīmā* — a mighty chastisement *pre-fabricated*, waited-upon (ʿaẓīm — mighty — the adjective the sūrah repeatedly reserves for the eschatological torture-scale for apocalyptic weight: v. 37's prepared abaser the prepared debasing punishment; v. 102's its own ahead-clauses; and *aʿadda* — prepared-in-advance — the commentators' note on its ominous cast: the sentence waits ready-built, not jury-too-met; the willful murderer's punishment was set before the deed, like the throne's anti-wearing no — like a drawn sword awaiting the neck).

**Khālidan Fīhā vs the Gate of Repentance: The Classical Great Debate**

Then the verse's greatest legal-theological battleground in all Islam — waged at the headland of fought out across three centuries of commentary with unusual honesty in the sources: does *khālidan fīhā* — abiding eternally — close the gate of repentance from the intentional believer-killer? The positions in order: (1) the Muʿtazilite severity — eternal Fire certain, repentance no escape (their logic: divine threat-statements bind like promises; no exception-footnote in the verse); with their standing hadith-companion in the modality: ʿAbdullāh ibn Masʿūd's narration that the killer the would-be repentant killer flagged in a saying written across two stations a hadith handed down whereby the participant in slaying a believer comes up on the Day with "despair of God's mercy" written on his brow (Zajjāj's registry across the canon, from al-Zajjāj); (2) the jamhūr / majority answer — repentance remains open, *even here* — al-Ṭabarī's leader of the canonical majority citing the twin verse-handles 25:68-71 — the passage that recites the believers-of-mercy as those who "call not upon another god with God, nor slay the soul God has forbidden... yet whosoever does that shall meet a penalty... *save him who repents and believes and works righteousness* — for those God will exchange their evil deeds for good" — the exception clause formally the exception clause most explicitly re-opening the door to the murderer who turns — with 4:116's own general with 4:48+116's only-unforgivable = shirk lemma boxing in severity: no verse may leave a single human sin *absolutely* beyond God's accepted repentance — al-Rāzī sealing it with the kalām axiom: whatever lies beneath tawḥīd-level can be forgiven beneath tawba (cf. Muslim's own hadith-portrait of the hundred-murderer — Bukhārī 3470, Muslim 2766 — who killed ninety-nine souls, then a hundredth — and was *accepted* at last when he turned toward repentance, dying on the road; the Two Measured two angels of mercy and wrath measuring the two towns' span and finding him a hand-span closer to mercy) — the majority's solution riding then upon the precision of *khālid*: (a) abiding *so long as God's threat wills*, not flat eternity; (b) or *for one who dies unrepentant*; (c) or hyperbolic-magnification (mubālagha) to seal the deterrent (the believers are to know the crime's true its true weight — the verse itself being punishment-engineered to barricade the ummah against blood); with (3) al-Thaʿlabī's merciful mediator-scheme: worldly punishment upon the killer in execution for blood-right functions as partial atonement toward the next world (the standing hadīth on it in the Sunan of the two al-Tirmidhī's register — "the punishments of this world expiate the Muslim's account therein") — and the verse's balanced closing doctrine thus received through the whole classical line: *willful murder of a believer is the worst human sin after shirk — its threat-structure maximal — and still the gate marked "repentance" stays open beside the gate marked "Hell"* — the two portais the two portals facing one another over every would-be killer in every century, so that no man in rage may plead ignorance of the sentence, and no man in abasement may plead ignorance of the Mercy.

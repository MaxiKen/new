## Sūrah al-Nisāʾ 4:107

> **And plead not on behalf of those who betray their own selves. Surely God loves not the one given to treachery and sin.**

**Expanded Commentary**

**Wa Lā Tujādil ʿan Alladhīna Yakhtānūna Anfusahum: No Advocacy for the Self-Betrayers**

The judicial-block turns from the Prophet's own washing (v. 106) to the deeper diagnosis of the accused: *wa lā tujādil ʿan alladhīna yakhtānūna anfusahum* — "and plead not on behalf of those who betray their own selves": *tujādil* — dispute-plead — from j-d-l — the standing contention-verb (the verse's own word-pairing now echoing v. 105's *khaṣīm* — advocate-adversary — with jidāl as the dispute's process-form: do not *carry their litigation* — do not *wield law-speech on their side*) — and the phrase that becomes the block's theological axis: *yakhtānūna anfusahum* — they betray *their own selves*: the standing pause-word of the tradition: theft framed as *self-theft*; the burglar's actual booty, measured in the last account, being nothing but a raiding of his own soul's credit — the classical commentary sharpening the Quranic ontology of sin in general (the Book's recurrent formula — *mā ẓalamnāhum wa lākin kānū anfusahum yaẓlimūn* — "We wronged them not, but they wronged themselves" — 2:57; 3:117; 9:70; 16:118; 29:40 — every wrong ultimately returning to its carrier like a missile reversed at its owner's chest) — the legal-block thereby uncovering the full picture of its lesson: the verdict of the verdict of courts, addressed outward, rests upon the ontology of inward accounts — every accepted plea on behalf of a self-betrayer is a second betrayal of his soul; and the Prophet's court, had it cleared Ṭuʿmah, would have compounded the man's inward ruin upon his outward one.

**Inna Allāha Lā Yuḥibbu man Kāna Khawwānan Athīman: The Trenchant-Name**

Then heaven's own verdict-profile: *inna Allāha lā yuḥibbu man kāna khawwānan athīman* — "surely God loves not the one habituated who is a treacherous sinner": *khawwān* — the intensive habit-form of kh-w-n — treachery raised to professional grade (its Book-roll being short and stern: 8:58's "if thou fearest treachery from a people, throw back their covenant to them equally — surely God loves not the treacherous"; 22:38's—"surely God defends those who believe — surely God loves not any treacherous ingrate"; and 4:107 here — the intensive *khawwān* meaning: the man who has *settled into* perfidy — whose breach has become his standing gait rather than his stumble); and *athīm* — steeped-in-sin (the ithm-word intensive — the standing paired-form the Book reserves for the steeped-in-guilt: "whoever earns a sin, earns it only against himself" — verse 111's own sequel clause below) — *lā yuḥibbu* — loves not — the stark non-love clause the commentators ring ever since with the Book's own register: God's *not-loving* of a soul being a verdict far more precise than mere wrath — wrath can pass; being unfavored being excluded from His love means being left without the inner sweetness that saves (the Sunnah confirming alongside — cf. 9:4–7; 2:195; 3:134 — God loving the God-fearing, the patient, the just, the benevolent — and the opposite — God's non-love going deliberately to the *character-classes*: the treacherous, the extravagant, the arrogant, the corrupt) — the seals behind v. 107 thus sealing the entire court-block's essence: it is not merely that judges may not *favor* the treacherous — the one who *pleads* treachery's case stakes himself against a Divine attribute-line against God's own declared non-love of the class — with the tradition's transpositions standing ever since: (1) the qāḍī's mercies may never cross into pleading the accused's *cause*; (2) the advocate catches the advocate must not manufacture *speech* on behalf of betrayers; (3) even sympathetic onlookers fall under the clause — the comment-doctors spreading the command *to all who hear of betrayal* — every man forbidding himself from becoming a mere *onlooker-lawyer* of the khawwān (the standing hadith-consequence in Bukhārī's rolls: *"whoever aids an oppressor in his injustice — God grants that oppressor mastery over him"* — Bukhārī 6953 — the standing rule: aid given to a treacherous cause *joins the cause*); and the canonical court-book thereafter knits the entire block into one standing charter: judges wash in istighfār (v. 106), plead no self-betrayers' case (v. 107), fear the muḥīṭ encompassing (v. 108), bar their clan-pleaders likewise (v. 109), and leave one door — the door of repentance — standing open at last for the self-betrayer himself (v. 110).

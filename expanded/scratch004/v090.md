## Sūrah al-Nisāʾ 4:90

> **Save those who seek refuge with a people with whom you have a covenant, or those who come to you with hearts reluctant to fight you, or to fight their own people. Had God willed, He could have given them authority over you, and then surely they would have fought you. So if they withdraw from you, and do not fight you, and offer peace, God allows you no way against them.**

**Expanded Commentary**

**Illā Alladhīna Yaṣilūna ilā Qawmin Baynakum wa Baynahum Mīthāq: The Treaty-Harbor Exception**

The sword-clause of v. 89 collapses, by God's own hand, at its very rim: *illā alladhīna yaṣilūna ilā qawmin baynakum wa baynahum mīthāq* — "save those who reach a people between whom and you there is a covenant": *yaṣilūna* — from w-ṣ-l — to join, connect, arrive-at (the joining-word chosen deliberately over mere flight — the man *re-anchors himself*: an accession to the covered body); and *mīthāq* — the weighty covenant — from w-th-q — firm-binding trust — the Book's heaviest covenant-noun (its seal-pattern: mīthāq everywhere marks bonds God Himself witnesses — 4:21's marriage-mīthāq — 33:7's prophets' mīthāq — and now the nation-of-madīnah's treaty network) — and the asbāb-knowers telling the Sīrah-application which taught the ummah for all centuries (al-Ṭabarī; al-Wāḥidī): certain retractors of Madinah fled to confederate tribes under treaty with the Prophet (the Khuzāʿah-figures and their like in the standing accounts) — and thereby *re-crossed into immunity by treaty*, not by Islam — the covenant's umbrella sheltering even those beneath it who were themselves outside the ummah: the signature of the emerging polity's covenant order now covering third parties whose contractual friendship extends its own shadow (with the commentators' standing legal point from it: the mīthāq-people acquire war-cover as being *of your covenant* — reaching them = acceding to covenant-security, whoever the refugee is) — the Book thereby building into its war-law the whole asylum-door forever: the man who escapes the believers' sanction by attaching himself to your *friends* becomes untouchable for the friends' sake — not because his own past is pardoned but because *his sanctuary is God's ratified architecture*.

**Aw Jāʾūkum Ḥaṣirat Ṣudūruhum an Yuqātilūkum: The Constricted-Chest Men**

The second safe-conduct is stranger still — God's own profiling of a soul: *aw jāʾūkum ḥaṣirat ṣudūruhum an yuqātilūkum aw yuqātilū qawmahum* — "or those who come to you, their chests constricted against fighting you — or fighting their own people": *ḥaṣirat* — from ḥ-ṣ-r — compressed, narrowed, hemmed-in (the Quranic root of *iḥṣār* — besiegement — and of ḥaṣīr 17:29's "make not thy hand tied to thy neck"; here the inward siege of a conscience) — *ṣudūr* — chests — the Book's seat-of-heart vessel (the soul's chamber where conviction and hesitation wrestle — 3:154's "their own souls whispering: \'we have no share in the affair\'"; 6:125's breast opened-to-Islam vs. narrowed-to-unbelief) — the verse thereby profiling Madinah's strangest war-casualty: a man from that scattered band who comes back *too bridled for war on either side* — too much at war with himself — whose chest speaks: *I will not fight you, and I cannot bear to fight my own kinsmen* either — the middle-man of tribal war, no longer Muslim enough to battle for Madinah nor idolater enough to battle against it — and heaven itself licenses him, in his very paralysis, a protected status: *no war applied against the man whose constricted breast refuses either way* (Ibn Kathīr's summary being the standing interpretation: *"kāfūn" — abstainers — neither with you nor against you; and some of that band indeed belonged to this kind*) — and the entire juristic tradition of Islam's laws-of-war drawing from this clause its standing neutrality-doctrine: the unbelligerent non-citizen, the man in the neutral zone, is *immune in himself*, treaty or no treaty — his own torn breast being its own protected charter.

**Fa-in Iʿtazalūkum fa-Lam Yuqātilūkum wa Alqaw Ilaykumu al-Salam fa-Mā Jaʿala Allāhu Lakum ʿAlayhim Sabīlā: No Way Against the Peace-Inclined**

Then the ending formula — one of the war-law canon's capstone-sentences: *fa-in iʿtazalūkum fa-lam yuqātilūkum wa alqaw ilaykumu al-salam fa-mā jaʿala Allāhu lakum ʿalayhim sabīlā* — "so if they withdraw from you, and fight you not, and cast peace toward you — God assigns you no way against them": three conditions, one after another — *iʿtazala* — withdrawal (they disengage — the step-out-of-war itself); *lam yuqātilūkum* — actual cessation-of-hands (the un-fired side of the line); *wa alqaw ilaykumu al-salm* — they cast *al-salm* — peace, surrender-truce — the peace-token thrown (from s-l-m, the same root-ground as islām and salām both — the Book's word-choice deliberately threading the truce of warring parties into the peace-vocabulary of heaven — war-ending itself as a kind of islām-gesture) — and the verdict: *fa-mā jaʿala Allāhu lakum ʿalayhim sabīlā* — *God appointed for you no way against them*: *sabīl* again — the way-lexicon of the sūrah closing twice in two verses (v. 88's *no way found for the astray*; v. 90's *no way opened against the peace-offerer*) — the possibilities of warfare's last words being simply a legal blockade from above (with its sister-charge the Book's other half-capitol at 8:61 — "and if they incline to peace, incline thou to it, and trust in God" — matching this verse into the standing peace-doctrine) — and the commentators' comprehensive war-canon seals here the sūrah's block: agitators *can be judged*; active enemies *can be fought*; but every gate of exit must stand open throughout — treaty-cover, constricted chests, peace-casting — so that no believer's sword ever reaches the no-fight zones heaven drew around its own people — with al-al-Qurṭubī's summary, never bettered, lodging the whole: *the battle-line is God's, the peace-doors are wider than men's: when men withdraw, withdraw; when they cast peace, gather it — the way-appointer Himself barred every other way.*

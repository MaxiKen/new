## Sūrah al-Nisāʾ 4:17

> **God only accepts the repentance of those who do evil in ignorance and then turn quickly in repentance: these are the ones unto whom God relents, and God is Knowing, Wise.**

**Expanded Commentary**

**Tawbatan min ʿIndi Allāh: Repentance as Divine Promise**

The verse opens with a possessive construction that recasts the whole enterprise: *innamā al-tawbatu ʿalā Allāhi li-lladhīna yaʿmalūna al-sūʾa bi-jahālatin* — "repentance is upon God — an office He owes Himself — for those who work evil in ignorance": rendering literally, repentance is *binding* on God toward a described class — the phrase the commentators incline to unpack as His self-written pledge: God has *kept repentance upon Himself* for such people, as He has written mercy upon Himself (6:12, 54). Al-Qurṭubī gathers the theological care this clause required: no creature can *obligate* God — ʿqawm al-wujūb* (the Muʿtazili doctrine of rational duty upon God) al-Qurṭubī answers with the Sunnah's own dictum: the obligation here is *promise*, laid on God by God — *"He is too truthful for His promise to fail"* — so that repentance-forgiveness is as certain as a covenant, yet remains *grace* in kind, because no hand but His bound Him. The door-post of v. 17 therefore stands inscribed with two truths: the acceptor of tawbah is the Lord who *swore it open*; its opening is to a described people — and the description follows.

**Bi-Jahālatin: Ignorance Widened to the Whole Colony of Sin**

*Those who do evil in ignorance* — and the clause worried the early centuries, for sinners frequently know exactly what they do. The tradition's answer, recorded in its full gallery from ʿAbd Allāh ibn ʿAbbās through ʿIkrimah and Qatādah and received by al-Ṭabarī, al-Rāzī, and al-Qurṭubī as the majority's resolution: *jahālah* here is the Book's own widened ignorance — the ignorance-in-act of every disobeyer, who at his sin's moment stands fully ignorant of God: of the punishment the thing entails, of the Witness then present, of the wages of the one he wrongs — so that "whoever disobeys God is jāhil, knowingly or not" runs Qatādah's maxim. The Book's own precedent is two-sided and decisive: Joseph cries *"unless You turn their guile from me, I shall incline toward them and be among the ignorant"* (12:33) — the solicited man, well-aware, pleading against entry into *jahālah*; and Zulaykhā's own excuse — *"I was of the ignorant ones"* (12:89's context of her confession) — while yet everything about her intrigue was intended. Al-Rāzī adds the argument's neat inversion: sins can *only* be repented from if they were *in some measure* voluntary; the unwitting error needs no tawbah at all — ergo the ignorance the verse owns must span intention itself. The clause is thus a promise wide as the whole fallen colony: any son of Adam who wronged, even while seeing, was in the Book's deep sense *not-seeing* — and for every such, God's pledge of acceptance stands.

**Thumma Yatūbūna min Qarīb: Quickness Before the Veil**

*Then they repent quickly* — *min qarīb*: "from near" — and the commentators divide its nearness across three refiners: repent while in *health and capability*, before the sick-bed writes evidence of delay; repent before the *death-foretoken* locks certainty; repent simply *before death* — and the law-books keep all three in view, since each sternness levels consoles a different procrastinator. The tradition marshalled its two appointment-verses for the outer walls of the delay-permitted:

> *"God accepts the repentance of His servant so long as the gurgling (al-ghargharah) has not come"* — the spirit's rattle at the throat (al-Tirmidhī 3537, grades ḥasan; Ibn Mājah 4253, from ʿAbd Allāh ibn ʿUmar)

and the cosmic shutter's hour:

> *"The door of repentance stands open until the sun rises from its west."* (Muslim 2703 — also Abū Dharr's long form at Muslim 2758–59 bis.)

Between ghargharah and the western sunrise, *min qarīb* stretches — but only across the daylight of a life that can still act, for tawbah, the doctors insist, includes amendment and amends, neither of which a throat-gurgle performs. And the seal *wa Allāhu kāna ʿalīman ḥakīman* — Knowing, Wise — shows God, at the door's very lintel, policing the thing the clause could be misread into: He *knows* the ignorance from the engineered "ignorance"; is *wise* in delimiting the promise — lest the pledge of forgiveness, made unconditional, become the charter of the deliberate.

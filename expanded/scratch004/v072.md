## Sūrah al-Nisāʾ 4:72

> **Among you indeed is one who would tarry; then, if misfortune should befall you, he would say, "God has blessed me, in that I did not accompany them."**

**Expanded Commentary**

**Wa Inna Minkum la-Man la-Yubaṭṭiʾanna: The Lagger Inside the Ranks**

The battle-mobilization arch now trains its lamp upon the reader's closest danger — the straggler *inside*: *wa inna minkum la-man la-yubaṭṭiʾanna* — "and verily among you there is indeed one who would tarry": *yubaṭṭiʾanna*, doubled-emphatic an intensified verbal manṭ an emphatic-der a strong form from b-ṭ-ʾ — to be slow, drag feet — rendered by the commentators through its two calibrations (al-Ṭabarī's chains and al-Rāzī's review): (1) *the mere lagger* — one who simply holds himself back, arriving after the contest is settled (al-Rāzī's own preferred reading — the particles' grammar suggesting the sluggard's simply *avoiding battle*, not a conspiracy to brake the army); and (2) *the brake-man* — one who weighs down the march of others with hesitation-infection (some early glossers tying it to 9:38's "you sink heavily to the earth" — thāqaltum — and the heavy the heavyweight inertia against jihād summons) — either way a portrait of *sluggishness as stance*: the soldier formally enrolled and functionally absent. The occasion-reports the tradition carries to the clause name its archetype in Madinah's deepest stain (al-Wāḥidī, Ibn Kathīr, al-Ṭabarī, al-Zamakhsharī's collations): **ʿAbdullāh ibn Ubayy ibn Salūl** — chief of the hypocrites — at Uḥud: marching out with the Muslims until the approaches of the Peak, then peeling off with a third of the host — three hundred souls — back to the city — pleading grievance that his counsel to fight inside Madinah had been rejected — its companion-report marking the exact morale-tax such drag-kings levy upon young converts (some of the Anṣār nearly following him back — Bukhārī's Uḥud-era registers and the Sīrah chronicles recounting the wavering before the hardy stood fast) — the verse's *inna minkum* — verily among you — thereby carrying its cruelest disclosure: the army already compromised not by infiltration, but by *membership*.

**Fa-In Aṣābatkum Muṣībatun: Disaster Photographed by the Absentee's Eyes**

Then the narrative-horror clause: *fa-in aṣābatkum muṣībatun qāla qad anʿama Allāhu ʿalayya idh lam akun maʿahum shahīdan* — "then if misfortune befall you, he says: God has blessed me, in that I was not with them as witness": the lagger's true interior unmasked in a single sentence spoken over fellow-believers' pain — misfortune (the Uḥud reversal being its immediate night-dress — seventy slain Companions among the seventy martyrs arrayed upon that hillside) read by him not as national tragedy but as *vindication*: his absence converted into *divine favor* — *anʿama Allāhu ʿalayya* — God conferred upon me — the pronoun-fixation of the clause (*ʿalayya* — upon *me*) itself a self-analysis the Quran the Qur'an meant for every generation's ears: the hypocrite can speak God's name yet mean *his own safety* — religion vocabulary worn over creaturely self-alarm (the commentators noting the grotesque invertedness the inverted use: the believer too says *anʿama Allāh* — but over hardships shared and martyrs mourned; the tarrier says it over *escaped* hardship and *distant* graves) — and *shahīdan* — as a witness — poignant with the word's double current: witness of the scene, and — the overheard secondary — *shahīd*-martyr — refusal to arrive as witness shading into refusal to *become* one. The Prophet's own Uḥud-hour pronouncements stand beside the clause as its living contrast-picture — the genuine believing brothers mourning at their wounds yet counting martyrdom not loss but success — the Prophet's own words at the slain (Bukhārī 4078's account of the burial-pairings and the tenderness toward Ḥamzah's body; the angel-washed shuhadāʾ reports across the maghāzī) — against which portrait the tarrier's *"blessed me — not there"* chills into one of the Book's coldest character-analyses: *some men convert only in the direction of pain-avoidance*; they are enrolled in religion so long as religion costs nothing — the first misfortune priced into faith, and they cash out — not with apostasy-words (they will never say them plainly) but with a *thanks-giving over their own absence*.

**The Morale-Weapon of the Second Clause — and the Reader's Inoculation**

The verse's diagnosis therapy the tradition's diagnosis was needed as inoculation for the first ummah as much as indictment: the Prophet's community had to be *told in revelation* what the tarrier's speech meant — lest its lest its surface-form of gratitude infect others ("maybe God *does* reward staying home!") — revelation thereby naming the tactic and drawing draining it: the mercy-sentence misused is *not mercy*, only the hypocrite's self-witness (his very testimony a self-disclosure — *idh lam akun maʿahum shahīdan* stands in the criminal-file of the disinclination of the Book's inside-the-psyche reportage as one of its most precise) — and every later reader of military, market, ministry, or family fronts inherits the template: watch the ones whose gratitude blooms only in the shadow of others' wounds — the pericope's diagnostic-arc now proceeding (vv. 73–74) to expose his *other* symptom — jealousy at the returning victors — and then to prescribe the counter-stance: fight for the sale of this world for the Hereafter, where *shahādata being* — presence at the appointment — is itself the blessing, whether of survival or of shahādah — for God is not mocked by absences; He blesses presence — *wa Allāhu yaʿlamu mā fi ṣudūrikum* — and the appointment of the faithful stands always among the ranks.

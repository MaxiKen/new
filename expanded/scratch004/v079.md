## Sūrah al-Nisāʾ 4:79

> **Whatever good befalls thee, it is from God, and whatever evil befalls thee, it is from thyself. We sent thee as a messenger unto mankind, and God suffices as a Witness.**

**Expanded Commentary**

**Mā Aṣābaka min Ḥasanatin fa-mina Allāh: Blessing's True Return-Address**

Now comes the balancing clause that reconciles the whole providence-question — the bookend to v. 78's "all is from God": *mā aṣābaka min ḥasanatin fa-mina Allāhi wa mā aṣābaka min sayyiʾatin fa-min nafsik* — "whatever good befalls thee, it is from God, and whatever evil befalls thee, it is from thyself": addressed to the Prophet — and addressed *through* him to everyone (al-Ṭabarī and al-al-Qurṭubī both noting — the tradition's alert: the address is single-person in form, universal in content — since the law stated is metaphysical, not private) — its first clause aligning with the whole Book's gratitude-doctrine: every good — rain, fruit, health, knowledge, victory — streams downward from the King of grace (16:53's parallel blanket: "whatever bounty you have is from God"; with James-like evenness across the creed's with its evenness across the Book's many stations: all *flow* of niʿmah from above) — thus requiring the believer's posture toward blessing *to look upward, not inward* — with the corresponding hadith-practice the sunnah-protocol set out plainly in the practiced devotions: when experiencing blessing — praise; when experiencing affliction — patience (the Muslim 2999 wonder-hadith quoted just above, its double-posture proclamation of the believer's all-good affair).

**Wa Mā Aṣābaka min Sayyiʾatin fa-min Nafsik: Evil Owns the Self as Author**

Then the second — fearful — clause: evil — in Book-language simultaneously *misfortune, sin-harvest, and pain*: *from thyself* — fa-min nafsik — the commentators' two foundational reading-houses the two streams of classical interpretation both permanently assigned here: (1) *causality-of-deeds* (al-Ṭabarī, al-Zamakhsharī's direction): affliction enters a life in consequence of its own choices — requital-lines visible if we could read them — the big reconciliation-chapter of 42:30 standing beside: "whatever misfortune befalls you is for what your hands earned — and He pardons much" — with 3:165's Uḥud-lesson its direct ancestor its near-sister — "when a disaster struck you... say: from yourselves" — matched to the archers' hill abandonment the battlefield-fault that turned the day — so evil's root-cause assigned morally to the person; (2) *decree-comprehension* underwriting both readings (al-Rāzī's theological his characteristic depth-charge): even what comes through your own sin arrives only as God's larger wisdom permits it — decree overarching without being authorship of wrong — the Muʿtazilite-Ashʿarite quarrel of v. 78 continuing here, the fine spinning$ the fine-spun school-debate already sketched standing permanent — both houses bound by the frontier the Book itself sets: God's *origination* of events is never injustice; man's *agency* in sin is never excused; hence the twin-ascription-split of vv. 79 is not contradiction with vv. 78's with v. 78's *kullun min ʿindi Allāh* — but precision within it: *nothing comes except from His will; nothing blamable belongs to Him* — good as downpour, ill as harvest-of-hands — with the Qur with the greater mercy-tokens the same tradition lodges here: whatever affliction *purifies and lifts* is thereby *good in disguise* (the belts the beloved prophetic windows of the belts of the belt prophetic consolation-charters: Muslim's abiding the radiant hadith (Muslim 2999, already above), and the expiation-window: *"no fatigue, illness, worry, grief, harm, or distress befalls a Muslim — not even the prick of a thorn — but that God expiates thereby some of his sins"* — al-Bukhārī 5641 & Muslim 2573 — calamity converted into the ledger's eraser, evil-moment into secret-income).

**Wa Arsalnāka li-l-Nāsi Rasūlan: The Universal Commission Within the Verse**

The verse's second half abruptly turns to office — and the sill$ the sequence itself is the lesson: *wa arsalnāka li-l-nāsi rasūlan wa kafā bi-Allāhi shahīdan* — "and We have sent thee to mankind as a messenger, and God suffices as a witness": *li-l-nāsi* — to *the people*, general and indefinite — the universal scope pinned (the commentators' register of this clause is always lined up with Islam's universality-claim: 4:1's own with 7:158's *"My messenger to all of you* — O mankind..." and 21:107 — "We have not sent thee but as a mercy to the worlds"; 34:28 — "We have sent thee to mankind entire as bearer of good tidings and a warner" — the line of prophets' *bounded* commissions ended at Muhammad: the last Messenger's *humanity-wide* warrant declared inside the sūrah of law) — and *wa kafā bi-Allāhi shahīdan*: God sufficing as witness — the kafā-clause's fifth attestation in our chapter in the pericope (helper — 45; manifest sin-prover — 50; knower — 70; and here as witness) — providence itself carrying the office; the attestation thereby covering the entire case: God witnesses the Prophet's truthfulness against the hypocrites' accusations (v. 78's "this is from thee" — *answered by God's own standing testimony*) and witnesses the entire drama of mankind's responses — every "from God" spoken falsely and every "from thyself" spoken humbly filed on the record of the Watch of the Watching — so that the whole chapter's double apologetic closes in the placed in the triple posture now owed: receive the good with His name over it; own the evil with your soul's name over it; and stand upon the Messenger sent to all mankind — His sufficiency as Witness overriding every objection that the plain offices of time ever raised.

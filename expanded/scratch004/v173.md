## Sūrah al-Nisāʾ 4:173

> **And as for those who believe and do righteous deeds — He shall pay them their rewards in full, and increase them of His bounty. And as for those who disdain and are arrogant — He shall punish them with a painful punishment, and they shall find for themselves no protector or helper apart from God.**

**Expanded Commentary**

**Fa-Ammā Alladhīna Āmanū wa ʿAmilū al-Ṣāliḥāt: The Standing Formula and Its Standing Wage**

The Two-Way verdict follows with the Book's most familiar classification-formula — the standing double-classification that is itself the chapter's own moral center of gravity: *fa-ammā alladhīna āmanū wa ʿamilū al-ṣāliḥāti fa-yuwaffīhim ujūrahum wa yazīduhum min faḍlih* — "and as for those who believe and do righteous deeds — He shall pay them their rewards in full, and increase them of His bounty": the *ammā... fa-* construction — "as for... then..." — the standing apportionment-grammar of the Book's judgment-scenes (its register: 2:26; 4:162's own *lākini*; 7:30 — "a party He guided, and a party upon whom error was rightfully due"; 7:42; 30:15-16; 32:18-20; 39:70-73; 45:28-32; 78:31-40 — the standing paired-files of the judgment) — and the content of the first class so familiar to the chapter's ear that its words carry the chapter's whole argument: īmān plus ʿamal ṣāliḥ — the standing pairing sounded over seventy times in the Book (its stations within this sūrah alone: 4:57; 4:122; 4:124; 4:152; 4:162; 4:173) — creed and deed one currency. And the wage is stated in two movements: (1) *yuwaffīhim ujūrahum* — "He shall pay them their rewards in full": *waffā* — Form II of w-f-y — the fullness-verb (its standing register: 3:25 — "the day each soul shall find present what it did of good and what it did of evil"; 3:57 — "as for those who believe and do righteous deeds — He shall pay them their rewards in full"; 3:161; 4:173; 11:111; 16:111; 24:39; 35:30; 39:70 — "every soul shall be paid in full for what it did"; 46:19) — the standing full-payment-verdict — nothing clipped from the account, no arrears, no rounding-down: the standing the standing comment-canon marking the emphatic grammar: God as the Perfect Paymaster — not the purse that rations; and then, beyond the exactness: (2) *wa yazīduhum min faḍlih* — "and increase them of His bounty": the z-y-d of overflow — beyond-wage generosity outpacing desert — the standing doctrine the commentators name under this verse and its kin: God's requiting is not arithmetic parity but *faḍl* — bounty — bounty beyond earning (its standing register: 3:152; 4:67-70's great-reward file; 10:26 — "for those who do good, the best, and more besides"; 24:38; 29:27; 30:39's zakāt-doublers; 35:30; 42:26 — "He answers those who believe and do righteous deeds and increases them of His bounty"; "theirs shall be whatsoever they wish therein — and with Us is more yet") — the two-movement formula thereby setting the standing ratio of requital: exactness first (no laborer defrauded), bounty beyond (no love contained in arithmetic) — and the tradition's standing hadith-gloss (Muslim's register of the qudsī promise — "I have prepared for My righteous servants what no eye has seen, no ear has heard, and has never entered the heart of man" — Bukhārī 3244; Muslim 2824) enlarging the *increase* into its standing incomprehensibility.

**Wa Ammā Alladhīna Istankafū wa Istakbarū: The Disdainers' Fourfold Forfeit**

Then the second class — its naming deliberately pairing the two verbs of v. 172 to compose one indictment-word: *wa ammā alladhīna istankafū wa istakbarū fa-yuʿadhdhibuhum ʿadhāban alīman* — "and as for those who disdain and are arrogant — He shall punish them with a painful punishment": *istankafū* — refusing servanthood as beneath them — and *istakbarū* — the arrogance that the refusal grows from and feeds — the commentators (al-Ṭabarī's standing gloss) marking the generalization achieved by the pairing: the verse's polemic completing its movement from Jesus-file to universal law — what began as the correction of one community's exaggeration stands now as the standing condition-name of all who disdain servanthood anywhere: the Christian exaggerator of Jesus' station and the pagan disdainer of obedience meet at one root — istinkāf-istikbār — and receive one fate: *ʿadhāb alīm* — the standing painful-punishment (the phrase tolling through the chapter's doom-register: 4:18; 4:161; the several standing stations across the Book: 2:10; 3:91; 5:36; 9:34; 14:22; 22:25; 31:7; 45:8; 58:5).

**Wa Lā Yajidū Lahum min Dūni Allāhi Waliyyan wa Lā Naṣīran: The Null-Pair Seventh Sounded**

The sentence closes with the chapter's signature annihilation-pair — its seventh tolling in the chapter — the standing double-denial by which every doom-verse of this sūrah has closed: *wa lā yajidū lahum min dūni Allāhi waliyyan wa lā naṣīran* — "and they shall find for themselves no protector or helper apart from God": *walī* — the protecting patron; *naṣīr* — the succoring helper — both *min dūni Allāh* — "apart from God" — the commentators (al-Rāzī's standing note at these paired null-clauses) marking the two-sided foreclosure: alliance-resource and rescue-resource both sought *outside God* and both found *empty*: the standing Book-register of the formula: 4:45 — "God suffices as an Ally — and God suffices as a Helper" — the sūrah's own positive posting of the same two Names for the believers; and its paired-negation file: 4:52 ("whomsoever God curses — thou shalt find no helper for him"); 4:89; 4:123; 4:145 ("thou shalt never find for them a helper"); 4:173; its standing companions at 2:48; 2:107; 2:120; 3:22; 3:56; 9:74; 29:22; 33:17; 33:65; 42:31; 42:46 — the several standing null-stations) — and within this verse the pair performing its standing pastoral office: the disdainer had imagined an address-book of alternatives — networks, patrons, prestige-stations — all filed under *min dūni Allāh* — and the verdict is that the ledger *outside* God reads zero — not diminished, not delayed: *lā yajidū* — they shall not *find* — the verb of search concluding in void. And the verse thereby closes the Jesus-file argument into the opening of vv. 174-176: the exaggerators taught that the Messiah would never disdain service (172), the disdainers measured (173) — and on the other register, the believers-and-workers assigned double-wage security — "He shall pay them their rewards in full, and increase them of His bounty" — the chapter's Two Ways running in parallel to its final legal-verse (176) — servanthood with wage, or disdain with punishment, and *no third estate* — the standing Book-verdict already declared in the triptych of vv. 150-152 and sealed here with the Names of patronage and help: everything under the description "apart from God" — found wanting; everything under the description "God" — sufficient.

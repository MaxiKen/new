## Sūrah al-Nisāʾ 4:121

> **Such will have their refuge in Hell — and will find no refuge therefrom.**

**Expanded Commentary**

**Ulāʾika Maʾwāhum Jahannam: The Refuge-Irony Sealed Twice**

The shirk-file ends with its own answer — and the answer is in the purest grim irony of the Book's own standing register: *ulāʾika maʾwāhum jahannamu wa lā yajidūna ʿanhā maḥīṣā* — "those — their refuge is Hell — and they shall find no refuge from it": *maʾwā* — refuge-home — from ā-w-y (the standing the Quran's own irony-word: the Hell-user's refuge named by the very word of shelter promised to the faithful — v. 97's its Book-roll of the same grim usage: 3:151 — "their refuge is the Fire — and evil is the abode of the wrongdoers"; 4:97 — for the hijrah-shirkers; 8:16 — for the turners-back on the day of encounter; 13:18; 24:57; 45:34; 79:39 — the standing the standing register-usage of "home" attributed to the to their God naming hell by the word of lodging-for-the-damned — the Book's standing rhetorical cruel mercy: so the so the mind runs to *maʾwā* in expectation — then shudders) — and the lock-clause: *wa lā yajidūna ʿanhā maḥīṣā* — "and they shall find from it no asylum": *maḥīṣ* — from ḥ-y-ṣ — escape, evasion, refuge-out (the Book's standing exitless-verdict for the for the terminal group — its standing companions: 14:21 — its standing Book-kin: 14:21 — "they appear before God altogether — and the weak say to the arrogant: we were your followers — will you avail us aught they answer: had God guided us we would have guided you — equal it is for us whether we bewail or bear patiently — we have no asylum — min maḥīṣ"; 41:48 — "they shall have no asylum — maḥīṣ"; 42:47's "answer your Lord before a day comes from God — for you there is no asylum that day"; 75:11's — "never! — no refuge — kalā lā wazar") — the sūrah the sūrah thereby ending its whole long arc (from Ṭuʿmah's own from Ṭuʿmah's night-lobby, through the accursed one's vow, to the idol-shrines of Arabia) upon the single vista: every choice ending somewhere — God's God's promise-currency payable in gardens — Satan's promise-currency payable at last in walls without exits.

**The File's Standing Lesson for the Ummah

The tradition then distills vv. 116–121 in its standing summary its standing catechism-form — recited ever since in the commentator in the comment-canons as the sūrah's shirk-catechism: (1) shirk alone falls outside automatic 116 — though it can be repented-from in life; (2) every false call lands upon Satan's 117 — idolatry standing as demon-service even unwitting; (3) the accursed works under a curse-license — 118 — his vow long since defeated from its own his very vow failing by its own ledger; (4) his program has four limbs — 119 — misguidance, wish-fires, cult-mutilation, creed-alteration — with the walī-choice filing any client of that program under khusrān mubīn; (5) his promise-book is ghurūr-paper — 120 — the God-promise alone being true currency; and (6) both currencies settle on the Day — 121 — with every false shelter collapsing into the final shelter — with the final shelter rendering itself as a residence from which none escape — with Ibn ʿAjībah's standing with Ibn ʿAjībah's own soul-level reading, the standing close the tradition adds for the centuries: each of these each of these six clauses has its twin inside the believer's breast — the soul's subtle riyāʾ adorned as piety, appetite altared as devotion, self-trust dressed as reliance, wish-dilations standing as delay-works of prayer — the shirk-file inside the breast reproducing every clause of the external file — Satan's four limbs running in miniature across the heart's own map — and maḥīṣ therefore also read inwardly: whatever escape one finds from the service of one's own inner ṭāghūt must be taken *now*, while exits still open — for the Book teaches only two kinds of men: those who those who are leaving, and those who have those who have ceased to find a way out.

**Wa Lan Yajidū ʿAnhā Maḥīṣan: No Narrowing of the Penalty**

The verse's last clause supplies the sūrah's own lexicon for inescapability: *wa lan yajidū ʿanhā maḥīṣan* — "and they shall find no escape therefrom." *Maḥīṣ* — from *ḥāṣa / yaḥīṣu*, to turn aside, to shrink away as a shrinking retreat (a rare word, its register: 14:21's "no place of refuge for them"; 41:48; 42:47's "no refuge for you that Day, and no denial"; 75:11's "nay — there is no refuge") — pairs with *maʾwā* (4:121's refuge-word) to form the chapter's standing double-lock: the Fire *is* their refuge — and from it there is no narrower corner, no bolt-hole, no slipping-off; the jail is built with no recess to recede into. The commentators mark the doubled negation's rhetorical weight — *lan* with the negated verb, then the negation-zone of *ʿanhā* — the Book speaking with every grammatical bolt because the listeners being addressed (the waverers of vv. 118–120, the followers of the Shayṭān-faction) nursed precisely the fantasy of an exit-door from consequences. And the mercy-seam of the whole pericope stands intact beneath it: vv. 114–121 roll through Ṭuʿmah's clan, slander, barring, beseeching devils, and delusive promises to this dead-end — so that the next verse (v. 122) may swing the reader into its opposite with redemptive force: *those who believe and work righteousness — We shall bring them into Gardens beneath which rivers flow*. The sūrah never builds its dead-ends without the counter-door in the next line: the maḥīṣ-less refuse for the shirk-faction, the garden-entrance for the believing-worker — between them, only the choice of whom to follow into the narrow.

## Sūrah al-Nisāʾ 4:56

> **Those who disbelieve in Our signs, We shall surely cause them to burn in a Fire. As often as their skins are consumed, We shall replace them with other skins, that they may taste the punishment. Truly God is Mighty, Wise.**

**Expanded Commentary**

**Sa-Nuṣlīhim Nārā: Fire-Admission in the First-Person Future**

The pericope moves from the sufficiency-receipt of Hell (v. 55) into a single, unblinking mechanism of its execution: *inna alladhīna kafarū bi-āyātinā sawfa nuṣlīhim nārā* — "verily those who disbelieve in Our signs — We shall make them burn in a Fire": *kafarū bi-āyātinā* — disbelieved *in Our signs* — the object precise for the pericope: the book-holders and the mockers who heard *and knowingly refused the delivered evidence* (not mere pagans by birth and unawares — the Book's sharpened kufr is the one *vis-à-vis* received signs); *sawfa* — the deferred-certain future (the particle of distance-yet-irrevocability; as the sages note: in human promises distance may soften, in divine decree it *only loads*); and *nuṣlīhim* — We shall roast we shall make them *arrive into the fire* — from the same ṣ-l-y root heard at 4:30's *fa-sawfa nuṣlīhi nāran* — the first-person plural of majestic agency making admission no accident of nature but the King's own decree *executed by His hand* — al-Rāzī's note catching the procedure: God will not send them there as a sheriff sends a prisoner; He will *convey* them — attendance adduced by sovereignty itself — the court no longer speaking of punishment as a policy but as a personal act of the Remembered.

**Kullamā Naḍijat Julūduhum: The Endless Reroasting**

Then the measure of torment disclosed through the body: *kullamā naḍijat julūduhum baddalnāhum julūdan ghayrahā* — "as often as their skins are roasted through, We shall exchange them for other skins": *kullamā* — *every time that* — the temporal formula of unending recurrence (its series across the Book always opening into endlessness: 22:22's expulsion-and-return-loop — "every time they wish to leave it from grief they are returned into it"; 36:57 the same *kullamā* of fruit-signing at 2:25 too); *naḍijat* — roasted, cooked-through — the flesh-word used nowhere else in the Book — *julūd*, skins, the organ of sensation (the body's own boundary of feel — the dermis where pain and shame both register) — and *baddalnāhum julūdan ghayrahā* — We *replace* — substitution of another skin for the consumed one: the classical discussions at this point organized themselves into the two readings al-Ṭabarī and al-Qurṭubī bequeath: (1) *somatic resurrection continuous*: genuine skins, sensation restored — the reading the Tradition had already pinned in the famous debate between the Prophet and the Meccan questioners — reported in the transmitted registers as the verse’s occasion-class: the scene stands among the most instructive of the sources — Ubayy ibn Khalaf the Meccan sneering at resurrection ("will my Lord really revive us when we are bones and fragments?") and the Prophet answering that He can do more; the answer converging here: *He who created them the first time* — and (2) the second register (al-Ṭabarī's register, cited in al-Qurṭubī's own pages): the *pitch-garments* of 14:50 — their faces covered by Fire, their clothes made of pitch — being consumed and replaced round the real body beneath — a garment-of-flame reading that softens nothing of the horror, only changes its textile — the tradition concluding across both: *recurring in kind, unbroken in time* — al-Rāzī's metaphysical aside being thereby made useful: the changing of *attributes* (skins) around an *essence* (the punished self) — the sufferer abideth, his rind his surface replenished — eschatology teaching plainly what it needn't teaching by sheer anatomy: *whoever demanded literal proof of torture's machinery has been answered in literal anatomy; whoever feared it as metaphor may read the metaphor — for both the depth is the same: never-endingness.*

**Li-Yadhūqū al-ʿAdhāb: Taste as the Purposed Instrument**

The clause of purpose seals the machinery with the Book's favorite eschatological verb: *li-yadhūqū al-ʿadhāb* — "so that they may *taste* the punishment": *dhāqa*, to taste — the Qur'an's selected apparatus for experience-not-news (the verb family running through dozens of verdicts — 4:57's own contrast arriving the very next verse at 44:56's "they shall not taste death therein beyond the first death"; 3:106's whitening-and-blackening faces; 8:50's "taste the punishment of the Burning") — and the choice of *tasting* is doctrinal, as al-Rāzī's page insists: punishment is not *information* but *felt* — the skin being replaced precisely *because* tasting holds it — the organs exchanged for nothing but renewal of the taste-circuit: the wisdom shown as education-in-reverse: *in the world, taste was the locus of choosing; on That Day taste becomes the locus of serving the sentence.*

**Inna Allāha Kāna ʿAzīzan Ḥakīmā: Might and Wisdom Above the Furnace**

The seal pairs its two attributes with the pericope's own specific geometry: *inna Allāha kāna ʿazīzan ḥakīman* — "God is Mighty, Wise": *ʿazīz* — the Invincible — answering the reader's involuntary wince (*can such power really?*): yes — ʿazīz — nothing stands between His decree and its skins; *ḥakīm* — the Wise — answering the theological recoil (is endlessness itself just?): yes — ḥakīm — His wisdom has calibrated eternity with the offense — and the commentators' note for the pairing is the sūrah's now-consistent doctrine: after every severity-clause the two attributes reappear (4:17, 26, 30's pattern of *ʿalīm ḥakīm* pairs) to keep terror from collapsing into arbitrariness and mercy from collapsing into permissiveness. With v. 56 the portion-trials find their furnace-door — and v. 57 immediately throws open the opposite garden-gates: the Two Abodes set facing one another (as is the Book's perpetual manner) — skin-taste answered by shade-cool by shade-taste; the reader suspended in between, still free to choose — *and that suspension is the only unsealed page in the entire register*.

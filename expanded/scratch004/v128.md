## Sūrah al-Nisāʾ 4:128

> **And if a woman fears from her husband ill-treatment or aversion, there is no sin upon them both that they make terms of peace between themselves with terms of peace — and peace is better. But greed is made present in the souls. And if you do good and keep from evil — then God is ever Aware of what you do.**

**Expanded Commentary**

**Wa Imini Raʾatun Khāfat min Baʿlihā Nushūzan: The Fear From the Other Side of the House**

The marriage-file continues, now from the wife's vantage — the sūrah's standing equity of address: as v. 34 regulated the husband's fear of *her* nushūz, so now: *wa imini raʾatun khāfat min baʿlihā nushūzan aw iʿrāḍan* — "and if a woman fears from her husband ill-treatment or aversion": *nushūz* — again (v. 34's standing word) — from n-sh-z — rising, elevation: here the husband's high-handedness — the rising of the stronger partner above the marriage-covenant in arrogance, harshness, or contempt; and *iʿrāḍ* — from ʿ-r-ḍ — turning the side, presenting the back: the cold withdrawal — the husband emotionally abandoning the marriage while legally remaining in it (the standing Book-file of the word: "who turns away from My reminder — his is a straitened life" — 20:124; "turn not away thy cheek from men" — 31:18). The commentators (al-Ṭabarī, Ibn Kathīr, al-Qurṭubī) note that the Book here legislates from the *woman's perception* — *khāfat* — "she fears" — the same epistemic standard used for the husband in v. 34 ("whose nushūz you fear"): relational breakdown is adjudicated first at the level of what is sensed and reasonably feared, not only after public scandal — the law honors the interior weather of the house before the walls crack audibly.

**Lā Junāḥa ʿAlayhimā an Yuṣliḥā: Permission Before Permission Is Asked — The Primacy of Ṣulḥ**

The verse's central mercy opens a door neither side must be shamed for seeking: *lā junāḥa ʿalayhimā an yuṣliḥā baynahumā ṣulḥan — wa al-ṣulḥu khayr* — "there is no sin upon them both that they make terms of peace between themselves with terms of peace — and peace is better": *junāḥ* — from j-n-ḥ — the wing, the leaning side: hence sin as a *leaning from straightness* — and the Book removes the lean of shame from reconciliation: the couple that negotiates — she perhaps relinquishing some of her dowry or nights, he perhaps agreeing to reforms — commits no fault; the arrangement need not mimic the default pattern to be valid before God. Then comes one of the Book's most economical axioms — jurists and sages have made it a standing title of the law of settlement: *wa al-ṣulḥu khayr* — "and peace is better": four words in English, two in the Arabic, containing what al-Qurṭubī marveled at as a whole jurisprudence: not merely *permitted* but *better* — amendment preferred over adjudication, voluntary concession over compulsory verdict — its standing Book-kin: "and their case is consultation among them" — 42:38; "and reconciliation between brothers" — 49:9-10's standing command ("make peace between your two brothers"); and the celebrated Ḥasan-al-Baṣrī dictum quoted in this place by Ibn Kathīr: to give up a right for the sake of unity is itself worship. The Sabāb-gloss brings it to earth: ʿĀʾishah's standing report (Bukhārī's standing source — Bukhārī 5212; Muslim's companion-narration): Sawdah bint Zamʿah, fearing the Prophet's iʿrāḍ and wishing to remain his wife, gifted her night to ʿĀʾishah — and "in her case," the commentators report, the verse revealed its mercy: no fault upon them in the settlement they chose — the law ratifying the selfless generosity of the aging wife and the quiet acceptance of the Prophet, so that she remained among the Mothers of the Believers to her last day.

**Wa Uḥḍirati al-Anfusu al-Shuḥḥ: Greed Seated in the Soul Itself**

Then the sentence that commentators call the piercing X-ray of the human condition — *wa uḥḍirati al-anfusu al-shuḥḥa* — "but greed is made present in the souls": *shuḥḥ* — from sh-ḥ-ḥ — avarice of the clingy, comprehensive kind — deeper than bukhl (stinginess) and broader than ḥirṣ (eagerness): the soul's instinct to hoard its own allotment and eye its neighbor's. And the verb is the marvel: *uḥḍirati* — passive of iḥḍār — *is made present* — as though greed were a permanent attendee summoned to every human sitting, seated within the self rather than imported from outside: al-Rāzī pauses here to observe that the Book does not say greed *afflicts* souls from without but that it is *brought to sit inside* them — hence no spouse enters settlement-negotiations innocent of it, and hence the next clause's command. The Book's standing shuḥḥ-file is the standing commentary: 59:9 — "and they prefer others over themselves though poverty be theirs — and whoever is preserved from the greed of his soul — those are the successful"; 64:16 — "fear God as much as you are able... and whoever is preserved from the greed of his soul — those are the successful"; 3:180's standing warning to the miserly. In marriage the application is exact: both parties come to the ṣulḥ-table with a seated guest named Greed — he wants the whole dowry, she wants the whole schedule; he counts his provisions, she counts her concessions — and the Book, rather than pretending the guest absent, names him, so that his operations can be watched: the spiritual genius of the legislation being that it reforms the inward before adjudicating the outward.

**Wa In Tuḥsinū wa Tattaqū: The Two Wings of Every Settlement**

The verse then sets the flight-condition: *wa in tuḥsinū wa tattaqū fa-inna Allāha kāna bimā taʿmalūna khabīran* — "and if you do good and keep from evil — then God is ever Aware of what you do": iḥsān and taqwā — the standing Book-pair (2:195 — "do good — for God loves the good-doers"; 16:128 — "God is with those who keep from evil and those who are good-doers"; 5:93's doubled commendation) — here applied to marital settlement: do good — beyond the bare contract — and keep from evil — within the bare contract: taqwā as the floor and iḥsān as the ceiling of conduct between spouses. And the seal — *khabīr* — the Expert-Aware, the Intimately-Informed (v. 35's standing anticipation — "God is ever Knowing, Aware" — ʿalīm khabīr; 31:16's standing weightlessness of the mustard-seed under His awareness) — reminding the settling couple that no doled-out kindness within the settlement goes unaudited by heaven, and no buried resentment escapes its ledger. The tradition adds the standing price-tag for the path refused: the hadith in al-Tirmidhī's standing narration — "among the lawful things most odious to God is divorce" (its standing source: Abū Dāwūd 2178; the Prophet's standing caution balancing the concession-doctrine of this verse) — reconciliation exalted, dissolution demoted to last resort — and the sūrah will enforce the order in vv. 129–130 which follow.

## Sūrah al-Nisāʾ 4:32

> **And covet not that by which God has favored some of you above others—unto men a share of what they have earned, and unto women a share of what they have earned—but ask God for His Bounty. Truly God is the Knower of all things.**

**Expanded Commentary**

**Umm Salamah's Question: Allowed to Be Asked at Heaven's Desk**

The verse was born of a question the tradition keeps tenderly, a question only a believing woman could ask and only revelation could answer — the occasion-reports (al-Wāḥidī, al-Ṭabarī, Ibn Kathīr from Mujāhid's chain) fixing it in the Prophet's own house: Umm Salamah, the Prophet's ﷺ wife, reflected upon what the new law and the new army-life had given the men — the double share of inheritance (v. 11), the fortune of the jihād-field, and with them the doubled opportunities for worldly and other-worldly storehouse — and she said in the presence:

> *"O Messenger of God, the men go out to battle and we do not; and they receive two shares of the inheritance — would that we could share in what they obtain of reward!"*

And God answered her with the verse: *wa lā tatamannaw mā faḍḍala Allāhu bihi baʿḍakum ʿalā baʿḍ* — "covet not that by which God has favored some of you above others." The occasion is essential to the verse's meaning: this was no rebuke of an envier's malice — the Prophet's household were not a court of spite — but a *pedagogy over the very question*: the believer, male or female, who notices disparity of *means* is taught where to look instead. Completing it, the companion-revelations — 3:195's *"I waste not the work of a worker among you, male or female"* and 33:35's spread of equal tenfold rewards for each sex's obedience — give Umm Salamah's anxiety the complete divine answer: *spirit wages are paid perfectly; only the instruments differ.* And the wish of the sisters who said "would our burden on the Day be half as theirs, like our share of the estate?" (a saying of the transmitters the Shiʿi collections preserve) received the same lesson: heaven's desk is not a tribunal that takes proportion from commerce in goods.

**Li-l-Rijāli Naṣībun Mimmā-ktasabū: The Earning Arithmetic for Both Sexes**

The verse then re-states the apportioning in the two matched clauses — *li-l-rijāli naṣībun mimmā-ktasabū wa lil-nisāʾi naṣībun mimmā-ktasabna* — "unto the men a share of what they earned, and unto the women a share of what they earned": the *kasb*-word — earning — applied symmetrically to both estates of mankind, ending forever any theory that one sex is the other's is ranked as the other's *share-less* dependent: each *earns*, each is *paid*, by his and her own earning — and the Book's insistence on *mimmā-ktasabū* (from one's earning) turns the discussion from *garments and gold* to *works and wages* — precisely the swap Umm Salamah's question had circled. The commentators (al-Qurṭubī's full tracing) locate the clause's promise: men hear — even your shares must be *earned*; women hear — your works are *seen* in the same arithmetic; and both hear in the wage-theology: no prejudice in heaven's payroll: *mimmā ktasab* — from earnings — not *from sexes.*

**Covet-Not: The Prohibition Targeting the Disease of Comparison**

The command against *tamannī* — wishing, coveting the allotments of others — is the first member of the Book's famous anti-envy corpus, and this verse is its anchoring verse in the law of gendered and provisioned disparity. The commentators identify the disease precisely in the object-form: *mā faḍḍala Allāhu bihi baʿḍakum ʿalā baʿḍ* — *what God Himself has preferred some by* — so the coveting is *a wish against the Distributor* — forbidden because it double-sins: envy of the favored, implicit complaint against the Favorer. Al-Rāzī's kalām-surgical reading carries the carries the clause to the theology of contentment: disparity of provision is *wisdom's artifact*, grounding the world's economy — *by Him are your various grades* (6:165's *darajāt*) — and the believer's task is not to flatten the scales but to sail with his own cargo. The companion-hadith-file of the Prophet's own counsel — the hadith of the envy's ledger:

> *"Be satisfied (iqnaʿ) with what God has apportioned for you, and you will be the richest of people"* (al-Tirmidhī 2305, ḥasan — from ʿAbd Allāh ibn ʿAmr); and *"Look to those below you [in provision], and not to those above you — it is worthier that you not belittle the blessing of God upon you."* (al-Bukhārī 6490; Muslim 2963 — from Abū Hurayrah)

— these are the verse's two practical rules, standing in the companion-file of the revealed word.

**Wa-sʾalū Allāha min Faḍlihi: The Lawful Desire**

But the statute's mercy holds one desire always open: *wa-sʾalū Allāha min faḍlihi* — "and ask God of His bounty": the door marked *request* is never locked — coveting being barred exactly because asking is available, complaint against distribution being punished exactly by prayer-repair replacing it — the very point the commentators fix: *do not watch your neighbor's tray; lift your hands:* Covet-not belongs to *comparison*; ask-bounty belongs to *petition*. The Prophet's own encouraged formula of this life and the next is the is the brother-reported ḥadīth of general instruction: *"Ask God of His bounty — for He loves to be asked"* (al-Tirmidhī 3384, ḥasan), with groups of the salaf glossing the *faḍl* here as both the provision of of the world and the greater *faḍl* of faith-works in which alone the judgment sits. And the seal — *inna Allāha kāna bi-kulli shayʾin ʿalīma* — Knower of all things — is the court's last stamp on the whole commerce of comparison: He knows *why* each vessel holds its measure; knows how wishes migrate from heart to covenant; and knows the one petition that is always answered at its at its station: ask *Him*, and stop watching the neighbor's plate.

## Sūrah al-Nisāʾ 4:172

> **The Messiah would never disdain to be a servant of God, nor would the angels brought near. And whosoever disdains His service and is arrogant — He shall gather them unto Himself all together.**

**Expanded Commentary**

**Lan Yastankifa al-Masīḥu an Yakūna ʿAbdan li-llāh: Servanthood Not Beneath the Messiah**

The doctrinal summit of v. 171 receives its climbing-proof — one of the Book's most polished inversions: *lan yastankifa al-Masīḥu an yakūna ʿabdan li-llāh* — "the Messiah would never disdain to be a servant of God": *yastankif* — from n-k-f — to disdain, to consider beneath one's dignity, to flinch from as degrading (its standing register: 4:172; 4:173's paired response; 21:19 — "those who are with Him are not too proud to worship Him, nor do they weary") — and the particle *lan* — the perpetual never — fencing the whole matter at the level of the person claimed for divinity: the Messiah himself — the very subject of the exaggeration — would never count servanthood beneath him. The commentators (al-Rāzī gathering the standing context-note) record the probable address-scene: the Christian delegation of Najrān, come to Madīnah for their standing doctrinal exchange with the Prophet (the standing reports of the delegation-file in the sīrah-literature), had balked at the Book's designation of Jesus as *ʿabd* — servant, even slave — hearing in it a depreciation of their Lord; the verse answers inside their own scale: ʿubūdiyyah — servanthood-to-God — is not a station beneath greatness but the station *by which* the greatest are great — and the standing counter-witnesses are then offered in the verse's own second clause: *wa lā al-malāʾikatu al-muqarrabūn* — "nor would the angels brought near" — the *muqarrabūn* — the near-stationed (its standing register: 56:11 — "those are the ones brought near"; 83:21; 83:28; 3:45 — "eminent in this world and the Hereafter and of those brought near"; 4:172; 21:19; 37:164-166 — "there is none of us but has a known station — and verily we are the ranged ones — and verily we are the glorifiers"; 66:6's standing non-disobeyer-file) — the highest beings of the Book's cosmology volunteering precisely the posture the exaggerators imagined degrading: if the near-stationed angels and the Word-and-Spirit Messiah both count servanthood as glory, the geography has inverted: the summit of creation is *ʿabd*, and the refusal of it is the sin named next.

**ʿAbd in the Book's Standing Theology: From Taxonomy to Honor**

The commentators expand the term's standing analysis (the standing distinctions gathered across the comment-canon): *ʿabd* marks in its first register sheer created dependence — every creature a servant under the Owner — and in its exalted register the chosen posture of willing submission: servanthood-as-vocation — and the Book's own title-usage honors precisely this word at the peaks of prophetic biography: 2:23's "Our servant" of the Prophet; 17:1 — "glory be to Him who carried His servant by night" — the greatest journey-tribute in the Book paid with the title servant; 18:1; 19:30 — Jesus from the cradle: "verily I am the servant of God — He gave me the Book and made me a prophet" — the standing first-person self-designation already in the Book's own Jesus-file; 25:1; 38:17; 38:30 — "an excellent servant" of David and Solomon; 38:44 — "We found him patient — an excellent servant" of Job; 39:36 — "is not God sufficient for His servant?"; 43:59 — addressing the exaggerators directly of Jesus: "he is but a servant upon whom We bestowed favor — and We made him a sign for the Children of Israel"; 57:27 — his file as messenger-chain; and 72:19 — "when the servant of God stood calling upon Him..." The sūrah's own argument thereby finds its capstone: the Messiah whom the exaggerators would crown as god is in the Book crowned with **servanthood — the very predicate to which the near-stationed angels hold fast** — "the Messiah would never disdain" — and the title trades upward: the greatest name beneath Creator is not "son" or "god" but *ʿabd Allāh* — the name, the commentators note, most beloved in the prophetic biography itself (the standing report in Muslim's register 2132 — Bukhārī's standing reporting of the most beloved names to God: ʿAbd Allāh and ʿAbd al-Raḥmān).

**Wa Man Yastankif ʿan ʿIbādatihi wa Yastakbir: Disdain and Arrogance as One Cord**

The verse then names the refusal and its root: *wa man yastankif ʿan ʿibādatihi wa yastakbir* — "and whosoever disdains His service and is arrogant": disdain of service exposed as *istikbār* — arrogance (its standing Book-dossier: 2:34 — "save Iblīs — he refused and waxed arrogant and was of the disbelievers"; 38:74-76 — Iblīs' own self-elevation: "I am better than he"; 7:206 — "those who are with thy Lord are not too proud to worship Him — they glorify Him and to Him they prostrate"; 16:49-50; 39:72 — "enter the gates of Hell, abiding therein — wretched is the lodging of the arrogant"; 40:60; the standing register) — the commentators marking the psychology of the linkage: to disdain servanthood is not a neutral theology-opinion but the springing of the creature above its created station — the standing first-sin reenacted: Iblīs' refusal of the prostration-command being precisely the refusal of *ʿibādah* out of *kibr* — 4:172 thereby filing the exaggerated-Jesus theologians not under error-of-information but under the family-line of the original disdain — with Jesus himself and the near-stationed angels exhibited as its standing counterexamples: no station in creation is too high for worship — the refusal of worship therefore dignifies no one.

**Fa-Sa-Yaḥshuruhum Ilayhi Jamīʿan: The Gathering to the One Disdained**

The sentence closes with the gathering-verdict: *fa-sa-yaḥshuruhum ilayhi jamīʿan* — "He shall gather them unto Himself all together": *ḥ-sh-r* — the standing eschatological mustering (its register in the sūrah: 4:87 — "He shall surely gather you to the Day of Resurrection — no doubt of it"; 4:140's gatherer-file; and the standing Book-wide stations: 2:203; 3:12; 5:96; 6:22; 6:38; 6:51; 8:36; 10:45; 18:99; the several standing summons-verses) — with *jamīʿan* — "all together" — the totality-adverb sealing the escape-proof: none filtered out; and the participle's own standing irony carried by the commentators: the disdainers of servanthood will yet be gathered — *in service* — to the Master they declined to serve: the posture refused in freedom shall be borne in muster; the summit avoided voluntarily met by summons. The verse thereby plants, at the edge of vv. 173's Two-Way wages, the chapter's final Christological lesson in its purest form: the two highest registers of creation — the Messiah and the near-stationed angels — define honor as *service*, and disdain of service as arrogance leading only to the Gathering: **ʿubūdiyyah is not the floor of the divine household — it is its elevation**: let the exaggerators learn rank from its exemplars.

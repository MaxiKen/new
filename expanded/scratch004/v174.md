## Sūrah al-Nisāʾ 4:174

> **O mankind! There has now come to you a proof from your Lord; and We have sent down to you a clear light.**

**Expanded Commentary**

**Yā Ayyuhā al-Nāsu Qad Jāʾakum Burhānun min Rabbikum: The Proof Arrived, Not Merely the Report**

The chapter's penultimate doctrinal address opens with the same universal vocative sounded at v. 170 — and the commentators instantly mark the intensification: where v. 170 announced "the Messenger has come to you with the truth," v. 174 announces the arrival of the *proof itself*: *yā ayyuhā al-nāsu qad jāʾakum burhānun min rabbikum* — "O mankind! There has now come to you a proof from your Lord": *burhān* — from b-r-h-n — a word the Book uses sparingly and weightily: the demonstration that compels — not mere evidence (*bayyinah* — the clear sign, its standing general file) but the irrefutable argument that ends debate: the standing *ḥujjah* raised to exhibition (its standing register: 4:174; 12:24 — Joseph's temptation-scene — "she desired him, and he would have desired her, had he not seen the proof of his Lord — *burhāna rabbih*"; 21:24 — "bring your proof — *burhānakum* — this is the reminder of those with me and the reminder of those before me"; 23:117; 27:64 — "produce your proof, if you are truthful"; 28:32; 28:75) — the commentators (al-Ṭabarī's standing gloss; al-Rāzī's scholastic note) identifying the referent by the verse's own placement: the Burhān that has now arrived = the Prophet-and-Qur'ān complex just detailed through vv. 153-173 — the revelation whose chain was established (163-165), witnessed by God and the angels (166), deployed against every file of error (167-173) — the whole preceding People-of-the-Book movement standing under this one noun: the chapter having argued its case, the argument itself is now named: *burhānun min rabbikum* — and the possessive *rabbikum* — "your Lord" — keeping the argument in the standing covenantal frame: the proof comes not from an adversary but from *their* Lord (4:1's standing opening-address answered at the close).

**Wa Anzalnā Ilaykum Nūran Mubīnan: The Clear Light Sent Down — The Qur'ān Named as Illumination**

The second arrival is named in image: *wa anzalnā ilaykum nūran mubīnan* — "and We have sent down to you a clear light": *nūr* — light — the standing Qur'ānic self-designation of the Book itself (its standing register: 5:15 — "there has come to you from God a light and a clear Book"; 5:44; 5:46 — the Torah and Gospel each carrying guidance-and-light; 6:1, 37:117; 42:52 — "thus did We reveal to thee a Spirit from Our command — thou knewest not what the Book was, nor belief — but We made it a light whereby We guide whom We will of Our servants"; 57:9; 64:8 — "believe in God and His Messenger and the light We sent down"; 65:11; and the standing cosmological 24:35 — "God is the Light of the heavens and the earth") — the commentators reading the two arrivals as the standing dyad of divine aid: Burhān in the *mind* — argument that cuts off debate; Nūr in the *heart and conduct* — illumination by which one walks: the proof convinces, the light guides — and *mubīn* — clear, manifest, self-evidencing (its standing collocation through the Book: 2:92 — Moses coming with clear signs; 3:20; 7:73's standing clear-proof of Ṣāliḥ; 12:1 — the Qur'ān as clear Book; 15:1; 26:2; 27:1; 28:2; 43:2; 44:2; and the standing sūrah-file: 4:153's clear-proofs; 4:174's own usage) — the light described by its own shining: not a hidden lantern requiring unpacking but brightness itself, so that the standing sinner-file ("a deep darkness amid the Sea, wave upon wave, and clouds above... when he puts out his hand, he can hardly see it" — 24:40) marks by contrast what has now arrived: a light *mubīn* — needing no mediator to certify that it shines.

**The Standing Address-Architecture: Burhān and Nūr as the Chapter's Two Keys**

The commentators observe the verse's architectural precision within the closing movement: vv. 174-175 standing as the standing key-pair of the whole People-of-the-Book movement: (1) *burhān* names retrospectively the chapter's dialectical achievement — the indictment-file (155-161), the Jesus-correction (156-159), the messengers-register (163-165), the witness (166), the doctrinal summit (171-173) — every argument of vv. 153-173 gathered under one banner-word: the chapter's polemics are not accusation-but-proof, arriving *from their Lord*; and (2) *nūr mubīn* names prospectively what vv. 175 will articulate as its use: those who "believe in God and hold fast to Him" shall be guided "unto Himself upon a straight path" — light becoming movement, illumination becoming locomotion toward God. The standing comment-canon's structural note (al-Rāzī's summary standing at the verse's head) ties the two nouns to the chapter's two audiences: for the People of the Book, the Burhān is the covenant-proof making fidelity to their own revelation lead inevitably to this one (as 4:162's rāsikhūn filed); for the believers, the Nūr is the possession by which the rest of the way is walked (57:28's standing companion-file — "O you who believe — fear God and believe in His Messenger — He shall give you a double portion of His mercy and appoint for you a light in which you walk"). And the verse thereby stands as the sūrah's own epigraph for its closing movement: everything legislated in 176 verses — the orphan-trusts, the marriage-files, the justice-summit, the hypocrisy-anatomy, the covenant-pedagogy, the Christological definitions — delivered not as an anthology but as *one proof and one light*: the chapter of 4:1's watching Lord concluded by 4:174's arriving proof and descending light — the Watching of the first verse met at the last by Illumination — so that the reader stands, at the threshold of the final legal-verse, between what is proven above him and what shines before him — *a proof from your Lord, and a clear light*.

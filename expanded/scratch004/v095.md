## Sūrah al-Nisāʾ 4:95

> **Those who stay behind among the believers — except for the disadvantaged — and those who strive in the way of God with their goods and lives are not equal. God favors those who strive with their goods and their lives a degree above those who stay behind. Unto both God has promised that which is most beautiful. But He favors those who strive with a great reward above those who stay behind.**

**Expanded Commentary**

**Lā Yastawī al-Qāʿidūna min al-Muʾminīna Ghayru Ulī al-Ḍarar: The Sitters and the Strivers Weighed**

The long battle-scroll (vv. 71–94) closes its first arch with the ummah's most sobering comparative: *lā yastawī al-qāʿidūna mina al-muʾminīna ghayru ulī al-ḍarar* — "those who stay seated among the believers — except the disadvantaged — and those who strive in the way of God with their goods and their selves are not equal": *qāʿidūn* — sitters — from q-ʿ-d — to remain seated (the word chosen for those who stay across the Book's whole across the Book's whole jihād-code: 9:83's "sit, then, with those who sit"; 9:90's muʿadhdhirūn coming to sit-with-sitters — the physical posture itself turned moral diagnosis: while God summons men to *go forth*, these *sit*) — and the key-exception inserted mid-verse: *ghayru ulī al-ḍarar* — save those with *ḍarar* — affliction, disadvantage, constraint (the asbāb for the exception being one of the Book's tender scenes: al-Ṭabarī's, al-Zamakhsharī's and al-al-Qurṭubī's roll: the blind Companions — the account leads with ʿAbdullāh ibn Umm Maktūm of the tradition, or Ibn its standing name ibn Umm Maktūm — coming to the Prophet grieving that blindness kept him from the field — and the Prophet falling silent beneath the descent of the exception-clause; the verse arriving with its exemption sewn into the judgment itself) — *ḍarar* interpreted across the generations as blindness, lameness, chronic illness — and extended (in al-Rāzī's widening) to the man lacking mount, provision, or weapon — the equipment-poverty counting as bonafide affliction (cf. 9:91 — "no blame upon the weak, nor upon the sick, nor upon those who find nothing to spend, when they are sincere to God and His Messenger"; and 9:92's tear-streaked band who came seeking mounts and were turned away *crying because they could find nothing* — the Book mourning them instead of blaming them — the Quran's own canon of the weeping-exempt).

**Faḍḍala Allāhu al-Mujāhidīna... Darajatan: The Degree Word and the Two Promises**

Then the verse's royal equality-clause and its qualification — the sūrah of sun's justice of calibrated justice: *faḍḍala Allāhu al-mujāhidīna bi-amwālihim wa anfusihim ʿalā al-qāʿidīna darajatan* — "God favors the strivers with their goods and their selves over the sitters — by a degree": *darajah* — step, rank (the Book's own step-vocabulary — 6:132's "unto all are degrees according to what they have done"; 9:20's "those who believe, and migrate, and strive... are greater in rank with God"; 58:11's "God raises those among you who believe and those given knowledge by degrees") — *darajatan* left indefinite, which the classical commentators (al-Rāzī's own musing) deliberate as deliberately open: a degree — or *degrees* — for how many grades the singular-indefinite may stand plural (and the next verse's own plural *darajāt* at 4:96 answering the question immediately: not one step but *steps-many* — single darajah in this world, layered darajāt in the next) — then the ummah-consoling clause that prevents the elite from breaking the community: *wa kullan waʿada Allāhu al-ḥusnā* — "to both He has promised the Most Beautiful": *kullan* — each — the sitters *as believers* and the strivers — both are sworn to al-ḥusnā (Paradise definitive — the Book's short-form name for the Garden — with the faqīh al-Ḥasan al-Baṣrī's summary that God has not created has not made the believing sitter God-forsaken: promise-lines only differ in ranking, not in truth) — and *wa faḍḍala Allāhu al-mujāhidīna ʿalā al-qāʿidīna ajran ʿaẓīman* — "yet He favors the strivers over the sitters with a mighty wage": the schema of Islamic spiritual sociology the doctors keep citing — promise common, *rank differentiated*: fard-kifāyah logic at its root (jihād not upon every neck individually — the ummah honored by its band going forth and by its body conferring legitimacy upon it — upon both groups the promise, but upon the ransomed ones — the degree) — al-Rāzī's spiritual-diagnosis placing its permanent counter-question against every Muslim: do you belong to the band that *travels toward the rank*? or to the company that merely sits within the promise?

**Striving With Goods and Selves: The Two-Currency Striving**

The verse's war-canon set its double standard of sacrifice — *bi-amwālihim wa anfusihim* — with their wealth and their selves — giving to jihād the two classical tracks which the Sunnah then parades in hadith-roll: the wealth-striver equipping fighters (cf. the Prophet's own definition at Muslim 1884 — *"whoever equips a fighter in the way of God has himself fought; and whoever looks after his family in his absence has himself fought"*) and the self-striver himself in the saddle (with 61:11's plenary formula — "believe in God and His Messenger and strive in the way of God with your wealth and your selves — that is better for you, did you but know") — the commentators cherishing the *order* — amwāl first, anfus second — noting thereby that property-sacrifice is the cheaper gate, becoming often the preparation the established training for self-sacrifice (the soul practiced first at carrying lighter burdens, then heavier) — and the Book's standing summation phrase for this entire twofold code provided by the very sūrah by the End by 9:111's: "God has bought from the believers their selves and their wealth, that the Garden may be theirs" — the metaphysical sale-contract jurisprudence underneath every page of it: *God the Purchaser of lives-and-wealth offered at His path* — the sūrah's war-canon run through at last as one great sale-agreement — with the sitters left sitting upon unsold goods (save the afflicted, whom God exempted from the auction without penalty), and the strivers collecting their first payment tranche already: *ajran ʿaẓīman* — the mighty wage.

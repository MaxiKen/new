## Sūrah al-Nisāʾ 4:49

> **Hast thou not seen those who deem themselves pure? Rather, it is God Who purifies whomsoever He will, and they shall not be wronged so much as the thread of a date stone.**

**Expanded Commentary**

**Alladhīna Yuzakkūna Anfusahum: The Self-Certifiers at Their Glass**

After the shirk-canon (v. 48), the gaze-formula returns for the fourth time in the pericope — *alam tara ilā alladhīna yuzakkūna anfusahum* — "Hast thou not seen those who deem themselves pure?": *yuzakkūna*, from *zakāh* — purification, growth, attestation of worth — applied here in the reflexive: they *tazkiya* themselves, self-certifying — presenting their own soul's baptismal certificate (the Book always catching the pose at its starkest: 53:32's even-blunter twin — *"so ascribe not purity to yourselves; He knows best who is God-wary"* — with the schools agreeing that self-conferred holiness is here the enemy). The commentators' report-bundles (al-Qurṛṭubī, al-Ṭabarī, al-Zamakhsharī, al-Wāḥidī) fix the first addressees yet again among the Book-holders of Madinah whose self-certifications had taken run-of-an had taken the registe the recorded forms: *"we are God's children and His beloved"* (5:18's quotation); *"none shall enter the Garden save Jew or Christian"* (2:111 — the privatizing of heaven itself); and, in the finer anecdotes, the claim that their daily sins rinse off by nightfall (the errand the evening-sponge theology of the anecdote reported at Ṭabarī's hand from Ibn ʿAbbās: "our daytime sins are erased at night"), or that they absolve one another by intercession — a whole spiritual insurance-scheme issued *by themselves upon themselves* — a certificate with no bank behind it. The verse cuts the whole enterprise at the root: *self-declared purity is the very opposite of purity* — for the ground of Islam's anthropology (17:11's man "prays for evil as he prays for good"; 12:53 — *"I absolve not my own soul; the soul ever commands unto evil"* — from Joseph's own unwounded mouth, prophet though he was) is that holiness is *received verdict*, never self-writhen wreath.

**Bal Allāhu Yuzakkī Man Yashāʾ: The One Office That Certifies**

The correction arrives in the horizontal in the emphatic adversative: *bal Allāhu yuzakkī man yashāʾ* — "rather, it is God who purifies whom He wills": the *bal* — "rather" — functioning as scripture's sharp eraser-stroke (retrieval-language: the whole preceding boast x-ed out); and *yuzakkī* now in God's own commission: purification is Heaven's prerogative — only the Maker of hearts authorizes their certificates. Al-Rāzī's structure-note seizes the clause's pairing with the earlier with its twin at its own root: the same *li-man yashāʾ* — whom He wills — that attached to forgiveness at v. 48 attaches now to tazkiyah — *will* standing guard at every gate of grace, so the whole celestial economy runs on gifting, not on self-application — and thereby converting the theology into its moral application: *the only commendable pursuit is the pursuit of being among* man yashāʾ — not the manufacture of the certificate. The practical hadith-keys are needed in this very spot — the Prophet's own manuals of heart-audit:

> *"Verily in the body there is a lump of flesh: if it is sound, the whole body is sound, and if it is corrupt the whole body is corrupt — verily it is the heart."* (al-Bukhārī 52; Muslim 1599)

— soundness known only to its auditor — and its twin-ledger of warning:

> *"When you see a man given what he loves of this world while he persists in his disobedience — know that it is only istidrāj — a drawing-on." Then he recited: ...* (al-Ḥākim's channel via Abū Mūsā, with the Qur'an's own istidrāj-curses at 6:44 and 68:44 anchoring it)

— worldly success no certificate either: the believer's only comfort is the Looking-Upward audit, *wa man yashāʾ* written above its door.

**Fatīlā: The Thread of the Date-Stone — Justice to the Split-Hair**

The seal's promise comes wrapped in the Book's smallest of justice-units: *wa lā yuẓlamūna fatīlan* — "and they shall not be wronged a *fatīl*": the fatīl itself the thin white fibre-thread inside the split of the date-seed — among the smallest divisions of mote-weight the Arab eye could name (its threefold roster running through the sūrah: 4:77 and 4:124 echoing the same word; with naqīr — the pit's speck-groove — at 4:53 and 4:124, and qitmīr — the stone's film-skin — at 35:13: the classic trio of infinitesimals function as Judgment's measurement-scale) — and its employment here is two-edged with the verse's own logic: (1) those self-purifiers will not be *robbed* one thread of any true good they did (the the fearful symmetry of the Reckoning: not even their smallest real merit will be erased — v. 40's doctrine applied to the enemy too — so God remains *just even toward exact toward hypocrites and boasters*) and (2) they will not be *left* one thread's margin by which to smuggle in their fake certificates — the audit will not fuzz, downward or upward, past a thread's breadth. The commentators tie the clause steady tightly to the whole pericope (vv. 40, 44–48, 49): God wrongs nobody — the boasters' *payment self-stamped* meed boast-discount comes from *their own ledger*, never from divine trimming — and the tradition thereby nourishes at once the humble and the exact: *aim at God's tazkiyah; fear nothing of audit-loss; hope nothing of audit-loophole* — the three prepositions of the believer before Judgment standing in one date-stone thread: *lā ẓulm* — no wrong — to anyone, on any line-item, to any degree.

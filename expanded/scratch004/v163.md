## Sūrah al-Nisāʾ 4:163

> **Verily We have revealed to thee, as We revealed to Noah and the prophets after him; and We revealed to Abraham and Ishmael and Isaac and Jacob and the Tribes, and Jesus and Job and Jonah and Aaron and Solomon; and unto David We gave the Psalms —**

**Expanded Commentary**

**Innā Awḥaynā Ilayka Kamā Awḥaynā ilā Nūḥin: The Sunna of Revelation Itself**

The answer to the tablet-demanders of v. 153 now arrives in the form of a certificate-chain: *innā awḥaynā ilayka kamā awḥaynā ilā Nūḥin wa al-nabiyyīna min baʿdih* — "verily We have revealed to thee, as We revealed to Noah and the prophets after him": *kamā* — "as" — the comparative particle doing the whole theological work: the revelation to Muḥammad is not a novelty requiring new categories of proof but an *instance* of the standing divine *sunna* of waḥy — the same sending, the same character, the same chain reaching back to Noah — the patriarch-figure deliberately placed as chain-head in the standing List-verses (its register: 2:136; 3:84; 6:83-89's standing extended roster; 19:49-58; 33:7) — the first messenger of the *sent-with-a-code* class in the standing taxonomy (6:84-89; 10:71-72's standing Noah-file; 23:23; 57:26 — "We sent Noah and Abraham and placed prophethood and the scripture among their descendants"). Al-Ṭabarī's and al-Zamakhsharī's standing gloss names the verse's precise polemical address: the demanders of v. 153 had asked for a *separate Book from heaven* as though the present revelation were unevidenced — the answer: the Book they demand *is this recitation itself*, and its method of arrival — waḥy upon a prophet — is precisely the standing method by which every scripture they hold came: no new mechanism is on trial here, only the same old fidelity to what arrives. And the community-reference is double: to the People of the Book, the verse says *your whole canon proves our canon by precedent*; to the believers, it anchors the Prophet's standing within the unbroken succession (42:13's standing roster of the same continuity).

**The Named Roll: From Abraham to Solomon — One Chain, Many Files**

The verse then unrolls the standing register of names — and the commentators note the selection's arc: *wa ātaynā Ibrāhīma wa Ismāʿīla wa Isḥāqa wa Yaʿqūba wa al-asbāṭ* — "and We revealed to Abraham and Ishmael and Isaac and Jacob and the Tribes": the Abrahamic trunk-line — father, the two sons, the grandson Israel, and the twelve-tribal prophets of his seed (al-asbāṭ — the standing collective for the tribal chieftain-prophets of Jacob's house — 2:136; 2:140; 3:84; 7:160's standing tribal file) — then *wa ʿĪsā wa Ayyūba wa Yūnusa wa Hārūna wa Sulaymān* — "and Jesus and Job and Jonah and Aaron and Solomon": five names spanning five modes of prophecy — Jesus the annunciation-prophet of the Book's own dossier (vv. 156-159 above); Job the patience-prophet — the tradition's standing archive of affliction-endurance (21:83-84 — "and Job, when he cried to his Lord: verily harm has touched me — and Thou art the most merciful of the merciful — so We answered him and lifted what was upon him of harm"; 38:41-44's standing oath-file) — here named, the commentators note, at his *first explicit investiture in the register of prophethood* alongside Jonah (the standing comment-canon observing: the verse is the Book's first explicit numbering of Ayyūb and Yūnus among the prophets — later rosters at 6:83-86 enrolling them explicitly); Jonah — Yūnus — the storm-and-whale prophet, his standing register: 10:98 — "why was there not a town that believed, and its belief availed it — save the people of Jonah — when they believed, We removed from them the punishment of disgrace in the life of this world and gave them enjoyment for a while"; 37:139-148; 68:48-50 — and the standing lesson-figure of the called-yet-fleeing-yet-answered; Aaron — Hārūn — the standing minister-prophet beside Moses (19:53 — "We gave him of Our mercy his brother Aaron, a prophet"; 20:29-32 — Moses' own supplication: "appoint for me a minister from my family — Aaron, my brother — strengthen my back by him, and share him in my affair"); Solomon — Sulaymān — the king-prophet (27:15-44's standing Sulaymān-file; 38:30-40).

**Wa Ātaynā Dāwūda Zabūrā: The Psalms as the Named Scripture**

The verse closes its roll with the one title-station among them: *wa ātaynā Dāwūda zabūrā* — "and unto David We gave the Psalms": *zabūr* — the standing name of David's book (its register: 17:55 — "and unto David We gave the Zabūr"; 21:105 — "We have written in the Zabūr after the Reminder that My righteous servants shall inherit the earth") — the commentators marking the definite-singular (al-Zabūr, tied to David) as the Psalter — the hymnic scripture of prayer and praise — to be distinguished from the plural-file usage (*zubur* — scriptures, documents generally — its standing stations: 3:184; 16:44 — the *bayyināt wa al-zubur* pairing; 26:196 — "verily it is in the scriptures of the ancients"; 54:52 — "everything they did is in the scriptures"). The singular singling-out of David within the roster carries its standing curiosity: among all the named prophets, David alone receives a *named book* in this verse — and the commentators draw the standing implication the verse doubtless intends for the Madīnan demanders: even the Books you know by name — Torah to Moses (implied in v. 164's standing distinction), Psalms to David here, Gospel to Jesus in the standing register (5:46; 57:27) — arrived precisely as this one arrives: *waḥy revealed to a man* — the exception you demand (a book dropping from the sky upon no messenger) is a mechanism God's own canon nowhere employs — even the *tablets of Moses*, the one nearly-physical exception your file prizes, came framed within prophethood-and-conversation (v. 164's standing sequel: kalām) rather than as substitute for it. The verse thereby converts a name-list into an argument: the long succession — Noah, the Abrahamic line, the tribal prophets, Jesus, Job, Jonah, Aaron, Solomon, David — one sunna of revelation, one divine practice — and Muḥammad enrolled within it not at its edge but at its center of gravity: *as We revealed to Noah and the prophets after him.*

**The Standing Posture Before the Roll: Belief in All, Preference in Rank**

The commentators close the verse's treatment with its standing balancing-note — the two cords by which Muslim prophetology holds: (1) assent: each name on the roll is binding on the believer's faith — 2:285 — "we make no distinction between any of His messengers" — no messenger refused; (2) honor-gradation: 2:253's standing register in place — "those are the messengers — We preferred some of them over others — among them one to whom God spoke — and He raised some of them in degrees" — the preference of station acknowledged without jealousy or refusal — v. 164 immediately exhibiting the principle with the very name the People of the Book hold dearest: Moses — to whom the Book gives, in the words of the next verse, the standing distinction the tradition ever after inscribed in his honorific: Kalīm Allāh.

## Sūrah al-Nisāʾ 4:170

> **O mankind! The Messenger has come to you with the truth from your Lord; so believe — it is better for you. And if you disbelieve — then unto God belongs whatsoever is in the heavens and the earth; and God is ever Knowing, Wise.**

**Expanded Commentary**

**Yā Ayyuhā al-Nāsu Qad Jāʾakumu al-Rasūlu: The Universal Address After the Particular Indictments**

The chapter's final movement widens its address to the widest possible circle: *yā ayyuhā al-nāsu qad jāʾakumu al-rasūlu bi-al-ḥaqqi min rabbikum* — "O mankind! The Messenger has come to you with the truth from your Lord": *yā ayyuhā al-nās* — the standing universal vocative of the Book (its stations across the chapter's own architecture: 4:1 — "O mankind — fear your Lord..."; 4:174 below repeating; the standing register across the Book: 2:21; 2:168; 10:57; 22:1; 22:5; 22:49; 22:73; 31:33; 35:3; 35:5; 35:15; 49:13) — the commentators marking the widening sequence of the chapter's addresses: the believers (4:19; 4:29; 4:43; 4:59; 4:71; 4:135-136; 4:144), the People of the Book (153-171), and now *mankind* — the ceiling of address — every tribunal of the chapter (orphan-file, family-file, justice-file, hypocrisy-file, covenant-file, Jesus-file) folded into a single announcement delivered to *all*: the Messenger's arrival — *qad jāʾakum* — the perfect tense declaring accomplished advent — "has come": the sending not awaited but completed; and the payload defined: *bi-al-ḥaqqi min rabbikum* — "with the truth from your Lord": al-ḥaqq — the standing name of the whole cargo: the revelation itself and the whole fabric of truth it disciplines (its standing register: 2:119; 2:147; 2:176; 5:48; 5:83; 10:47's 17:105 — "We sent thee with the truth"; 13:1; 34:6; 39:2; 39:41; 42:17 — the several standing stations of the rasūl-bil-ḥaqq complex) — and the possessive *rabbikum* — "your Lord" — anchoring the address in the first covenant: the One addressing is not a foreign God but *their* Lord — the standing argument the chapter opened with at 4:1 (rabbukum alladhī khalaqakum) closed with it here: the Lord who created now addresses through His Messenger.

**Fa-Āminū Khayran Lakum: Belief as Self-Interest, Not as Charity to Heaven**

Then the imperative with its standing self-referential motivation: *fa-āminū khayran lakum* — "so believe — it is better for you": *khayran lakum* — from 2:110's standing its standing formula-stations: 2:54 — "turn to your Maker... that is better for you"; 2:110 — "whatever good you send before you for your souls, you shall find it with God"; 5:35; 5:45-46's better-files; 66:5; 73:20) — the commentators (al-Ṭabarī's standing gloss; al-Rāzī's scholastic note expanding) drawing the verse's standing theological reordering of motivation: belief is legislated not as tribute offered upward but as benefit accruing downward — *better for you*: faith assigned to the believer's own ledger of interests — the obedience of the creature an act of self-investment rather than a favor to the Creator — a principle the chapter has already embedded in its ownership-files (4:126; 4:131): everything is His already, so nothing one "gives" God enriches Him; every virtue is rescue of one's own soul (2:286's standing prayer closing the standing pure-theology: "our Lord — take us not to task if we forget or err"; and the standing qudsī-file at Muslim 2577 — "O My servants — you will not attain to harming Me... nor to benefiting Me... it is but your actions that I register for you — then I requite you — whoever finds good, let him praise God — and whoever finds other, let him blame none but himself").

**Wa In Takfurū: The Third Ownership-Declaration — Indifference in Majesty**

Then the disbelief-clause — in the chapter's own familiar indifference-construction: *wa in takfurū fa-inna li-llāhi mā fī al-samāwāti wa mā fī al-arḍ* — "and if you disbelieve — then unto God belongs whatsoever is in the heavens and the earth": the ownership-cadence now tolling its fourth time in the chapter's closing register (its stations herein: 4:126; 4:131; 4:132; and again 4:170-171 — the final verse of the Jesus-file ending with the same cadence) — and the commentators marking the device at its standing summation: your disbelief subtracts nothing from the Estate; your refusal of a deed of property does not unwrite the deed — the standing theological point pressed by al-Rāzī at these clauses: the verse answers the human instinct to imagine that refusal injures the Refused — the Book counter-announcing the standing non-dependence of the Refused Upon the acceptance of tenants: the heavens and earth and all they hold remain His, pledged to His account, traveling to His appointment — with or without the confessor's consent. Its standing Book-kin: 4:131 above; 14:2 — "God — unto Him belongs whatsoever is in the heavens and the earth"; 16:52; 31:26; 34:24; 39:38; 53:31 — the standing estate-roll; and the standing consolation-parallel for the Prophet of "We know indeed that what they say grieves thee..." — the disbelief of the addressed never imperiling the estate nor the address.

**Wa Kāna Allāhu ʿAlīman Ḥakīman: The Knower-and-Wise Seal**

The verse seals with the standing Name-pair of calm omniscience: *wa kāna Allāhu ʿalīman ḥakīman* — "and God is ever Knowing, Wise": *ʿalīm* — the Knower — matching the verse's standing concern: the arrival of the truth *known* perfectly by its Sender, the hidden reception of that truth by every individual hearer *known* without remainder (the standing sūrah-file of this Name-pair — 4:11; 4:12; 4:17; 4:24; 4:26; 4:92; 4:104; 4:111; the recurring ʿalīm-ḥakīm stations); and *ḥakīm* — the Wise — matching the standing design: truth sent as guidance, belief recommended as benefit, disbelief answered with the estate-declaration — every motion of the dance within Wisdom's choreography. The commentators (Ibn Kathīr's standing filing) read vv. 170–171 together as the chapter's own summary-pair: v. 170 to all mankind — "believe; it is better for you"; v. 171 to the People of the Book — "exaggerate not; the Messiah is God's servant and word and spirit; believe; refrain — it is better for you" — the same *khayran lakum* in both — the Book's standing motive-appeal sounded at both universal and particular registers — and then 4:171's own close cadences the chapter's last legal-echo: "unto Him belongs whatsoever is in the heavens and the earth — and God suffices as a Guardian" — the wakīl-sufficiency of 4:132 returning at the chapter's far shore: the estate, the guidance, the guardianship — all His — "and mankind" addressed at last to choose: to believe (better for them) or to refuse (weightless upon Him) — the sūrah of the Watcher keeping, to its final movement, the standing freedom of the human deed and the standing completeness of the divine account.

## Sūrah al-Nisāʾ 4:143

> **Wavering between this and that — belonging neither to these nor to those. And whomsoever God leads astray, thou shalt never find for him a way.**

**Expanded Commentary**

**Mudhabdhabīna Bayna Dhālika: The Physics of the Wavering Self**

The anatomy of the hypocrite's posture receives its definitive word — one of the rarest and most expressive in the Book: *mudhabdhabīna bayna dhālika lā ilā hāʾulāʾi wa lā ilā hāʾulāʾ* — "wavering between this and that — belonging neither to these nor to those": *mudhabdhab* — from the quadriliteral root dh-b-dh-b — a reduplicated formation whose very sound enacts its meaning: the dithering, seesawing back-and-forth of something that cannot settle — a word-form the linguists note is nearly onomatopoeic, the doubled syllable swinging like the pendulum it describes (its standing uniqueness in the Book: the root appears nowhere else in the Qur'ān — reserved as the one-word portrait of the hypocrite's inner weather). *Bayna dhālika* — "between this and that" — the occupant of the middle claimed by neither side: *lā ilā hāʾulāʾi* — not toward these (the believers), *wa lā ilā hāʾulāʾ* — nor toward those (the disbelievers). Al-Zamakhsharī's standing gloss: they waver between faith and unbelief under the sway of Satan and their own passions — belonging fully to no covenant, and therefore owned fully by no community: the standing existential penalty of the hedged life — to be the trusted native of no country. The commentators mark the diagnostic precision: the verse does not call them disbelievers (the sharīʿah must continue treating them by their profession) nor believers (reality refuses the title) — but names them by their *motion*: dhabdhabah — the standing theological category for those whose religion is an instrument panel of readings rather than a settled direction. Al-Rāzī, surveying the surah-dossier of these passages, registered the standing judgment that flows from the whole anatomy: hypocrisy is a state more evil than bare disbelief — the liar compounding unbelief with fraud, espionage, and belittling — the moral instinct the tradition knows from every community: the open adversary may yet be honored; the smiling saboteur can only be mourned — and the Book itself will ratify the comparative verdict at v. 145: the *lowest* depth for the waverers.

**Wa Man Yuḍlili Allāhu: The Leading-Astray of God and Its Schools**

Then the phrase that anchors one of the standing debates of Muslim theology: *wa man yuḍlili Allāhu fa-lan tajida lahu sabīlan* — "and whomsoever God leads astray, thou shalt never find for him a way": iḍlāl — leading astray — attributed to God Himself, in a verse-cluster the commentators treat with admitted gravity (its standing register: 4:88 above in this very dossier — "would you guide him whom God has led astray?"; 7:186 — "whomsoever God leads astray, no guide has he — and He leaves them in their insolence, wandering blind"; 13:33 — "whomsoever God leads astray has no guide"; 39:23; 39:36; 40:33 — "whomsoever God leads astray has no guide"; 42:46 — "whomsoever God leads astray has no way"; 18:17 — "whomsoever God guides is the guided — and whomsoever He leads astray, thou shalt never find for him a guiding protector"; 2:7's standing seal-passage: "God has sealed their hearts"); and the standing school-readings: the Muʿtazilite position — iḍlāl as God's *withdrawal of favor* (luṭf) and active guidance, a withholding imposed *as punishment* upon prior disobedience: God abandons the obstinate to the waywardness they chose — astray-labels marking the consequence, not the cause; the Ashʿarite position — affirming this punitive withholding while also upholding God's sovereign freedom to mislead whom He wills, guidance and misguidance alike lying within divine creation (16:93's standing statement — "God leads astray whom He wills and guides whom He wills" — alongside 14:4 — "He leads astray whom He wills and guides whom He wills — and He is the Mighty, the Wise"); and the eschatological gloss — some readings locating the "leading astray" of the confirmed hard-hearted in the Hereafter itself (17:72’s standing: "whosoever was blind in this life shall be blind in the Hereafter and further astray from the way"). Ibn Taymiyyah's standing reconciliation-order across the Book's register: the Book never says God misleads the *seeker* — every "leading-astray" verse lands upon one who first sold his way (2:16 — "those are the ones who purchased error at the price of guidance — so their commerce profited not"); divine iḍlāl is the sealing of a human purchase, never the prevention of a human request — the standing rule the commentators distill: man chooses the direction; God authors the consequence.

**Fa-Lan Tajida Lahu Sabīlan: The Prophet's Helplessness as the Verdict's Seal**

The closing clause turns to the Prophet himself — *fa-lan tajida lahu sabīlan* — "thou shalt never find for him a way": *lan* — never — and the addressee the most powerful intercessor of the community: if even the Messenger cannot unseal the sealed heart — cannot locate a road back for the fixed waverer — then the verdict is not social (excommunication by men) but providential (a closure enacted above the sky of every advocate). Its standing Book-kin gathers the theme: 28:56 — "thou guidest not whom thou lovest — but God guides whom He wills"; 4:88 above — "would you guide whom God leads astray?"; 7:186; 13:33's register. The pastoral point the commentators draw for the community: the wavering-state is not static; the dhabdhabah-pendulum, given time, slows toward the heavier side — and the side it settles upon is the side the soul keeps feeding. Hence the dossier's exact placement of hope: vv. 145–146 below will announce the lowest depth — and immediately legislate the exception: "save those who repent and make amends and hold fast to God and devote their religion entirely to God" — the pendulum, while the heart still beats, retains the freedom to swing *toward* the side it has so far only visited. The dhabdhabah-portrait thereby closes its own file with the standing admonition the spiritual commentators inscribe beneath it: lukewarmness is not innocence; it is a direction — and every direction, continued, arrives.

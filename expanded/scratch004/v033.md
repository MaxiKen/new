## Sūrah al-Nisāʾ 4:33

> **To each We have appointed heirs for what parents and kinsfolk leave. Those to whom you have given your oath, give them their share. Truly God is Witness over all things.**

**Expanded Commentary**

**Wa li-Kullin Jaʿalnā Mawālī: Everyone Has His Written Heirs**

The inheritance-architecture opens its last aperture — a verse in the cipher of ancient alliances: *wa li-kullin jaʿalnā mawālī mimmā taraka al-wālidāni wa-l-aqrabūn* — "and for every one We appointed *mawālī* — successor-kin — of what parents and relations leave": *mawālī* — plural of *mawlā*, from the copious root *w-l-y* of nearness, whose branches give the law its vocabulary of heirs, protectors, freedmen, and allies — arrived in this clause at the newest seat of succession: *your heirs are by Us installed*, kin seated by the kinship-bond God assesses — with the commentators of the schools unanimous in the clause's measure: a declarative of the *kin-based inheritance system* now whole — the grave opened, the bonds of blood (and marriage — v. 12's spouses) alone called to the seat — every person born into a written line of succession that no club, no tribe, no friendship-of-oath may rearrange. The al-Qurṭubī's jurists at this sentence never tire of marveling at the clause's floorwork: probate ceased with one line to be a *tribal-revenge* event and became *identity's own settling* — son and daughter, parent and sibling, by God's appointment of each to each.

**Alladhīna ʿAqadat Aymānukum: The Oath-Companies of Ancient Arabia**

Then the verse's difficult second half — *wa-lladhīna ʿaqadat aymānukum fa-ātūhum naṣībahum* — "and those with whom your oaths have compacted — give them their share": the *ʿaqd* of the right-hand — the hand-clasp contract — the pact-brotherhood of the Jāhiliyya and the early days, in which two men — or a man and an adopted stranger — would swear inheritance-rights mutually: *"your blood is mine, your estate is mine, your avenger is my sword"* — the ḥalīf-inheritance institution, standing beside the Prophet's own *mu'ākhāh*, the brothering between Emigrant and Helper in Madinah's first year (the brothering-reports of al-Bukhārī and Muslim in many channels: ʿAbd al-Raḥmān ibn ʿAwf paired with Saʿd ibn al-Rabīʿ and refusing Saʿd's offer of half his household, "may God bless you in your family and wealth, show me the market" — al-Bukhārī 2049 — the mu'ākhāh had at its first stage carried a real succession-claim). The commentators of the occasions agree that this verse addresses those *oath-inheritances*, and its ruling depends upon when it fell beside 8:75's *ulū al-arḥām*:

**From Sworn Shares to Blood Shares: The Sunna's Sealing of the Grave's Ledger**

The most weighed doctrine — al-Ṭabarī's and Ibn Kathīr's consolidated voice — holds v. 33 *superseded* in its oath-clause by the definitive ring of 8:75 — *"possessors of wombs are nearer among them in the Book of God"* — inheritance finally confined to the relatives-by-blood: the pact-inheritance of the early brotherhood lapsing with the community's consolidation, so that Saʿd's brother ʿAbd al-Raḥmān took no estate at his death but what kin-law gave. The bridge-readings the tradition also preserved stand beside: (1) the *old* pacts made before the sealing are *honored* — the verse commanding fulfillment of the prior compacts while new ones are barred (Ibn Kathīr's balance-note, with the Shiʿi authorities likewise distinguishing past-honor from future-license); (2) the *share* assigned the oath-companions is read as a share *from the bequest-third* — the testamentary free-third any man may still volunteer to non-heirs — or else as the blood-brotherhood's duties themselves: *support, counsel, vengeance*, the *naṣīb* of alliance honored now by things richer than gold (al-Qurṭubī's reconciliation, the road by which the schools interpret the verse among the *muḥkam* verses while the exact money-right sleeps). Either way the moral-order announced by verses 33-and-8:75 stands forever: *at death, kin come before companions*; at life, the oath-brother was brother indeed. The emigrant-binding did not disintegrate with the grave-law: it became *honor without probate*, affection and *naṣr* without title, the purest brotherhood-money free.

**Inna Allāha Kāna ʿAlā Kulli Shayʾin Shahīdan: The Last Seal of the Grave-Arch**

The verse closes the whole probate-arch (vv. 7–13's seats, v. 33's alliances) with the same Witness-attribute the marriage-pericope's clauses also carried: *inna Allāha kāna ʿalā kulli shayʾin shahīdā* — "God is ever Witness over all things": the Shahīd-seal where the sūrah's recurring *shahādah*-vocabulary enforces an audit of the very materials this law cleans: *kinship*, the making of whose claims is a thing only God sees whole: actual mothers' lineage, the family-tree of men, whether the hidden claimant grieves or the cloak of the pact-brother has been honored true. The commentators of the pericope remark that no other *attribute* could better close the marriage-plus-inheritance complex than the attribute of *witnessing*, for both bodies of law live and die by what occurred in *un-filmed* rooms — the wedding bed, the deathbed — thin as walls that only God sees through. In this the reader now stands at the sūrah's great hinge: the *family's* inner laws (vv. 1–33) have been sealed; the *household's* daily weather — the qawwāmūn-universe of breadward duty, arbitration, and iḥsān (vv. 34–36) — comes forward next, precisely in the same spirit: laws that must be *lived, unseen*, under the eye of the Watching One.

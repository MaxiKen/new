## Sūrah al-Nisāʾ 4:153

> **The People of the Book ask thee to bring down upon them a Book from heaven. Yet they asked Moses for a greater thing than that, for they said: "Show us God openly" — so the thunderbolt seized them for their wrongdoing. Then they took up the calf, even after the clear proofs had come to them; yet We pardoned that, and We gave Moses a manifest authority.**

**Expanded Commentary**

**Yasʾaluka Ahlu al-Kitāb: The Old Question at a New Door**

The final movement of the sūrah opens upon its addressed community with a demand: *yasʾaluka ahlu al-kitābi an tunazzila ʿalayhim kitāban min al-samāʾ* — "the People of the Book ask thee to bring down upon them a Book from heaven": the commentators (al-Ṭabarī's chains; reports naming specific leaders among the Jewish scholars of Madīnah) specify its intended shape: a physical writ — an inscribed tablet or sealed document descending visibly from the sky, akin to the tablets of Moses (28:48's standing parallel: "why was he not given the like of what Moses was given?"; and 17:93's standing Meccan twin from the idolaters: "or thou bring down upon us a book that we may read"). The demand is forensic in costume — "give us documentary proof of prophethood" — but the Book immediately strips the costume by narrating *the same people's precedent*: they had once asked Moses for something *greater* — *fa-qad saʾalū Mūsā akbara min dhālik* — "yet they asked Moses for a greater thing than that": the comparative exposing the demand's rank within their own history of demands: they had already outstripped it — and the greater demand had already shown what such demands are worth as instruments of belief.

**Arinā Allāha Jahrahan: The Demand to See — and the Thunderbolt's Answer**

The greater demand is quoted verbatim: *fa-qālū arinā Allāha jahratan* — "for they said: show us God openly": *jahrahan* — openly, with unveiled eyes (the same root as v. 148's jahr — publicness): vision demanded as the entrance-fee of belief — 2:55's standing twin recounting the explicit condition-currency of the demand: "and when you said: O Moses — we shall never believe thee until we see God openly — whereupon the thunderbolt seized you while you looked on; then We raised you up after your death, that perhaps you might give thanks": the standing sabab-reading (al-Ṭabarī, al-Qurṭubī) locating the petitioners among the seventy elders Moses selected for the covenant-mīqāt — the story in the fullest register at 7:155's seventy ("And Moses chose of his people seventy men for Our appointment") — the elite delegation itself perishing in the demand's own answer: *fa-akhadhathumu al-ṣāʿiqatu bi-ẓulmihim* — "so the thunderbolt seized them for their wrongdoing": *ṣāʿiqah* — the standing punishment-word of the skies (2:55; 41:13 — "warned them of a thunderbolt like the thunderbolt of ʿĀd and Thamūd"; 41:17; 51:44) — with the causal-clause *bi-ẓulmihim* — "for their wrongdoing" — marking the seizure not as refusal of a lawful wish but 7as verdict upon its arrogance: the demand to see God made not as thirst (Moses' own *arini* at 7:143 — "my Lord — show me, that I may look upon Thee" — answered gently with the mountain's collapse and "thou shalt not see Me") but as ultimatum: *we will not believe until* — the grammar of extortion, not of longing — 2:260's standing counter-case clinched the rule: Abraham's "show me how Thou givest life to the dead" — asked from within belief ("that my heart may be at rest") — was granted without thunder. The commentators gather the standing jurisprudence of the three cases: the desire for vision is of itself neither sin nor right; it is weighed by *intention* — Moses the beloved asking as a lover (answered with pedagogy), Abraham the friend asking for heart's rest (answered with demonstration), the seventy asking as negotiators (answered with the bolt).

**Thumma Ittakhadhū al-ʿIjla: The Golden Calf After the Clear Proofs**

The verse then compresses the standing chronicle of relapse: *thumma ittakhadhū al-ʿijla min baʿdi mā jāʾathumu al-bayyināt* — "then they took up the calf, even after the clear proofs had come to them": the standing calf-file (2:51-54; 2:92-93; 7:148-156; 20:86-97) cited here for its exact pedagogic wedge — *min baʿdi mā jāʾathumu al-bayyināt* — after the clear proofs: al-Ṭabarī's gloss reading the bayyināt as precisely the thunderbolt-and-resurrection itself (they had *seen* — their own bodies dying and being raised — what no demand could make clearer: that the God they must worship is not an object for the eyes); al-Qurṭubī widening the register to the full Moses-miracle inventory (the sea's parting; 17:101's standing nine signs; 28:36). The commentators mark the dark lesson the sūrah intends its Madīnan audience to absorb: spectacular proof does not manufacture faith — a generation that watched thunderbolts and parted seas could still, within weeks, worship a calf cast from earrings: *proof* is God's part; *response* is man's — the demanders of 4:153's present tense being addressed through their own ancestors' file: you ask for a book from heaven? Your fathers received thunder, seas, and tablets — and made a calf.

**Fa-ʿAfawnā ʿan Dhālik: Pardoned — and Authority Confirmed**

Yet the verse descends — astonishingly, after thunderbolt and calf — to the standing note of mercy that the commentators prize as the verse's own portal: *fa-ʿafawnā ʿan dhālika wa ātaynā Mūsā sulṭānan mubīnan* — "yet We pardoned that, and We gave Moses a manifest authority": *ʿafw* — the effacing pardon (v. 149's Name-file immediately above: *ʿafūwwan qadīran* — Pardoning, Powerful — the sūrah's own pairing of pardon-with-power here displayed in history: God pardons the very generation He could justly have erased) — 2:52's standing record of the pardon ("then We pardoned you after that — that perhaps you might give thanks"); and the second half restores the office the rebellion had impugned: *sulṭānan mubīnan* — a manifest authority — glossed two ways in the standing readings (al-Qurṭubī's and al-Ṭabarī's: the proof and powers by which Moses' prophethood stood manifest; al-Zamakhsharī's complementary: the concrete *authority* over the Israelites by which he could command their repentance and enforce the atonement of 2:54 — "then turn to your Maker and slay yourselves" — authority surviving their rebellion: pardoned people, reaffirmed prophet). The verse thereby delivers to its present demanders its folded answer: they demanded a tablet; the verse gives them a history — in which (a) demands for visible proof have already exceeded their present request and been answered with consequences; (b) the proofs given produced — in unrenewed hearts — calves, not belief; (c) pardons, not bolts, form the standing divine default toward those who return; and (d) God's authority rests upon His prophets *regardless of* who demands paperwork — the Book from heaven they seek having in fact already descended: it is the very Book in which their question is recorded — the Qur'ān itself the tunazzila-ʿalayhim kitāban standing fulfilled, the demand answered by the demand's being written into revelation — the standing irony the commentators savor: they asked that a Book be brought down; a Book was being brought down upon them *as they asked*.

## Sūrah al-Nisāʾ 4:152

> **But those who believe in God and His messengers, and make no distinction between any of them — unto those He shall give their rewards. And God is ever Forgiving, Merciful.**

**Expanded Commentary**

**Wa Alladhīna Āmanū bi-llāhi wa Rusulih: The Counter-Registry of the Complete**

The Book turns its face from the distinction-makers to their opposites — the *wa*-adversative swinging the ledger: *wa alladhīna āmanū bi-llāhi wa rusulih* — "but those who believe in God and His messengers": the counter-formula stated first in its standing positive — unqualified, inclusive assent: God *and* His messengers — no parenthesis of exception admitted between the Sender and His sent. The commentators mark the echo-architecture reaching back through the sūrah: v. 136 above had erected the archipelago of faith ("believe in God and His Messenger, and the Book sent down upon His Messenger, and the Book sent down aforetime"); v. 150-151 had charged those who break the archipelago as "disbelievers in truth"; v. 152 now files the island-keepers — those whose assent follows the contiguity entirely given. And the commentators' standing note on register-placement: the chapter of law and hypocrisy ends its doctrinal section precisely here — in creed-completeness stated as reward-worthiness — the sūrah's several audiences (believers, hypocrites, People of the Book) all addressed finally in the same creedal currency: whoever you are, the question is the same — whole faith, or the art of the partial?

**Wa Lam Yufarriqū Bayna Aḥadin Minhum: The Non-Distinction as Creed**

The qualification-clause seals the condition: *wa lam yufarriqū bayna aḥadin minhum* — "and make no distinction between any of them": *aḥadin* — "any one" — the indefinite-singular in a negation carrying the Book's absolute: not one exception — not between God and messenger (v. 150's first severance), not between messenger and messenger (its second): the standing creed-text of 2:285 sounded once more as this verse's own interpretive anthem ("the Messenger believes in what has been sent down to him from his Lord — and the believers: each one believes in God and His angels and His books and His messengers — we make no distinction between any of His messengers — and they say: we hear and we obey — Thy forgiveness, our Lord — and unto Thee is the journeying"); with 2:136 and 3:84 beside it (the standing triad of prophecy-inclusiveness: "say: we believe in God and in what was revealed to us and revealed to Abraham, Ishmael, Isaac, Jacob, and the Tribes, and in what was given to Moses and Jesus and the prophets from their Lord — we make no distinction between any of them — and unto Him we submit"). The commentators (al-Rāzī's standing ladder-note) mark that the clause describes not mere non-discrimination but *positive theological posture*: the believer receives prophets as one receives chapters of a single Book — differences of tongues, codes, and eras standing as variety within a unity, never as warrant to strike chapters — 35:24's standing census of mercy ("there is no community but a warner has passed among them"); 10:47; 16:36; 21:7-8 — the testimony of God's wide mercy: no nation left unaddressed, no messenger a rival to another — all witnesses to the one Lord (11:50-60's standing sequence: Hūd among ʿĀd, Ṣāliḥ among Thamūd — each saying "O my people — worship God — you have no god but He").

**Fa-Ulāʾika Sawfa Yuʾtīhim Ujūrahum: The Rewards of the Undivided**

Then the promise-formula in its standing future-form: *fa-ulāʾika sawfa yuʾtīhim ujūrahum* — "unto those He shall give their rewards": *ujūr* — the plural of *ajr* — wages, rewards — pluralized deliberately (the standing note of the language-commentators: the plural registers the multiplicity of what full faith earns — each article believed, each messenger received, each undisputed truth submitting a separate wage-slip) — and *sawfa* — the future-particle with its standing note of solemnity: *shall* — assured but deferred — the reward pledged upon God's own calendar. The standing promise-file of the Book assembles beside it: its register: 4:100 — the emigrant to God "his wage has fallen upon God"; 18:30 — "verily those who believe and do righteous deeds — We shall not waste the wage of him who did good work"; the grand wage-list of 33:35 for the men and women of every virtue; "that God may acquit them of the worst of what they did and pay them their wage with the best of what they used to do"; "theirs is a wage unfailing" — *ajr ghayr mamnūn* — the standing superlative: wages without interruption, reduction, or grudge.

**Wa Kāna Allāhu Ghafūran Raḥīman: The Names Against the Figures of the Ledger**

The verse seals with the sūrah's most familiar Name-pair — *wa kāna Allāhu ghafūran raḥīman* — "and God is ever Forgiving, Merciful": the standing seal of the sūrah's family-file (4:23; 4:24; 4:96; 4:100; 4:106) now sealing the creed-file — and the commentators' standing question — "why Forgiving where the verse describes *success*?" — answers itself: because the promise of reward is issued to humankind *as found*: even the undivided believer arrives freighted with slips and debts; his ujūr rest not on a perfect account but on a Forgiver standing between his ledger and his wage — and even the erstwhile distinction-maker, repenting into wholeness (the dossier's standing exception-pattern — 4:146's repentant hypocrites now mirrored by v. 152's reachable selective believers), finds the door named Ghafūr-Raḥīm already open at the archive of the verdicts. The triad vv. 150–152 thus stands as the sūrah's standing creed-triptych: the distinguished-and-disbelieving (150), the verdict with its prepared wage of disgrace (151), and the undivided with their pending rewards under Forgiveness-Mercy (152) — and the verse-number's own neatness (152 = v. 136's archipelago + the 16 intervening verses of hypocrisy-anatomy) marking how the sūrah's long shadow-section on the false and the partial closes finally in its two gentlest Names — as if the Book, having spoken so long of the basement and the humiliation, would now leave the reader at the doorway of the Forgiving, the Merciful — before turning, in the address to come (v. 153 onward), to the People of the Book with their standing questions and the sūrah's standing answers.

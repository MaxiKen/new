## Sūrah al-Nisāʾ 4:104

> **Slacken not in the pursuit of these people. If you are suffering, verily they suffer even as you suffer, while you hope from God that for which they do not hope. Surely God is Knowing, Wise.**

**Expanded Commentary**

**Lā Tahinū fī Ibtighāʾi al-Qawm: No Listlessness at the Chase**

The sūrah's Uḥud-cycle closes with the charge addressed to a wounded army: *lā tahinū fī ibtighāʾi al-qawm* — "grow not feeble in the pursuit of this people": *lā tahinū* — from w-h-n — slackness, losing-of-back languor (its battlefield lexicon the Book reserves for the aftermath-threshold: 3:146's own adjacent lesson — "how many a prophet with whom a mighty band fought — and they faint they flagged not for what struck them in the way of God, and they weakened not, neither did they humble themselves — and God loves the patient") — *ibtighāʾ* — seeking-pursuit (from b-gh-y — the track-and-reach of a quest — war-craft given the grammar-of-seeking: the enemy now run-analyze now objectively a target sought, not terror feared) — and *al-qawm* — *this* people — the demonstrative naming the particular foe (the standing asbāb the commentators preserve — al-Ṭabarī, al-Jaṣṣāṣ: after Uḥud's wounding, the Prophet urging the march in pursuit of Abū Sufyān's departing force — exhorted on foot himself, the surrounded the camp bruised, the loss-of-seventy fresh — and answering the murmur of fatigue with the doctrine spelled out below) — the Book's own syntax: pursuit not optional but *no-lahinī command lā-tahinū-command* — slackening at the moment of enemy withdrawal potentially breeding reversals (the sīrah-logic: the force that can re-chase can re-shape the peace-map — demoralized withdrawal unpursued teaches the enemy that your pain has overturned your will).

**In Takūnū Taʾlamūna Fa-Innahum Yaʾlamūna kamā Taʾlamūn: The Equal-Pain Equation**

Then the verse's celebrated therapy-of-suffering — its psychology spelled as logic: *in takūnū taʾlamūna fa-innahum yaʾlamūna kamā taʾlamūna* — "if you suffer pain — verily they suffer pain as you suffer pain": *taʾlamūna* — from a-l-m — pain (the Book's own verb repeated word-for-word on both sides — the symmetry itself the lesson) — and the object of the sūrah of the commentators' standing expansion: how the wounded at Uḥud bled — sword-cuts, broken teeth, slain comrades — while the Makkans withdrew believing themselves victors — heaven's ledger announcing: *they, too, left bodies upon your field — their wounds sting just as yours do — your after-shock was shared with them at close of strike* (with al-al-Qurṭubī's genuin al-al-Qurṭubī's standing reading among the justice-canon: no stroke lands upon your camp without its echo also landing upon theirs — the war-pain of one side never being the absolute proof of the other side's peace) — the verse thereby feeding the believers the first great counter-factual of spiritual military lore: *never measure the enemy's morale by your own aches* — pain is universally military; what divides camps is *what the pain is purchased for*.

**Wa Tarjūna mina Allāhi Mā Lā Yarjūn: The Hope-Advantage, the War's Decisive Commodity**

Then the asymmetrical counter the discrepancy that ends the argument: *wa tarjūna mina Allāhi mā lā yarjūna* — "while you hope from God that for which they do not hope": the sūrah's own earlier the tradition classifying it precisely: the believing army fights *expecting from God* three treasuries the enemy is incapable of even conceiving: (1) reward in the next world the other-home ledger: paradise upon the slain — 3:169's alive-with-their-Lord doctrine the doctrine at 3:169: "count not those slain in God's way as dead — nay — living with their Lord, provided for"; 3:157's "surely forgiveness from God and mercy are better than whatever they amass"; the renamed the same hope-treasury of v. 74's two-wage clause — and (2) victory-and-divine-support even in this world — the niṣrat-of-nasr the promised naṣr — Ibn Kathīr's own note: 24:55's "God has promised those who believe and work righteousness that He will make them successors in the land"; 61:13's "help from God and a near victory"; 110:1's "when the help of God comes, and the victory" — and (3) the standing engine the dignity-engine: strength through hope — the argued-from the hope-advantage itself announced by Ibn Kathīr and al-Jaṣṣāṣ as a field-advantage: *the fighting man with heaven to hope from is harder to break than the fighting man with only spoils to hope for* — the army whose paymaster is God keeps its edge whether the day ends in plunder or in graves (its classic counter-image being the idolater's: he fights for the world's ledger alone — when the ledger bleeds red he collapses) — and the seal lands with the twin-audits the attribute-seal closing the war-canon: *wa kāna Allāhu ʿalīman ḥakīmā* — "God is Knowing, Wise": Knowing — the One who gauges every drop of suffering (the Ledger-holder of both camps' pains); Wise — the One who proportions each campaign's outcome to its covenant-condition (the standing structure of the entire war-block — vv. 71–104 — finally summed at its last seal: precaution — obedience — referral of rumors — covenant — treaty — battle-order — travel-rites — war-prayer — and then this last field-psychology — *all of it authored by the Knowing, never by accident; all of it dispensed by the Wise, never by spite*) — with the tradition's own standing teacher-line upon v. 104 passed down since al- since al-Ṭabarī: *fatigue reverses nothing; pain is shared but hope divides — and the wise keep marching*.

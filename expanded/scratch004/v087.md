## Sūrah al-Nisāʾ 4:87

> **God, there is no god but He. He will surely gather you all unto the Day of Resurrection, about which there is no doubt. And who is truer than God in speech?**

**Expanded Commentary**

**Allāhu Lā Ilāha illā Hū: The Creed-Anchor Amid the Laws**

Between the peace-protocol of v. 86 and the two-party verdict-battle of v. 88, the sūrah plants its bald creed-obelisk: *Allāh — lā ilāha illā hū* — "God — there is no god but He": the kalimah placed in absolute form, the detached pronoun carrying the whole of Divinity in this its most compressed formulation (its family-station across the Book, in the same emphatic arrangement: 2:255's Āyat al-Kursī opening; 3:2; 3:18's like attestation of oneness in the selfsame early-Madinan tract) — and the structure-critics note its function here: the sūrah's long string of communal duties and battle-decrees pauses for one breath — and resumes after — as though heaven itself breathed once through the law-text — recalling every reader from secondary matters to the First Thing, the One God — before descending into the hypocrite-verdicts, where men *talk* of God endlessly without knowing Him. Al-Rāzī's small precision catches the theology in the word-order: *Allāh* first, as the proper noun; then the entire monotheistic sentence is reduced to a pronoun — the whole of Divinity stated by *He* alone, no predicates added — huwiyya-language in its purest formulation: everything else in existence is describable by titles; Him — by *He*.

**La-Yajmaʿannakum ilā Yawmi al-Qiyāmati Lā Rayba Fīh: The Gathering Oath**

Then the resurrection-oath — doubled-emphatic: *la-yajmaʿannakum ilā yawmi al-qiyāmati lā rayba fīh* — "He will surely gather you unto the Day of Resurrection, wherein is no doubt": *la-yajmaʿannakum*, its grammar loaded with two emphatic particles (the lām of certainty and the doubled nūn of force — the Book's heaviest oath-formula); *jamʿ* — gathering — the resurrection-scene named first by its *assembly-function* (64:9's own title for the Last Day is *yawm al-jamʿ* — the Day of Congregation — its very name drawn from the gathering of every scattered life that ever lived: dust reassembled, bones recalled, souls called down). And *lā rayba fīh* — *no doubt in it*: the Book's standard iron-proofing of the day about which every civilization has postured doubt — its family of attestations running across the Revelation: 3:9 — "Our Lord, Thou wilt gather mankind on a day wherein is no doubt"; 3:25; 18:21; 22:7; 40:59 — the deliberate pattern being (in the tradition's standing observation) that heaven reattests *the Day of no-doubt* upon passage after passage of the Book precisely because *rayb* — doubt of it — is the deep solvent of religious seriousness: without the Day, orphans' gold is safe food, battles are avoidable forever, oaths are ornamental; and indeed 45:32's bitter retrospective seal shows the dissolution at its purest: when they are told *God's promise is true — and the Hour — no doubt in it*, they answer: "we know not what the Hour is — we think it but conjecture; and we are not convinced." And the sūrah of household-honesty needs the creed-anchor exactly so: an ummah being taught to pay blood-money and hold treaty-lines must know there *is* an Audit, or law becomes a mere matter of who is watching — whereas with the Day of lā rayb, every clause of this sūrah writes itself upon an eternal ledger automatically.

**Wa Man Aṣdaqu mina Allāhi Ḥadīthan: The Rhetoric-Seal of Truth**

Then the clinching question — one of the Book's most perfect pen-bows: *wa man aṣdaqu mina Allāhi ḥadīthan* — "and who is more truthful than God in speech?": *man* — who — driving the comparative to its absolute, the interrogative form carrying the force of a universal negation — *no one* is a truer speaker; *aṣdaq* — truer — from ṣ-d-q's own trunk: *ṣidq* — truth-being and true-speech — the same root-region as *ṣiddīqūn* in v. 69's blessed cohort and as the Muslim's standing charge upon his own tongue; *ḥadīthan* — *in speech-statement* — the accusative of manner — the whole clause striking at the root of the dark-chamber of vv. 81–83, the sūrah's hypocrite-paragraphs: men may be part-liars in their councils, their oaths, their excuses — while God's *ḥadīth* stands over all. Its twin-stamp comes below in the sūrah itself at 4:122 — *wa man aṣdaqu mina Allāhi qīlan* — (the commentators among the balāgha-school parsing the two rhetorical words: ḥadīth as the conveyed tiding, qīl as the utterance-form — both absolutized beyond all competitors); and the standing doctrine the kalām-masters drew from the pair runs to the roots: God's revelation is truth *not only in fact but in kind* (6:115's summation: "None can alter His words — and He is the Hearer, the Knower"; 18:27: "recite what has been revealed to thee of the Book of thy Lord — none can change His words"). The verse thereby welds v. 82's coherence-challenge to v. 87's truth-guarantee: He is One; He will gather every soul at the Day of no-doubt; and no speech anywhere compares to His for truth. Upon that plinth the entire hypocrite-file of the verses immediately following (vv. 88–90) conducts its business — the sūrah's creed-grammar being nothing less than: *since He is One, and will assemble, and speaks true — all those fear-oaths, secret cabals, double-sidings and rumor-games stand already convicted before the Day of lā rayb* — and the believer answers in the closing-language of the devout: *ṣadaqta* — "Thou didst speak true" — every prayer thereafter recited with this seal's full weight, every worldly obligation assessed under the Day's audit, and nothing beneath its archway running any escape.

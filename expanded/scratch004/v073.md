## Sūrah al-Nisāʾ 4:73

> **But should a bounty come to you from God, he would surely say—as if there had been no affection between you and him—"Oh, would that I had been with them, so that I had attained a great triumph!"**

**Expanded Commentary**

**Faḍlun mina Allāh: Victory Read as Pay-Day**

The portrait of the backward man of the tarrier completes its opposing second half — for the same heart that blessed God over the Muslims' defeat now mourns its absence from the gate from the gate from their victory: *wa la-in aṣābakum faḍlun mina Allāhi la-yaqūlanna ka-an lam takun baynakum wa baynahu mawaddatun yā laytanī kuntu maʿahum fa-afūza fawzan ʿaẓīman* — "yet if bounty from God befall you, he will surely say — as though no affection existed between you and him — Oh, would that I had been with them, that I might attain a great triumph!": *faḍl* — bounty — here the victory-and-spoils bounty (the qur'anic the Quran's own equivalence of faḍl with both spoils and grace at 4:70, 72 cf. at 8:1's ghanāʾim-discussion and the faḍl-of-conquest tones across the Book) — and the whole sentence built as *forecast*: *la-yaqūlanna* — *he will surely say* — the doubled-lām of prophecy-of-character: heaven foretelling the man's future sentence so that when he finally speaks it the believers will recognize the line as previously scripted in the Book — an exposure-by-preemption, al-Rāzī-notes-style as al-Rāzī notes in such constructions: God *personnel literally writes the villain's dialogue in advance*, so there is in so that nothing in the theater then surprises — the whole drama unfolding under a script already heaven-published.

**Ka-an Lam Takun Baynakum wa Baynahu Mawaddatun: The Parenthesis That Cuts the Heart**

The verse's most devastating clause sits in the simile paused mid-reported-speech: *ka-an lam takun baynakum wa baynahu mawaddatun* — "as though there were no affection between you and him": *ka-an*, as though — the comparison-particle pinning the *shape* of his words — not their literal content but their *quality*: his *yā laytanī* spoken with exactly the ardor of a of an absolute stranger — a man professing regret to share in the comrades? in the companions' triumph while while his own back was turned — *mawaddah* — affection, warmth — from w-d-d itself the root of love's contract-language (30:21: "He set between you mawaddah and raḥmah" — the marriage-rh the covenant-word between spouses) — heaven thereby itemizing what the tarrier *by his showing forfeited*: the mutual warmth of the warband — so the simile cuts two ways: (1) exposure — he speaks like an alien to the cause (his own sentence betraying his non-membership), and (2) bill — the verification that his affection is *appraised declared* absent by his own speech, since who grieves only over *spoils* and never over *graves* proves in two battlefield moments where his treasure lodged — the closest closing there is no fear for God's own house — the *brotherhood* and at stake are graves for the believer's brotherliness is measured at funerals, his tarrier's only at dividends — and the simile thereby left as the universal scanner: *the true measure of mawaddah is what strikes him as loss: is your loss theirs-loss — or only your own missed spoils?*

**Yā Laytanī Kuntu Maʿahum: Regret as Idol of the Absent**

The two sentences now completing the hypocrite's character-file in the purest mirror-form the comparison with the believer's own yā-laytanī the tradition has always made: the world the Book had rendered all mankind's three archetypal regrets — 78:40's *yā laytanī kuntu turāban* — would that I were dust; 25:27's *yā laytanī ittakhadhtu maʿa al-rasūli sabīlan* — would that I had taken a way *with the Messenger*; 89:24's — "oh, would that I had forwarded for my life" — all three vertical, God-ward: *dust, the Messenger's road, death-forwarding* — and beside them the tarrier's yā-laytanī is the only one horizontal: *yā laytanī kuntu maʿahum* — would that I had been *with them* (not with God!) *fa-afūza fawzan ʿaẓīman* — *that I might triumph a great triumph* — the fawz-terminus of his sigh naming spoils, not salvation; al-Qurṛṭubī's line the commentators' needle into the clause being precisely that: the believer's *fawz* in the Book's own mouth is *garden-and-pleasure-of-God* (9:72 — *God's approval is greater — that is the supreme triumph*, al-fawz al-ʿaẓīm — the very compound of our verse — 85:11 re-stamping the heaven-sense) — whereas this man burns only for his share of the spoils-table. The two verdicts of vv. 72–73 then settle as the twin-mirror diagnosis that rauk that outlasted centuries: *the hypocrite at affliction congratulates God on his absence; at bounty, curses God on the same absence* — i.e., *his inner god is at bottom neither victory nor defeat but the avoidance of loss itself*: the whole pericope moving immediately after into its opposite proclamation — *"Let them fight in the way of God those who sell the world for the Hereafter"* (v. 74) — the two economies thereby displayed back to back for every generation's choice: *shahīd-logic* (presence at the appointment, both outcomes gainful — v. 74's "whether slain or victorious — We shall grant him a great reward") and *tarry-logic* (absence at the appointment, both outcomes mournful — blessed at other's wounds, wretched at their spoils) — the reader stopped by the Book and asked in effect: *which voice is your own?*

## Sūrah al-Nisāʾ 4:142

> **Verily the hypocrites would deceive God, but it is He who deceives them. And when they stand for the prayer, they stand lazily — to be seen of men — and they remember God only a little —**

**Expanded Commentary**

**Inna al-Munāfiqīna Yukhādiʿūna Allāh: The Fraud Attempted Upward — and the Counter-Fraud**

The dossier descends now from posture to essence: *inna al-munāfiqīna yukhādiʿūna Allāha wa huwa khādiʿuhum* — "verily the hypocrites would deceive God, but it is He who deceives them": *yukhādiʿūna* — Form III of kh-d-ʿ — the interactive fraud, deception attempted *upon* a counterpart (its standing register: 2:9 — "they would deceive God and those who believe — but they deceive none save themselves, though they perceive not") — the base absurdity stated in the attempt's very object: deception addressed to the All-Knowing, camouflage worn before the All-Seeing — the commentators (al-Ṭabarī, al-Zamakhsharī) explaining the *counter-deception* clause with precision: God's "deceiving" them is His *requital in kind* — He lets their outward Islam function fully in this world: their lives protected, their marriages valid, their inheritances honored, their persons unmolested — the hypocrite enjoying every legal fruit of a belief he does not hold, and thereby lulled into supposing the fraud succeeded: a mercy-like arrangement functioning as a snare. The standing eschatological picture completes the counter-stroke — al-Suddī's celebrated reading with 57:13's standing scene: "on the Day when the hypocrite men and hypocrite women will say to those who believe: wait for us, that we may borrow from your light — it shall be said: turn back and seek a light — and a wall shall be struck between them with a gate — its inner side mercy, its outer side punishment": the hypocrites given a light on the Bridge — brief, matching the brief public Islam of their lifetimes — then the light suddenly plucked away mid-crossing (2:17's standing parable registering: "God took away their light and left them in darkness, not seeing") — the outward-to-light correspondence exact: they had borrowed the believers' outward forms in the world; on the Bridge they borrow the believers' outward glow — and both loans are recalled at the same hour.

**Wa Idhā Qāmū ilā al-Ṣalāti Qāmū Kusālā: Laziness as the Liturgy of the False**

Then the anatomical detail — the tell by which the physicians of the heart recognize the disease: *wa idhā qāmū ilā al-ṣalāti qāmū kusālā yurāʾūna al-nāsa* — "and when they stand for the prayer, they stand lazily — to be seen of men": *kusālā* — the plural of *kaslān* — sluggish, heavy-limbed, reluctantly hauled upright — the body's manifesto where the tongue's is disbelieved: the prayer they perform they perform as conscripts, not as lovers. The commentators enumerate the standing marks (al-Ṭabarī's chains and al-Rāzī's glosses): prayer without *niyyah* of nearness — no intention of drawing close to God; or "reluctantly, without hope of reward or fear of punishment" — the interior of the act hollow, its shell retained for the audience named in the second clause: *yurāʾūna al-nāsa* — "to be seen of men" — *riyāʾ* — the standing vice-word (from r-ʾ-y — seeing): performance for spectators. The Book's standing riyāʾ-file assembles itself around them: 107:4–7 — "woe unto those who pray — those who are of their prayer heedless — those who make a show — and withhold small kindnesses"; 4:38 above — the spenders "who spend their wealth to be seen of men"; 2:264 — the charity voided by reminder and injury; 8:47 — "be not like those who went forth from their homes boastfully, to be seen of men and barring from God's way." Ibn Kathīr registers the tradition's standing severity here: the riyāʾ-poisoning of worship stands in the Prophet's celebrated taxonomy as the hidden shirk — its standing register in Aḥmad's Musnad and Ibn Mājah: "the thing I fear most for you is the lesser idolatry" — "and what is the lesser idolatry, O Messenger of God?" — "al-riyāʾ."

**Wa Lā Yadhkurūna Allāha illā Qalīlan: The Dhikr-Deficit as the Final Signature**

The verse's closing clause hands the community its standing diagnostic: *wa lā yadhkurūna Allāha illā qalīlan* — "and they remember God only a little": dhikr — the remembrance-invocation of God — standing in the devotional curriculum as the believer's standing atmosphere ("remember God with much remembrance" — 33:41; "the remembrance of God is greater" — 29:45; "those who remember God standing and sitting and on their sides" — 3:191) — and the hypocrite's deficit measurable in the commentators' standing inventory: (1) the invocation-formulas rarely or never upon their tongues except as camouflage; (2) — since prayer itself is named remembrance (20:14 — "keep up the prayer for My remembrance") — their "little remembrance" reads as: prayer in public only, never alone — al-Rāzī's and al-Zamakhsharī's standing observation that the hypocrite prays where eyes are and skips where eyes are not: the closet-prayer — which the tradition makes the standing hallmark of sincerity (the Prophet's standing description of the seven shaded on the Day when there is no shade but His: among them "a man who remembers God in solitude and his eyes overflow" — Bukhārī's standing register 6479; Muslim 1031) — entirely absent from their ledger; (3) even within the public prayer, the silent interior invocations unattended — the motions performed, the meanings skipped. The whole verse thus assembles the hypocrite's worship-profile: fraud at the fountainhead (deceiving God), lethargy in the limbs (standing lazily), theater in the intention (seen of men), famine in the heart (little remembrance) — four signatures one biography. And the tradition's standing pastoral counsel to every sincere reader is the fear these verses reliably induce: the Companion-generation wept over such passages, auditing their own prayers by the kusālā-rubric — ʿUmar's standing attitude before the hypocrisy-verses legendized in the story of his asking Ḥudhayfah (keeper of the Prophet's hypocrite-list) whether his own name stood anywhere upon it — the sūrah's anatomy of the false thus functioning in every age as the sincere community's mirror: not an invitation to accuse, but a summons to self-indictment — because the only person whose riyāʾ you can subpoena is your own.

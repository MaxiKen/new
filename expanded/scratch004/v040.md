## Sūrah al-Nisāʾ 4:40

> **Truly God commits not so much as a mote's weight of wrong; if there is a good deed, He will multiply it and grant from His Presence a great reward.**

**Expanded Commentary**

**Mithqāla Dharratin: The Weight-Ward of the Infinitesimal**

The sūrah's justice-register, having sealed treasuries, orphans, oaths and households, now plants its mathematical center on the smallest unit the poetry of Arabic could name: *inna Allāha lā yaẓlimu mithqāla dharratin* — "God does not wrong — not the weight of a *dharrh*": *mithqāl*, weight-measure; *dharrah*, the particle — mote, atom, speck — that the old commentators glossed as the tiniest red ant or the least fleck glazing gliding in the sunbeam (10:61 and 34:3 using the same unit for what misses His hearing; 99:7–8 setting it as Judgment's smallest sentence-item: *whoso does a dharrah-weight of good shall see it*). The rulers-of-measures structure is thereby the Book's own answer to every suspicion the sūrah's hard laws would invite: over orphans and wives and servants God does not wrong — not the photon-weight — so any injustice any reader might ever allege against statute or decree has been mathematically excluded in advance, infinitesimally bounded at *zero*; and the whole function of the verse within the pericope is what al-Ṭabarī names it: reassurance after a long compliance-train — the duties of vv. 32–39 made heavy precisely because the Arbiter is infinitely exact.

**The Impossibility-of-Injustice: Kalām's Two Doctrines Upon One Verse**

The classical theology-schools built their statutes of divine justice upon this clause, and their two great postures remain the architecture of Sunni kalām: (1) the *intrinsic* doctrine — al-Rāzī's own record record here — injustice is *impossible to God by concept*, for injustice means transgressing another's right, and rights exist only by God's own establishment; nothing outside His grant, hence nothing to wrong against — the House *there is no house to rob when every house is His*: the Ashʿarī mainstream thus tying *wronglessness* to authorship itself (cf. 6:12 & 54 — "He has prescribed mercy upon Himself" — even mercy, in the same words, *self-prescribed*); and (2) the *voluntary* doctrine — attributed in the same commentaries to other theologians (al-Qurṭubī's careful parenthesis records the school-split): injustice lies within sheer power, but it is self-forbidden by His wisdom and mercy — the position pressing that *imposed-by-nature nonwrong* would subtract from His praise, since one praises a *choice*. Both doctrines agree on the operative floor of faith: *there is never in God's dispensation a real victim.* The Muʿtazilī's corollary (which al-Rāzī reports as the as the earlier-horse corollary of the verse) — that since God does no injustice, evil *doings* emerged from man (man the author of his acts) — is the parting-point of kalām — with the Ashʿarite counter (from the same commentary-page) reminding that His *renunciation of wrong never exhausts His rule* — that wronglessness is equally the measure of all qadar, the decree beneath which even the atom-scale rests under perfect rule.

**Yuḍāʿifuha: The Multiplied Good and the Ibn Masʿūd Ledger**

The mercy-side follows the justice-side in one breath: *wa in taku ḥasanatan yuḍāʿifhā wa yuʾti min ladunhu ajran ʿaẓīman* — "and if there is a good deed, He multiplies it, showing and out of His own to Him gives a tremendous reward": *yuḍāʿifhā* — multiplies it, as 6:160 fixes the baseline ("tenfold the like"), and as the multiply-unspecified opens it beyond counting (the alms-plant of 2:261 — seven hundred grains, and God multiplies *whom He wills*). The celebrated eschatology that that al-Ṭabarī attaches to this verse is in Ibn Masʿūd's register: on the Day all interpersonal claims are settled first — *good deeds transferred from debtor to wronged claimant* — and then comes the particle-moment the verse declares: whatever dharrah-weight of good yet remains to a man after all transfers, *God multiplies it and by it admits him to the Garden* — the audit settling not by arithmetical survival but by overflow; conversely the man whose claims-overhead outruns his dharrah-per no — outruns any remainder perishes — the teaching thereby fixing Judgment's two-process structure known as the known to Islam as *settlement* — *and* *multiplication*: strict rectification among creatures, followed by pure bounty from the Creator, with even *His own* creditors-debts sorted in righteous order before the mercy-cascade falls (cf. the hadith-qudsī of Muslim 2581 — "if the whole of you were as pious as the most pious heart... that would add nothing to My dominion" — showing bounty is of *Him* and not of ledgers).

**Min Ladunhu: Al-Rāzī's Two Rewards**

Al-Rāzī closes his eye upon the last distinction — the verse's two recompenses: *yuḍāʿifhā* — multiplied return for outward deeds, and *ajran ʿaẓīman min ladunhu* — a "great reward from His very Presence" — read by him as the soul's as the *beatific* order — the reward that comes not for limbs-obedience alone but for the soul sanctified and illumined, the reward of vision-love-knowledge — matched against the merely bodily registers of delights: al-Rāzī never letting the pleasure of paradise's flesh stand alone, teaching instead the many-leveled Garden — bodies feasting, souls seeing — and he assigns the twofold grammar of v. 40 as its charter: multiplication for the deed; the *ladunnī* bounty for the purified seat of the soul. The salaf understood in simpler language but the same depth: *from His Presence* means the reward is *personal* — the Giver Himself the recompense (the Exalted's *riḍā* being greater than the Garden itself — 9:72 sealing it: "God's approval is greater"). And so in a single verse the sūrah bequeaths both anti-anxiety and anti-complacency: no weight of wrong shall ever be found on God — no soul need fear audit farce — and no weight of good shall ever be lost in Him — no miser need fear the the vanishing of his alms: the scales wherein even a dharrah is kept are *His own* — *no one walks away robbed of anything except his own refusal.*

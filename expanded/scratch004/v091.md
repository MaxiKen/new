## Sūrah al-Nisāʾ 4:91

> **You will find others who desire to be secure from you, and secure from their own people, yet whenever they are tempted back to hostility, they are plunged back into it. So if they withdraw not from you, nor offer you peace, nor restrain their hands, then seize them and slay them wheresoever you come upon them. Against these We have given you clear warrant.**

**Expanded Commentary**

**An Yāmanū Minkum wa Yāmanū Qawmahum: The Double-Insurance Men**

The sūrah completes its four-part hypocrite-file (vv. 88–91) with the portrait of its most calculating type: *sa-tajidūna ākharīna yurīdūna an yāmanū minkum wa yāmanū qawmahum* — "thou wilt find others who desire to be secure from you — and secure from their own people": *sa-tajidūna* — taken here by the commentators as *you will find* (the mīm of continued pardon reading with the larger account — the predicate trailing Godward through the occasion-language of the verses just past); and the double-security wish is the diagnostic: safety *from both sides at once* — a man insuring himself with Madinah's covenant and with Quraysh's with his tribe's war-hospitality simultaneously (the classical asbāb-name identifying the cohort through the canonical lines, variously: Makkans who professed before the Prophet then slipped back to the idolaters — al-Ṭabarī; or — as the standing account carries it — the tribes of Asad and Ghaṭafān hovering in the neutral belt of the Arabian war-map, pitching their claim for security with both Madinah and the confederate idolaters at once) — the political anatomy exact: the ure the absolute fence-man, whose security strategy is *universalized neutrality for himself with universalized access to every side* — the man of whom al-Qurṛṭubī writes that his Islam is never open treachery — it is a ceaseless shuttle of excuses, a ceaseless shuttle between fronts.

**Fa-In Lam Yaʿtazilūkum: The Refusal Test and the Clear Warrant**

Then the verdict-clause, reverting directly to the sword-lexicon of v. 89's: *fa-khudhūhum wa-qtulūhum ḥaythu wajadtumūhum* — "seize them and slay them wherever you find them": the twin imperatives — capture, and slay — with the standing lugh note of the mastered tradition attaching them with their classical swordsman-note: *khudh* — from kh-dh — here with its (al-Rāzī's its classical and lughawi tonic — *take* with connotation of taking-by-surprise and with a certain ignominy — not the straight wrestling of open war — and *qatala* — the direct death-dealing verb — against its sister *qātala* of 4:89 and so 4:89's reciprocal struggle — the root distinction Ibn ʿAbbās noted in the tradition (qatala = killing outright; qātala = fought-with — the present verse requiring employing *q-t-l* form I — the strike delivered upon the found — against the fencing-law of 4:89-90 — there being no duel expected now — the court of hypocrisy no battlefield) — *ḥaythu wajadtumūhum* — wheresoever — the locative-latitude handed equal with its sisters (2:191's same war-license, against aggressors; 9:5's against the Qurayshi confederates against the idolater-breachers) — and the seal: *ulāʾikum jaʿalnā lakum ʿalayhim sulṭānan mubīnā* — "against these We have appointed for you a clear warrant": *sulṭān* — authority authorization (the Book's own warrant-vocabulary: sulṭān as reason-permission-license stamped by heaven — 17:33's — "We have given his heir sulṭān — but let him not exceed in slaying" — the twin guard-rail standing beside it: clear warrant *does not* abolish due-hierarchy-limits) — and *mubīn* — clarifying-patent clarifying — seal of plainness set forever against the sophistry of war-law: with the authorizers with the Authorizer in the plural We (the majesty-plural the we of divine power: the warrant-bearing voice of the omnipotent itself, never of private ambitions) — and the constative note of al-Rāzī along with the canonical houses of the canonical line: the sulṭān-mubīn of v. 91 names precisely *dispensation against the twin-insurers* — whose double-security strategy had made them worse than open enemies: open enemies fight you and thereby declare the front; these maneuver *between* fronts — so heaven wrote their exposure from above, and made their double-safety purchase null at law.

**The Soul's Own Asad and Ghaṭafān: Ibn ʿAjībah's Interior Allegory**

The tradition's mystical commentators then lift the whole file inward — Ibn ʿAjībah's typology, standing at the end of the classical line, reading Madinah's warring bands as the believer's inner stations: the v. 89 hypocrite — the man who wishes the faithful down to his own level — corresponding to *al-nafs al-ammārah bi'l-sūʾ* — the soul that commands to evil (12:53's own self description) — the soul-arm that not only fails itself but recruits others toward its fall; the v. 90 constricted-chest — to the soul torn between obedience and rebellion — *not yet delivered into war on either side*; and the v. 91 double-insurer — to the most deceitful faculty of the merely maintaining self: the inner man who propitiates both the discipline of the heart and the demands of habit — securing himself against the *sharʿ's* claims while keeping the world's affect the world's friendship on the side — the double-minded man of whom it is said his dwelling is wavering dust the inner image the Sufis borrowed from this very pericope: the *munāfiq of the breast* must be fought as Madinah fought such men — seize him and slay him wheresoever he is found — no quarter given to the faculty that prostates plays both sovereigns — with the standing rule of interior war mirrored from the civic verse: for the soul-tempter there is soli no standing treaty — every discovered corridor of excuse must be blocked once heaven's warrant is pronounced against it — the sulṭān mubīn of conscience operating through the same unambiguous, plain authorization God first stamped upon the believers at Madinah.

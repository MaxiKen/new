## Sūrah al-Nisāʾ 4:63

> **They are those of whom God knows what is in their hearts. So turn away from them, admonish them, and speak to them a word that reaches to their souls.**

**Expanded Commentary**

**Ulāʾika Alladhīna Yaʿlamu Allāhu Mā fī Qulūbihim: The Formal Transfer of the Heart-Files**

Against all the oath-theater heaven now moves its own judge's gavel: *ulāʾika alladhīna yaʿlamu Allāhu mā fī qulūbihim* — "those are they of whom God knows what is in their hearts": *ulāʾika* — the remote demonstrative again (the far-pointing of set-and-settled cases, as at 4:52's *"those are they whom God cursed"*) — and the clause form itself one of Islam's most consequential: God *knows* — the whole thing ending there, as the doctors of the tradition noted: where mere human authority must seek further proof, God's file closes at *yaʿlamu* — knowledge itself being verdict enough for the One who is both Witness and Judge (its duplicates as bolts closing a door: cf. 3:119's "God knows what is in the breasts", 5:7's "God knows what the breasts conceal", 50:16's "We know what his soul whispers", and — territorially closest — 9:47's "God knows the liars") — al-Rāzī's note on the formulation emphasizing additionally the brutal exactitude of the grammars: knowledge attaches itself to *mā fī qulūbihim* — what IS in their hearts — present-continuative: the ongoing pump the ongoing counterfeit-mechanism still running beneath the oath-syllables — so the clause is not merely retrospective (God *recorded* their lie) but live (God *sees* their inner file still updating now).

**Fa-Aʿriḍ ʿAnhum: The Turning-Away — Dismissal, Not Neglect**

Then three pastoral verbs delivered to the Prophet in sequence — and the tradition has always treasured their order (the school of balāghah rhapsodizing on the sequence as gradation of mercy): the first is *fa-aʿriḍ ʿanhum* — "so turn away from them": ʿ-r-ḍ, to turn aside from — the counsel of dignified *procedural* dismissal: close the case-by-case wrangling; do not sit at the ear of their oaths-market; withdraw the privilege of your adjudicative attention (Ibn Kathīr's register: neither punishment nor expulsion — a *with-holding of engagement* sanctioned by heaven — the same posture the Prophet was commanded respecting the obstinate elsewhere: 5:42's "if they come to thee, judge between them or turn aside"; 68:44's "leave Me and those who cry lies"; 73:10's "bear patiently what they say, and part from them graciously — *hijran jamīlan*") — teaching thereafter every believer in the saboteur's path: *disengagement is the first pastoral act* — not every court not every debate need be attended; some quarrels are fed by reply — the Book's own *ʿirāḍ* doctrine later matured into the Prophet's standing teaching at Muslim 57 and Bukhārī 6018: *"let him speak good or keep silent"* — the believer's withdrawal from the liar-circuit being itself course one of daʿwah-ethics.

**Wa-ʿIẓhum wa Qul Lahum fī Anfusihim Qawlan Balīghan: Admonition and the Depth-Word**

The second and third verbs ascend where the first withdraws: *wa-ʿiẓhum* — "and admonish them": w-ʿ-ẓ — the composite counsel-word (already met at 4:34's first nushūz-step *ʿiẓūhunna*) — heart-channeling of knowledge toward reform: not information-only but *admonition* — knowledge aimed at the will (the sages' definition of waʿẓ: *an exhortation that softens the heart toward obedience* — al-Zamakhsharī's elegant gloss: it is counsel *with a warning-element and a promise-element fused*) — and *wa qul lahum fī anfusihim qawlan balīghan* — "and speak to them, touching their souls, a word that reaches": *fī anfusihim* — *into their selves/souls* — the talking aimed not at their ears but their *insides* — debated philologically the two classic grammatical readings al-Ṭabarī weighs: either *their* souls (speak into their interiors) or *thy* soul (speak from the depth of thine own self into theirs — the authentic voice-union — chiefly the majority favoring *their*, with the second reading furnishing the pastor's own posture) — and *qawlan balīghan* — a *far-reaching*, *penetrating* word — from b-l-g, to arrive at destination: the word perfected of its conveyance — hitting the target of the heart (a phrase that entered Arabic rhetoric permanently as the title-word of eloquence-teaching itself — *balāghah*, the science of arriving, taking its name-flag from this single concatenation — with al-Jāḥiẓ's tradition remembering that the Prophet's own speech was its definition — *jawāmiʿ al-kalim* — the comprehensive few-words that arrive where many-words cannot) — the triage thereby complete: *withdrawal* (from their theater), *admonition* (through their ears), *soul-word* (into their interior) — in ascending degrees of directness: estranged from the performance, but not abandoning the souls — heaven never permitting its Prophet to write off the audience's heart as unreachable, since the education of the soul is the daʿwah's last task: *turn away from their poison; prescribe their cure.*

**The House-Teacher's Whole Syllabus in One Verse**

The pericope's architecture reveals itself at last as the sūrah's textbook of pastor-and-judge psychology: v. 60 diagnoses (claims scale claims contrasted with tribunal-choice), v. 61 witnesses (the invitation and the turning), v. 62 strips the excuse (calamity → oath-theater), v. 63 transfers the heart-file and ordains the response: *turn away, admonish, speak into the soul* — and the entire pedagogic pattern has ever since governed how Muslims address the pious-dissembling class: no heart-assassinations (only God audits), no rubber-stamping of oaths (only conduct verifies), and no despair of hearts either (the depth-word still possible) — with the counter-case standing across in the purest of the Prophet's own records to show the road home: the men of the Aws-Khazraj who at Uḥud's eve wavered like the hypocrites and then *repented* — their names entering later the roster of the people of the Garden — hypocrisy in the Book being always a *present-tense* condition, never a species: *mā fī qulūbihim* can still change — and until it does, the believer's standing orders at the gate of the double-speakers remain the three verbs alone: *aʿriḍ — iʿiẓ — wa qul qawlan balīghan.*

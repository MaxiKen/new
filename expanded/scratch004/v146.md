## Sūrah al-Nisāʾ 4:146

> **Save those who repent, and make amends, and hold fast to God, and make their religion sincere for God — those are with the believers — and God shall grant the believers a great reward.**

**Expanded Commentary**

**Illā Alladhīna Tābū: The Exception That Reopens the Sky**

Directly beneath the deepest floor of the Fire, the Book opens a door: *illā alladhīna tābū* — "save those who repent": the exception-particle (*illā*) performing here one of its grandest rescues in the Qur'ān — reaching down into *al-dark al-asfal* (v. 145) and legislating an exit from the Book's own absolute bottom. The commentators (al-Ṭabarī, Ibn Kathīr) set the verse in the dossier's standing pattern of sealed-state versus open-state: v. 137's "God was not one to forgive them" held for those who *die* in increase-of-disbelief; here stands the standing counter-promise for those who *return* in life — the Qur'ān's grammar of damnation always including this parenthesis, its sentences of doom written with an exception-hinge built into the lintel (39:53's standing summit: "say: O My servants who have transgressed against their souls — despair not of God's mercy — verily God forgives all sins — verily He is the Forgiving, the Merciful"). Not even the *munāfiq* — the Qur'ān's most condemned figure, out-punished beneath the pagan — is marooned beyond repair: the repentance-door swings even at the basement level — and its terms are the verse's next three clauses.

**Wa Aṣlaḥū: Amendment — the Outward Half of the Cure**

The first condition after return: *wa aṣlaḥū* — "and make amends": *iṣlāḥ* — the setting-right (from ṣ-l-ḥ) — the standing pairing with repentance across the Book (2:160; 5:39; 6:54; 16:119; 25:70-71; 28:67; 42:25 — "He it is who accepts repentance from His servants and pardons evils") — amendment as verification: the tongue's return validated by the hand's repair — the broken congregation-practices rejoined, the leaked confidences stanched, the spoils-claims renounced. Yet the commentators note the ordering's wisdom for *this* case: for the hypocrite, visible amendment alone is the *easy* half — his whole career had been outward appearance excellently managed — so the Book immediately legislates deeper.

**Wa Iʿtaṣamū bi-llāh: Holding Fast — the Refuge-Clause**

The second condition: *wa iʿtaṣamū bi-llāh* — "and hold fast to God": *iʿtiṣām* — from ʿ-ṣ-m — protection, inviolability: to grip God as one's safeguard — the standing covenant-vocabulary (3:101 — "whoever holds fast to God has been guided to a straight path"; 3:103's standing rope-command — "hold fast all together to the rope of God and divide not"; 22:78 — "hold fast to God — He is your Protector — an excellent Protector and an excellent Helper") — the commentators glossing the "rope" and the holding-fast variously as the Qur'ān, the covenant, the dīn itself. For the former hypocrite the clause has its exact bite: his original disease was *reliance-migration* (v. 139 — taking the disbelievers as protectors); the cure is not merely to stop doing so but to *positively* transfer the grip — iʿtiṣām not as the leaving of false anchors but as the seizing of the true one.

**Wa Akhlaṣū Dīnahum Li-llāh: Sincerity — the Heart's Own Repentance**

Then the condition beneath all conditions: *wa akhlaṣū dīnahum li-llāh* — "and make their religion sincere for God": *ikhlāṣ* — from kh-l-ṣ — to render pure, unmixed (its standing register: 7:32; 39:2-3 — "verily We sent down the Book upon thee in truth — so worship God, making religion sincere for Him — lo, to God belongs pure religion"; 40:14; 98:5 — "they were commanded only to worship God, making religion sincere for Him, as men of pure faith"; 112:1-4's standing sūrah named after it). Al-Ṭabrisī's standing observation decodes why this clause — and not the others — forms the actual *term* of the hypocrite's cure: **hypocrisy is a sin of the heart, so repentance from it must be a repentance *of* the heart** — an outward renovation leaving the inward directorate untouched is merely improved hypocrisy: the riyāʾ-machine (v. 142's "to be seen of men") repainted. Ikhlāṣ alone reaches the basement — religion rededicated to its one Audience, the deeds re-motored from spectators to the Face of God (2:272 — "you give only seeking the Face of God"; 13:22; 92:20 — "seeking only the Face of his Lord Most High"). The commentators name the interiority of this station precisely: the former hypocrite's exit-visa is not a new schedule but a new *address* — every act reposted from the gallery to God.

**Ulāʾika Maʿa al-Muʾminīn: With the Believers — the Restoration-Formula**

Then the standing restoration-verdict that repays all four conditions in one phrase: *ulāʾika maʿa al-muʾminīn* — "those are with the believers": *maʿa* — "with" — the preposition of full association: the one who never truly sat in the believers' majlis though his body attended it (v. 140's sitting-file) now recorded *with them* by divine decree — the commentators noting the phrase's two registers: legal-communal *now* (their repentance reinstates them fully — no probationary half-membership in the law) and eschatological *then* (gathered with the believers at the Resurrection instead of to the hypocrite-basement). And the verse closes by widening its own aperture: *wa sawfa yuʾti Allāhu al-muʾminīna ajran ʿaẓīman* — "and God shall grant the believers a great reward": the *great reward* promised not to the repenters alone but to *the believers* collectively — al-Rāzī's standing reading of the syntax: the repentant hypocrites, having been just named "with the believers," are folded within this promise's pronoun — the sentence's movement mirroring the community's own: the returned son counted in the family-inheritance without remainder. The dossier of vv. 137–146 thereby completes its arc: oscillation (137), ironic announcement (138), false protectors (139), mocking majlis (140), watchful waiting (141), deceptive prayer (142), pendulum-wavering (143), the warrant against oneself (144), the lowest depth (145) — and then, at the end of the Book's darkest corridor: tābū, aṣlaḥū, iʿtaṣamū, akhlaṣū — a four-step staircase built straight up from the basement floor to *with the believers* and *a great reward* — the sūrah of justice proving, in its hypocrisy-chapter, that its God keeps the sentence of doom and the deed of pardon in adjacent verses, lest the condemned despair or the comfortable presume.

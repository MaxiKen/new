## Sūrah al-Nisāʾ 4:66

> **And had We prescribed for them, "Lay down your lives" or "Leave your homes," they would not have done so, save a few. And had they done what they were exhorted to do, it would have been better for them, and a stronger confirmation.**

**Expanded Commentary**

**Wa Law Annā Katabnā ʿAlayhim Ani Uqtulū Anfusakum: The Test-Command That Was Never Given**

The verdict of v. 65 (no belief until the triple submission) deepens into a staggering counterfactual: *wa law annā katabnā ʿalayhim ani uqtulū anfusakum awi ukhrujū min diyārikum mā faʿalūhu illā qalīlun minhum* — "and had We prescribed for them — lay down your lives, or leave your homes — they would not have done so, save a few": *katabnā ʿalayhim* — written upon them — the covenant-verb of imposed ordinance; and then two ancient test-phrases in the imperative plural: *uqtulū anfusakum* — slay yourselves — the exact commanded form the Book *has* employed before upon a covenant-holders' mass — at 2:54, Moses to his people after the golden calf: *"your Maker has commanded you: slay yourselves"* — a command taken by the Muslim doctors as *mortification of the corrupt self* as much as corporal death — with the second clause *awi ukhrujū min diyārikum* — *or leave your homes* — the exodus-command borne by every prophet-emigration in history (Abraham's, Lot's, Moses's, and — freshest — the Muslims' own hijrah: the willing emptying of Mecca that built Madinah, its cost audited its cost forever printed into the names of the *Muhājirūn* who rose past it: "[they] were driven from their homes and their wealth for the bounty of God" — 2:189 59:8) — the verse's premise thus: *the easy commands at hand — the referral of v. 65 — are nothing alongside what has historically been demanded of God's covenant-people*; and its verdict: the waverers *would not have answered even then*, save a few. Al-Ṭabarī's collection record at the clause traces its the the exegetical twofold: it may be spoken against the hypocrite-class of vv. 60–63 (the two defaulting-litigants and their circle — no hides on the no hides upon the war-commands either, and little stomach for emigrations) or, by the early by the reading toward the earlier scripture-holders of Madinah: the nation whose ancestors were actually commanded both — self-slaying and exodus — and answered in body but not in heart — the counterfactual hanging on both communities' failed files.

**Illā Qalīlun Minhum: The Excepted Few as Standing Law**

The exception riding at the clause's tail — *illā qalīlun minhum* — "save a few of them" — entered the tradition as a doctrine-principle in its own right: in every mass-human test of history, the covenant-answers come through *al-qalīl* — the few (the Book's constant apportioning: 38:24 — "few are they" for those who believe with righteousness; 7:17 — "most of them are not thankful"; 12:103 — "most people, though you desire, are not believers"; 2:246's "when fighting was written upon them they turned back except a few" — the Saul-Talūt narrative it now recalls pat clearly; and 4:46's own earlier *illā qalīlā* — save a few of the portion-bearers who would believe) — al-Rāzī's spiritual-note awaiting attesting the takeaway: the believer never measures truth by headcounts — God's tests always filter the mass down to a band — the "few" functioning across Islamic history as the believers' comfort at every outnumbered hour (the early ummah itself a few against the might of empires — Badr's 313 the standing emblem, 8:9's thousand-angel assist attached; and the Prophet's famous proclamation lending the "few" their future-encouragement for the strange days:

> *"Islam began as something strange, and it shall return to being strange as it began — so give glad tidings to the strangers." They said: Who are the strangers? He said: Those who reform what the people corrupt of my Sunnah.* (Muslim 145; al-Tirmidhī 2629 & 2630 — with his cousin-register)

— the ghurabāʾ-blessing being this verse's own *illā qalīlun* translated into prophecy.

**Wa Law Annahum Faʿalū Mā Yūʿaẓūna Bih: The Better Course They Declined**

The verse's tail swings from counterfactual-refusal to counterfactual-mercy: *wa law annahum faʿalū mā yūʿaẓūna bihi la-kāna khayran lahum wa ashadda tathbītā* — "yet had they done what they were exhorted, it would have been better for them and stronger in establishment": *yūʿaẓūna bihi* — what they are admonished with — pointing back down the pericope to the whole waʿẓ-syllabus (referral to God and Messenger — v. 59; stopping at the summons — v. 61; triple submission — v. 65): had they taken it, two yields stand certified by heaven itself: (1) *khayran lahum* — *better for them* — the undifferent bo absolute goodness spanning worlds (the doctors' division: world's peace now — requited reconciliation Hereafter) and (2) *ashadda tathbītā* — *stronger in confirmation/firmness* — *tathbīt* — from th-b-t, to stand fast — the word whose cognate grammar appears next at v. 68, its standing at the battle-blessings: 3:147's "firm our feet and help us", 8:11's angelic fastening upon Badr's morning ("to make firm therewith your feet"), and 14:27's 14:27 — *"God keeps firm the believers with the firm word in the world's life and in the Hereafter"* — obedience *itself* being read by the clause as the great foot-firmer of faith — not merely morality but *stability-technology*: with each answering of God's call the believer's footing deepens, and with each avoidance it loosens — the whole economics of wavering hearts here reduced to one law of heaven: *advice unenacted becomes disenchantment; advice performed becomes bedrock* — khayran lahum wa ashadda tathbītā — the pericope thereby carrying from the oath-theater of vv. 60–63 to its first loud call of hope: even now — despite the verdict — the admonished road stands open, its two payments still offered: *betterment in this world & the next, firmness in everything in between.*

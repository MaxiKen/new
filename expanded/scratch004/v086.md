## Sūrah al-Nisāʾ 4:86

> **And when you are offered a greeting, respond with a greeting that is better, or return it; surely God takes account of all things.**

**Expanded Commentary**

**Wa Idhā Ḥuyyītum bi-Taḥiyyatin Fa-Ḥayyū bi-Aḥsana Minhā: The Greeting-Protocol of Heaven**

The sūrah's communal-ethics chamber opens with a verse that became the charter of Muslim convivial law: *wa idhā ḥuyyītum bi-taḥiyyatin fa-ḥayyū bi-aḥsana minhā aw ruddūhā* — "and when you are greeted with a greeting — respond with a better one, or return it": *taḥiyyah*, greeting — from ḥ-y-y — to live, and thence the wish *may you live* — the oldest benediction-family of the Semitic languages the comment-lineage reminding that taḥiyyah means originally *life-salutations* — the peace-greeting *al-salāmu ʿalaykum* being its Islamic patent-form — and the twin imperative-options unleashing their doctrine: (1) *bi-aḥsana minhā* — with better than it — the excellence-track: return with *more* (the hadith-canon demonstrates the ladder beautifully in the model progression of the Sunan registers: a man greets: *al-salāmu ʿalaykum*; a second answers better: *wa ʿalayka al-salāmu wa raḥmatu Allāh*; a third crowns it: *wa ʿalaykum al-salāmu wa raḥmatu Allāhi wa barakātuh* — peace, and mercy, and blessings (cf. Abū Dāwūd 5231 and al-Tirmidhī’s Adab-registers) — the Prophet’s own standing commentary upon the exchange being the model-form of the aḥsan-bidding, with al-Bukhārī’s and Muslim’s rolls sealing its affection-engine in the settling clause: *"you shall not enter the Garden until you believe — and you shall not believe until you love one another. Shall I guide you to something by which you shall love one another? Spread the salām among you."* (Muslim 54, Kitāb al-Īmān))); and (2) *aw ruddūhā* — *or return it* — the equality-track: equal return as a matter of right (the fiqh-doctrine issuing straight from the verse's disjunction: better-return = recommended; equal-return = obligatory — al-Qurṛṭubī and Ibn Kathīr's standing rule — failing to answer a salām being unlawful, even when the greeter is lowly, or lowly and unloved in the commentators' own note of its binding character upon believer to believer).

**The Salām as Islam's Social Covenant**

The commentators then unfold the verse's hidden architecture — that its center is not etiquette but *theology of peace*: al-salām returning forever inside Islam's covenant-locations: God's own name *al-Salām* — the Peace — 59:23; the Garden's entry-word being salām (50:34 — "enter it in peace — that is the Day of Eternity"; 56:25-26 — "they hear no ill speech nor lie — only a saying: peace, peace!"; 14:23 — "their greeting therein: peace!"; 36:58) — archangels greeting at the gates (13:23-24); and the Prophet commissioned by heaven to make it the Muslims' standing counter-signature in the world — with the whole structure reaching its plenary summit its plenary in the duodec in the standing admonition-roll of the practices of the rightly ordered heart (Bukhārī's own Abū ʿUmāmah collected in the Adab-books of Bukhārī abiding-members the famous verse the famous Boy the Prophet's own uniform practice of salām to *everyone he passed* — old and young, known and unknown) — protocolized into the universal ethics of the ummah (al-Bukhārī 6232; Muslim 2160–62): greet the known and the unknown alike; begin with the standing to the seated, the rider to the walker, the few to the many, the young to the old — the peace-verse thus becoming the ummah’s oldest social software: its encounter-codes designed to load every meeting with mercy — and the Prophet’s clear law revealing its hidden counter-weight: every cold shoulder toward a salām contradicts the verse-born covenant outright*.

**Inna Allāha Kāna ʿalā Kulli Shayʾin Ḥasībā: The Auditor of Greetings**

The attribute-seal twines the verse's seal closes its covenant with its own heaven-watch: *inna Allāha kāna ʿalā kulli shayʾin ḥasīban* — "God surely is a Reckoner over all things": *ḥasīb* — accounter-tracker — from h-s-b — the counter of everything counted (with its standing honors 4:6's *kafā bi-Allāhi ḥasībā* above — and the sufficiency-inflection at 4:81's wakīl/70's ʿalīm/45's aʿlamu — the pericope's audit-attr audit-attributes triangulating together in the seal-cluster) — here assigned specifically over *greetings*: heaven weighing even the "salāmu ʿalaykum" on its ledger — peace-return being no small coin: the hadith-tradition at the the widely-recited allocation-system of aḥsan-return (cf. the standing tip Tirmidhī's own register of Tirmidhī 2689 — greeting-response weights certified in the hadith-books) — while beyond arithmetic the attribute plays its silent pastoral role: men may well where men might count blessings-currency as beneath their stature, the Auditor's ledger never does — the smallest peace-offering between two passers-by is filed in the Book that forgets nothing (99:7-8's atom-weight again) — and the whole sūrah thereby moves from battle-holds into everyday covenant — vv. 85-86 constituting the ummah's two coming two operating protocols of commun of civic grace: intercede for good; greet with peace; know that *both directions of human speech — the plea and the salutation — are banked with God* — so that the believer greets *every soul* with the peace of a man whose Auditor is Ḥasīb, and advances *every case* with the advocacy of a man whose Nourisher is Muqīt.

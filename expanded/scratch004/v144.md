## Sūrah al-Nisāʾ 4:144

> **O you who believe! Do not take the disbelievers as protectors instead of the believers. Do you wish to give God a clear warrant against you?**

**Expanded Commentary**

**The Repetition That Is Not Mere Repetition: v. 144 After v. 139**

The Book now formally reissues, in direct vocative, the prohibition it stated descriptively five verses earlier: *yā ayyuhā alladhīna āmanū lā tattakhidhū al-kāfirīna awliyāʾa min dūni al-muʾminīn* — "O you who believe! Do not take the disbelievers as protectors instead of the believers": v. 139 having exposed the *hypocrites* for this very act in third-person diagnosis, v. 144 turns to *the believers* in second-person command — the standing Qur'ānic method of transmuting others' pathology into the community's immunization: you have seen what the waverers do; now you — the addressed, the professionally believing — are bound by name never to do it. The commentators mark the rhetorical architecture (al-Qurṭubī noting the reinforcement-purpose of the repetition): between the two statements stand vv. 140–143 — the sitting-with-mockers file, the watching-for-outcomes file, the deceiving-God file, the wavering-pendulum file — so that when the prohibition returns in v. 144, it returns *loaded*: no longer an abstract rule of alliance but the seal of a completed anatomy. The believer has been shown the whole career of the false heart: it hedges, it sits, it watches, it prays lazily, it oscillates — and at each station the enabling act is the same — taking its ultimate reliance outside the household of faith. Hence the command's return, now as standing law: **lā tattakhidhū** — take not.

**Min Dūni al-Muʾminīn: The "Instead-of" Clause as the Line Itself**

The boundary-phrase deserves its renewed dwelling — *min dūni al-muʾminīn* — "instead of the believers": the sin located not in contact but in *substitution* — the disbelieving party admitted into the precise slot the Book reserves for God (5:55: "your Protector is but God and His Messenger and those who believe") and for the fraternity it welds (9:71: "the believing men and the believing women are protectors one of another"). The standing legal ethic — reaffirmed at every commentary-generation — keeps the verse from brutal application: dealings, treaties, neighborliness, commerce, and kindness stand lawful with all peaceful people (60:8's standing injunction balancing the prohibition-file); what stands forbidden is the walāyah-transfer — the heart's own embassy moved to a foreign capital: protection sought there first, secrets deposited there first, futures wagered there first. The tradition's standing counsel-name for this posture: "whoever resembles a people is one of them" (the standing register in Abū Dāwūd 4031's hadith-collection) — resemblance not of dress but of ultimate reliance — the believer's signature being precisely *where* he deposits his back-up plan (4:132's standing fiduciary clause hovering above the entire dossier: "God suffices as a Guardian").

**A-Turīdūna an Tajʿalū Li-llāhi ʿAlaykum Sulṭānan Mubīnan: Handing God the Brief Against Oneself**

Then the verse's formidable second half — its interrogative thunderclap: *a-turīdūna an tajʿalū li-llāhi ʿalaykum sulṭānan mubīnan* — "do you wish to give God a clear warrant against you?": *sulṭān* — from s-l-ṭ — authority, warrant, power-of-proof: the word the Book uses for a *demonstrated authorization* — an open-and-shut evidentiary warrant (its standing register: 4:91 above — "against them We have given you a clear warrant"; 6:81; 14:10 — "have you brought us a clear warrant?"; 27:21; 37:156; 52:38); and *mubīn* — manifest, self-evidencing — the sulṭān that needs no elaboration because the record itself speaks. The rhetorical shape of the phrase the commentators (al-Rāzī dwelling on the horror of its logic) unfold: ordinarily, the accused before God might plead complicated circumstances, mixed motives, partial innocence — the standing hope of every defendant; but the believer who *knowingly, covenantedly* transfers his reliance to the enemies of his faith, while bearing the name of believer, has done something juridically unique — he has *prepared and handed over the prosecution's complete brief*: his conduct itself becomes God's sulṭān mubīn against him — a warrant so clear no cross-examination is needed. The image is courtroom-reversed: the defendant furnishing the court its decisive evidence — and the question *a-turīdūna* — "do you *wish* this?" — exposing the act's voluntariness: nobody sleepwalks into this warrant; it is signed. The standing spiritual application generalizes its discipline: every deliberate sin performed open-eyed after knowledge is, in miniature, the furnishing of a sulṭān — the tradition's standing counsels of concealment and repentance (v. 148 below; the Prophet's standing word in Muslim's register 2990: "all my community shall be pardoned except those who sin openly") arranged precisely to keep the believer from ever converting his falls into *warrants* — which is why the dossier's next verse, announcing the lowest depth, hurries to add its exception-clause: the door of repentance the only legal instrument that voids a sulṭān already issued.

## Sūrah al-Nisāʾ 4:135

> **O you who believe — be ever upstanding in equity, witnesses for God, even though it be against yourselves, or parents, or kindred. Whether one be rich or poor — God is worthier of them both. So follow not caprice, lest you swerve. And if you twist or turn away — then God is ever Aware of whatsoever you do.**

**Expanded Commentary**

**Kūnū Qawwāmīna bi-l-Qisṭ: The Standing-Posture of the Whole Religion**

The sūrah now arrives at one of the one of the load-bearing walls of the entire Qur'ānic ethic — a verse the community's jurists, preachers, and reformers have quoted without pause: *kūnū qawwāmīna bi-l-qisṭi shuhadāʾa li-llāhi wa law ʿalā anfusikum* — "be ever upstanding in equity, witnesses for God, even though it be against yourselves": *qawwāmīn* — the intensive plural of qāʾim — those who stand, remain standing, stand to maintain: not occasional dispensers of justice but its standing custodians — the intensive form (faʿʿāl) carrying constancy through difficulty — and it is the very posture the Book attributes to God Himself: 3:18's standing predicate — "God — bearing witness that there is no god but He — and the angels and those possessed of knowledge — *qāʾiman bi-l-qisṭ* — standing-firm in equity" (and 5:8's standing twin: "be upstanding for God, witnesses in equity — and let not hatred of a people move you to be unjust — be just — that is nearer to mindfulness"). *Qisṭ* — the portion-justice, the apportioning of each his exact due (v. 127's standing station — "that you stand firm for the orphans with qisṭ") — versus *ʿadl* — the rectification-justice: the two words of the Book's justice-vocabulary meeting here in the community's standing charter. The commentators remark on the command's economy: *kūnū* — "be" — not "do" — justice demanded as a state of being, an identity, before it is demanded as an act; the ummah called to *be* upstanders the way it is called to *be* believers.

**Shuhadāʾa Li-llāhi: Testimony as Property of God**

Then the phrase that converts every courtroom into a sanctuary: *shuhadāʾa li-llāh* — "witnesses for God": the testimony belongs to God before it belongs to the plaintiff — al-Ṭabarī's standing paraphrase fixing the meaning: "uphold justice for the sake of God when you bear witness" — and the standing mystical commentary of Rūzbihān al-Baqlī deepening it: "the spirit of tawḥīd will not take root in a person's heart so long as God holds upon him a claim he has not fulfilled" — truthful witnessing being precisely one of those standing claims, a debt owed to God that speech must discharge. The standing witness-file of the Book stands behind the phrase: testimony required for commercial writing (2:282's standing ordinance — "and bring witnesses when you transact"); for the orphan's inheritance-handover (4:6 above — "when you deliver to them their property — take witnesses over them"); for the gravest criminal charges (4:15's standing four-witness rule; 24:2–9's liʿān machinery) — the whole legal order resting on human throats that tell the truth — and 22:78's standing honor-title — "that the Messenger may be a witness over you, and you may be witnesses over mankind" — showing the courtroom-duty to be the local form of the ummah's cosmic vocation. Hence the verse's terrifying pronoun: *li-llāh* — *for God* — not for the clan, not for the co-religionist, not for the poor against the rich nor the rich against the poor: the witness serves a third Party who is present at every testimony — and 25:72’s standing praise of "those who do not bear false witness" marking the counterfeit as the standing disqualification.

**Wa Law ʿAlā Anfusikum: The Hardest Direction First**

The test-cases then proceed in ascending order of cost: *wa law ʿalā anfusikum awi al-wālidayni wa al-aqrabīn* — "even though it be against yourselves, or parents, or kindred": the self named *first* — the commentators (Ibn Kathīr's standing summary) noting the order as deliberate pedagogy: whoever will testify against his own interest has inoculated himself against the subtler corruptions; and parents and kindred placed next because kinship is the oldest pressure-chamber of false witness — the Ṭuʿmah episode of vv. 105–115 being the sūrah's own standing illustration (a clan rallying to defend its guilty kinsman against the innocent Jew — al-Ṭabarī's standing Sabāb-file, which the commentators fold back under this verse: had Ṭuʿmah's clan lived by v. 135, the whole affair would have ended in a confession instead of an apostasy). The standing duty-priority established: the deference owed to parents (17:23's standing ordinance — the very next-nearest command in the Book's ethics) does not extend to shielding them from truthful testimony — kindness to parents operates everywhere *except* inside the witness-box; there, only God is parent. The hadith-register preserves the standing report of the Prophet's own calibration — asked to do justice in the case of a poor reported in connection with this verse: an incident in which the Prophet inclined in judgment toward a poor man, thinking him incapable of truly wronging the wealthier, whereupon the reminder descended: wealth and poverty are both false objects of tilt — *immā yakun ghaniyyan aw faqīran fa-Allāhu awlā bihimā* — "whether one be rich or poor — God is worthier of them both": *awlā* — nearer, worthier, more entitled — the poor man's advocacy belongs to God, not to the sentimental witness; the rich man's customary immunity likewise belongs to God, not to the bought witness — judge neither *for* the wallet nor *against* it: the arbitration of the classes already assigned to the Court above, which (the commentators add, folding v. 130 back in) has promised to enrich *both* from His bounty.

**Fa-Lā Tattabiʿū al-Hawā an Taʿdilū: Caprice — the One Banned Advocate**

The verse then names the only counsel excluded from the bar: *fa-lā tattabiʿū al-hawā an taʿdilū* — "so follow not caprice, lest you swerve": *hawā* — from h-w-y — falling, then the falling-desire, caprice (its standing book-file: 2:120; 2:145; 5:48–49's standing triple-warning to the Prophet himself — "judge between them by what God has revealed — and follow not their caprices"; 6:150; 18:28 — "obey not him whose heart We have made heedless of Our remembrance and who follows his caprice"; 23:71 — "had the truth followed their caprices — the heavens and the earth and whosoever is in them had been corrupted"; 38:26 — "David — We have made you a vicegerent in the earth — so judge between the people in truth and follow not caprice — for it shall lead you astray from God's way"; 45:23; 53:3's standing exemption — "nor does he speak from caprice" — of the Prophet alone; 79:40–41) — and the Book's standing double-reading of *taʿdilū*: the verb from ʿ-d-l meaning both "to act justly" and — by a celebrated lexical fork noted by al-Rāzī and al-Bayḍāwī — "to swerve aside": so the clause reads equally "follow not caprice *that you may be just*" (caprice the enemy of justice) or "follow not caprice *lest you swerve*" (caprice the author of deviation) — and the commentators remark that the two senses compose one counsel: justice is precisely the straight line from which caprice bends the witness. The verse's own book-kin finish the inventory of banned bends: 5:8's standing twin ("let not hatred of a people move you not to be just"); 24:2's standing flogging-verse ("let not pity for them seize you concerning the religion of God") — hatred banned in one verse, pity in another, self-interest and kinship in this one — the Book having by now fenced the witness-stand against every affection, noble or base.

**Wa In Talwū aw Tuʿriḍū: The Twist and the Turn, and the Khabīr Who Sees Both**

The ending names the two failings of the actual witness and hands both to the Expert-Aware: *wa in talwū aw tuʿriḍū fa-inna Allāha kāna bimā taʿmalūna khabīran* — "and if you twist or turn away — then God is ever Aware of whatsoever you do": *talwū* — from l-w-y — twisting — the standing term for distorting testimony or scripture alike (its standing file: 3:78 — "they twist their tongues with the Book that you may suppose it from the Book"; 2:75’s men "who heard the speech of God then tampered with it"; the very shape of the tampering witness: the words turned on the tongue until they face falsehood's direction); *tuʿriḍū* — turning away (v. 80's standing word — v. 128's iʿrāḍ) — the sin of silence: withholding the testimony altogether, declining the witness-duty when it is owed (its standing prohibition: 2:283 — "let not the witness refuse when he is called"; and 2:282's terminal warning — "he who conceals it — his heart is sinful") — so that the verse covers the whole crime-spectrum of the compromised witness: active distortion and passive refusal. And over both: *khabīr* — the Expert-Aware — the standing auditor-Name of the sūrah (v. 35 — "God is ever Knowing, Aware"; v. 94's standing seal; v. 128's station just above) — here placed with alchemical precision: a witness can twist words past the whole courtroom and the whole press of Madīnah, and turn away from the whole bench — but the Khabīr's expertise is precisely in interiors, in the grain of intentions under the grain of words: the verse sealing thereby the sūrah's longest-running lesson — from v. 86's greeting-judgment through the Ṭuʿmah-file and the family-file — that the community which would stand for equity must stand first before the Aware One who reads the standing posture itself.

## Sūrah al-Nisāʾ 4:176

> **They seek a ruling from thee. Say: "God gives you a ruling concerning the one without direct heir. If a man dies having no child, and he has a sister, then unto her belongs half of what he leaves; and he shall inherit from her if she has no child. And if there be two sisters, then unto them belongs two-thirds of what he leaves. And if they be brethren, men and women, then unto the male a share equal to that of two females." God makes this clear to you, lest you should go astray; and God is ever Knower of all things.**

**Expanded Commentary**

**Yastaftūnaka: The Consultation That Closes the Sūrah — and Its Occasion**

The sūrah ends as its family-file began — with a question brought to the Prophet: *yastaftūnaka quli Allāhu yuftīkum fī al-kalālah* — "they seek a ruling from thee. Say: God gives you a ruling concerning the one without direct heir": the *yastaftūnaka*-vocative completing the sūrah's own consultation-ring of standing responsa (4:127 above — "they consult thee concerning the women — say: God gives you the ruling concerning them"; and the standing Book-file: 2:217; 2:219-220; 5:4; 8:1) — the commentators marking the symmetrical architecture: the Women-consultation opened the sūrah's second great legal movement; the Kalālah-consultation closes the whole chapter — law as dialog with heaven from first to last. And the standing occasion-reports anchor the verse in a named Companion's household: Jābir ibn ʿAbd Allāh's celebrated question — its standing register in the tafsīr-files (al-Qurṭubī's standing report; al-Ṭabarī's chains; al-Wāḥidī's asbāb-file): Jābir, sick, spoke to the Prophet — "I have sisters; how shall they inherit?" — being a man without direct heirs, his sisters watching his estate — and the verse descended in answer: nine sisters in the standing reports — their share under the earlier ruling of v. 12 (one-third among them, undifferentiated) now enlarged to two-thirds — the case of Jābir's sisters standing in the tradition as the concrete estate-file behind the final verse. A second standing context-name enters the commentators' register here — the reports of al-Barāʾ ibn ʿĀzib that this was the last verse of the Qur'ān revealed to the Prophet: "the last verse revealed was the verse of kalālah" — its standing attestation in the tafsīr-records (others of the standing file attaching last-verse candidacy to 2:281 — "and fear a day when you shall be returned to God" — and to 5:3 — "this day I have perfected your religion for you"; the standing aggregate of the commentaries balancing these multiple eyewitness-traditions) — the sūrah's final verse thereby standing, in one prominent Companion's memory, at the very threshold of revelation's completion: law's last address on earth settling the estate of a man without heirs of his own body.

**Fī al-Kalālah: The Orphaned Estate — the Standing Definition**

The ruling-object is named: *al-kalālah* — the one without direct heirs: its standing lexical definition in the comment-canon: one who dies without *father or son* (per the dominant early register, via ʿUmar's own celebrated perplexity-report standing in the collections — "three things I would rather the Prophet had settled for us before leaving us: the share of al-kalālah (kalālah), and the doors of ribā" — its standing register in the Musnad and Sunan-files) — some readings extending to *parent or child* (al-Qurṭubī's standing note of the definitional span: no direct line ascending or descending), with Ibn ʿAbbās's standing refinement in the commentators' files: a daughter (like a son) prevents kalālah-status — the Twelver Shīʿī register concurring — so the sister-inheritance of this verse activates only when no son *and no daughter* survives (others of the early register reading the verse's sister-share as additive co-inheritance alongside a daughter).

**The Standing Shares: Half, Two-Thirds, and the Male-Equals-Two Rule**

The verse then legislates its three estate-cases with mathematical finality: (1) *in imruʾun halaka laysa lahu waladun wa lahu ukhtun fa-lahā niṣfu mā tarak* — "if a man dies having no child, and he has a sister, then unto her belongs half of what he leaves": the single sister of the kalālah raised from the undifferentiated "fī al-kalālah" of v. 12 (one-third jointly for siblings-unspecified) to a full half — *niṣf* — the largest single collateral-share in the whole inheritance-canon; *wa huwa yarithuhā in lam yakun lahā walad* — "and he shall inherit from her if she has no child": the standing reciprocity-clause — the brother inheriting *all* of his kalālah-sister's estate (the jurists' standing note: the sister's share of her brother is *assigned* (f-arḍ — half), while the brother's share of his sister is *residuary* (ʿaṣabah — everything after assigned shares) — the standing asymmetry matched to the sūrah's own standing male-maintenance rationale of 4:11's commentary-file); (2) *fa-in kānatā ithnatayni fa-lahumā al-thuluthāni mimmā tarak* — "and if there be two sisters, then unto them belongs two-thirds of what he leaves": the two-sister file of the kalālah matched to the standing 4:11 rule for two-or-more daughters (two-thirds) — Jābir's nine sisters, in the sabab-reports, reaching this station; (3) *wa in kānū ikhwatan rijālan wa nisāʾan fa-li-l-dhakari mithlu ḥaẓẓi al-unthayayn* — "and if they be brethren, men and women, then unto the male a share equal to that of two females": the mixed-brethren file applying the standing pair-rule of the sūrah's inheritance-tract (4:11's principle standing across the verse) — the commentators' standing clarification: the mixed siblings sharing the whole kalālah-estate as residuary (ʿaṣabāt) with the male double-share principle governing internally. And the standing architecture the jurists registered: the two kalālah-verses (4:12 and 4:176) legislated as one doctrine across the two ends of the chapter — v. 12 fixing the *uterine* siblings (the mother's-side brethren: one-sixth singles, one-third jointly, shared equally male-and-female — the standing exception-cases), v. 176 fixing the *full/consanguine* siblings of the kalālah — the jurists' standing commentary (al-Ṭabarī's standing derivation; al-Qurṭubī's chapters; the codified fiqh-registers descending from both) reading the verse-number of the sūrah itself as the place where the inheritance-canon of the Qur'ān reaches its completion: beginning at 4:7-12 among spouses-parents-children, closing at 4:176 among brothers-and-sisters-of-the-childless — the chapter of kin-and-justice framing the whole law of bequest between its own alpha and omega.

**Yubayyinu Allāhu Lakum an Taḍillū: Clarification Against Error — and the Closing Name**

The final verse of the sūrah seals with the standing clarity-clause and the final Name: *yubayyinu Allāhu lakum an taḍillū wa Allāhu bi-kulli shayʾin ʿalīm* — "God makes this clear to you, lest you should go astray; and God is ever Knower of all things": *yubayyin* — the standing making-clear (4:26 — "God wishes to make clear to you"; 2:70's its standing stations: 2:109; 2:187; 2:221; 2:242; 2:266; 3:103; 5:89; 24:18; 24:58-59; 24:61) — the commentators gathering its standing formulation: the rulings of inheritance are called *bayān* — clarification — because estates-truths are precisely standing temptations to self-dealing (the sūrah's own opening-file of orphan-consumption standing at 4:2-10; 4:30's false-consumption warning at its counterweight) — *an taḍillū* — "lest you should go astray" — the particle of precaution sealing the entire legal corpus as navigation-aids against error: law as lighthouse, not as cage. And the closing Name — "*bi-kulli shayʾin ʿalīm*" — Knower of all things — the standing talismanic concluding Name of the sūrah's most repeated cadence (its stations throughout the chapter: 4:32; 4:176; and the wider standing Book-register: 2:29; 2:284; 5:97; 8:75; 9:115; 24:64; 33:54; 57:3; 64:11) — sealing the estate-file with the same Watching-Knowledge that the sūrah's first verse posted over the whole chapter: *fa-inna Allāha kāna ʿalaykum raqīban* — "verily God is ever over you a Watcher" (4:1). Between 4:1's Watcher and 4:176's Knower-of-all-things, 176 verses have legislated a whole cosmos of conduct: orphans guarded, women apportioned, marriages fenced, names honored, households arbitrated, orphans-and-widows restored, prayers disciplined, hypocrites anatomized, covenants remembered, witnesses exalted, Jesus confessed, estates divided — the sūrah of al-Nisāʾ closing on the small print of a sister's half-share, as if to declare one last time that heaven's proof-and-light descends into the dustiest estate-file no less than into the creed: God Knower of all things — of the heart of the orphan, of the scale of the arbitrator, of the hope of Jābir's nine sisters — and that is where the chapter, and this commentary, sets down its pen.

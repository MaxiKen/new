## Sūrah al-Nisāʾ 4:110

> **Whosoever does evil or wrongs himself, and then seeks forgiveness of God, he will find God Forgiving, Merciful.**

**Expanded Commentary**

**Wa Man Yaʿmal Sūʾan Aw Yaẓlim Nafsahu: Evil-Work and Self-Wrong, the Two Formal Doors**

The court-block's arraignments close at last with the one door heaven itself leaves open: *wa man yaʿmala sūʾan aw yaẓlim nafsahu* — "whosoever does evil or wrongs himself": the two terms being the tradition's standing predicate-pair for the whole sin-map: (1) *sūʾ* — evil — the generic ill-work (from s-w-ʾ — badness itself — the Book's standing term for deeds that foul the ledger — with the commentators distinguishing its register: sūʾ the wide net — every transgression, small or great minor or major — and (2) *yaẓlim nafsahu* — wrongs his own self — the sūrah's now-established reflexive formula (v. 107's yakhtānūna anfusahum; v. 111's yakṣib ithman fa-innamā yakṣibuhu ʿalā nafsihi — the self-dealt the deal-self grammar recurring in this block like the tolling of a bell: all wrongdoing finalizes upon the doer — the object-noun carrying the doer's own name) — with the majority rolling the pair as two-fold as a rhetorical doubling for breadth (wrong done and wrong sustained: act of perpetration plus the self-damage of perpetration) — al-Ṭabarī's own reading al-Ṭabarī's particular elegance: the two phrases mapping the treacherous thief and his pleading-clan alike — Ṭuʿmah's sūʾ lying in the theft-and-plant; the clan's his own ẓulm-on-himself lying in leading the accused lying in recruiting his clan to campaign for the frame-up the hadith-comment register the Ṭabarī-register expanding the pair over both sinner and defender, exactly as the block's own final as the commentary tradition's overall as Ibn Kathīr summarized it: the promise is extended both to the treacherous wrongdoers and to those who would wrongfully seek to defend them.

**Thumma Yastaghfir Allāh Yajid Allāha Ghafūran Raḥīmā: The Forgiving-Sequence Undertaken**

Then the protocol — exactly the two-move rite: *thumma yastaghfir Allāh* — "then seeks forgiveness of God": *thumma* — then — the sequence-particle (the sin first, the istighfār second — God's merciful grammar never the grammar omitting the commentators' standing subtlety: thumma may denote either chronological sequence — the natural order of remorse — or the standing hadith-grammar of or an allowance that God's prerogative takes place *after* the deed has occurred, not in place not in sanction of it — never before it) — *yastaghfiru* — seeks forgiveness — the verb-form active (the standing reflection: forgiveness must be *asked*, not merely presumed — heaven's mercy being free but not *draftable*: the beloved hadith-teaching at its station the classic hadith at Muslim 2759 — "by Him the Prophet's own emblem: *by Him in Whose hand is my soul, did you not sin, God would do away with you and bring a people who do sin and seek His forgiveness, and He would forgive them"* — Muslim 2749 — with its tonic pair with the Prophet's own plenary seal of tawbah-petitioning: "all the children of Adam are sinners — and the best of sinners are the repenters" — Tirmidhī 2499, Ibn Mājah 4251) — and the respond response-verb following with symmetry poetic to letter-place with the symmetry the whole tafsīr-canon cherishes: *yajid Allāha ghafūran raḥīman* — "he finds God Forgiving, Merciful": *yajida* — he *finds* — from w-j-d — discovery (the commentators' standing awe at this one verb's choice: the seeker does not merely obtain is not informed of pardon — he *finds* — forgiveness offered standing already-present — God antecedent-positioned at the very station of the seeker's arrival — likened in the tradition drawing it against the wording image the standing comment-image: (al-Qushayrī's register among the Sufi schools: istighfār as petition for what God *already has* — the finding-verb lifting forgiveness from contingent-decree to everlasting-attribute — the pardon- the Pre-Eternal turning where the repentant raises his hands, he finds that God had preceded his uplifted hands by forever) — with the coupling returning: *ghafūran raḥīman* — Forgiving, Merciful — the block's own closing twin-attribute its own closing attributes — v. 106's identical pair — the block's mercy-frame thus completely arched: judge commanded to istighfār — v. 106 — treacherous promised pardon on the same rite — v. 110 — each receiving the same attribute-guarantee — Ghafūr upon the upon the ledger, Raḥīm upon the seeker's road thereafter.

**Whom the Verse Addresses: The Canon of Tawbah upon a Treachery-File**

The commentators' lasting treatment thereby widens v. 110 into the standing tawbah-canon: (1) its subject — *man* — whosoever — unrestricted by sin-class (even the frame-up-artist, even the night-conspirator, even the man who planted evidence on the innocent); (2) its condition — istighfār — and the classical theology at once unfolds the operation beyond its mere prayer to the required its standing definition from the hadith-canon: true istighfār carries its three its classical shaping the standing teaching-shape of the classical shaping: regret (nadm) — cessation (iqlāʿ — immediate stopping) — resolve (ʿazm — a binding resolve against return) — with right-restitution (radd al-maẓālim) wherever a third party's rights stand — and here the tafsīr-canon's own standing note exactly fitting the Ṭuʿmah-block: had the thief sought forgiveness *with* restitution — the planted the framed victim exonerated the framed Jew publicly cleared, the armor returned — repentance would have been live; without it, the istighfār-voice stands alone as mere petition as mere affecting as mere word-work; and (3) its certainty — *yajida* — the verb's own performative pledge (like the standing exegetical placed side-by-side with 39:53's — "O My servants who have been prodigal against their own souls — despair not of God's mercy — God forgives all sins — He is the Forgiving, the Merciful") — thereby ending the sūrah's whole judicial affair of Ṭuʿmah ibn Ubayriq not with condemnation but with its final standing syllabus of hope: cheat crimes wrought by night, pled for by clans, and exposed by revelation all stand under the same final clause: *whosoever wrongs — let him, by the rite of two moves, seek the Forgiving — and he will find.* The eloquent pleas of men were shut; the door of God's finding remained open — and that is the sentence under which the entire canopy of the entire sūrah-block takes its rest finally.

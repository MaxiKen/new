## Sūrah al-Nisāʾ 4:94

> **O you who believe, when you journey in the way of God, exercise discernment, and say not to one who offers you peace: "Thou art no believer" — seeking the ephemeral goods of the life of this world; for with God are gains in abundance. Even so were you aforetime, but God was gracious unto you; so exercise discernment; surely God is Aware of whatsoever you do.**

**Expanded Commentary**

**Yā Ayyuhā Alladhīna Āmanū Idhā Ḍarabtum fī Sabīli Allāhi fa-Tabayyanū: The Discernment Command of the Road**

The war-canon completes its educe its edifice with the operational rule for borderlines: *yā ayyuhā alladhīna āmanū idhā ḍarabtum fī sabīli Allāhi fa-tabayyanū* — "O you who believe — when you march in the way of God — discern": *ḍarabtum* — you struck-traveled (ḍ-r-b — the Arabic idiom of road-threshing — the road struck underfoot — the march given archaic physicality) — and *fa-tabayyanū* — *exercise tabayyun* — clarify, verify, distinguish (b-y-n — the word drew down the whole verification-law of Islam: under its from it all the comment-traditions deduce the juridical war-sift — distinguish combatant from surrenderee, treaty-man from hostile, shahādah-bearer from battle-flag man) — from the asbāb-line which the tradition's own preserved the most-accepted report of the occasion being the Companions' foray upon a band of herdsmen i.e., a raiding-E the canonical account (al-Wāḥidī; al-Ṭabarī collations; Ibn Kathīr) of al-Miqdād ibn ʿAmr (or Usāmah ibn Zayd in its sannah in the Sunan's adjacent account): the Muslims overtake a man from the tribes on the way, whose gossip whose flocks he was guarding who, seeing the raid, declares *aslamtu* — "I have submitted" — and is slain notwithstanding by the pursuer, his spoils taken — whereupon the verse descends chastising: he offered you *peace* before your sword — and the seal-history standing as the tradition's own marker: the Prophet's standing rebuke to Usāmah — the hadith-engraved exchange pairing recorded at Bukhārī at Bukhārī at Muslim 96 — "You killed him after he said: *lā ilāha illa Allāh*!" — Usāmah: "It was but the fear-cry of the sword." — The Prophet: *"did you split open his heart — to know whether he said it truly or not?"* — and its echo without date until its second line — *"what will you do with lā ilāha illa Allāh, on the Day of Resurrection, when it comes?"* — Usāmah's own testimony: from that day he resolved never to strike anyone who says lā ilāha illa Allāh Usāmah: I wished I had not become Muslim before that the shock of the report being its own standing canon.

**Lā Taqūlū li-Man Alqā Ilaykumu al-Salāma Lasta Muʾminan Taghtamma bi-Ajn Tabtaghū ʿAraḍa al-Ḥayāti al-Dunyā: Spoil-Motive Invalidates Sword-Reading**

Then the prohibited utterance named: *lā taqūlū li-man alqā ilaykumu al-salāma lasta muʾminan — tabtaghū ʿarada al-ḥayāti al-dunyā* — "say not to him who casts peace toward you: Thou art no believer — pursuing the fleeting stuff of worldly life": *alqā ilaykumu al-salām* — the peace-tossing recur recurring v. 90's affection-root (al-salm — peace-casting) — and the revelation's psychology, one of its keenest dissections of warfare-justification: the slayer who overrides peace-declaration *because spoils await* does not merely make an error — the verse identifies the real engine: *tabtaghū ʿaraḍa al-ḥayāti al-dunyā* — "thou seekest the ephemeral goods of the mundane life": *ʿaraḍ* — ephemera, trade-wares, an article passing in offer (the classical turning of the root across the Book: what lies to hand to be used-up — cf. 8:67's and the worldly doctrines) — the depth-anatomy: booty rewrites the operational diagnosis: if the only evidence you slay him is his flocks and his camel-goods, *you did not verify a threat; you wanted his goods* — the verse erecting the standing prohibition of *motivated verification-failure*: where men define threat with their purse — classic with the Book's standing cousin-law confirming the same engine across the whole canon-family of battle-abuse texts (9:5's treaty-exception clause; 60:8's "God forbids you not those who fought you not in religion"; plus the great canonical import of 4:94 into the law of combatant-safety: al-salām or shahādah uttered at sword-point grants *safety presumptively* — the battle ʿamal the operative presumption shifts: certify later, strike never).

**Fa-ʿinda Allāhi Maghānamu Kathīrah: The Spoils at God's Treasury, Not at Men's Throats**

Against that petty spoil-lust the verse holds up its mirroring counter-treasury: *fa-ʿinda Allāhi maghānam kathīrah* — "with God are gains in abundance": *maghānam* — spoils-plural — from gh-n-m — war-gain-word itself (the Book's own classic term for godly war-boo for warfare spoils — 8:69's "enjoy what you take in ghanīmah as lawful and good"; 8:41's khums-code; and the exact antithesis set out: spoil-eyes drawn down lift the spoil-desire from roadside corpses from roadside flock-keeping — the war-economy the sūrah now draws as exact antithesis: the wealth-fields of heaven's own treasury where gains exist in *plural-abundance* — kathīrah — the plenty hanging from God, whom no raid limits that no raid can corner) — then the sūrah's tender reversal: kadhālika kuntum min qablu fa-manna Allāhu ʿalaykum* — "so were you aforetime — but God was gracious to you": you — the Madinan fighters — were yourselves once like that herdsman — a despised, poor, hidden, weak — Mohammed before the war-booty era, hiding your shahādahs from persecutor-swords in Makkah (cf. the sūrah's own prior memory at v. 75 — the mustaḍʿafūn; 3:123's Badr-address — "you were lowly, and He supported you"; and 2:267 8:26 — "remember when you were few, deemed weak in the land") — *fa-manna Allāhu ʿalaykum* — *but God conferred grace* — mann-word (the Book's own verb for the gracious-bestowal that arriveth down that uplifts the lower without demeaning — it is also the word of every prophetic biography — 28:86 God favored Moses with messengership; 49:17's "He favored you by guiding you to faith") — and then the closing of the whole counter-file: *fa-tabayyanū inna Allāha kāna bimā taʿmalūna khabīrā* — "so discern — God is ever Aware of whatsoever you do": *khabīr* — Aware through-and-through (the audit-attribute returning the sūrah's own pericope — v. 35's khabīr-seal above; the internal-edges the engine that counts even impulse) — the war-law thereby closing on the same ground it opened upon at 4:84-86: an army under God audits its own trigger-hands triggers — because the treasury of true gains is with God, the motive of every strike must be so too — and from this one verse the tradition drew its standing military ethics: presumption of brotherhood upon peace-cry; audit of suspicion before strike; spoils regarded as God's own estate; and the believer-warrior remembering *once you were the low man at the roadside too*.

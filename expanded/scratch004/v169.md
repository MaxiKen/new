## Sūrah al-Nisāʾ 4:169

> **Save the path of Hell — abiding therein forever; and that is ever easy for God.**

**Expanded Commentary**

**Illā Ṭarīqa Jahannam: The Exception That Is the Sentence — The Only Road Left**

The verse completes v. 168 with the Book's most terrible exception-construction: *illā ṭarīqa jahannama khālidīna fīhā abadan* — "save the path of Hell — abiding therein forever": the grammatical exceptive (*illā* — "save/except") performing here its darkest possible office: after the twin withdrawal of v. 168 (no forgiveness, no guidance *unto a path*), the exception should — by every habit of the exceptive form — soften the sentence; instead it *specifies the one withholding still permitted to reach them*: one way only — and it is the way to the Fire. The commentators (al-Ṭabarī's gloss standing first) mark the rhetorical thunder of the construction: God does not withhold *all* paths from them — He withholds all paths but one, and that one He opens — direction itself becoming penalty: guidance now reduced to the road down (its standing structural companions in the Book's register of total-negation exception-clauses: 4:48's pairing above; 4:137's earlier arrangement; and the parallel anatomy at 47:34-35 — "those who disbelieve and bar from God's way, then die as disbelievers — God will not forgive them. So do not slacken and call for peace, while you are the uppermost — and God is with you and will not deprive you of your deeds" — the standing whole-context: the obstructor's way closed to all destinations but the refusal-earned terminus).

**Khālidīna Fīhā Abadan: Permanence Doubled**

The abode-clause doubles perpetuity upon perpetuity: *khālidīna fīhā abadan* — "abiding therein forever": *khālidīna* — from kh-l-d — to abide without departure — the standing adjective of no-exit already drilled through the sūrah's standing register (4:13's Garden-abiding; 4:14's Fire-abiding; 4:57; 4:93; 4:121-122's doubled pair) — followed by *abadan* — everlastingly, without terminus — the standing adverbial doubling the participle (its standing stations: 4:57; 4:122 — "abiding therein forever"; its register across the Book: 2:167; 3:116; 5:37 — "they will wish to exit the Fire — and they will not exit it — and theirs is a lasting punishment"; 9:100's opposite-register; 18:108; 33:65; 43:28; 50:34 — "enter it in peace — that is the Day of abiding"; 64:9; 72:23; 98:6; 98:8) — the commentators (al-Rāzī's standing scholastic note upon every abad-verse) marking that the Book's usages of doubled permanence deliberately foreclose the speculation of termination: not age upon age, but the standing absolute: no exit-clause, no sunrise — and beside it the standing counter-quadrant held through this very chapter: 4:57; 4:122's *khālidīna fīhā abadan* for the believing worker — permanence promised with the identical grammar to the opposite direction — the symmetrical architecture insisting that between the two permanences stands only the road taken here.

**Wa Kāna Dhālika ʿAlā Allāhi Yasīran: The Ease Declared**

The verse closes with the standing ease-formula — and the commentators mark its placement as its meaning: *wa kāna dhālika ʿalā Allāhi yasīran* — "and that is ever easy for God": *yasīr* — from y-s-r — ease, facility: not "light" as trivial, but: *costless to the Doer* — the standing Book-register of the formula at sites of overwhelming divine acts (14:20 — "nor was that difficult for God" — of the removal-and-replacement of peoples; 29:19 — "have they not seen how God originates creation, then repeats it — verily that is easy for God"; 30:27 — "He it is who originates creation, then repeats it — and it is easier yet for Him"; 35:41; 50:44 — "the day the earth splits asunder about them — [they come] hastening — that assembling is easy for Us"; 57:22); — the commentators reading its audience-prepared gravity here in two standing modes: (1) against presumption: let none suppose the sentence upon the obstructor-class arduous to execute, against old ties, tribal immunities, public standing — *easy* — requiring of God no effort, no negotiation, no hesitation: the standing hadith-register of the divine oath at service ("I have made injustice unlawful for Myself..." — Muslim 2577) standing beside this ease: what He reserves to Himself by covenant, He executes by facility; and (2) against presumption's mirror — despair in the opposite audience: for the mercy-files of the Book bear the same ease-vocabulary in their direction (the standing complement at 35:17 — "that is not difficult for God"; 11:4; "the matter of the Hour is but as the twinkling of an eye, or nearer") — the ease-attribute resting beside every announced divine act, so that the reader understands: judgment's execution and mercy's bestowal share the same standing facility — neither is deferred for effort. The verse takes its standing place within the People-of-the-Book movement's two-way register: vv. 162-166 having just delivered the promise-lines (great reward; witness and sufficiency-file), vv. 167-169 now deliver the mirror-file — disbelief-plus-obstruction, disbelief-plus-wrongdoing — no forgiveness, no guidance, no way but one, forever, at no cost to the Judge — the architectural pattern the whole sūrah has kept from its first verses: doors in pairs: the door of the orphan carefully apportioned and the fire for the devourer (4:10); the Garden for the just and the flame for the limit-transgressor (4:13-14); the repentance-door ever open on earth, and the no-exit door beyond it (4:17-18) — 4:169 the bookend of this standing diptych, written not to close hope but to make its remaining corridor unmistakable: every gate beside the one is still open — *now* — and "that is easy for God."

## Sūrah al-Nisāʾ 4:137

> **Verily those who believe, then disbelieve, then believe, then disbelieve, and then increase in disbelief — God was not one to forgive them nor to guide them unto a way.**

**Expanded Commentary**

**Inna Alladhīna Āmanū Thumma Kafarū: The Oscillation-Diagnosis**

The archipelago-verse of v. 136 having defined what belief must hold, the Book now diagnoses the heart that cannot hold it: *inna alladhīna āmanū thumma kafarū thumma āmanū thumma kafarū thumma izdādū kufran* — "verily those who believe, then disbelieve, then believe, then disbelieve, and then increase in disbelief": a four-step oscillation crowned by a fifth movement that is different in kind — *izdādū* — from z-y-d — increase: the state graduating from alternation into a settled momentum. The commentators (al-Ṭabarī gathering the earliest readings) localize the pattern: most likely the hypocrites of Madīnah, whose outward-inward oscillation the coming verses (vv. 138–146) will anatomize at length — the structural argument being decisive: this verse forms the heading of that entire hypocrisy-file, so its referent must be the wavering-in-sincerity rather than those who never claimed Islam at all. Others among the early reading-traditions localized it in those among the People of the Book who believed in their own scriptures, disbelieved in what came after, "believed" outwardly in the Prophet's presence, disbelieved in his absence, and increased thereby in a compound estrangement (3:72's standing stratagem-file: "a faction of the People of the Book said: believe in what was sent down upon the believers at the day's opening — and disbelieve at its end — perhaps they will turn back"); early Shīʿī tafsīr applied it to those who manifested faith in the Prophet's lifetime then refused ʿAlī's authority; but the flow of context — the hypocrisy-chapter opening here and running to v. 146 — anchors the first reading as the standing one.

**The Threefold Repentance-Question: A Door the Verse Both Opens and Narrows**

Yet the verse's most debated legacy lives in its own grammar: disbelief is mentioned after belief *twice* — and then the increase — before the warning is issued. Al-Ṭabarī records the juristic corollary drawn by some early authorities: since the pattern allows two returns before the hardening, some held the repentance of an apostate might be accepted a limited number of times — "three" being the count some derived. But the standing majority — al-Ṭabarī himself leading — held the door of repentance never legislated shut in this life: the verse acknowledges, by its very alternation-clause, the *possibility of return* — communal and legal acceptance of one who leaves and repents — while warning that each oscillation *diminishes* the likelihood of a final return, because the heart is being trained in reversibility: al-Zamakhsharī's psychological note — each relapse engraves the soul's facility in sliding — and al-Rāzī's corresponding note: wavering of this depth indicates belief was never rooted at all. The closing-clause is thus read not as a juristic count but as an x-ray of trajectory — the danger named is not the number of falls but the *direction of the sum*: *thumma izdādū kufran* — the checkbook of alternation ending in deficit-increase.

**Lam Yakuni Allāhu Li-Yaghfira Lahum: The Rare Negative Will**

The verdict that follows belongs to the Book's rarest register — explicit divine non-forgiveness announced in the past-continuous form: *lam yakuni Allāhu li-yaghfira lahum wa lā li-yahdiyahum sabīlan* — "God was not one to forgive them nor to guide them unto a way": *lam yakun* with the following imperfect — "He was never of a disposition to..." — and the commentators' standing boundary-drawing: (1) the majority — forgiveness is closed for the one who *dies* upon the increase — death-in-kufr being the seal that ends the repentance-window (its standing Book-kin: 4:18 — "repentance is not for those who do evil deeds until death attends one of them, and he says: now I repent"; 3:90 — "those who disbelieve after their belief then increase in disbelief — their repentance shall not be accepted — and those are the astray" — the nearly word-for-word parallel); (2) the standing theological schools' glosses: the Muʿtazilite reading — God "will not guide them" in the Hereafter to the Garden, guidance forfeited when the state was sealed by death; or His guidance removed in this very life as standing punishment; the Ashʿarite rejoinder — a plain assertion that God does not guide disbelievers in this life itself, with the "increase" clause standing for them as proof that faith and disbelief have real degrees — obedience and sin genuinely increasing or diminishing them (the Ashʿarite locus classicus in the faith-debate). And v. 145–146 below frames the hope-window precisely: the lowest depth belongs to the hypocrites — "*except those who repent and make amends*" — the Book itself reopening, four verses on, the door it seems here to close — confirming the majority reading: the closure is not upon the repentant but upon the state-in-which-one-dies.

**The Standing Tremor-Application: Oscillation as a Soul-Disease**

The psychological filing of the verse — the standing register from the spiritual commentators inward — universalizes the pattern without shrinking its severity: oscillation between commitment and betrayal is a disease with a trajectory — and trajectories, not single points, judge lives. ʿAbd Allāh ibn Masʿūd's standing dictum (its standing register in the moral literature) gathers the point: "the believer sees his sins as a mountain beneath which he sits, fearing it will fall upon him; the profligate sees his sins as a fly that passes across his nose, and he flicks it away" — the difference between the two being precisely the presence or absence of one-directional increase. The verse stands as the heading of the sūrah's hypocrisy-dossier: first the oscillation diagnosed (v. 137), then the ironic announcement (v. 138), then the false alliances exposed (v. 139), then the sitting-with-mockers forbidden (v. 140) — an anatomy of the two-faced soul that will close, at vv. 145–146, with both its depth and its escape: "verily the hypocrites are in the lowest depth of the Fire... save those who repent and make amends and hold fast to God and make their religion sincere for God." The sūrah of social justice thus pauses its courtroom to examine the hiddenest courtroom of all — the heart before its Lord — where belief must either increase or it will, by the physics of the soul, decline.

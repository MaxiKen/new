## Sūrah al-Nisāʾ 4:140

> **He has already sent down upon you in the Book that when you hear the signs of God being disbelieved in and mocked at, do not sit with them until they engage in some other discourse — else you are indeed like them. Verily God will gather the hypocrites and the disbelievers in Hell all together —**

**Expanded Commentary**

**Wa Qad Nazzala ʿAlaykum fī al-Kitāb: The Citation of an Earlier Ruling — Legislating by Quotation**

The dossier's fourth verse takes an extraordinary legislative step: it *quotes the Book itself as precedent*: *wa qad nazzala ʿalaykum fī al-kitāb* — "He has already sent down upon you in the Book": the commentators (al-Ṭabarī with his standing chains; Ibn Kathīr citing the consensus) identify the referent nearly unanimously: 6:68 — revealed in the Meccan period, years earlier — "and when thou seest those who engage in vain discourse concerning Our signs, turn away from them until they engage in some other discourse — and if Satan should make thee forget, then do not sit, after the reminding, with the wrongdoing people" (and its standing companion 6:69 — "those who fear God are in no way accountable for them — but a reminder, that perhaps they may fear"). The Medīnan verse thus does not merely repeat the Meccan ruling; it *files the earlier verse as standing law* — the act itself a standing lesson in Qur'ānic legal method: earlier revelation is not a draft but an accumulating corpus; each new sūrah builds with the already-built. Al-Qurṭubī dwells on the legal significance: the prohibition is thereby doubly anchored — revealed once about Meccan mockers of the revelation, reactivated here about the Madīnan mockers in the hypocrite-majlis — the same ruling governing two capitals and two calendars: the Book's own demonstration that its rulings are not occasion-bound fossils.

**Fa-Lā Taqʿudū Maʿahum: The Seat as a Vote — The Sociology of Complicity**

The ruling itself is of disarming simplicity — and therefore of unending force: *idhā samiʿtum āyāti Allāhi yukfaru bihā wa yustahzaʾu bihā fa-lā taqʿudū maʿahum ḥattā yakhūḍū fī ḥadīthin ghayrihi — innakum idhan mithluhum* — "when you hear the signs of God being disbelieved in and mocked at, do not sit with them until they engage in some other discourse — else you are indeed like them": the command drills into the sociology of presence — *taqʿudū* — sitting — the most passive of acts — legislated as though it were speech: because presence is broadcast. Al-Zamakhsharī's standing gloss distills it: it is not enough to abstain from mocking and disbelieving oneself; sitting passively in the mocking-circle *lends silent support and tacit approval* — the absent counter-signature registering as consent. The jurists built the standing analogy-architecture upon it: attendance is endorsement in matters of communal boundaries (the principle echoing through rulings on gatherings of open sin — the standing maxim that the silent occupant of the wrong assembly shares its classification — grounded precisely in *innakum idhan mithluhum* — "else you are indeed like them": the threat of *equation* — the witness of the mockery classified by the Book with the mocker, though he never mocked: guilt-by-assembly formalized in revelation). Yet the exit-clause is equally deliberate: *ḥattā yakhūḍū fī ḥadīthin ghayrihi* — "until they engage in some other discourse" — the boycott conditional and conversational, not absolute and social — when the topic moves, the seat may return: the Book regulating boundaries without abolishing society, displeasure expressed as temporary standing-to-one's-feet rather than as permanent excommunication. And the *kh-w-ḍ* root of *yakhūḍū* — wading — the standing Book-word for plunging into discourse (its register: 6:68’s twin usage; 9:65; 43:83; 52:12; 74:45 — "we used to wade with the waders") — paints the mockery-session as people splashing in shallows they mistake for depths: the believer is forbidden to wade with them, not to converse with them.

**The Hypocrisy-Barometer: Association as Revelation of Identity**

Al-Zamakhsharī's standing note — gathered with approval in the later literature — generalizes the verse into a barometer of the heart: the sincerity of one's faith, indeed one's true religious identity, is revealed not only by one's own words and deeds but by *the character of the company one keeps when truth is being dishonored*. With v. 139's false-protector taking, this sitting-with-mockers forms the two behaviors by which the hypocrite outs himself: the alliance he contracts in fear, and the majlis he keeps in comfort — outward profession untouched in both, inner allegiance exposed in both. Ibn Kathīr adds the standing delimitation that balances the ruling: the prohibition targets the gathering *where the mockery occurs and continues* — 6:68's own forgetfulness-clause ("if Satan should make thee forget, then do not sit, after the reminding" — the accidental attender purified by immediate rising) — the law legislating for the living, not for perfection's mannequins: memory returns, and the feet follow.

**Inna Allāha Jāmiʿu al-Munāfiqīna wa al-Kāfirīna: The Assembly Where Assemblies Are Judged**

The verse seals with its standing eschatological gathering-threat: *inna Allāha jāmiʿu al-munāfiqīna wa al-kāfirīna fī jahannama jamīʿan* — "verily God will gather the hypocrites and the disbelievers in Hell all together": *jāmiʿ* — the Gatherer (its standing register: 4:87 above — "God — there is no god but He — He shall surely gather you to the Day of Resurrection"; and 3:9 — "our Lord — Thou shalt surely gather mankind unto a Day about which there is no doubt") — and the classification it enforces the exact inverse of the sitting it forbids: you feared to be *unlike* the mockers by rising from their majlis; beware instead being *like* them in the final majlis — for *innakum idhan mithluhum* finds its eschatological twin here: the sitten-with and the sat-with are gathered *jamīʿan* — all together — one assembly sealing all assemblies. The sūrah's own earlier audience-principle of 4:87 (gathering as certainty) now specialized: every human circle is practice for the final Circle; choosing one's sitting-room is choosing one's Resurrection-neighborhood. The dossier will continue below (v. 141's wavering watchers: "those who lie in wait for you — if a victory comes to you from God, they say: were we not with you?") — but v. 140 has already delivered its permanent legislation across the centuries: the rule of the seat — hear revelation mocked, and the body must file the soul's dissent with its feet — seven Arabic verbs containing a whole jurisprudence of protest without violence, withdrawal without severance, and testimony without a word spoken.

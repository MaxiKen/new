## Sūrah al-Nisāʾ 4:39

> **What would it have cost them had they believed in God and the Last Day and spent from that which God has provided them? And God knows them well.**

**Expanded Commentary**

**Mādhā ʿAlayhim: The Question of the Impossible Bargain**

The sūrah's treasury-sermon closes its block of greed and display with one of the Qur'an's gentlest and most cutting rhetorical forms: *wa-mādhā ʿalayhim law āmanū bi-Allāhi wa al-yawmi al-ākhiri wa anfaqū mimmā razaqahumu Allāh* — "and what would have been *upon them* — what would they have lost — had they believed in God and the Last Day and spent of what God provided them?": *mādhā ʿalayhim*, literally "what would it have been against them?" — the interrogative of the free benefit, God's invitation phrased as *cost-accounting*: the whole proposal laid out — believe, and spend — and the absurdity of refusal laid bare by the arithmetic of loss: *nothing* would have been lost; everything would have been gained. Al-Rāzī treasures this mode as one of the Book's reasons-with-man — the arguer who is made to carry his *own case*, the divine reasoner showing that faith's price is *zero* while its return is immeasurable — and al-Ṭabarī's note notes the form's continuity with the Qur'an's whole evangel of *cheap godliness*: *forgive and be forgiven* — 24:22's own logic; *spend and be repaid* — 34:39's; *fear God and He will teach you* — 2:282's: nowhere does obedience cost a single thing that was not better spent. The doctrine-of-spending verse that 4:39 delivers from this shape is therefore double: its sting for the miser (you lost *nothing* — you only lost your loss) and its balm for the giver (nothing you spend actually departs — it *transfers*: from the perishing wallet to the everlasting ledger).

**Mimmā Razaqahumu Allāh: Out of What *He* Provided**

The qualifying phrase is the entire theology of Islamic wealth: *of that which God provided them* — the money being invited out was never theirs: it is it is *rizq*, provision, God's continuous act of supplying (the Book's omnipresent name for the flow: 2:3's initial character-quark of the God-ware: the God-fearing spend *from what We provided them*), and the possessive grammar itself thus patents the indictment: the *donor's* capital being quarreled over by the *trustee's* fist. The tradition folds the whole thing into a single coupon—hadīth the commentators never tire of laying beside it:

> *"The son of Adam says: My wealth! My wealth! Do you possess of your wealth anything but what you eat and consume, what you wear and wear out, or what you give in charity and send forward?"* (Muslim 2958 — Kitāb al-Zuhd)

— the human wallet audited down to three offices — stomach, back, ledger — of which only one survives the funeral. From that audit, stinginess reads at last as the *category error* it deep-down is: guarding in the name of "mine" what remains, at last accounting, either eaten or buried-in-cloth — with the charity-column alone carrying forward; rizq-theology makes treasuring the spending-stream not merely piety but sanity; hence the sūrah's earlier command to gift the asylum-seekers from it (v. 8) now becomes here the whole economics of existence: keep flowing what is flowing — trust's own conduct for what was never ceded to you — and the escrow of the world hands over on the Day when the only *owned* wealth is the forwarded.

**Wa Kāna Allāhu Bihim ʿAlīmā: And God Knows Them — A Double Seal**

The seal steps softly and lands dreadful lands heavy: *wa kāna Allāhu bihim ʿalīman* — "and God is knowing of them": *ʿalīm*, knowing-entire — the clause at first sight a mild statement of omniscience, but in the rhetoric of the Book the *awareness-seal after an offer refused* is a veiled alarm (al-Rāzī's standing observation for this style: when God closes an entreaty with His knowing-name over people, it signals that every hidden motive behind the refusal is unhidden from Him — the avarice that masqueraded as prudence; the unbelief that masqueraded as policy); and it equally seals *comfort* for every reader who complies: of such too God is knowing — the heart's purity in the unrecorded gift, the wife's private coin, the servant's coin-halved al the servant's split coin — reaching the Knower with no other witness — so the clause completes the register of v. 38's riyāʾ-hell with the knowledge-heaven opposite: *what eyes never saw, ʿAlīm saw.* The sūrah's little treatise of wealth — custody in vv. 5–6, share in v. 7, kind-word in vv. 8–9, fear of unjust eaters in v. 10, and now the anti-twin of greed and display in vv. 36–39 — is thereby sealed as the ummah's permanent finance-charter: worship God alone; beautify downward along the seven-class compass of v. 36; withhold nothing at most that flows; spend toward none but the Face; and know — in both warning and in mercy — that the Knower of the servants is the auditor of the is He who audits the closed seals, the fountains never exhausted, the ledger already written: *you could not have given away a single grain without Him knowing it from before the worlds were made.*

## Sūrah al-Nisāʾ 4:139

> **Those who take the disbelievers as protectors instead of the believers — do they seek might through them? Nay — might belongs to God altogether.**

**Expanded Commentary**

**Alladhīna Yattakhidhūna al-Kāfirīna Awliyāʾa: The Insurance-Policy of the Two-Faced**

The dossier now names the hypocrites' characteristic foreign policy: *alladhīna yattakhidhūna al-kāfirīna awliyāʾa min dūni al-muʾminīn* — "those who take the disbelievers as protectors instead of the believers": *awliyāʾ* — the plural of *walī* — the protector-patron-ally (from w-l-y — nearness) — a word the commentators insist carried, in the tribal climate of Arabia, the weight of a *total* bond: mutual defense, shared fortune, absolute loyalty — walāyah being the social cement of pre-Islamic allegiance, which the Book does not abolish but consecrates-redirects: God the believers' ultimate Walī (2:257 — "God is the Protector of those who believe — He brings them out of darkness into the light"; 2:107; 3:68; 4:45's standing within this very sūrah; 5:55 — "your Protector is but God and His Messenger and those who believe — those who keep the prayer and give the alms, bowing down"; 6:14; 7:196; 9:116), fellow believers one another's true awliyāʾ (8:72; 9:71 — "the believing men and believing women are protectors one of another"), and the counterfeit patronages exposed: the idolaters' vanity-shrines (13:16), the disbelievers' Satanward lean (7:30; 16:100 — "his patronage is over those who take him as protector"), the disbelievers one another's closed guild (8:73 — "those who disbelieve are protectors one of another — unless you do this, there will be tribulation in the land and great corruption"). The standing comment-canon localizes the act here: the Madīnan hypocrites cultivating secretly the Jewish clans (al-Rāzī, al-Zamakhsharī's reading) or the Meccan idolaters (other reports) as a hedge — an insurance-policy against the possibility that the Prophet's enterprise fail — "instead of the believers" carrying its precise sting: not *alongside* them but in *preference* to them, the heart's first loyalty already transferred to the enemy camp that the tongue publicly opposes.

**Min Dūni al-Muʾminīn: The Loyalty-Migration as Perfidy**

The commentators draw the verse's standing legal-sociological line: the Book prohibits not neighborliness, trade, covenants, or kindness with non-believers (60:8's standing balance — "God does not forbid you, regarding those who have not fought you over religion and have not expelled you from your homes, from being righteous toward them and dealing justly with them — God loves the just") but precisely *yattakhidhūna awliyāʾa min dūn* — the adoption of the outside camp as one's *protector-fraternity of first resort*: the transfer of ultimate reliance. Its standing prohibition-file: 3:28 — "let not the believers take the disbelievers as protectors instead of the believers — whoever does that has nothing from God"; 4:89 above (regarding those who turn back); 4:144 below — the verse's own near-repeat in this dossier ("O you who believe — take not the disbelievers as protectors instead of the believers — do you wish to give God a clear warrant against you?"); 5:51 — "take not the Jews and Christians as protectors — they are protectors one of another — and whoever of you takes them as protectors is surely one of them"; 5:80-81's standing rebuke; and the extreme case — 9:23 — even family members, where loyalty to them means standing against faith. The perfidy-diagnosis stands triple: such alliance (1) betrays the community's trust, (2) reveals opportunism — hedges placed with two bookmakers at once, and (3) — the point of the verse's second half — manifests a *theological* misjudgment: distrust in the sufficiency of God's protection, as though the guarantee of 4:132 above ("God suffices as a Guardian") were inadequate collateral.

**A-Yabtaghūna ʿIndahumu al-ʿIzzah: Might Sought at the Wrong Address**

Then the withering question: *a-yabtaghūna ʿindahumu al-ʿizzah — fa-inna al-ʿizzata li-llāhi jamīʿan* — "do they seek might through them? Nay — might belongs to God altogether": *ʿizzah* — from ʿ-z-z — might, power, dignity, invincibility — the withstanding-strength that cannot be subdued — and the interrogative framed as exposure: your secret alliance with the disbelievers is, at bottom, a *quest for might* — prestige, safety, backup in the day things go wrong — conducted at the false address. The Book's standing ʿizzah-file pronounces the asset's true registry: 63:8 — the Medīnan-surah's standing counter-claim, spoken against the very words of the arch-hypocrite Ibn Ubayy himself ("might belongs to God and His Messenger and the believers — but the hypocrites know not"); 10:65 — "let not their speech grieve thee — verily might belongs to God altogether — He is the Hearing, the Knowing"; 35:10 — "whoever desires might — to God belongs might altogether"; and 29:41’s standing parable concluding the search forever: "the parable of those who take protectors apart from God is that of the spider that makes a house — and verily the frailest of houses is the spider's house — if they but knew": the hedger's web of secret alliances, woven strand by strand, hangs exactly there — in air, adhesive only to itself. And *jamīʿan* — "altogether" — sealing the totality: not a majority-share but the whole asset class — every quantum of might concentrated in one Owner, who distributes derivatives to His believers as He wills (63:8's standing assignment) and leaves the false address permanently unfunded. The practical register descends to every age's version of the same hedge: alliances with the powerful against one's own community out of fear that God's promise is slow; prestige sought through disavowal; the quiet trimming of loyalties to keep every door open — the verse detecting in each the identical theological error: a census of might that omitted its only Holder. The hypocrites of Madīnah sought ʿizzah with Quraysh and the clans; the clans dissolved, Quraysh surrendered, Ibn Ubayy died chewing his bitterness at Khaybar's aftermath — and 4:141 below will complete the portrait: waiting "with you" in the days of victory, claiming "were we not with you?" when the spoil is divided — might having migrated, as it always does, to the Side that never needed it.

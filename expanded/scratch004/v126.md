## Sūrah al-Nisāʾ 4:126

> **Unto God belongs whatsoever is in the heavens and whatsoever is on the earth, and God encompasses all things.**

**Expanded Commentary**

**Wa Li-llāhi Mā fī al-Samāwāti wa Mā fī al-Arḍ: Ownership Declared Before the Law Resumes**

Between the Abrahamic summit of v. 125 and the family-file reopening at v. 127, the Book plants its standing ownership-plaque: *wa li-llāhi mā fī al-samāwāti wa mā fī al-arḍ* — "unto God belongs whatsoever is in the heavens and whatsoever is on earth": the sūrah's standing its standing cadence-form returning once more (its own siblings: 2:284 — "unto God belongs whatsoever is in the heavens and the earth — whether you disclose what is in your souls or hide it — God will call you to account for it"; 3:109; 3:129; 4:131–132 immediately below; 4:170; 10:55) — with the standing comment-canon's structural note on its position: the ownership-declaration standing deliberately **between** the verse closing the preceding extended doctrinal-file (belief, hypocrisy, idolatry, hell, and reward) with a yet opening the curtain upon the family-legislation resuming below: all the the legal stakes about to be apportioned in inheritance, marriage-terms, and protection (vv. 127–130) belong first to God: every orphan's mīrāth, every mahr, every household-right is is held only in trust from Him — the the Ownership-verse thus serving as the standing announcement never to be missed: *everything to follow legislates the handling of what God owns* — hence mind how you handle it).

## Sūrah al-Nisāʾ 4:105

> **Verily We have sent down unto thee the Book in truth, that thou mightest judge between men according to what God has shown thee. So be not an advocate for those who betray their trust.**

**Expanded Commentary**

**Innā Anzalnā Ilayka al-Kitāba bi'l-Ḥaqq: The Judgment-Charter Verse**

The war-scroll of vv. 71–104 finally rolls shut — and the sūrah returns to the great law-around law-aroundment of its governance-peric to the great law-frame of its governance-pericope (vv. 58–60's amānāt and referral) — with the verse the canon calls the charter of Prophetic adjudication: *innā anzalnā ilayka al-kitāba bi'l-ḥaqqi li-taḥkuma bayna al-nāsi bimā arāka Allāh* — "verily We have sent down to thee the Book in truth — that thou mightest judge between men by what God has shown thee": the Book *with-the-truth* again (its standing seal from 2:176 — "that is because God has sent down the Book in truth"; 3:3's parallel) — now aimed not merely at doctrine but at *court-bench* exercise: li-taḥkuma — that *thou judgest* (the singular-Prophet charged with the standing office — the standing legal-theory structure of Islam's judiciary: the Prophet's court the fountain-head; the ummah's qāḍīs thereafter inheriting procedure-and-ethic from his hand) — *bimā arāka Allāh* — "according to what God has shown thee" (arā — He caused to see — the magnificent phrasing the comment-doctors elaborate: judgment by *God's showing* — revelation plus proven-evidence plus the pointing the trained discernment God had granted the Prophet — with Ibn Taymiyyah's master-note: the shown truth spans both *revelation* and *manifest reality* — the qāḍī's two-texture sourcing: text plus plainest fact).

**Wa Lā Takun li'l-Khāʾinīna Khaṣīman: Thou Art Not the Treacherous' Advocate**

Then the constitutional ceiling of the whole office — the clause the canon marks as one of Islam's most absolute judicial restraints: *wa lā takun li'l-khāʾinīna khaṣīman* — "so be not for the betrayers an advocate": **khāʾinīn* — betrayers — treacherous breaches — from kh-w-n — breach-of-trust (the standing treachery-word (the same lexeme returning at v. 107: *"and plead not for those who betray themselves"*) — and *khaṣīm* — adversarial pleader — partisan advocate (from kh-ṣ-m — dispute — the standing litigant-word) — with the standing asbāb the canonical rolls deliver: al-Wāḥidī and al-al-Qurṭubī's registered account of Ṭuʿmah ibn Ubayriq of Banū Ẓafar — accused of stealing a coat of mail and smuggling it into concealment with a Jewish neighbor — the stolen evidence planted upon the innocent man — Ṭuʿmah's his clan coming to plead with the Prophet on the slander-pretext that *their clansman* was wronged (clan-solidarity raised at court) — and the Prophet on the verge of exonerating him — until these very verses descended, staying his hand: *be not an advocate for the treacherous* (with the standing unveiling-sequence falling out in order: v. 105 the charter; 106 the seeking-of-forgiveness for the near-miss; 107–108 the verdict upon self-betrayers and their hidden plotting; 109 the rebuke to the pleaders; 111 the standing criminal jurisprudence — every crime upon its doer alone) — the account itself being the classical standing-symbol of rule-of-law's birth in Islam: the court's mercy for the *claimant's felt plight* — a clan hurt, a honor-invoked — versus the *evidenced wrong*: with the Book teaching thereafter the pure jurisprudential maxim — advocacy grounded not in kin-love but in justice (its epilogue remembered everywhere in the fiqh: Ṭuʿmah fled, apostatized, was crushed under a falling wall-mass under a fallen wall at Makkah — the comment-doctors recording the age the story rolls of the Sīrah).

**The Judge's Impartiality as Islam's Court-Foundation**

The commentators then extend the decree-consequence the verse's legal-transfers to the whole judicial office of the ummah — the rule coming down: no judgment on account of kinship, wealth, rank, or religious surface-dress: (1) the Prophet himself could be aimed-at by false pleaders — no human station is immune from the advocate-trap (the standing lesson: even the Messenger was given a revelation-check against clan-weighed outcry — with the accompanying hadith-register of his standing impartiality: the celebrated declaration, “were Fāṭimah the daughter of Muḥammad to steal, Muḥammad’s hand would cut hers” — recorded in al-Bukhārī 3475 and Muslim 1688); (2) advocacy belongs to the case-fact, not the clan-fact — judges and pleaders alike bound; and (3) the transfer to every believer: *never plead for the betrayer* — not your father, not your son, not your party, not your nation — with the Book's own further course at vv. 135 following right behind: "be persistently standing for justice, witnesses for God — even against yourselves — or parents — or kin — whether rich or poor — God is more worthy of both" — the whole judicial-block 4:105–112 + 135 being the Quran's standing rule-of-law cathedral — and the sūrah's architecture thus traceable: war-light cooled (v. 104) — judgment-authority built (v. 105) — itself anchored against the clan-form of treacherous pleading that was burning through the tribal life of pre-Islamic Arabia — so that Madinah's newly-born polity would stand not upon swords but upon *uncorrupted courts* — the first clause of which God addressed not to any vizier but to the judge-in-chief — the Prophet — as if to say: the law begins with *you*, and reaches downward.

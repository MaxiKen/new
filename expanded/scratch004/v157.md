## Sūrah al-Nisāʾ 4:157

> **And for their saying: "We slew the Messiah, Jesus son of Mary, the messenger of God" — though they slew him not, nor did they crucify him, but it was made to appear so unto them. Verily those who differ concerning him are in doubt about him; they have no knowledge of him, but follow only conjecture — and they slew him not for certain —**

**Expanded Commentary**

**Wa Qawlihim Innanā Qatalnā al-Masīḥa: The Boast That Filed Itself as Evidence**

The indictment's sixth count quotes the boast itself: *wa qawlihim innanā qatalnā al-Masīḥa ʿĪsā ibna Maryama rasūla Allāh* — "and for their saying: we slew the Messiah, Jesus son of Mary, the messenger of God": the commentators (al-Zamakhsharī's standing gloss leading) read the utterance in its performative register: not a legal confession but a *taunt* — the dismissal of Jesus' prophethood by claiming his judicial disposal: we dealt with your "messenger" as the Law deals with claimants — death by the civic procedure — the same psychology of v. 155's prophet-slaying file now applied to its most famous name. The Book's own framing is exact: four full titles attached to him inside the quoted boast — *al-Masīḥ* (the Messiah — the standing title, its register: 3:45 — "his name is the Messiah, Jesus son of Mary — eminent in this world and the Hereafter, and of those brought near"; 4:171-172; 5:17; 5:72; 5:75; 9:30-31), *ʿĪsā ibna Maryam* (Jesus son of Mary — the matronymic that is itself the Book's standing refutation of the paternity-slanders: named for his mother, as no son of Joseph-narrative ever was in the slanderers' file), and *rasūlu Allāh* (the messenger of God — the Book inside the quotation correcting the boast's estimate of its victim) — the indictment thus quoting its perpetrators at their most self-satisfied, immediately before reversing the claim's factual basis wholesale.

**Wa Mā Qatalūhu wa Mā Ṣalabūhu wa Lākin Shubbiha Lahum: The Fourfold Negation**

Then the Book's famous negation — the only passage addressing the crucifixion-event in the entire Qur'ān: *wa mā qatalūhu wa mā ṣalabūhu wa lākin shubbiha lahum* — "though they slew him not, nor did they crucify him, but it was made to appear so unto them": *shubbiha* — passive of sh-b-h — resemblance-making: a likeness imposed — and the tradition of interpretation built its standing accounts upon the passive's open agency: the appearance engineered — by God — upon some figure of the arrest-scene. The standing accounts in the comment-canon (al-Ṭabarī's chains, through Ibn Kathīr's and al-Zamakhsharī's collections): (1) the arresting party could not distinguish Jesus among his gathered followers — a divine resemblance cast so that the circle looked alike — and another was taken in his place; (2) the specific report-variants naming a disciple who *volunteered* to assume the likeness and bear the death — sometimes specified as having asked Jesus for "the likeness and the promised companion-rank in Paradise" — and being crucified (i.e., publicly exposed upon the beam — the standing lexical note: ṣalb in the commentators' usage here often post-mortem exposure — matching the verse's order: "slew him not, nor crucified him": slaying first, exposure second) in Jesus' stead; (3) the Wahb ibn Munabbih account-line — the perfidious insider (the Judas-figure of the gospel-file) made to wear the likeness as his treachery's own recompense — betrayer executed in betrayed's image. The commentators' standing note for the record: since *shubbiha lahum* covers the perception of *them* — addressed to the boast-makers but naturally extending to the watching crowd — the appearance ruled the scene for almost everyone present — al-Ṭabarī's standing pastoral clause following: no charge of dishonesty lies upon those Christians who, trusting the dramatic public record of the event, believe the crucifixion occurred — the Book's target throughout being the *boast* and the *rejection*, not the witness of deceived eyes.

**Al-Rāzī's Standing Objection: The Deception-Problem**

The comment-canon also preserves the tradition's own internal audit — and no treatment of this verse is complete without it: al-Rāzī's celebrated objection to the substitution-accounts: if God casts likenesses upon persons such that sensory identification fails, the entire edifice of testimony-based law trembles — marriage-identity, witness-identification, the whole witness-economy of the sharīʿah resting on perceived personhood; the Quran itself builds upon sight its own procedures. Al-Rāzī therefore inclines to readings avoiding mass deception of identity — the standing alternatives gathered in his long discussion: death occurring but not at their hands as claimed (they neither killed nor crucified — the event otherwise than their boast, its appearance interpreted by them in boast-fashion); or the semblance attaching to the mode of Jesus' exit rather than to another's execution. The tradition's standing counter-notice from Ibn Kathīr's register and others: providentially altered perception is not without Book-precedent — 8:43-44 at Badr: "when God showed them to thee in thy dream as few... and when you met, He made them seem few in your eyes and made you seem few in their eyes — that God might accomplish a thing foreordained" — the audience-perception at Badr adjusted on both sides by decree: where the purpose is prophecy's deliverance, the commentary-record judges, the Ruler of perception may adjust the perceived — the disagreement itself being the tradition's standing testimony that the verse's grammar holds its mystery: *shubbiha lahum* states the appearance without inventorying its mechanics.

**Inna Alladhīna Ikhtalafū Fīhi La-Fī Shakkin Minhu: Doubt as the Standing Verdict of Difference**

The sentence then widens from event to epistemology: *inna alladhīna ikhtalafū fīhi la-fī shakkin minhu mā lahum bihi min ʿilmin illā ittibāʿa al-ẓann* — "verily those who differ concerning him are in doubt about him; they have no knowledge of him, but follow only conjecture": the pronoun *fīhi* — "concerning him" — read by its standing double-gloss (al-Zamakhsharī preferring "concerning *it*" — concerning the whole affair): the differing-parties placed by the Book's judicial grammar inside *shakk* — doubt — and *ẓann* — conjecture — the standing Book-epistemology of awe-files (its register of ẓann as the false coin of disputed truth: 4:157's own neighbor 53:23 — "they follow naught but conjecture and what the souls desire"; 6:116 — "if thou obeyest most of those on earth they will mislead thee from God's way — they follow naught but conjecture"; 10:36; 10:66; 45:24's standing would-be cosmology built on conjecture; 53:28 — "they have no knowledge thereof — they follow only conjecture — and conjecture avails naught against truth") — the standing referent of "those who differ" read two ways: the Jewish authorities themselves uncertain whether the execution had truly reached Jesus (al-Ṭabarī's and al-Zamakhsharī's prime reading — the boast's own cover-story already fraying); or, in al-Qurṭubī's register, the later Christian differentiations over the event's nature. The verse's diagnostic here is less about the first century than about the standing epistemology of contested salvation-events: where parties differ over a matter the Book has ruled upon, their difference itself is named the proof of their epistemic station: *lā ʿilm* — no knowledge — only *ittibāʿ al-ẓann* — the following of conjecture — as though conjecture were a guide-dog that could be followed home.

**Wa Mā Qatalūhu Yaqīnan: The Closing Certainty**

The verse doubles its negation into seal: *wa mā qatalūhu yaqīnan* — "and they slew him not for certain": *yaqīnan* — the accusative of emphasis — either as standing certainty-adverb ("certainly did not slay him" — the majority reading: God's *repeated* negation terminating every debate by restating the fact: whatever was done, to whomever, under whatever appearance — they did not slay him) or as the standing idiomatic reading registered in the comment-canon (al-Qurṭubī noting the usage): "they killed it not with certainty" — their *doubt* itself remaining unslain — the uncertainty persisting unrectified beneath the boast: the sentence thereby, on this register, convicting them even internally: the claim-cry "we slew the Messiah" delivered over an inquest their own hearts never closed. Either register terminates at the same standing verdict the Book now springs in the next verse: the entire earthly file — arrest, appearance, execution-scene, boast — overruled by one celestial motion announced with the standing Name-pair of majesty-and-wisdom: *bal rafaʿahu Allāhu ilayh* — "but God raised him unto Himself" — v. 158's own file: the deliverance as divine act, the failure of the boast as divine design — the account of Jesus in the Book closing not on a beam but in an ascent.

## Sūrah al-Nisāʾ 4:60

> **Hast thou not seen those who claim that they believe in that which was sent down unto thee and in that which was sent down before thee, desiring to seek judgment from the ṭāghūt, although they were commanded not to believe in it? But Satan desires to lead them far astray.**

**Expanded Commentary**

**Yazʿumūna Annahum Āmanū: Claim as Currency**

The authority-block, having built the tripod of obedience (v. 59 — God, Messenger, ulū al-amr), now surfaces its saboteurs under the Book's special gaze-command: *alam tara ilā alladhīna yazʿumūna annahum āmanū bi-mā unzila ilayka wa mā unzila min qablik* — "Hast thou not seen those who *claim* to believe in what was sent down to thee and in what was sent down before?": *yazʿumūna* — they claim — *zaʿm* being the Qur'an's chosen verb for assertions unweighed (its assembly-line of claimants running from the pagans' genealogy from their God-kinship claims at 37:150 to v. 62's upcoming oath-claims; al-Rāzī's note every time: *zaʿm* never certifies; it merely relays) — the class here disclosed being the Madinan *hypocrites* (munāfiqūn): outward confession, inward refusal — their asbāb-portrait the tradition preserves with a court-case made famous (al-Tirmidhī's transmission through al-Ḥasan's channel from Ibn ʿAbbās; al-Wāḥidī's and al-Ṭabarī's similar rolls): a dispute broke between *a believing Jew* — the litigant with a case too strong to lose — and *a hypocrite* among the ostensibly Muslim (the named cast varying among reports: Mughīrah Ḥuyayy-side a man of Banū al-Naḍīr's orbit the hypocrite in any register): the Jew demanded the Prophet's arbitration; the hypocrite steered toward the kāhin — the soothsayer of Juhaynah set up for such cases in Medina's pagan-adjacent milieu (Ibn Sayyād in one chain; a Jewish in another, a rabbinical tribune): the believer the dispute landed at the Prophet's gate, and heaven judged the epidemic: *for someone to profess the whole testimony-belief in *every* revelation — Torah, Gospel, and Muhammad's Book — and still bypass God's appointed tribunal for Satan's outpost (a soothsayer's tent! a clan-broker's palm-court!) — exposes the claim as currency of convenience only*.

**Yurīdūna an Yataḥākamū ilā al-Ṭāghūt: The Second Ṭāghūt within Nine Verses**

The desire-word indicts as soon as it is uttered: *yurīdūna an yataḥākamū ilā al-ṭāghūti wa qad umirū an yakfurū bih* — "they desire to seek judgment from the ṭāghūt, though they were commanded to disbelieve in it": *yataḥākamū ilā* — the litigant's reflexive — *to take their mutual case unto* — and the illative *wa qad* — *though they had been commanded* — making the betrayal of covenant the heel-fragile the covenant's very first term: *the receipt of revelation carries the explicit instruction to disbelieve in the ṭāghūt* (2:256's exact pairing — *"whoever disbelieves in the ṭāghūt and believes in God has grasped the firmest bond"* — with 16:36's identical cousin: *worship God and shun the ṭāghūt*) — and *ṭāghūt* here gathering all its accumulated load (from 4:51's idol-pair nine from 4:51 above its meaning-theaters: idols, soothsayers, sorcerers, satanic proxy-authorities, and — by the juristic extension that became Islam's constitutional vocabulary — *any authority that substitutes its own ordinance for God's* — the following piety-tradition making no mince: Imām Mālik's dictum style the maximaries the fuqahāʾ's named formula — *al-ḥukm bi-ghayri mā anzala Allāh* — ruling against what God sent down — seeking judgment elsewhere being *functional* worship of that elsewhere). ʿUmar ibn al-Khaṭṭāb's famous definition-answer stands at the register's head: asked what the ṭāghūt was, he said (Tirmidhī-fre reported through the the register of his Companions' responses: "the ṭāghūt is the devil" — and, in the wording of his companions: *everything worshipped beside God* — judgments by other lights being its adjudicative mode).

**Wa Yurīdu al-Shayṭānu an Yuḍillahum Ḍalālan Baʿīdā: Distance as Satan's Aim**

The verse's third term discloses the true litigant behind the rival tribunals: *wa yurīdu al-shayṭānu an yuḍillahum ḍalālan baʿīdā* — "while Satan desires to lead them into a far misguidance": *ḍalālan baʿīdā* — astrayness *remote* — distance-adjective chosen on purpose (the commentators' question-note — why *far* answered in two registers: far *from God's law* — distance in standards; and far *from return* — distance in repentance, since each bypass of the Prophet's court digs the trench deeper: al-Rāzī's al-Rāzī's counsel here belongs to his finest psychology: a man may begin by outsourcing one quarrel to a soothsayer and end by building his whole faith upon counterfeit altars: ḍalāl *increases in its own direction*; and 4:119's Satan's self-confession later in this very sūrah — *"I will surely command them, and they will alter God's creation"* — standing behind the arbitration-abstacle: every alternative tribunal is Satan's *creed-workshop* — the slow replacement of judgment by counterfeit until the name is all that remains of the claim). The believer's safeguard against this drift is the very anchor the verse presupposes — *yakfurū bih*: to *disbelieve in* the ṭāghūt — unbelief, the Book's own paradox, as a positive required act (the sages' aphorism multiplying from this root: *faith's first pillar is refusals* — one rejects idols *into* God; so every court of appeal's legitimacy tests sound the same: *where does the decision come home to — God's ordinance, or the ṭāghūt's convenience?* — the boundary line not between ancient and modern tribunals but between judgment that fears God and judgment that serves appetite) — and the pericope's cinematic next its next frames (vv. 61–63) march the hypocrites through the remaining diagnostic: the invitation answered by turning away (v. 61), the misfortune met with oath-theater (v. 62), and heaven's triage — turn aside, admonish, and speak into their souls (v. 63): three stations of pastoral triage standing beneath one indictment — *the zaʿm-claim collapses upon the first tribunal-choice.*

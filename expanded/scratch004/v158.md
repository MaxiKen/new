## Sūrah al-Nisāʾ 4:158

> **But God raised him unto Himself; and God is ever Mighty, Wise.**

**Expanded Commentary**

**Bal Rafaʿahu Allāhu Ilayh: The Ascent That Overrules the Beam**

The Book closes the crucifixion-file with its counter-event — five words in Arabic that the entire tradition reads as the axis of the Jesus-history: *bal rafaʿahu Allāhu ilayh* — "but God raised him unto Himself": *bal* — "but/rather" — the particle of amendment — cancelling the whole boast-narrative in one corrective stroke (its standing usage: as at 2:88's "nay — God has cursed them..." — the abrupt divine override); *rafaʿa* — to raise: the raising-verb whose selection the commentators mark as a precise technical choice: not *baʿatha* (to resurrect — the standing word for raising *from the dead*, as at 2:56; 19:33's own standing Jesus-file: "peace be upon me the day I was born, and the day I die, and the day I am raised alive — *ubʿathu ḥayyan*" — the resurrection-lexicon there), but *rafaʿa* — the elevation-word that needs no antecedent death — and *ilayh* — "unto Himself" — the destination stated as the Divine Presence itself. The verse joins its standing twin 3:55 as the two-source Qur'ānic foundation of the ascension: "when God said: O Jesus — verily I shall take thee and raise thee unto Myself and purify thee of those who disbelieve" — *mutawaffīka wa rāfiʿuka ilayya* — the commentators noting the twin-verse's double vocabulary (taking + raising) and the present verse's economy (raising, direct) — together establishing the standing doctrine: Jesus ascended to God directly from the earthly scene — the file closing not in a tomb but in the raising.

**The Mortality-Puzzle the Tradition Filed Under This Verse**

The verse's implications set the standing theological inventory, and the commentators unfold them in order: (1) the Book is unwavering that Jesus is *not* divine (5:116's standing arraignment-dialogue; 5:72; 5:75 — "the Messiah son of Mary is but a messenger — messengers have passed away before him — and his mother a truthful woman — they both used to eat food"); (2) therefore, mortal nature attaches to him as to every human messenger ("We did not give them bodies that ate no food — nor were they immortal" — 21:8; 21:34 — "We granted not immortality to any man before thee"); (3) therefore his death is *real but pending* — the tradition locating it after the return at the end of time: the standing eschatology-file (its register in the sunni hadith-collections: the descent of Jesus at the white minaret in Damascus, the slaying of the Dajjāl, the breaking of the cross and the just rule, then death and funeral among the Muslims — its standing registers in Muslim's Ṣaḥīḥ and the Sunan-collections) — Jesus' biography in the standing Muslim account thus running: virgin birth — mission — raising alive to God (this verse) — return — death (19:33's own words) — resurrection with all mankind. Ibn Kathīr's standing note within the exegetical file resolves the apparent double-scene of the last hours: some of his followers witnessed the raising itself and knew he had not died; others witnessed the appearance and believed the Jewish account — the two witness-strata coexisting from the first hour, and the Book adjudicating between them: *bal rafaʿahu Allāh*. And the standing boundary-gloss some commentators add: that "raising" does not logically preclude a *death-and-raising* sequence (2:259's standing hundred-year sleeper, *then* raised — baʿatha) — but the verse's own syntax — negating both slaying and crucifixion, then substituting raising — deploys its weight on the direct-ascension side, where the standing tradition consolidated.

**Wa Kāna Allāhu ʿAzīzan Ḥakīman: Might and Wisdom as the Twin Seals of the Deliverance**

The verse seals with the standing Name-pair that the commentators read as the explanatory signature of the whole affair: *wa kāna Allāhu ʿazīzan ḥakīman* — "and God is ever Mighty, Wise": *ʿazīz* — the Mighty, the Unconquerable (its standing register sealing act-of-power verses across the Book: 2:209; 2:220; 2:228; 3:6; 5:38; 8:10; 8:63; 8:67; 9:40 — "God is Mighty, Wise"; 48:7; 48:19) — the Might specifying *how* the deliverance succeeded: the arresting authorities held the square, the guards, the sentence, the crowd — and the outcome fell entirely outside their writ because Might does not negotiate with jurisdiction: the boast "we slew the Messiah" undone by the attribute "*ʿAzīz*" — One whom no coalition outbids; and *ḥakīm* — the All-Wise — specifying *why* the deliverance took this extraordinary form rather than the ordinary: Wisdom choosing dramaturgy that would instruct all parties forever: the boastful authorities taught that their writ ends at the sky; the watching disciples taught that messengers are delivered by means beyond the visible cordons; the slanderers of Mary answered by the elevation of her son past every earthly tribunal; and the later centuries of theological controversy already ruled upon in advance (5:116's dialogue; 4:171-172 below) — Wisdom engineering an event whose *appearance* would be debated exactly where Wisdom wanted debate to die: in the standing acknowledgment that *no one knows the scene finally except God* — "they have no knowledge of it" (v. 157) — because the event's decisive motion happened in the register where *rafaʿahu Allāhu ilayh* operates: not in the arena of the accusers, but in the vertical direction of the Mighty, the Wise. The Jesus-file of the sūrah thereby closes its first panel (vv. 156–158): calumny answered by exaltation; execution-boast answered by negation; earthly closure answered by heavenly opening — and the sequel the Book immediately legislates: v. 159 — the standing eschatological remainder: "there is not one of the People of the Book but shall surely believe in him before his death" — the raising not being the end of the Jesus-ministry, but its adjournment — to a session where every one of the addressed communities shall at last confess the truth of him, and "on the Day of Resurrection he shall be a witness against them."

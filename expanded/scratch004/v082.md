## Sūrah al-Nisāʾ 4:82

> **Do they not contemplate the Quran? Had it been from other than God, they would surely have found much discrepancy therein.**

**Expanded Commentary**

**A-Fa-Lā Yatadabbarūna al-Qurʾān: Contemplation as Standing Obligation**

After the night-plots clause, heaven turns the beam upon the very text that has just been quoted against the conspirators — and issues the question that built a science: *a-fa-lā yatadabbarūna al-Qurʾāna* — "do they not contemplate the Quran?": *tadabbur*, from d-b-r — to follow behind a thing, to consider its outcomes — to walk toward the back of the words where their consequences grow (the Book's own four stations of the command: 4:82; 23:68; 38:29 — "a Book We sent down to thee, blessed, *that they may contemplate its signs*"; and 47:24 — "Do they not then contemplate the Quran — or are there locks upon their hearts?" — the sister-clause that seals the diagnosis: hearts acquire locks exactly where tadabbur is left off) — the commentators read the question in two directions at once: as challenge to the doubters — had they truly read this Book, which now publishes their own night-schemes within its pages, they would confess its Divine provenance, for it names their secrets — and as standing *obligation upon the believers*: the Recitation is not meant to be merely intoned but entered into understanding — 38:29's blessing tied directly to the very act of pondering. The Prophet's own practice was the lifelong demonstration of the verb — his night-standing with the sūrahs until his feet swelled — and the preserved reports describe his manner of recitation as measured and halted, dwelling upon each clause of mercy or punishment, as in the record of his reading the closing verses of Sūrah al-Nisāʾ's own sister-sūras and pausing at the warnings and promises to press them into supplication (Muslim 772's register of his pondering-style); from this single command grew, in due course, the entire tadabbur-genre of the libraries of Islam — the treatises that taught the world to hear the Book as a living Speaker rather than a museum-text.

**Wa Law Kāna min ʿIndi Ghayri Allāh: The Coherence-Test as Authenticity's Signature**

Then comes the counterfactual that has never stopped serving as an open challenge to humanity: *wa law kāna min ʿindi ghayri Allāhi la-wajadū fīhi ikhtilāfan kathīrā* — "and had it been from other than God, they would have found in it much discrepancy": the logic-spine, as al-Zamakhsharī and al-Rāzī both sharpen it, is not merely *absence of contradiction* but absence of the natural human entropy of speech: any human author composing over twenty-three years — amid war and truce, bereavement and victory, persecution and triumph, hunger and rule — would accumulate *ikhtilāf kathīr*, massive inconsistency: tones shifting, loyalties bending, self-corrections lurching in, factions courted, early positions quietly disowned. Yet the Recitation descends across the most violent biography ever given a messenger — through migration, battlefield defeat, public scandal, famine, and absolute sovereignty — and emerges as *one voice*, one doctrinal geometry: the whole Book resolving always into the same tawḥīd, the same ethic, the same eschatology.

From this verse the proof-method itself was patented into the sciences of Islam: the inimitability doctrine (iʿjāz) with its standing challenge-verses — 2:23 ("then produce a sūrah like it"), 10:38, 11:13, and 17:88:

> *"Say: If mankind and the jinn came together to produce the like of this Quran, they could not produce its like, even if they backed one another"* (17:88)

— and the consistency-theology of 39:23 ("God has sent down the fairest discourse, a Book self-consistent, oft-repeated, at which the skins of those who fear their Lord shiver"). As for the critics' rejoinders — the varied canonical readings (qirāʾāt) and the abrogating and abrogated verses (nāsikh and mansūkh) — the classical frame answers without flinching (al-Ṭabarī's collation carried down by al-Suyūṭī's circles): divergent readings and revealed development are *intended divine pedagogy*, disclosed upon the face of the Book rather than concealed — an honest Book's evolution is stamped and marked in public, which is the very opposite of a forger's method: the pretender would hide revisions; the Prophet instead publicly recited abrogations, announcing changed rulings before the world exactly as the Book records the scoffers' retort — "thou art but a forger!" — precisely at the open management of sequence (16:101).

**The Standing Promise: Tadabbur as Policy for Every Age**

The verse's legacy runs wider than apologetics, as the classical masters long ago taught from it. For the *seeker*: the Book repays deep reading with coherent sight — surface-readers find puzzle after puzzle; tadabbur-workers find convergence — and whenever apparent disagreement arises among verses, the remedy is harmonization, not combat: the Prophet grew visibly angry upon hearing Companions wielding verses against one another in dispute, and warned them:

> *"Nothing destroyed the peoples before you but this."* (al-Bukhārī 2410; Muslim 2666 — the celebrated report on Qur'an-dueling)

— making one verse fight another without a reconciling hermeneutic is *man-made ikhtilāf*, the very flaw the Book demonstrably lacks; the clear verses (muḥkam) govern, and the interwoven (mutashābih) yield to them, per 3:7's architecture. For the *scholar*: al-Qurṛṭubī and al-Rāzī read the clause as the warrant for analogical reasoning (qiyās) and systematic reconciliation — since no contradiction exists, interpretive labor is entrusted with *finding* the seamless reading — the muḥkam-mutashābih structure never being license for two camps at war but a summons to one integrated vision. And for the *ordinary believer*: tadabbur remains daily devotion — the standing antidote to locked hearts being open pages — crowned by the Prophet's promise upon every reader:

> *"The best of you are those who learn the Quran and teach it."* (al-Bukhārī 5027)

The sūrah's pericope has just displayed, in the night-contrivers of v. 81, what a community looks like when held up against this un-discrepant Light: the Book knows its readers before they know it — and asks them only one open question, permanent in every century, written to every locked heart it finds: *Do you not contemplate the Quran?*

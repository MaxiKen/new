## Sūrah al-Nisāʾ 4:28

> **God desires to lighten [your burden] for you, for man was created weak.**

**Expanded Commentary**

**Takhfīf: The Lightened Law as Divine Design**

The trilogy of divine desires finds its third lamp in the statute-book's own easy chair: *yurīdu Allāhu an yukhaffifa ʿankum* — "God wills to lighten for you": *takhfīf*, the taking-off of weight, announced here not as concession reluctantly granted but as legislated *purpose* — and the placement seals the lesson of the whole pericope: when the law allowed the poor man the slave-bride's marriage (v. 25), when it received the repentant sinner mid-law (vv. 17, 26, 27), when it opened khulʿ beside ṭalāq (2:229's symmetry remembered at vv. 19–21), it was not patching weakness in religion but *revealing* the religion's own economy: *its Maker intends it portable*. The verse hangs therefore beside the Qur'an's greatest declarations of statutory mercy — "God wills for you ease, and wills not for you hardship" (2:185); "He has chosen you, and has laid no hardship upon you in religion" (22:78); "We shall ease you to ease" (87:8) — beneath which al-Ṭabarī and Ibn Kathīr situate the entire ramadan-concessions code, the combats' reduction at Badr-era from one-against-ten to one-against-two (8:66's "now God has lightened for you, knowing there is weakness in you"), the tayammum of v. 43 below, the five prayers descended from fifty at the Night Journey (the Muslim 162 report from the Prophet's own narrative), and the traveler-and-a's concessions throughout the books of fiqh — a full doctrine in a single clause: God builds His law *around human scale*, not around angelic.

**Wa Khuliqa al-Insānu Ḍaʿīfan: Weakness in the Blueprint**

Then the reason-clause, one of the Book's most quoted anthropology-items in every pulpit since: *wa khuliqa al-insānu ḍaʿīfan* — "and man was created weak": *ḍaʿīf*, weakened-made — the passive of createdness itself, the weakness planted *at formation*, not acquired — covering, per the commentators' anatomy, the full sweep of human fragility: resolve weak before desire (the occasion-context: sexual appetite's long endurance breaking down, per al-Ṭabarī's explicit occasion-reading), patience thin under burden, memory slipping the covenant (20:115's "he forgot, and We found no firmness in him" of Adam the father), the body's own set-limits of hunger, vigilance, hour of sleep and age. Al-Rāzī's crucial theological note stands beneath: the clause *answers* an accusation that would later grow heavy in kalām (how can God command what men sustain not?), by lodging the remedy inside the fact: since weakness is *created*, mercy must be *legislated* — the Lawgiver accounting in advance for His clay's known tolerance — so that at no statute does the God of the Qur'an play the taskmaster who measures timber loads on mice. The Prophet's own legislation-idiom echoed the doctrine in his ministry:

> *"This religion is *yusr* — smooth ease; and no one will do battle with religion but that it will overcome him."* (al-Bukhārī 39: from Abū Hurayrah — the mosque-school's standing maxim)

— his own sūrah of pilgrimage direction, always the gentle gradation: fasting's permissions, witr's lightening, the standing offer of qasr and jamaʿ on roads.

**Between the Two Edges: Ease Without License, Burden Without Crushing**

The commentators are unanimous in fencing the verse from its two misreadings: it is *not* license for the sluggard — God lightens within the statutes' lines, He does not dissolve them: the degree-map of vv. 23–25 stands entire; the prayers were five, not one — nor is it *pessimism* about a contemptible creature: weakness spoken as the *opening* in God's compassion-machine, not as the slur of a creation once more disfavoured. Al-Ḥasan al-Baṣrī's school left the twin-edges in proverbs the preachers have kept: *"make religion approachable to us as You have made the fire decreed?"* — no; the line from their pulpits runs rather: *"the people are weak; and God desired ease for them — so do not be harder on them than their Lord."* Every teacher of Islam, jurist-mufti, qāḍī, marriage-counselor and general caller to God has worked beneath v. 28's directive: measure the load to the back; take the seeker to easy halal-granted rooms before the hard; remember that the Prophet's sunnah taught without all-at-once — "the religion was not revealed in a day" — for the takhfīf was not only in *what* was ruled but in *how* it was handed.

**The Statute-Mercy at the Reader's Own Meal**

The verse finally lands, within Sūrah al-Nisāʾ's architectural design, as the assembly's keystone: the heavy blocks around it — indecency's prosecutions (vv. 15–16), the mother's and daughter's titles at graves (vv. 11–12), wealth and murder's forbiddance (vv. 29–30 below) — all sit *inside* this lightness-clause, so that strictness itself is weighed in mercy's scales. And Ibn ʿAbbās's memory enrolled v. 28, with vv. 26–27, in the sūrah's eight mercy-verses (al-Rāzī's citation of his pupil-chains): *yurīdu*, God's will to lighten, is placed among the community's miḥrāb-anchors — for the Muslim at every ritual and legal fork may say to his soul exactly what the Lawgiver announced to His ummah at its founding: *I was made weak; my Lord knew it; He desired my ease; and in His book there is a lawful door* — so that law falls from above, no more as burden-drop, but as grace carefully measured: line by line, statute by statute, makruh and mubāḥ balancing man's frail palm: the law of a Knowing, Wise, and *lightening* Lord.

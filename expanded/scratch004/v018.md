## Sūrah al-Nisāʾ 4:18

> **And repentance is not accepted from those who do evil deeds till, when death confronts one of them, he says, "Truly now I repent," nor from those who die as disbelievers. For those We have prepared a painful punishment.**

**Expanded Commentary**

**Ḥattā Idhā Ḥaḍara Aḥadahumu al-Mawt: The Last Call That Comes Too Late**

The partner-statute to v. 17 maps the terrain where the door stands shut: *wa laysa al-tawbatu li-lladhīna yaʿmalūna al-sayyiʾāti ḥattā idhā ḥaḍara aḥadahumu al-mawtu qāla innī tubtu al-ān* — "and repentance is not for those who work the evils until, when death has arrived to one of them, he says: I repent now." Two portraits are denied: the *deathbed-late*, tawbah at the arrival — root *ḥ-ḍ-r*, presence — of death itself, and the *death-dwellers in kufr*, those who *die while disbelieving*. Al-Rāzī marks the semantic division precisely: the defect in the first is *time*, not class — the same sinner who might be embraced at morning is rejected at the gurgle — turning a promise-of-acceptance into the sharpest alarm of all; the defect in the second is *state* — death seals as it finds, belief or unbelief, and unsealed thereafter no more. The commentators of the occasions link the first clause to the old ones who spent a life in evil and, sighting the angel, scrambled for the formula; Ibn Kathīr places beside it the Pharaoh of the Book's memorial — "until, when drowning overtook him, he said: I believe there is no god but He in whom the Children of Israel believe, and I am of the submitters" — met with heaven's one-word courtroom: *"Now? — and you had disobeyed before, and were of the corrupters"* (10:90–91) — the standing precedent that the acceptance-gate outruns exactly one hour of sighted proof.

**Why Now Is Not Now: The Logic of the Shut Gate**

Al-Rāzī, whose kalām-precision summons the theological question (why should the latest honest turn be refused?), renders the school's answer in layers: (1) tawbah under God's rule is *a trust made credible by freedom*, and the death-arrival is freedom's terminus — hence no faith at that hour counts, "for faith profits not a soul which had not believed before, nor earned good in its faith" (6:158's full clause); (2) the late-turner's every earlier hour had the chance, so the refusal is the logical last mercy: had God ratified the death-minute conversion, *every* scoffer could license himself; the one denied hour guards the sincerity of all the earlier ones — (3) and the *why can't He?* answer is simply: He can; the verse records what He has *willed* — and appointed that the Book announce the rule universally so that no one can claim surprise at the border. The tradition adds the soul's own eyewitness-condition from the ḥadīth of the sun-and-breath already noted — while the spirit remains unravished, the sun unrisen-in-the-west, and the ghayb (unseen) unseen — the door stands "outstretched as the Lord stretches His hand by night that the sinner of the day may repent, and by day that the sinner of the night may repent, until the sun rises from its setting-place" (al-Tirmidhī 3379 to this effect; the *night-and-day hands* of Muslim 2759, from Abū Mūsā, is the standing text).

**The Disbeliever's File: Death as Binding Verdict**

The second portrait — *wa-lladhīna yamūtūna wa hum kuffār* — "those who die while they are disbelievers" — carries the seal that needs no clock because the file itself is closed at the divider: death-in-kufr — and for these the verse pronounces the prepared room: *ulāʾika aʿtadnā lahum ʿadhāban alīman* — "for those We have prepared a painful punishment": *aʿtadnā* — the verb of prepared-readiness, as of banquet or army laid out before the guests arrive — reserved in this chapter (vv. 37, 93, 151, 161, 167) for the case of disobedience-filed-at-death. The commentators register the parallel of the last-hour seeker cited by Ibn Kathīr with the Book's own light on the death-criers: "when they see Our punishment, they say: I believe... their faith availed them not when they had seen Our punishment — the wont of God that has passed among His servants — and there the disbelievers lost" (40:84–85) — sunnah-of-God being the tradition-term for unalterable precedent. The chapter's adjacent theology is in plentiful supply: 2:161–162's died-kuffār-abiding; 3:91's earth-ful of gold refused; 4:97 below blaming angels' questioning the self-harmers — *in what condition were you?* The teaching stands through them all: the grave is an appointment with a verdict matching the condition died-in; and no horizon-prayer after fixes it.

**The Two Doorside Lamps for the Living**

With v. 17's open door beside v. 18's shut one, the sūrah has set the two lamps under which every sinner-plan forms — and the commentators bundle them as one counsel: count no hour yours; the door's open date is unpublished. The salaf's devotional practice lived the order literally: Ḥasan al-Baṣrī's well-known exchange with a youth who postponed — *"when my beard is white, I shall repent"* — answered in the spirit of these two verses with one question: *"and who promised you that you will grow a beard?"* The community is thereby trained into the verse's own arithmetic: *min qarīb* (v. 17) is the genuine *now*, and any later *now* dreamed of is, by the grammar of the refuse-clause, v. 18's *innī tubtu al-ān*. The pericope therefore ends exactly as the orphan-pericope began — at watchfulness: keep accounts short; repentance done *quickly* is the only repentance promised; the quick one is the promise; the postponed one is the gamble against the gurgle and the sun.

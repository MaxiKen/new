## Sūrah al-Nisāʾ 4:58

> **God commands you to return trusts to their rightful owners and, if you judge between men, to do so with justice. Excellent indeed is the instruction God gives you. Truly God is Hearing, Seeing.**

**Expanded Commentary**

**Inna Allāha Yaʾmurukum an Tuʾaddū al-Amānāti ilā Ahlihā: The Great Mandate of Return**

The sūrah opens its celebrated authority-block (vv. 58–70) with the verse that became the charter-text of Islamic governance and ethics alike: *inna Allāha yaʾmurukum an tuʾaddū al-amānāti ilā ahlihā* — "God commands you to deliver trusts to their people": *amānah*, from the root of *amn* — security — the deposit that makes its keeper safe-guarded by conscience: the word's classical breadth spanning (1) *literal deposits* — goods, moneys, documents, borrowings, findings (al-al-Qurṭubī's jurists counting every species of custody: wadīʿah safekeeping, ʿāriyah borrowings, laqṭ findings, securities of every stamp; with the direct contrast the Book already drew at 3:75 — some People of the Book "if you trust them with a single dinar they will not return it" — dishonesty in trust being itself the marker of unfaith); (2) *offices and authorities* — the reading Ibn Kathīr and al-Ṭabarī's chains hold alongside (every delegated charge being an amānah toward its beneficiaries), and (3) *all religious obligations* — prayer, fast, the soul itself — Ibn Kathīr's note the shared gloss of the early masters: everything God charged man with is a trust, down to the tongues and bellies and organs of the body (36:65's hand-and-foot testimony corroborating). The revelation-occasion the historians never fail to retell — for it is the most beautiful *return* in Islam's annals — the conquest-day at the Kaʿbah (Bukhārī 2498 traceable register; al-Wāḥidī, al-Ṭabarī, and Ibn Kathīr's narrative weave): the Prophet took the sanctuary's key from ʿUthmān ibn Ṭalḥah — hereditary keeper by Quraysh-treaty; al-ʿAbbās, his uncle, standing ready with the petition for its transfer to the Hashimite line of custodianship; and the Prophet, opening his hand, handing back the key with the verse descending upon his hand: *"Deliver trusts to their people"* — returning custodianship to the family that had *earned* it by centuries of office — ʿUthmān accepting Islam upon the justice that reached him past the conqueror's own kin: the single deed that settled forever the law of Islam upon delegated offices: *merit and custodianship outrank nearness, even the Prophet's own family* — mercy and anti-nepotism sealed in one key.

**Wa Idhā Ḥakamtum Bayna al-Nāsi an Taḥkumū bi-l-ʿAdl: The Judicial Pair**

Directly upon the trust-command rides its brother: *wa idhā ḥakamtum bayna al-nāsi an taḥkumū bi-l-ʿadl* — "and when you judge between people, judge with justice": the conditional *idhā* — *when* — reads as normative whenever-adjudication (fiqh-books counting the verse among the foundations of qadāʾ-discipline — al-Qurṭubī's extended ruling-chapters flowing from it: competence preconditions, evidence-standards, even sentence-formality) — and *bi-l-ʿadl* — with equity — the mid-justice the Qur'an's favorite among its justice-words (its sister *qisṭ* — apportioned equity — running at 4:135 below and 5:42's domains) — the command's reach deliberately universalized by the commentators past judges to *every seat of decision*: parent over children, shepherd over flock, husband adjudicating wife-grievances, a man ruling upon *himself in his own weighing* (al-Qurṭubī's beautiful extension: each man is a judge between his outward and inward selves), with the corresponding hadith's terrors and joys both built upon the office (Muslim 1716, from the justice-triad traditions: *"the just shall be upon pulpits of light with God — those who are just in their judgments, their families, and whatsoever they are put in charge of"*) — and its counter-terror lodged in the disobedience-books as one of the seven shaded — standing in al-Bukhārī 680's roster — *"a just ruler"* — the first of the seven, because no human action spreads further into both worlds.

**Inna Allāha Niʿimmā Yaʿiẓukum Bih: The Excellent Counsel — and the Two Attributes Guarding It**

The verse's middle-seam — *inna Allāha niʿimmā yaʿiẓukum bih* — "excellent indeed is what God admonishes you with": the Book's own doxology inserted mid-command (*niʿimma* — how excellent! — the exclamatory form that stamps the instruction itself as grace, not burden — the same insert-habit as 16:90's *God commands justice and excellence...* — the twin-verse whose minbar-mandate echoes at every sermon of Islam — and 4:58's own standing sermon-right beside it in the preacher's libraries; the *yaʿiẓukum* — He admonishes you — making legislation itself pastoral: authority-law wrapped inside *ʿiẓah*, counsel — as though governance were admonishment before decree) — and the seal brings its two justice-attributes: *inna Allāha kāna samīʿan baṣīran* — "Hearing, Seeing": *samīʿ* — All-Hearing — interrogatories, oaths, depositions, whispers inside chambers: *baṣīr* — All-Seeing — the tilted scale that no camera catches, the trustee's private hand, the judge's private prejudice: the commentators across the register noting the deliberate doublet's guard-posts upon both halves of the verse: the Hearer of grievance-words stands behind the deposit-holder; the Seer of courtroom-gestures stands behind the magistrate — hence the generalization tradition-wide: the verse became, in caliphal-era edicts and mosque reading alike, *the* standing command-sentence of state-ethics (ʿUmar ibn al-Khaṭṭāb's famous judicial instruction to Abū Mūsā ringing with it; and every khaṭīb's framing ever since) — whereby the authority-block opens: *power is a deposit; office is a custody; God watches the ledger through two attributes that no appeal-court can fool: the Ear and the Eye.*

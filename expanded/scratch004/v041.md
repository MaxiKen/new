## Sūrah al-Nisāʾ 4:41

> **How will it be when We bring forth a witness from every community, and We bring thee as a witness against these?**

**Expanded Commentary**

**Fa-Kayfa Idhā: The Opening Cinematic of the Reckoning**

The sūrah pivots from reckoning-arithmetic (v. 40: no wrong of a dharrah-weight) into the very theater of the Reckoning itself, opening with its with the Qur'an's stock scene-verb of awe: *fa-kayfa idhā jiʾnā min kulli ummatin bi-shahīdin wa jiʾnā bika ʿalā hāʾulāʾi shahīdan* — "How then shall it be, when We bring from every community a witness, and bring thee against these as a witness?": *kayfa* — *how shall it be!* — the interrogative-without-answer whereby the Book inducts its reader into an event no description can carry (cf. its fearful siblings: "How shall it be when We gather them on a day of no doubt" — 3:25; "How will you guard if you disbelieve a Day that makes children gray" — 73:17): imagination itself tasked, since the scene is too large for stages. The specific assembly-line is unveiled in one clause: from *every ummah* of history a designated *shahīd* — testimony-bearer — is brought forward, and that bearer is its prophet (al-Ṭabarī's unanimous verse-chains: each community's own messenger testifies over his people — acceptance or refusal — with 16:84's parallel summons and 16:89's double of our exact clause; 4:159 adding that even Jesus witnesses upon the people of the Book given him; 28:75 folding the same draw-from-each-community into judgment-day liturgy). The Lucan The Rule stands established: *prophethood shapes itself finally as testimony* — every messenger's whole career summed up, on the Last Day, as a witness-stand deposition for or against the ones he was sent among.

**Jiʾnā Bika ʿalā Hāʾulāʾi Shahīdan: Muhammad Over the Middle Proof-Community**

The second summons is the verse's drama and its crown: *and We bring thee against these as a witness*: the last Prophet's case is singled-out not merely as one shahīd among the shahīds (his would be sequential) but as the culminating witness over *"these"* — the demonstrable the demonstrative pointing variously read: over *his own ummah* (the plainest line, matching 2:143 and 22:78 — "We made you a middle community that you may be witnesses over mankind *and the Messenger a witness over you*": the elaborate architecture wherein the Muslims themselves function as witnesses over the earlier communities — on the ground that they acknowledged the earlier prophets whom their nations denied — and the Prophet Muhammad ﷺ is witness *over the Muslims*, the great circle closing with the ummah's accreditation under its own master) — and, in al-Qurṭubī's amplified sense, over *the foe-pagans of his of his day* whose wars and sneers fill this very surah (the clause a quiet dread-exact a quiet threat to the men then legislating prayer-drunkenness and mocker-imprecation around his mosque: you will stand opposite him at that bar). Either way the table-order is transcendent: prophets as evidence-bearers, communities as cases — and above all the and at the zenith the seal of prophets given his double commission: witness over the believers, intercessor between them and wrath (al-Qurṭubī's window on the ummah's comfort — he who must testify against is likewise he by whose appeal they may be freed).

**The Weeping Prophet: When the Verse Was Recited to Its Owner**

The tradition preserved at this verse belongs to the highest clas s of Prophet-psychology disclosures — the verse was a burden-felt, not an honor-boasted: Ibn Kathīr and al-Qurṭubī both anchoring the celebrated scene of ʿAbdullāh ibn Masʿūd (in al-Bukhārī 4582 and Muslim 800):

> *"The Messenger of God ﷺ said to me: Recite to me. I said: Messenger of God, shall I recite to you when it was revealed to you? He said: I love to hear it from other than myself. So I recited Sūrah al-Nisāʾ until I reached: *How will it be when We bring forth a witness from every community, and We bring thee as a witness against these?* He said: Enough now. I looked at him, and behold, his eyes were flowing with tears."*

— the Prophet crying at his own summons to the stand — Abu Abū Hurayrah's nuq for no verse could touch him deeper than the one where *he* was conscripted to bear testimony upon beloved and disobedient alike: the *daʿwah-cost* of prophethood there at its heaviest — to be compelled forever to speak truth against people one ached to save. The wisdom of the weeping long outlasted its scene — the early community recited this verse as their mine as their mirror-house: if its shaft if even its addressee wept at the coming bar, how shall any grafter in orphan-gold or bedroom cruelty not weep who stands numbered among *the witnessed*?

**The Identity Clause of All Witnesses**

Behind the summons sits the principle each prophet had already announced while alive — the cited covenant-refrain (5:117) placed on Jesus's lips in the Book and embraced, the Prophet reports, by Muhammad ﷺ upon his own deathbed-adjacent counsels: *"I was a witness over them as long as I was among them; when Thou tookest me, Thou wast the Watcher over them; and Thou art witness over all things"* — the witness's term of office ending at death, while the ever-living *Witness over all things* — God — remains upon the case. The whole file of 4:41 thereby swings on one hinge: heaven employs prophets as its prosecution-witnesses — the ummah of the last-prophet as its certification-arm of history — and the Prophet of mercy as its supreme deponent; the sūrah's reader, still tarrying within the laws of caretakers and coins, is startled into the true register by this courtroom-verse: every clause he has just read *will be entered as evidence* — and the mad-mockery of the townsmen, the orphan-swallowers, the hoarders and the display-givers: all shall meet, at that summons, *a witness they knew and wept over* — "if you disbelieve — God is free of the worlds" — and the safe soul is the one that joined the witnessing-community on the right side: under the Prophet's flag beneath the Prophet's witnessing, not beneath his tears-as-evidence; for he loved his people more than his office, and the office prevails at the bar of God.

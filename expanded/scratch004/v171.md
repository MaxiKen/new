## Sūrah al-Nisāʾ 4:171

> **O People of the Book! Commit not excess in your religion, and say not of God aught save the truth. Verily the Messiah, Jesus son of Mary, is but the messenger of God, and His Word which He committed to Mary, and a Spirit from Him. So believe in God and His messengers, and say not "Three." Refrain — it is better for you. God is but One God — glory be to Him, above having a child! Unto Him belongs whatsoever is in the heavens and whatsoever is on the earth, and God suffices as a Guardian.**

**Expanded Commentary**

**Lā Taghlū fī Dīnikum: Excess Forbidden at the Doctrinal Summit**

The chapter's final direct address to the People of the Book opens with the prohibition that defines the Christian-side error as the Book measures it — the standing counter-charge to the Jewish-side attenuation files above (vv. 150-155: refusal, barring, calumny): *yā ahla al-kitābi lā taghlū fī dīnikum* — "O People of the Book! Commit not excess in your religion": *gh-l-w* — to overshoot, to exaggerate past measure: the commentators (al-Rāzī's standing formulation) drawing the chapter's standing two-error map: the error of the Jews in the Book's file was *deficiency* — refusing prophets sent to them (vv. 155-156; 2:91's standing register); the error of the Christians was *excess* — ascribing to their prophet a station beyond humanity: the two communities erring in opposite directions about the same axis — the status of God's envoys — and the verse opening with the standing prohibition of *ghuluww* itself: excess not as a variant vigor of devotion but as a named sin (its standing hadith-counterpart in the Prophet’s file — his celebrated warning (al-Nasāʾī 3057; Ibn Mājah 3029; the Musnad): “beware of exaggeration in religion — for those before you perished by exaggeration in religion” — with his standing counterbalance: the best of affairs is the middle course); and *wa lā taqūlū ʿalā Allāhi illā al-ḥaqq* — "and say not of God aught save the truth": speech about God confined to the truthful, naming the doctrinal offense as precisely a *qawl ʿalā Allāh* — an assertion upon God (its standing register: 3:94; 4:50 above; 6:93; 7:37; 11:18 — "who does greater wrong than he who fabricates a lie against God"; 16:116; 18:15; 72:4-5) — exaggeration classified not as private enthusiasm but as testimony — testimony unlicensed by the witness of revelation.

**Innamā al-Masīḥu...: The Fourfold Identification of Jesus — Station, Word, Spirit, and Their Measures**

Then the verse's doctrinal core — a fourfold naming of Jesus with the most majestic honorific-vocabulary in the Book, fenced at each end: *innamā al-Masīḥu ʿĪsā ibnu Maryama rasūlu Allāhi wa kalimatuhu alqāhā ilā Maryama wa rūḥun minhu* — "verily the Messiah, Jesus son of Mary, is but the messenger of God, and His Word which He committed to Mary, and a Spirit from Him": (1) *innamā* — "only/but" — the restrictive particle opening the definition — the ceiling drawn first: the Messiah is *nothing beyond* what follows; (2) *rasūlu Allāh* — the messenger of God — his standing station: among the envoys of God's choice (v. 163's roll; its standing theological station in the Book: human, commissioned, meal-eating — 5:75 — "the Messiah son of Mary is but a messenger — messengers passed away before him — and his mother was a woman of truth — they both used to eat food"); (3) *kalimatuhu alqāhā ilā Maryam* — "His Word which He committed/cast to Mary": the standing identification of Jesus as God's Word (its Book-register: 3:45 — "the angels said: O Mary — God gives thee glad tidings of a Word from Him — his name is the Messiah, Jesus son of Mary"; 19:34 — "such is Jesus son of Mary — a statement of the truth, concerning which they doubt"); and the commentators' standing double-reading of the *kalimah*: (a) the annunciation-word — the glad tiding delivered to Mary ("a Word from Him" as the message announcing the child — al-Ṭabarī's register); and (b) the creative command itself — the *kun* — "Be!" — by which the fatherless child was formed in the womb (3:59's standing parallel: "verily the likeness of Jesus with God is as the likeness of Adam — He created him from dust — then said to him: Be — and he was") — al-Rāzī gathering both and marking the tradition's ruling insight: though *all* creation issues through God's command, Jesus alone is *named* His Word — the singling-out being honor, not essence-participation: the title distinguishing his *mode of arrival* (word alone, no father) without conferring the nature of the Speaker; (4) *wa rūḥun minhu* — "and a Spirit from Him": the standing rūḥ-identification (its register: 2:87 — "We gave Jesus son of Mary the clear proofs and strengthened him with the Holy Spirit"; 2:253; 5:110; 66:12 — "We breathed into her of Our Spirit") — with the commentators' standing caution-register: the min of *minhu* read as the min of emanation-at-command ("of His creating," as every created spirit is "from Him" — its standing companion at 15:29 — "when I have proportioned him and breathed into him of My Spirit"), some earlier readings glossing rūḥ here as mercy (raḥmah) or purity — al-Rāzī gathering the tradition's theological settlement: "from Him" marks origin and honor, never composition — the same diction the Book uses of houses "of God" and camels "of God" (91:13 — naqata Allāh — "the she-camel of God" — belonging, not consubstantiality). The tradition thus enrolled Jesus' honorifics from this verse — Rūḥ Allāh, Kalimat Allāh — the commentators marking what the chapter itself performs: **the Book that refuses deification is itself the deepest honorer of Jesus** — it is the Book that calls him Messiah, Word, Spirit, raised-to-God (v. 158), miracle-working (5:110), eminent in both worlds (3:45) — while drawing the one fence the Christian file crossed: servanthood (v. 172 below sealing: "the Messiah would never disdain to be a servant of God").

**Wa Lā Taqūlū Thalāthatun: Say Not "Three" — the Standing Command of Refrain**

Then the prohibition upon the doctrinal word itself: *wa lā taqūlū thalāthatun — intahū khayran lakum* — "and say not 'Three.' Refrain — it is better for you": *thalāthah* — three — the standing terse reference to the "third of three" file (its standing companion at 5:73 — "those who say: God is the third of three — have disbelieved — and there is no god save One God — and if they refrain not from what they say, a painful punishment shall touch those of them who disbelieve"); and the command *intahū* — refrain! — with the identical self-interest motivation sounded at v. 170 (*khayran lakum* — the standing benefit-clause pointing downward: correct belief as rescue, not as concession). The commentators' standing doctrinal-careful register (al-Rāzī's scholastic discussion most explicit in the file): the verse and its 5:73 companion address the assertion of *three deities* — a triad — an assertion orthodox trinitarian Christians themselves formally disavow ("not three gods, but one"); al-Rāzī acknowledging the claim of three hypostases/aspects within a single Essence, yet pressing the tradition's standing critique that the attribution of full divinity to the human Jesus collapses the claimed unity into effective plurality — and contrasting trinitarian hypostases with the Islamic file of divine Names/Attributes, which are never posited as distinct entities within God — the standing distinction the verse installs pastorally, however, is simpler and prior: *say not "Three"* — speech-discipline upon God — refrain — for the tongue that recites "three" of God has already left the ground in which v. 171's other clauses stand: "believe in God and His messengers."

**Subḥānahu an Yakūna Lahu Walad: The Glory-Formula as Ontology — Against Divine Offspring**

Then the verse's standing crown — the Book's signature formula against offspring: *subḥānahu an yakūna lahu walad* — "glory be to Him, above having a child!": *subḥān* — the standing exclamation of transcendence (its standing register: the Book's glory-formula appearing at passages where false attribution peaks: 2:116 — "they say: God has taken to Himself a son — glory be to Him — nay — unto Him belongs whatsoever is in the heavens and the earth"; 4:171; 6:100; 9:30-31; 10:68; 17:111; 18:4's warning-file; 19:35 — "it is not for God to take a son — glory be to Him — when He decrees a matter, He but says to it: Be"; 19:88-92 — the standing cosmic-recoil verse-file: "they say: the Beneficent has taken a son... the heavens are nearly rent thereat"; 21:26; 23:91; 37:152-159; 39:4; 43:81-82) — the commentators reading the formula's standing logic: offspring in creation-categories presupposes (a) consort and process, (b) composition, (c) need of succession — all denied by the standing perfection-clause that follows: the One who *has no need*, whose attribute is *ṣamadiyyah* (112:2 — the Self-Sufficient upon whom all depend), does not generate dependents — and the verse's own next clause supplies its standing proof-text.

**Lahu Mā fī al-Samāwāti wa Mā fī al-Arḍ: The Estate as Refutation — and the Guardian-Sufficiency**

The rebuttal of sonship is given, in the verse's closing cadence, as estate-declaration: *lahu mā fī al-samāwāti wa mā fī al-arḍ — wa kafā bi-llāhi wakīlan* — "unto Him belongs whatsoever is in the heavens and whatsoever is on the earth, and God suffices as a Guardian": the commentators (al-Zamakhsharī's standing rhetorical note) mark the theology packed in the possessive: sonship-talk imagines a dependent receiving inheritance and essence from a parent; the Book displays instead a King whose *whole estate* — heaven, earth, every candidate for worship, Jesus included — stands under His title: every being related to Him as possession, not progeny — the created-creature file closed against consubstantiality by the title-deed itself. And the final Name-post the sūrah has already planted thrice (4:81; 4:132) — *wa kafā bi-llāhi wakīlan* — "God suffices as a Guardian": the wakīl-file now sealing the Jesus-file, after sealing the family-file (4:132) and the trust-file (4:81): the believer need no son-mediator to stand surety with heaven — God Himself suffices as Guardian — the standing theological completion of the chapter's whole movement: protection, guardianship, estate, and intercession are offices the Book assigns to God in sufficiency — *kafā*. Thereby the sūrah's final theological peak stands: messengers honored (163-165), witness sufficient (166), disbelief-and-obstruction indicted (167-169), mankind summoned (170), Jesus confessed in maximal honor — and fenced from divinity — in the same breath (171), servanthood installed at the summit of rank (172), the Two-Way wages announced (173), proof and light descended (174), mercy-and-bounty and straight-path guidance promised (175) — and the chapter closing on earth with a ruling again: v. 176 — the kalālah-inheritance — law returning after theology, as if to say the Lord of heaven and earth is also the divider of legacies between sisters and brothers — the sūrah of the Watcher watching from Sinai-heights to the minutest estate-file — one Truth, one Guardian, one Book holding both.

## Sūrah al-Nisāʾ 4:43

> **O you who believe! Draw not near unto prayer when you are drunken until you know what you are uttering, nor in a state of ritual impurity — unless you are passing through — until you have washed. But if you are ill, or on a journey, or one of you has come from satisfying a call of nature, or you have touched women, and you find no water, then resort to clean earth, and wipe your faces and your hands. Truly God is Pardoning, Forgiving.**

**Expanded Commentary**

**Lā Taqrabū al-Ṣalāta wa Antum Sukārā: The Third Stage of the Wine War**

The sūrah that has consecrated family-wealth turns now to consecrate the body's own approach to God, and its first clause carries inside it a whole history of revelation: *lā taqrabū al-ṣalāta wa antum sukārā ḥattā taʿlamū mā taqūlūn* — "approach not the prayer while drunken, until you know what you say": *sukārā*, plurals of *sakrān* — drunken, sense-muddied — and the tradition calibrates the clause as the *third of four stations* by which the heavens weaned the believers from wine: (1) 16:67's neutral mention — wine as provender among the "signs"; (2) 2:219's two-edged assessment — *its sin greater than its profit*; (3) *our verse* — a time-fence around the five prayers leaving virtually no lawful drinking-hour, and (4) 5:90–91's absolute ban — *an abomination of Satan's handiwork: avoid it*. The classical cause-of-revelation locks the scene in the mosque-days before the final ban (al-Wāḥidī, al-Ṭabarī, and al-Zamakhsharī converging): a Companion's feast — ʿAbd al-Raḥmān ibn ʿAwf hosting with drink still then permitted — the guest-Imām reciting at prayer: *"Say: O disbelievers — I worship what you worship"* — and heaven's notice coming down the next coming down in our verse: guard the prayer's speech until the tongue knows its own Owner — al-Qurṭubī's registering the community's own consequent practice: the drinkers withdrew their cups forward the believers timed their drinking behind the evening prayer thereafter, until 5:90 made the clock swivel forever — while a minority maḏhab-line (held by figures in al-Ṭabarī's roll, kept alive in fiqh-gloss) extends *sukārā* to the slumber-drunk as well — the prayer approached only with a present mind — so that the law's core outlives its occasion: *consciousness is the vessel of ṣalāh.*

**Wa Lā Junuban: Major Impurity and the Mosque's Threshold**

The second fence is ritual-purity's: *wa lā junuban illā ʿābirī sabīlin ḥattā taghtasilū* — "nor in major impurity — except passing through — until you bathe": *junub*, from *janābah*, distance — the one held at distance from prayer and mosque until the full ghusl-bath restore him — and *illā ʿābirī sabīl*, save the passer-through of a way: the tradition's mercy-exceptions unfolding in two offices (al-Qurṭubī's juridical inventory): the traveler in janābah who must cross sheltering ground (to the mosque as wayfarer's refuge), and the Medinese with houses opening *through* the mosque's space — the lane-bearers absolved of trespass in passing (the case of the Prophet's own house-doors noted in the law-books). The purity-grammar then adds its own says-it-all derivation: from the same *j-n-b* the Book draws *jār al-junub* just above (v. 36's distant neighbor) — the whole root-family a lesson in *closeness calibrated*: God drawing His servant near Him a man at prayer, distancing him in his unwashed state — not in wrath but in pedagogy, the body trained by real abstinence for the mindfulness the prayer requires. The fiqh-houses extended the tira extended the registry from it: junub-purity as key furthermore to Qur'an-touching and extended recitation, with al-Shāfiʿī's roll and Mālik's detailing modes of remembrance always left open (supplication and mention never barred).

**Fa-Tayammamū Ṣaʿīdan Ṭayyiban: Earth in the Place of Water — The Lost Necklace**

Then comes earth instead of water — the verse's capstone mercy: *fa-tayammamū ṣaʿīdan ṭayyiban fa-msaḥū bi-wujūhikum wa aydīkum* — "then resort to clean high-earth, and wipe your faces and your hands": *tayammum* — aim, repair-to — and *ṣaʿīdan ṭayyiban* — the clean rise of dust — the whole namesake-legislation for the ummah's substitute ablution, granted where water fails through illness, travel, privation (*coming from the privy* — al-ghāʾiṭ, the low hollow, euphemism of relieving nature) or marital contact (*lāmastum al-nisāʾ* — touched women — the schools dividing, as al-Qurṭubī logs, whether bare-hand-touch or intimacy is meant). Its revelation-occasion is one of Islam's most treasured travel-stories, Ṣaḥīḥ al-Bukhārī 334 and Muslim 367 preserving ʿĀʾishah's own narrative: on the return-march her borrowed necklace slipped; the Prophet camped the army in a waterless patch searching the sand; dawn prayer neared, no water for anyone — *and then this verse came down* — Usayd ibn Ḥuḍayr turning to Abu Bakr's house with the celebrated tribute: *"May God reward you with good, O family of Abū Bakr! By God, this is not the first blessing you have brought — nor the first difficulty lifted through you"* — the ummah thereby taking dry-purity from a mishap of the beloved wife, and the Prophet's own larger proclamation stamping it forever (Muslim 522, from the list of favors):

> *"The earth has been made for me a mosque and a purifier; so wherever the prayer overtakes any man of my ummah, he has his mosque and his source of purity."*

**ʿAfūwwan Ghafūrā: Pardoning, Forgiving — and the Theology of the Eased Law**

The seal settles the whole pericope under two of the softest divine names: *inna Allāha kāna ʿafūwwan ghafūrān* — "God is ever Pardoning, Forgiving": *ʿafūww*, the effacer — from *ʿafāʾ*, wiping-away, the active erasure of the offense itself — with *ghafūr* the coverer who receives what repentance hands to Him — and the commentators' cluster-tie the pair precisely to the *eased road* just legislated: the Lawgiver who narrowed wine step by step, who fenced prayer-state by gentler rules than any people before, who substituted dust for water four clauses later — is One who *buries lapses* with alacrity, who multiplies pardon at each stage of the pedagogy: Ibn Kathīr's standing theme — God's gentle dealing in *graded* law (the assembly of stepwise legislation documented already in our vv. 26–28's takhfīf-doctrine) — is here unfolded at the very level of the body. Beside the legal yields (the madhhabs' full tayammum-chapters — when, of what earths, the niyyah-order of face-then-hands, whether prayer repeats on finding water — al-Qurṭubī's own page-bazaar on every register) remains the devotional center the sūrah intends: *God wants consciousness over cups and purity over mud* — He will take earth in His name when water cannot be reached — and beneath the whole soil-sealed liturgy the promise: the Pardoner accompanies the traveler, the sick man, the new bride-couple, the unwashed guest of every sand-road: *any clean place is now your mosque, and any dust your water* — with which the world itself became ustensil became the believer's vessel of worship.

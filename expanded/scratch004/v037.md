## Sūrah al-Nisāʾ 4:37

> **Those who are miserly and enjoin miserliness upon the people and conceal what God has bestowed upon them of His bounty — We have prepared for the disbelievers a humiliating punishment,**

**Expanded Commentary**

**Yabkhalūna wa Yaʾmurūna al-Nāsa bi-l-Bukhl: The Double Miserliness**

The previous verse's iḥsān-compass meets its inverted portrait in two antique greed-words: *alladhīna yabkhalūna wa yaʾmurūna al-nāsa bi-l-bukhl* — "those who are miserly and enjoin miserliness upon the people": *bukhl*, from *b-kh-l*, withholding what is due — the closed hand — and its first category is plain enough, the stingy man; but the second is morally darker and much rarer in any scripture: *the miserliness-propagandist* — the man who admires his own vice so much that he makes a school of it (the Prophet's two-eyed diagnosis standing beside:

> *"Beware of stinginess, for stinginess destroyed those before you: it commanded them to miserliness so they were miserly; it commanded them to sever the womb-ties so they severed; it commanded them to obscenity so they committed it."* (Abū Dāwūd 1698 — narrated by Abū Hurayrah)

— the chain the hadith uncovers precisely our three: personal greed clenched fist; the teaching of greed severing kin; and obscenity as its third its summit-offshoot). Al-Rāzī's psychology of the double sin notes the ruin's structure: the ordinary miser harms only himself, and the preache d-miser corrupts the world — for the vice *transmitted* becomes a custom, the custom a culture, culture a doom; consequently the therefore the Lawgiver's vice-list is ascending here — individual theft punished less than the theft-schools of men; and the miser-preacher, by recruiting others into his closed circle, is judged as causing the as causing the stifling of iḥsān across generations, guilt multiplying in chains without increase of his burden *beyond* likeness (cf. 16:25 — they bear their burdens in full together with the burdens of those they misguide).

**Wa Yaktumūna Mā Ātāhum Allāhu min Faḍlih: Concealing the Bounty — Wealth or Word**

The third clause — *wa yaktumūna mā ātāhum Allāhu min faḍlih* — "and conceal what God has bestowed on them of His bounty" — drew the tradition into its own two classical excavations: (1) the immediate-context excavation (al-Rāzī's context-rule: verses thirty-six through thirty-nine are *spending-talk*, one unbroken treasury-sermon) reads the concealed bounty as *wealth* — the hoarded fortune, the deductible-right never told, the poor-neighbor kept from every ledger — and al-Qurṭubī's law-eye deriving the whole zakāh-machinery behind it: *whoever holds wealth has God's bounty; whoever conceals it has joined the miser-ranks* — with the wealth-flow's the wealth-flow's theology unpacked at 9:34–35 (hoarders' gold branded to their foreheads in Fire) and 3:180's collar of collar readied for the Day of Resurrection: *let not those who are miserly with God's bounty think it is better for them*; and (2) the report-backed excavation (al-Ṭabarī's chains, al-Zamakhsharī's register) reads bounty as *knowledge* — the verse an early Madinan arrow at the scripture-concealers among the People of the Book who hid what their Torah told of the Prophet ﷺ: *they concealed their bounty* in the very sense 2:42, 140 and 2:159 charge against them — God inscribing both treasuries in the single faḍl-word since Heaven's logic made no distinction: *goods given are a trust; knowledge given is a greater one; and both perish only in concealment.* The two excavations do not quarrel — each is the Book's plenary style, treasures of hand and treasures of tongue taxed under the same Name.

**Li-l-Kāfirīna ʿAdhāban Muhīnan: Why the Misers Enter Under "the Disbelievers"**

The seal's horror, on first glance, overshoots its target: *wa aʿtadnā li-l-kāfirīna ʿadhāban muhīnan* — "We have prepared for the *disbelievers* a humiliating punishment" — where the verse spoke of misers — and the commentators drew the single entire doctrine the sūrah repeatedly encodes: *bukhl at its root-state is kufr* — stinginess perfected amounts to disbelief, for the miser has made the gold his trusted god (the seven-destroyers hadith of v. 31 already placing *stinginess with what God gave* — * the *shuhḥ* exactly beside sorcery and blood). The Prophet's widow-report ties even ordinary petty greed to faith-exit in the famous indication indicated beneath this clause's branch: *"the miser does not enter the Garden"* (a report circulating in the Musnad channels), and al-Bukhārī's great theology-book (Ṣaḥīḥ al-Bukhārī 11 — the Kitāb al-ʿIlm related register) echoing from the companions' tongues the doctrine's understanding: sins of *that seed* — stinginess, deception, betrayal — were ranged under * their doom the doom of the kāfirūn because their root is the same — *dis-trusting God's provision: the miser refuses to believe that what goes out of his hand returns seventy-fold from God's* (2:261); so the withholding hand is, inside, an unbelieving heart: hence Hell-ward twin to God's statute for partner-setters. *Wayl to him who died owning* — the old proverb of the salaf — and the Garden belongs to the open hand.

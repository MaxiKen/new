## Sūrah al-Nisāʾ 4:45

> **God knows best your enemies. God suffices as a Protector, and God suffices as a Helper.**

**Expanded Commentary**

**Wa Allāhu Aʿlamu bi-Aʿdāʾikum: The Intelligence of Heaven Over the Enemy-Circle**

The answer to the recruiters of v. 44 comes as a single declarative of surveillance: *wa Allāhu aʿlamu bi-aʿdāʾikum* — "and God knows best your enemies": the clause is repeated in several places in the Book (cf. 4:25's *wa Allāhu aʿlamu bi-īmānikum* — God knows best your faith) but its two polemic-settings in our sūrah (4:45 here; cf. also 60:1's "take not My enemy and your enemy as allies") are the spearheaded ones: the believers' own intel is short — they see gift-bringers, neighbors, jurists' smiles — *but God knows best* — the commentators (al-Ṭabarī's transmission-chains and al-Rāzī's reasoning) underlining the cut both ways: it unmasks covert hostility (the error-market of v. 44, whose real shop-sign was friendship) — *and* it unshackles the believers from every over-reading, since not every kind-visiting portion-bearer is an enemy — only as God knows: the Muslim thereby saved from both naïveté and paranoia by delegating the intelligence-work Upstairs. The clause thus placed at the head of the Madinan confrontation-verses teaches the Book's community the posture it must hold through the whole engagement: vigilance without busybody-enemy-building; calm custody of the path under God's register of whoever opposes it. Al-Rāzī's fine point on the Arabic structure (the *aʿlam* — knows-best — intimating that human readings may be right only in part) remains the standing pastoral gem: heaven's file on one's enemies is *always* more accurate than the believer's — both on the hidden enemy inside the congregation and on the imagined enemy wrongly feared.

**Kafā bi-Allāhi Waliyyan: Sufficiency of the Deputy-Nearest**

Then the sufficiency-pair that warms the rest of the Qur'an: *kafā bi-Allāhi waliyyan wa kafā bi-Allāhi naṣīran* — "God suffices as a Protector, and God suffices as a Helper": *kafā*, "suffices" — the compact verb of adequacy, predicated with the quasi-prepositional *bi-* (suffices *by* God — no second subject needed) — belongs to the Book's own battalion of *ḥasbunā Allāh* declarations (eighteen *kafā* occurrences across the Qur'an tradition counts the counting itself a classical commonplace: 2:137's "God suffices thee against them"; 3:173– 39:36's "is not God sufficient for His servant?"; 39:38's "God suffices me"; 8:64's "sufficient for thee is God and those of the believers who follow thee" — the root of a whole family of tawakkul-verses) — and *walī*, our recurring root-word of v. 33's heir-half, here at its broadest: the near-champion, the deputized guardian, the patron-with-closeness (whose standing opposite in the Book is precisely *walī against walī* battles: 3:28's believers-not-taking-disbelievers-as-*awliyāʾ* to the exclusion of believers; 9:71's counter-declaration — "the believing men and women are awliyāʾ of one another"): the choice announced as starkly personal — for every enemy that hands you his friendship-contract, God answers: *I am the only walī whose sufficiency never runs out.* Ibn Kathīr's commentary-summary distills the clause's Madinan rôle: the portion-bearers had invited alliance and offered support as the price of compromise — God's response: *you need no ally but Him* — the exact line by which the Prophet ﷺ unim on his exact word the community un-imported itself from every senior-partner offer in town.

**Naṣīran: The Help That Has a History**

The second name in the pair — *naṣīr*, Helper — drags behind it the whole campaign-memory of the Book's use: *naṣr* is help that *decides* — victory-assistance — and the Qur'an's register had already notched with it a growing war-archive: Badr's own fight-clause falling under naṣr (3:123 "God helped you at Badr while you were few"; 8:9–10's "I help you with a thousand of the angels in succession"); the Hunayn instruction-tag noted at 9:25 ("God helped you on many a field"); and the seal of seals — 8:62's "God is sufficient for thee — He it is who supported thee with His help (naṣrihi) and with the believers": the word being God's *external* sufficiency — the walī's interior custody become manifest as battlefield and courtroom aid for the beleaguered believers of Madinah — so the pair walī/naṣīr covers *both chambers of sufficiency*: friendship within and force without. The classical preachers loved pairing them through life-applications (al-Qurṭubī extending the du'a the devotional spine: whoever makes God his *walī* loses no friendship by obeying Him; whoever counts Him *naṣīr* needs nobody's sword-contracts, no foreign sponsors, no protection-money) — and the Medinese context hands the doctrine its sharpest edge: *every alliance the Muslim polity would ever contract must be audited against this double sufficiency* — no friendship that divorces the walī, no sponsors that sideline the naṣīr — for if an "ally's" price is the path of v. 44, then the Qur'an replies in two words: *independent & sufficient:* God — both.

**The Psychological Charter for Every Outnumbered Reader**

Beneath the politics the verse carries its perennial pastoral code for the beleaguered of every age: God's *knowing-best* absolves the believer from the endless spiral-scan of "who among them is the enemy?" — one lives honestly, guards the path, and deposits the intelligence-file with the Omniscient; and His *double sufficiency* bankrupts fear at its two roots — loneliness (I have a walī) and weakness (I have a naṣīr). The sealed doctrine of the sūrah's confrontation-section is thereby in place before its first command arrives (it will come in v. 71's "take your precautions"): God knows the enemy-map; God fills both needs; so the believer may neither surrender his path to the enemy's friendship-price nor panic for shortage of backers — and the Prophet's standing habit polishes the lesson to its famous edge:

> *"Whoever strikes a If you relied upon God with true reliance, He would provide you as He provides the birds: they set out in the morning hungry and return in the evening full."* (al-Tirmidhī 2344 — ḥasan ṣaḥīḥ)

— reliance being not passivity but *sufficiency-underwriting*: move, fight, ally — all inside the knowledge that your two accounts, friendship and aid, are *fully funded from above* — *kafā* — enough — the word is final.

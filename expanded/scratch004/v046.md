## Sūrah al-Nisāʾ 4:46

> **Among those who are Jews are some who distort the meaning of the word, and say, "We hear and disobey," and "Hear, as one who hears not!" and "Attend to us," twisting their tongues and disparaging religion. And had they said, "We hear and obey," and "Listen," and "Regard us," it would have been better for them and more proper. But God cursed them for their disbelief, so they believe not, save a few.**

**Expanded Commentary**

**Mina Alladhīna Hādū: A Portion, Within a Portion, Charged**

The indictment of the scripture-shareholders now becomes specific: *mina alladhīna hādū yuḥarrifūna al-kalima ʿan mawāḍiʿih* — "from among those who are Jews [are those who] distort words from their places": the double-sift of *min* — *some* among them — tempering the charge at its start (as the tradition insists whenever the Book speaks of error-communities: the verse closes likewise with *save a few*), while the verb *yuḥarrifūna* — *taḥrīf*, to edge aside, to to dis-place — names the signature sin of scripture-keepers: not forging the Book outright but *edging words out of their berths* (the noun *ḥarf*, edge/limit/letter, inside). The classical inventory of taḥrīf developed from this verse's four poster-appearances in the Book (2:75; 4:46; 5:13; 5:41) runs in three grades: (1) *mis-siting* itself — adding, omitting, or replacing actual words of the Torah (Ibn Kathīr's and al-Qurṭubī's heavy grade — deliberate textual tampering, as the jurists altered stoning-lines and prophecy-notices); (2) *mis-interpretation* — words retained and their *sense* twisted — al-Zamakhsharī's grammarian-eye inclining here (the rearrangement of context, *ʿan mawāḍiʿih*, being semantic as much as codicological); and (3) *mis-reporting* of what the Prophet said — al-Qurṭubī's minority prong — twisting the words of Muhammad ﷺ himself into caricature. The taḥrīf-doctrinology The doctrine that matured from this seed — that God's earlier Books were preserved as *His speech* but their custodies imperfectly guarded — anchors the whole later Islamic account of scriptures: the Book confirms yet audits (5:48 *muhayminan ʿalayh* — sentinel over it) — and the crime of the custodians is here named in its most precise possible definition: *the Word is not denied — it is dis-placed.*

**Samiʿnā wa ʿAṣaynā: The Inverted Creed Both Heard and Said**

Then the three mock-clauses, each one the inversion of Islamic discipleship: *wa yaqūlūna samiʿnā wa ʿaṣaynā* — "and they say: We hear — and disobey!" — the deliberate retort-counterfeit of the believers' own covenant-formula (2:285 — "we hear and obey," *samiʿnā wa aṭaʿnā*; and 24:51 — the description of true believers: *"their only saying when called to God and His Messenger is: We hear and we obey"*) — the two-syllable swap from *aṭaʿnā* to *ʿaṣaynā* converting faith-protocol into defiance-flag; *wa ismaʿ ghayra musmaʿin* — "and: Hear! — as one un-heard!" — the commentators weighing its evil registers (al-Ṭabarī's roll: either *listen — may you never be heard [answered]* — a curse of silence upon the Prophet's call; or *listen as one who cannot hear* — the insult of treating the Messenger as a deaf instrument) — either way a curse or jeer flung within syllable-range of courtesy; and *wa rāʿinā layyan bi-alsinatihim* — "and: rāʿinā! — twisting with their tongues": the celebrated *rāʿinā* — shepherd-us!, attend to us — almost courteous, yet twisted with a sideways articulation (a lengthening, a winking spin among themselves) so the word slid toward insult or, in the reports' own glossing, toward a sour play on *ruʿūnah* — foolishness (2:104's parallel prescription confirms the clinic: believers were thenceforth commanded to say *unẓurnā* — "regard us" — rather than *rāʿinā* — and *unbelievers eavesdropped the difference* — al-Bukhārī 5947 and Muslim 2990 holding the whole exchange: *"They used the word rāʿinā and smirked; so the believers were commanded to say unẓurnā"* — with ʿUmar's own court punishing misuse later). The three clauses together are the portrait of *obedience's saboteurs*: hear-only-to-alter hearing only to alter, addressing only to deride — the mind of the seminar-mocker substituted for the disciple's *hearing-and-obeying*.

**Wa Ṭaʿnan fī al-Dīn: Disparagement of Religion as Their Destination**

The sentence's tail names the slander-clauses' true intent: *wa ṭaʿnan fī al-dīn* — "and as a stab into religion": *ṭaʿn*, lance-thrust — sneering branded as spearwork on the faith itself (the Book's own rhetoric of word-wounds: cf. 9:61's "they hurt the Prophet saying he is an ear"; 33:57's "those who hurt God and His Messenger" — heaven counting verbal stabs within ḥarm-crime) — and the tradition notes the clause's range beyond the moment-echo: every tongue-twist against the Messenger is *fi al-dīn* — aimed at religion, not merely at a person; the Prophet himself articulated the logic in his in his general counsel to the community — narrated of him that he was wounded in the cause of God and sneered at, but *hurt to the messenger equals hurt to the Sender* (al-Bukhārī's Kitāb al-Adab registers the expansion: "he who wrongs one of God's friends — God declares war against him" — the ḥadīth qudsī of Bukhārī 6502). The clause thereby serves as the scene's judgment-summation: the curses did not merely stain etiquette — they disfigured religion in the eyes of the bystanding poor and new converts (al-Zamakhsharī noticing the audience-form: twists done *bi-alsinatihim* — tongues plied theatrically — the performance the medium of the crime) — hence the verdict: *little courtesy-crimes are never little when their object is God's road.*

**Wa Law Annahum Qālū... La-ʿAḍūhum: Mercy Recommended Even to Mockers**

Then the verse does what few indictments do — it writes the neighboring correct speech, all three emendations: *wa law annahum qālū samiʿnā wa aṭaʿnā wa ismaʿ* — "We hear and obey — and: Listen!" — *wa wa-unẓurnā la-kāna khayran lahum wa aqwam* — "it would have been better for them and more upright": heaven itself line-editing the mockers' script — showing that the whole tragedy sits only syllables away from repair (the comparative *aqwam* — straighter, more proper — the same root of *qawwāmūn* one arc above — conduct being, everywhere, the standing-up of words); the teachers have always prized the clause as the Qur'an's pastoral pattern: indictment *with template* — God never only convicts; He dictates the alternative sentence — so the entire psychology of true daʿwah stands in micro-cosm in clauses: never rebuke *without teaching the substitute-word.* And then the seal swings shut on what their chosen syllables earned them: *wa lakin laʿanahumu Allāhu bi-kufrihim fa-lā yuʾminūna illā qalīlā* — "but God cursed them for their disbelief — so they believe not, save a few": *laʿn*, banishment from mercy — the doctrinal weight(2:161's universal curse-word for those who die upon kufr; 2:159's cult of concealers laid beside our earlier conceal our vv. 37's concealers of bounty) — and *illā qalīlā* — save a few — cutting either as *few believing* (al-Qurṭubī: the saved remnant among them — the ʿAbdullāh b. Salām-line who converted) or *lightly believing* (the gloss of the fine print: belief so thin it cannot stand). Between the two readings the reader breathes — because the ʿAbdullāh-ibn-Salām-line *proves the double-sift of* min *at the verse's head*: some from among them — not all — broke pattern and washed themselves from the laʿnah the curse — and their conversion among Madinah's honest scholars (the "men of scripture whom the verse moved" of v. 47's reports) remains the Book's standing door to every portion-bearer since: *the script was dictated; the choice remains yours.*

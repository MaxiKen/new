## Sūrah al-Nisāʾ 4:130

> **And if the two separate, God shall enrich each of them from His bounty; and God is ever All-Encompassing, All-Wise.**

**Expanded Commentary**

**Wa In Yatafarraqā Yughni Allāhu Kullan min Saʿatih: The Divine Alimony-Guarantee**

The marriage-file of vv. 128–130 closes with a promise so gentle that commentators have prized it as the Book's own consolation to every ending household: *wa in yatafarraqā yughni Allāhu kullan min saʿatih* — "and if the two separate, God shall enrich each of them from His bounty": *yatafarraqā* — the dual of separation — should the reconciliation-attempts of v. 128 and the repair of v. 129 fail, and the marriage lawfully end — no disaster-note sounds; instead the verse assigns the aftermath to God Himself: *yughni Allāhu kullan* — "God shall enrich each" — the verb *yughni* from gh-n-y — sufficiency, the condition of needing no one — with God as its standing Name al-Ghanī — the Self-Sufficient (2:263's standing pairing "God is Self-Sufficient, Forbearing"; 6:133 — "your Lord is the Self-Sufficient, possessing mercy"; 35:15 — "O mankind — you are the poor unto God — and God is the Self-Sufficient, the Praised") — and its stunning grammatical placement here: not "perhaps He shall make them content" but the unqualified future-verb of divine undertaking — enrichment guaranteed *to each* — *kullan* — the singular pronoun deliberately individualizing: the husband and the wife, separately, severally, each one — the exalted pledge extending to the departing wife who may face the severest economic exposure of the separation, written as surely as to the husband. Al-Ṭabarī reads the clause as God's standing comfort against the fear that holds bad marriages frozen — the fear of destitution — and Ibn Kathīr marks its precise sense: God shall enrich the one with another spouse, with provision otherwise, with contentment itself — *min saʿatih* — "from His bounty" — *saʿah* from s-ʿ-w — width, amplitude — the bounty of God being the standing Qur'ānic superlative of resources ("God's earth is wide" — 29:56; "God is Possessor of great bounty" — 2:105; 3:174; 8:29; 57:21's standing *al-faḍl al-ʿaẓīm*) — the married pair having shared one household under God's width, each departs under that same width — the house dividable, the Provider undivided.

**The Consolation-File: When Endings Are Not Punishments**

The verse stands within a wider Book-comfort that the commentators fold together here: 65:2–3's standing promise — "and whoever keeps from evil — God makes for him a way out — and provides for him from whence he does not reckon" — revealed, the standing Sabāb-reading says, precisely in the wake of the divorce-legislation of Sūrah al-Ṭalāq; and 65:6's provision-duties framing the separated wife's maintenance. The practical wisdom is the standing pastoral point: the law permits divorce as last resort — "among the lawful things most odious to God" (v. 128's standing hadith-caution — Abū Dāwūd 2178's register) — yet once the door is walked through with iḥsān, God does not exit with the marriage; He remains as the Sponsor of both households that were formerly one. The tradition-level pastoral register adds the standing observation of the sages: how many a harmful union ended, and each party found in the years after — through new marriage, through livelihood, through sheer interior peace — the "enrichment" this verse promised, discovering that the feared door had been in fact two doors. And the psychological note deserves its own line: the verse re-narrates divorce theologically — removal from the ledger of shame to the ledger of divine providence — not celebrating separation (v. 128's *al-ṣulḥu khayr* still standing) but removing the terror-argument that freezes couples in cruelty: the breakable marriage a healthier institution than the unbreakable prison.

**Wa Kāna Allāhu Wāsiʿan Ḥakīman: The Two Names That Seal the Promise**

The verse seals with a Name-pair unique in the sūrah's family-file: *wa kāna Allāhu wāsiʿan ḥakīman* — "and God is ever All-Encompassing, All-Wise": *wāsiʿ* — the Vast, the Ample-Containing — a Name the Book folds around every constraint it loosens (its standing file: 2:115 — "whithersoever you turn — there is the face of God — God is All-Encompassing, Knowing"; 2:268 — "God promises you forgiveness from Him and bounty — and God is All-Encompassing, Knowing"; 5:54's standing "and God is All-Encompassing, Knowing"; 24:32 — the sūrah of marriage-regulation — "if they should be poor — God shall enrich them from His bounty — and God is All-Encompassing, Knowing") — the name itself the etymological match of *saʿatih* earlier in the verse: bounty wide because the Giver is Vast; His amplitude guarantees the amplitude of the promise. And *ḥakīm* — the All-Wise — seals the legislation's other flank: the permission of separation is no concession to chaos but the decree of Wisdom that saw what counselors and courts cannot — sometimes iṣlāḥ fails, sometimes the two plants grow only apart — and the Wise legislated release with dignity. The two Names together answer the two anxieties of the divorcing: the Wāsiʿ answers the economic fear ("how shall we each live?"), and the Ḥakīm answers the juridical-moral fear ("was this release itself lawful, wise, right?") — God Vast enough to enrich both, and Wise enough that His permission needs no apology. The marriage-file thus closes the way the sūrah's whole legal style closes — law folded into Names: the ordinance given, the anxiety named, the divine attribute posted as standing guarantee — and the reader exits the family-chapters as he entered them (4:1's standing exordium — "fear your Lord... God is ever over you a Watcher") — between taqwā at the threshold and bounty at the farewell. From here the sūrah will lift its gaze once more from households to the All (v. 131's standing crescendo: "unto God belongs whatsoever is in the heavens and the earth... God is sufficient as a Guardian") — and the ownership-cadence of v. 126, planted deliberately above this family-file, now gathers it up: every household settled here was settled upon God's own estate, under the Encompassing — the Wāsiʿ — the Wise.

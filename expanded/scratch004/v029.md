## Sūrah al-Nisāʾ 4:29

> **O you who believe! Consume not each other's wealth falsely, but trade by mutual consent, and slay not yourselves. Truly God is Merciful unto you.**

**Expanded Commentary**

**Lā Taʾkulū Amwālakum Baynakum bi-l-Bāṭil: The Forbidden Economy of the Stomach**

The sūrah's statute-line resumes with one of the Book's mightiest economic sentences — *yā ayyuhā alladhīna āmanū lā taʾkulū amwālakum baynakum bi-l-bāṭil* — "O you who believe, do not consume your wealth among yourselves in falsehood": wealth harvested by *bāṭil* — by the vain, the void, the title-less — and consumed among the community as though lawful. The commentators enumerate the devouring instruments in the full register, as al-Qurṭubī sets them from the Companions' interpretations through the law-books' maturity: **usury** (ribā — 2:275–278's own war-declaration against its eaters), **gambling** (maysir — 5:90–91's abomination-pair), **fraud and measure-cheating** (the people of Shuʿayb's signature sin: 11:84–85's "give full measure and weight in justice"; 83:1–3's woe upon the *muṭaffifīn*), **theft, embezzlement, bribery** (2:188's direct command from the same divine mouth: "do not consume your wealth among yourselves falsely, nor proffer it to judges, that you may devour a known portion of people's wealth sinfully"), and every contract whose consent has been tricked, coerced, or misinformed. The offense is measured at the *eat*: *akl al-māl* — consumption of property — because the Book's moral eye fixes wealth's telos: provision; the false-taken dinar feeds its taker only as illusion — khabīth — and the Prophet's own inventory seals the file:

> *"The flesh grown from the unlawful — the Fire has first claim to it."* (al-Tirmidhī 616; from Kaʿb ibn ʿUjrah — among the standing hadiths of the market-sermons; and Muslim's parallel from Jābir on the supplicant with disheveled hair: *"his food is unlawful, his drink unlawful... how then shall he be answered?"* — Muslim 1015)

**Illā an Takūna Tijāratan ʿan Tarāḍin Minkum: Consent, the Soul of Lawful Trade**

The verse then answers its own ban with the lawful road in five words — *illā an takūna tijāratan ʿan tarāḍin minkum* — "except that it be commerce by mutual consent among you": *tijārah*, open trade, *ʿan tarāḍ* — *upon mutual contentment* — the word tarāḍī from *riḍā*, the pleasure of consent — the commentators deriving from this tiny circumstantial clause the entire law of valid sale: offer-acceptance without coercion, fraud-free, substance-known — al-Ṭabarī's authorities — Ibn ʿAbbās' school at their head — took *tarāḍ* as the sine qua non that *bāṭil* lacks: what is wrested, swindled, or concealed-defective never met *riḍā*. Al-Qurṭubī hereby grounds pillars of the fiqh of transactions — gharar's reduction, option-cooling (*khiyār al-majlis*, from the hadith: "the two parties of a sale hold option until they part" — al-Bukhārī 2119; Muslim 1531/1532), and the Prophet's blessed dua for the honest trader:

> *"God has mercy on the man who is easy when he sells, easy when he buys, and easy when he claims his due."* (al-Bukhārī 2076, from Jābir)

And beneath *consent* the Book plants a soul *within* trade that law alone cannot manufacture: marketplace-love — commerce between people who deal as brothers — as the hadith again shaped it: the two traders are the two friends till they separate honestly.

**Wa Lā Taqtulū Anfusakum: Self-Killing and Brother-Killing in One Command**

The verse's second prohibition — *wa lā taqtulū anfusakum* — "and slay not yourselves" — the commentators read in its blessed breadth, three mutually-supporting senses standing in the tradition from Ibn ʿAbbās's school on: (1) *slay not one another* — your *anfus* comprising your own community-body, so that the murdered Muslim's blood is *self*-blood of the ummah (al-Ṭabarī's first reading, coeval with vv. 92–93's with vv. 92–93 on the believer-slayer's doom and 17:33's "slay not the soul God has forbidden except by right"); (2) *slay not your own souls* — suicide flatly — directly barred: the life-breath is God's deposit, unlawful at one's own hand as at another's — and the hadith-collection stands terrifyingly explicit here: Thābit ibn al-Ḍaḥḥāk's treaty-story: "Cursed is he who kills himself with iron — his eternal punishment at Hell with iron forever in his hand" (al-Bukhārī 1363, Muslim 110 — the standing text); and the wounded man of the Khaybar-campaign who impatient of pain finished himself with his sword: *"God said: My servant hastened before Me to his soul; I have forbidden him Paradise"* (al-Bukhārī 1363; Muslim 111, from Abū Hurayrah — the exact wording of the qudsi sentence); (3) the subtler derivative the the ascetics treasured from the Sufi commentators: do not *kill your souls* through sin's slow strangulation — sin also a self-killing (the killing of the spirit), like ākil-māl, the inward consumption — three warnings drawn in one phrase.

**Inna Allāha Kāna Bikum Raḥīman: Why the Severe Sentence Ends Soft**

Then the mercy-seal whose deep placement the commentators never stop wondering at: *inna Allāha kāna bikum raḥīman* — "truly God has ever been Merciful toward you" — following bans as grave as fraudulent eating and bloodshed: the mercy is *in the forbidding* itself — for a community permitted to eat each other and slay each other consumes itself. Al-Qurtubī's own summary holds the theological register precisely: God's statutes around wealth and blood are mercy-architecture; the verses immediately adjacent (v. 30's fire-warning to the defiant; v. 31's absolution-promise to the avoiding) complete the mercy-proof-balance — covenant-limits *itself* being divine care. Thus the marketplace and the blood-price were placed, by this twenty-ninth verse of the chapter, forever inside the shrine of mercy: for the honest traders and alive bodies of an ummah that stops eating each other, life itself becomes a masjid-canopy — *wealth neither devoured nor devourer; souls neither killed nor squandered; and over both, the Merciful watching His own mercy at work in His law.*

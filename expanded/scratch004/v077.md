## Sūrah al-Nisāʾ 4:77

> **Hast thou not seen those unto whom it was said, "Restrain your hands, and perform the prayer, and give the alms"? But when fighting was prescribed for them, behold, a party of them feared men, even as they should have feared God, or with greater fear, and they said, "Our Lord! Why hast Thou prescribed fighting for us — If only Thou wouldst grant us reprieve for a term nigh!" Say: "Scant is the enjoyment of this world, and the Hereafter is better for those who are reverent, and you shall not be wronged so much as the thread of a date stone."**

**Expanded Commentary**

**Kuffū Aydiyakum: The Meccan Posture — Hands Held, Prayer and Zakāh Blooming**

The battle-arch now flashes back to the *earlier dispensation* — the Meccan years — and the contrast is the whole pedagogy: *qīla lahum kuffū aydiyakum wa aqīmū al-ṣalāta wa ātū al-zakāh* — "it was said unto them: hold back your hands; establish the prayer; give the alms": the believers of Mecca — chronically provoked, tortured, some begging to take the sword against their oppressors — were ordered *restraint* instead: the three command-pillars of the Meccan stage listed as a whole policy: (1) *hold your hands* — no retaliatory violence; (2) *prayer in place of strike* — vertical devotion as the method of holding; (3) *alms as cement of the bruised fellowship* — thus teaching the world (al-Rāzī's standing observation at this verse) that *Ṣalāh and Zakāh were Islam's first twin ordinances* — duty to God and duty to man — preceding the war-ordinance for them — the sequence henceforward fixed in the books of jihād-theory: spiritual formation *before* armed action (the community not given swords until it had first been given prayer and zakāh: discipline-of-soul must precede discipline-of-arms, or the sword teaches only savagery) — and the Meccan oppressed's own petitions for permission to fight were gently declined by the Prophet until the hijrah until the Madinan ground was prepared (the Sīrah-scrolls' many registers the well-known petitions — restless men wanting to settle scores with Quraysh, told again: hold; I have not been commanded to fight).

**Fa-lammā Kutiba ʿAlayhimu al-Qitālu... Khashaw al-Nāsa: The Turnabout of the Hearts**

Then the scandal-clause: *fa-lammā kutiba ʿalayhimu al-qitālu idhā farīqun minhum yakhshawna al-nāsa ka-khashyati Allāhi aw ashadda khashyatan* — "yet when fighting was prescribed for them — lo! a faction of them feared men as the fear of God, or with worse fear": *kutiba* — written-prescribed — the same covenant-verb of v. 66's hypothetical (revelation writing hard writing obligation, not suggestion) — whereupon the role-reversal in the deep heart is disclosed by the Lord Himself: the same people who longed the same mouths that itched to fight in Mecca now, after permission arrives, *fear men* — *ka-khashyati Allāhi* — *with a fear like* the fear of God — *aw ashadda khashyatan* — or a fear exceeding it — a clause of frightening honesty: heaven granting that human fear of men can swell to can swell past reverential fear of Creator (the commentators separating into two camp two eternal lessons: (1) the salaf-readers' — fear has only one lawful object, and mislocated KHW is a creed-malfunction; and (2) al-Rāzī's pastoral classification — the attribution of this shrinking even to true believers is a mercy-reading debate the safer reading being the verses address *the hypocrite the pretender-class among the nominal masses* — some commentators indeed placing the verse against the people who *said* "why didst Thou prescribe fighting upon us" — the same voices really seeking postponement (per 47:20's parallel — "when a firm sūrah is sent down and fighting mentioned in it, thou seest those in whose hearts is sickness looking at thee with the look of one fainting from death") — the Prophet's own ranks containing men who faltered at Uḥud the Sīrah's own scenes showing how even the sincerest soldiers of Madinah needed steadying (and the registers of the Remus of the resolute examples standing eternally beside: ʿAbdullāh ibn Rawāḥah's ʿAbdullāh ibn Rawāḥah's own conducted at the Muʾtah-courage tradition of Bukhārī more apt more apt here: leave it) — the honest doctrine the tradition settled upon: *fear is natural; allowing it to veto obedience is the disease* — this verse separating the trembling that continues marching from the trembling that petitions for postponement).

**Rabbanā Lima Katabta ʿAlaynā al-Qitāla... Lawlā Akhkhartanā ilā Ajalin Qarīb: The Postponement-Prayer**

Their recorded murmur — the clause every generation of the comfort-loving recognizes: *rabbanā lima katabta ʿalayna al-qitāla lawlā akhkhartanā ilā ajalin qarīb* — "our Lord, why hast Thou prescribed fighting for us — If only Thou wouldst defer us to a near term": *lawlā* — would that — yearning's own particle — and *ilā ajalin qarīb* — to a near term — the asker asking not for abolition but *delay* — the classical psycho-commentary of the commentators' dissection is the verse's standing precision-instrument: postponement-seeking is *disobedience's cleanest costume* — never "never," always "later" — the criminal the sages tabulating the carnal soul's standard progresses: it argues not against God's right to command, only against the timing — and heaven's reply (next clause) must aim therefore not at duty but at *time* — refuting the whole scheme of deferral — for the *ajal* every man craves is in fact already laid (4:78's next-verse seals it: "wheresoever you may be, death will overtake you — though in towers raised high") — the soul's "a little later" being precisely the delusion the grave buries daily (the standing hadith the tradition binds forever to this verse: the Prophet's words on the Resurrection's nearness (Tirmidhī 2338 — *"the Hour and I have been sent like these two"* — two fingers) — no procrastination-lease being henceforth ever granted).

**Qul Matāʿu al-Dunyā Qalīlun: The Scanty Present — and the Fatīl Promise Again**

The Prophet's heaven-dictated answer arrives in the balanced tradesman's in the measured clauses of inventory-closure: *qul matāʿu al-dunyā qalīlun wa al-ākhiratu khayrun li-man ittaqā wa lā tuẓlamūna fatīlan* — "Say: the enjoyment of the world is scant; the Hereafter is better for those who fear God; and you shall not be wronged so much as a thread": *matāʿ*, enjoyment, utilizable good — *qalīl*, scant — the whole of worldly delight devalued in a single adjective (its homeric? its many Book-companions bearing the same word: 3:197, 4:102 9:38's "the enjoyment of this world is but little beside the Hereafter"; 13:26 — "the life of this world, beside the Hereafter, is nothing but enjoyment" — and 87:16–17's blazing exchange — "Nay! You prefer the worldly life — while the Hereafter is better and more lasting") — *khayrun lit khayrun li-man ittaqā*: the Hereafter's better is conditional upon *taqwā* — the weari the fear-reverence that has just been mislocated upon men now correctly re-seated upon God — evaluation and allocation of fear being the verse's whole pedagogy — and the seal returns the sūrah's own talismanic justice-unit: *lā tuẓlamūna fatīlan* — not a date-stone thread (v. 49's motif) — the promised fidelity of the audit extended even to the fainters: their reluctance will not be exaggerated against them (al-Rāzī and al-Zamakhsharī's comfort-note: whoever feared and *then obeyed* loses nothing of his wage — the fatīl-clause guaranteeing honest accounting of every trembling step marshaled mastered into the march) — so that the whole verse stands as the ummah's clinic for war-fear and life-fear alike: hold hands until the time ripens, pray, give; when the command comes, march; fear God only; ask not for delays; measure the world as *scant* — and trust the thread-promise: *no trembling believer ever was cheated of even a film of a date-skin of his reward.*

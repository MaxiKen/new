## Sūrah al-Nisāʾ 4:109

> **There you are, pleading on their behalf concerning the life of this world! But who will plead with God on their behalf on the Day of Resurrection, or be a guardian for them?**

**Expanded Commentary**

**Hāʾ Antum Hāʾulāʾi Jādaltum ʿAnhum fī al-Ḥayāti al-Dunyā: Mocked Advocates of Earth**

The sūrah's judicial-verse now turns its gaze from the Prophet's washed bench to the clamoring clan — and the pronoun's change carries the drama: *hāʾ antum hāʾulāʾi jādaltum ʿanhum fī al-ḥayāti al-dunyā* — "there you are! — you! — you pleaded for them in the worldly life": the emphatic double-marking (the commentators' standing note: *hāʾ antum* — behold, you-yourselves — the particle of astonished rebuke — heaven's address-formal doubling to the crowd, with *hāʾulāʾi* — these-here — pointing at them like a bailiff pointing at the the meaning: the astonishment-form of a court-dramatur the address-grammar is itself theatrical — behold *you* — professional pleaders-at-law of Ṭuʿmah's kin) — and the address now in the *plural*, not the singular — the standing grammatical consensus (al-Ṭabarī, al-Rāzī, al-Qurṛṭubī): vv. 105–107's commands were singular, to the Prophet; v. 109's blast opens upon the *clan-pleaders* — the whole contingent of Banū Ẓafar who had lobbied the Prophet's court — name heaven addressing the supporting attorneys directly — with the charge named exactly: *jādaltum ʿanhum* — you pled-l you disputed on their behalf (the jidāl-verb of v. 107 again — the sūrah's own triplication: 105's khaṣīm-address to the Prophet, 107's general ban — now 109's factual indictment against the men who were in fact doing it) — and the key-limiting phrase *fī al-ḥayāti al-dunyā* — *in the worldly life*: their whole advocacy-arsenal — clan-honor, press-grief press-gang press-party assembled tears, threats, standing-in public shame-play and genealogical appeal — having exactly one jurisdiction: *this world's hearing*.

**Fa-Man Yujādilu Allāha ʿAnhum Yawma al-Qiyāmah: The Unrepresented at the True Assize**

Then the after-life after-bench question that dissolves every earthly bar- every plea-bench of men: *fa-man yujādilu Allāha ʿanhum yawma al-qiyāmati* — "but who will plead with God on their behalf on the Day of Resurrection?": the interrogative *man* — who in its peculiar legal-torque its taunt-force: earthly advocacy is a transferable profession any bar of men hires or commands its orators — but the Final Court binds every man to his own arraignment to his own dossier (the Book's own counter-verdicts already lodged against future against every worldly bar: 2:123's "fear a day when no soul shall avail another aught"; 2:254's "before a day comes when there is neither bargaining, nor friendship, nor intercession"; 6:51's "they have, besides Him, neither protector nor intercessor"; 19:87's famous 19:87 — "none shall own intercession save he who hath taken a covenant with the Merciful") — the commentators then spelling v. 109's unique position: *advocacy before God is different not in degree but in kind* — no defense-table, no counsel, no pleading-as-profession before the All-Knowing (with the rank-of-God with its only remaining channel standing under covenant-law itself: *shafāʿah bi-idhn* — intercession under leave — 2:255's "who is it that intercedes with Him save by His leave?"; its classic 21:28's "they intercede not save for him with whom He is pleased"; and 20:109's "on that day intercession avails naught save for one to whom the Merciful grants leave, and whose word He approves" — intercession existing only as *God-commissioned speaking*, never as hired tongue) — with the cl the classical reflection from al-Qurṛṭubī and Ibn Kathīr: the Ṭuʿmah-clan canvassers discovered this on that very day on that very night — that all the lobbying-strength of a clan cannot carry a case beyond the world's lobby; and the injustice framed by night collapsed the moment revelation moved the moment heaven itself sat on the case.

**Aw Man Yakūnu ʿAlayhim Wakīlan: Who Will Be Their Trustee-Guardian?**

Then the climax-question pairing with its seal: *aw man yakūnu ʿalayhim wakīlan* — "or who will be for them a guardian-disposer": *wakīl* returning once again from the sūrah's own earlier from the sūrah's own perh the lexeme of vv. 81, 83 81 and 132's wakīl-seals — attorney-trustee by divine appointment — placed now in interrogative-taunt: who will arrange for them on that Day what their clan-arrangements arranged here with the commentators' standing reflection crossing over crossing to the standing principle of of 74:38 — "every soul is pledged for what it has earned" — and 19:95 — "and each of them shall come to Him on the Day of Resurrection, alone" — the whole eschatological infrastructure stripped of defense-teams (with the enduring al-Rāzī-like gloss final with the standing comment-canon's plainest sum-up, found in the tafsīr-houses ever since: earthly courts = persuasion-halls; the final Court = clarity-hall; in the hall of clarity the file is already written, already witnessed by hand and by hands, feet, skin (36:65 — "We seal their mouths today; their hands speak to Us, and their feet bear witness to what they earned"; 24:24's tongues-hands-feet testimony; 41:20-22's skin-witness) — so a wakīl-of-earth's art has no hone no task to perform) — and the sūrah's legal-canon thereby ending its advocacy-clinic with its own final signature: *pleading exists in this world's corridors of prestige, lineage, and eloquence; on the Day of Gathering only God's own advocacy exists — and only the trustworthy need a trustee* — hence back at v. 105's same sanctified charge: the believers' courts meanwhile should mimic the Final Hall as far as truth serves as far as flesh allows: no advocacy for betrayers, even under clan-tears — since the Last Judge will not be swayed either, and the duty of every earthly bench is to be the shadow of His.

## Sūrah al-Nisāʾ 4:149

> **If you do good openly or conceal it, or pardon an evil — God is ever Pardoning, Powerful.**

**Expanded Commentary**

**In Tubdū Khayran aw Tukhfūhu: Goodness Despotic Over Its Own Broadcast**

The speech-ethic of v. 148 — which forbade the open speaking of *evil* — now receives its counterpart governing the open display of *good*: *in tubdū khayran aw tukhfūhu* — "if you do good openly or conceal it": the verse's plain architecture (the standing note of the commentators from al-Zamakhsharī's register forward): where evil-speech is *licensed only by exception* (the wronged one's complaint), good-action is *free in both modes* — display and concealment equally lawful, the Book declining to criminalize the open good — for the open charity builds the social fabric it depicts (the standing hadith-balance: "secret charity extinguishes the Lord's anger" — al-Ṭabarānī's standing register — set beside the public charity that commands the standing blessing of 2:271: "if you make the alms public — excellent — and if you hide them and give them to the poor — that is better for you — and it shall expiate for you some of your sins" — concealment named *better*, display never named *sin*) — the hypocrisy-dossier above having made the true line unmistakable: the question is never the act's visibility but its *address* (v. 142's riyāʾ-file visibly in the background: laziness in solitude, display for the gallery — there the visibility was the offense because the address was the audience; here, where God is the address, visibility varies freely).

**Aw Taʿfū ʿan Sūʾin: Pardon as the Verse's Own Pinnacle**

Then the verse's own moral peak — and its connection to the exception-clause of the preceding verse is the commentators' standing insight: v. 148 had licensed the wronged to complain publicly; v. 149 immediately builds the higher road: *aw taʿfū ʿan sūʾ* — "or pardon an evil": *taʿfū* — from ʿ-f-w — to efface, to leave behind, to pardon so that the trace itself dissolves (the root's own image in the standing lexical notes: the wind effacing the footprints) — the pardon that *erases* rather than merely suspends. Al-Zamakhsharī's standing framing: having permitted the redress-seeker's outcry, the Book now points to the nobler option — ʿafw as the emulated divine attribute — the standing Book-file of pardon exalted: 5:13 — "pardon and overlook — do you not love that God should forgive you?" (its twin 24:22 — to the well-dowered believers regarding their poor kinsfolk — "let them pardon and overlook — do you not love that God should forgive you?" — the standing formula binding human pardon to hope of divine); 42:40 — "whoever pardons and makes amends — his wage falls upon God"; 42:43 — "whoever is patient and forgives — verily that is of the firmness of purpose"; 3:134 — "the restrainers of anger and the pardoners of men — and God loves the good-doers"; 7:199 — "take to pardon, and enjoin what is right, and turn from the ignorant"; 15:85; 45:14 — "tell those who believe to forgive those who hope not for the days of God"; 2:237; 64:14. The wronged soul of v. 148 thereby faces the sūrah's standing staircase of options: conceal (commended), complain (licensed), pardon (exalted) — the last rung introducing the two Names that explain why it is the highest.

**Fa-Inna Allāha Kāna ʿAfūwwan Qadīran: Pardon From Power, Not From Weakness**

The seal joins two Names that commentators (al-Qurṭubī leading the standing note) treat as a deliberate jurisprudence of pardon: *fa-inna Allāha kāna ʿafūwwan qadīran* — "God is ever Pardoning, Powerful": *ʿafūw* — the standing intensive of pardon — He whose erasure of sins is overwhelming, habitual, unceasing (its standing register: 4:43 above — "God is ever Pardoning, Forgiving"; 4:99 — "God is ever Pardoning, Forgiving"; 22:60; 58:2 — "God is ever Pardoning, Forgiving"; the Prophet's standing supplication-formula of the Ramadan nights — ʿĀʾishah's celebrated question: if I know the Night of Decree, what shall I "say: O God, Thou art Pardoning and lovest pardon — so pardon me" — Tirmidhī's standing register 3513); and *qadīr* — the All-Powerful — and the pairing's exact pedagogic point (al-Qurṭubī's standing note now a standing talisman of the whole ethic): God pardons *from power* — vengeance perfectly within His grasp, pardon perfectly within His choice — and this is the model pardon the verse teaches: **to forgo retaliation when retaliation is available** — pardon from a position of strength approximating the divine attribute; pardon from weakness, by contrast, being merely helplessness wearing a halo. The tradition's standing virtue-file confirms the measurement: the Prophet's celebrated testimony about the man who adds to his affairs nothing but increase — Muslim's standing register 2588: "charity does not decrease wealth; and God does not increase the servant who pardons except in might (ʿizzan); and no one humbles himself for God's sake but that God raises him" — the pardoners' ʿizzah — remember v. 139's hypocrites seeking might at false addresses — arriving by precisely the opposite road: not alliance-acquisition but injury-effacement. The hypocrisy-dossier's two closing verses (148–149) thereby make a single counsel: control your tongue about others' evil, open your hand in goodness, and where you have been wronged — climb: silence, complaint if needed, pardon if able — and the Names stand over the staircase as its guarantors: the Hearing-Knowing audit of v. 148, and here the Pardoning-Powerful — the God whose own pardon from unlimited power writes the standing template for every wronged believer standing over an offense with a writ already licensed and a hand that can rise above it.

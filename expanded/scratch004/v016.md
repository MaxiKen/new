## Sūrah al-Nisāʾ 4:16

> **And if two of those among you are guilty thereof, punish them both; but if they repent and make amends, then let them be. Truly God is Relenting, Merciful.**

**Expanded Commentary**

**Alladhāni: The Dual Pronoun and Its Reading**

Where v. 15 addressed the women with its plural feminine, v. 16 arrives with the dual masculine: *wa-lladhāni yaʾtiyānihā minkum fa-ādhūhumā* — "and the two among you who commit it — punish them both" — and the pronoun's gender has occupied every school of tafsīr. The great current of transmitted reading — al-Ṭabarī, al-Qurṭubī, Ibn Kathīr with them — resolves the masculine dual by the Arabic rule of *taghlīb*, the predominant gender's covering of the mixed pair: the verse takes the two of the act, male and female together, and appoints them one penalty in common, closing the asymmetry the female-singular of v. 15 might have allowed. A second reading binds the verses as distribution — v. 15 married adulteresses, v. 16 the unmarried fornicators, male and female both (a reading al-Qurṭubī traces to the Companions' generation) — and a third, preserved with the source-reporting it requires, takes the masculine dual *of males with males*, al-Rāzī and al-Qurṭubī noting the opinion with the doubt-marked distance the chains impose. The settled register of the schools cleaves to the first; and the load of the verse rides on the clause at its close, which does not vary between readings.

**Ādhūhumā: Harm Appointed, Then Softened to a Word**

The penalty-word is *ādhū* — the imperative of *adhā*, harm, injury — light in etymology, stern in law: *fa-ādhūhumā* — "hurt them both" — the harm the commentators discipline into its era: the *taʿzīr*, rebuke-and-scold, of the early dispensation, speech cutting where nothing yet cut flesh — al-Qurṭubī and Ibn Kathīr both naming the public tongue-lash and the assembly's rebuke the core of it, with some of the Salaf adding chastisement mild as an option beneath the fuller ḥadd then unrevealed. And it was, again, *temporary*: 24:2 cancelled it into the hundred lashes for the free fornicator of either sex, so that v. 16, like v. 15, stands as a station of the revealed pedagogy — the community's training in stages — al-Rāzī's perpetual lesson on the sequence: Islam's penal law was not flung whole upon a desert habit, but struck step upon step, each station real until the next arrived. What never lapses in the verse is its other half, which no abrogation has ever approached, since it is the chapter's unexpiring door.

**Tābā wa Aṣlaḥā: The Two Verbs of the Door**

*Fa-in tābā wa aṣlaḥā fa-aʿriḍū ʿanhumā* — "if the two repent and make amends, then turn from them": the pardon-clause appoints its two joint-credentials, *tawbah* the inward turn — remorse, resolve, abandonment — *iṣlāḥ* the outward amendment of life, and then binds the *community* itself: *aʿriḍū* — withdraw from them; let them be; re-open their names. The commentators dwell tenderly upon this: repentance in God's law does not only erase the higher register; it commands society's register to close, the tongue to stop, the file to be boxed. Ibn Masʿūd's oft-quoted maxim at this clause, cited by Ibn Kathīr and the commentators after him — *"one who repents from a sin is as one who never sinned"* — stands inseparable from a hadith precise to the scandal-management of the verse — the Prophet's reported word when the case of the confessed woman al-Ghāmidiyyah reached its bloody end and a man reviled her after death:

> *"Had you reviled her? She has repented a repentance that, were it divided among seventy of the people of Madinah, would suffice them all. Have you found better than that she gave her soul to God Almighty?"* (Muslim 1695, from Buraydah)

**Tawwāb Raḥīm: The Seal That Names the Habit of God**

The verse closes with the Name-pair the sūrah's repentance-files will claim as their own: *inna Allāha kāna tawwāban raḥīman* — "truly God is ever Relenting, Merciful": *tawwāb*, the intensive-pattern of turning-back — the One whose habit is to turn again and again toward who turns — matched against man's single turn; *raḥīm*, the Merciful-in-effect, the mercy not merely owned but *run down* upon the forgiven like the rain that runs down walls. The attributes' placement in this exact verse is medicine measured: between v. 15's dreadful process and v. 16's rebuke-office, sinners might despair of return; the seal informs the fearful that the court of return belongs not to the harsh but to *the Relenting-Merciful* — that Abd Allāh ibn Masʿūd's fellow, asked why God bothered to command the repenting sinner new works, answered in the old Talmud-spirit of the Madinan schools: *because only preachers of despair call repentance enough without a changed life.* Reversed lives, sealed names, pardoned storyboards — these are the wares of Tawwāb-Raḥīm; and the sūrah's repentance-arch will now open fully at vv. 17–18, whose clauses fix exactly *who* may pass this door, and *how late* is too late — the twin questions of every sinning reader since the morning the verses were spoken.

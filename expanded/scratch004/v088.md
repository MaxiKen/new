## Sūrah al-Nisāʾ 4:88

> **What is with you that you are [divided into] two groups concerning the hypocrites, when God Himself has cast them back for that which they have earned? Do you seek to guide those whom God has led astray? Whomsoever God leads astray, thou wilt not find a way for him.**

**Expanded Commentary**

**Fa-Mā Lakum fī al-Munāfiqīna Fi'atayn: The Believers' Debated Hypocrite-File**

The sūrah's governance chamber opens its tightest hypocrite-court with a courtroom question: *fa-mā lakum fī al-munāfiqīna fi'atayn* — "what is with you — that you are divided into two parties concerning the hypocrites?": *fi'atayn* — two factions (the Book choosing the body-of-men word deliberately, to emphasize the *dual-party* split inside Madinah itself — the believers' community dividing *within its own walls* over how to judge the half-committed: the Sīrah-background the commentators preserve in their asbāb-roll (al-Ṭabarī's collation; al-Wāḥidī; Ibn Kathīr's summary) tells of a band of professing Muslims — said variously to be certain Medinans who set out with the Prophet for Uḥud but then withdrew back to Madinah (the same band indicted at 3:167 with their hollow excuse — *"had we known there would be fighting, we would have followed you"* — the words of men who weigh devotion on odds) — or, on the more common asbāb-line, a band of Makkans who had claimed Islam, came to Madinah, then departed under pleas and excuses and settled again among the idolaters — leaving behind them the question of their final disposition) — and the Companions themselves divided between two camps: one party calling for stern judgment upon the deserters; another urging that they be left alone in patience — whereupon heaven intervened in the debate itself. The verse's stinging diagnostic: *your division into two parties about them is already a mark upon you* — the question of blatant treachery had drifted from clear covenant-assessment into *partisan camps arguing over hypocrites* — a sign of how deeply the fence-sitting contagion can work inward through a community's gates, not by changing beliefs but by *paralyzing verdicts*. With al-al-Qurṭubī's and al-Rāzī's master-point: the question rebukes not the *discussion* but the *two-grouping itself* — judgment upon daylight-manifest treachery should require no faction (the covenant's criteria are already drawn: profess, settle, answer the call, and leave not — and the man who oscillates between the signs is judged by them) — the believers' duty being *verification*, followed by a single verdict — never factional stasis over the manifest.

**Wa Allāhu Arkasahum bimā Kasabū: The Casting-Back as Divine Verdict**

Then the theological center of the verse: *wa Allāhu arkasahum bimā kasabū* — "God Himself has cast them back for that which they have earned": *arkasahum*, from r-k-s — to throw backward, to invert, to cause to return reversed (its Book-kin: the same verb haunting 4:91's own alert three verses below, and used throughout the Revelation only in judgment-settings) — the hypocrites *cast backward by God into the unbelief they had secretly carried all along* — divine inversion as settled judicial sentence. And the causal fire-doctrine the commentators crowd upon the clause: *bimā kasabū* — *for what they acquired* — the inverted status being *earned fruit*, not arbitrary stroke (the Book backing the formula with its standing philosophy of moral harvest: 45:22 — "God created the heavens and the earth in truth, and so that every soul may be recompensed for that which it has earned — and they shall not be wronged"; 16:111 — "the day every soul comes pleading for itself, and every soul is paid in full for what it wrought — and they shall not be wronged") — so heaven's repudiation here is not caprice but *accounting completed*: their profession of faith was already unbelief inside; God merely finalized the verdict which their own works authored — al-Ṭabarī's classical summary: what they were outwardly, they had never believed within; He returned them to the truth of their own inner being, the external sign adjusted at last to the internal fact. Thereby the question answers the two-party debate from above: do not squabble over their state as though it belonged to your competence — God's verdict preceded your councils.

**A-Turīdūna an Tahdū man Aḍalla Allāh: Guidance Denied to the Reverse-Sentenced**

Then the deepest question-clause, closing the file: *a-turīdūna an tahdū man aḍalla Allāh* — "do you seek to guide those whom God has led astray?": the rhetorical impossible — seeking to cancel a judgment the Judge has already handed down (the commentators' settling doctrine — the Ashʿarī school laying its heaviest weight exactly here: *iḍlāl* — leading astray — in the Divine mouth being ultimate, and unanswerable by any creature's hope; with the countering kalām-line at their side — iḍlāl as *sealing-upon-earning* — pairing astrayness with voluntary wrong-work: cf. 28:50 — "who is more astray than he who follows his own whims without guidance from God? — indeed God guides not the wrongdoing folk"; 14:27 — "God leads astray the wrongdoers — and God does what He wills" — the seal enclosing a subject-cohort already defined by its deeds). And the verse's own seal delivers the Book's most quotable build-axiom: *wa man yuḍlili Allāhu fa-lan tajida lahu sabīlā* — "whomsoever God leads astray — thou wilt find for him no way": *sabīl* spelled out as *way-of-access* — road, exit, rescue-route — men being told flatly: God's astray-judgment leaves no road by which *you* can re-guide them — so cease organizing your ummah around their rescue (with its standing twin-seals in the Book — 18:17: "whom God guides is rightly guided; and whom He leads astray, thou wilt find for him no guiding protector"; 7:186: "whom God leads astray has no guide — and He leaves them in their insolence wandering blindly"). And the sūrah's master-precision for the believers then emerges: *the guidance-debate concerning fence-sitters is not yours to fix:* judge actions openly, follow only where God's Word rules, keep covenant-honor with those who stand clear — and never let the half-committed's temperature set Madinah's climate. Verse 88 thus delivers all three arms of the hypocrite-file's governance: the warning against two-party stasis; the verdict-exposition (God inverted them for what they earned); and the pastoral release (leave the astray to their astrayness — you cannot find them a way) — the believers' community emerging *purified by release*: free at last from the fatal burden of trying to carry the half-hearted as if half-heartedness were a faith-and-a-half.

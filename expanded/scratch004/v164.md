## Sūrah al-Nisāʾ 4:164

> **And messengers We have recounted to thee before, and messengers We have not recounted to thee; and unto Moses God spoke directly —**

**Expanded Commentary**

**Wa Rusulan Qad Qaṣaṣnāhum ʿAlayka min Qablu: The Recounted and the Unrecounted**

The roll of v. 163 opens here upon its own horizon — the Book admitting, with deliberate instruction, that it has not registered the census of prophecy: *wa rusulan qad qaṣaṣnāhum ʿalayka min qablu wa rusulan lam naqṣuṣhum ʿalayk* — "and messengers We have recounted to thee before, and messengers We have not recounted to thee": *qaṣaṣ* — from q-ṣ-ṣ — narration, the telling of accounts (its standing register: 11:100 — "those are the tidings of the towns We recount to thee — of them some are standing, some harvested"; 11:120 — "all that We recount to thee of the stories of the messengers is that wherewith We make firm thy heart"; 12:3; 40:78 — the standing twin-verse: "We sent messengers before thee — among them We recounted to thee, and among them We recounted not to thee") — the twice-stated division (here and at 40:78 — the only two places the Book confesses its own editorial selectivity concerning prophets) forming one of the standing credal-side doors of the religion: the Book names some twenty-six prophets in its pages, yet declares explicitly that the complete roster lies beyond its telling — the tradition's standing figures for it (the reports in the musnad-collections and tafsīr-files naming totals vast beyond enumeration — 124,000 in the standing report, three hundred and fifteen messengers among them, the register in Aḥmad's Musnad through Abū Dharr's question-hadith) — the theological point of the commentators being structural: prophethood is *universal* — God's guidance never geographically or ethnically parochial (10:47 — "for every community a messenger"; 16:36 — "We raised in every nation a messenger: worship God and shun the false gods"; 35:24 — "there is no community but a warner has passed among them"; 14:4 — "We sent no messenger save with the tongue of his people, that he might make things clear to them") — and the unrecounted category preserves the standing humility of revelation-history: what God's Book recounts is didactic selection, not exhaustive registry — the door left open, as the standing comment-canon notes, for sacred figures beyond the addressed horizon to be recognized as among the unrecounted warners of their peoples — without the Book's silence being read as nonexistence, and without speculation being enthroned as certain knowledge: "we know them not — Thou art the knower of the Unseen" — the standing formula by which the commentators pass the unrecounted to their Recorder.

**Wa Kallama Allāhu Mūsā Taklīman: Kalīm Allāh — The Spoken-To**

The verse then plants, at the end of its cadence, one of the Book's most resonant distinctions: *wa kallama Allāhu Mūsā taklīman* — "and unto Moses God spoke directly": *kallama* — Form II of k-l-m — to address in speech — with the cognate-accusative *taklīman* — "a speaking" — the standing intensifier that seals the reality of the event: not allusion, not metaphor, not impression — *speech*: God speaking, Moses hearing — the very phrase from which the tradition coined Moses' everlasting honorific: *Kalīm Allāh* — the One Spoken-To by God — the title entering devotional Islam through this verse and its standing companions (the commentators cross-reading with 2:253's standing gradation: "among them one to whom God spoke — and He raised some of them in degrees" — read in the standing gloss of the majority as pointing precisely to Moses among other distinctions; 7:143-144 — "and when Moses came to Our appointed time and his Lord spoke to him..." — "O Moses — I have chosen thee above men through My messages and through My speech — so take what I have given thee"; 19:52 — "We called him from the right side of the Mount and brought him near in communion"; 20:12-14 — the valley of Ṭuwā: "verily I am thy Lord — so remove thy sandals"; 26:52; 28:30's burning-bush file; 42:51's standing taxonomy: "it is not for any man that God should speak to him, save by revelation, or from behind a veil, or by sending a messenger to reveal by His leave what He wills" — Moses' direct-address placed within, and at the summit of, this threefold classification of divine speech-to-creatures).

**The Honor-Architecture of the Register: Titles in the Standing Tradition**

The commentators (al-Rāzī here with his standing register of distinctions) gather the verse's placement within the Book's honor-architecture — noting how the People-of-the-Book movement of the sūrah arranges its laurels: v. 125's standing Abraham-file just delivered ("and God took Abraham for an intimate friend — khalīlan" — Khalīl Allāh); v. 158-159's Jesus-file just past (raised unto God; Rūḥ Allāh as at v. 171 below — "a Spirit from Him" — and His Word committed to Mary); v. 163's David-station named with the Psalter; and here Moses' kalām-station — each prophet's own standing title drawn from what this very Book confers: the tradition's standing honorifics — Kalīm (Moses), Khalīl (Abraham), Rūḥ (Jesus) — all minted in Qur'ānic vocabulary — the subtle standing apologetic the commentators observe: the Book which the People of the Book are urged by later verses not to *exaggerate* (lā taghlū — v. 171) nor to *attenuate* (v. 150's distinction-making) is itself the deepest grantor of honor to their own prophets — honoring Moses above every Israelite honor-file by naming his distinction from the tongue of God Himself, and honoring Jesus by the loftiest vocabulary while refusing his deification — the balance of the standing balance encapsulated in the sūrah's own ratio: "those are the messengers — We preferred some over others" (2:253) — preference acknowledged, divinity refused, blanket-equality avoided, jealousy silenced.

**Standing Application: The Prophet's Own Reception of This Register**

And the register's standing pastoral note — gathered by the commentators — turns to the recipient addressed in the verse's first person: the Prophet himself, being told *his own* story lies inside this chain ("We have recounted to thee..."), is thereby instructed in the standing posture of all messengers before the unseen ledger of prophethood: there were — and are — worlds of God's envoys whose stories even *he* was not told; the creed stands upon *generic assent* to all (ʾāmantu bi-rusulihi — the standing creed-phrase), while knowledge-by-name remains a grace of selection — the same principle the verse lays upon the community: faith's completeness lives in the undivided assent (4:150-152's standing triptych), not in the completeness of the catalog. The verse thus performs, in one breath, registry and humility: the named roll of v. 163 proved the continuity of the cable; the unrecounted register of v. 164 proves the modesty of the cable's archive; and the kalām-distinction of Moses proves the cable's conductivity at its most direct — God, in prior covenant-history, having *spoken* — and if with Moses He spoke, and with David He psalmed, and with Jesus He spirited-and-worded, then the newest address of the same Speaker asks of its hearers no new category of belief — only the standing act: "we hear, and we obey" (2:285) — the standing response that the chain of messengers, recounted and unrecounted alike, was sent to secure.

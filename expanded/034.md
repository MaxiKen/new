# Sūrah Saba' (Chapter 34) — Expanded Verse-by-Verse Commentary

## Introduction to the Sūrah

**Expanded Commentary**

**A Makkan Sūrah Named for a Fallen Civilization**

Sūrah Saba', "Sheba," is the thirty-fourth chapter of the Qur'an, comprising fifty-four verses revealed for the most part in Makkah, before the Hijrah — though some authorities, as noted by al-Qurṭubī and others, considered verse 6 to belong to the Madinan period. It takes its name from the people of Saba' in Yemen, whose story occupies verses 15–19: a civilization granted two gardens, security, and prosperity, which then turned away in ingratitude until its dam burst and its gardens turned bitter. The name itself is therefore a warning. Every time the believer pronounces "Saba'" he remembers that no prosperity is permanent without gratitude, and no civilization is immune to moral collapse.

The sūrah follows Sūrah al-Aḥzāb, which closed with the theme of the Trust (al-amānah) offered to the heavens, the earth, and the mountains, and carried — with all its weight — by man. Classical commentators such as al-Biqāʿī observed the fitness of this sequence: Saba' opens with praise of God, affirming that He is wholly independent of those who carry the Trust and those who betray it. The bearers of trust benefit themselves; the betrayers harm themselves. God loses nothing and gains nothing, for all praise already belongs to Him in this world and the next.

**The Architecture of the Chapter**

The sūrah moves with a clear, purposeful order. After two opening verses establishing God's praise, ownership, knowledge, mercy, and forgiveness, it turns to its central Makkan theme: the reality of the Hour and the folly of those who deny it (vv. 3–9). Then, as if answering the deniers' claim that power and permanence belong to wealth and denial, it presents two models of true power: the prophets David and Solomon, to whom God granted kingdoms, knowledge, and command over nature itself — yet who remained grateful servants (vv. 10–14). Against these grateful kings it sets the ungrateful kingdom of Saba', destroyed by a flood (vv. 15–19), followed by a brief but profound passage on Iblīs and the limits of his authority (vv. 20–21).

The second half of the sūrah is largely dialectical: a series of challenges the Prophet ﷺ is commanded to pose to the idolaters concerning their false gods, intercession, provision, and responsibility (vv. 22–30); a dramatic scene of the mutual recriminations between misleaders and misled on the Day of Judgment (vv. 31–33); a critique of the delusion that wealth and children prove divine favor (vv. 34–45); a clarification of the Prophet's own mission — sincere, unrewarded, truth-casting, guided by revelation (vv. 46–50); and a closing scene of terror, belated belief, and the barrier between the damned and their desires (vv. 51–54).

**Why This Sūrah Matters Now**

Modern readers live, like Saba', amid gardens: material abundance, engineered security, and connective infrastructure unprecedented in history. They also live, like the Makkan deniers, amid confident denials of the Hereafter dressed in new vocabularies. Saba' speaks to both conditions. It teaches that knowledge is the opposite of mockery; that gratitude is the opposite of arrogance; that leadership is a trust, not a license; that wealth is a test, not a verdict; and that truth, once it comes, exposes falsehood as something that "originates naught, nor restores." What follows treats each of the fifty-four verses as an independent discourse, with attention to Arabic language, cross-references, Hadith, classical scholarship, and living application.

## Sūrah Saba' 34:1

> **Praise be to God, unto Whom belongs whatsoever is in the heavens and whatsoever is on the earth. His is the praise in the Hereafter, and He is the Wise, the Aware.**

**Expanded Commentary**

**One of Five Openings with Praise**

This sūrah is one of five in the Qur'an that open with *al-ḥamdu lillāh* — "Praise be to God" — alongside al-Fātiḥah (1:2), al-Anʿām (6:1), al-Kahf (18:1), and Fāṭir (35:1). Each occurrence carries its own emphasis. Here the praise is immediately joined to universal ownership and to praise "in the Hereafter," framing the entire chapter: before any argument about resurrection, prophethood, or idols begins, the reader is placed in the posture of praise. Ibn Kathīr notes that praise here is both information and instruction — God informs us that praise belongs to Him, and thereby teaches us to praise Him. The word *ḥamd* itself, from the root *ḥ-m-d*, differs from mere thanks (*shukr*): thanks responds to a favor received, while praise celebrates perfection itself, whether or not one has personally benefited. God is praised not only because He gives, but because He is perfect.

**Ownership That Excludes All Partnership**

*Unto Whom belongs whatsoever is in the heavens and whatsoever is on the earth* — the Arabic *lahu mā fī al-samāwāti wa mā fī al-arḍ* — is among the most repeated sentences in the Qur'an:

> *"To God belongs whatsoever is in the heavens and whatsoever is on the earth."* (Qur'an 2:284)

> *"And to God belongs the dominion of the heavens and the earth, and God is Powerful over all things."* (Qur'an 3:189)

Al-Ṭabarī explains the point plainly: everything other than God is His possession and His creation; none of it owns itself, and none of it can therefore be His partner. This single clause quietly demolishes the whole logic of idolatry that the sūrah will later attack in verses 22 and 27. If everything belongs to Him, then those whom the idolaters call upon "have not a mote's weight of power" — they are themselves owned. Psychologically, the sentence also reframes human ownership: houses, wealth, and even children are trusts within a larger ownership, not absolute possessions. The believer who internalizes this is cured of both arrogance in abundance and despair in loss.

**Praise in the Hereafter: A Distinctive Claim**

*His is the praise in the Hereafter* — *wa lahu al-ḥamdu fī al-ākhirati* — is the distinctive note of this verse. Elsewhere the Qur'an says His is the praise "in the heavens and on the earth" (30:18) and "at the beginning and at the end" (28:70). Taken together, the meaning is that God is praised in every place and at every time. But why single out the Hereafter here? Al-Rāzī and others explain: in this world, praise of God is mixed with the praise of others — men praise kings, wealth, and themselves — and the praise of the heedless is absent altogether. In the Hereafter, veils are lifted; even those who denied Him will acknowledge the truth, and the dwellers of Paradise will utter praise spontaneously:

> *"And the last of their call is: Praise be to God, Lord of the worlds."* (Qur'an 10:10)

> *"And they will say: Praise be to God, Who has removed sorrow from us."* (Qur'an 35:34)

The verse thus anticipates the sūrah's central dispute about the Hour: the deniers refuse praise now, but praise in the Hereafter is already His — willingly from the saved, and by compelled acknowledgment from the damned.

**The Wise, the Aware: Names That Govern the Chapter**

The verse closes with two Divine Names: *al-Ḥakīm* (the Wise) and *al-Khabīr* (the Aware). *Ḥakīm* comes from *ḥukm*, judgment and wisdom — He who places everything in its exact place. *Khabīr* comes from *khibrah*, intimate inward knowledge — He who knows things from within, not merely from without. Ibn Taymiyyah emphasized that these two Names together answer two perennial objections: why does God decree suffering and resurrection (answered by Wisdom), and does God truly know hidden deeds and motives (answered by Awareness)? Every subsequent theme of the sūrah — the recording of motes, the recompense of deeds, the testing through provision — flows from these two attributes. Nothing is random, and nothing is hidden.

## Sūrah Saba' 34:2

> **He knows that which enters the earth and that which issues therefrom, that which descends from Heaven and that which ascends thereto. And He is the Merciful, the Forgiving.**

**Expanded Commentary**

**Four Movements That Encompass All Existence**

This verse draws a complete map of motion in creation: two earthly movements (entering and issuing) and two heavenly movements (descending and ascending). The classical commentators, drawing on Qatādah, Mujāhid, and others, filled in each category. *That which enters the earth* includes rain that soaks the soil, seeds planted by farmers, corpses buried in graves, and treasures hidden away. *That which issues therefrom* includes vegetation, springs, minerals, and — on the Last Day — the resurrected dead. *That which descends from Heaven* includes rain, angels, decrees (*maqādīr*), blessings, and revelation itself, including the Qur'an. *That which ascends thereto* includes the angels returning, righteous deeds and "the good word" rising, and the souls of the dead ascending. As al-Rāzī observed, these four phrases together allude to all phases of existence: burial and growth, sending down and return. Nothing moves in any direction without His knowledge.

The Qur'an elsewhere confirms each movement:

> *"To Him ascends the good word, and righteous work raises it."* (Qur'an 35:10)

> *"He knows what penetrates the earth and what issues from it, what descends from Heaven and what ascends thereto."* (Qur'an 57:4)

**A Quiet Proof of Resurrection**

Placed between verse 1 (praise and wisdom) and verse 3 (the denial of the Hour), this verse functions as a silent argument. The deniers will say resurrection is impossible; God first reminds them of the cycle they witness daily: seeds enter the earth and plants issue forth; rain descends and vapors and deeds ascend. He who manages this ceaseless circulation can surely manage the final return. Ibn Kathīr draws the connection explicitly: the One who knows every seed and every soul is fully able to resurrect and reckon. Modern readers may add their own illustrations — the water cycle, the carbon cycle, the burial and sprouting of every harvest — each is a small resurrection rehearsing the great one.

**The Merciful, the Forgiving: Why These Names Here**

After a verse of all-encompassing knowledge — a knowledge that might terrify, since it includes every buried sin — God closes with *al-Raḥīm* (the Merciful) and *al-Ghafūr* (the Forgiving). The pairing is deliberate and tender. Al-Rāzī suggested: He is the Merciful in what He sends down to the earth — rain, provision, revelation — and the Forgiving in allowing souls to return and ascend to Him despite their sins. Knowledge without mercy would be despair; here knowledge is wrapped in mercy. The Prophet ﷺ expressed the same pairing when he said:

> *"God is more merciful to His servants than a mother to her child."* (al-Bukhārī 5999; Muslim 2754)

The verse thus teaches the believer to hold two truths together: nothing escapes Him, and no sincere return is refused by Him.

## Sūrah Saba' 34:3

> **Those who disbelieve say, "The Hour will never come upon us." Say, "Nay! By my Lord, by the Knower of the Unseen, it will surely come unto you." Not a mote's weight evades Him in the heavens or on the earth, nor smaller than that, nor larger, but that it is in a clear Book.**

**Expanded Commentary**

**The Central Denial of Makkah**

*Those who disbelieve say, "The Hour will never come upon us"* — this denial of the Last Hour was the characteristic doctrine of the Makkan opposition, repeated in many forms:

> *"And they say: There is naught but our life in this world; we die and we live, and naught destroys us but time."* (Qur'an 45:24)

> *"What! When we are bones and dust, shall we indeed be resurrected as a new creation?"* (Qur'an 17:49)

Ibn Kathīr explains that this denial was not merely an opinion about the future; it was a moral choice. To deny the Hour is to deny accountability, and to deny accountability is to license the present. The verse uses the emphatic form *lā ta'tīnā al-sāʿah* — "the Hour will never come to us" — with the particle of absolute negation, showing their certainty in denial. Against this false certainty, God commands His Prophet to swear a true certainty.

**An Oath Sworn by the Knower of the Unseen**

*Say: Nay! By my Lord* — the command to swear is striking. The Prophet ﷺ is told to take an oath by God that the Hour will come. The oath is intensified by the attribute *ʿālim al-ghayb* — "Knower of the Unseen." Al-Ṭabarī notes the fitness: those who deny the Unseen are answered by the One who knows the Unseen. The particle rendered "Nay" (*balā*) is among the most emphatic affirmatives in Arabic; it overturns a preceding negation with certainty. The verse then piles emphasis upon emphasis in the Arabic — *la-ta'tiyannakum* — "it shall most surely come to you," with both the oath and the doubled energetic form. The Qur'an swears to the resurrection in only a few places, each time with maximum force:

> *"And those who disbelieve claim they shall not be resurrected. Say: Yea! By my Lord, you shall surely be resurrected."* (Qur'an 64:7)

**Not a Mote's Weight Evades Him**

*Not a mote's weight evades Him* — *lā yaʿzubu ʿanhu mithqālu dharratin* — introduces one of the Qur'an's most precise images of divine knowledge. The *dharrah* is the smallest conceivable thing: a speck of dust, an ant, an atom. The verse insists that neither this mote, nor anything smaller, nor anything larger, escapes Him — and all of it is *in a clear Book* (*fī kitābin mubīn*), the Preserved Tablet (*al-lawḥ al-maḥfūẓ*) in which all things are inscribed, as well as the records of deeds handed out on Judgment Day. The same truth appears in:

> *"And not a mote's weight in the earth or in Heaven escapes thy Lord, nor smaller than that nor larger, but that it is in a clear Book."* (Qur'an 10:61)

> *"So whosoever does a mote's weight of good shall see it, and whosoever does a mote's weight of evil shall see it."* (Qur'an 99:7–8)

Al-Qurṭubī draws the moral: if no particle escapes His knowledge, then no deed — however secret — escapes His reckoning. The modern analogy of total recording — every keystroke logged, every transaction traced — is only a dim shadow of a Book that records intentions as well as acts. For the believer this is comfort (no good is lost); for the defiant, it is warning (no evil is lost either).

## Sūrah Saba' 34:4

> **That He may recompense those who believe and perform righteous deeds; theirs shall be forgiveness and a generous provision.**

**Expanded Commentary**

**The Purpose of the Hour: Recompense**

This verse begins with *li-yajziya* — "that He may recompense" — answering the question: why must the Hour come? The answer is moral necessity. Without a Day of Recompense, faith and defiance would end in the same dust, and justice would be an unkept promise. The Qur'an states the principle plainly elsewhere:

> *"Or shall We make those who believe and perform righteous deeds as those who work corruption on the earth? Or shall We make the reverent as the wicked?"* (Qur'an 38:28)

Al-Rāzī observed that this verse divides the recompense with precision: *forgiveness* (*maghfirah*) for the deficiencies in faith and the sins that stained it, and *a generous provision* (*rizq karīm*) for righteous deeds — a provision described as *karīm* (noble, generous) because it is abundant, pure, continuous, and given with honor, unlike worldly provision, which is mixed with toil and anxiety.

**Faith Joined to Righteous Deeds**

*Those who believe and perform righteous deeds* — *alladhīna āmanū wa ʿamilū al-ṣāliḥāt* — is the Qur'an's most frequent description of the saved, occurring scores of times. The pairing is never accidental. Faith without deeds is a claim without evidence; deeds without faith are a body without a soul. Ibn Taymiyyah stressed that in Qur'anic usage, *īmān* itself includes deeds, but the Qur'an mentions deeds explicitly to remove any excuse for the one who claims belief while abandoning action. The Prophet ﷺ defined the relationship when he said:

> *"Faith has seventy-odd branches, the highest of which is the testimony that there is no god but God, and the lowest of which is removing harm from the road."* (Muslim 35)

**Forgiveness First, Then Generosity**

Notice the order: forgiveness before provision. The believer's first need is not reward but pardon, for no record is clean. Only a forgiven soul can enjoy Paradise purely; otherwise even bliss would be shadowed by shame. Then comes *rizq karīm* — interpreted by Ibn ʿAbbās and others as Paradise itself, with its food, drink, and dwellings that never perish. The same pairing appears in:

> *"Those who believe and perform righteous deeds, for them is forgiveness and a great reward."* (Qur'an 35:7)

Practically, the verse orders the believer's own priorities: seek forgiveness constantly, do good steadily, and trust the generosity of the Recompenser rather than calculating the wage of each deed.

## Sūrah Saba' 34:5

> **But those who endeavor to thwart Our signs, for them there shall be a painful punishment of torment.**

**Expanded Commentary**

**Striving Against the Signs**

*Those who endeavor to thwart Our signs* — *wa alladhīna saʿaw fī āyātinā muʿājizīn* — describes not passive disbelief but active opposition. The verb *saʿā* means to strive, hasten, exert oneself; the word *muʿājizīn* comes from a root meaning to render helpless or to outstrip — the opposition imagines it can outrun God or frustrate His signs, whether by mockery, argument, persecution, or distortion. Al-Ṭabarī explains: they work to turn people away from God's verses and to nullify them. The same group is described elsewhere as:

> *"And those who endeavor to thwart Our signs, those are the inhabitants of Hellfire."* (Qur'an 22:51)

The word *āyāt* (signs) here is comprehensive: revealed verses, prophetic miracles, and the signs in creation. To strive against any of them — by ridiculing revelation, by suppressing its conveyers, or by insisting that nature points to nothing — is to join this category.

**A Painful Punishment of Torment**

*For them there shall be a painful punishment of torment* — *lahum ʿadhābun min rijzin alīm*. The word *rijz* is among the Qur'an's severest terms for punishment; it can mean torment, defilement, or plague, and in some readings the phrase is rendered "the torment of a painful punishment." Al-Qurṭubī notes that *rijz* combines the ideas of pain and disgrace — a punishment that both hurts and humiliates, corresponding to a crime that both denied truth and degraded it. The Qur'an uses the same word for the plagues sent upon Pharaoh's people:

> *"So We sent upon them the flood, the locusts, the lice, the frogs, and blood — signs set forth in detail."* (Qur'an 7:133, where the punishment is called *rijz* in surrounding verses)

The contrast with verse 4 is exact: forgiveness and generous provision for believers; painful torment for active opponents. The symmetry teaches that the Hereafter does not confuse categories — each striving finds its fitting recompense.

**The Logic of Severity**

Why so severe? Because the crime is not private doubt but public obstruction — placing barriers between others and guidance. The Prophet ﷺ said:

> *"Whoever calls to guidance has the reward of all who follow him without diminishing their reward, and whoever calls to misguidance bears the sin of all who follow him without diminishing their sins."* (Muslim 2674)

Modern parallels are not hard to find: organized campaigns to mock faith, to normalize corruption, or to silence conscience. The verse does not condemn sincere questioning; it condemns the industrious manufacturing of unbelief.

## Sūrah Saba' 34:6

> **Those who have been given knowledge see that what is sent down upon thee from thy Lord is the truth and that it guides to the path of the Mighty, the Praised.**

**Expanded Commentary**

**Knowledge That Recognizes Truth**

Against the strivers of verse 5 the Qur'an sets *those who have been given knowledge* — *alladhīna ūtū al-ʿilm*. Who are they? Al-Ṭabarī reports views identifying them as the Companions of the Prophet ﷺ or as learned among the People of the Book who recognized the Qur'an, in line with:

> *"Those who were given knowledge before it — when it is recited unto them, they fall down upon their faces, prostrating."* (Qur'an 17:107)

But as al-Rāzī argues, the most comprehensive reading includes everyone who possesses authentic knowledge, for genuine knowledge leads to truth. The verb is *see* (*yara*) — not merely "believe" or "think." True knowledge produces a kind of sight: the knower perceives the Qur'an's truthfulness the way a jeweler perceives a genuine gem. The Qur'an elsewhere links knowledge and faith in sequence:

> *"And God will raise in degrees those among you who believe and those who have been given knowledge."* (Qur'an 58:11)

**What Was Sent Down Is the Truth**

*That what is sent down upon thee from thy Lord is the truth* — *annahu al-ḥaqq* — with the definite article: *the* truth, not merely *a* truth among others. The learned recognize three things at once: the Qur'an's origin (*from thy Lord*), its nature (*the truth*), and its function (*it guides to the path*). Ibn Kathīr notes the contrast: the ignorant see the Qur'an as poetry, sorcery, or fabrication (as verses 7–8 will show); the knowledgeable see it for what it is. The criterion is not cleverness but receptivity — the same sun hardens clay and melts wax.

**The Path of the Mighty, the Praised**

*It guides to the path of the Mighty, the Praised* — *ilā ṣirāṭ al-ʿAzīz al-Ḥamīd*. These two Names are carefully chosen. *Al-ʿAzīz* (the Mighty) is He who cannot be resisted or frustrated — answering those who "endeavor to thwart" His signs. *Al-Ḥamīd* (the Praised) is He who is praiseworthy in Himself and praised by all creation — echoing the sūrah's opening verse. The path is described with the same word as in the Fātiḥah (*ṣirāṭ*), the straight, broad road. Al-Qurṭubī observes that the mighty are often feared but not loved, and the praised are often loved but not feared; God alone is both irresistibly Powerful and infinitely Praiseworthy, so His path inspires both awe and love. The Prophet ﷺ captured the Qur'an's guiding function when he said:

> *"I have left among you that which, if you hold fast to it, you shall never go astray: the Book of God and my Sunnah."* (al-Ḥākim, al-Mustadrak 1/93; Mālik, Muwaṭṭa' 1594, with authentic meaning)

## Sūrah Saba' 34:7

> **And those who disbelieve say, "Shall we show you a man who will inform you that, when you have been completely torn to pieces, you shall be in a new creation?"**

**Expanded Commentary**

**Mockery as an Argument**

Having described the people of knowledge, the sūrah returns to the deniers — this time quoting their mockery verbatim. *Shall we show you a man* — *hal nadullukum ʿalā rajul* — is spoken in the tone of a sideshow barker: "Come see the oddity!" They refuse even to name the Prophet ﷺ, reducing him to "a man" (*rajul*), as if to say he is nobody special. This rhetorical strategy — mockery in place of refutation — is ancient and perennial. The Qur'an records its many variants:

> *"What! When we are bones and dust, shall we indeed be resurrected as a new creation?"* (Qur'an 17:49)

> *"Does he promise you that when you die and become dust and bones, you shall be brought forth?"* (Qur'an 23:35)

Al-Rāzī notes that mockery is the argument of those who have no argument. Unable to refute resurrection by reason — since the One who created first can create again — they resort to tone: incredulity performed as sophistication.

**Torn to Pieces: The Deniers' Own Description**

*When you have been completely torn to pieces* — *idhā muzziqtum kulla mumazzaq* — uses one of the most emphatic constructions in Arabic: the verb and its verbal noun together (*torn a complete tearing*), meaning utter disintegration. Ironically, the deniers describe decomposition accurately; their error lies not in the premise but in the conclusion. They assume disintegration is irreversible. The Qur'an answers this assumption directly elsewhere:

> *"Say: He Who originated them the first time shall give them life, and He is Knower of all creation."* (Qur'an 36:79)

> *"Does man think We shall not gather his bones? Yea! We are able to restore his very fingertips."* (Qur'an 75:3–4)

Ibn Taymiyyah argued that the resurrection is more rational than its denial: the same God who assembles a human being from a drop, through stages of utter transformation, can reassemble him from dust. The modern reader, who watches deleted data restored from fragments and demolished buildings reconstructed from plans, has even less excuse for the old incredulity.

**A New Creation: What the Phrase Means**

*You shall be in a new creation* — *innakum la-fī khalqin jadīd* — is, from the deniers' mouths, a sneer; from the Qur'an's perspective, it is the truth. *Khalq jadīd* (a new creation) refers primarily to the bodily resurrection. Some Sufi and philosophical commentators, as al-Rāzī notes, also read in it the continuous renewal (*tajdīd*) of creation at every instant — the world perpetually re-created by divine act. Both readings reinforce each other: the God who renews creation moment by moment can surely renew it finally. The deniers meant the phrase as absurdity; God preserves it in His Book as prophecy.

## Sūrah Saba' 34:8

> **"Has he fabricated a lie against God, or is he possessed?" Nay, but those who believe not in the Hereafter shall be in punishment and extreme error.**

**Expanded Commentary**

**The Dilemma of the Deniers**

The mockery continues with a forced choice: either the Prophet ﷺ has *fabricated a lie against God* (*aftarā ʿalā Allāhi kadhiban*) — inventing revelation knowingly — or *he is possessed* (*bihi jinnah*) — imagining it under the influence of jinn. Al-Rāzī notes these are the only two explanations the deniers can offer, since they rule out the third possibility — that he is truthful — in advance. The same two accusations were leveled against earlier messengers:

> *"And they say: A poet possessed!"* (Qur'an 37:36, said of the Prophet ﷺ)

> *"Truly we say naught but that one of our gods has afflicted thee with evil."* (Qur'an 11:54, said to Hūd)

Ibn Kathīr responds with the Qur'an's own challenge: a fabricator seeks worldly gain, yet the Prophet ﷺ lived in austerity and refused kingship; a possessed man speaks incoherently, yet the Qur'an is the most coherent and eloquent of speech. The accusations refute themselves.

**Nay: The Verdict Reversed**

*Bal* — "Nay" — overturns their dilemma entirely. The truth is not that the warner is mad, but that *those who believe not in the Hereafter shall be in punishment and extreme error* — *fī al-ʿadhābi wa al-ḍalāl al-baʿīd*. The word *baʿīd* (far, extreme) depicts error as distance: they have wandered so far from the road that return seems impossible. Al-Qurṭubī observes that the punishment (*ʿadhāb*) and the error (*ḍalāl*) are linked — indeed, from one perspective, extreme error is itself already a punishment: the punishment of living without hope, without meaning, and without preparation, followed by the punishment of the Fire. The Qur'an states the same reversal elsewhere:

> *"Nay, but they denied the Hour, and We have prepared for those who deny the Hour a blazing Fire."* (Qur'an 25:11)

**Why Denial of the Hereafter Is Extreme Error**

Denying the Hereafter is not one error among others; it is the error that corrupts all reasoning. If there is no reckoning, then justice is a dream, sacrifice is folly, and cruelty pays. The denier must then explain why conscience condemns what nature rewards — an explanation no materialism has ever supplied. The Prophet ﷺ said:

> *"The wise one is he who calls himself to account and works for what comes after death; the fool is he who follows his caprice and entertains false hopes about God."* (al-Tirmidhī 2459; Ibn Mājah 4260)

The verse thus quietly invites the reader to judge between two claims to rationality: the "rationalists" who cannot account for morality, and the "possessed" man whose message grounds justice, mercy, and hope.

## Sūrah Saba' 34:9

> **Have they not considered that which is before them and that which is behind them of the sky and the earth? If We will, We cause the earth to engulf them or fragments from the sky to fall upon them. Truly in that is a sign for every penitent servant.**

**Expanded Commentary**

**The Evidence Above and Below**

*Have they not considered* — *a-fa-lam yaraw* — "Have they not seen?" The Qur'an appeals from mockery to observation. *That which is before them and that which is behind them of the sky and the earth* surrounds them on every side; they are enveloped in evidence. The sky above — vast, ordered, upheld — and the earth below — firm, fruitful, spread out — testify to a Power capable of resurrection. The argument is developed fully elsewhere:

> *"Is not He Who created the heavens and the earth able to create the like thereof? Yea, indeed!"* (Qur'an 36:81)

> *"And among His signs is that the sky and the earth stand fast by His Command."* (Qur'an 30:25)

Al-Ṭabarī explains: the One who holds up this immense canopy and spreads out this vast carpet without pillar or prop can surely reassemble scattered bones. Abraham's argument in Sūrah al-Anʿām (6:75–78) follows the same path — from creation to Creator.

**Power to Punish as Proof of Power to Resurrect**

*If We will, We cause the earth to engulf them or fragments from the sky to fall upon them* — *in nasha' nakhsif bihim al-arḍa aw nusqiṭ ʿalayhim kisafan min al-samā'*. The same sky and earth that testify can also punish. The Qur'an reminds the secure of precedents: Qārūn was engulfed by the earth (28:81), and Shuʿayb's people were threatened with falling fragments (26:187). The rhetorical question recurs:

> *"Do those who plot evil feel secure that God will not cause the earth to engulf them, or that punishment will not come upon them whence they perceive not?"* (Qur'an 16:45)

Ibn Kathīr notes the logic: vulnerability proves dependence, and dependence proves the need for a Sustainer. Every earthquake and every meteor is a small demonstration that the order of nature is held, not self-subsisting — and what is held can be released.

**A Sign for Every Penitent Servant**

*Truly in that is a sign for every penitent servant* — *inna fī dhālika la-āyatan li-kulli ʿabdin munīb*. This is the only verse in the Qur'an that speaks of a sign *for every penitent servant* (*munīb* — one who repeatedly turns back to God). Most verses speak of signs "for those who reflect" or "for the believers." Al-Rāzī draws out the beauty: the sky and earth preach two sermons — one of power (which terrifies) and one of mercy (which attracts) — and only the penitent heart hears both. The arrogant see nature and feel secure; the penitent sees the same nature and feels awe. The same wonders are described elsewhere as:

> *"A source of insight and a reminder for every penitent servant."* (Qur'an 50:8)

The practical lesson is immediate: the reader is invited to become *munīb* — one who turns — for turning is the precondition of seeing.

---

## Sūrah Saba' 34:10

> **And indeed We gave David bounty from Us: "O mountains! Echo God's praises with him, likewise you birds!" And We made iron supple for him.**

**Expanded Commentary**

**From Deniers to Prophets: The Turn in the Sūrah**

With verse 10 the sūrah pivots from refuting deniers to presenting models: David and Solomon. The contrast is deliberate. The deniers claimed permanence for themselves; God shows where true, lasting power lies — with grateful prophets, not arrogant hoarders. *And indeed We gave David bounty from Us* — *wa laqad ātaynā Dāwūda minnā faḍlan* — the word *faḍl* (bounty, surplus favor) encompasses prophethood, revelation (the Psalms), kingship, judgment, and the miracles that follow. The Qur'an says elsewhere:

> *"And unto David We gave the Psalms."* (Qur'an 4:163)

> *"And We gave judgment and knowledge to David and Solomon."* (Qur'an 21:79)

**Mountains and Birds in Chorus**

*"O mountains! Echo God's praises with him, likewise you birds!"* — *yā jibālu awwibī maʿahu wa al-ṭayr*. The verb *awwaba* means to repeat or echo — the mountains are commanded to resound with David's glorification. The Qur'an confirms this miracle in two other places:

> *"And We made the mountains subservient, glorifying with him at evening and sunrise, and the birds gathered — all turning unto him."* (Qur'an 38:18–19)

> *"And We made the mountains and the birds subservient unto David, glorifying."* (Qur'an 21:79)

Al-Qurṭubī explains that David's voice in the Psalms was of such beauty and devotion that creation itself joined him. The deeper point, noted by al-Rāzī, is vicegerency: David, as God's deputy (*khalīfah*) on earth (38:26), gathers creation around him in worship. When man worships rightly, nature harmonizes with him; when man rebels, nature itself groans. The Prophet ﷺ, whose own relationship with creation included the weeping palm trunk (al-Bukhārī 3583) and the greeting stones of Makkah (Muslim 2277), is the heir of this harmony.

**Iron Made Supple: The Miracle of Craft**

*And We made iron supple for him* — *wa alannā lahu al-ḥadīd*. Before David, armor was made of heavy plates; God taught David to work iron as if it were wax, fashioning flexible coats of mail. The Qur'an says:

> *"And We taught him the fashioning of coats of mail for you, to protect you in your wars."* (Qur'an 21:80)

Ibn ʿAbbās reportedly said that God made iron soft in David's hand like thread, so he needed neither fire nor hammer. Whether taken as direct miracle or as divinely taught metallurgy, the meaning converges: human craft itself is a divine gift. The verse sanctifies honest industry — the prophet is not only a psalmist but a smith, earning his living by his hand. The Prophet ﷺ said:

> *"No one eats better food than that earned by the work of his own hand; and David, the prophet of God, would eat from the work of his own hand."* (al-Bukhārī 2072)

## Sūrah Saba' 34:11

> **[Saying], "Make coats of mail, and measure well the links thereof, and work righteousness. Truly I see whatsoever you do."**

**Expanded Commentary**

**Divine Instruction in Craftsmanship**

This verse completes the command to David: *make coats of mail* (*sābighāt* — full-length, ample coats), *and measure well the links* (*wa qaddir fī al-sard* — proportion the rings precisely). Al-Ṭabarī explains *sard* as the interlinking of rings in chainmail: neither too loose (leaving gaps for spears) nor too tight (restricting movement). The Qur'an thus legislates quality control. Work done for protection of life must be precise; sloppiness in craftsmanship is, in this ethic, a moral failing. The Prophet ﷺ generalized the principle:

> *"God loves that when one of you does a deed, he perfects it."* (al-Ṭabarānī, al-Muʿjam al-Awsaṭ 897; graded ḥasan by al-Albānī)

Modern readers may extend the verse to every craft affecting human welfare — medicine, engineering, construction, software: to "measure well the links" is to honor the trust placed in the maker.

**Work Righteousness: Craft Joined to Piety**

*And work righteousness* — *wa ʿmalū ṣāliḥan* — addressed in the plural to David and his household (or his people): professional excellence must accompany moral excellence. The armor-maker must also be righteous, for skill without righteousness produces instruments of oppression. Al-Rāzī notes the pairing answers a perennial divide: the devout who neglect worldly competence, and the competent who neglect devotion. David's house is commanded to both — forge iron and forge souls. The Qur'an elsewhere joins the two domains:

> *"And seek, through that which God has given thee, the Abode of the Hereafter, and forget not thy portion in this world, and do good as God has done good unto thee."* (Qur'an 28:77)

**Truly I See Whatsoever You Do**

*Truly I see whatsoever you do* — *innī bimā taʿmalūna baṣīr* — closes the command with divine watchfulness. Every link measured or mismeasured, every deed righteous or otherwise, falls under His sight. For the sincere craftsman this is encouragement: no precision is wasted. For the corrupt, it is warning: no corner-cutting is hidden. The Name *Baṣīr* (All-Seeing) recurs throughout the Qur'an as the guardian of public and private integrity:

> *"And God is Seer of what you do."* (Qur'an 2:96, and many others)

The verse thus establishes an Islamic work ethic in miniature: skill, precision, righteousness, and God-consciousness — the armor of the body and the armor of the soul forged together.

## Sūrah Saba' 34:12

> **And unto Solomon the wind, whose morning course was a month and whose evening course was a month. And We caused a fount of molten copper to flow for him. And of the jinn some worked before him by the leave of his Lord — and those of them that deviated from Our Command, We would cause them to taste the punishment of the Blaze.**

**Expanded Commentary**

**The Greater Kingdom of Solomon**

The sūrah moves from father to son: *unto Solomon the wind* — the verb "We gave" or "We made subservient" is understood from the previous verse. Solomon's kingdom exceeded David's, in answer to his own prayer:

> *"My Lord, forgive me and grant me a kingdom such as shall not belong to any after me."* (Qur'an 38:35)

God answered with command over wind, jinn, birds, and speech of creatures. The Qur'an describes the wind elsewhere:

> *"So We made the wind subservient unto him, coursing gently by his command whither he wished."* (Qur'an 38:36)

> *"And unto Solomon the violent wind, coursing by his command toward the land that We blessed."* (Qur'an 21:81)

*Whose morning course was a month and whose evening course was a month* means his wind-borne journeys covered in half a day what ordinary travelers covered in a month — two months' distance in a single day. Ibn Kathīr cites reports that Solomon would ride upon a carpet of wind with his court, armies, and provisions, traveling between Jerusalem, Yemen, and beyond. The detail matters: God compresses distance for whom He wills — a truth every age rediscovers through its own means of travel, all of which remain His gifts.

**A Fount of Molten Copper**

*And We caused a fount of molten copper to flow for him* — *wa asalnā lahu ʿayn al-qiṭr*. Just as iron was softened for David, copper (*qiṭr* — brass, bronze, or molten copper) was made to flow for Solomon like a spring. Al-Ṭabarī explains that God caused a fountain of liquid copper to gush forth, enabling Solomon's grand constructions. The parallel between father and son is exact: each receives a metallurgical miracle suited to his mission — David the smith of armor for defense, Solomon the builder of temples and cities. Both miracles teach that natural resources and technical knowledge are divine outpourings; modern mines and smelters are later chapters of the same bounty.

**The Jinn in Service — and Their Limits**

*And of the jinn some worked before him by the leave of his Lord* — the jinn, beings of smokeless fire normally unseen and ungoverned by men, were placed under Solomon's command. Their service is detailed in the next verse. But the verse adds a warning: *those of them that deviated from Our Command, We would cause them to taste the punishment of the Blaze* — whether the Blaze of the Hereafter (the majority view) or a scorching strike by angels in this world. The theological point is crucial: the jinn served not because Solomon was one of them or worshipped them, but *by the leave of his Lord*. Power over the unseen world belongs to God alone and is lent to prophets as a sign. This directly refutes the idolaters — addressed later in verses 40–41 — who worshipped jinn as if they held independent power.

## Sūrah Saba' 34:13

> **Making for him whatsoever he willed: places of worship, statues, basins like reservoirs, and cauldrons firmly anchored. "Work, O family of David, in thankfulness, though few of My servants are thankful."**

**Expanded Commentary**

**The Works of the Jinn**

*Making for him whatsoever he willed* — the jinn built at Solomon's command. Four products are named. *Places of worship* (*maḥārīb*, plural of *miḥrāb*) — al-Ṭabarī interprets these as fine buildings, chambers, or places of prayer; the word later came to denote the prayer niche in mosques, but here it signifies noble edifices, possibly including the Temple in Jerusalem. *Statues* (*tamāthīl*) — images fashioned for decoration, not worship; Ibn Kathīr notes that image-making was permitted in Solomon's law, though later prohibited in Islamic law — the Prophet ﷺ said:

> *"The makers of images will be punished on the Day of Resurrection; it will be said to them: Give life to what you created."* (al-Bukhārī 5951; Muslim 2108)

*Basins like reservoirs* (*jifān ka-al-jawāb*) — enormous vessels for food and water, showing the scale of Solomon's hospitality. *Cauldrons firmly anchored* (*qudūr rāsiyāt*) — cooking pots too large to move, fixed in place, feeding multitudes. The picture is of a kingdom whose supernatural labor served worship, beauty, and generosity — not tyranny.

**Work in Thankfulness: Gratitude as Action**

*"Work, O family of David, in thankfulness"* — *iʿmalū āla Dāwūda shukran*. Al-Ṭabarī explains: gratitude here is not merely words but work — obedience, worship, and service. The verse makes *shukr* (thankfulness) the job description of the most powerful family on earth. The Prophet ﷺ embodied this when he prayed through the night until his feet swelled; asked why, since his sins were forgiven, he replied:

> *"Should I not be a thankful servant?"* (al-Bukhārī 4837; Muslim 2819)

The lesson extends to every recipient of bounty: the proper response to power is not pride but increased service. Al-Ghazālī later systematized this: gratitude of the heart (recognition), of the tongue (praise), and of the limbs (obedience) — and the verse commands all three under the single word "work."

**Few of My Servants Are Thankful**

*Though few of My servants are thankful* — *wa qalīlun min ʿibādiya al-shakūr*. This solemn coda is among the Qur'an's most quoted sentences. ʿUmar ibn al-Khaṭṭāb reportedly wept upon hearing a man pray to be among "the few," remembering this verse. The Qur'an confirms the rarity of gratitude:

> *"And Thou wilt not find most of them thankful."* (Qur'an 7:17, the boast of Iblīs)

> *"And few of My servants are thankful."* (this verse, quoted as history's verdict)

Psychologically, ingratitude is the default of comfort: blessings normalize, expectations inflate, and the Giver is forgotten. The verse therefore functions as a mirror for every reader — especially readers in ages of abundance. To be among the few is a daily choice: to notice, to praise, and to work.

## Sūrah Saba' 34:14

> **And when We decreed death for him, naught showed his death to them save a creeping creature of the earth eating his staff. And when he fell down, the jinn saw clearly that, had they known the Unseen, they would not have tarried in humiliating punishment.**

**Expanded Commentary**

**The Death of a King No One Noticed**

*And when We decreed death for him* — Solomon, the king commanding winds and jinn, died as all men die. The reports preserved by Ibn Kathīr and al-Ṭabarī describe him remaining upright, leaning on his staff — perhaps in prayer or supervision — while the jinn continued their labor, unaware. *Naught showed his death to them save a creeping creature of the earth eating his staff* — *dābbat al-arḍ ta'kulu minsa'atah*: a termite (or woodworm) gnawed through his staff until it broke and he fell. The mightiest kingdom on earth was supervised, for a time, by a corpse — and none of its supernatural servants perceived it.

**The Exposure of the Jinn's Claim**

*Had they known the Unseen, they would not have tarried in humiliating punishment* — the jinn claimed knowledge of hidden things, and men worshipped them for it. Solomon's death exposed the claim: creatures who cannot detect the death of the man standing before them cannot know the Unseen. Al-Qurṭubī draws the theological conclusion: knowledge of the Unseen (*ʿilm al-ghayb*) belongs to God alone:

> *"Say: None in the heavens or on the earth knows the Unseen, save God."* (Qur'an 27:65)

> *"And with Him are the keys of the Unseen; none knows them but He."* (Qur'an 6:59)

The verse thus completes the sūrah's polemic against jinn-worship (vv. 40–41): why worship beings who toil in humiliation without even knowing their master's death? The Prophet ﷺ reinforced the point when he said that the jinn snatch fragments of heavenly news mixed with a hundred lies (al-Bukhārī 6213).

**Death as the Great Leveler**

Beyond polemic, the verse meditates on mortality. If Solomon — prophet, king, commander of winds — dies leaning on a stick, what security has anyone? The termite becomes a teacher: the smallest creature fells the greatest king, by God's decree. The Qur'an generalizes:

> *"Every soul shall taste death."* (Qur'an 3:185)

> *"Wheresoever you may be, death shall overtake you, even if you be in fortified towers."* (Qur'an 14:78 in some counts; 4:78)

For the believer, the verse is not morbid but liberating: since even Solomon died, death is no disgrace; since even Solomon's kingdom passed, no worldly loss is ultimate. What endures is what Solomon's house was commanded to in the previous verse — thankful work.

## Sūrah Saba' 34:15

> **For Sheba too there was certainly a sign in their dwelling — two gardens, one to the right and one to the left: "Eat of the provision of your Lord and give thanks to Him — a good land, and a forgiving Lord."**

**Expanded Commentary**

**The Sign in Their Dwelling**

The sūrah now reaches its namesake: *For Sheba too there was certainly a sign in their dwelling* — *laqad kāna li-Saba'in fī maskanihim āyah*. The people of Saba' inhabited Yemen, in the region of Ma'rib, where they built the great dam whose remains archaeology confirms. Al-Ṭabarī and Ibn Kathīr describe their land as uniquely blessed: pure air, fertile soil, abundant water, free of pests and vermin. The "sign" (*āyah*) was their very prosperity — prosperity meant to be read as a message from God, not merely enjoyed. The Qur'an presents abundance as a test throughout:

> *"And We tried them with good things and evil things, that they might return."* (Qur'an 7:168)

**Two Gardens, Right and Left**

*Two gardens, one to the right and one to the left* — *jannatāni ʿan al-yamīni wa al-shimāl*. Travelers described the valley of Ma'rib as flanked by gardens on both sides, irrigated by the dam — a green corridor through Arabia. Some commentators say a woman could walk through carrying a basket on her head and find it filled with fallen fruit without picking. The image deliberately echoes Paradise (*jannah* — the same word), suggesting that gratitude can make earth itself paradisiacal, while ingratitude turns gardens into deserts, as the next verses show.

**Eat and Give Thanks: The Covenant of Prosperity**

*"Eat of the provision of your Lord and give thanks to Him"* — the command came through God's messengers: enjoy, but acknowledge. The formula *a good land, and a forgiving Lord* — *baldatun ṭayyibatun wa rabbun ghafūr* — is among the Qur'an's most beautiful summaries of grace: a land without defect, and a Lord who forgives defects. Al-Rāzī notes the asymmetry: the land is merely "good," but the Lord is "forgiving" — as if to say, even if you spoil the good land by your sins, the Forgiving Lord can restore it, provided you turn back. The Prophet ﷺ said:

> *"How wondrous is the affair of the believer: all of it is good, and that is for none but the believer. If prosperity befalls him, he gives thanks, and that is good for him; and if adversity befalls him, he is patient, and that is good for him."* (Muslim 2999)

Saba' was offered the first half of this bargain — prosperity with thanks — and refused it.

## Sūrah Saba' 34:16

> **But they turned away. So We sent the flood of ʿArim upon them and exchanged their two gardens for two gardens bearing bitter fruit, tamarisks, and a few lote trees.**

**Expanded Commentary**

**They Turned Away: The Verb of Ingratitude**

*But they turned away* — *fa-aʿraḍū*. The verb *iʿrāḍ* means to turn the face aside — the gesture of one who hears and ignores. Ibn Kathīr explains: they turned from worshipping God alone, from obeying His messengers, and from giving thanks. Their prosperity itself became the cause: comfort bred complacency, complacency bred arrogance, and arrogance bred apostasy — some reportedly turning to sun-worship. The pattern is universal enough that the Qur'an states it as a law:

> *"And when We bless man, he turns away and withdraws aside."* (Qur'an 17:83)

> *"Nay, truly man exceeds all bounds, when he deems himself self-sufficient."* (Qur'an 96:6–7)

**The Flood of ʿArim**

*So We sent the flood of ʿArim upon them* — *fa-arsalnā ʿalayhim sayl al-ʿarim*. The word *ʿarim* is explained variously: as the name of the great dam of Ma'rib, as the word for dam itself (supported by South Arabian inscriptions), or as meaning violent, powerful flood. Ibn Kathīr relates that God sent rats or breaches that undermined the dam until it burst, unleashing stored waters that destroyed the irrigation system and drowned the valley. Modern historians date the final collapse of the Ma'rib dam to around the sixth century CE — within living memory of the Prophet's ﷺ generation. The sūrah thus cites, for its first hearers, a recent and verifiable catastrophe: ruins they could visit, refugees they could meet.

**Gardens Exchanged for Wilderness**

*And exchanged their two gardens for two gardens bearing bitter fruit, tamarisks, and a few lote trees* — *wa baddalnāhum bi-jannatayhim jannatayni dhawātay ukulin khamṭin wa athlin wa shay'in min sidrin qalīl*. The bitter parallelism is deliberate: gardens (*jannatayn*) for gardens (*jannatayn*) — but the new "gardens" yield only *khamṭ* (bitter, inedible fruit), *athl* (tamarisk, a salt-tolerant shrub of deserts), and a few *sidr* (lote trees, thorny with sparse berries). Al-Qurṭubī notes the Qur'an's irony: it still calls them "two gardens," but gardens in name only. The transformation is ecological, economic, and spiritual at once. The same law operates in every age: when gratitude departs, blessing departs — sometimes suddenly, through catastrophe, and sometimes gradually, through the slow salting of the soil.

## Sūrah Saba' 34:17

> **Thus did We recompense them for having disbelieved. And do We recompense any but the disbeliever?**

**Expanded Commentary**

**Recompense Fitted to the Crime**

*Thus did We recompense them for having disbelieved* — *dhālika jazaynāhum bimā kafarū*. The flood was not random misfortune but *jazā'* — recompense, requital, the measured return of a deed. Al-Ṭabarī stresses the causality: disbelief and ingratitude (*kufr* covers both, since both deny the Giver) produced destruction as effect follows cause. The Qur'an states the general rule:

> *"And whatever affliction befalls you is for what your hands have earned — and He pardons much."* (Qur'an 42:30)

Ibn Kathīr adds that God does not wrong: Saba' was warned by messengers, enjoyed every blessing, and persisted — the flood came only after the case against them was complete.

**Do We Recompense Any but the Disbeliever?**

*And do We recompense any but the disbeliever?* — *wa hal nujāzī illā al-kafūr*. This sentence has been read in two ways, both supported by classical commentary. In one reading (*hal* as interrogative), it asks a rhetorical question expecting the answer "No." In another, *hal* carries the force of negation: "We recompense none but the disbeliever." Al-Qurṭubī explains the implication: believers are forgiven many of their sins and disciplined lightly for the rest, while persistent, unrepentant disbelief draws full recompense. The same principle appears in:

> *"Whosoever does evil shall be recompensed only with the like thereof."* (Qur'an 40:40)

Some commentators, including al-Ṭabarī, note an alternate reading (*yujāzā* — "is any recompensed but...") with the same meaning. Theologically, the verse affirms both divine justice (punishment is earned) and divine mercy (punishment is largely reserved for those who refuse all mercy).

**The Warning to Every Later Saba'**

Every prosperous society is a potential Saba'. The verse invites self-examination: are our gardens — our economies, technologies, and securities — met with gratitude or with the assumption of self-sufficiency? The Prophet ﷺ warned:

> *"By God, it is not poverty that I fear for you; rather I fear that the world will be spread out for you as it was spread out for those before you, and you will compete for it as they competed, and it will destroy you as it destroyed them."* (al-Bukhārī 6425; Muslim 2961)

The flood of ʿArim is thus not ancient history but a standing parable: civilizations do not drown only in water — they drown in ingratitude first.

## Sūrah Saba' 34:18

> **And We set between them and the towns that We had blessed towns easily seen, and We measured the distance between them: "Journey between them in security by night and by day."**

**Expanded Commentary**

**The Blessing of Safe Passage**

Before describing Saba's final ingratitude, the sūrah recalls another forgotten blessing: *We set between them and the towns that We had blessed towns easily seen* — *wa jaʿalnā baynahum wa bayna al-qurā allatī bāraknā fīhā quran ẓāhiratan*. Ibn Kathīr explains: between Yemen (Saba') and Syria (*al-Shām* — "the towns We blessed," the fertile lands of the north) God placed a chain of visible, connected settlements, so that travelers on the trade route always saw the next town ahead. *We measured the distance between them* (*wa qaddarnā fīhā al-sayr*) — the stages were proportioned: short, regular, with water and rest at each. No one needed to carry heavy provisions or brave desolate wastes.

**Journey in Security by Night and by Day**

*"Journey between them in security by night and by day"* — *sīrū fīhā layāliya wa ayyāman āminīn*. Night travel in Arabia was normally perilous — bandits, beasts, and thirst. For Saba', God made even nights safe. Al-Qurṭubī compares this to the blessing granted the Quraysh:

> *"Who fed them against hunger and secured them against fear."* (Qur'an 106:4)

Security (*amn*) is, in the Qur'anic order, among the greatest of blessings — greater than wealth, for wealth without security is torment. The Prophet ﷺ said:

> *"Whosoever among you wakes secure in his home, healthy in his body, possessing his day's provision — it is as if the whole world has been gathered for him."* (al-Tirmidhī 2346; Ibn Mājah 4141)

**Infrastructure as Divine Gift**

Modern readers, accustomed to highways, lighting, policing, and logistics, may miss the wonder: every safe road is a divine gift before it is an engineering achievement. Saba' enjoyed what states spend fortunes to build — a secure trade corridor — as a grace. The verse teaches governments and citizens alike: public safety, connective infrastructure, and the conditions of commerce are blessings to be acknowledged, maintained with justice, and never taken for granted. For Saba' took them for granted — and, as the next verse shows, even complained of them.

## Sūrah Saba' 34:19

> **But they said, "Our Lord, increase the distance of our journeys." And they wronged themselves; so We made them as tales of old and tore them completely to pieces. Truly in that are signs for all who are patient, thankful.**

**Expanded Commentary**

**The Complaint of the Spoiled**

*But they said: Our Lord, increase the distance of our journeys* — *fa-qālū rabbanā bāʿid bayna asfārinā*. Al-Ṭabarī and Ibn Kathīr explain this astonishing request: instead of thanking God for short, safe stages, Saba' grew bored with ease. They wished for long, romantic desert crossings where the rich could display their mounts and provisions and the poor would be excluded — pride preferring hardship-with-distinction over comfort-with-equality. Some commentators add they grew tired of continuous settlement and craved the adventure of the void. Whatever the motive, the request reveals a diseased soul: blessing itself had become tedious. The Qur'an records a parallel complaint from the Children of Israel, weary of manna and quails:

> *"O Moses, we shall not bear one kind of food."* (Qur'an 2:61)

Prosperity, unaccompanied by gratitude, breeds not contentment but restlessness — the consumer's disease of every affluent age.

**They Wronged Themselves**

*And they wronged themselves* — *wa ẓalamū anfusahum*. Not God — God cannot be wronged. Every sin is self-harm before it is anything else. The Qur'an repeats this truth relentlessly:

> *"And We wronged them not, but they wronged themselves."* (Qur'an 11:101)

*So We made them as tales of old* (*fa-jaʿalnāhum aḥādīth*) — they became stories told around fires, like ʿĀd and Thamūd. *And tore them completely to pieces* (*wa mazzaqnāhum kulla mumazzaq*) — the same emphatic phrase the deniers used mockingly in verse 7 (*muzziqtum kulla mumazzaq*) now describes Saba's actual fate: scattered through Arabia — the Ghassān to Syria, the Aws and Khazraj to Yathrib, the Azd to Oman — their unity shredded. The Qur'an's irony is exact: those who mocked "tearing to pieces" should study a people torn to pieces.

**Signs for All Who Are Patient, Thankful**

*Truly in that are signs for all who are patient, thankful* — *inna fī dhālika la-āyātin li-kulli ṣabbārin shakūr*. This pairing — *ṣabbār* (intensely patient) and *shakūr* (intensely thankful) — appears in four places (14:5; 31:31; 34:19; 42:33), always in connection with divine signs in history or nature. Al-Rāzī explains: patience interprets adversity rightly, and gratitude interprets prosperity rightly; together they form the complete reader of God's signs. The Prophet ﷺ said:

> *"Faith is of two halves: half patience and half gratitude."* (al-Bayhaqī, Shuʿab al-Īmān 4716; with supporting narrations)

Saba' failed both halves: impatient with ease, ungrateful for blessing. Their tale remains — a signpost for all who would not become tales themselves.

---

## Sūrah Saba' 34:20

> **And Iblīs did indeed prove his opinion of them to be true; and they followed him, save for a group among the believers.**

**Expanded Commentary**

**The Opinion of Iblīs**

*And Iblīs did indeed prove his opinion of them to be true* — *wa laqad ṣaddaqa ʿalayhim Iblīsu ẓannah*. What was his "opinion" (*ẓann*)? The Qur'an records it in his own words:

> *"If Thou dost grant me reprieve till the Day of Resurrection, I shall surely gain mastery over his progeny, all save a few."* (Qur'an 17:62)

> *"And Thou wilt not find most of them thankful."* (Qur'an 7:17)

Iblīs predicted that most humans would prove ungrateful and follow him. With Saba' — and with most of mankind — the prediction came true. Al-Ṭabarī notes the verb form: Iblīs "proved true" his opinion not by power but by invitation; men validated his low estimate of them freely. The verse is thus a rebuke to humanity, not a tribute to Satan: the enemy boasted he would mislead us, and we obliged him.

**They Followed Him — Save a Group**

*And they followed him, save for a group among the believers* — *fa-ttabaʿūhu illā farīqan min al-mu'minīn*. The exception is precious: a *farīq* (company, contingent) of believers resisted. Ibn Kathīr identifies them as those who held fast to faith and obedience despite Satan's whispers. Their existence proves two things: resistance is possible (so the followers have no excuse), and the saved are a minority (so the believer should not be dismayed at being outnumbered). The Prophet ﷺ said:

> *"Islam began as something strange, and it will return to being strange as it began; so glad tidings to the strangers."* (Muslim 145)

**The Saba' Connection**

Why does this verse follow the Saba' narrative? Because Saba' is the case study of Iblīs's opinion fulfilled: a blessed people, invited to gratitude, chose ingratitude — exactly as he predicted. Al-Rāzī observes the sequence: the sūrah showed David and Solomon (the thankful few) and Saba' (the unthankful many), then names the whisperer behind the difference. Every reader must ask: in which group am I proving Iblīs right or wrong? Each act of gratitude falsifies his opinion; each act of heedlessness confirms it.

## Sūrah Saba' 34:21

> **And yet he had no authority over them, save that We might know one who believes in the Hereafter from one who is in doubt thereof. And thy Lord guards over all things.**

**Expanded Commentary**

**No Authority — Only Invitation**

*And yet he had no authority over them* — *wa mā kāna lahu ʿalayhim min sulṭān*. *Sulṭān* means compelling power, proof, or mastery. Satan possesses none of these over human beings. He cannot force, only suggest; he cannot prove, only insinuate. The Qur'an is emphatic:

> *"As for My servants, truly thou hast no authority over them, save for those in error who follow thee."* (Qur'an 15:42)

> *"Truly he has no authority over those who believe and trust in their Lord. His authority is only over those who befriend him and those who associate partners with Him."* (Qur'an 16:99–100)

Ibn Taymiyyah explained: Satan's power is like that of a tempter calling from a doorway — he cannot drag anyone through it. Those who enter do so walking. This destroys the excuse "Satan made me do it": Satan proposes; man disposes.

**That We Might Know: The Wisdom of the Test**

*Save that We might know one who believes in the Hereafter from one who is in doubt thereof* — *illā li-naʿlama man yu'minu bi-al-ākhirati mimman huwa minhā fī shakk*. God already knows; as al-Ṭabarī and al-Rāzī explain, "that We might know" means "that We might make manifest" — distinguishing in actuality what was known in eternity, so that judgment rests on demonstrated reality, not merely foreknowledge. The test requires a tester: without temptation, faith and doubt would remain indistinguishable claims. The same principle appears in:

> *"And We shall surely test you until We know those among you who strive and the patient."* (Qur'an 47:31)

> *"God already knows those who are truthful, and surely He knows the liars."* (Qur'an 29:3)

The verse thus answers the problem of evil's permission: Satan is granted scope not because God is weak, but because moral distinction requires moral trial.

**Thy Lord Guards Over All Things**

*And thy Lord guards over all things* — *wa rabbuka ʿalā kulli shay'in ḥafīẓ*. The Name *Ḥafīẓ* (Guardian, Preserver) closes the passage with assurance: Satan operates within a guarded universe. He cannot exceed his leash, cannot mislead the sincerely guarded, and cannot escape his own reckoning. For the believer assailed by whispers, this is comfort: the Guardian watches the whisperer. The Prophet ﷺ taught refuge explicitly:

> *"Say: I seek refuge with the Lord of mankind, the King of mankind, the God of mankind, from the evil of the retreating whisperer."* (Qur'an 114:1–4, taught as daily protection; cf. al-Bukhārī 5017)

## Sūrah Saba' 34:22

> **Say, "Call upon those whom you claim apart from God; they have not a mote's weight of power in the heavens or on the earth, and they have no share in either; nor has He a helper among them."**

**Expanded Commentary**

**The Challenge to Call**

*Say: Call upon those whom you claim apart from God* — *qul idʿū alladhīna zaʿamtum min dūni Allāh*. The Prophet ﷺ is commanded to challenge the idolaters directly: invoke your alleged gods. The verb *idʿū* (call upon) means both to pray to and to summon for help. Similar challenges recur:

> *"Say: Call upon those whom you claim apart from Him; they have no power to remove affliction from you, nor to transfer it."* (Qur'an 17:56)

> *"Show me what they created of the earth."* (Qur'an 46:4)

Al-Rāzī notes the challenge is practical, not abstract: the test of divinity is efficacy. If these beings are gods, let them act like gods — relieve suffering, answer prayer, avert death. Their silence is their refutation.

**Not a Mote's Weight: The Scale of Their Power**

*They have not a mote's weight of power in the heavens or on the earth* — *lā yamlikūna mithqāla dharratin fī al-samāwāti wa lā fī al-arḍ*. The *dharrah* (mote, speck) appeared in verse 3 for God's knowledge; here it measures the idols' power. The contrast is total: God knows every mote; they own not one. Al-Qurṭubī observes the comprehensiveness: neither independent ownership (*mulk*) in any realm, however small. The Qur'an hammers the point with varied images:

> *"As for those upon whom you call apart from Him, they do not possess so much as the husk of a date stone."* (Qur'an 35:13)

> *"Those whom you call upon apart from God shall never create a fly, even if they gathered together for it."* (Qur'an 22:73)

**No Share, No Helper**

*And they have no share in either; nor has He a helper among them* — *wa mā lahum fīhimā min shirk, wa mā lahu minhum min ẓahīr*. Two further claims are demolished: partnership (*shirk* — a share in governing creation) and assistance (*ẓahīr* — a helper God needs). The idolaters sometimes conceded God's supremacy but claimed their gods assisted or interceded, saying:

> *"We do not worship them, save to bring us nigh in nearness unto God."* (Qur'an 39:3)

The verse answers: God needs no assistant and has no partner. Ibn Taymiyyah made this the center of his theology: all forms of *shirk* — in lordship, worship, or names — collapse before the sole Sufficiency of God. For modern readers, the verse interrogates subtler idols: wealth, power, ideology, self — each claimed as helper or partner, each weightless on the scale.

## Sūrah Saba' 34:23

> **And intercession will benefit none with Him, save whomsoever He gives leave, such that when terror is banished from their hearts, they will ask, "What did your Lord say?" They will reply, "The truth, and He is the Exalted, the Great."**

**Expanded Commentary**

**Intercession Only by Leave**

*And intercession will benefit none with Him, save whomsoever He gives leave* — *wa lā tanfaʿu al-shafāʿatu ʿindahu illā li-man adhina lah*. Having denied the idols power, partnership, and assistance, the Qur'an now addresses their last supposed function: intercession (*shafāʿah*). The verse does not abolish intercession — it regulates it. No one intercedes by right or by status; intercession operates only *by permission*, for those God approves. The doctrine is elaborated elsewhere:

> *"Who is there that intercedes with Him, save by His leave?"* (Qur'an 2:255)

> *"And how many an angel is there in the heavens whose intercession avails naught, save after God gives leave to whomsoever He wills and is pleased with."* (Qur'an 53:26)

Islamic tradition affirms the intercession of prophets, angels, martyrs, and the righteous — supremely the Prophet Muhammad's ﷺ "praiseworthy station" (*al-maqām al-maḥmūd*) — but always as a granted honor, never as an independent power. The Prophet ﷺ said:

> *"My intercession is for the people of major sins among my community."* (Abū Dāwūd 4739; al-Tirmidhī 2435, ṣaḥīḥ)

**When Terror Is Banished from Their Hearts**

*Such that when terror is banished from their hearts* — *ḥattā idhā fuzziʿa ʿan qulūbihim*. The scene shifts to the angels: when God issues a decree, they are seized by awe and terror until it passes, then ask one another what was said. The Prophet ﷺ explained this verse vividly:

> *"When God decrees a matter in Heaven, the angels beat their wings in submission to His word, like a chain upon a rock. Then when terror is banished from their hearts, they say: What did your Lord say? They say: The truth, and He is the Exalted, the Great."* (al-Bukhārī 4701, 7481)

Al-Qurṭubī draws the implication: if the angels — the highest creatures, nearest to God — tremble before His word and dare not intercede without leave, what folly to imagine that stones, saints, or jinn intercede automatically? The verse replaces a casual, mechanical view of intercession with one of awe.

**The Truth, and He Is the Exalted, the Great**

*They will reply: The truth, and He is the Exalted, the Great* — *qālū al-ḥaqq, wa huwa al-ʿAlī al-Kabīr*. The angels' answer confesses two things: God's word is truth (*al-ḥaqq*), and God Himself is supremely high (*al-ʿAlī*) and great (*al-Kabīr*). No higher testimony exists. If the deniers will not accept the Prophet's word about intercession, let them hear the angels' — spoken in trembling. The verse leaves the idolater with nothing: no powerful gods, no partners, no helpers, no automatic intercessors — only the One Exalted God, whose leave alone opens every door.

## Sūrah Saba' 34:24

> **Say, "Who provides for you from the heavens and the earth?" Say, "God. And surely either we or you are upon guidance or in manifest error."**

**Expanded Commentary**

**The Question of Provision**

*Say: Who provides for you from the heavens and the earth?* — *qul man yarzuqukum min al-samāwāti wa al-arḍ*. The Prophet ﷺ is commanded to pose a question whose answer even the idolaters concede. From the heavens: rain, light, warmth, decreed measures. From the earth: crops, livestock, minerals, water. The Qur'an poses it repeatedly:

> *"Say: Who provides for you from Heaven and earth? Or who possesses hearing and sight?"* (Qur'an 10:31)

> *"Is there a creator other than God who provides for you from Heaven and earth?"* (Qur'an 35:3)

Al-Ṭabarī notes the strategic wisdom: begin with common ground. The idolaters admitted God as Creator and Provider (*"If thou askest them who created the heavens and earth, they will surely say: God"* — 31:25); the question exposes the inconsistency of worshipping others while living off His provision.

**Say: God — The Answer Supplied**

*Say: God* — *qul Allāh*. The Prophet ﷺ is told to answer his own question, since the opponents, though knowing the answer, hesitate to pronounce it — for pronouncing it condemns their worship. Ibn Kathīr observes the eloquence: the answer is short, absolute, and inescapable. He who provides is He who deserves worship; gratitude follows sustenance by every logic of loyalty. The Qur'an presses the point:

> *"Who is it that will provide for you if He withholds His provision?"* (Qur'an 67:21)

Modern man, who imagines provision comes from employers, markets, or states, is asked the same question at one remove: and who provides the providers?

**Either We or You: The Gentle Challenge**

*And surely either we or you are upon guidance or in manifest error* — *wa innā aw iyyākum la-ʿalā hudan aw fī ḍalālin mubīn*. This is among the Qur'an's most courteous polemics. Instead of declaring "we are guided and you are astray" (though that is the truth), the Prophet ﷺ is told to frame it as an open disjunction: one of us is guided and the other astray — let the evidence decide. Some commentators read *aw* (or) here as gently inviting reflection rather than asserting division. Al-Qurṭubī praises the wisdom: this formulation disarms, invites fair comparison, and places the burden of proof where it belongs — without surrendering truth. It models how believers should argue: with confidence in the answer but courtesy in the question, leaving the opponent dignity enough to change his mind.

## Sūrah Saba' 34:25

> **Say, "You will not be questioned concerning that of which we were guilty; nor will we be questioned concerning what you do."**

**Expanded Commentary**

**Disavowal Without Aggression**

This verse continues the courteous polemic: *Say: You will not be questioned concerning that of which we were guilty; nor will we be questioned concerning what you do* — *qul lā tus'alūna ʿammā ajramnā wa lā nus'alu ʿammā taʿmalūn*. Note the asymmetry, marked by classical commentators: our side's hypothetical wrongdoing is called *ijrām* (crime, guilt — the harsh word), while your side's deeds are called merely *ʿamal* (doing — the neutral word). The believer takes the harsher term for himself and the gentler for his opponent — the height of polemical courtesy. Al-Rāzī observes this teaches debate etiquette: do not load language against your interlocutor while exonerating yourself.

**No Bearing of Another's Burden**

The substance is individual responsibility: none shall answer for another's deeds. The Qur'an states this law in many places:

> *"And if they deny thee, say: Unto me, my deeds, and unto you, your deeds. You are quit of that which I do, and I am quit of that which you do."* (Qur'an 10:41)

> *"And no bearer of burdens shall bear the burden of another."* (Qur'an 6:164; 17:15; 35:18; 39:7; 53:38)

Ibn Kathīr explains the context: the idolaters urged compromise and shared responsibility; the verse severs the bargain. Each party meets God alone. This is both a warning to the deniers (your leaders cannot answer for you) and a consolation to the Prophet ﷺ (their rejection is not your burden).

**The Logic of Moral Individualism**

The verse establishes a principle that protects both justice and freedom: justice, because punishment must track personal guilt; freedom, because no one may coerce another's conscience on the pretext of shared liability. The Prophet ﷺ applied it even within the family:

> *"O Fāṭimah, daughter of Muhammad, ask of my wealth what you will, but I avail you naught against God."* (al-Bukhārī 2753; Muslim 206)

Modern ideologies of collective guilt and inherited blame dissolve before this verse: every soul stands in its own file, judged for its own deeds.

## Sūrah Saba' 34:26

> **Say, "Our Lord will gather us together; then He will decide between us with truth; and He is the Arbiter, the Knower."**

**Expanded Commentary**

**Gathering Before Decision**

*Say: Our Lord will gather us together* — *qul yajmaʿu baynanā rabbunā*. After disavowing shared liability, the Prophet ﷺ points to shared assembly: we will all be gathered — believers and deniers — on one plain. The Qur'an calls that Day by this very act:

> *"The Day of Gathering — that is a Day whereunto mankind shall be gathered."* (Qur'an 11:103)

> *"And warn of the Day of Gathering, wherein there is no doubt."* (Qur'an 42:7)

Ibn Kathīr explains: disputes unresolved in this world — where the loud, the numerous, or the powerful seem to win — will be reopened before a Judge who cannot be deceived. The verse thus counsels patience to the wronged: every case is docketed; none is dismissed.

**He Will Decide Between Us with Truth**

*Then He will decide between us with truth* — *thumma yaftaḥu baynanā bi-al-ḥaqq*. The verb *yaftaḥ* (from *f-t-ḥ*, to open) means to judge by laying open — to disclose the truth that settles dispute. Truth here is both the standard and the verdict: He judges *by* truth and *reveals* the truth. The Qur'an promises:

> *"And We shall set up the just balances for the Day of Resurrection, so no soul shall be wronged in aught."* (Qur'an 21:47)

Al-Rāzī notes the comfort for honest disputants: human courts err for lack of knowledge or integrity; the divine court lacks neither.

**The Arbiter, the Knower**

*And He is the Arbiter, the Knower* — *wa huwa al-Fattāḥ al-ʿAlīm*. *Al-Fattāḥ* (the Opener, the Arbiter) is He who opens closed matters — distinguishing truth from falsehood, granting victory to the wronged, opening hearts and provisions. *Al-ʿAlīm* (the Knower) grounds His arbitration in perfect knowledge. The pairing appears also in:

> *"Our Lord, decide between us and our people with truth, and Thou art the best of arbiters."* (Qur'an 7:89, the prayer of Shuʿayb)

For the believer in dispute — theological, legal, or personal — the verse teaches the ultimate appeal: argue courteously (v. 24), disavow false compromise (v. 25), and entrust the final verdict to the Arbiter who knows.

## Sūrah Saba' 34:27

> **Say, "Show me those whom you have joined with Him as partners. Nay! Rather, He is God, the Mighty, the Wise."**

**Expanded Commentary**

**Show Me: The Demand for Evidence**

*Say: Show me those whom you have joined with Him as partners* — *qul arūnī alladhīna alḥaqtum bihi shurakā'*. The challenge is concrete: produce them, point to them, display their credentials. Al-Ṭabarī explains: the idols are stones that neither hear nor answer; the jinn and angels disown worship (as vv. 40–41 will show); so what remains to be "shown"? The Qur'an issues the same demand elsewhere:

> *"Show me what they created of the earth. Or have they a share in the heavens?"* (Qur'an 46:4)

Ibn Kathīr notes the word *alḥaqtum* (you have joined, attached) implies the partners are artificial additions — appended to divinity by human imagination, not found in reality. The demand to "show" exposes that they cannot be shown as gods, only as creatures.

**Nay: The Refutation**

*Kallā* — "Nay! By no means!" The refutation is immediate and absolute: there are no partners to show, because there are no partners. As al-Rāzī observes, the negation covers both existence and possibility: they do not exist, and they could not exist, for divinity admits no plurality:

> *"Had there been gods in them other than God, they would surely have been corrupted."* (Qur'an 21:22)

**He Is God, the Mighty, the Wise**

*Rather, He is God, the Mighty, the Wise* — *bal huwa Allāhu al-ʿAzīz al-Ḥakīm*. The verse closes by affirming what the partners lack. *Al-ʿAzīz* (the Mighty, Irresistible) — none can rival or resist Him; the alleged partners are themselves overpowered. *Al-Ḥakīm* (the Wise) — all His decrees are perfect; the alleged partners decree nothing. This pair of Names, among the Qur'an's most frequent, also answers the sūrah's deeper questions: His Might guarantees the resurrection the deniers mock, and His Wisdom guarantees the justice they doubt. The Prophet ﷺ summarized the testimony when he said:

> *"Whoever dies knowing that there is no god but God shall enter Paradise."* (Muslim 26)

## Sūrah Saba' 34:28

> **And We sent thee not, save as a bearer of glad tidings and a warner to mankind entire. But most of mankind know not.**

**Expanded Commentary**

**A Universal Mission**

*And We sent thee not, save as a bearer of glad tidings and a warner to mankind entire* — *wa mā arsalnāka illā kāffatan li-al-nās bashīran wa nadhīran*. The word *kāffah* (entire, all together) establishes the universality of the Prophet Muhammad's ﷺ mission. Earlier messengers were sent to their peoples; he was sent to all peoples, in all lands, till the Last Day. The Qur'an affirms:

> *"Say: O mankind, truly I am the Messenger of God unto you all."* (Qur'an 7:158)

> *"And We sent thee not, save as a mercy unto the worlds."* (Qur'an 21:107)

The Prophet ﷺ himself declared:

> *"I was sent to all of mankind, while the prophets before me were sent each to his own people."* (al-Bukhārī 335, 438; Muslim 521, from Jābir)

> *"By Him in Whose Hand is Muhammad's soul, none of this community — Jew or Christian — hears of me and dies without believing in what I was sent with, but that he shall be among the inhabitants of the Fire."* (Muslim 153)

Ibn Kathīr notes some commentators read *kāffah* as describing his two functions combined — bearing glad tidings *and* warning together — but the majority and strongest reading is universality of scope.

**Bearer of Glad Tidings and Warner**

*Bashīran wa nadhīran* — the Prophet's ﷺ double office: good news (*bishārah*) for believers (forgiveness, Paradise, divine pleasure) and warning (*nidhārah*) for deniers (accountability, loss, punishment). Every prophet held both offices:

> *"Messengers bearing glad tidings and warning, that mankind might have no argument against God after the messengers."* (Qur'an 4:165)

Al-Qurṭubī observes the balance: glad tidings without warning breeds complacency; warning without glad tidings breeds despair. The Prophet ﷺ held both in perfect proportion — so much so that God described his grief over the unwarned:

> *"Perhaps thou wouldst kill thyself with grief over them, if they believe not."* (Qur'an 26:3)

**But Most of Mankind Know Not**

*But most of mankind know not* — *wa lākinna akthara al-nāsi lā yaʿlamūn*. Despite universality, most remain ignorant — not for lack of evidence but for lack of attention. The Qur'an repeats this refrain as a diagnosis of the human condition:

> *"But most of mankind know not."* (Qur'an 12:21; 30:6; 34:28, 36)

Ibn Taymiyyah noted that ignorance here is culpable: the message is clear, the proofs accessible, the warner sent — those who do not know have chosen not to learn. For the believer, the clause is a call to convey: universality of mission implies universality of invitation, and every knower bears a share of the Prophet's ﷺ trust.

## Sūrah Saba' 34:29

> **And they say, "When will this promise come to pass, if you are truthful?"**

**Expanded Commentary**

**The Mockers' Question**

*And they say: When will this promise come to pass, if you are truthful?* — *wa yaqūlūna matā hādhā al-waʿd in kuntum ṣādiqīn*. This question — asked verbatim in six places (10:48; 21:38; 27:71; 34:29; 36:48; 67:25) — was the standard taunt of the deniers. *Al-waʿd* (the promise/threat) means the Day of Resurrection and Judgment. The taunt assumes that a prediction without a date is empty; the deniers demand scheduling as the price of belief. Al-Rāzī notes the irony: they ask "when" as if earliness would convince them — yet every warning they received, dated or undated, was mocked alike. Their question is not inquiry but derision, as the context shows:

> *"They ask: When is the Day of Judgment?"* (Qur'an 51:12; 75:6)

**Why the Date Is Withheld**

The Qur'an consistently refuses to name the date, and explains why:

> *"They ask thee of the Hour: when shall it come to pass? Say: Knowledge thereof is only with my Lord. None shall reveal it in its time but He."* (Qur'an 7:187)

Ibn Kathīr explains the wisdom: an announced date would breed procrastination ("we have time") or fatalism ("it is too late"); an unannounced certainty breeds perpetual readiness. The Prophet ﷺ, asked "When is the Hour?", replied with a counter-question:

> *"What have you prepared for it?"* (al-Bukhārī 3688; Muslim 2639)

The questioner had asked for a calendar; the Prophet ﷺ gave him a mirror. The same applies to individual death — each soul's "hour" — whose date is likewise hidden for the same mercy.

**If You Are Truthful: Truthfulness Is Not Date-Giving**

*If you are truthful* — the deniers make date-specification the test of prophethood. The Qur'an rejects the test: truthfulness is proven by signs, consistency, and the coming of lesser warnings, not by satisfying curiosity. Noah's flood came without a published timetable; the destruction of ʿĀd arrived "in days of ill fortune" (41:16) unannounced to the mockers. Al-Qurṭubī notes that partial fulfillments — the victories, the deaths of mockers, the fall of Saba' — already vindicated the warner. The demand for "when" is answered in the next verse — not with a date, but with a certainty.

## Sūrah Saba' 34:30

> **Say, "Yours shall be the tryst of a Day that you cannot delay by a single hour, nor advance."**

**Expanded Commentary**

**The Tryst of a Day**

*Say: Yours shall be the tryst of a Day* — *qul lakum mīʿādu yawm*. *Mīʿād* is an appointed meeting — a tryst that neither party can miss. The Day has been fixed; attendance is compulsory. The Qur'an describes its fixity:

> *"Truly when the term of God comes, it will not be delayed."* (Qur'an 71:4)

> *"And thy Lord shall gather them on a Day whose coming there is no doubt."* (cf. Qur'an 18:47–48)

Al-Ṭabarī explains: the answer gives not the date but the nature — a Day appointed with absolute precision, known to God, hidden from men, and unchangeable by either.

**Neither Delay nor Advance by a Single Hour**

*That you cannot delay by a single hour, nor advance* — *lā tasta'khirūna ʿanhu sāʿatan wa lā tastaqdimūn*. The *sāʿah* (hour, moment) is the smallest unit of appointment; not even it can shift. The same formula governs the doom of guilty nations:

> *"And for every community there is a term; when their term comes, they cannot delay it by a single hour, nor advance it."* (Qur'an 7:34; 10:49; 16:61)

Ibn Kathīr draws out the terror and the mercy: terror, because no pleading postpones the end; mercy, because no haste precipitates it either — every soul receives its full, exact measure of time. The Prophet ﷺ said:

> *"God grants respite to the wrongdoer, but when He seizes him, He does not release him." Then he recited: 'Thus is the seizing of thy Lord when He seizes the towns while they do wrong.'"* (al-Bukhārī 4686; Muslim 2583)

**The Practical Urgency**

The verse converts eschatology into ethics: since the Day cannot be moved, the wise move themselves. Procrastination assumes negotiable deadlines; this deadline is not negotiable. The believer's response is the Prophet's ﷺ counsel:

> *"Hasten to deeds before trials like pieces of a dark night."* (Muslim 118)

Every "single hour" is therefore precious — not because the Day might come this hour, but because this hour might be the last granted before it.

## Sūrah Saba' 34:31

> **The disbelievers say, "We shall not believe in this Qur'an, nor in that which was before it." Couldst thou but see when the wrongdoers are made to stand before their Lord, hurling reproach upon one another! Those who had been weak and oppressed will say to those who had waxed arrogant, "If not for you, we would have been believers."**

**Expanded Commentary**

**Refusal of All Revelation**

*The disbelievers say: We shall not believe in this Qur'an, nor in that which was before it* — *wa qāla alladhīna kafarū lan nu'mina bi-hādhā al-Qur'āni wa lā bi-alladhī bayna yadayh*. The majority of commentators understand "that which was before it" as the Torah and Gospel; more generally, it covers every prior revelation. Al-Rāzī notes the comprehensiveness of the refusal: not ignorance of one book but rejection of all divine guidance — a closed posture, not an open inquiry. Having refused the messengers, the resurrection, and the signs, they now refuse the Scriptures that teach them. The Qur'an notes the same blanket denial elsewhere:

> *"And when it is said unto them: Believe in what God has sent down, they say: We believe in what was sent down upon us. And they disbelieve in what is beyond it, though it is the truth."* (Qur'an 2:91)

Here the denial is even broader: neither this nor that — nothing revealed, from any source, will be accepted.

**Couldst Thou But See: A Scene of Recrimination**

*Couldst thou but see when the wrongdoers are made to stand before their Lord, hurling reproach upon one another!* — *wa law tarā idh al-ẓālimūna mawqūfūna ʿinda rabbihim yarjiʿu baʿḍuhum ilā baʿḍin al-qawl*. The conditional sentence omits its conclusion — "thou wouldst behold a terrible sight" — a Qur'anic device for horrors beyond description (cf. 32:12). *Mawqūfūn* (made to stand, detained) depicts arrest: they are held for interrogation. And their first act is mutual blame — *yarjiʿu baʿḍuhum ilā baʿḍin al-qawl* (literally, "some of them return speech to others"): the alliance of denial collapses into a quarrel. The Qur'an stages this quarrel in several passages:

> *"And they shall dispute with one another in the Fire."* (Qur'an 40:47)

> *"Every time a community enters, it curses its sister."* (Qur'an 7:38)

**If Not for You: The Plea of the Followers**

*Those who had been weak and oppressed will say to those who had waxed arrogant: If not for you, we would have been believers* — *yaqūlu alladhīna us-tuḍʿifū li-alladhīna istakbarū law-lā antum la-kunnā mu'minīn*. The *mustaḍʿafūn* (the deemed-weak, the followers) blame the *mustakbirūn* (the arrogant leaders) who used wealth, status, and propaganda to block belief. There is partial truth in the charge — leaders bear real guilt for misguidance — but, as the next verses show, it is no excuse. The Qur'an distinguishes the truly helpless (4:97–99) from those who chose comfortable conformity:

> *"As for those whom the angels take while they wrong themselves, they say: In what were you? They say: We were oppressed in the land. They say: Was not God's earth spacious, that you might emigrate therein?"* (Qur'an 4:97)

The verse warns followers in every age: "I was only following" is spoken in Hell, not in Paradise.

---

## Sūrah Saba' 34:32

> **Those who waxed arrogant will say to those who had been weak and oppressed, "Did we turn you away from guidance after it had come unto you? Nay! But you were guilty."**

**Expanded Commentary**

**The Leaders' Rebuttal**

The arrogant reply with a counter-charge: *Did we turn you away from guidance after it had come unto you?* — *a-naḥnu ṣadadnākum ʿan al-hudā baʿda idh jā'akum*. Their defense has two limbs. First, compulsion is denied: we invited, we did not chain; guidance *came to you* — you heard the messengers as we did. Ibn Kathīr explains: the leaders argue they merely called, while the followers chose to answer. The same defense is recorded from Satan himself:

> *"And Satan will say, when the matter is decided: Truly God promised you the promise of truth, and I promised you, but I failed you. And I had no authority over you, save that I called you and you answered me. So blame me not, but blame yourselves."* (Qur'an 14:22)

**Nay! But You Were Guilty**

*Bal kuntum mujrimīn* — "Nay! But you were guilty." The leaders turn prosecutors: you followed us not from ignorance but from appetite — loving the world we offered, hating the restraint the messengers demanded. Al-Qurṭubī notes the painful truth both sides share: the leaders are guilty of misleading, the followers of following. Neither absolves the other. The Qur'an assigns double punishment accordingly:

> *"Our Lord, give them a twofold punishment and curse them with a mighty curse."* (Qur'an 33:68, the followers' plea against leaders)

> *"For each there is a twofold punishment, but you know not."* (Qur'an 7:38)

**The Mirror for Every Hierarchy**

The exchange judges every structure of influence — political, religious, cultural, corporate. Leaders cannot say "we only offered"; followers cannot say "we only obeyed." Each soul chose. The Prophet ﷺ drew the line precisely:

> *"There is no obedience to a creature in disobedience to the Creator."* (Aḥmad 1095, ṣaḥīḥ)

> *"Each of you is a shepherd, and each of you is responsible for his flock."* (al-Bukhārī 893; Muslim 1829)

The verse thus teaches followers to verify and leaders to fear: authority amplifies guilt; conformity does not erase it.

## Sūrah Saba' 34:33

> **Those who had been weak and oppressed will say to those who waxed arrogant, "Nay! But there was plotting by night and day when you ordered us to disbelieve in God, and to set up equals unto Him." And they will hide their remorse when they see the punishment, and We put shackles upon the necks of those who disbelieve. Will they be requited for aught but what they used to do?**

**Expanded Commentary**

**Plotting by Night and Day**

The followers answer: *Nay! But there was plotting by night and day* — *bal makru al-layli wa al-nahār*. *Makr* is scheming — patient, continuous deception. The leaders did not merely invite; they engineered an environment: propaganda by day, conspiracy by night, commanding disbelief (*ta'murūnanā an nakfura bi-Allāh*) and the fabrication of equals (*najʿala lahu andādan*). Ibn Kathīr explains: they confused the masses with doubts, beautified idolatry, threatened dissenters, and monopolized discourse. The Qur'an describes the machinery:

> *"And they plotted a mighty plot."* (Qur'an 71:22, of Noah's opposers)

> *"And when those who disbelieve plot against thee, to confine thee, or slay thee, or expel thee."* (Qur'an 8:30)

Modern readers recognize the instruments — captured media, bought scholars, social pressure, economic threats. The verse names them all *makr al-layl wa al-nahār*: a round-the-clock operation.

**They Will Hide Their Remorse**

*And they will hide their remorse when they see the punishment* — *wa asarrū al-nadāmata lammā ra'aw al-ʿadhāb*. Both parties — leaders and followers — conceal their regret (*asarrū* — to keep secret). Al-Rāzī offers several explanations: each hides remorse from the other out of shame; or they suppress it because avowal is useless; or they manifest it first (as in 32:12) then swallow it when hope is cut. The same phrase appears in:

> *"And they will hide their remorse when they see the punishment."* (Qur'an 10:54)

Regret in the Hereafter is the most useless of emotions — perfectly clear, perfectly late. The Prophet ﷺ said:

> *"Remorse is repentance."* (Ibn Mājah 4252, ṣaḥīḥ) — but only when remorse can still act, in this world.

**Shackles and Just Requital**

*And We put shackles upon the necks of those who disbelieve* — *wa jaʿalnā al-aghlāla fī aʿnāqi alladhīna kafarū*. The *aghlāl* (shackles, fetters binding hands to necks) are described elsewhere:

> *"Truly We have set shackles upon their necks, up to the chins, so that they are stiff-necked."* (Qur'an 36:8)

Al-Qurṭubī notes the correspondence: necks that stiffened in arrogance are stiffened in shackles. *Will they be requited for aught but what they used to do?* — *hal yujzawna illā mā kānū yaʿmalūn* — closes with the law of exact recompense:

> *"And you shall not be requited, save for that which you used to do."* (Qur'an 37:39)

Leaders and followers, schemers and conformists — each receives precisely his own earnings, neither more nor less.

---

## Sūrah Saba' 34:34

> **And We sent no warner unto a town, but that those living in luxury therein said, "We disbelieve in that wherewith you have been sent."**

**Expanded Commentary**

**The Universal Pattern of Elite Rejection**

*And We sent no warner unto a town, but that those living in luxury therein said: We disbelieve in that wherewith you have been sent* — *wa mā arsalnā fī qaryatin min nadhīrin illā qāla mutrafūhā innā bimā ursiltum bihi kāfirūn*. The *mutrafūn* — those steeped in luxury, the indulged elite — are named as the standard opponents of every warner. The same verse appears nearly verbatim in:

> *"And thus did We never send a warner before thee unto a town, but that those living in luxury therein said: Truly we found our fathers upon a community, and truly we follow in their footsteps."* (Qur'an 43:23)

Al-Ṭabarī explains the mechanism: luxury entrenches interest in the status quo; revelation threatens it with accountability, redistribution, and moral restraint. So the elite — *mala'* (notables) in other passages — lead denunciation in every prophetic story: Pharaoh's court against Moses, the chiefs against Noah, Hūd, Ṣāliḥ, and Shuʿayb, and the Quraysh aristocracy against Muhammad ﷺ.

**Why Luxury Opposes Revelation**

The Qur'an diagnoses the psychology:

> *"Nay, truly man exceeds all bounds, when he deems himself self-sufficient."* (Qur'an 96:6–7)

> *"And when We desire to destroy a town, We command those living in luxury therein, and they commit iniquity therein."* (Qur'an 17:16)

Wealth creates the illusion of self-sufficiency; self-sufficiency breeds resentment of a Law above oneself. Ibn Khaldūn later observed the civilizational pattern: luxury softens and corrupts dynasties from within. The Prophet ﷺ, though offered kingship and wealth to abandon his call, refused — proving the warner seeks no share of the luxury he condemns. His life of austerity amid the possibility of riches remains the standing refutation of the elite's charge that prophets seek power.

**A Warning to Every Age's Elite**

The verse is not against wealth but against the *mutraf* — the one whom wealth has made heedless, arrogant, and hostile to truth. Believing wealthy — Khadījah, ʿUthmān, ʿAbd al-Raḥmān ibn ʿAwf — are praised, not condemned. The criterion is response to warning: does prosperity open the ear or close it? Every comfortable class in every century must ask whether it resembles the *mutrafūn* of this verse — and every warner must expect that the fiercest opposition will come not from the poor but from the invested.

## Sūrah Saba' 34:35

> **And they say, "We are greater in wealth and children, and we shall not be punished."**

**Expanded Commentary**

**Wealth as Proof of Favor**

*And they say: We are greater in wealth and children* — *wa qālū naḥnu aktharu amwālan wa awlādan*. The elite's argument is quantitative: more money, more sons, more followers — therefore more favored by God, therefore immune. The Qur'an records the same boast from the owner of the two gardens:

> *"I am greater than thee in wealth and mightier in manpower."* (Qur'an 18:34)

And from the Quraysh, who despised the Prophet's ﷺ humble followers:

> *"Shall we believe you, when the lowliest follow you?"* (Qur'an 26:111)

Al-Rāzī exposes the fallacy: abundance measures God's *giving*, not God's *approval*. He gives to those He loves and those He does not — to Solomon and to Pharaoh, to ʿUthmān and to Qārūn. To read favor from fortune is to misread the test as the result.

**We Shall Not Be Punished: The False Immunity**

*And we shall not be punished* — *wa mā naḥnu bi-muʿadhdhabīn*. From the premise of favor they deduce immunity: God would not enrich those He intends to punish. The Qur'an quotes the reasoning and reverses it:

> *"Do they suppose that in the wealth and children We extend to them, We hasten unto them good things? Nay, but they perceive not."* (Qur'an 23:55–56)

> *"And let not their wealth or their children impress thee. God desires but to punish them thereby in the life of this world."* (Qur'an 9:55)

Ibn Kathīr notes the elite confused *immediate* favor with *ultimate* favor — like a student who mistakes the examiner's silence during the test for approval of his answers. The punishment deferred is not punishment cancelled; indeed, blessing unthanked becomes evidence against its recipient.

**The Modern Form of the Boast**

The boast survives in secular dress: developed nations deem their prosperity proof of their worldview; the rich deem their portfolios proof of their wisdom; the followed deem their followers proof of their truth. The verse answers all with one principle: numbers are not verdicts. The Prophet ﷺ said:

> *"God does not look at your forms or your wealth, but He looks at your hearts and your deeds."* (Muslim 2564)

---

## Sūrah Saba' 34:36

> **Say, "Truly my Lord outspreads and straitens provision for whomsoever He will, but most of mankind know not."**

**Expanded Commentary**

**Outspreading and Straitenting: The Divine Economy**

*Say: Truly my Lord outspreads and straitens provision for whomsoever He will* — *qul inna rabbī yabsuṭu al-rizqa li-man yashā'u wa yaqdir*. *Yabsuṭ* (outspreads, expands) and *yaqdir* (measures, straitens) describe God's two movements in provision: widening for some, narrowing for others — both by will and wisdom, not by merit alone. The formula recurs throughout the Qur'an (13:26; 17:30; 28:82; 29:62; 30:37; 39:52; 42:12), marking it as a foundational doctrine. Al-Ṭabarī explains: expansion is a test of gratitude, straitening a test of patience — neither is, in itself, honor or humiliation:

> *"As for man, when his Lord tries him and honors him and blesses him, he says: My Lord has honored me. But when He tries him and straitens his provision for him, he says: My Lord has humiliated me. Nay!"* (Qur'an 89:15–17)

**For Whomsoever He Will: Will Joined to Wisdom**

*Li-man yashā'* — "for whomsoever He will" — attributes distribution to divine will, not human claim. Ibn Taymiyyah emphasized that God's will is never arbitrary; it operates with wisdom that may be hidden from the recipient. Qārūn received treasure as a trial and failed; Job lost everything as a trial and passed. The same event — gain or loss — is blessing or curse depending on response. The Prophet ﷺ said:

> *"If the son of Adam had a valley of gold, he would wish for a second; and nothing fills his mouth but dust. And God relents unto whosoever repents."* (al-Bukhārī 6436; Muslim 1048)

**But Most of Mankind Know Not**

*Wa lākinna akthara al-nāsi lā yaʿlamūn* — most do not know this doctrine, so they misread every ledger: envying the expanded, despising the straitened, and judging themselves by balances. Al-Rāzī notes the verse's placement as a direct answer to verse 35: the elite claimed wealth proves immunity; God replies that wealth proves nothing but His will to test. The knower of this verse is freed from both arrogance in plenty and bitterness in poverty — seeing in each only a question posed by the Provider, awaiting the answer of gratitude or patience.

## Sūrah Saba' 34:37

> **It is not your wealth or your children that bring you nigh in nearness unto Us, save those who believe and work righteousness — theirs is a manifold reward for what they did, and they will be secure in lofty chambers.**

**Expanded Commentary**

**Neither Wealth nor Children Avail**

*It is not your wealth or your children that bring you nigh in nearness unto Us* — *wa mā amwālukum wa lā awlādukum bi-allatī tuqarribukum ʿindanā zulfā*. The verse severs the elite's equation explicitly: proximity (*zulfā*) to God is not purchased with money or multiplied with sons. The Day will expose the truth:

> *"The Day when neither wealth nor children avail, save for him who comes to God with a sound heart."* (Qur'an 26:88–89)

> *"Your property and your children are only a trial."* (Qur'an 8:28; 64:15)

Al-Qurṭubī notes the exception proves the rule: wealth *spent* righteously and children *raised* righteously do benefit — but then it is the righteousness that avails, not the wealth or the children themselves. The Prophet ﷺ said:

> *"When a man dies, his deeds cease except for three: ongoing charity, knowledge that benefits, or a righteous child who prays for him."* (Muslim 1631)

**Save Those Who Believe and Work Righteousness**

*Illā man āmana wa ʿamila ṣāliḥan* — the exception names the true currency: faith and righteous deeds. Grammatically excepted from wealth and children, the phrase means: only believers who work righteousness are brought near — and their nearness comes through belief and deeds, not possessions. Ibn Kathīr stresses that this demolishes both aristocratic pride (lineage avails nothing) and plutocratic pride (riches avail nothing). The Qur'an states the positive law:

> *"Truly the most noble of you before God is the most reverent of you."* (Qur'an 49:13)

**Manifold Reward in Lofty Chambers**

*Theirs is a manifold reward for what they did, and they will be secure in lofty chambers* — *fa-ulā'ika lahum jazā'u al-ḍiʿfi bimā ʿamilū wa hum fī al-ghurufāti āminūn*. *Ḍiʿf* (manifold, doubled) refers to the multiplication of good deeds — ten to seven hundred times and more:

> *"The likeness of those who spend their wealth in the way of God is as the likeness of a grain that sprouts seven ears, in each ear a hundred grains."* (Qur'an 2:261)

> *"Whosoever brings a good deed shall have tenfold the like thereof."* (Qur'an 6:160)

*Ghurufāt* (lofty chambers, upper rooms) are the highest dwellings of Paradise, described by the Prophet ﷺ:

> *"In Paradise there are chambers whose outside is seen from their inside and whose inside is seen from their outside, prepared by God for those who feed others, speak gently, fast continuously, and pray by night while men sleep."* (al-Tirmidhī 1984, ḥasan)

And they are *āminūn* (secure) — from death, fear, and loss. The elite sought security in wealth and were destroyed; believers find security in chambers wealth could never buy.

## Sūrah Saba' 34:38

> **And those who endeavor to thwart Our signs, they will be arraigned unto the punishment.**

**Expanded Commentary**

**The Return of the Strivers**

*And those who endeavor to thwart Our signs, they will be arraigned unto the punishment* — *wa alladhīna yasʿawna fī āyātinā muʿājizīna ulā'ika fī al-ʿadhābi muḥḍarūn*. This verse nearly repeats verse 5, forming a bracket around the intervening passage: the sūrah opened its polemic with the strivers' fate, developed the arguments (knowledge, provision, nearness), and now returns to seal the verdict. Al-Rāzī notes such repetition (*takrār*) in the Qur'an is never redundant; it re-anchors the reader after a long development. The *muʿājizūn* — those who imagine they can outstrip or frustrate God's signs — face the same end:

> *"And those who endeavor to thwart Our signs, those are the inhabitants of Hellfire."* (Qur'an 22:51)

**Arraigned unto the Punishment**

*Muḥḍarūn* (arraigned, presented, hauled in) depicts arrest and production before judgment. All mankind will be presented (*muḥḍarūn*) before God (36:32, 53), but only the strivers are *presented unto the punishment* (*fī al-ʿadhābi muḥḍarūn*) — delivered into it, as criminals are delivered into custody. Ibn Kathīr notes the contrast with verse 37's *āminūn* (secure): the righteous dwell in secure chambers; the strivers are secured in punishment. Security belongs to one group or the other — there is no third lodging.

**Why Repetition Here Is Mercy**

The reiteration serves the elite just addressed: after hearing that wealth and children avail nothing, they might still hope their opposition escapes notice. The verse answers: striving against the signs is itself the surest path to arraignment. Yet even here mercy glimmers — the verse speaks in the future tense, of what *will* be. The door of repentance stands open until presentation begins. The Prophet ﷺ said:

> *"God extends His Hand by night for the sinner of the day, and extends His Hand by day for the sinner of the night, until the sun rises from the west."* (Muslim 2759)

## Sūrah Saba' 34:39

> **Say, "Truly my Lord outspreads and straitens provision for whomsoever He will among His servants. Whatsoever you have spent, He will replace; and He is the best of providers."**

**Expanded Commentary**

**Provision Among His Servants**

*Say: Truly my Lord outspreads and straitens provision for whomsoever He will among His servants* — the formula of verse 36 returns with the addition *min ʿibādih* (among His servants). Al-Rāzī suggests the addition particularizes: here the address concerns believers and their spending — God's distribution *among His servants* includes the secret replenishment of charity. The return of the formula after the elite's boasts frames the whole passage: provision is His to widen and narrow, and no boast alters it.

**Whatsoever You Spend, He Will Replace**

*Whatsoever you have spent, He will replace* — *wa mā anfaqtum min shay'in fa-huwa yukhlifuh*. This is among the Qur'an's most beloved promises for givers. *Yukhlif* (replace, succeed) means God sends a successor (*khalaf*) to every charity: replacement in this world (opened doors, blessed remainder, averted calamity) and immeasurably in the next. The Prophet ﷺ conveyed God's own words:

> *"Spend, O son of Adam, and I shall spend on you."* (al-Bukhārī 5352; Muslim 993)

And he swore:

> *"Charity does not decrease wealth."* (Muslim 2588)

Ibn Kathīr notes the condition understood from context: spending *in God's way* — lawfully earned, sincerely given, without reminding or harm. Such spending never truly leaves the giver; it travels ahead of him.

**He Is the Best of Providers**

*And He is the best of providers* — *wa huwa khayru al-rāziqīn*. Men provide wages, parents provide children, states provide welfare — all are *rāziqīn* (providers) in a borrowed sense. God is the *best* of them: His provision is inexhaustible, need-free, precise, and constant. The phrase appears also in:

> *"And provide for us, and Thou art the best of providers."* (Qur'an 5:114)

> *"And truly thy Lord is the best of providers."* (Qur'an 62:11, in some renderings)

Al-Tustarī (d. 283/896) reportedly distinguished two provisions: provision of bodies (food, drink) and provision of spirits (remembrance, knowledge) — the latter being the angels' own sustenance. The verse's promise covers both: the giver in God's way finds his purse and his heart alike replenished.

---

## Sūrah Saba' 34:40

> **Upon the Day when He will gather them all together, then He shall say unto the angels, "Were these the ones worshipping you?"**

**Expanded Commentary**

**The Gathering of All**

*Upon the Day when He will gather them all together* — *wa yawma yaḥshuruhum jamīʿan*. *Ḥashr* (gathering, mustering) is the resurrection-assembly: all — worshippers and worshipped — herded onto one plain. The pronoun covers the idolaters (and, in the fullest reading, all mankind), while the angels are summoned as witnesses. Ibn Kathīr notes the scene's purpose: public vindication of truth and public exposure of falsehood, before all parties at once. The Qur'an stages parallel interrogations:

> *"And on the Day He gathers them and that which they worship apart from God, He will say: Was it you who caused these servants of Mine to go astray, or did they themselves go astray from the way?"* (Qur'an 25:17)

> *"And when God shall say: O Jesus son of Mary, didst thou say unto mankind: Take me and my mother as two gods apart from God?"* (Qur'an 5:116)

**Why the Angels Are Questioned**

*Then He shall say unto the angels: Were these the ones worshipping you?* — *thumma yaqūlu li-al-malā'ikati a-hā'ulā'i iyyākum kānū yaʿbudūn*. Al-Rāzī explains: the angels are questioned because they are the highest of the beings falsely worshipped — some Arabs worshipped them as "daughters of God." Questioning the highest answers for the lowest: if even angels disown worship, how much more the stones and jinn? God asks not for information — He knows — but for testimony: to establish the record before the accused, and to honor the angels with public exoneration.

**Worship They Never Sought**

The question exposes the tragedy of misdirected devotion: worship — the most precious of human acts — poured out upon those who never asked for it and never accepted it. The angels, who themselves worship God unceasingly:

> *"They glorify Him night and day, and they flag not."* (Qur'an 21:20)

— stand accused of receiving what they abhor. The verse warns every age against venerating the righteous into divinity: the saints, like the angels, will disown the worship of their venerators. True love of the righteous is shown by following them to God, not by stopping at them instead of God.

## Sūrah Saba' 34:41

> **They will reply, "Glory be to Thee! Thou art our Protector, apart from them!" Nay, they worshipped jinn, most of them believing in them.**

**Expanded Commentary**

**Glory Be to Thee: The Angels' Exoneration**

*They will reply: Glory be to Thee!* — *qālū subḥānak*. *Subḥān* (glory, transcendence) is the angels' first word — declaring God far above partners before defending themselves. Then: *Thou art our Protector, apart from them* — *anta waliyyunā min dūnihim*: our allegiance (*walāyah*) is to Thee alone; we have no bond with these worshippers. Al-Ṭabarī explains: the angels sever every tie — no consent, no kinship, no partnership. Their disowning fulfills the Qur'anic law:

> *"And on the Day of Resurrection they will disown your associating."* (Qur'an 35:14)

**They Worshipped Jinn**

*Nay, they worshipped jinn* — *bal kānū yaʿbudūna al-jinn*. The angels redirect the charge: the real objects (and orchestrators) of this worship were the jinn — devils who beautified idolatry, whispered its rites, and inhabited its shrines. Ibn Kathīr explains: the idols were fronts; the jinn were the agents behind them, deceiving men into prostration. The Qur'an confirms the alliance:

> *"And they have set up a kinship between Him and the jinn, though the jinn know well that they shall be arraigned."* (Qur'an 37:158)

*Most of them believing in them* — *aktharuhum bihim mu'minūn*: most of the worshippers believed in the jinn — trusting their whispers, fearing their harm, seeking their favor. Al-Qurṭubī notes the bitter irony of the word "believing": faith, meant for God, was squandered on devils.

**The Anatomy of False Worship**

The verse dissects idolatry into three layers: the worshippers (men), the fronts (idols, images, revered names), and the agents (jinn who inspire). Modern idolatries follow the same anatomy: fronts of ideology, celebrity, or nation; agents of whispering interest; worshippers surrendering judgment. The angels' testimony teaches the only safe *walāyah* (protectorship): God's alone. The Prophet ﷺ said:

> *"The likeness of one who remembers his Lord and one who does not is as the living and the dead."* (al-Bukhārī 6407; Muslim 779)

— for remembrance binds the heart to the true Protector, leaving no room for the jinn's tenancy.

---

## Sūrah Saba' 34:42

> **And on that Day no one of you will have power over what benefit or harm may come to the other, and We shall say to those who do wrong, "Taste the punishment of the Fire that you used to deny!"**

**Expanded Commentary**

**No Power to Benefit or Harm**

*And on that Day no one of you will have power over what benefit or harm may come to the other* — *fa-al-yawma lā yamliku baʿḍukum li-baʿḍin nafʿan wa lā ḍarran*. The mutual aid the idolaters expected — gods aiding worshippers, worshippers honoring gods — evaporates. Al-Rāzī notes the verse can be read as addressed to angels and worshippers (the angels cannot shield their false devotees) or to the deniers among themselves (leaders cannot shield followers). Both are true, and both fulfill the general law:

> *"A Day when no soul will avail another soul in any way."* (Qur'an 2:48; 82:19)

> *"They have no power of intercession, save the one who has made a pact with the Compassionate."* (Qur'an 19:87)

The networks of patronage — the very thing idolatry promised — dissolve exactly when needed.

**Taste the Punishment: The Sentence Pronounced**

*And We shall say to those who do wrong: Taste the punishment of the Fire that you used to deny!* — *wa naqūlu li-alladhīna ẓalamū dhūqū ʿadhāba al-nār allatī kuntum bihā tukadhdhibūn*. *Dhūqū* (taste) — the punishment is made sensory, inescapable as flavor on the tongue. *Allatī kuntum bihā tukadhdhibūn* (that you used to deny) — the sentence names the crime: denial. They denied the Fire; now they taste it. The Qur'an repeats this sentencing formula:

> *"Taste the punishment of burning."* (Qur'an 3:181; 8:50; 22:9)

Al-Qurṭubī observes the correspondence: the mouth that denied now tastes; the certainty they mocked now envelops them. *Ẓalamū* (who do wrong) here means specifically the wrong of *shirk* — the supreme injustice of giving worship to other than its Owner:

> *"Truly associating is a mighty wrong."* (Qur'an 31:13)

**The Collapse of Every False Patronage**

The verse generalizes beyond idols: every reliance other than God — wealth, connections, status, self — will speak this same bankruptcy on that Day. Only one patronage endures:

> *"God is the Protector of those who believe; He brings them out of darkness into light."* (Qur'an 2:257)

The wise therefore transfer their reliance in advance, while transfer is still possible.

## Sūrah Saba' 34:43

> **And when Our signs are recited unto them as clear proofs, they say, "This is naught but a man who desires to turn you from that which your fathers used to worship." And they say, "This is naught but a fabricated perversion." And those who disbelieve say to the Truth when it comes to them, "This is naught but manifest sorcery."**

**Expanded Commentary**

**Clear Proofs Met with Three Charges**

*And when Our signs are recited unto them as clear proofs* — *wa idhā tutlā ʿalayhim āyātunā bayyināt*. *Bayyināt* (clear, self-evident proofs) — the recitation is luminous; the response is triple darkness. Al-Ṭabarī notes this pattern recurs wherever the Qur'an is recited to entrenched deniers (8:31; 19:73; 22:72; 31:7; 46:7). Three charges follow, each revealing a distinct fear.

**Charge One: He Would Sever You from Your Fathers**

*"This is naught but a man who desires to turn you from that which your fathers used to worship"* — *mā hādhā illā rajulun yurīdu an yaṣuddakum ʿammā kāna yaʿbudu ābā'ukum*. The first charge appeals to ancestry: the Prophet ﷺ is reduced to "a man" (*rajul*, as in v. 7), and his mission to filial betrayal. Al-Rāzī exposes the fallacy: truth is not inherited; if fathers erred, imitating them perpetuates error. The Qur'an confronts ancestor-worship directly:

> *"And when it is said unto them: Follow what God has sent down, they say: Nay, we follow that wherein we found our fathers. What! Even if their fathers understood naught and were not guided?"* (Qur'an 2:170)

Every reformer faces this charge; every reform requires answering it.

**Charges Two and Three: Fabrication and Sorcery**

*"This is naught but a fabricated perversion"* — *mā hādhā illā ifkun muftarā*: *ifk* (perversion, inversion) — they call monotheism itself a deviation, inverting the charge (cf. 37:86; 46:22). *"This is naught but manifest sorcery"* — *mā hādhā illā siḥrun mubīn*: the Qur'an's effect on hearts — its power to move, convince, and transform — is attributed to magic rather than truth. Ibn Kathīr notes the contradiction: the same Book is called both human fabrication (their charge two) and superhuman sorcery (their charge three) — it cannot be both, and the contradiction betrays bad faith. The Qur'an answers:

> *"And it is not the speech of a poet; little do you believe. Nor the speech of a soothsayer; little do you reflect. A revelation from the Lord of the worlds."* (Qur'an 69:41–43)

The three charges survive today as: "you betray tradition," "you invent extremism," "you brainwash" — leveled at every faithful call. The verse forewarns the caller and forearms him.

## Sūrah Saba' 34:44

> **Though We have not given them any books that they study; nor have We sent them a warner before thee.**

**Expanded Commentary**

**No Books They Study: The Baselessness of Their Claims**

*Though We have not given them any books that they study* — *wa mā ātaynāhum min kutubin yadrusūnahā*. The deniers spoke with authority about God, the Unseen, and the Prophet's ﷺ status — yet they possessed no revealed scripture supporting their claims. Al-Ṭabarī explains: their assertions about partners, intercessors, and denial proceed from conjecture, not revelation. The Qur'an presses the challenge elsewhere:

> *"Or did We give them a book, such that they stand upon a clear proof from it?"* (Qur'an 35:40)

> *"Bring me a book before this, or some trace of knowledge, if you are truthful."* (Qur'an 46:4)

> *"Or have you a book wherein you study, that you shall have whatsoever you choose?"* (Qur'an 68:37–38)

Ibn Kathīr notes the force: the party with a Book (the Prophet ﷺ) is accused of fabrication by the party with no book at all. The burden of evidence lies entirely on the accusers, and they carry nothing.

**Nor a Warner Before Thee**

*Nor have We sent them a warner before thee* — *wa mā arsalnā ilayhim qablaka min nadhīr*. The Arabs of the Prophet's ﷺ generation had received no direct messenger in the immediate past — Ishmael's legacy had faded into idolatry over the centuries. Al-Qurṭubī draws two implications. First, this magnifies the Prophet's ﷺ favor: a people long unwarned are now personally addressed. Second, it removes their excuse going forward: unwarned yesterday, they are warned today. The Qur'an states the principle:

> *"And We never punish until We send a messenger."* (Qur'an 17:15)

**Ignorance Compounded by Arrogance**

The verse diagnoses a compound disease: ignorance (no books, no warner) plus arrogance (confident denunciation). Ignorance alone is treatable — the student learns. Arrogance alone is treatable — the humbled submit. But ignorant arrogance — speaking authoritatively without knowledge — resists both cures. The Prophet ﷺ warned:

> *"Whoever is given a legal verdict without knowledge, his sin is upon the one who gave it."* (Abū Dāwūd 3657; Ibn Mājah 53, ḥasan)

The verse counsels every speaker on sacred matters: cite your book, name your warner — or hold your tongue.

## Sūrah Saba' 34:45

> **And those before them denied; and these have not attained a tenth of that which We gave them; yet they denied My messengers. How, then, was the change I wrought!**

**Expanded Commentary**

**Those Before Them Denied**

*And those before them denied* — *wa kadhdhaba alladhīna min qablihim*. Noah's people, ʿĀd, Thamūd, Pharaoh's host — the roll of deniers stretches back through every messenger's story. The verse places the Quraysh in this lineage: their denial is unoriginal, their arguments recycled, their fate precedented. The Qur'an frequently invokes this history as evidence:

> *"Have they not journeyed in the land and seen how was the end of those before them? They were mightier than them in strength."* (Qur'an 30:9; 40:82)

The ruins of Arabia — Madā'in Ṣāliḥ, the dam of Ma'rib — stood as open-air museums of denial's end, visible to the Prophet's ﷺ contemporaries on their trade routes.

**Not a Tenth of What We Gave Them**

*And these have not attained a tenth of that which We gave them* — *wa mā balaghū miʿshāra mā ātaynāhum*. *Miʿshār* (a tenth, a tithe) — the present deniers possess not one-tenth of the power, wealth, longevity, stature, and civilization granted their predecessors. ʿĀd raised pillars unmatched (89:6–8); Thamūd carved homes in mountains (7:74); Pharaoh commanded the Nile (43:51). Al-Rāzī draws the argument: if God destroyed those titans for denying His messengers, what protects these weaker deniers? The logic is *a fortiori* — from greater to lesser:

> *"Are your disbelievers better than those? Or have you an immunity in the scriptures?"* (Qur'an 54:43)

The verse humbles every age that imagines its technology exempts it: ʿĀd too thought their strength exempted them (*"Who is mightier than us in strength?"* — 41:15).

**How Was the Change I Wrought!**

*Yet they denied My messengers. How, then, was the change I wrought!* — *fa-kadhdhabū rusulī fa-kayfa kāna nakīr*. *Nakīr* means both denial-requital and transformative punishment — the "change" (from blessing to ruin) that God wrought upon them. The same exclamation recurs:

> *"And how was My reproach!"* (Qur'an 22:44; 35:26; 67:18)

Al-Qurṭubī notes the question is rhetorical and terrible: look at the ruins and answer. The verse ends the history lesson with an assignment: visit the tells, read the strata, and compute the odds of denial succeeding now when it never succeeded then.

## Sūrah Saba' 34:46

> **Say, "I exhort you unto one thing only, that you stand for God in pairs or singly and then reflect. There is no madness in your companion. He is naught but a warner unto you before a severe punishment."**

**Expanded Commentary**

**One Thing Only: The Simplicity of the Call**

*Say: I exhort you unto one thing only* — *qul innamā aʿiẓukum bi-wāḥidah*. After the long polemic, the Prophet ﷺ reduces his appeal to a single counsel (*wāḥidah* — one thing). Al-Bayḍāwī and al-Zamakhsharī praise the rhetoric: amid clamor, simplicity pierces. The "one thing" is not a doctrine to memorize but a procedure to follow — a method of honest inquiry. The verse thus offers the Qur'an's own epistemology: truth is found not by crowds or ancestors but by sincere, undistracted reflection.

**Stand for God in Pairs or Singly**

*That you stand for God in pairs or singly and then reflect* — *an taqūmū li-Allāhi mathnā wa furādā thumma tatafakkarū*. *Taqūmū li-Allāh* (stand/rise up for God) means to devote oneself sincerely, free of caprice, tribalism, and imitation (*taqlīd*). *Mathnā wa furādā* (in pairs or singly) — discuss with one trusted companion, or ponder alone — but away from the mob, whose passions cloud judgment. Al-Rāzī and Ibn Kathīr explain: crowds generate conformity; solitary or paired reflection permits honesty. The same counsel underlies:

> *"Have they not reflected? There is no madness in their companion."* (Qur'an 7:184)

Modern psychology confirms the wisdom: group pressure distorts perception (as the Asch experiments showed), while structured reflection restores it. The Qur'an prescribed the remedy fourteen centuries early: step out of the crowd to find the truth the crowd hides.

**No Madness in Your Companion**

*There is no madness in your companion* — *mā bi-ṣāḥibikum min jinnah*. *Ṣāḥibukum* (your companion) — the Prophet ﷺ is no stranger; they knew his forty years of truthfulness, earning him the title *al-Amīn* (the Trustworthy). Madness (*jinnah*) produces incoherence, inconsistency, and self-harm; his life shows coherence, consistency, and self-sacrifice. Honest reflection on the known man refutes the strange charge. *He is naught but a warner unto you before a severe punishment* — *in huwa illā nadhīrun lakum bayna yaday ʿadhābin shadīd*: his function is warning, his timing merciful (*before* the punishment, while escape remains). The verse closes the case for the Prophet's ﷺ sanity with an appeal no honest mind can refuse: examine the man you know, by the method that works, before the Day that warns.

## Sūrah Saba' 34:47

> **Say, "I ask not of you any reward; that shall be yours. My reward lies only with God, and He is Witness over all things."**

**Expanded Commentary**

**No Reward Asked: The Proof of Sincerity**

*Say: I ask not of you any reward* — *qul mā sa'altukum min ajrin*. Every true messenger speaks these words. Noah, Hūd, Ṣāliḥ, Lot, and Shuʿayb each declared:

> *"And I ask of you no reward for it; my reward lies only with the Lord of the worlds."* (Qur'an 26:109, 127, 145, 164, 180)

The Prophet Muhammad ﷺ is commanded to the same disclaimer in multiple places (6:90; 25:57; 38:86; 42:23). Ibn Kathīr explains the logic: a fabricator seeks wages — wealth, status, or power. The Prophet ﷺ sought none: he refused the Quraysh's offers of kingship and riches, lived in a clay room, and died with his armor pawned for grain (al-Bukhārī 2916). *That shall be yours* — *fa-huwa lakum* (literally, "it is for you"): even if there were a reward, keep it — I want nothing of yours. The disclaimer converts his poverty from an embarrassment (as the elite insinuated) into evidence.

**My Reward Lies Only with God**

*My reward lies only with God* — *in ajriya illā ʿalā Allāh*. The warner's wage is drawn on heaven, not on earth — and therefore his message cannot be bought, threatened, or flattered into silence. Al-Qurṭubī notes this frees the caller from his audience: needing nothing from men, he fears nothing from men. The Prophet ﷺ lived the principle:

> *"Say: I ask of you no reward for it, save affection among kinsfolk"* (Qur'an 42:23) — even this, Ibn ʿAbbās explained, was not payment but the plea not to sever kinship.

The verse sets the standard for every religious teacher: the moment the call becomes a career, its credibility bleeds. Sincerity (*ikhlāṣ*) is not decoration but authentication.

**He Is Witness over All Things**

*And He is Witness over all things* — *wa huwa ʿalā kulli shay'in shahīd*. *Shahīd* (Witness, Present Observer) — God observes the warner's sincerity and the deniers' rejection alike. The same Name appears in:

> *"Truly God is Witness over all things."* (Qur'an 4:33; 5:117; 22:17; 58:6)

Al-Rāzī notes the comfort and the threat: comfort, because no sincere effort escapes His sight; threat, because no secret motive escapes it either. The verse ends the Prophet's ﷺ self-defense (sanity in v. 46, sincerity in v. 47) with the only Witness whose testimony finally matters.

## Sūrah Saba' 34:48

> **Say, "Truly my Lord doth cast the truth, Knower of things unseen."**

**Expanded Commentary**

**He Casts the Truth: The Image of Overwhelming**

*Say: Truly my Lord doth cast the truth* — *qul inna rabbī yaqdhifu bi-al-ḥaqq*. The verb *yaqdhif* (casts, hurls, flings) depicts truth as a projectile launched by God — forceful, directed, irresistible. The same verb describes truth's crushing of falsehood:

> *"Nay, We cast truth against falsehood, and it crushes it, and behold, it vanishes."* (Qur'an 21:18)

> *"Say: Truth has come, and falsehood has vanished. Truly falsehood is ever vanishing."* (Qur'an 17:81)

Al-Rāzī offers two complementary readings: God casts truth *into the hearts* of believers (illumination from within) and casts truth *against falsehood* (vindication from without). Both are divine acts: the believer's conviction and the argument's victory alike descend from above. The idolaters' whisperings, however industrious, are pebbles against a catapult.

**Knower of Things Unseen**

*Knower of things unseen* — *ʿallām al-ghuyūb* (literally, "Superb Knower of the unseen things" — the intensive form). The epithet appears in several verses (5:109, 116; 9:78; 34:48; 49:18 in variant), always where hidden counsels are exposed. Al-Ṭabarī explains: God casts truth effectively because He knows every hidden thing — the deniers' secret plots, the believers' secret sincerity, the Hour's hidden date. Nothing in the unseen realm frustrates His casting:

> *"And with Him are the keys of the Unseen; none knows them but He."* (Qur'an 6:59)

**The Confidence of the Caller**

The verse arms the Prophet ﷺ — and every caller after him — with complete confidence: you do not manufacture truth's victory; you witness it. Your task is conveyance; the casting is God's. Opposition, however organized, cannot intercept a projectile launched by the Knower of the Unseen. The Prophet ﷺ promised the outcome:

> *"Truth shall remain manifest, and those who oppose it shall not harm it, until God's command comes."* (Muslim 1920, paraphrased from the "victorious group" narrations)

## Sūrah Saba' 34:49

> **Say, "Truth has come, and falsehood originates naught, nor restores."**

**Expanded Commentary**

**Truth Has Come: The Arrival Announced**

*Say: Truth has come* — *qul jā'a al-ḥaqq*. The perfect tense announces a completed arrival: with the Qur'an and the Prophet ﷺ, truth is no longer awaited — it has entered history. The same proclamation appears in:

> *"Say: Truth has come, and falsehood has vanished. Truly falsehood is ever vanishing."* (Qur'an 17:81)

— words the Prophet ﷺ himself recited on the day of the conquest of Makkah, striking the idols around the Kaʿbah with his staff as each fell (al-Bukhārī 2478; Muslim 1781). Ibn Kathīr notes the verse's tense carries certainty: truth's coming guarantees falsehood's departure, as dawn guarantees the night's retreat.

**Falsehood Originates Naught, nor Restores**

*And falsehood originates naught, nor restores* — *wa mā yubdi'u al-bāṭilu wa mā yuʿīd*. *Al-bāṭil* (falsehood — idols, lies, devils) can neither *begin* creation (*yubdi'*) nor *repeat* it (*yuʿīd*, i.e., resurrect). The phrase looks back to verses 40–42: the false gods create nothing and resurrect no one. Al-Rāzī explains: origination and restoration are the two signatures of divinity — beginning life and returning it. Falsehood forges neither. The Qur'an states the criterion:

> *"But they have taken gods apart from Him, who create naught and are themselves created, and who have no power over what harm or benefit may come to themselves, and who have no power over death, or life, or resurrection."* (Qur'an 25:3)

**The Sterility of Falsehood**

Beyond theology, the verse states a law of unreality: falsehood is sterile. It imitates, corrupts, and consumes — but it does not create or renew. False ideologies produce no lasting civilization; lying souls produce no lasting peace; idolized powers produce no lasting salvation. Truth alone *originates* (new life, new hope, new community) and *restores* (repentance, revival, resurrection). The believer, aligned with truth, shares its fertility; the follower of falsehood shares its barrenness — busy, perhaps, but bringing forth nothing.

## Sūrah Saba' 34:50

> **Say, "If I have gone astray, I have only gone astray to my own loss. And if I am rightly guided, it is through that which my Lord reveals unto me. Truly He is Hearing, Near."**

**Expanded Commentary**

**If I Have Gone Astray: The Fair Concession**

*Say: If I have gone astray, I have only gone astray to my own loss* — *qul in ḍalaltu fa-innamā aḍillu ʿalā nafsī*. The Prophet ﷺ is commanded to a stunning fairness: grant, for argument's sake, the possibility of his own error — and note its consequence falls on himself alone. He claims no privilege, no immunity from the law of requital. Al-Qurṭubī praises the humility: the infallible messenger speaks as if fallible, to model accountability. The principle is universal:

> *"Whosoever is guided is guided only for his own soul, and whosoever goes astray, goes astray only against it."* (Qur'an 17:15)

No leader's error excuses his followers, and no follower's error excuses his leader — each soul carries its own straying.

**If I Am Guided: It Is by Revelation**

*And if I am rightly guided, it is through that which my Lord reveals unto me* — *wa in ihtadaytu fa-bimā yūḥī ilayya rabbī*. Guidance, unlike error, is not self-generated: error I could produce alone, but guidance comes only *by what my Lord reveals*. Ibn Taymiyyah made this verse a cornerstone of his epistemology: sound reason confirms revelation, but revelation, not unaided reason, is the source of sure guidance. The Prophet ﷺ, the wisest of men, attributes his guidance entirely to *waḥy* (revelation) — humbling every thinker who imagines philosophy alone suffices. The Qur'an confirms:

> *"And thus have We revealed unto thee a Spirit of Our Command. Thou knewest not what the Book was, nor faith."* (Qur'an 42:52)

**Truly He Is Hearing, Near**

*Truly He is Hearing, Near* — *innahu samīʿun qarīb*. This is the only verse joining *Samīʿ* (Hearing) with *Qarīb* (Near) — elsewhere the Hearing is paired with the Knowing or the Seeing. Al-Rāzī savors the pairing: He *hears* (every supplication, every argument, every whisper of this debate) and He is *near* (to answer, to aid, to judge). The verse evokes the nearness of God to the supplicant:

> *"When My servants ask thee about Me, truly I am near. I answer the call of the caller when he calls Me."* (Qur'an 2:186)

> *"And We are nearer to him than his jugular vein."* (Qur'an 50:16)

The sūrah's long argument thus ends its doctrinal movement on an intimate note: the God who casts truth and restores creation is not distant — He hears the debate and stands near its honest seeker.

## Sūrah Saba' 34:51

> **Couldst thou but see when they are terrified and there is no escape, and they are seized from a place near at hand.**

**Expanded Commentary**

**Couldst Thou But See: Terror Beyond Description**

*Couldst thou but see when they are terrified* — *wa law tarā idh faziʿū*. The conditional again omits its conclusion — "thou wouldst behold a dreadful sight" — for the terror (*fazaʿ*) of the Resurrection defies description. The same verb describes the blast of the Trumpet:

> *"And on the Day the Trumpet is blown, all who are in the heavens and on the earth shall be terrified, save whomsoever God wills."* (Qur'an 27:87)

Ibn Kathīr explains: this is the terror of the deniers at the resurrection, or at death, or at the moment of punishment — the sudden realization that the mocked promise is true. The Prophet ﷺ described the gathering:

> *"You shall be gathered barefoot, naked, and uncircumcised."* (al-Bukhārī 6527; Muslim 2859)

— and terror will seize every soul that arrives unprepared.

**No Escape: The Closure of Every Exit**

*And there is no escape* — *fa-lā fawt*. *Fawt* means to escape, outstrip, or elude. On that Day, flight is impossible: no distance outruns God, no cave hides from Him, no intercessor delays Him. The Qur'an depicts the futile search:

> *"Upon that Day man shall say: Whither to flee? Nay! No refuge! Unto thy Lord that Day is the recourse."* (Qur'an 75:10–12)

Al-Rāzī notes the contrast with earthly escapes — the deniers eluded messengers, mocked warnings, and fled admonition. The Day reverses every evasion: the pursued becomes the surrounded.

**Seized from a Place Near at Hand**

*And they are seized from a place near at hand* — *wa ukhidhū min makānin qarīb*. Seizure (*akhdh*) from *nearby* emphasizes inescapability: they are caught as if by a hand at their shoulder. Ibn Kathīr explains: they are seized immediately upon rising from their graves — no head start, no delay. Some commentators connect this to the earthly seizure of Badr or of doomed nations; the majority read it as the Hereafter's grasp. Either way, the theology is one: God's reach is never far:

> *"And He is with you wheresoever you may be."* (Qur'an 57:4)

The verse leaves the denier with the sūrah's starkest image: terrified, trapless, and taken — from close at hand.

## Sūrah Saba' 34:52

> **And they will say, "We believe in it." But how can they attain from a place far off.**

**Expanded Commentary**

**We Believe in It: The Late Confession**

*And they will say: We believe in it* — *wa qālū āmannā bih*. "It" (*bih*) — the truth, the Qur'an, the Prophet ﷺ, the resurrection, God Himself: the pronoun's breadth covers all they denied. Confronted with the Fire, the deniers finally confess. The Qur'an records the same belated faith from Pharaoh:

> *"I believe that there is no god but He in whom the Children of Israel believe, and I am of those who submit."* (Qur'an 10:90)

— and its rejection:

> *"Now? When thou hadst disobeyed before and wast of those who work corruption?"* (Qur'an 10:91)

Ibn Kathīr explains: faith at the sight of punishment is compulsion, not conviction — the shade of belief without its substance. Genuine faith believes the Unseen *while* it is unseen; to "believe" once veils are torn is merely to acknowledge the obvious.

**How Can They Attain from a Place Far Off?**

*But how can they attain from a place far off?* — *wa annā lahum al-tanāwushu min makānin baʿīd*. *Tanāwush* (attaining, reaching for) depicts grasping at something: they reach for faith, repentance, and return — but *from a place far off* (*min makānin baʿīd*): the Hereafter is infinitely distant from the world of action. Al-Rāzī explains: the marketplace of deeds was the worldly life; having left it, they stand too far to buy. The Qur'an states the closure:

> *"Until, when death comes to one of them, he says: My Lord, send me back, that I may work righteousness in that which I left behind. Nay! It is but a word he speaks."* (Qur'an 23:99–100)

The image is of a traveler who ignored the provisions shop, then stretches his hand toward it from miles down a waterless road.

**The Urgency of Nearness**

The verse's question answers itself: they cannot. Nearness was available — God was *Near* (v. 50), the warner was *before* the punishment (v. 46), the signs were *clear proofs* (v. 43) — and they chose farness. Now farness is fixed. The Prophet ﷺ said:

> *"God accepts the repentance of the servant so long as the death-rattle has not begun."* (al-Tirmidhī 3537; Ibn Mājah 4253, ḥasan)

The verse is the sūrah's final call to attain while attaining is near — before distance becomes doom.

## Sūrah Saba' 34:53

> **When they had disbelieved in it beforehand, while impugning the unseen from a place far off?**

**Expanded Commentary**

**Disbelief Beforehand: The Record Cited**

*When they had disbelieved in it beforehand* — *wa qad kafarū bihi min qabl*. The late confession is weighed against the long record: a lifetime of denial (*min qabl* — before, in the world) against a moment of compelled assent. Al-Ṭabarī notes the legal precision: judgment considers the whole file, not the final outburst. The guilty themselves will admit the record:

> *"Our Lord! We have seen and we have heard; so send us back that we may work righteousness. Truly we are certain."* (Qur'an 32:12)

— certainty at last, but certainty that arrives after the examination ends. Ibn Kathīr adds: their present "belief" does not erase their past *kufr*, because it is not repentance but reaction — the reflex of the caught, not the return of the penitent.

**Impugning the Unseen from Afar**

*While impugning the unseen from a place far off* — *wa yaqdhifūna bi-al-ghaybi min makānin baʿīd*. The verb *yaqdhifūn* (they cast, hurl) is the same root as God's *yaqdhif* (He casts the truth) in verse 48 — a deliberate antithesis noted by al-Rāzī: God casts truth from nearness and knowledge; they cast conjectures about the Unseen from farness and ignorance. *Yaqdhifūna bi-al-ghayb* (casting at the unseen) is an Arabic idiom for wild guessing — throwing stones at a target one cannot see. Their denials of resurrection, mockery of promise, and doctrines of idols were all blind throws. The Qur'an describes the method:

> *"They follow naught but conjecture, and conjecture avails nothing against truth."* (Qur'an 53:28)

> *"And they have no knowledge thereof; they follow naught but conjecture."* (Qur'an 45:24)

**The Contrast of Two Castings**

The sūrah thus closes its polemic with a symmetry: two castings, two distances, two outcomes. God, the Knower of the Unseen, casts truth that crushes falsehood. Men, ignorant of the Unseen, cast conjectures that return upon their own heads. The verse asks every mocker of revelation: from what distance do you throw? With what knowledge do you aim? The honest answer — far, and none — is itself the beginning of guidance.

## Sūrah Saba' 34:54

> **And a barrier is set between them and that which they desire, as was done with the likes of them before. Truly they were confounded by doubt.**

**Expanded Commentary**

**A Barrier Between Them and Desire**

*And a barrier is set between them and that which they desire* — *wa ḥīla baynahum wa bayna mā yashtahūn*. *Ḥīla* (a barrier is interposed, a separation is made) — passive, absolute, divine. *Mā yashtahūn* (that which they desire): commentators list the candidates — return to the world to repent (al-Rāzī), repentance itself (Ibn Kathīr), the delights of Paradise, or even the worldly luxuries they once enjoyed. Al-Qurṭubī embraces them all: every desire of the damned — to go back, to make amends, to taste joy — is walled off. The Qur'an depicts the same wall elsewhere:

> *"And between them and it there is a barrier."* (cf. Qur'an 41:5; 17:45 for the worldly veil; and 57:13 for the wall on Judgment Day)

The ultimate punishment is thus not only pain but privation: to want forever what one can never have — the exact inversion of Paradise, where:

> *"Therein they shall have whatsoever they desire."* (Qur'an 41:31)

**As Was Done with the Likes of Them Before**

*As was done with the likes of them before* — *kamā fuʿila bi-ashyāʿihim min qabl*. *Ashyāʿ* (likes, counterparts, parties) — Pharaoh's host, ʿĀd, Thamūd, Saba' itself: each desired reprieve at the end and found the door shut. Al-Ṭabarī notes the consolation for the Prophet ﷺ and the warning for his opponents: the pattern never varies. History is not a series of exceptions but a single law, applied without favoritism:

> *"The wont of God with those who passed before, and thou shalt never find alteration in the wont of God."* (Qur'an 33:62)

Every Saba' that chose ingratitude met the barrier; every denier who demanded "when" met the Day. The present generation is not exempt from the precedents it resembles.

**Confounded by Doubt: The Final Diagnosis**

*Truly they were confounded by doubt* — *innahum kānū fī shakk in murīb*. *Shakk murīb* (disquieting, agitating doubt) — not honest questioning that seeks answers, but corrosive doubt that refuses them: doubting the messengers, the Hour, the Book, while certain only in denial. The same diagnosis appears in:

> *"Truly they were in disquieting doubt."* (Qur'an 11:62, 110; 14:9; 41:45; 42:14)

Al-Rāzī reflects on the sūrah's end: it began with praise, wisdom, and awareness, and it ends with doubt's doom — as if to say, certainty about God is praise, and doubt about Him is the barrier. The Prophet ﷺ taught the exit from doubt:

> *"Leave what makes you doubt for what does not make you doubt."* (al-Tirmidhī 2518; al-Nasā'ī 5711, ṣaḥīḥ)

And the sūrah's final word lingers as its final mercy: doubt confounds only those who nurse it. The penitent servant of verse 9, the thankful worker of verse 13, the patient believer of verse 19 — all began where the doubters stand, and chose instead to cross: from doubt to certainty, from mockery to praise, from the barrier to the gardens. The choice, until the barrier falls, remains open.

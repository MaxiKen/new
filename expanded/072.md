# Sūrah al-Jinn (Chapter 72) — Expanded Verse-by-Verse Commentary

## Introduction to the Sūrah

**Expanded Commentary**

**A Chapter Where the Unseen Becomes a Witness**

Sūrah al-Jinn is a late Makkan chapter of twenty-eight verses. Its name comes from the opening report that a group of jinn listened to the Prophet Muhammad ﷺ reciting the Qur'an, recognized it as divine guidance, believed in it, and returned as warners to their own people. This alone gives the sūrah a striking position in the Qur'an: human beings are not the only audience of revelation, nor are they the only moral community standing before God. The chapter allows the unseen world to testify to the truth that many visible people refused.

The word *jinn* comes from the Arabic root *j-n-n*, whose basic sense is concealment. A fetus is called *janīn* because it is hidden in the womb; a shield is called *junnah* because it covers; a garden is called *jannah* because its dense growth conceals the ground. The jinn, therefore, are not imaginary figures in the Qur'anic worldview. They are created beings whose ordinary condition is hiddenness from human sight. The Prophet ﷺ taught their created origin in a sound report from ʿĀʾishah:

> *"The angels were created from light, the jinn were created from a smokeless flame of fire, and Adam was created from what has been described to you."* (Muslim)

This single hadith establishes an ordered spiritual cosmology. Angels, jinn, and human beings are distinct creations. Angels are not fallen humans; jinn are not angels; human beings are not merely visible animals. Each has a place in God's wise arrangement. The Qur'an speaks of the jinn as morally responsible beings: some believe, some disbelieve, some are righteous, some are wicked, and all are under God's knowledge and judgment.

**A Makkan Sūrah of Consolation After Rejection**

The sūrah is commonly connected with a period of hardship in the Prophet's Makkan mission. Many reports place the episode after the Prophet ﷺ faced intense opposition from Quraysh and sought support beyond Makkah. In the well-known account preserved in the books of tafsīr and sīrah, he traveled to Ṭāʾif, hoping its leaders might receive the message, but he was rejected and harmed. Whether the precise encounter of the jinn occurred on that journey or in another Makkan setting, the meaning is unmistakable: when many of the Prophet's own people would not listen, God caused another unseen community to listen.

This is one of the Qur'an's quiet ways of consoling a messenger. Human rejection does not mean the message has failed. A preacher may look at visible numbers and think the truth is being buried, while God is opening doors he cannot see. Sūrah al-Jinn teaches the Prophet ﷺ and his followers that daʿwah is never measured only by the audience in front of one's eyes. A word of truth uttered sincerely may be heard by hearts, generations, and even beings beyond the speaker's awareness.

The Qur'an elsewhere gives a related account in Sūrah al-Aḥqāf:

> *"And when We directed to you a group of jinn listening to the Qur'an. When they attended it, they said, 'Listen attentively.' And when it was concluded, they returned to their people as warners. They said, 'O our people, indeed we have heard a Book sent down after Moses, confirming what came before it, guiding to the truth and to a straight path. O our people, respond to the caller of God and believe in him; He will forgive you some of your sins and protect you from a painful punishment.'"* (Qur'an 46:29–31)

The two passages complement one another. Sūrah al-Aḥqāf describes the jinn's attentive listening and their return as warners; Sūrah al-Jinn gives the content of their confession. Together they show that the Qur'an did not merely impress them aesthetically. It reformed their theology, corrected their cosmology, reordered their fear, and made them callers to God.

**The Jinn Between Denial and Superstition**

A central benefit of this sūrah is balance. Some people deny the unseen altogether because they cannot reduce it to laboratory measurement. Others become obsessed with the unseen, attributing to jinn powers of protection, harm, knowledge, and control that belong only to God. Sūrah al-Jinn rejects both errors. It affirms that the jinn exist, hear, reason, choose, and are accountable. But it also strips them of divinity, omniscience, and independent power.

The believing jinn themselves confess that they cannot escape God, cannot thwart Him, do not possess knowledge of the unseen, and are divided into righteous and unrighteous groups. This is important because many ancient and modern superstitions grow from exaggerating hidden beings. When people fear what they cannot see, they may surrender their judgment to anything that claims secret power. The Qur'an breaks that spell by letting the jinn speak: they are servants, not lords; accountable creatures, not cosmic authorities.

This balance is repeated throughout revelation. God says:

> *"And I did not create the jinn and mankind except to worship Me."* (Qur'an 51:56)

The verse joins jinn and mankind under one purpose: worship. The unseen creature and the visible creature are both servants. The fact that the jinn may perceive aspects of reality hidden from us does not make them guides. A person standing on a hill may see farther than one in a valley, but if he is dishonest, confused, or rebellious, his elevated position only makes his misinformation more dangerous. Access to hidden information is not the same as nearness to God.

**The Architecture of the Sūrah**

The sūrah unfolds in a clear movement. Verses 1–15 report the testimony of the believing jinn. They announce that they heard a wondrous Qur'an, believed in it, renounced shirk, confessed that foolish leaders had lied about God, admitted the error of humans seeking refuge in jinn, described the closing of the heavenly routes by guards and flaming stars, acknowledged their own moral diversity, and divided themselves into submitters and unjust ones.

Verses 16–19 then turn to divine instruction and commentary. Steadfastness upon the path brings provision, but provision itself becomes a test. Places of worship belong to God alone, and the servant of God who rises to call upon Him becomes the center of intense attention, whether from eager listeners or hostile crowds.

Verses 20–24 command the Prophet ﷺ to declare the purity of his mission: he calls only upon his Lord, associates no partner with Him, possesses no independent power to harm or guide, and finds no refuge from God except in faithful conveyance of God's messages. Those who disobey God and His Messenger will finally learn who is truly weak and few in helpers.

Verses 25–28 close by correcting human impatience about the promised end and by defining the doctrine of the unseen. The Prophet ﷺ does not know the timing of the promised punishment or the Hour except what God reveals. God alone is Knower of the Unseen, and He discloses from it only what He wills to a messenger He approves, protecting revelation by guards so that the message reaches humanity intact.

**The Sūrah as a Cure for Three Modern Illusions**

The chapter speaks with special force to modern life. First, it cures the illusion of visible-only reality. A person may live as if only matter exists, yet the Qur'an insists that unseen moral realities surround him: angels, jinn, revelation, accountability, and divine knowledge. Second, it cures the illusion of occult control. The believer does not chase horoscopes, fortune-tellers, spirit messages, or secret energies. He seeks refuge in God, follows revelation, and knows that the unseen belongs to the Lord of the unseen. Third, it cures the illusion of numerical proof. Quraysh could count their supporters and see the Prophet ﷺ as isolated; yet unseen listeners were accepting his message. The truth is not weak because its audience is hidden, nor falsehood strong because its crowd is visible.

Sūrah al-Jinn therefore trains the believer to live with humility before what he cannot see, confidence in what God has revealed, and freedom from both superstition and materialism. It says, in effect: there is more to creation than your eyes, but nothing in creation deserves your worship; there are beings beyond your senses, but none beyond God's authority; there is hidden knowledge, but the safe path to God is not secret technique — it is revelation, worship, repentance, and truthful submission.

## Sūrah al-Jinn 72:1

> **Say, “It was revealed unto me that a group of jinn listened, and said, ‘Truly we have heard a wondrous Qur'an**

**Expanded Commentary**

**The Command “Say” Makes the Unseen Public Knowledge**

The sūrah begins with *Qul* — “Say.” This word is not decorative. It means the Prophet ﷺ is commanded to announce something he did not discover by personal speculation, occult investigation, or ordinary observation. The report is not framed as, “I saw,” or “I found,” but as, “It was revealed unto me.” The knowledge of this event comes through revelation, and that matters because the subject itself belongs to the unseen. If the matter concerns jinn, heavenly guards, and the protection of revelation, then the only reliable source is the One who knows the unseen fully.

The Prophet ﷺ is therefore both recipient and conveyor. He is not asked to invent a theology of the jinn. He is commanded to recite what God has made known. This teaches a permanent principle: the unseen is not a field for curiosity without discipline. The believer does not build creed upon rumor, séances, dreams, fear, or cultural superstition. He receives what God and His Messenger ﷺ have authentically taught and stops where revelation stops.

This is why the Qur'an repeatedly links truthful knowledge of the unseen to God alone:

> *"Say: None in the heavens and the earth knows the unseen except God, and they do not perceive when they will be resurrected."* (Qur'an 27:65)

The first verse of Sūrah al-Jinn is already preparing the last verses of the chapter, where God is called *Knower of the Unseen*. The beginning and end hold one another: God reveals what He wills, and the Prophet ﷺ says only what has been revealed.

**A Hidden Community Listens While Visible People Refuse**

The verse says that *nafarun mina al-jinn* — a group of jinn — listened. *Nafar* usually refers to a small band, often between three and ten. The point is not their large number but their serious response. History often changes through small groups who hear truth deeply. Quraysh had chiefs, assemblies, markets, poets, alliances, and tribal prestige; the jinn in this verse had only attentive listening. Yet the small unseen group becomes honored by permanent mention in the Qur'an.

This is a profound consolation for the Prophet ﷺ. The Makkan leaders often heard the Qur'an while resisting it, mocking it, or calculating its social consequences. The jinn hear and say, “We have heard a wondrous Qur'an.” The contrast is deliberate. Human proximity to revelation does not guarantee benefit. A person may be physically near the Prophet ﷺ and spiritually far; another may hear one recitation in the desert and be transformed.

The Qur'an gives the related scene in Sūrah al-Aḥqāf:

> *"When they attended it, they said, 'Listen attentively.' And when it was concluded, they returned to their people as warners."* (Qur'an 46:29)

Their first instruction to one another is not argument but silence: listen. Before guidance enters the heart, noise must be reduced. This applies not only to sound but to ego, prejudice, fear, and haste. Many people fail to receive truth not because it is unclear, but because they never truly listen to it.

**The Word Jinn and the Discipline of Believing Beyond Sight**

As noted in the introduction, *jinn* comes from concealment. Their hiddenness is part of the test. A believer accepts their existence not because he can domesticate the subject with complete empirical control, but because God has informed him. Yet Islamic belief is not credulity. The Qur'an does not tell us to believe every story told about jinn. It gives a limited, meaningful, morally useful account: they are created, hidden, accountable, diverse, capable of belief and disbelief, and unable to escape God.

This makes the Islamic approach neither materialist nor superstitious. Materialism says, “If I cannot see it, it is not real.” Superstition says, “If it is hidden, it must be powerful and spiritually decisive.” Revelation says: some hidden things are real, but all real things are servants of God. The unseen expands humility; it must never become a rival to tawḥīd.

The Prophet ﷺ taught the distinct creation of the jinn:

> *"The angels were created from light, the jinn were created from a smokeless flame of fire, and Adam was created from what has been described to you."* (Muslim)

The hadith dignifies difference without allowing confusion. Different origin does not mean different Lord. Fire, light, and clay all belong to God.

**Listening as the First Act of Guidance**

The Arabic verb *istamaʿa* indicates attentive listening, not accidental hearing. There is a moral difference between sound entering the ear and guidance entering the heart. Many heard the Prophet ﷺ recite; these jinn listened. The Qur'an itself commands this etiquette:

> *"And when the Qur'an is recited, listen to it and pay attention, that you may receive mercy."* (Qur'an 7:204)

Listening is a form of humility. It says: perhaps I need to be corrected; perhaps my inherited assumptions are incomplete; perhaps the truth is greater than the habits of my people. The believing jinn will soon confess that they had accepted false statements about God because they assumed no one would lie about Him. Their reform begins here, at listening.

Modern people often confuse exposure with understanding. They may have heard verses, watched clips, read fragments, or encountered religious vocabulary, but have never listened with the seriousness of someone seeking rescue. The jinn's example rebukes casual consumption of sacred speech. They do not treat the Qur'an as background sound. They recognize an address from the Lord of the worlds.

**“A Wondrous Qur'an”: The Amazement of Meaning and Form**

The jinn call what they heard *Qur'ānan ʿajaban* — a wondrous, astonishing, marvel-producing Qur'an. *ʿAjab* is not mere entertainment. It is the astonishment that arises when something exceeds expectation and disrupts ordinary categories. The Qur'an amazed Arabs by its language, law, prophetic knowledge, moral force, spiritual authority, and its ability to expose the soul to itself. Here it amazes the jinn too.

The wonder is especially meaningful because the jinn, according to the sūrah, had experience of subtle realms and had previously sought access to heavenly information. Yet when they hear the Qur'an on the tongue of the Prophet ﷺ, they recognize something superior to stolen fragments and occult whispers. They do not say, “We heard interesting information.” They say, “We heard a wondrous Qur'an.” Revelation is not merely data from the unseen; it is guidance from God.

The Qur'an itself challenges mankind and jinn together:

> *"Say: If mankind and jinn gathered together to produce the like of this Qur'an, they could not produce the like of it, even if they were helpers of one another."* (Qur'an 17:88)

This verse becomes more powerful in light of Sūrah al-Jinn. The jinn are not only unable to imitate the Qur'an; the sincere among them confess its wonder.

**The Solace of Hidden Acceptance**

A preacher, teacher, parent, or reformer may become discouraged when the visible audience appears resistant. Sūrah al-Jinn teaches that God may place receptivity where the servant least expects it. The Prophet ﷺ did not build his mission upon the approval of social elites. His duty was recitation, conveyance, patience, and trust. God handled the reach of the message.

This is not a call to passivity. It is a correction of despair. The believer speaks truth with wisdom, but he does not control hearts. He may see one door closing while God opens another beyond his sight. The first verse of this sūrah therefore places hope inside the unseen: not all listeners are visible, and not all acceptance is immediate, public, or measurable.

## Sūrah al-Jinn 72:2

> **that guides to sound judgment; so we believe in it and will ascribe none as partner unto our Lord.**

**Expanded Commentary**

**Wonder Becomes Guidance, Not Mere Admiration**

The jinn do not stop at amazement. They say the Qur'an *yahdī ilā al-rushd* — it guides to sound judgment, right direction, maturity, and uprightness. This is crucial. Many things can amaze a person without guiding him. Eloquence may impress, music may move, philosophy may stimulate, and power may dazzle. The Qur'an's wonder is different: it leads the listener somewhere. Its beauty is joined to direction.

The word *rushd* carries the sense of reaching mature soundness in thought and action. It is the opposite of *ghayy*, deviation that ruins a person, and of *safah*, foolishness that wastes judgment. A *rashīd* person does not merely possess information; he uses truth rightly. The Qur'an produces this kind of maturity because it joins knowledge of God, knowledge of the self, knowledge of moral consequence, and knowledge of the Hereafter.

The Qur'an describes its own guiding function in another verse:

> *"Indeed, this Qur'an guides to that which is most upright and gives glad tidings to the believers who do righteous deeds that they will have a great reward."* (Qur'an 17:9)

The phrase “most upright” is close in meaning to *rushd*. The Qur'an guides not to novelty, tribal pride, mystical excitement, or intellectual display, but to the straightest arrangement of life.

**The Grammar of Immediate Faith**

The jinn say, *fa-āmannā bihi* — “so we believed in it.” The particle *fa* indicates sequence and consequence: we heard it, recognized its guidance, therefore believed. Their faith is not delayed by negotiations. They do not ask whether belief will preserve their social status among other jinn. They do not request a private exception from monotheism. They submit to the implication of what they have heard.

This immediacy exposes a moral issue in human hesitation. Sometimes a person says, “I need more evidence,” when the real obstacle is not evidence but the cost of obedience. The jinn's response shows that sincere recognition should become commitment. When guidance becomes clear, delay becomes spiritually dangerous.

This does not mean Islam condemns honest inquiry. The Qur'an repeatedly invites reflection. But inquiry is different from evasion. The jinn listened, understood enough, and acted. Their response resembles the believers praised elsewhere:

> *"The only statement of the believers when they are called to God and His Messenger to judge between them is that they say, 'We hear and we obey.' And those are the successful."* (Qur'an 24:51)

They heard; then they believed. That is the clean movement of an uncorrupted heart.

**Faith Immediately Purifies Worship**

The jinn's first theological conclusion is, “We will ascribe none as partner unto our Lord.” This shows that true faith in the Qur'an necessarily becomes tawḥīd. One cannot believe the Qur'an while preserving rivals to God in worship, fear, devotion, or ultimate trust. The phrase *lan nushrika bi-rabbinā aḥadan* is emphatic: we shall never associate anyone with our Lord.

It is significant that they say *our Lord* (*rabbinā*). They recognize God as the One who created, sustains, owns, and governs them. Lordship is not abstract. If He is our Lord, He alone deserves our worship. This is the Qur'an's repeated argument against shirk: the One who creates and provides is the One who must be worshipped.

God says:

> *"O mankind, worship your Lord, who created you and those before you, that you may become mindful; He who made for you the earth a resting place and the sky a canopy and sent down from the sky water and brought forth thereby fruits as provision for you. So do not set up rivals to God while you know."* (Qur'an 2:21–22)

The believing jinn grasp this logic. Their experience of the unseen does not lead them to multiply sacred powers. It leads them to purify worship.

**Guidance Is Not Only Knowing the Road, but Escaping False Roads**

To be guided to *rushd* means not only finding the right path, but recognizing the wrongness of alternatives. The jinn had lived in a world where some among them made outrageous claims about God, and some humans sought refuge in jinn. Hearing the Qur'an reorganized the map. Practices that once seemed normal became unacceptable. Claims that once seemed plausible became lies against God.

This is how revelation works in a human life as well. It does not merely add religious information to an unchanged personality. It reclassifies things. What was once “just business” may become oppression. What was once “just entertainment” may become heedlessness. What was once “culture” may become shirk. What was once “private choice” may become disobedience. Guidance matures the conscience.

The Qur'an says:

> *"There is no compulsion in religion. Right guidance has become clear from error."* (Qur'an 2:256)

The phrase *rushd* appears here too. Religion cannot be forced into the heart, but truth can become clear enough that a morally serious person recognizes the difference between guidance and ruin.

**Aqīdah Before Social Reform**

The jinn do not begin their report by proposing social improvements among their people. They begin with belief in the Qur'an and rejection of shirk. This order matters. Social reform without sound theology may improve surface behavior while leaving the deepest bondage untouched. A person can become disciplined, generous, intellectual, or politically active while still worshipping status, wealth, ideology, or hidden powers. The Qur'an begins by freeing the servant's center.

Tawḥīd is not a dry doctrine. It is the liberation of the heart from ultimate dependence on anything created. When the jinn say they will not associate anyone with their Lord, they are also saying: no jinn chief, no demon, no star, no idol, no inherited myth, no force of nature, and no hidden voice deserves divine attachment.

For modern readers, the partners may not be carved idols. They may be approval, career, nationalism, race, ideology, romantic obsession, or the self's own appetite. The Qur'anic principle remains: anything treated as an ultimate source of meaning, fear, obedience, or rescue has begun to occupy sacred space. Guidance to *rushd* returns that space to God alone.

## Sūrah al-Jinn 72:3

> **And He—exalted be the Majesty of our Lord—has neither consort nor child.**

**Expanded Commentary**

**The Believing Jinn Begin Theology with Transcendence**

After affirming faith and rejecting partners, the jinn declare God's transcendence: “Exalted be the Majesty of our Lord.” The Arabic phrase *taʿālā jaddu rabbinā* is rich. *Taʿālā* means He is high above, exalted beyond, utterly transcendent over what is falsely attributed to Him. *Jadd* can mean majesty, greatness, grandeur, or exalted fortune and authority. It does not mean “grandfather” here, as it might in another context. The phrase means that the greatness of the Lord is far above every claim of spouse, child, rival, or need.

This is theology spoken as reverence. The jinn do not say only, “God has no consort or child.” They first glorify Him above such a thought. Correct doctrine in the Qur'an is not cold negation; it is adab before divine majesty. The believer does not merely reject false beliefs. He feels the impropriety of attaching creaturely limitations to the Lord of the worlds.

The Qur'an teaches the same reverent negation in Sūrah al-Ikhlāṣ:

> *"Say: He is God, One. God, the Self-Sufficient. He neither begets nor is begotten. And there is none comparable to Him."* (Qur'an 112:1–4)

Sūrah al-Jinn and Sūrah al-Ikhlāṣ meet in the same doctrine: God's oneness excludes biological extension, genealogical dependence, and all resemblance to creation.

**Why a Consort or Child Is Impossible for God**

The verse denies both *ṣāḥibah* — consort, spouse, companion in that sense — and *walad* — child. The logic is clear. A child belongs to beings who reproduce through need, species, division, succession, and vulnerability. A spouse belongs to beings completed, helped, or accompanied by another of their kind. God is not a member of a species. He does not reproduce. He is not lonely. He does not require completion. He does not age, die, or need an heir.

The Qur'an gives this argument explicitly:

> *"Originator of the heavens and the earth. How could He have a child when He has no consort? He created all things, and He is Knowing of all things."* (Qur'an 6:101)

The verse is logically powerful: the One who originates all categories cannot be contained within one of them. The Maker of male and female is not Himself male or female. The Creator of family is not inserted into a family tree. To attribute offspring to God is not merely one error among many; it is to confuse Creator with creature at the most basic level.

**The Qur'an's Stern Rejection of Attributing Children to God**

The Qur'an treats the claim that God has a child as spiritually grave because it violates both His transcendence and His mercy. It turns the Lord of all into the tribal possession of some, or into a being whose love requires biological partiality. Revelation rejects this sharply:

> *"And they say, 'The Compassionate has taken a child.' You have brought forth an atrocious thing. The heavens almost rupture from it, and the earth splits open, and the mountains collapse in devastation, that they attribute to the Compassionate a child. It is not befitting for the Compassionate to take a child."* (Qur'an 19:88–92)

The force of this passage shows that tawḥīd is not a minor abstraction. The moral order of reality trembles, as it were, at a theology that lowers God into creaturely dependence. The believing jinn, having heard the Qur'an, now speak with the same purified instinct.

The Qur'an also addresses claims that jinn themselves had some kinship with God:

> *"And they made between Him and the jinn a lineage, but the jinn have already known that they will be brought forth."* (Qur'an 37:158)

Sūrah al-Jinn becomes the answer placed in the mouths of believing jinn: we are not divine relatives; we are accountable servants.

**Correcting Inherited Myths About the Divine**

The jinn's statement suggests that they had heard or inherited false claims about God. Verse 5 will say that they assumed mankind and jinn would not lie about Him. This matters. Many theological errors survive not because they are rationally strong, but because they are old, emotionally familiar, socially reinforced, or attached to revered authorities. The Qur'an does not excuse such claims merely because they have tradition behind them.

Human beings often inherit images of God from family, culture, trauma, or imagination. Some imagine Him as distant power without mercy; others as indulgent affection without judgment; others as a tribal deity; others as an energy within the world. The Qur'an corrects all such projections by revelation. God is known by what He says of Himself, not by what frightened, proud, or speculative creatures project onto Him.

Ibn Taymiyyah repeatedly emphasized a principle central to Sunni theology: God's names and attributes are known through revelation, not invented by human imagination. In meaning, his principle may be stated as follows:

> *The servant describes God only with what God described Himself with, or what His Messenger ﷺ described Him with, without distortion, denial, asking how, or likening Him to creation.*

This principle protects the heart from two opposite errors: stripping God of what He affirmed, and attributing to Him what He never revealed.

**The Psychological Fruit of Divine Self-Sufficiency**

The denial of consort and child also affects worship. If God has no need, then His commands are not attempts to fill a deficiency. He does not need our prayer, charity, fasting, or praise. We need them. His self-sufficiency means His guidance is mercy, not exploitation. A needy god would command to gain; the true God commands to guide.

The Qur'an states:

> *"O mankind, you are the poor in need of God, and God is the Free of Need, the Praiseworthy."* (Qur'an 35:15)

This creates spiritual security. The believer worships a Lord who does not manipulate creation to satisfy loneliness, rivalry, or insecurity. He worships the One whose majesty is complete before all worlds and whose mercy creates without need.

When the jinn say, “Exalted be the Majesty of our Lord,” they are not merely solving a theological puzzle. They are entering a purified relationship: creature before Creator, servant before Lord, worshipper before the One who needs nothing and gives everything.

## Sūrah al-Jinn 72:4

> **Truly the fool among us has uttered outrages against God,**

**Expanded Commentary**

**False Theology Is Named Foolishness Before It Is Named Intelligence**

The believing jinn now diagnose the source of their former confusion: “the fool among us” spoke outrage against God. The Arabic *safīhunā* comes from *safah*, a lightness and instability of judgment. A *safīh* is not merely uninformed; he is morally foolish, intellectually reckless, and spiritually immature. The word is used in the Qur'an for those whose judgment is too unsound to be trusted with property or truth.

Calling the speaker “the fool among us” is significant. False claims about God may sometimes be sophisticated in language, old in history, or popular in community, but the Qur'an asks a deeper question: are they sound before divine reality? A claim can be culturally prestigious and spiritually foolish. It can be rhetorically clever and theologically outrageous.

The word translated “outrages” is *shaṭaṭā*, meaning an excessive statement far beyond the bounds of truth. It suggests overreach, deviation, and transgression. To attribute spouse, child, rival, or limitation to God is not a small mistake near the truth; it is a statement that has traveled far from proper reverence.

**Who Is “the Fool Among Us”?**

Many commentators, including Ibn Kathīr, report the view that “the fool” refers especially to Iblīs, who is from the jinn and who became the archetype of rebellious arrogance. Others take it more generally: the foolish ones among the jinn — their devils, leaders, or misguided speakers — had uttered falsehoods about God. The two readings are compatible. Iblīs is the root figure of jinn rebellion, and every false caller who repeats his pattern shares in his foolishness.

The Qur'an identifies Iblīs as connected to the jinn:

> *"And when We said to the angels, 'Prostrate to Adam,' they prostrated except Iblīs. He was of the jinn and departed from the command of his Lord."* (Qur'an 18:50)

This verse explains why arrogance is not intelligence. Iblīs knew more than many creatures, had worshipped among angels according to many reports of the early commentators, and directly received God's command. Yet he became *safīh* because he rejected truth through pride. Knowledge without submission can become the most dangerous form of foolishness.

**The Inner Link Between Arrogance and Lying About God**

Why does false theology often begin in arrogance? Because to speak about God without revelation is to place oneself above one's proper rank. The servant begins to behave as if divine reality must conform to his desires, tribe, mythology, or philosophical taste. This is why the Qur'an repeatedly condemns speaking about God without knowledge:

> *"Say: My Lord has only forbidden immoralities, what is apparent of them and what is concealed, sin, oppression without right, that you associate with God that for which He has sent down no authority, and that you say about God what you do not know."* (Qur'an 7:33)

Notice that speaking about God without knowledge is listed among the gravest prohibitions. It corrupts the source from which all other moral judgments flow. If a person lies about a human judge, he may distort one case; if he lies about God, he distorts the meaning of existence.

The Prophet ﷺ defined arrogance with precision:

> *"Arrogance is rejecting the truth and looking down upon people."* (Muslim)

This definition fits Iblīs and every later imitator. Rejecting the truth leads to false claims; looking down upon others leads to manipulation. A foolish leader can misguide a community not because he has no intelligence, but because his intelligence is severed from humility.

**The Courage to Criticize One's Own Community**

The jinn say “the fool among us.” They do not blame only outsiders. This is a sign of sincerity. When guidance enters a community, it gives people the courage to name internal corruption. They no longer protect falsehood merely because it came from “our side.” The believing jinn identify the error within their own group.

This is a lesson for religious communities. Loyalty to truth must be higher than loyalty to faction. If a scholar, preacher, elder, activist, or ancestor speaks falsely about God, the believer's duty is not to defend the statement out of tribal pride. It is to return respectfully but firmly to revelation. The Qur'an praises those whose loyalty is governed by God:

> *"O you who believe, stand firmly for God, witnesses in justice, and let not hatred of a people cause you to be unjust. Be just; that is nearer to mindfulness."* (Qur'an 5:8)

If hatred must not make us unjust, then love of our group must not make us dishonest. The jinn's confession is therefore intellectually and morally mature.

**Modern Forms of “Outrage Against God”**

Outrageous speech against God is not limited to ancient claims of divine offspring. It includes any claim that diminishes His oneness, justice, mercy, knowledge, or authority without revelation. Some speak as if God cannot know the future; others as if He is obligated to approve every human desire; others as if His law is outdated; others as if He is one spiritual option among many equal powers. In all cases, the creature is judging the Creator by a standard higher than Him — and that is the essence of *shaṭaṭ*.

This does not mean every confused person is malicious. Verse 5 will show that some believed lies because they assumed no one would invent such things about God. But the verse does teach that ideas have moral weight. A society may tolerate careless God-talk as opinion, but revelation treats it as spiritually dangerous.

The practical discipline is simple: speak about God with evidence, humility, and reverence. Silence without knowledge is safer than eloquence without truth. The believing jinn moved from repeating inherited errors to confessing, “the fool among us has uttered outrages against God.” That confession is the beginning of theological repentance.

## Sūrah al-Jinn 72:5

> **though we had thought that mankind and jinn would not utter lies regarding God.**

**Expanded Commentary**

**A Dangerous Assumption About Religious Speech**

The jinn now explain why they were deceived: they had assumed that neither mankind nor jinn would lie about God. The Arabic *ẓanannā* means “we thought,” “we supposed,” or “we assumed.” Their mistake was not that they denied God, but that they trusted religious claims too easily. They could not imagine that creatures would dare fabricate statements about the Lord.

This assumption appears noble at first. It comes from reverence: surely no one would lie about God. But the Qur'an shows that reverence without verification can become vulnerability. Some lies are so grave that sincere people may underestimate the possibility of their existence. The jinn learned, through the Qur'an, that falsehood can wear sacred clothing.

The Qur'an repeatedly warns against attributing claims to God without proof:

> *"And do not say, concerning what your tongues falsely describe, 'This is lawful and this is unlawful,' in order to invent lies against God. Indeed, those who invent lies against God will not succeed."* (Qur'an 16:116)

The verse is not only about ancient pagans. It applies wherever people declare divine approval or prohibition without knowledge, evidence, or authority.

**Blind Trust Is Not the Same as Good Opinion**

Islam encourages good opinion of believers, but it does not command intellectual gullibility. There is a difference between refusing suspicion and refusing verification. The jinn's error was that they treated the seriousness of a subject as proof of the truthfulness of its speakers. In reality, the seriousness of a subject increases the need for evidence.

The Qur'an criticizes inherited religion when it is detached from revelation:

> *"And when it is said to them, 'Follow what God has sent down,' they say, 'Rather, we follow what we found our fathers upon.' Even if their fathers understood nothing and were not guided?"* (Qur'an 2:170)

The problem is not ancestry itself. The prophets honored righteous forefathers. The problem is making inherited speech immune from correction. The jinn assumed that mankind and jinn would not lie; many humans assume that ancestors, spiritual leaders, cultural authorities, or popular speakers would not mislead them. Revelation teaches respect, but it also teaches testing claims by God's guidance.

**Lying About God Is the Most Serious Form of Falsehood**

A lie about an ordinary matter may harm property or reputation. A lie about God may redirect worship, corrupt law, excuse oppression, or close the door of repentance. That is why the Qur'an treats it as one of the greatest injustices:

> *"And who is more unjust than one who invents a lie against God or denies His signs? Indeed, the wrongdoers will not succeed."* (Qur'an 6:21)

The Prophet ﷺ also warned with exceptional severity against lying about him, because lying about the Messenger becomes lying about the religion he conveys:

> *"Whoever intentionally lies about me, let him take his seat in the Fire."* (al-Bukhārī; Muslim)

If this is the warning for fabricating words from the Prophet ﷺ, then lying directly about God — His nature, His law, His promises, His permissions, His prohibitions — is still more grave. The believer therefore treats religious speech with careful trust: open-hearted, but not careless; respectful, but not uncritical.

**The Social Machinery of Sacred Falsehood**

How do lies about God spread? Sometimes through deliberate manipulation: a leader claims divine sanction to control followers. Sometimes through exaggeration: a story told for inspiration becomes doctrine. Sometimes through fear: people invent rituals to feel protected from unseen forces. Sometimes through profit: spiritual claims become a market. Sometimes through laziness: people repeat what they have not verified because it sounds religious.

Sūrah al-Jinn is especially concerned with the overlap between humans and jinn in false religion. Some humans sought refuge in jinn; some jinn enjoyed being feared; false claims circulated between the two. The result was mutual corruption. The Qur'an describes a wider pattern of misleading speech:

> *"And thus We have made for every prophet enemies — devils from mankind and jinn, inspiring to one another decorative speech in deception."* (Qur'an 6:112)

The phrase “decorative speech” is psychologically precise. Falsehood rarely arrives labeled as falsehood. It comes ornamented — with charisma, mystery, poetry, outrage, novelty, or ancient prestige. The jinn's confession teaches the believer to ask: is this claim beautiful because it is true, or merely decorated enough to avoid examination?

**Modern Information Culture and the Old Sin of Fabrication**

The verse speaks directly to an age of rapid religious misinformation. A statement can be falsely attributed to God, the Prophet ﷺ, a Companion, or a scholar and travel across the world in minutes. People may share it because it feels uplifting or confirms their view. But good feelings do not authenticate sacred claims.

The prophetic warning about lying should make the believer cautious with posts, sermons, forwarded messages, and confident claims about divine will. Before saying “Islam says,” “God wants,” “this is ḥalāl,” or “this is ḥarām,” a person should feel the weight of verse 5. The believing jinn discovered that even religious communities can utter lies about God. That discovery should produce humility in speech.

The practical rule is not complicated: verify before repeating; distinguish certainty from opinion; do not turn cultural preference into divine command; do not quote a hadith without concern for authenticity; do not assign motives to God beyond what He revealed. In ordinary life, responsible people check financial contracts and medical prescriptions. Religion deserves greater care, not less.

**From Naive Trust to Revelation-Based Trust**

The jinn's statement is not cynical. They do not conclude, “Trust no one.” They conclude, “Believe the Qur'an and reject lies against God.” Revelation does not destroy trust; it purifies it. The believer learns to trust God absolutely, trust the Messenger ﷺ through authentic transmission, trust the scholarly tradition with understanding, and test individual claims against evidence.

This is mature faith. It is neither childish suspicion nor childish gullibility. It recognizes that human and jinn communities contain truth-tellers and liars, righteous guides and foolish speakers. The Qur'an becomes the criterion that separates them. The jinn's previous assumption failed them; the Qur'an guided them to *rushd*.

## Sūrah al-Jinn 72:6

> **Indeed, individuals among mankind did seek refuge with individuals among the jinn, and so increased them in oppression.**

**Expanded Commentary**

**Fear of the Unseen Became a Door to Shirk**

The verse describes a pre-Islamic practice: some human beings sought refuge with some jinn. Classical commentators mention the custom of travelers entering a lonely valley and saying, in meaning, “I seek refuge in the master of this valley from the foolish among his people.” The traveler feared hidden beings and tried to secure protection from a jinn authority. The Qur'an exposes the practice not as harmless folklore, but as a relationship that increased *rahaq* — oppression, burden, sin, arrogance, fear, or transgression.

The word *rijāl* — “men” or “individuals” — is used for both sides: men from mankind and men from jinn. This shows a kind of social transaction between two communities. Humans gave the jinn reverence and fear; the jinn gained inflated importance and perhaps increased humans in terror, misguidance, or sin. The relationship was spiritually poisonous because it transferred refuge — a heart-act of worship — from God to a creature.

The Qur'an repeatedly teaches that refuge belongs to God:

> *"Say: I seek refuge in the Lord of daybreak."* (Qur'an 113:1)

> *"Say: I seek refuge in the Lord of mankind, the King of mankind, the God of mankind."* (Qur'an 114:1–3)

The believer does not deny danger. He redirects fear to the only true Protector.

**What Is Wrong with Seeking Refuge from Jinn?**

There is nothing wrong with seeking ordinary help from a capable creature in ordinary matters. A person may ask a doctor for treatment, a neighbor for assistance, or a judge for justice. The prohibited matter is seeking unseen protection from a hidden being in a manner that belongs to worship, reliance, fear, and reverence. When a person calls upon a jinn for safety, believing it has hidden authority over his fate, he has given a created being a share of the heart's dependence that belongs to God.

Islam replaces this with revealed words of protection. The Prophet ﷺ taught a supplication for entering a place:

> *"Whoever stops at a place and says, 'I seek refuge in the perfect words of God from the evil of what He created,' nothing will harm him until he departs from that place."* (Muslim)

This hadith is a direct cure for the practice mentioned in the verse. The traveler still recognizes that there may be harm in unfamiliar places, but he seeks refuge in God's perfect words, not in the alleged lord of a valley. Tawḥīd does not make a person reckless; it makes his caution worshipful.

**Rahaq: When Dependence Increases the Disease It Tries to Cure**

The verse says this practice increased “them” in *rahaq*. Commentators differed over the pronoun. It may mean the humans increased the jinn in arrogance and oppression because the jinn saw themselves feared and honored. It may also mean the jinn increased the humans in fear, sin, and humiliation. Both meanings are spiritually true and mutually reinforcing.

This is how false refuge works. The more a person depends on a false protector, the more powerful that protector appears. Fear feeds the system that imprisons the fearful. A tyrant becomes more tyrannical when people treat him as untouchable. A superstition becomes more controlling when people organize their decisions around it. An addiction becomes stronger when the addict turns to it for relief from the pain it helped create. In the same way, seeking refuge in jinn increases the very darkness from which one seeks escape.

The Qur'an describes the general principle of false protectors:

> *"The example of those who take protectors besides God is like the spider who takes a house; and indeed the weakest of houses is the house of the spider, if they only knew."* (Qur'an 29:41)

A spider's web may look like shelter to the spider, but it collapses before real danger. So too every refuge besides God fails when the soul faces what only God controls.

**The Difference Between Ruqyah and Occult Dependence**

This verse also helps distinguish lawful spiritual protection from occult practice. Lawful *ruqyah* uses Qur'anic recitation, authentic supplications, and clear reliance upon God. It is a request that God heal, protect, and remove harm. Occult dependence seeks hidden forces, bargains with jinn, unknown names, talismans of ambiguous meaning, blood rituals, astrological timing, or secret formulas whose power is treated as independent or semi-divine.

The Prophet ﷺ allowed ruqyah so long as it was free of shirk. The principle is preserved in the sound tradition:

> *"There is no harm in ruqyah so long as there is no shirk in it."* (Muslim)

The difference is not merely external. It is a difference of theology. In lawful ruqyah, the healer, patient, words, and means are all servants of God. In occult practice, the heart often begins to fear and obey hidden intermediaries. Verse 6 closes that door.

**The Modern Forms of Seeking Refuge with Jinn**

Modern people may imagine themselves beyond such practices, yet the same spiritual pattern appears in new forms. Some still consult magicians, mediums, spirit-workers, and fortune-tellers. Others build their emotional life around astrology, “energy” manipulation, or secret entities. Some wear objects believing the object itself controls fate. Others fear curses more than sins. These are not harmless cultural ornaments when they redirect dependence away from God.

The Prophet ﷺ warned against fortune-telling because it corrupts reliance and prayer:

> *"Whoever goes to a fortune-teller and asks him about something, his prayer will not be accepted for forty nights."* (Muslim)

The warning is severe because the act wounds the heart's orientation. Even asking in curiosity trains the soul to look for guidance where God did not place it.

**Tawḥīd as Psychological Liberation**

The verse is also psychologically liberating. A person who fears every hidden force cannot live with dignity. He becomes manipulable by anyone claiming secret access: a magician, cult leader, abusive relative, fraudulent healer, or charismatic liar. Tawḥīd cuts through this. It teaches: you may be cautious, you may seek lawful treatment, you may recite protection, but you do not surrender your heart to anything created.

God says:

> *"Is not God sufficient for His servant? Yet they frighten you with those besides Him."* (Qur'an 39:36)

This is the emotional core of the verse. False religion frightens people with created beings; revelation returns sufficiency to God. The believing jinn themselves testify that seeking refuge in them only increased oppression. The safest path is to seek refuge in the Lord of jinn and mankind.

## Sūrah al-Jinn 72:7

> **They thought, as did you, that God would resurrect no one.**

**Expanded Commentary**

**A Shared Error Across Visible and Hidden Communities**

The believing jinn continue their confession: some among the jinn thought as some humans thought — that God would not *yabʿath* anyone. The verb *baʿatha* can mean to resurrect, to raise up, or to send forth. Many commentators understand the verse as denial of resurrection: they assumed God would not raise anyone after death. Others note that it may also include denial that God would send a messenger. Both meanings fit the Makkan context, because Quraysh rejected both resurrection and the final messenger sent to warn them of it.

The verse is powerful because it shows that falsehood is not limited to one species. Humans and jinn can share the same errors, reinforce one another, and normalize disbelief through mutual assumption. Error becomes stronger when communities echo it back to each other. “Everyone thinks this” is not proof; it may only mean that many have inherited the same blindness.

The Qur'an records the human denial of resurrection in many places:

> *"Those who disbelieve claim that they will never be resurrected. Say: Yes, by my Lord, you will surely be resurrected; then you will surely be informed of what you did. And that is easy for God."* (Qur'an 64:7)

The oath “by my Lord” answers the arrogance of “never.” The One who created the first time is not unable to restore.

**Resurrection Is the Moral Completion of Creation**

Belief in resurrection is not an optional extra added to monotheism. Without resurrection, the moral order of life remains unfinished. Many oppressors die without worldly justice. Many righteous people suffer without visible reward. Many hidden intentions never become known. If there is no resurrection, then enormous portions of human moral experience remain unresolved. The Qur'an insists that God's justice does not leave the ledger open forever.

God says:

> *"Then did you think that We created you uselessly and that to Us you would not be returned?"* (Qur'an 23:115)

This verse links denial of return with the idea of purposeless creation. If human beings are created only to live briefly, desire, compete, suffer, and disappear, then existence becomes morally absurd. Resurrection reveals that life is a test whose results are not fully displayed in the test-room.

The Qur'an argues from first creation to second creation:

> *"And he presents for Us an example and forgets his own creation. He says, 'Who will give life to bones when they are decayed?' Say: He will give them life who produced them the first time; and He is Knowing of every creation."* (Qur'an 36:78–79)

The logic is simple and decisive. The One who made life from nonexistence can restore life from remains. Resurrection is not difficult for God; it is only difficult for imaginations that measure divine power by human limitation.

**Denial of Resurrection Changes Behavior**

When people believe there is no final accounting, their ethical calculations change. Some may still behave decently out of temperament, culture, or worldly benefit, but the deepest accountability has been removed. The soul begins to ask: what can I get away with? What can I hide? What can I delay until death erases it? The Qur'an repeatedly connects denial of the Last Day with moral corruption:

> *"Have you seen the one who denies the Recompense? That is the one who repulses the orphan and does not encourage feeding the poor."* (Qur'an 107:1–3)

The denial of judgment does not remain in the mind; it moves into the hand that pushes away the weak. If there is no Day of Recompense, the orphan's claim weakens in the eyes of the selfish. If there is a Day of Recompense, even the powerless have a Lord who will speak for them.

**The Link Between Sending Messengers and Raising the Dead**

If *yabʿath* is read also as “send,” the verse carries another meaning: they thought God would not send anyone as a messenger. The two denials are connected. A messenger announces resurrection; resurrection vindicates the messenger. The Makkan opponents often found both claims unbelievable: that a man among them was receiving revelation and that dead bodies would rise.

The Qur'an records their objection:

> *"Has God sent a human messenger?"* (Qur'an 17:94)

And it answers:

> *"Say: If there were angels walking securely upon the earth, We would have sent down to them from heaven an angel as messenger."* (Qur'an 17:95)

Human beings need a human messenger because guidance must be embodied in a life they can imitate. Jinn likewise need divine address suited to their accountability. Sūrah al-Jinn shows that the Prophet's message reached them as well, even though he was sent from mankind.

**The Jinn's Repentance from a Wrong Assumption**

The believing jinn do not hide their previous error. They say, in effect: we too thought wrongly. This honesty is part of repentance. Guidance does not require pretending one was always correct. It requires surrendering former assumptions when revelation corrects them.

Many people resist faith because accepting it would require admitting that long-held beliefs were wrong. The jinn model the opposite. They do not protect their past selves. They protect truth. This is spiritual maturity: to say, “I thought this, but God has shown otherwise.”

For modern readers, resurrection remains the great dividing belief. If a person truly believes he will stand before God, his private life changes. His speech changes. His use of money changes. His treatment of the powerless changes. His grief changes too, because death is no longer annihilation. Verse 7 is therefore not merely about a doctrinal mistake among jinn; it is about the foundation of moral seriousness.

## Sūrah al-Jinn 72:8

> **We reached out to Heaven and found it filled with mighty sentries and flaming stars.**

**Expanded Commentary**

**A Cosmic Change Discovered by the Jinn**

The jinn say, “We reached out to Heaven.” The Arabic *lamasnī al-samāʾ* literally carries the sense of touching, seeking, feeling out, or attempting to reach. They tried to access the heavenly region from which they had previously gained fragments of information. But they found it filled with *ḥarasan shadīdan* — strong guards — and *shuhubā* — flaming stars or meteoric flames.

The verse describes a change in the unseen order surrounding revelation. The jinn had known a former pattern; now they encountered barriers. The heavens were not empty space available to any subtle creature. They were guarded by God's command. When revelation to the final Prophet ﷺ began, the routes of eavesdropping were blocked with a severity that alerted the jinn that a major divine event had occurred.

The Qur'an describes the same protection elsewhere:

> *"And We have placed within the heaven great stars and beautified it for the observers. And We have protected it from every accursed devil, except one who steals a hearing and is pursued by a clear flaming fire."* (Qur'an 15:16–18)

The heavens are both beautiful and guarded. What human beings see as beauty also participates, by God's will, in a hidden order of protection.

**Revelation Is Not Exposed to Theft**

A major theme of the sūrah is the protection of revelation. If jinn and devils could freely steal heavenly speech, mix it with lies, and deliver it to fortune-tellers, one might wonder whether revelation itself could be contaminated. These verses answer: no. God guards what He sends. Unauthorized fragments may once have been snatched, but the final revelation is protected by sentries and flames.

The Prophet ﷺ explained the matter of stolen heavenly words in authentic hadith. When asked about fortune-tellers, he said:

> *"They are nothing."* The people said, *"O Messenger of God, sometimes they tell us of something that turns out to be true."* He said: *"That is a word of truth which a jinn snatches and pours into the ear of his ally, but they mix with it a hundred lies."* (al-Bukhārī; Muslim)

This hadith is essential for understanding verses 8–9. It does not deny that a fortune-teller may sometimes report something apparently true. Rather, it explains why this does not make fortune-telling a source of guidance. A stolen fragment mixed with a hundred lies is more dangerous than pure ignorance because it uses a little truth to sell falsehood.

**The Guards of Heaven and the Limits of Created Power**

The “mighty sentries” are understood by the commentators as angels appointed by God. Angels are not independent protectors; they execute God's command. Their presence shows that the unseen world is governed, not chaotic. There is hierarchy, command, obedience, and boundary.

This matters because human fascination with the unseen often imagines it as a lawless realm of powers competing with God. The Qur'an gives a different picture: even the unseen has gates, guards, permissions, and prohibitions. Jinn cannot go where God bars them. Devils cannot access what God protects. Revelation descends through a guarded order, not through an open marketplace of spirits.

God says:

> *"And We have certainly beautified the nearest heaven with lamps and made them missiles against the devils, and We have prepared for them the punishment of the Blaze."* (Qur'an 67:5)

The verse joins visible beauty, unseen repelling, and final punishment. A single created phenomenon may have more than one divine function.

**The Qur'an Does Not Teach Astrology**

Because the verse mentions flaming stars, it is important to distinguish Qur'anic cosmology from astrology. The Qur'an does not teach that stars control human fate or that human personalities are governed by constellations. It condemns attempts to seek hidden knowledge apart from God. The heavenly bodies are signs, measures of time, adornments, and, by God's will, means in unseen protection — not gods and not fate-writers.

God says:

> *"And landmarks; and by the stars they are guided."* (Qur'an 16:16)

Guided here refers to navigation, not divination. The stars may guide travelers across land and sea, but they do not reveal the moral destiny of souls. Sūrah al-Jinn actually refutes occult misuse of the heavens by showing that attempts to steal unseen knowledge are blocked.

**A Modern Analogy: Protected Communication**

One may compare the image, carefully and imperfectly, to secure communication. Sensitive messages are encrypted, guarded, and protected from interception. Unauthorized listeners may try to breach the system, but stronger security is placed around what must arrive intact. Revelation is far greater than any human communication, but the analogy helps modern readers understand the principle: God's final message is not an unsecured rumor moving through a contaminated channel. It is delivered under divine protection.

This is why the end of the sūrah returns to guards before and behind the messenger. The same theme begins here and concludes there. The believer can trust the Qur'an not because human institutions happened to preserve it, but because God willed its preservation from the unseen route of descent to the visible community of recitation.

## Sūrah al-Jinn 72:9

> **We used to sit in places thereof to listen, but whosoever listens now finds a flaming star lying in wait for him.**

**Expanded Commentary**

**The End of Unauthorized Listening**

The jinn continue: they used to sit in *maqāʿid* — stations, seats, or positions — for listening. The image suggests habitual access. They had known locations or modes by which they attempted to overhear something from the heavenly realm. But “now” anyone who listens finds a flaming star waiting in ambush. The word *raṣadā* carries the sense of lying in wait, surveillance, readiness, and guarded response.

The contrast between “we used to” and “now” is important. Revelation changed the spiritual security of the cosmos. The coming of the final Messenger ﷺ was not only an event in Makkah. It affected the unseen routes through which devils had misled fortune-tellers. The jinn recognized that something immense had happened because their old access no longer worked.

This fits the report of Ibn ʿAbbās preserved in Ṣaḥīḥ al-Bukhārī: the jinn found themselves prevented from heavenly news and struck by flaming missiles, so they searched the earth to discover the cause, until a group heard the Prophet ﷺ reciting Qur'an in prayer. They then returned to their people, saying that this Qur'an was the cause of the barrier.

**A Little Truth Mixed with Much Falsehood Is Still Misguidance**

The previous verse introduced the general issue; this verse emphasizes the former practice of sitting to listen. The hadith about fortune-tellers explains the moral danger of that practice:

> *"That is a word of truth which a jinn snatches and pours into the ear of his ally, but they mix with it a hundred lies."* (al-Bukhārī; Muslim)

This is a permanent warning about the seduction of partial truth. People are often deceived not by pure falsehood, but by falsehood carrying enough truth to gain credibility. A prediction that sometimes appears accurate, a spiritual worker who knows a hidden detail, a charismatic speaker who says some correct things, an ideology that identifies a real injustice while prescribing a false cure — all can use fragments of truth as bait.

Islam does not judge guidance by occasional accuracy. It judges by source, method, theology, and moral fruit. A stolen word mixed with a hundred lies is not guidance. It is corruption with evidence attached.

**Why Fortune-Telling Damages Tawḥīd**

The Prophet ﷺ warned severely against going to fortune-tellers:

> *"Whoever goes to a fortune-teller and asks him about something, his prayer will not be accepted for forty nights."* (Muslim)

The hadith does not mean the person should stop praying; rather, it shows the gravity of the sin and the loss of reward. The reason is that fortune-telling competes with trust in God. It trains the heart to seek security in prohibited knowledge. It makes the servant impatient with God's concealment and vulnerable to manipulation.

Human beings naturally fear uncertainty. Will I be safe? Will I be loved? Will I succeed? What will happen tomorrow? Revelation does not answer every curiosity because not every curiosity is good for the soul. Instead, it teaches the servant how to live rightly without knowing everything. That is more dignified than dependence on occult prediction.

God says:

> *"Perhaps you dislike a thing and it is good for you, and perhaps you love a thing and it is bad for you. God knows, and you do not know."* (Qur'an 2:216)

This verse is the believer's cure for forbidden curiosity. The servant does not need stolen knowledge of tomorrow; he needs trust, obedience, consultation, effort, and patience today.

**Heavenly Boundaries and Human Boundaries**

The image of guarded stations also teaches the value of boundaries. The jinn had places they should not occupy and speech they should not access. When they trespassed, they were repelled. Modern people often resent limits, especially limits on knowledge. But not all access is a right. A doctor may know a patient's private information, but a stranger may not. A judge may hear evidence sealed from the public, but a gossip may not. Parents may withhold certain knowledge from a child until maturity. Boundaries protect order.

If human institutions require confidentiality, how much more the communication of heaven? Revelation descends by God's permission, not by theft. The Prophet ﷺ receives as messenger; devils attempt to snatch as intruders. The difference between the two is the difference between lawful appointment and criminal trespass.

**The Flaming Star as Warning and Mercy**

The flaming star lying in wait is punishment for the intruding devil, but mercy for human beings. It protects them from a world in which devils could more easily decorate falsehood with stolen heavenly fragments. Every blocked theft is a protection of guidance. Sometimes a barrier that frustrates one creature is mercy for another.

This is a broader divine pattern. A person may be prevented from something he desires and later discover that the prevention protected him. The jinn found blocked access; the believing among them interpreted the event correctly and searched until they found the Qur'an. The same event that punished devils guided sincere seekers.

**From Hidden Eavesdropping to Open Revelation**

The spiritual contrast is beautiful: devils strain upward to steal fragments in secret, while God sends down the Qur'an openly for mankind and jinn. The forbidden path is difficult, dangerous, and contaminated; the lawful path is recitation, prayer, and response. Why chase whispers when God has revealed speech? Why seek stolen sparks when the sun of guidance has risen?

This verse therefore redirects religious desire. The servant does not need secret stations in heaven. He needs to sit humbly with the Qur'an, listen attentively, and obey. The jinn who tried the old way found flames; the jinn who listened to the Prophet ﷺ found guidance.

## Sūrah al-Jinn 72:10

> **We do not know whether evil is desired for those upon the earth, or whether their Lord desires guidance for them.**

**Expanded Commentary**

**The Jinn Admit the Limit of Their Knowledge**

After describing the heavenly barriers, the jinn say that they do not know whether evil is intended for the inhabitants of earth or whether their Lord desires guidance for them. This is a remarkable confession. Beings that had sought access to heavenly information now openly admit ignorance. They do not pretend to know God's purpose behind the cosmic change.

This admission is itself guidance. The believing jinn have moved from illicit seeking of hidden knowledge to humility before God's wisdom. They know something has happened; they do not know its final interpretation. This is often the believer's position in life. He sees events but not all meanings. He sees doors close but not every mercy concealed within the closure. He sees hardship but not the full future shaped through it.

The Qur'an teaches this humility:

> *"God knows, and you do not know."* (Qur'an 2:216)

A short sentence, but enough to discipline the whole soul.

**The Adab of Attributing Guidance to God**

The wording of the verse is subtle. Regarding evil, the jinn use the passive: “whether evil is desired for those upon earth.” Regarding guidance, they say explicitly: “or whether their Lord desires guidance for them.” Many scholars point to this as an example of adab in speech about God. Evil is not attributed to Him directly in the same way as guidance, although nothing occurs outside His will and power. Guidance, mercy, and wise direction are attached openly to the Lord.

The Qur'an gives a similar etiquette in the words of Abraham:

> *"And when I am ill, it is He who heals me."* (Qur'an 26:80)

Abraham attributes healing directly to God, while illness is mentioned without saying, “He makes me ill,” although illness too occurs within divine decree. This is not denial of God's comprehensive power; it is reverent speech that recognizes His perfection.

The Prophet ﷺ used the same etiquette in supplication:

> *"Here I am in Your service, and all good is in Your hands, and evil is not attributed to You."* (Muslim)

The meaning is not that evil exists outside God's control, but that pure evil is not ascribed to Him as injustice, aimlessness, or deficiency. What creatures experience as painful may be part of a wise decree whose full goodness they do not yet see.

**One Event May Carry Warning or Mercy**

The jinn do not know whether the blocking of the heavens means punishment for earth's inhabitants or guidance for them. In reality, the coming of the Prophet ﷺ contains both warning and mercy. For those who reject, it removes excuses and threatens punishment. For those who respond, it opens guidance and forgiveness.

The Qur'an often presents revelation in this double role:

> *"And We send down of the Qur'an that which is healing and mercy for the believers, but it increases the wrongdoers only in loss."* (Qur'an 17:82)

The same Qur'an heals one heart and exposes another. The difference lies not in the light, but in the eye receiving it. Sunlight helps the healthy eye see and pains the diseased eye; the fault is not in the sun.

**The Dangers of Over-Interpreting Events**

Verse 10 warns against confidently interpreting every unusual event as divine punishment or divine approval. People often rush to explain disasters, successes, political changes, illnesses, or personal setbacks as if they had access to God's hidden purpose. The believing jinn refuse that arrogance. They say, “We do not know.”

This does not mean events have no meaning. It means their full meaning belongs to God unless revelation clarifies it. The correct response to uncertainty is repentance, gratitude, patience, and obedience — not speculative certainty.

The Qur'an says:

> *"Every soul will taste death, and We test you with evil and good as trial; and to Us you will be returned."* (Qur'an 21:35)

Both hardship and ease are tests. Therefore a painful event may be purification, warning, elevation, consequence, or all of these in ways unknown to us. Ease may be mercy, test, or gradual entrapment for the heedless. The servant's responsibility is not to decode every decree, but to respond to every decree with servitude.

**Guidance as the Lord's Intention of Mercy**

The phrase “their Lord desires guidance for them” is deeply hopeful. The jinn are considering the possibility that the barrier they encountered was not merely punitive but a sign that God was sending guidance to earth. This is what the Prophet's mission was: mercy in the form of warning, law, recitation, and example.

God says about the Prophet ﷺ:

> *"And We have not sent you except as mercy to the worlds."* (Qur'an 21:107)

“The worlds” includes more than one human tribe or generation; many commentators understand it broadly enough to include mankind and jinn. The jinn's own conversion is evidence. A human messenger becomes mercy to hidden listeners.

**Living with Uncertainty Without Losing Trust**

Modern life generates constant uncertainty: economic shifts, illness, conflict, technological change, family instability, and private anxiety. People crave explanations. Some turn to conspiracy; others to despair; others to occult prediction; others to denial. Verse 10 offers a disciplined alternative: admit the limits of knowledge, trust the Lord's wisdom, and seek guidance.

A person driving through fog slows down, turns on the lights, follows the lane markers, and avoids reckless guesses. He does not pretend the fog is not there, nor does he abandon the road. Taqwā works similarly. When the meaning of events is unclear, revelation becomes the lane marker. The servant may not know whether a particular event conceals harm or guidance, but he knows the Lord, the prayer, the lawful, the unlawful, repentance, and the Hereafter.

The jinn's “we do not know” is not weakness. It is the humility that makes real knowledge possible.

## Sūrah al-Jinn 72:11

> **Some among us are righteous, and some among us are otherwise; we are on paths divided.**

**Expanded Commentary**

**The Jinn Are a Moral Community, Not a Single Stereotype**

The believing jinn now describe their own society: some are righteous, and some are less than that. They are on *ṭarā'iq qidadā* — divided paths, separated ways, varied courses. This verse is one of the clearest Qur'anic statements that the jinn are morally diverse. They are not all devils, nor are they all saints, nor are they all sources of danger, nor are they all objects of fascination. They are accountable creatures with differences in belief and conduct.

This corrects two common errors. The first is demonization: imagining every jinn as evil. The second is romanticization: imagining hidden beings as spiritually superior simply because they are hidden. The Qur'an refuses both. Moral worth is not determined by material origin. Being created from fire does not make a creature noble; being created from clay does not make a creature low. Nobility comes through faith, obedience, and God-consciousness.

God gives the universal criterion:

> *"Indeed, the most noble of you in the sight of God is the most mindful of Him."* (Qur'an 49:13)

Although this verse directly addresses human tribal pride, its principle harmonizes with Sūrah al-Jinn: origin is not righteousness. Taqwā is.

**“Some Are Righteous” Means They Can Obey God**

The word *ṣāliḥūn* — righteous ones — comes from the root *ṣ-l-ḥ*, indicating soundness, repair, fitness, and moral wholesomeness. A righteous being is one whose belief and action are put right. The jinn's use of the word shows that they are capable of moral repair and obedience.

This connects directly to the purpose of creation:

> *"And I did not create the jinn and mankind except to worship Me."* (Qur'an 51:56)

Worship is not exclusive to human beings. Jinn too are addressed by divine command according to their nature. They can listen to revelation, believe, call others, and fear punishment. Their righteousness may not take every outward form of human worship, because their bodily nature differs, but the foundation is the same: submission to God.

The Qur'an also addresses both communities on the Day of Judgment:

> *"O company of jinn and mankind, did there not come to you messengers from among you, reciting to you My signs and warning you of the meeting of this Day of yours?"* (Qur'an 6:130)

The address proves accountability. Warning comes before judgment; revelation before reckoning.

**“Otherwise” Includes Degrees, Not One Flat Category**

The phrase *dūna dhālik* — “otherwise,” “less than that,” or “below that” — is broad. It may include outright disbelievers, sinners, weak believers, hypocritical tendencies, and all forms of moral deficiency beneath righteousness. The Qur'an's language is precise: it does not flatten everyone into two crude labels at this point. It acknowledges moral gradation.

This is also true among human beings. Some are deeply righteous; some are openly wicked; many are mixed, struggling, inconsistent, negligent, repentant, or drifting. The Qur'an often speaks in categories while leaving room for degrees because God knows souls with perfect precision.

God says:

> *"And for all are degrees from what they have done, and your Lord is not unaware of what they do."* (Qur'an 6:132)

The verse immediately after addressing jinn and mankind in Sūrah al-Anʿām states that both communities have degrees. Divine judgment is not careless grouping. It is measured knowledge.

**Paths Divided: Belief Creates Direction**

The phrase *ṭarā'iq qidadā* suggests paths cut apart from one another. A path is not merely an opinion; it is a direction of travel. Beliefs become habits, habits become character, character becomes destiny. The jinn are saying that their community, like ours, is not spiritually uniform. They have sects, tendencies, loyalties, and moral roads.

This makes the earlier claim of “the fool among us” more understandable. A community with divided paths can produce callers to truth and callers to falsehood. It also explains why the believing jinn return to warn their people: they know some paths among them lead to ruin.

The Qur'an warns mankind similarly:

> *"And this is My path, straight, so follow it; and do not follow the other paths, lest they separate you from His path."* (Qur'an 6:153)

The image is universal. The straight path unites by direction toward God; side paths divide by desire, speculation, pride, or fear.

**A Lesson Against Essentializing Communities**

Verse 11 teaches intellectual justice. If the jinn themselves say they are morally varied, then believers should be careful about simplistic judgments of whole groups. Among humans too, nations, schools, families, professions, and communities contain righteous and unrighteous people. Justice requires distinction.

This does not erase collective responsibility or patterns of corruption. The Qur'an can criticize communities for dominant sins while still recognizing righteous individuals within them. But it forbids lazy moral thinking. To say “they are all the same” is often a way of avoiding justice.

The Qur'an says about the People of the Book:

> *"They are not all alike. Among the People of the Book is an upright community, reciting the signs of God during the night while they prostrate."* (Qur'an 3:113)

If revelation makes distinctions even when criticizing error, then believers must learn careful speech.

**The Practical Meaning: Choose the Path, Not the Myth**

For the believer, the point is not to become preoccupied with mapping jinn societies. The point is to learn that every accountable creature belongs to a path. The question for a human reader is: which path am I on? Am I among the righteous, or among “otherwise”? Am I moving toward repair, or merely belonging to a group name?

Religious identity alone does not replace actual righteousness. The jinn could not say, “We are jinn, therefore our nature determines our fate.” Humans cannot say, “We are from this family, nation, school, or community, therefore we are safe.” The Qur'an makes each soul answer for belief and deeds.

The verse also reduces unhealthy fear. A Muslim should not imagine that every hidden being has the same intention or power. Nor should he seek relationships with them. He simply recognizes that they too are under God's judgment. The safest concern is not curiosity about their paths, but steadfastness upon ours.

## Sūrah al-Jinn 72:12

> **We knew for certain that we could never thwart God on earth; nor could we ever thwart Him by fleeing.**

**Expanded Commentary**

**The End of the Myth of Escape**

The believing jinn declare a certainty: they can never render God incapable on earth, nor escape Him by fleeing. The Arabic root *ʿ-j-z* relates to inability, incapacity, or being beyond someone's power. To “thwart” God would mean to escape His authority, frustrate His will, or place oneself beyond His reach. The jinn deny this absolutely.

This confession is especially meaningful because jinn are hidden from ordinary human sight and can move in ways humans cannot. A creature with subtle abilities might imagine itself freer from accountability. Yet the believing jinn know that hiddenness before humans is not hiddenness before God. Mobility through creation is not escape from the Creator.

The Qur'an states the principle to both jinn and mankind:

> *"O company of jinn and mankind, if you are able to pass beyond the regions of the heavens and the earth, then pass. You will not pass except with authority."* (Qur'an 55:33)

No creature exits God's kingdom. Even extraordinary movement remains inside permission.

**On Earth and by Flight: Two Failed Strategies**

The verse mentions two forms of imagined escape: remaining on earth and fleeing away. “On earth” may suggest hiding, resisting, building power, or operating within the world as if God could be defeated there. “By fleeing” suggests movement elsewhere — escaping place, circumstance, or consequence. The jinn deny both. Whether one stays or runs, God encompasses him.

Human beings attempt the same two strategies morally. Some try to thwart accountability where they are: through secrecy, influence, legal manipulation, wealth, or intimidation. Others try to flee: changing locations, identities, narratives, or communities. But no change of address becomes a change of Lord. A sin hidden from people is still present before God; an injustice buried in paperwork is still alive in the divine record.

God says:

> *"And you will not cause failure upon the earth, nor in the heaven, and you have besides God no protector or helper."* (Qur'an 29:22)

The verse removes every illusion at once: no escape, no alternate protector, no helper against Him.

**The Jinn Refute the Very Power People Attributed to Them**

This verse directly undermines the practice mentioned in verse 6. If jinn themselves cannot escape God, how could they protect humans from God? If they cannot thwart divine judgment for themselves, how could they function as ultimate refuges for others? The believing jinn are, in effect, saying: do not exaggerate us. We are not sovereigns. We are subjects.

This is one of the Qur'an's great methods for dismantling shirk: it makes the alleged protectors confess their helplessness. On the Day of Judgment, false objects of worship will disown those who worshipped them. Angels, prophets, righteous servants, and even devils will not accept divine status.

The Qur'an says:

> *"And who is more astray than one who calls upon besides God those who will not respond to him until the Day of Resurrection, and they are unaware of their supplication? And when the people are gathered, they will be enemies to them and will deny their worship."* (Qur'an 46:5–6)

The tragedy of shirk is that the worshipper gives his heart to what cannot save him.

**Certainty About God's Power Produces Moral Seriousness**

The jinn say *ẓanannā* in verse 7 about their previous false assumption, but here the meaning is certainty in many translations: we knew for certain. Guidance has moved them from assumptions to conviction. They now understand their true place in existence.

Belief in God's inescapable authority is not meant to produce despair. It produces seriousness. If no one can escape Him, then repentance is urgent, injustice is foolish, and obedience is rational. The sinner's best hope is not to flee from God, but to flee to Him.

The Qur'an gives the command:

> *"So flee to God. Indeed, I am to you from Him a clear warner."* (Qur'an 51:50)

This is the only successful flight: not from God as though one could outrun Him, but to God from everything that distances the soul from Him. The same reality that terrifies the arrogant comforts the repentant. There is no refuge against God, but there is refuge with God.

**Modern Forms of Imagined Escape**

Technology can create a false sense of concealment. People hide behind encrypted messages, anonymous accounts, financial structures, private browsing, or social masks. Institutions hide wrongdoing behind bureaucracy. Nations hide crimes behind power. Individuals rewrite their stories until even they half-believe them. Verse 12 speaks across all of this: none of it thwarts God.

The Qur'an repeatedly affirms God's comprehensive knowledge:

> *"He knows the stealthy glance of the eyes and what the breasts conceal."* (Qur'an 40:19)

If the glance and hidden motive are known, then the file, room, border, and screen offer no ultimate concealment. This should make a believer more honest in private than in public, because public accountability is weaker than divine knowledge.

**Freedom Through Knowing One Cannot Escape**

Paradoxically, knowing that one cannot escape God can free the soul. Much anxiety comes from trying to control the uncontrollable, hide the unhideable, and secure the impossible. When the servant accepts that he lives entirely within God's authority, he can stop pretending. He can repent honestly. He can ask for help. He can face consequences with trust.

The jinn's confession is therefore not only a threat. It is a return to reality. Reality is safer than illusion, even when reality humbles us. The one who admits he cannot outrun God has already taken the first step toward running to Him.

## Sūrah al-Jinn 72:13

> **And when we heard the guidance, we believed in it, for whosoever believes in his Lord shall fear neither detraction nor oppression.**

**Expanded Commentary**

**The Qur'an Is Called Guidance Because It Leads Home**

The jinn now return to the decisive moment: “When we heard the guidance, we believed in it.” Here *al-hudā* — the guidance — refers to the Qur'an. The definite article suggests not one guidance among many equal options, but the guidance now recognized as divine. They had heard a wondrous Qur'an; they now name it guidance because its wonder had direction.

The Qur'an frequently names itself guidance:

> *"This is the Book in which there is no doubt, guidance for the mindful."* (Qur'an 2:2)

> *"O mankind, there has come to you instruction from your Lord, healing for what is in the breasts, and guidance and mercy for the believers."* (Qur'an 10:57)

Guidance is more than information. A map in a drawer does not guide a traveler unless it is read and followed. The jinn heard and believed; the guidance entered action.

**Faith in “His Lord” Is Personal Accountability**

The verse shifts from “we believed” to the general principle: whoever believes in *his Lord* will not fear detraction or oppression. The phrase “his Lord” makes faith personal. It is not merely belief in a distant Creator, but recognition of the One who owns, nurtures, commands, and will judge the individual servant.

This personal lordship matters because moral life is personal. Communities believe, but each soul stands before God. Families inherit religion, but each heart must affirm. The jinn believed as a group, yet the rule applies to “whoever” believes.

The Qur'an says:

> *"Whoever does righteousness, whether male or female, while being a believer — We will surely give him a good life, and We will surely reward them according to the best of what they used to do."* (Qur'an 16:97)

God's promise reaches the individual within the crowd.

**Neither Detraction Nor Oppression: Perfect Justice for the Believer**

The words translated “detraction” and “oppression” are *bakhs* and *rahaq*. *Bakhs* means reducing something from its due, diminishing its value, shortchanging, or depriving a person of rightful measure. *Rahaq*, already encountered in verse 6, indicates burden, oppression, excessive imposition, sin, or being overwhelmed. The meaning is that the believer need not fear that God will reduce his good deeds or add to his sins. He will not be shortchanged and will not be unjustly burdened.

This is a magnificent statement of divine justice. Human systems often fail by both errors: they fail to recognize good, or they punish beyond guilt. God does neither. No sincere deed is lost; no soul is charged with another's sin.

The Qur'an states:

> *"And We place the scales of justice for the Day of Resurrection, so no soul will be wronged at all. And if there is the weight of a mustard seed, We will bring it forth. And sufficient are We as accountant."* (Qur'an 21:47)

And again:

> *"So whoever does an atom's weight of good will see it, and whoever does an atom's weight of evil will see it."* (Qur'an 99:7–8)

These verses explain why the believer's fear is transformed. He still fears his sins and shortcomings, but he does not fear injustice from God.

**Faith Does Not Remove All Fear, but It Removes the Wrong Fear**

The verse does not mean believers never feel fear in this world. Prophets feared harm, believers feared enemies, parents fear for children, and the righteous fear their own accountability. The meaning is more precise: the believer does not fear being wronged by his Lord. He does not fear that God will erase sincere good or fabricate guilt against him.

This distinction is spiritually healing. Many people carry fear of unfair judgment because human judgment has wounded them. They were misunderstood, misrepresented, ignored, or punished unfairly. The Qur'an teaches that God's judgment is not an amplified version of human injustice. It is perfect knowledge joined with perfect justice and mercy.

God says:

> *"Indeed, God does not wrong even the weight of an atom; and if there is a good deed, He multiplies it and gives from Himself a great reward."* (Qur'an 4:40)

God not only refuses to reduce good; He multiplies it. The believer's Lord is more generous than strict equivalence.

**A Direct Reversal of the Earlier Rahaq**

The sūrah's use of *rahaq* is instructive. Seeking refuge in jinn increased *rahaq* in verse 6; believing in the Lord removes fear of *rahaq* in verse 13. False refuge burdens; true faith relieves. Shirk makes the heart more frightened, more dependent, and more vulnerable to manipulation. Īmān gives the soul a just Lord.

This does not mean faith removes responsibility. It means responsibility becomes meaningful because it is measured by One who knows. A student may fear an unfair examiner more than the exam itself. If he knows the examiner is perfectly just, his anxiety changes: he still studies, but he does not fear arbitrary failure. Similarly, the believer strives, repents, and hopes before a Lord who will not wrong him.

**The Ethical Fruit: Do Not Shortchange Others**

Belief in a Lord who does not commit *bakhs* should train believers not to shortchange people. The Qur'an condemns those who reduce measure and value:

> *"Woe to those who give less, who when they take a measure from people take in full, but when they give by measure or weight to them, they cause loss."* (Qur'an 83:1–3)

A person who believes God will not diminish his deeds should not diminish wages, praise, rights, time, attention, or fairness owed to others. Theology becomes ethics. Divine justice becomes a human obligation.

The believing jinn heard guidance and believed; their reward is security from divine injustice. The human reader should hear the same guidance and become both trusting toward God and just toward creation.

## Sūrah al-Jinn 72:14

> **Some among us submit and some among us are unjust; those who submit seek guidance;**

**Expanded Commentary**

**Islam Exists Among the Jinn Too**

The jinn divide themselves into two categories: *al-muslimūn* and *al-qāsiṭūn*. The first are those who submit — Muslims in the root sense of surrendering to God. This is a remarkable phrase because it shows that Islam, as submission to the Lord through His guidance, is not limited to human ethnicity, language, or visible culture. Jinn too may be Muslim.

The Qur'an elsewhere presents Islam as the religion of all prophets in its essential meaning:

> *"Indeed, the religion with God is Islam."* (Qur'an 3:19)

And:

> *"Whoever seeks other than Islam as religion, it will never be accepted from him, and in the Hereafter he will be among the losers."* (Qur'an 3:85)

For human beings after the coming of Muhammad ﷺ, Islam means following the final Messenger. For jinn who heard his recitation, it likewise meant believing in the revealed guidance and submitting to the Lord who sent it.

**Qāsiṭ and Muqsiṭ: Same Root, Opposite Direction**

The word translated “unjust” is *qāsiṭ*. It comes from the root *q-s-ṭ*, which can produce two opposite meanings depending on form. *Muqsiṭ* means one who acts justly; *qāsiṭ* means one who deviates from justice and turns away from truth. Al-Qurṭubī and other commentators draw attention to this contrast: the *muqsiṭ* gives what is due, while the *qāsiṭ* departs from the right course.

The Qur'an loves the just in the positive form:

> *"Indeed, God loves those who act justly."* (Qur'an 49:9)

But here the *qāsiṭūn* are those who have made themselves unjust by deviation. This linguistic contrast is morally illuminating. Justice is direction toward the truth; injustice is not merely harming others outwardly, but departing from the path God made straight.

**Submission Is an Active Search for Guidance**

The verse says, “those who submit seek guidance.” The Arabic *taḥarrā rashadā* carries the sense of deliberately seeking, aiming for, investigating, and choosing the right way. Submission is not passivity. A Muslim is not someone who accidentally inherited a label and then stopped seeking. A true submitter actively looks for *rushd* — sound direction.

This returns to verse 2, where the Qur'an was said to guide to *rushd*. The submitter hears guidance and then continues to seek its mature application. Faith begins with surrender, but it grows through pursuit.

The Qur'an praises those who strive toward God:

> *"And those who strive for Us — We will surely guide them to Our ways. And indeed, God is with the doers of excellence."* (Qur'an 29:69)

The verse joins human striving and divine guidance. The servant seeks; God guides. Neither cancels the other.

**Free Will and Moral Responsibility Among Jinn**

The division between submitters and unjust ones proves moral responsibility. The jinn are not compelled into one destiny by their nature. They choose paths, respond or refuse, believe or rebel. The same is true of human beings. Creation from fire or clay does not eliminate accountability.

This is important for understanding evil. Islam does not teach that Iblīs disobeyed because fire forced him, nor that Adam sinned because clay forced him. Both acted under responsibility. The Qur'an records Iblīs's false reasoning:

> *"He said, 'I am better than him. You created me from fire and created him from clay.'"* (Qur'an 7:12)

His mistake was to turn material origin into moral superiority. Sūrah al-Jinn corrects that error by showing jinn who submit and jinn who deviate. Fire can obey; clay can obey. Fire can rebel; clay can rebel. The criterion is not substance, but submission.

**The Social Meaning of “Those Who Submit”**

A community becomes healthy when its members seek guidance rather than merely defend identity. The jinn do not say, “Those among us who belong to the correct tribe are guided.” They say the submitters sought right direction. This challenges Muslims too. Islam is not meant to become a badge worn without pursuit. A Muslim community must keep asking: where is the guidance of God in our worship, family life, trade, politics, technology, speech, and treatment of the weak?

When Muslims stop seeking *rushd*, they may keep the name of Islam while losing the maturity of submission. The Prophet ﷺ warned that outward forms can remain while inward meaning weakens. The Qur'an repeatedly calls believers back to living obedience, not inherited confidence.

God says:

> *"O you who believe, respond to God and to the Messenger when he calls you to that which gives you life."* (Qur'an 8:24)

The address is to believers, yet they are still commanded to respond. Faith must remain alive.

**Guidance Requires Choosing Over and Over**

The submitters “seek guidance” because life presents new situations. A person may know the general path but still need guidance in marriage, money, anger, grief, leadership, speech, doubt, and repentance. The straight path is stable, but our circumstances change. Seeking *rushd* means applying revelation wisely as new tests arise.

Taqwā is like careful driving through a dangerous mountain road. The driver does not say, “I chose the correct road at the beginning, so I no longer need attention.” He keeps watching turns, signs, weather, and speed. In the same way, the believer does not say, “I am Muslim, therefore I am safe from deviation.” He keeps seeking guidance, because heedlessness can turn even a good road into a dangerous journey.

## Sūrah al-Jinn 72:15

> **and as for the unjust, they are kindling for Hell.’”**

**Expanded Commentary**

**A Severe Image for a Severe Deviation**

The jinn complete their division: the submitters seek guidance, while the unjust become fuel for Hell. The Arabic *ḥaṭab* means firewood or kindling — material fed into a fire so that it burns. The image is intentionally severe. A life of rebellion does not merely lead a person to punishment; it prepares him to become, by his choices, part of what fuels punishment.

The Qur'an uses similar language elsewhere:

> *"O you who believe, protect yourselves and your families from a Fire whose fuel is people and stones, over it angels harsh and severe; they do not disobey God in what He commands them, and they do what they are commanded."* (Qur'an 66:6)

A human being was created for honor through worship, knowledge, mercy, and nearness to God. To become fuel is therefore a terrible reversal of purpose. The accountable creature who should have burned with love, repentance, and remembrance instead becomes material for the Fire through injustice.

**Can Beings Created from Fire Be Punished by Fire?**

Because jinn are created from smokeless flame, some may wonder how fire can punish them. The question misunderstands both creation and punishment. Human beings are created from earth, yet earthly things can harm them: stones crush, dust suffocates, metal cuts, and hunger returns the body to its material weakness. Being made from a substance does not make one immune to every form or intensity of that substance.

Moreover, the Fire of the Hereafter is not identical to worldly fire in all respects. It is a created punishment of God, with properties He wills. The Qur'an does not ask the servant to reduce the Hereafter to the physics of this world. It asks him to recognize that the Creator who formed beings from fire can punish fire-created beings however He wills.

God says:

> *"And the word of your Lord will be fulfilled: 'I will surely fill Hell with jinn and mankind all together.'"* (Qur'an 11:119)

This verse explicitly joins jinn and humans in liability to Hell. The matter is therefore not speculative.

**Hell as Justice, Not Divine Cruelty**

The verse says “as for the unjust.” Hell is connected to *qisṭ* violated — justice abandoned. The Qur'an does not present punishment as arbitrary. It comes after creation, signs, warnings, messengers, conscience, and opportunities for return. Those who become fuel are not punished because God needs to punish. They are punished because they turned away from the purpose for which they were made and persisted in injustice.

God says:

> *"We did not wrong them, but they wronged themselves."* (Qur'an 11:101)

This principle is vital. Divine punishment is never the tantrum of power. It is the final manifestation of truth against falsehood, justice against oppression, and reality against chosen delusion.

**The Fire Begins as a Direction Before It Becomes a Destination**

A person's path in this world already resembles his destination in the next. Envy burns the heart; rage burns relationships; greed consumes time; lust consumes dignity; arrogance scorches humility. These are not Hell itself, but they are hellish qualities. The one who lives by them becomes compatible with the Fire unless he repents.

This is not metaphor replacing reality. The Qur'an teaches a real Hell. But moral states in this life prepare the soul for realities in the next. The unjust become *ḥaṭab* because they spent life feeding flames: flames of oppression, desire, pride, and rebellion.

The Qur'an says of those who consume orphan wealth unjustly:

> *"Indeed, those who consume the wealth of orphans unjustly are only consuming fire into their bellies, and they will burn in a Blaze."* (Qur'an 4:10)

The sin already contains the fire in unseen form; the Hereafter unveils it.

**Warning as Mercy**

A severe warning in revelation is mercy when it comes before the punishment. A sign that says “bridge out ahead” is not cruelty; it is rescue. Sūrah al-Jinn allows the believing jinn themselves to warn their people. They know the danger and name it plainly.

Modern readers sometimes prefer religious language stripped of warning. But a religion that never warns cannot protect. A doctor who refuses to mention a deadly diagnosis out of politeness is not compassionate. The Qur'an's warnings are precise because the stakes are real.

The Prophet ﷺ described his mission with an urgent image:

> *"My example and your example is like a man who kindled a fire, and moths and insects began falling into it while he tried to prevent them. I am holding you back from the Fire, but you are slipping from my hands."* (al-Bukhārī; Muslim)

The Messenger's warning is not hatred of people; it is the mercy of someone pulling them from flames.

**The Door Before the Fire Remains Open in This Life**

Even after such a warning, the Qur'an keeps the door of repentance open. The unjust are not told this so they can despair, but so they can stop being unjust. The jinn who speak these words had themselves come from a community containing foolishness, false theology, and wrong assumptions. They changed when they heard guidance. Their warning proves that change is possible.

God says:

> *"Say: O My servants who have transgressed against themselves, do not despair of the mercy of God. Indeed, God forgives all sins. Indeed, He is the Forgiving, the Merciful."* (Qur'an 39:53)

The one who is walking toward the Fire can turn around. The kindling is not thrown into the Fire while life and repentance remain. Verse 15 is severe because mercy wants the servant to wake before severity becomes final.

## Sūrah al-Jinn 72:16

> **And [say], “[It was revealed unto me] that if they hold firm to the path, We shall give them abundant water,**

**Expanded Commentary**

**The Sūrah Turns from Reported Speech to Divine Instruction**

After the testimony of the believing jinn, the sūrah shifts into divine instruction. The verse may refer to the jinn, to the people of Quraysh, or more broadly to all morally responsible beings addressed by revelation. “If they hold firm to the path” — *law istaqāmū ʿalā al-ṭarīqah* — God promises abundant water. The principle is wide: steadfastness upon God's path brings provision, life, and blessing by His permission.

The word *istaqāmū* comes from the root *q-w-m*, to stand upright. It suggests not a momentary emotional response, but steady alignment. A person may be moved by guidance briefly, but *istiqāmah* means remaining upright after the feeling passes. It is the difference between touching the road and walking it.

The Qur'an praises this quality:

> *"Indeed, those who say, 'Our Lord is God,' then remain upright — the angels descend upon them, saying, 'Do not fear and do not grieve, but receive glad tidings of Paradise which you were promised.'"* (Qur'an 41:30)

Faith is stated; steadfastness proves it.

**The Path Is Singular Because Truth Has Direction**

The verse says “the path” — *al-ṭarīqah*. In the immediate context, it is the path of guidance recognized by the jinn. It is the path of tawḥīd, revelation, worship, and obedience. The Qur'an often uses road imagery because human life is movement. No one remains spiritually stationary. Every choice advances a direction.

The Prophet ﷺ gave a concise explanation of uprightness when a Companion asked for a statement in Islam after which he would need no one else:

> *"Say, 'I believe in God,' then remain upright."* (Muslim)

This hadith could stand as a commentary on the verse. Belief without uprightness becomes claim; uprightness without belief has no ultimate root. Together they form the path.

**Abundant Water as Provision, Life, and Mercy**

God promises *mā'an ghadaqā* — abundant, overflowing water. In the Arabian environment, water meant life, pasture, crops, animals, settlement, and survival. To promise water was to promise provision in its most concrete form. The image would not have sounded abstract to the first hearers. Water was the difference between life and death.

The Qur'an repeatedly links faithfulness with earthly blessing while maintaining that all provision is from God:

> *"And if only the people of the towns had believed and been mindful, We would have opened upon them blessings from the heaven and the earth; but they denied, so We seized them for what they used to earn."* (Qur'an 7:96)

And Noah told his people:

> *"Ask forgiveness of your Lord. Indeed, He is ever Forgiving. He will send the sky upon you in abundant rain and increase you in wealth and children and provide for you gardens and provide for you rivers."* (Qur'an 71:10–12)

The connection is not mechanical superstition. It is moral causality under divine wisdom. God may bless obedience outwardly; He may also test believers with hardship. But societies aligned with justice, gratitude, chastity, honesty, and worship are more fit for sustainable blessing than societies built on corruption.

**Not a Prosperity Gospel**

This verse must not be misread as saying every righteous individual will become materially wealthy or every hardship proves sin. Prophets suffered, righteous people were persecuted, and some disbelievers enjoyed worldly abundance. The Qur'an itself warns that provision can be either mercy or test. Therefore the promise of abundant water is true, but it operates within God's wisdom, timing, and larger trial.

God says:

> *"And if God were to extend provision for His servants without measure, they would surely transgress throughout the earth; but He sends down in due measure whatever He wills. Indeed, He is, of His servants, Aware and Seeing."* (Qur'an 42:27)

Sometimes abundance would ruin a person, and withholding becomes mercy. Sometimes abundance is given to expose gratitude or ingratitude. The verse teaches dependence on God, not entitlement over Him.

**Water as Symbol of Revelation and Inner Life**

Many commentators also see abundant water as a symbol of all good — provision, ease, blessing, knowledge, or even Paradise. The Qur'an often uses water for life because revelation revives hearts as rain revives earth.

God says:

> *"Know that God gives life to the earth after its death. We have made clear to you the signs that you may understand."* (Qur'an 57:17)

Just as dry soil cannot produce without water, a dry heart cannot flourish without guidance. Holding firm to the path opens the soul to a kind of inner water: tranquility, clarity, repentance, and useful knowledge. A materially poor believer with living faith may possess more real “water” than a wealthy person dying of spiritual thirst.

**The Social Logic of Uprightness and Provision**

There is also a practical social logic. Communities that honor God's path are commanded to avoid theft, fraud, oppression, intoxication, sexual exploitation, family breakdown, and neglect of the poor. These commands are not arbitrary. They preserve trust, labor, inheritance, public safety, and compassion. When societies abandon them, resources may still exist, but blessing drains away through corruption.

Water is a perfect image. It must be received, channeled, protected, and shared. A corrupt society may have rain but poison its rivers; wealth but destroy its families; technology but lose its purpose. *Istiqāmah* is the moral irrigation system that allows provision to become blessing.

## Sūrah al-Jinn 72:17

> **that We may try them therewith, and whosoever turns away from the remembrance of his Lord, He leads him to a grievous punishment,**

**Expanded Commentary**

**Provision Is a Test, Not Proof of Final Approval**

The previous verse promised abundant water; this verse explains why: “that We may try them therewith.” The pronoun refers to the blessing — water, provision, ease, or abundance. The Qur'an immediately prevents a shallow reading of prosperity. If God gives, He is testing. Provision is not automatically proof that the recipient is beloved, nor is hardship automatically proof that the sufferer is rejected.

The Arabic *naftinahum* comes from *fitnah*, a test that reveals what is inside, like fire revealing the quality of metal. Abundance reveals gratitude or arrogance, generosity or greed, remembrance or heedlessness. A hungry person knows he needs God; a wealthy person may forget. Scarcity tests patience; abundance tests gratitude and restraint.

The Prophet ﷺ taught this principle clearly:

> *"The world is sweet and green, and God is making you successors in it to see how you will act."* (Muslim)

The world is attractive, fresh, and inviting. That is precisely why it tests.

**The Harder Test May Be Ease**

Many people recognize hardship as a test but fail to recognize comfort as one. Yet comfort can be more spiritually dangerous because it often arrives disguised as success. When life becomes easy, prayer may become delayed, charity optional, repentance postponed, and dependence on God less felt. The person does not openly reject God; he simply becomes too comfortable to remember Him.

The Qur'an warns of this pattern:

> *"So when they forgot that by which they had been reminded, We opened to them the doors of all things, until when they rejoiced in what they were given, We seized them suddenly, and they were then in despair."* (Qur'an 6:44)

This is one of the most frightening verses about abundance. The doors of all things may open not as reward, but as gradual exposure. The danger is not wealth itself, but heedlessness inside wealth.

**Turning Away from Remembrance**

The verse then says: whoever turns away from the remembrance of his Lord will be led to a grievous punishment. *Dhikr* may refer specifically to the Qur'an, to revelation generally, to prayer, to remembrance of God, or to the whole life of servitude. These meanings are connected. The Qur'an is the revealed remembrance; prayer embodies remembrance; a mindful life extends remembrance into conduct.

God says:

> *"And whoever turns away from My remembrance — indeed, he will have a constricted life, and We will gather him on the Day of Resurrection blind."* (Qur'an 20:124)

The constricted life may exist even amid abundance. A person can have water, wealth, and opportunity while his heart lives in anxiety, restlessness, envy, and meaninglessness. Turning from remembrance narrows the soul even when the house is large.

**“His Lord”: The One He Forgot Is the One Sustaining Him**

The phrase “the remembrance of his Lord” deepens the rebuke. The one forgotten is not a stranger. He is the Lord — the One who created, provides, nurtures, forgives, and keeps the servant alive moment by moment. Forgetfulness of such a Lord is not neutral. It is ingratitude.

Imagine a patient kept alive by constant care who refuses even to acknowledge the physician, or a child nourished daily who denies dependence on the parent. These analogies are limited, because God's sustaining is infinitely greater, but they help reveal the moral ugliness of heedlessness. The servant lives on gifts and forgets the Giver.

The Qur'an says:

> *"So remember Me; I will remember you. And be grateful to Me and do not be ungrateful."* (Qur'an 2:152)

Remembrance and gratitude are joined because forgetting God is the root of ingratitude.

**A Grievous, Ascending Punishment**

The punishment is described as *ʿadhāban ṣaʿadā*. The word *ṣaʿad* carries the sense of ascending, climbing, increasing, or becoming difficult and severe. Commentators explain it as a punishment that is burdensome, increasing, or from which there is no relief. The image suggests not a flat penalty but a rising hardship.

This fits the psychology of turning away from God. Sin often escalates. What begins as neglect becomes habit; habit becomes identity; identity becomes hostility to reminder. A person who refuses remembrance may find each return harder than the last, like climbing a steep slope while carrying more weight. The Hereafter then unveils the final consequence of that chosen ascent into difficulty.

**Abundance Without Dhikr Becomes a Trap**

The relationship between verses 16 and 17 is essential. Water is good, but water without remembrance can test a people into ruin. Wealth is good, but wealth without gratitude becomes arrogance. Knowledge is good, but knowledge without humility becomes pride. Influence is good, but influence without accountability becomes oppression.

The Prophet ﷺ feared the trial of abundance for his community. He said:

> *"By God, it is not poverty that I fear for you, but I fear that the world will be opened for you as it was opened for those before you, and you will compete for it as they competed for it, and it will destroy you as it destroyed them."* (al-Bukhārī; Muslim)

This hadith is a direct commentary on the verse's logic. The world opened can be more dangerous than the world withheld.

**Practical Protection: Turning Blessing into Worship**

How does one pass the test of abundant water? By converting blessing into remembrance. Drink and say *al-ḥamdu lillāh*. Earn lawfully. Spend responsibly. Give zakāh and charity. Use health for prayer and service. Use knowledge to benefit, not dominate. Use influence to protect rights. Use family as a trust, not an ego extension.

In this way, provision becomes a ladder rather than a trap. The same water that tests can purify when received with gratitude. Verse 17 is not anti-world; it is anti-forgetfulness. The world in the hand with God in the heart is a field of worship. The world in the heart with God forgotten becomes constriction and punishment.

## Sūrah al-Jinn 72:18

> **and that places of worship are for God; so do not call upon another alongside God,**

**Expanded Commentary**

**The Mosque Belongs to God Before It Belongs to Any Community**

The verse declares that *al-masājid* are for God. The word *masājid* is the plural of *masjid*, a place of prostration. In common usage it means mosques, but the word is deeper than architecture. It comes from *sujūd*, prostration — the act in which the servant places the noblest visible part of the body, the face, upon the ground before God. A mosque is therefore not merely a community hall, cultural center, or heritage building. It is a place defined by surrender.

Because the places of prostration are for God, the command follows naturally: “so do not call upon another alongside God.” The purpose of the mosque is tawḥīd. If a building is beautiful, crowded, historic, well-funded, or socially active but its worship is directed to other than God, it has betrayed the meaning of *masjid*. Sacred space is sacred because it is surrendered to the One.

The Qur'an says:

> *"And who is more unjust than one who prevents the name of God from being mentioned in His mosques and strives toward their ruin?"* (Qur'an 2:114)

The mosque is ruined not only by physical destruction, but by emptying it of sincere remembrance or filling it with what contradicts tawḥīd.

**The Whole Earth as a Place of Prostration**

The Prophet ﷺ expanded the meaning of *masjid* in a famous authentic hadith:

> *"The earth has been made for me a place of prostration and a means of purification; so wherever a man of my community is when prayer time comes, let him pray."* (al-Bukhārī; Muslim)

This is one of the honors given to the final community. Worship is not confined to one temple, mountain, or priestly location. The earth itself becomes a field of prostration, with exceptions defined by law. This does not reduce the special honor of mosques; it expands the servant's awareness that every place is under God.

In this reading, verse 18 means that all places of prostration — formal mosques and the earth on which one prays — belong to God. A believer may stand in an airport, hospital room, desert, prison, office, or field and turn that patch of earth into a place of worship by praying to God alone.

**The Seven Limbs of Prostration**

Some commentators also mention that *masājid* can refer to the bodily parts used in prostration. The Prophet ﷺ said:

> *"I have been commanded to prostrate on seven bones: the forehead"* — and he pointed to his nose — *"the two hands, the two knees, and the ends of the two feet."* (al-Bukhārī; Muslim)

If the “places of prostration” include the limbs, then the verse means that the very body by which one worships belongs to God. The forehead is for God; the hands are for God; the knees and feet are for God. This reading turns the body into a mosque. The servant should not use limbs created for prostration in rebellion against the One to whom they bow.

The ethical implication is powerful. The hand that touches the ground in sujūd should not steal. The forehead that bows should not carry arrogance. The feet that stand in prayer should not walk deliberately toward sin. The body remembers in worship what the soul must honor in life.

**Calling Upon God Is the Heart of Worship**

The command “do not call upon another alongside God” uses *duʿāʾ*, calling, supplicating, invoking. Duʿāʾ is not only request; it is worshipful dependence. The Prophet ﷺ said:

> *"Supplication is worship."* (Abū Dāwūd; al-Tirmidhī)

This explains why invoking others alongside God is shirk when the invocation gives them a share of divine hearing, hidden power, ultimate rescue, or control over fate. To call upon a created being in ordinary, present, capable matters is not the issue: asking a living person for water is ordinary assistance. But calling upon an absent or dead being as if it hears all, knows one's secret state, and can answer by unseen power enters the territory of worship.

The Qur'an gives the comprehensive rule:

> *"And do not call upon besides God that which neither benefits you nor harms you. If you did, then indeed you would be among the wrongdoers."* (Qur'an 10:106)

The mosque is the place where this truth is meant to be clearest.

**Sacred Places Must Not Become Personality Cults**

Verse 18 also warns communities not to let mosques become stages for ego. A mosque may be harmed when people attach worshipful loyalty to founders, donors, imams, saints, political movements, ethnic identities, or institutional pride. Honoring scholars and righteous people is part of Islam; worshipping them, invoking them, or making the mosque a monument to human greatness is not.

God says:

> *"Say: Indeed, my prayer, my sacrifice, my living, and my dying are for God, Lord of the worlds. He has no partner; and with that I have been commanded, and I am the first of those who submit."* (Qur'an 6:162–163)

If prayer and sacrifice are for God, then the spaces of prayer and the hearts within them must be purified for Him.

**Modern Applications: Architecture, Funding, and Intention**

A community may spend great effort building a mosque. This can be righteous when done for God, with lawful wealth, humility, accessibility, and service. But verse 18 asks a deeper question: after construction, who is the mosque for? Is it for God, or for prestige? Is it for prayer, knowledge, mercy, and remembrance, or for factional control? Are the poor welcome? Are converts supported? Are women treated with dignity within the bounds of Islamic guidance? Are children taught to love God's house? Are disputes handled with taqwā?

A mosque “for God” is not measured only by its dome or carpet. It is measured by tawḥīd, prayer, justice, humility, and sincere remembrance. The command not to call upon anyone with God must echo not only in words but in institutional character.

## Sūrah al-Jinn 72:19

> **and that when the servant of God rises to call upon Him, they well-nigh swarm upon him.”**

**Expanded Commentary**

**The Highest Title Given to the Prophet Is Servant**

The verse refers to *ʿabd Allāh* — the servant of God. Most commentators understand this as the Prophet Muhammad ﷺ. The title is profound. In moments of supreme honor, the Qur'an calls him God's servant. At the Night Journey:

> *"Glory be to Him who carried His servant by night from the Sacred Mosque to the Farthest Mosque."* (Qur'an 17:1)

At the revelation of the Book:

> *"Praise be to God who sent down upon His servant the Book and did not make in it any crookedness."* (Qur'an 18:1)

At the standing in prayer in this verse, again: the servant of God. The Prophet's greatness is not diminished by servanthood; it is defined by perfect servanthood. The nearer a creature is to God, the more completely he knows he is a servant.

The Prophet ﷺ himself warned against exaggeration:

> *"Do not exaggerate in praising me as the Christians exaggerated in praising the son of Mary. I am only His servant, so say: the servant of God and His Messenger."* (al-Bukhārī)

This hadith is a direct protection of the meaning of verse 19. Love of the Prophet ﷺ is obligatory and central, but it must remain within tawḥīd. His highest human honor is that he worshipped God most perfectly.

**Rising to Call Upon Him**

The servant “rises to call upon Him.” This may refer to standing in prayer, reciting the Qur'an, supplicating, or calling people to God. These meanings overlap in the Prophet's life. His prayer was recitation, remembrance, and supplication; his recitation was daʿwah; his daʿwah was an invitation to worship.

The scene may be the Prophet ﷺ standing in prayer while the jinn crowd near to listen. It may also describe the Quraysh pressing upon him in hostility when he prayed and called to God. Some commentators mention both possibilities. The ambiguity is meaningful: truth attracts crowds of different kinds — seekers who want guidance and opponents who want to stop it.

Sūrah al-Aḥqāf supports the first meaning:

> *"When they attended it, they said, 'Listen attentively.'"* (Qur'an 46:29)

The jinn gathered in eagerness around revelation. But Makkan history supports the second: the Prophet ﷺ often faced pressure when worshipping publicly.

**Libadā: Crowding, Layering, Pressing Together**

The word *libadā* suggests something piled, densely gathered, or layered together. It can describe people crowding so closely that they seem stacked over one another. If the referent is the jinn, the image conveys eagerness: they almost swarm over him to hear the Qur'an. If the referent is opponents, it conveys pressure: they nearly pile upon him to oppose his call.

Both possibilities reveal something true about revelation. The Qur'an is never neutral in its effects. It awakens longing in receptive hearts and agitation in resistant ones. A single recitation can make one listener draw near and another grow angry. The difference lies in the listener's state.

The Qur'an says:

> *"And when God alone is mentioned, the hearts of those who do not believe in the Hereafter shrink with aversion; but when those besides Him are mentioned, immediately they rejoice."* (Qur'an 39:45)

Tawḥīd exposes hearts. Those who love God lean in; those attached to rivals feel threatened.

**The Servant Stands Even When Crowds Gather**

Whether the crowd is eager or hostile, the Prophet ﷺ stands to call upon his Lord. This is a model of steadfastness. He does not stop worship because people watch; he does not alter tawḥīd because people object; he does not turn prayer into performance because listeners gather. His orientation remains vertical: servant before Lord.

This is essential for anyone engaged in religious work. Public attention is dangerous whether it comes as admiration or attack. Admiration may tempt the caller to perform for people. Attack may tempt him to soften truth wrongly or abandon the call. The Prophet's title “servant of God” protects both sides. The caller belongs to God before he belongs to audience, institution, tribe, or critics.

**The Beauty of Being Only a Servant**

Modern culture often treats servanthood as humiliation because it imagines freedom as having no master. The Qur'an teaches the opposite: every human being serves something. Some serve appetite, money, status, ideology, fear, resentment, or public approval. Servitude to God is liberation from servitude to lesser masters.

God says:

> *"There is none in the heavens and the earth but that he comes to the Compassionate as a servant."* (Qur'an 19:93)

The question is not whether one will be a servant, but whether one will be a servant knowingly and nobly. The Prophet ﷺ is honored as *ʿabd Allāh* because he chose and perfected what every creature already is.

**Prayer as the Center of Real Influence**

The verse pictures influence beginning not with strategy, wealth, or power, but with a servant rising to call upon God. The jinn's conversion did not begin with a marketing campaign. It began with Qur'anic recitation in prayer. This does not negate planning in daʿwah, but it restores priority. Spiritual influence flows from sincere connection to God.

A teacher whose private prayer is alive carries a different weight from one who relies only on technique. A parent who sincerely calls upon God for children has entered the deepest means. A community that wants guidance should not neglect the standing of servants before God in prayer.

The servant rises; unseen listeners gather. This is the sūrah's quiet lesson about spiritual causality.

## Sūrah al-Jinn 72:20

> **Say, “I call only upon my Lord, and I ascribe none as a partner unto Him.”**

**Expanded Commentary**

**The Prophet's Answer Is Pure Tawḥīd**

The Prophet ﷺ is commanded to say: “I call only upon my Lord, and I ascribe none as a partner unto Him.” This verse answers every crowd around him — the jinn who listened, the humans who opposed, and every later community tempted to misunderstand his mission. His call is not to himself. His worship is not divided. His dependence is not shared. He calls upon his Lord alone.

The Arabic *innamā* gives restriction: only, nothing but. The Prophet's daʿwah is exclusive in its object. He does not call upon idols, angels, jinn, ancestors, stars, or saints. Nor does he ask people to call upon him as a divine figure. The Messenger is the guide to worship of God, not a rival object of worship.

The Qur'an states the same prophetic mission elsewhere:

> *"Say: This is my way. I call to God upon insight — I and whoever follows me. Glory be to God, and I am not of those who associate partners with Him."* (Qur'an 12:108)

Calling to God upon insight is the prophetic path. Calling to oneself is a corruption of that path.

**Duʿāʾ and Daʿwah Meet in One Word**

“I call” may mean supplication — calling upon God in worship — and it may mean daʿwah — calling people to God. The two meanings are connected. The one who calls people to God must first call upon God himself. Daʿwah without duʿāʾ becomes technique without poverty before the Lord; duʿāʾ without daʿwah may become private piety without concern for others. The Prophet ﷺ combined both perfectly.

The Prophet ﷺ said:

> *"Supplication is worship."* (Abū Dāwūd; al-Tirmidhī)

Thus the first meaning of the verse is worshipful invocation: I invoke my Lord alone. But because the Prophet is also a messenger, his invocation becomes public proclamation: the only One I call upon is the only One to whom I call you.

**A Response to Pressure from Quraysh**

In the Makkan context, the Prophet ﷺ was pressured to compromise. Quraysh were not offended merely by private spirituality; they were offended by exclusive tawḥīd. They could tolerate another deity added to the religious landscape, but not a message that invalidated all rivals. “I call only upon my Lord” directly rejects negotiated polytheism.

The Qur'an records attempts at compromise in Sūrah al-Kāfirūn:

> *"Say: O disbelievers, I do not worship what you worship, nor are you worshippers of what I worship."* (Qur'an 109:1–3)

Sūrah al-Jinn gives the same refusal in another form. The Prophet's mission cannot be domesticated into one cult among many. Tawḥīd demands the whole heart.

**Religious Leadership Must Point Beyond Itself**

This verse is a safeguard for every scholar, preacher, spiritual teacher, activist, and institution. The caller must ask: am I calling people to God, or to loyalty to me? Am I teaching them to worship the Lord, or to depend emotionally and intellectually on my personality? Do people leave my presence more attached to revelation, prayer, repentance, and obedience, or more attached to my brand?

The Prophet ﷺ, the greatest of creation, says he calls only upon his Lord and associates none with Him. If even he does not center himself as an object of worshipful dependence, no lesser religious figure may do so.

The Qur'an warns that no prophet would call people to worship him:

> *"It is not for a human being that God gives him the Book, judgment, and prophethood, then he says to the people, 'Be servants to me instead of God,' but rather, 'Be devoted scholars because of what you have taught of the Book and because of what you have studied.'"* (Qur'an 3:79)

True religious authority produces servants of God, not servants of personalities.

**The Simplicity That Defeats Superstition**

After the sūrah's discussion of jinn, heavenly barriers, and unseen matters, this verse brings the believer back to simplicity: call upon your Lord and associate none with Him. Islam does not require the servant to master hidden maps of spirits. It requires sincere worship, lawful conduct, and trust in God.

This is spiritually protective. People fascinated by the unseen can become distracted from the obvious duties: prayer, honesty, repentance, family rights, charity, humility, and Qur'an. The Prophet's answer cuts through distraction. The unseen exists, but your worship belongs to God. Jinn exist, but do not call upon them. Hidden knowledge exists, but do not seek it through forbidden paths. Your Lord is enough.

**The Daily Practice of This Verse**

A believer lives verse 20 whenever he says *iyyāka naʿbudu wa iyyāka nastaʿīn* — “You alone we worship, and from You alone we seek help.” He lives it when he raises his hands in duʿāʾ, when he refuses occult help, when he begins tasks with *bismillāh*, when he accepts that no created being can answer like God, and when he loves the Prophet ﷺ as Messenger without turning love into worship.

The verse is short because tawḥīd is simple at its center. The struggle is not complexity; it is sincerity.

## Sūrah al-Jinn 72:21

> **Say, “I have no power over what harm or guidance may come to you.”**

**Expanded Commentary**

**The Messenger Denies Independent Control**

The Prophet ﷺ is commanded to say that he possesses no power over harm or guidance for his listeners. The Arabic *lā amliku lakum* means “I do not own” or “I do not possess” for you. The negation is not of his honor, truthfulness, or intercession by God's permission. It is a denial of independent lordship. The Prophet ﷺ cannot, by himself, force guidance into a heart or avert divine consequence from someone who rejects God.

This is one of the Qur'an's clearest safeguards against exaggerating prophets. Islam gives the Messenger ﷺ the highest human rank: final prophet, beloved of God, mercy to the worlds, intercessor by permission, teacher, model, and witness. But it does not make him divine. The messenger conveys; God owns harm and guidance.

The Qur'an says similarly:

> *"Say: I do not possess for myself benefit or harm except what God wills. If I had known the unseen, I would have acquired much good, and no harm would have touched me. I am only a warner and bringer of glad tidings to a people who believe."* (Qur'an 7:188)

If the Prophet ﷺ says this about himself, others have even less claim to independent power.

**Guidance Cannot Be Forced Into the Heart**

The word translated “guidance” here is *rashadā*, connected to *rushd* in verse 2 and verse 14. The Prophet ﷺ can recite the Qur'an, explain truth, embody guidance, pray for people, warn them, and invite them. But he does not own the inward turning of their hearts. That belongs to God.

God says to the Prophet ﷺ:

> *"Indeed, you do not guide whom you love, but God guides whom He wills; and He knows best those who are guided."* (Qur'an 28:56)

This verse was understood by many commentators in connection with the Prophet's grief over loved ones who did not accept faith. It comforts every caller to God: your duty is sincere conveyance, not ownership of results.

**The Difference Between Means and Ownership**

The Prophet ﷺ is certainly a means of guidance. God says elsewhere:

> *"And indeed, you guide to a straight path, the path of God."* (Qur'an 42:52–53)

There is no contradiction. In one sense, the Prophet guides by teaching, showing, and calling. In another sense, he does not guide by creating faith in the heart. The first is guidance of indication; the second is guidance of enabling. The first belongs to messengers and callers by God's permission; the second belongs to God alone.

This distinction is essential in theology and daily life. A parent may guide a child by teaching, example, and prayer, but cannot own the child's heart. A scholar may guide students by evidence, but cannot create sincerity. A friend may advise, but cannot force repentance. Recognizing this prevents despair and arrogance.

**No Power Over Harm**

The Prophet ﷺ also denies owning *ḍarr* — harm. He cannot independently decide punishment or protection. If people reject, the consequences come by God's justice, not personal revenge from the Messenger. If people are saved, they are saved by God's mercy, not by a prophet acting as a rival power.

This corrected the worldview of people accustomed to tribal holy men, idols, and spirits who were imagined to dispense harm or benefit. The Prophet ﷺ did not replace one magical center with another. He restored all power to God.

The Qur'an commands:

> *"Say: Who is it that can protect you from God if He intends harm for you or intends mercy for you? And they will not find for themselves besides God any protector or helper."* (Qur'an 33:17)

The question has no answer except tawḥīd.

**A Cure for Blaming the Messenger**

Disbelievers often treated prophetic warning as if the messenger himself were bringing the threat from personal hostility. Verse 21 clarifies: the Prophet ﷺ does not own harm. He warns of what God has revealed. Rejecting the messenger is like blaming a doctor for naming an illness or blaming a signpost for warning of a cliff. The warning is mercy precisely because danger is real.

This also protects the caller's heart. If people reject guidance, he grieves and continues, but he does not become tyrannical. He cannot compel what God did not place in his hand. The Qur'an tells the Prophet ﷺ:

> *"So remind; you are only a reminder. You are not over them a controller."* (Qur'an 88:21–22)

Daʿwah is serious without being coercive.

**The Practical Balance: Work Hard, Trust God**

Verse 21 teaches a balance needed by parents, teachers, imams, activists, and anyone who loves another soul. Work as though your effort matters, because God commanded effort. Trust as though results belong to God, because they do. Neglecting effort is disobedience; claiming ownership of hearts is arrogance.

A farmer plants, waters, and protects the field, but he does not create the seed's life. Likewise, the caller plants evidence, waters with compassion, protects with patience, and prays. God gives growth when He wills.

## Sūrah al-Jinn 72:22

> **Say, “None will protect me from God, and I shall never find refuge apart from Him**

**Expanded Commentary**

**Even the Messenger Stands Under God's Authority**

The Prophet ﷺ is commanded to say that no one can protect him from God. This is among the strongest Qur'anic statements of prophetic servanthood. If the final Messenger ﷺ himself cannot be shielded from God by anyone, then no saint, angel, jinn, ruler, tribe, institution, or ideology can shield anyone else.

The phrase *lan yujīranī mina Allāhi aḥad* uses the language of protection and asylum. In tribal Arabia, *ijārāh* — granting protection — was socially powerful. A person under the protection of a strong clan might be safe from enemies. The verse transfers the concept to the ultimate level and negates it: no protector can grant asylum against God.

The Qur'an states the same truth in a concise form:

> *"There is no refuge from God except to Him."* (Qur'an 9:118)

This is one of the most profound paradoxes of faith: the only safety from God's displeasure is return to God Himself.

**Multaḥad: No Alternate Shelter**

The verse continues: “I shall never find refuge apart from Him.” The word *multaḥad* indicates a place of turning aside, shelter, refuge, or escape-route. The Prophet ﷺ declares that no such place exists apart from God. Creation offers means, but not ultimate refuge. People may support, advise, shelter, or defend by God's permission, but none can protect from divine judgment.

This continues the logic of verse 12, where the jinn admitted they could never thwart God on earth or by fleeing. The Prophet ﷺ now declares the same principle from his own side. The highest human messenger and the believing jinn agree: there is no escape from God's authority.

**Fear of God as the Foundation of Courage**

If no one can protect from God, then fear of creation must be limited. The one who fears God properly becomes less enslaved to people. He may still take worldly threats seriously, but he does not obey creation in disobedience to the Creator. This is why prophetic courage is rooted in servanthood, not personality.

God praises those who convey His messages:

> *"Those who convey the messages of God and fear Him and do not fear anyone except God; and sufficient is God as accountant."* (Qur'an 33:39)

The verse describes the inner spine of all true messengers. They fear God enough to be freed from ultimate fear of people.

**The Prophet's Dependence Teaches Our Dependence**

Some may think spiritual rank reduces dependence. The Qur'an teaches the opposite. The greater the rank, the deeper the awareness of dependence. The Prophet ﷺ prayed, sought forgiveness, stood in night prayer, and called upon his Lord more than anyone. His declaration that no one can protect him from God is not a weakness in his mission; it is the purity of his mission.

The Prophet ﷺ taught Ibn ʿAbbās a principle every believer needs:

> *"If you ask, ask God; and if you seek help, seek help from God. Know that if the whole community gathered to benefit you, they would not benefit you except with something God had written for you; and if they gathered to harm you, they would not harm you except with something God had written against you."* (al-Tirmidhī)

This hadith is a practical commentary on verse 22. Created means exist, but the decree belongs to God.

**No Refuge Apart from Him Does Not Mean Despair**

At first, the verse may sound terrifying: no one can protect me from God. But its second half contains the door of hope: refuge is with Him. The believer is not trapped before a hostile power; he is summoned back to the Merciful Lord. God's inescapability is frightening only to the one determined to flee. To the repentant, it means the door is always near because God is not absent from any place.

The Qur'an says:

> *"And when My servants ask you concerning Me, indeed I am near. I answer the call of the caller when he calls upon Me."* (Qur'an 2:186)

The One from whom there is no escape is also the One who is near when called.

**Modern False Refuges**

People seek refuge in many things: money, credentials, social status, family name, political power, therapeutic language, religious affiliation, secrecy, or public image. These may help in limited worldly ways, but none protects from God. A corrupt leader may escape court, but not resurrection. A respected person may hide sins from the community, but not from the Knower of hearts. A religious worker may receive praise, but praise does not erase accountability.

Verse 22 teaches the believer to use worldly means without worshipping them. Insurance, medicine, law, planning, and community support have their place. But the heart knows: my final refuge is God. If He is pleased, no loss destroys me; if He is displeased, no shelter saves me.

## Sūrah al-Jinn 72:23

> **if I do not convey from God and His messages. And whosoever disobeys God and His Messenger, his shall be the Fire of Hell, abiding therein forever,**

**Expanded Commentary**

**The Prophet's Refuge Is Faithful Conveyance**

This verse continues the previous sentence. The grammar allows more than one reading, as the classical commentators explain. It may mean: none will protect me from God if I fail to convey from Him and His messages. It may also mean: I possess no power over you except the conveyance of God's message. Both meanings reinforce the same truth: the Prophet's duty is *tablīgh* — faithful transmission.

The Messenger ﷺ is not sent to control hearts, manipulate outcomes, or invent religion. He is sent to convey what God revealed, fully and faithfully. His safety, honor, and fulfillment of mission lie in obedience to this duty.

God commands him elsewhere:

> *"O Messenger, convey what has been sent down to you from your Lord. If you do not, then you have not conveyed His message. And God will protect you from the people."* (Qur'an 5:67)

The verse shows the gravity of conveyance. A messenger who concealed revelation would fail the purpose of messengership — and the Prophet ﷺ, by consensus of the Muslim community, conveyed completely.

**“From God and His Messages”**

The phrase “from God and His messages” indicates that revelation comes from God and consists of divine communications entrusted to the Messenger. He does not own the content. He receives, preserves, recites, explains, embodies, and delivers. This is why obedience to the Messenger is obedience to God, not because the Messenger is divine, but because he faithfully conveys the divine command.

The Qur'an states:

> *"Whoever obeys the Messenger has obeyed God."* (Qur'an 4:80)

This principle is often misunderstood. Prophetic authority is not independent rivalry with God's authority; it is God's authority reaching human beings through the messenger He chose.

**The Prophet ﷺ Confirmed His Conveyance Publicly**

At the Farewell Pilgrimage, before a vast gathering of Companions, the Prophet ﷺ asked whether he had conveyed the message. They answered yes. He then called God to witness:

> *"Have I conveyed?"* They said, *"Yes."* He said: *"O God, bear witness."* (al-Bukhārī; Muslim)

This moment is one of the great scenes of Islamic history. The Messenger ﷺ did not leave the religion as a private archive or elite secret. He delivered it publicly, repeatedly, and practically. The community became witness that the trust had been fulfilled.

**Disobedience to God and Messenger**

The verse then warns: whoever disobeys God and His Messenger will have the Fire of Hell, abiding therein forever. The context is rejection of the message, not an ordinary sinful lapse by a believer who remains within faith and may be forgiven by God's mercy. Sunni theology distinguishes between disbelief that eternally bars salvation if one dies upon it, and major sins committed by Muslims, which are grave but remain under God's will.

The Qur'an says:

> *"Indeed, God does not forgive association with Him, but He forgives what is less than that for whom He wills."* (Qur'an 4:48)

Thus verse 23 is not a license to declare every disobedient Muslim eternally damned. It is a warning against the disobedience of rejection, denial, and refusal of God and His Messenger.

**Why Obeying the Messenger Is Rationally Necessary**

Some people claim to obey God while dismissing the Messenger's authority. The Qur'an rejects this because revelation is not self-delivered into each person's private imagination. God chose messengers precisely so His will would be recited, explained, and embodied. To reject the messenger while claiming loyalty to the sender is like rejecting an official envoy while claiming obedience to the king who sent him.

God says:

> *"And it is not for a believing man or believing woman, when God and His Messenger have decided a matter, that they should have any choice in their affair."* (Qur'an 33:36)

This is not authoritarianism in the human sense. It is the logic of revelation. If God has spoken through His Messenger, the servant's freedom lies in submission, not in pretending neutrality after the truth is known.

**Scholars and Callers Inherit a Portion of Tablīgh**

The Prophet's conveyance is complete, but the responsibility to transmit continues within the community according to capacity. Scholars, teachers, parents, and ordinary believers convey portions of guidance. They are not prophets and receive no revelation, but they inherit the duty of truthful transmission.

The Prophet ﷺ said:

> *"Convey from me, even if it is one verse."* (al-Bukhārī)

This hadith democratizes responsibility. One need not be a great scholar to convey what one truly knows, but one must not convey what one does not know. Verse 23 and verse 5 together produce balance: convey, but do not lie about God.

**The Warning as Moral Clarity**

The mention of Hell is not an emotional threat detached from reason. If God creates, sustains, sends messengers, clarifies truth, warns, and offers forgiveness, then deliberate refusal has consequence. Moral choice would be meaningless if rejection and submission had the same end.

A road sign saying “danger ahead” is mercy; ignoring it has consequences. The Prophet ﷺ conveyed from God so that no one could say they were not warned. The seriousness of the Fire matches the seriousness of turning away from the Lord who sent guidance.

## Sūrah al-Jinn 72:24

> **such that when they see what they are promised, they will know who is weaker in helpers and fewer in number.”**

**Expanded Commentary**

**The Moment When Appearances Collapse**

The verse looks ahead to the time when rejecters see what they were promised. Then they will know who is weaker in helpers and fewer in number. In Makkah, the disbelievers could measure power by visible standards: tribe, wealth, numbers, public influence, and control of sacred space. By those measures the early Muslims appeared vulnerable. The verse announces that a day will come when the measurement itself changes.

“What they are promised” may refer to the punishment of this world, such as defeat, or to the Day of Judgment, or to both. Many commentators mention the Battle of Badr as an early worldly unveiling, when a small believing community was aided against a larger force. But the fullest meaning is the Hereafter, when all false appearances end.

The Qur'an gives a parallel statement:

> *"Say: Whoever is in error — let the Compassionate extend for him an extension, until when they see what they were promised, either the punishment or the Hour, then they will know who is worse in position and weaker in forces."* (Qur'an 19:75)

Both verses teach that worldly extension is not proof of truth. It may simply be time granted before exposure.

**Helpers Who Cannot Help**

The word “helpers” refers to supporters, allies, protectors, or forces relied upon besides God. In this world, falsehood often surrounds itself with helpers: crowds, institutions, wealth, propaganda, armed strength, social pressure, and spiritual claims. But the Qur'an repeatedly asks whether these helpers can stand when God's promise arrives.

God says:

> *"And they have taken besides God gods that they might be helped. They are not able to help them, and they will be for them soldiers brought forth."* (Qur'an 36:74–75)

The irony is severe. People take false gods for help, but those objects become part of the evidence against them. What they thought would defend them becomes a witness to their misguidance.

**Few in Number: Quantity Is Not Ultimate Strength**

The verse mentions being “fewer in number.” This does not mean numbers never matter in worldly planning. Islam is not naïve about organization, preparation, or communal strength. But numbers are not ultimate proof. A large crowd can be wrong; a small group can be upon truth. The jinn who believed were a small group, yet their testimony is immortalized. Quraysh were socially dominant, yet their rejection was exposed.

The Qur'an reminds believers of this principle through the words of those who met Goliath:

> *"How many a small company has overcome a large company by permission of God. And God is with the patient."* (Qur'an 2:249)

The phrase “by permission of God” is crucial. Smallness alone is not virtue, and largeness alone is not falsehood. The point is that truth and divine aid are not determined by human counting.

**The Psychology of Delayed Realization**

The verse says “when they see.” Some truths are accepted by believers through revelation before they are seen. Disbelievers often demand sight before submission. The problem is that some seeing comes too late. When punishment arrives or the Hour begins, recognition no longer functions as saving faith.

The Qur'an says:

> *"Then their faith did not benefit them when they saw Our punishment."* (Qur'an 40:85)

This is a sobering law. The test of faith is to trust God's warning before the unseen becomes visible. Everyone will eventually know; salvation belongs to those who respond before forced certainty.

**Worldly Badr and Final Judgment**

If the verse includes Badr, it teaches that God's promises may begin to unfold in history. Quraysh saw that the weak they mocked were not abandoned. Yet worldly victories remain signs, not the final reckoning. Some believers lose battles; some disbelievers win temporary power. The Hereafter is the complete unveiling.

The early Muslims needed this promise because they were outnumbered. Modern believers may need it in different ways. A person trying to live honestly may feel outnumbered in a corrupt workplace. A young Muslim preserving chastity may feel outnumbered in a culture of desire. A family holding prayer may feel strange among relatives who mock religion. Verse 24 reminds them: do not measure truth by immediate crowd support.

**Strength Is Being Helped by God**

The real question is not “How many are with me?” but “Am I with God?” A person may have few human helpers but be under divine care. Another may have many allies but no protection when God calls him to account.

The Prophet ﷺ and Abū Bakr were physically few in the cave during the Hijrah, yet God recorded the true balance:

> *"When the two were in the cave, when he said to his companion, 'Do not grieve; indeed, God is with us.'"* (Qur'an 9:40)

Two with God are not merely two. A multitude without God is not truly strong. Verse 24 teaches the final arithmetic of faith.

## Sūrah al-Jinn 72:25

> **Say, “I know not whether that which you are promised is nigh or whether my Lord has appointed a term;**

**Expanded Commentary**

**The Prophet ﷺ Does Not Claim What God Has Not Revealed**

The Prophet ﷺ is commanded to say that he does not know whether the promised event is near or whether his Lord has appointed for it a longer term. This is a striking statement of prophetic honesty. The same Messenger who receives revelation does not pretend to know what has not been revealed to him. His authority is therefore strengthened, not weakened, by his limits.

The promised matter may refer to punishment, the Hour, or both. The disbelievers often asked mockingly when the promised threat would arrive. The Qur'an answers not by satisfying their curiosity, but by clarifying the Prophet's role: he warns truthfully, but the timing belongs to God.

A parallel verse says:

> *"Say: I do not know whether what you are promised is near or whether my Lord will appoint for it a distant term."* (Qur'an 21:109)

The repetition across the Qur'an shows that date-setting is not part of prophetic guidance.

**Knowing the Reality Is Different from Knowing the Timing**

The Prophet ﷺ knows by revelation that resurrection, judgment, reward, and punishment are real. But he does not independently know the exact timing of what God has kept hidden. This distinction is important. Lack of knowledge about timing does not weaken certainty about reality.

A person may know death is certain without knowing the date of death. He may know an exam is scheduled without knowing every question. He may know a court summons is real without knowing the judge's full procedure. The rational response is preparation, not mockery.

The Qur'an records the disbelievers' repeated question:

> *"They say, 'When is this promise, if you are truthful?'"* (Qur'an 67:25)

And answers:

> *"Say: The knowledge is only with God, and I am only a clear warner."* (Qur'an 67:26)

This is exactly the doctrine of verse 25.

**The Hidden Timing of the Hour Protects the Test**

If the exact time of punishment or the Hour were known, many people would attempt to schedule repentance around it. Concealment preserves sincerity and vigilance. A student who knows the teacher may inspect the work at any time behaves differently from one who knows the inspection will occur only at a distant fixed hour. Hidden timing keeps the servant awake.

When the angel Jibrīl asked the Prophet ﷺ about the Hour in the famous hadith, the Prophet answered:

> *"The one questioned about it knows no more than the questioner."* (Muslim)

The greatest angel and the greatest human messenger, in that scene, do not possess the timing. This should end the arrogance of anyone who claims to calculate the Hour through numerology, astrology, political events, or private visions.

**“My Lord Has Appointed a Term”**

The word *amad* refers to an appointed span, limit, or term. The timing may be near or extended, but it is not random. God has appointed it. This gives the believer calm. Events are not drifting without decree. The Hour does not arrive early by accident or late by weakness. Punishment does not miss its appointed wisdom.

God says:

> *"For every nation is a term; when their term comes, they will not delay it by an hour, nor will they advance it."* (Qur'an 7:34)

The same principle applies to individuals, nations, and cosmic history. Time is a trust moving toward appointment.

**Date-Setting as a Spiritual Distraction**

Many religious communities become fascinated with predicting final events. They track signs, decode numbers, attach certainty to political developments, or circulate dramatic claims. Verse 25 disciplines this impulse. The Prophet ﷺ says, “I do not know” about the timing. The believer should be ashamed to claim certainty where the Messenger was commanded to deny independent knowledge.

The useful question is not “When exactly?” but “Am I ready?” A man once asked the Prophet ﷺ about the Hour. The Prophet redirected him:

> *"What have you prepared for it?"* (al-Bukhārī; Muslim)

This is prophetic pedagogy. Curiosity about timing may leave the soul unchanged; preparation reforms it.

**Living Between Nearness and Appointed Delay**

The verse leaves the promised matter suspended between nearness and a longer term. This uncertainty is spiritually productive. If it is near, repentance cannot wait. If it is delayed, steadfastness cannot be temporary. The believer must be ready for both immediate meeting and long obedience.

This balance protects against two diseases. Thinking the end is certainly far breeds heedlessness. Thinking it is certainly immediate can produce panic, irresponsibility, or neglect of worldly duties. The Qur'an gives neither disease room. The Hour may be near; the term may be extended. Therefore live prepared, patient, and upright.

## Sūrah al-Jinn 72:26

> **Knower of the Unseen, He does not disclose His Unseen to anyone,**

**Expanded Commentary**

**The Absolute Knowledge of the Hidden Belongs to God**

The verse names God *ʿĀlim al-ghayb* — Knower of the Unseen. *Ghayb* is what is absent from created perception: future events, hidden realities, inner secrets, angelic matters, the full meaning of decrees, and whatever God has not exposed to a creature. Human beings know only what God allows through senses, reason, experience, and revelation. Jinn know some things hidden from us, but not the unseen absolutely. Angels know what God teaches them, but not what He withholds.

The Qur'an states:

> *"And with Him are the keys of the unseen; none knows them except Him. And He knows what is on the land and in the sea. Not a leaf falls but He knows it, nor a grain in the darknesses of the earth, nor anything moist or dry, except that it is in a clear record."* (Qur'an 6:59)

This verse expands the meaning of *ʿĀlim al-ghayb*. God's knowledge is not only of great cosmic secrets; it includes the falling leaf and hidden grain.

**“His Unseen” Shows Ownership, Not Merely Awareness**

The phrase “His Unseen” is important. The unseen is not an independent realm God happens to observe. It belongs to Him. He owns its keys, boundaries, disclosure, and concealment. Creatures may discover relative unseen matters — what was hidden to them but knowable through investigation — but the true unseen remains under God's authority.

For example, a doctor may discover an illness hidden inside the body; a scientist may discover a law of nature previously unknown; a historian may uncover a buried document. These are unseen relative to human ignorance, but they are not the absolute *ghayb* reserved to God. The *ghayb* in this verse includes what no creature can access unless God discloses it.

God says:

> *"Say: None in the heavens and the earth knows the unseen except God."* (Qur'an 27:65)

The statement includes jinn, angels, humans, and every created being.

**The Verse Refutes Fortune-Tellers and Occult Claims**

In the context of Sūrah al-Jinn, this verse directly refutes the idea that jinn, soothsayers, magicians, astrologers, or spiritual intermediaries possess reliable access to the unseen. Earlier verses described how attempts to eavesdrop were blocked. Now the doctrine is stated plainly: God does not disclose His unseen to anyone as an open possession.

The Prophet ﷺ warned against seeking such claims:

> *"Whoever goes to a fortune-teller and asks him about something, his prayer will not be accepted for forty nights."* (Muslim)

The prohibition protects tawḥīd and sanity. A person who believes others know his future becomes vulnerable to fear, manipulation, and fatalism. Revelation frees him: your future is not in the hands of a reader of stars, cards, spirits, or numbers. It is in the knowledge and decree of your Lord, who commanded you to act righteously today.

**The Five Keys of the Unseen**

The Prophet ﷺ connected the “keys of the unseen” to the end of Sūrah Luqmān. Ibn ʿUmar reported that the Messenger of God ﷺ said:

> *"The keys of the unseen are five; none knows them except God: no one knows what will happen tomorrow except God; no one knows what is in the wombs except God; no soul knows what it will earn tomorrow; no soul knows in what land it will die; and no one knows when rain will come except God."* (al-Bukhārī)

This hadith should be understood carefully. Human beings may know some aspects of weather patterns or fetal development through signs and technology, but they do not possess comprehensive, independent, certain knowledge of all details, outcomes, destinies, and divine purposes. The absolute keys remain with God.

The Qur'anic verse behind the hadith says:

> *"Indeed, God — with Him is knowledge of the Hour; He sends down the rain; He knows what is in the wombs; no soul knows what it will earn tomorrow; and no soul knows in what land it will die. Indeed, God is Knowing, Aware."* (Qur'an 31:34)

The verse closes human pretension. We live one heartbeat away from realities we cannot command.

**Why Concealment Is Mercy**

People often imagine that knowing the unseen would comfort them. In many cases it would crush them. If a person knew every future grief, every betrayal, every loss, and every exact date of death, ordinary life might become unbearable. If he knew every future success, he might become arrogant or lazy. If he knew the hidden faults of everyone around him, relationships might collapse. God's concealment is often mercy.

Concealment also preserves moral choice. The servant acts without seeing the full ledger. He gives charity not knowing how God will multiply it. He resists sin not seeing the punishment. He repents not seeing the angels recording his tears. This makes faith meaningful.

The Qur'an begins its description of the mindful with belief in the unseen:

> *"Those who believe in the unseen, establish prayer, and spend out of what We have provided them."* (Qur'an 2:3)

Belief in the unseen is not escape from reality; it is trust in the part of reality God has revealed while accepting the part He has concealed.

## Sūrah al-Jinn 72:27

> **save to the one whom He approves as a messenger. Then He dispatches before him and behind him a guard,**

**Expanded Commentary**

**The Exception Is Revelation, Not Occult Access**

After stating that God does not disclose His unseen to anyone, the verse gives the exception: except to one whom He approves as a messenger. This exception does not open the door to fortune-tellers, magicians, or self-appointed mystics. It defines the lawful disclosure of unseen knowledge: God reveals what He wills to messengers He chooses.

The word *irtaḍā* — “He approves” or “He is pleased with” — indicates divine selection. Prophethood is not acquired through meditation, lineage, magic, intelligence, or spiritual ambition. It is God's choice. A messenger does not break into the unseen; he is entrusted with revelation.

God says:

> *"God chooses from the angels messengers and from the people. Indeed, God is Hearing, Seeing."* (Qur'an 22:75)

Selection belongs to God because revelation is His speech and His trust.

**A Messenger Knows Only What God Discloses**

The verse does not mean a messenger knows all unseen things. It means God discloses from His unseen what is necessary for the mission. The Prophet ﷺ knew realities of the Hereafter, past nations, angels, signs of the Hour, and divine law through revelation. But he did not claim unrestricted knowledge. Verse 25 already commanded him to deny knowledge of the exact timing of the promised matter.

The Qur'an says:

> *"Say: I do not say to you that I possess the treasures of God, nor do I know the unseen, nor do I say to you that I am an angel. I only follow what is revealed to me."* (Qur'an 6:50)

This is the balance: the Prophet ﷺ receives true unseen knowledge by revelation, but he does not possess the unseen independently.

**Guards Before and Behind the Messenger**

God then dispatches guards before and behind the messenger. The guards are understood by the commentators as angels who protect revelation from devils attempting to interfere. The image is one of complete security: in front and behind, the route is watched. Revelation is not left exposed.

This connects back to verses 8–9, where the jinn found heaven filled with strong guards and flaming stars. The same theme now reaches its doctrinal conclusion. God guards the descent of revelation so that His messages arrive faithfully.

The Qur'an describes the angelic delivery of revelation:

> *"Indeed, it is the word of a noble messenger, possessed of power, secure with the Owner of the Throne, obeyed there and trustworthy."* (Qur'an 81:19–21)

The “noble messenger” here is commonly understood as Jibrīl, who brings revelation by God's command. He is powerful, obeyed, and trustworthy. The chain from God to messenger is protected by trust, power, and divine command.

**The Qur'an Is Not the Word of a Devil**

The same passage continues:

> *"And your companion is not mad. And he certainly saw him on the clear horizon. And he is not withholding of the unseen. And it is not the word of an accursed devil."* (Qur'an 81:22–25)

This is directly relevant to Sūrah al-Jinn. The Qur'an is not a jinn-message, trance, poetic possession, or devilish whisper. The jinn themselves testify to its guidance, while the angels guard its descent. Both the content and the chain refute the accusations of the disbelievers.

**Chain of Custody: A Modern Analogy for Revelation**

In legal and scientific settings, the chain of custody matters. Evidence must be protected from contamination; medicine must be sealed against tampering; official communication must be authenticated. Revelation is infinitely more serious. Verse 27 teaches that God Himself secures the chain.

The analogy is limited because God's protection is unlike human procedure, but it helps illustrate the principle: revelation is not an unsecured rumor. It is disclosed by the Knower of the Unseen to an approved messenger, guarded in its descent, then conveyed by a truthful prophet whose life itself testifies to the message.

**Karāmāt and the Boundary of Prophethood**

The verse also helps distinguish prophetic revelation from other extraordinary matters. Islamic tradition recognizes that God may honor righteous servants with *karāmāt* — extraordinary gifts — but these are not revelation, not lawgiving, and not independent access to the unseen. They do not make a saint equal to a prophet, nor can they override Qur'an and Sunnah.

Any claimed spiritual experience must be judged by revelation. If it commands disobedience, claims new law, encourages shirk, or produces arrogance, it is rejected. The approved messenger is the standard, not the claimant of experiences.

**Trusting the Message in an Age of Suspicion**

Modern people often ask how revelation can be trusted. Verse 27 gives part of the Qur'anic answer: the Lord of the unseen chooses the messenger and guards the message. Trust is not blind dependence on human memory alone. It begins in God's own protection.

This does not cancel the historical work of preservation through recitation, writing, Companions, scholars, and transmission. Rather, those visible means are themselves part of divine preservation. The unseen guard and the visible community serve one decree: that guidance reaches those for whom God sent it.

## Sūrah al-Jinn 72:28

> **that He may know that they have indeed conveyed the messages of their Lord. And He encompasses whatsoever is with them and keeps a numbered count of all things.”**

**Expanded Commentary**

**The Purpose of Guarding Revelation**

The final verse explains the purpose of the guards: that it may be known that the messengers have conveyed the messages of their Lord. The wording “that He may know” has been discussed by theologians and commentators because God's knowledge is eternal and complete. He does not learn after ignorance. Therefore the phrase is understood in ways befitting divine perfection: that His knowledge becomes manifest in the world of occurrence, that the messengers know the message has been protected, that people know the messengers conveyed, or that the proof is established outwardly.

This is a common Qur'anic mode of speech. God knows all things before they occur, yet He tests servants so that what He eternally knows becomes actualized and manifest in deeds for which they are justly judged.

God says:

> *"And We will surely test you until We make evident those who strive among you and the patient, and We will test your affairs."* (Qur'an 47:31)

God's testing does not remove His prior knowledge; it manifests the truth of servants in the realm of action.

**The Messages of Their Lord Are a Trust Fully Delivered**

The messengers convey *risālāt rabbihim* — the messages of their Lord. The plural may refer to the many commands, revelations, warnings, promises, and instructions entrusted to messengers, or to the messages conveyed by all messengers across history. The phrase “their Lord” emphasizes that messengers are servants under lordship, not owners of religion.

The Qur'an praises the prophetic office:

> *"Those who convey the messages of God and fear Him and do not fear anyone except God; and sufficient is God as accountant."* (Qur'an 33:39)

The final phrase of that verse — “sufficient is God as accountant” — beautifully matches the end of Sūrah al-Jinn, where God keeps count of all things.

**God Encompasses What Is With Them**

The verse says God *aḥāṭa bimā ladayhim* — He encompasses whatever is with them. *Iḥāṭah* means surrounding comprehensively. God's knowledge surrounds the angels, the messengers, the revelation, the hearers, the hidden motives, the obstacles, the responses, and the consequences. Nothing in the process of guidance escapes Him.

This is especially fitting after a sūrah about hidden beings and unseen routes. The jinn cannot access what God withholds; devils cannot penetrate what He guards; humans cannot know the timing He conceals; messengers know what He reveals; and God encompasses all. The chapter ends by placing every layer of reality inside divine knowledge.

The Qur'an says:

> *"He knows what is before them and what is behind them, while they encompass nothing of His knowledge except what He wills."* (Qur'an 2:255)

Creation's knowledge is partial and granted; God's knowledge is complete and original.

**A Numbered Count of All Things**

The final phrase is majestic: God “keeps a numbered count of all things.” The Arabic *aḥṣā kulla shay'in ʿadadā* indicates precise enumeration. Nothing is vague before God. Not one deed, word, tear, intention, injustice, repentance, whisper, or atom is lost.

The Qur'an states:

> *"And everything they did is in the records. And every small and great thing is written."* (Qur'an 54:52–53)

And:

> *"On the Day when God will resurrect them all and inform them of what they did. God had enumerated it, while they forgot it; and God is witness over all things."* (Qur'an 58:6)

Human forgetfulness does not erase divine enumeration. This is warning for the oppressor and comfort for the unnoticed righteous.

**The Final Word Answers the First Event**

The sūrah began with a hidden group listening to the Qur'an. It ends with God counting all things. This means the event was never obscure to Him. The jinn's listening, their words, their return to their people, the Prophet's recitation, Quraysh's rejection, the heavenly guards, and every later reader of the sūrah — all are encompassed and counted.

This gives dignity to hidden obedience. A person may pray alone, give charity secretly, resist temptation privately, or endure hardship without being seen. God counts it. A teacher may speak truth to a small audience and never know who was transformed. God counts it. A believer may avoid a sin no one knew he was tempted by. God counts it.

The same truth warns hidden corruption. A secret lie, a concealed betrayal, an anonymous cruelty, a private indulgence, or an unseen oppression is also counted. The Lord of the unseen is not limited by the absence of witnesses.

**Revelation, Accountability, and Mercy Meet at the End**

The ending gathers the sūrah's themes. Revelation is protected; messengers convey; jinn and humans are accountable; the unseen belongs to God; false refuge fails; true guidance is heard and believed; worship belongs to God alone; and everything is counted. This is not a cold ledger, because the One who counts is the Lord who guides, sends messengers, warns before punishing, and opens repentance before the final account.

A believer should leave the sūrah with three states. First, humility: I know little of the unseen, and God knows all. Second, confidence: the message sent to the Prophet ﷺ is protected and sufficient. Third, vigilance: my life is counted, so I must live deliberately.

The final phrase can reshape a day. Before speaking, remember it is counted. Before despairing over unnoticed good, remember it is counted. Before belittling a small sin, remember it is counted. Before underestimating a small act of worship, remember it is counted. The Lord who counted the hidden listening of the jinn counts the hidden turning of every heart.

**Concluding Reflection on Sūrah al-Jinn**

**The Unseen Confirms the Seen Messenger**

Sūrah al-Jinn is not merely a chapter about another species. It is a chapter about revelation's authority. The unseen world that some humans feared, worshipped, consulted, or mythologized becomes a witness that the Qur'an is guidance. The jinn who had known something of hidden realms do not invite people to occult secrets; they invite them to faith, tawḥīd, resurrection, and obedience.

**A Balanced Creed for an Age of Extremes**

The sūrah builds a balanced Muslim imagination. It affirms unseen creation without allowing superstition. It recognizes jinn without fearing them as independent powers. It acknowledges hidden knowledge without permitting fortune-telling. It honors the Prophet ﷺ without deifying him. It warns of Hell without closing the door of repentance. It promises provision without turning religion into worldly entitlement. It teaches that all reality — visible and hidden — is under the Lordship of God.

**The Final Practical Message**

The practical message is clear: listen to the Qur'an as the believing jinn listened; believe when guidance becomes clear; do not speak about God without knowledge; seek refuge only in Him; abandon occult dependence; remain upright upon the path; use blessings as tests to pass, not toys to forget; keep mosques and bodies for God; love the Prophet ﷺ as servant and messenger; do not claim knowledge of the unseen; and live knowing that every hidden thing is counted.

Sūrah al-Jinn begins with “Say” and ends with enumeration. The Prophet ﷺ says what was revealed; God counts what everyone does with it. Between those two truths lies the whole responsibility of mankind and jinn.

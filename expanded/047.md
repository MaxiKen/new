# Sūrah Muḥammad (Chapter 47) — Original Expanded Commentary

## Introduction to the Sūrah

**Expanded Commentary**

Sūrah Muḥammad is a Madinan chapter concerned with the moral reality of allegiance. It contrasts belief with rejection, examines the pressure of conflict, exposes hypocrisy, and ends by calling the community to generosity. Its central question is not merely who claims faith, but whose conduct demonstrates that claim. The following commentary is newly written from the source themes, with the initial file used only for orientation and verse identification.

## Sūrah Muḥammad 47:1

> **1 Those who disbelieve and turn from the way of God—He has made their deeds go astray.**

**Expanded Commentary**

**The moral cost of active rejection**

The opening contrasts two movements: turning away from revelation and obstructing others. Kufr is not treated as a neutral absence but as a chosen covering that redirects effort away from its proper end.

**Arabic vocabulary and structure**

The verse’s key expression should be read in its Qur’anic setting, where words such as faith, guidance, disbelief, patience, covenant, and return describe processes rather than labels. Its verbal movement assigns responsibility to human choice while affirming that God knows, judges, guides, and grants the final outcome. The construction therefore joins theology to ethics: what is believed must shape what is done.

**Connections, implications, and application**

This verse is illuminated by the wider Qur’anic themes of accountability, mercy, justice, and the Hereafter. It asks the reader to distinguish sincere commitment from appearance, and principled action from reaction to pressure. In present life this means examining motives, honoring obligations, refusing injustice, and returning to God through repentance whenever conduct falls short. The verse is not a license to condemn others; it is a discipline for truthful self-assessment.

> **2 And those who believe and perform righteous deeds, and believe in what has been sent down unto Muhammad—and it is the truth from their Lord—He has absolved them of their evil deeds and set their state aright.**

**Faith that reforms the past**

Belief is paired with righteous action and with accepting the revelation given to Muhammad. The verse presents repentance as a transformation of moral history: God does not merely overlook a person; He repairs the direction of that person’s life.

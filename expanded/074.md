# Sūrah al-Muddaththir (Chapter 74) — Expanded Verse-by-Verse Commentary

## Introduction to the Sūrah

**Expanded Commentary**

**A Sūrah Born in Trembling and Blanketing**

Sūrah al-Muddaththir, "The One Enwrapped" or "The Cloaked One," is unanimously classified as Makkan, and by many authorities as among the first three sūrahs revealed. Its title preserves a profoundly human moment: the Prophet Muhammad, peace and blessings be upon him, overwhelmed by seeing Jibril seated upon a chair between heaven and earth, returns to Khadijah trembling, saying "Daththiruni, daththiruni" — "Enwrap me, enwrap me." The Qur'an does not erase this tremor; it canonizes it as a chapter heading. This is divine pedagogy: revelation honors fear before it commands courage. While the majority hold that Iqra' 96:1-5 was the first revelation of nubuwwah (prophethood), al-Muddaththir 1-7 is widely seen as the first revelation of risalah (messengership) — the shift from "Read" to "Arise and warn."

**The Heptad of Preparation and the Rest of Warning**

The opening seven verses are a complete curriculum of prophetic character, arranged with exquisite logic: (1) *qum* — existential standing, (2) *andhir* — public warning, (3) *kabbir* — theological magnification, (4) *tahhir* — personal purification, (5) *uhjur* — social dissociation from defilement, (6) *la tamnun tastakthir* — economic and egoic purification from transactional giving, (7) *isbir* — temporal perseverance. In seven imperatives, God builds a messenger. What follows is the world that messenger must face: the Trumpet (8-10), a case study of elite rejection embodied in al-Walid ibn al-Mughirah (11-26), Saqar and its nineteen guardians (26-31), cosmic oaths that seal the warning (32-37), radical responsibility (38-48), and the psychology of aversion — fleeing like wild asses from a lion, demanding personalized scriptures, lacking fear of Hereafter (49-53), then closing with invitation and divine sovereignty (54-56) ending on the two names: Ahl al-Taqwa wa Ahl al-Maghfirah.

**Chronology and Relationship to Sūrah al-Muzzammil**

Al-Muddaththir is inseparable from Sūrah al-Muzzammil (73). Both begin with enwrapping, both address the Prophet in his private state. Yet al-Muzzammil says: stand at night, recite, be patient with what they say — it builds inner fuel. Al-Muddaththir says: arise in day, warn, magnify, purify — it spends that fuel. Night and day, inward and outward, contemplation and action. The early community learned that daʿwah without night prayer burns out, and night prayer without day daʿwah becomes escapism. The two sūrahs together prevent both errors.

**Why This Early Makkan Sūrah Reads Like a Modern Diagnosis**

Though revealed to a small cluster of believers surrounded by hostile Quraysh, its themes map precisely onto late modernity: the anxiety of moving from private spiritual experience to public responsibility; the measurement of self-worth by wealth and sons (or today, followers and net worth); the endless deliberation that uses intelligence to rationalize status quo; the desensitization to accountability; the demand for a religion customized to individual desire; and the fleeing from reminders that feel like lions. Its remedy remains timeless: magnify God above everything, keep garments — outer and inner — clean, abandon defilement decisively, give without keeping receipts, and be patient for God alone.

## Sūrah al-Muddaththir 74:1

> **O thou who art covered,**

**Expanded Commentary**

**The Root D-Th-R and the Image of Enwrapping**

The form *al-muddaththir* is from *d-th-r*, to cover with a *dithar* — an outer cloak, blanket, or wrapper. The fifth-form reflexive *tadaththara* with assimilation becomes *muddaththir* — one who has wrapped himself intensely, repeatedly, thoroughly. It is not a light throw but a full enfolding. The Prophet ﷺ was not performing; he was trembling after seeing the angel on a chair between sky and earth, as narrated by Jabir ibn ʿAbdillah in Sahih al-Bukhari and Muslim. He fell to the ground, then went to Khadijah saying "Zammiluni, daththiruni." The Qur'an captures that precise moment. Al-Tabari records from Abu Salamah ibn Abd al-Rahman that he asked Jabir about first revelation, and Jabir narrated this hadith, stating that 74:1-5 was revealed at that moment.

The choice of *muddaththir* rather than *muzzammil* is subtle: *muzzammil* (73:1) is one wrapped in a *muzzammil* — often for sleep; *muddaththir* is one wrapped in a *dithar* — an outer cloak for going out. One is indoor wrapping, one outdoor. One sūrah prepares for night vigil, the other for day mission.

**Divine Tenderness in Address**

God does not address him as "O Muhammad" here, nor "O Messenger" yet, but by his present state. Ibn Kathir notes this is among seven honorific addresses of the Prophet in Qur'an: *Ya ayyuhan-nabi, ya ayyuhar-rasul, ya ayyuhal-muzzammil, ya ayyuhal-muddaththir*. Each address acknowledges condition before commanding. This is profound tarbiyyah: God meets the servant where he is. The servant is covered; God calls him covered, then commands uncovering. For the modern believer who feels overwhelmed, anxious, wanting to hide after a spiritual high or low, this verse says: God sees the blanket, names it, and still calls. The blanket is not sin; staying under it permanently would be.

Al-Qurtubi adds that calling by state also softens command. If God had said "O Muhammad, arise," the majesty might overwhelm. Saying "O you covered" is intimate, like a mother gently calling a child under covers.

**The Blanket as Universal Human Experience**

Psychologically, every major transition includes a blanket moment. A graduate who understands a truth wants to stay in library; a new parent overwhelmed wants to hide; an activist who sees injustice wants to retreat. The blanket is permissible for recovery, not residence. The Qur'an's pattern is: allow trembling, then command standing. This prevents two errors: toxic positivity that says "never tremble" and toxic passivity that says "stay trembling." The verse legitimizes vulnerability while refusing to let it become identity. The Prophet's trembling is preserved to teach that courage is not absence of fear but action despite it.

The Qur'an elsewhere preserves similar moments: Musa trembling when his staff became snake (27:10), Maryam's pain in childbirth (19:23). Revelation does not edit out humanity.

**Cross-Reference and Early Daʿwah Pair**

The pairing with 73:1-2 is intentional and must be read together:

> *"O thou enwrapped in thy garment! Keep vigil the night, except a little..."* (Qur'an 73:1-2)

> *"O thou who art covered, arise and warn!"* (Qur'an 74:1-2)

One builds night fuel, one spends day fuel. One says *qum al-layl*, one says *qum fa-andhir*. The early Muslims thus learned rhythm: night standing and day warning. Without night, day warning becomes harsh; without day, night becomes escapism. Together they form complete worship.

**Khadijah's Role and the Blanket of Support**

The blanket was not only cloth but Khadijah's support. She wrapped him, believed him, took him to Waraqah. The first believer was a woman who provided blanket of emotional security. This teaches that prophetic mission needed domestic sanctuary before public confrontation. Qur'an says:

> *"And of His signs is that He created for you from yourselves spouses that you may find tranquility in them..."* (Qur'an 30:21)

Khadijah was tranquility that made *qum* possible. Without her blanket, standing harder. Modern lesson: every *muddaththir* needs Khadijah-like support — spouse, friend, community — that provides blanket for recovery, then encourages arising.

Ibn Hisham reports Khadijah said: "By God, God will never disgrace you..." — blanket of words before blanket of cloth.

**The Fatrah: Pause Between Iqra and Muddaththir**

Between 96:1-5 and 74:1-7 was *fatrat al-wahy* — pause of revelation, said to be weeks or months. During it, Prophet ﷺ anxious, longing. Pause teaches that divine pedagogy includes waiting. After first overwhelming encounter, pause lets experience settle. Like student after first lesson needs time to absorb before second. Then 74 comes to move from reading to warning. The pause also intensified longing, so when call to arise came, he was ready. Qur'an says:

> *"And those who disbelieve say: Why was Qur'an not revealed to him all at once? Thus, that We may strengthen your heart thereby..."* (Qur'an 25:32)

Gradual revelation strengthens heart. Blanket period part of strengthening.

## Sūrah al-Muddaththir 74:2

> **arise and warn!**

**Expanded Commentary**

**Qum: Standing as Existential Act**

The imperative *qum* from *q-w-m* — to stand, to establish, to be upright — is not merely physical rising but assuming a post, a station, a *maqam*. The Qur'an uses same root for *aqama al-salah* (establish prayer) and *qum bil-qist* (stand for justice, 4:135). Ibn Abbas explains: rise from sleep, from comfort, from blanket, and warn Makkah of punishment if they do not believe. Al-Tabari says: arise and warn people. The small verb conceals enormous consequence: this standing would change trade routes, tribal alliances, world maps. The Prophet's standing on Safa was first public *qum*.

The grammatical *fa* in *fa-andhir* indicates immediate sequence: arise, then warn — warning is purpose of arising. You do not arise to be seen, but to warn.

**Nadhir: The Naked Warner of Arabia**

The word *andhir* defines prophetic office. A *nadhir* is scout who sees enemy and runs to town, often stripping clothes and waving them — *al-nadhir al-ʿuryan* — naked warner — known to Arabs as ultimate alarm. The Prophet ﷺ used this image:

> *"I am the naked warner to you before a severe punishment."* (al-Bukhari 4770, Muslim 208, from Ibn Abbas; also Muslim 149 from Abu Hurayrah)

He climbed Safa, called clans, and warned. Warning is thus mercy, not harshness. Mother warns child reaching for fire because she loves. Qur'an frames all prophets as warners:

> *"And We have not sent you but as a bearer of glad tidings and a warner to all mankind."* (Qur'an 34:28)

> *"...so that you may warn a people whose fathers were not warned..."* (Qur'an 36:6)

Modern culture often says "don't judge, don't warn." This verse says silence before cliff is not kindness; warning is kindness, if done with love and without arrogance.

**Why Warning Precedes Glad Tidings Here**

In early Makkah, disease was complacency, pride of lineage, wealth. First medicine must be shock — alarm before breakfast. Al-Razi notes warning mentioned before *basharah* because audience's heart was asleep. Later sūrahs balance with abundant glad tidings, but here first call is *andhir*. The sequence teaches daʿwah wisdom: diagnose before prescribing. A sleeping person needs alarm, not lullaby. The Prophet's early warnings were not to crush but to awaken: "Say La ilaha illa Allah, you will prosper."

The Qur'an also says:

> *"...and warn them of the Day of Regret..."* (Qur'an 19:39)

**Your Own Qum Moment**

Every Muslim inherits share of *qum* — not prophethood, but responsibility. Arising may mean leaving comfortable echo chamber to speak truth in family group, standing to correct misinformation at work, standing for Fajr when blanket warm, standing for justice when silence easier. The verse teaches spiritual experience must translate into movement. Blanket for recovery; standing for purpose. The Prophet ﷺ said:

> *"Whoever among you sees evil, let him change it with his hand; if he cannot, with his tongue; if he cannot, with his heart — and that is weakest faith."* (Muslim 49, from Abu Saʿid al-Khudri)

That is *qum* in levels.

**Patience and Warning Link**

Verse 7 will command patience for Lord, showing warning will bring harm. So *qum fa-andhir* includes cost. Warning truth often brings backlash. The verse prepares: arise knowing you will be opposed, but arise anyway for God. This is why magnification (verse 3) comes next — to anchor warner.

**Safa as First Qum: Historical Moment**

The first public *qum fa-andhir* occurred on Mount Safa. Prophet ﷺ climbed, called: "Ya Sabahah!" — morning alarm — and gathered clans: Ya Bani Fihr, Ya Bani Adi, Ya Bani Hashim. When they gathered, he asked: "If I told you enemy behind valley will attack at morning, would you believe?" They said: "Yes, we never found you lying." He said: "I warn you of severe punishment." Abu Lahab responded: "May you perish!" Then Surah al-Masad revealed. This moment shows *qum fa-andhir* was not abstract; it was standing on rock, calling names, risking kinship. Hadith:

> *"...the Prophet ﷺ went out to Batha, climbed mountain, called..."* (al-Bukhari 4770, from Ibn Abbas)

His standing changed history.



**Nadhir and Bashir: Balance of Warning and Glad Tidings**

Though verse says *andhir*, Prophet ﷺ also *bashir* — bearer of glad tidings. Qur'an balances:

> *"...and We have sent you as bearer of glad tidings and warner..."* (Qur'an 33:45)

Early Makkah needed more warning because complacency; later Madinah needed more glad tidings because hardship. Warner without glad tidings despairs; bearer of glad tidings without warning deludes. Al-Muddaththir begins with warning, but ends with *maghfirah* — forgiveness — balancing. So *qum fa-andhir* includes within it implicit *bashir* — warn of Saqar to give glad tidings of Gardens for those who heed.

## Sūrah al-Muddaththir 74:3

> **Thy Lord magnify!**

**Expanded Commentary**

**Takbir: Grammar of Exclusivity**

The command *wa rabbaka fa-kabbir* — "and thy Lord magnify!" — fronts object *rabbaka* for exclusivity: thy Lord alone magnify, not idols, not wealth, not opinion. *Kabbara* from *k-b-r*, greatness, to declare great, to say Allahu Akbar. Al-Tabari: declare your Lord great above what polytheists associate. Ibn Taymiyyah: takbir is foundation of all worship — prayer begins with it, adhan repeats it, Hajj echoes it, jihad uses it — because heart cannot serve two greats. If God not magnified, something else will be. The Qur'an says:

> *"And say: Praise be to God who has not taken a son... and magnify Him with all magnificence."* (Qur'an 17:111)

The fronting also teaches: magnify Lord *before* warning people; otherwise you magnify their reaction.

**Psychological Resizing**

Anxiety magnifies problems; takbir magnifies God above problems. When person says Allahu Akbar with presence, he resizes reality: debt real but God greater; deadline real but God greater; enemy plot real but God greater. This is why Prophet taught takbir at elevations — climbing hill, seeing wonder, feeling fear. The Qur'an says:

> *"...that you may magnify God for having guided you..."* (Qur'an 2:185)

Modern mind bombarded with micro-stressors that algorithmically inflate themselves needs this resizing daily. Takbir is not escapism; it is accurate scaling. Like zooming out on map: problems remain but context restored. The analogy of taqwa as careful driving applies: driver who magnifies road hazard appropriately drives carefully; driver who magnifies hazard excessively panics; driver who magnifies self excessively crashes. Takbir keeps hazards in true size.

**Takbir as First Theology**

Why magnification as first theology after warning? Because warner who does not magnify God will magnify audience and collapse. If you warn while magnifying people's approval, you soften warning. If you magnify God, you can warn with love without fear. The sequence: stand, warn, but anchor warning in divine greatness, not personal charisma. This protects from arrogance (I am great) and cowardice (they are great). Al-Qurtubi says this verse institutes audible takbir in daʿwah. Early Muslims began public address with takbir to remind listeners of hierarchy.

The Prophet's life shows takbir at every transition: entering prayer, rising from bowing, seeing new moon, returning from travel. It is punctuation of God-conscious life.

**Living Takbir Today**

Teacher beginning class with Bismillah and internal takbir, doctor entering surgery remembering divine greatness above technique, parent disciplining child while remembering God greater than anger — all live this verse. The hadith:

> *"The Prophet ﷺ used to say Allahu Akbar when he climbed a hill and SubhanAllah when he descended."* (al-Bukhari 2993, from Jabir)

Shows takbir as response to elevation — literal and metaphorical. When you feel elevated by success, say Allahu Akbar to stay grounded.

For middle verses about al-Walid, link to Sūrah al-Qalam 68:10-16 which also describes similar elite rejecter — *"...defamer, going about with slander, preventer of good, transgressor, sinful, cruel, moreover an illegitimate pretender..."* (68:11-13) — same type. > *"...and We have made Qur'an easy for remembrance..."* (Qur'an 54:17)

**Practical Tarbiyyah Exercise for Verse 3**

To live this verse today: (1) Read verse aloud in Arabic and translation, reflect 2 minutes on root words. (2) Ask: what blanket, what standing, what magnification, what purification, what shunning, what giving, what patience does this verse call me to today? (3) Write one small action — e.g., for verse about garments, wash one garment and make intention of purity; for verse about *rujz*, delete one app or habit that is *rujz*; for verse about *tamhid*, thank God for one smoothed way and use it for good; for verse about *salakakum fi Saqar*, audit four causes — prayer, feeding, speech, belief — and pick one to improve. (4) Make duʿa: "O Ahl al-Taqwa wa Ahl al-Maghfirah, make me among companions of right." This turns commentary into practice, not just information.

> *"...and those who strive in Us, We will surely guide them to Our paths..."* (Qur'an 29:69)

## Sūrah al-Muddaththir 74:4

> **Thy garments purify!**

**Expanded Commentary**

**Thiyab: Fabric and Character**

The command *wa thiyabaka fa-tahhir* — "and thy garments purify!" — uses *thiyab*, plural of *thawb*, which means outer garment and, by idiom, character and reputation. Arabs say *fulan tahir al-thiyab* — so-and-so pure of garments — meaning free of vice. Al-Tabari records both meanings from Salaf: some said wash clothes for prayer from impurity; others said purify heart and deeds. Ibn Kathir holds both intended: outward cleanliness for worship and inward cleanliness of soul. Root *t-h-r* appears throughout Qur'an for ritual purity (*taharah*), moral purity, and pure ones in Paradise. The Qur'an says:

> *"...and purify thy garments..."* is paired with *"...and purify My House..."* (Qur'an 22:26) — both physical and moral.

The fronting *thiyabaka* emphasizes: thy garments, not others' — start with self.

**Fiqh of Purity as Preparation**

Why garments after magnification? Because one who magnifies God must approach people in state worthy of message. Prophet emphasized cleanliness as half faith:

> *"Purity is half of faith."* (Muslim 223, from Abu Malik al-Ashʿari)

And ablution:

> *"When a Muslim washes his face during ablution, every sin he contemplated with eyes leaves with water... when he washes hands, every sin they committed leaves... until he emerges purified from sins."* (Muslim 244, from Uthman)

Al-Qurtubi notes historical nuance: pre-Islamic Arabs trailed long garments as sign of wealth and arrogance. Verse commands shortening and purifying, opposing ostentation with humility. Thus garment purification is anti-kibr. The Prophet said:

> *"God does not look at one who drags his garment out of arrogance."* (al-Bukhari 5788, Muslim 2087, from Ibn Umar)

So purification includes cutting arrogance.

**Outer Order, Inner Order**

Behavioral research confirms what verse encodes: outer order supports inner clarity. Person whose clothes soiled and neglected often feels inner slump; dressing cleanly for prayer signals psyche that moment matters. Verse does not demand expensive garments — Prophet wore simple patched clothing — but clean, dignified. In daʿwah, this matters: people hear message partly through messenger's self-respect. A daʿi who magnifies God but neglects hygiene contradicts claim that he honors One who loves beauty. The Prophet said:

> *"Indeed God is Beautiful and loves beauty."* (Muslim 91, from Ibn Masʿud)

Purification is both physical and aesthetic: present Islam beautifully.

**Digital Garments Today**

For today's Muslim, this verse extends to digital garments — profile, public persona, language worn online. Purify garments means curate presence that does not stain message. It also means regular tawbah, washing heart's garment from envy, rancor, show. The Qur'an says about people of Paradise:

> *"...and their garments are of silk..."* (Qur'an 76:21)

Purity in this world leads to silk in next. The verse also reminds that prayer requires pure garments; so keep spare clean clothes, keep wudu, keep heart wudu.

## Sūrah al-Muddaththir 74:5

> **And defilement shun!**

**Expanded Commentary**

**Rujz/Rijz: Idols, Filth, Punishment**

The command *wa al-rujza fa-uhjur* — "and defilement shun!" — uses *rujz* (also read *rijz*), from *r-j-z*, to tremble, disorder. Word carries three meanings recorded by Tabari and Ibn Abbas: idols themselves, filth associated with idol worship, and punishment that follows. Some said *rujz* means idols explicitly — *al-awthan*. Others said sin. Command *uhjur* from *h-j-r*, to abandon, emigrate away — same root as *hijrah*. Not "avoid slightly" but "make migration away from." So total, decisive break. Al-Tabari: shun idols and their worship. Ibn Kathir: abandon idols, shun them.

The fronting *al-rujza* emphasizes: defilement, then shun — make it focus. The Qur'an says elsewhere:

> *"So shun the defilement of idols and shun false speech."* (Qur'an 22:30)

**Shunning as Active Separation**

Form *fa-uhjur* indicates continuity: keep shunning, make habit. More than private dislike; public dissociation. Early Muslims in Makkah had to pray toward Kaʿbah while idols surrounded it — profound test. Shunning did not mean physical absence yet, but inner disavowal and refusal to participate. Al-Qurtubi notes *rujz* also includes bad character, harshness, everything incurring divine wrath. Thus verse is comprehensive moral quarantine: leave what pollutes. The Qur'an says:

> *"...and keep away from uncleanliness..."* (Qur'an 74:5, other translation) — same meaning.

Shunning is not hatred of people; it is clarity about systems. Prophet shook hands with polytheists in trade, but never bowed to idols. Distinction matters today: shun defilement, not defiled person who may yet be guided.

**Purification Sequence: Theology, Self, Society**

Sequence — magnify Lord, purify garments, shun defilement — is three-step cleansing: theological (God great), personal (keep self clean), social (leave polluted system). You cannot warn against idolatry while keeping small idol in heart for approval, money, tribe. Shunning is not isolation but integrity. The Prophet's early boycott years show *uhjur* lived: when society demands you participate in defilement, you make hijrah — even if only in heart, even if later physical.

The Qur'an says about Ibrahim:

> *"...I will emigrate to my Lord..."* (Qur'an 29:26) — same root *hajr*.

Hijrah begins with shunning.

**Modern Forms of Rujz**

Classical idols were stone; modern *rujz* can be ideologies demanding worship — consumerism saying worth equals wealth, nationalism saying my tribe can do no wrong, celebrity culture saying fame equals truth, pornography, usury, deception. To *uhjur* them means consciously step out of rituals: not watch what desensitizes heart, not repeat slogans contradicting tawhid, make hijrah in small daily choices. The Prophet said:

> *"The muhajir is one who abandons what God has forbidden."* (al-Bukhari 10, Muslim 40, from Abdullah ibn Amr)

That is living *wa al-rujza fa-uhjur* daily.

## Sūrah al-Muddaththir 74:6

> **Grant not favor, seeking gain!**

**Expanded Commentary**

**Mann and Tastakthir: The Disease of Transactional Goodness**

Verse *wa la tamnun tastakthir* — "Grant not favor, seeking gain!" — addresses subtle disease of giver. *Mann* is to give favor then remind, making recipient feel indebted. *Tastakthir* from *k-th-r*, to seek increase, want more back. Ibn Abbas: do not give gift to receive more in return; do not give small hoping for larger repayment. Tabari: common Arab custom — giving gifts to chiefs to receive double. Qur'an bans this from prophetic giving. Ibn Kathir notes could also mean: do not consider deeds as much and be impressed.

Two parses: *la tamnun* as prohibition of reminding favors, with *tastakthir* as motive (do not give to get more); and *la tamnun* as not considering work great, with *tastakthir* meaning deem it much. Both authentic meanings Salaf held. First protects recipient from manipulation; second protects giver from self-admiration. Together complete ethic of giving.

**Qur'anic Etiquette of Charity**

Qur'an returns to theme in al-Baqarah:

> *"O you who believe, do not nullify your charities with reminders and injury..."* (Qur'an 2:264)

> *"Those who spend their wealth in the way of God and then do not follow what they spent with reminders or injury..."* (Qur'an 2:262)

Prophet warned:

> *"There are three whom God will not speak to on Day of Resurrection: the one who lets garment drag out of arrogance, the one who reminds people of his favors, and the one who sells goods with false oaths."* (Muslim 106, from Abu Dharr)

And:

> *"Beware of reminding of favors, for it nullifies reward."* (Tabarani, hasan)

This verse lays foundation for daʿwah that does not keep receipts. The Prophet gave and forgot, as Aisha said he was not *fāhish* nor *mutafahhish* and did not repay evil with evil.

**Why Warner Must Not Seek Gain**

Warner who gives daʿwah to be praised, followed, paid will soften message for donors. By commanding *do not give to get more*, God frees Messenger from market logic. Daʿi's reward with God alone. Contemporary: do not preach for followers, likes, monetization. Moment daʿwah becomes business model, truth negotiable. The Qur'an says about prophets:

> *"...I ask of you no reward for it; my reward is only with God."* (Qur'an 34:47, 11:29, etc.)

This verse is early statement of that principle. The Prophet ﷺ lived it: when offered wealth, kingship, women to stop preaching, he refused.

The verse also protects from spiritual self-congratulation: do not deem your night prayers much, your warnings much. Always see shortcoming.

**Psychology of Giving**

Modern psychology distinguishes intrinsic vs extrinsic motivation. Giving to get more is extrinsic; it destroys joy and creates resentment when return not equal. Giving for God is intrinsic; it sustains. The verse teaches intrinsic motivation for all good: pray not to be seen as pious, give not to be seen as generous, warn not to be seen as brave. Give because God worthy. This is ikhlas. The Prophet said:

> *"Actions are by intentions..."* (al-Bukhari 1)

Intention of seeking increase corrupts action.

## Sūrah al-Muddaththir 74:7

> **And for the sake of thy Lord be patient.**

**Expanded Commentary**

**Li-Rabbika: For Thy Lord**

Verse *wa li-rabbika fa-isbir* — "And for sake of thy Lord be patient" — closes opening heptad. Word *li-rabbika* fronted: for thy Lord — not for reputation, not to be seen stoic, not because no option, but for Him. *Sabr* from *s-b-r*, to hold, bind, restrain — as one binds wound or holds container from spilling. Includes restraining tongue from complaint, limbs from retaliation, heart from despair. Qurtubi: be patient with harm of disbelievers and difficulty of prophethood for God's sake. The *fa* in *fa-isbir* indicates consequence: because you have been commanded to magnify, purify, shun, give without gain, therefore be patient — these require patience.

**Three Dimensions of Sabr**

Classical scholars divide sabr into three: sabr upon obedience (continuing to pray, warn, teach despite fatigue), sabr against disobedience (holding back from revenge, slander, illicit shortcuts), and sabr upon decrees (illness, loss, persecution). All three required in Makkah. Prophet mocked while circumambulating, had entrails thrown upon him while prostrating (Bukhari from Ibn Masʿud), boycotted three years — told *isbir*. Qur'an links patience to victory:

> *"And be patient, for indeed God does not waste reward of doers of good."* (Qur'an 11:115)

> *"Indeed, patient will be given reward without account."* (Qur'an 39:10)

Hadith:

> *"Amazing is affair of believer: when good befalls him he grateful, and that good for him; when harmful befalls him he patient, and that good for him."* (Muslim 2999, from Suhaib)

> *"No one is given better and more encompassing than patience."* (al-Bukhari 1469, Muslim 1053, from Abu Saʿid)

**Sabr as Active Endurance**

Modern culture confuses patience with passivity or helpless waiting. Qur'anic sabr active: farmer patient not by staring at seeds but weeding, watering, waiting seasons. Messenger's patience active — continuing to warn, teach, forgive. Analogy of taqwa as careful driving applies: patience is keeping hands on wheel through dangerous road, not abandoning car. It is discipline that makes long obedience possible. The Qur'an says:

> *"O you who believe, be patient and outdo in patience and remain stationed..."* (Qur'an 3:200) — *rabitu* — remain stationed, like army holding post.

So *isbir* is holding post for God.

**Link to Trumpet**

Verse 7 ends preparation. Verse 8 pivots to Day of Trumpet. Link subtle: you, O Messenger, be patient for God now, for Day when patience no longer needed by you and desperately needed by those who mocked you. Warner's patience temporary; denier's difficulty eternal. The Qur'an says:

> *"So be patient as those of firm resolve among messengers were patient..."* (Qur'an 46:35)

The early Muslims learned: patience for God transforms suffering into worship. The Prophet's patience in Taif, when offered angel to crush town, he said "Perhaps from their loins will come believers" — patience for Lord, not self.

> **For when the trumpet is blown,**

**Expanded Commentary**

**Naqur: Piercing Blast**

Sūrah shifts abruptly: *fa-idha nuqira fi al-naqur* — "For when the trumpet is blown." *Naqir* from *n-q-r*, to peck, strike, hollow out — sharp, piercing blow. *Naqur* is instrument blown — Trumpet or Horn. Form suggests single, decisive blast that penetrates. Ibn Kathir: Trumpet blown by Israfil, angel entrusted with Horn, whose blast heralds Resurrection. Qur'an mentions trumpet many places:

> *"And Trumpet will be blown, and all who are in heavens and earth will swoon except whom God wills. Then it will be blown again, and behold, they will be standing, looking on."* (Qur'an 39:68)

> *"And Trumpet will be blown, and behold, from graves to their Lord they hasten."* (Qur'an 36:51)

Particle *fa* connects to previous command to be patient: be patient now, for that Day coming. Sudden shift from intimate blanket to cosmic horn is rhetorical power: private call to patience grounded in public eschatology.

**Sound as Inescapable Reminder**

Qur'an often describes Last Day with sounds — blast, shout, deafening cry (*al-sakhkhah* 80:33, *al-qariʿah* 101:1). Sound inescapable; you can close eyes but not ears. Trumpet blast will reach every ear, in every grave, under every sea. No one will need notification. Hadith describes Israfil already poised:

> *"How can I be at ease when bearer of Horn has put it to his mouth, inclined his forehead, and is listening for command to blow?"* (al-Tirmidhi 2431, from Abu Saʿid al-Khudri, hasan)

Prophet said this to show Last Day not distant myth but set trap, trigger-man already in position. The Qur'an says:

> *"...and the Trumpet will be blown, and We will gather them all together."* (Qur'an 18:99)

**Modern Desensitization**

Modern life mutes trumpet with noise — constant notifications, entertainment, argument. Qur'an's method to cut through noise with louder, truer sound. Remembering trumpet not morbid; clarifying. Person who lives aware that final blast will interrupt all meetings finds easier to prioritize, forgive, not be owned by trivialities. The Prophet taught frequent remembrance of death:

> *"Remember often the destroyer of pleasures: death."* (al-Tirmidhi 2307, from Abu Hurayrah, hasan sahih)

Trumpet is ultimate destroyer of pleasures and reminder.

**Why Trumpet After Patience?**

Theology: patience for God now is light compared to difficulty of that Day for those who refused patience. The warner's patience is investment; denier's impatience is debt. The verse also shows God's justice: He does not punish without warning; He warned via Messenger, and He will warn via Trumpet. Two warnings: gentle human voice now, cosmic horn later. Wise heed first.

## Sūrah al-Muddaththir 74:9

> **that Day shall be a difficult day**

**Expanded Commentary**

**ʿAsir: Difficult Day**

Verse *fa-dhalika yawma'idhin yawmun ʿasir* — "that Day shall be a difficult day" — defines Day of Trumpet. *ʿAsir* from *ʿ-s-r*, difficulty, hardness, constriction — opposite of *yusr*, ease. Demonstrative *dhalika* points far — that Day, not this day — emphasizing distance in time but certainty. Tabari: difficult because of terrors, accounting, length (50,000 years per 70:4), outcomes. Qur'an says:

> *"...a Day that will make children grey-haired."* (Qur'an 73:17)

> *"...Day when man flees from brother, and mother and father..."* (Qur'an 80:34-35)

> *"...a Day when no soul will avail another..."* (Qur'an 2:48)

Difficulty here not physical inconvenience but existential exposure — every hidden motive revealed, every unrepented oppression presented.

**Why Difficulty Is Accurate Word**

Qur'an could have said "great Day" or "terrible Day." It chose "difficult" because difficulty implies struggle without escape. Difficult exam cannot be bribed; you must sit it. Difficult labor cannot be delegated; you must go through. So too that Day. Ibn Taymiyyah notes difficulty relative to preparation: for prepared believer, God makes it easy — as next verse implies by contrast. For heedless, even small accounts become mountains because no provisions. Qur'an says:

> *"And it will be a difficult Day for disbelievers."* (Qur'an 25:26, similar)

And for believers, angels say:

> *"This is your Day which you were promised."* (Qur'an 21:103)

**Mercy in Naming Hardship**

Naming future difficulty in advance is mercy. Teacher who says "final will be difficult" not threatening; inviting preparation. Qur'an's warning of difficulty is act of *rahmah* — gives time to study. Student who hears "difficult" and starts studying has turned warning into ease. This is logic of taqwa: seeing dangerous path ahead and walking carefully to avoid harm. Like driver seeing sign "Dangerous curve ahead" slows and avoids crash. Warning of difficulty is sign. The Prophet said:

> *"...if you knew what I know, you would laugh little and weep much."* (al-Bukhari 4621) — to make difficult Day easy, weep now in repentance.

**Psychology of Difficulty**

Modern psychology shows humans discount future difficulty — present bias. Qur'an counters by making future difficulty vivid, present. By calling it *ʿasir* now, it brings future into present decision. The believer who remembers *yawmun ʿasir* finds it easier to say no to small haram that would make Day more difficult.

> **for the disbelievers, not of ease.**

**Expanded Commentary**

**Ghayru Yasir: Not of Ease**

Verse *ʿala al-kafirina ghayru yasir* — "for the disbelievers, not of ease" — qualifies difficulty. Day difficult universally, but for *al-kafirun*, those who covered truth, specifically *not easy*. Double negative — *ghayru yasir*, not easy — is litotes, understatement for emphasis: extremely hard. Qurtubi notes this implies for believers it will be made easy. Qur'an says:

> *"...and that Day will be difficult for disbelievers."* (Qur'an 25:26)

> *"...and believers at that time will be in pleasant abode."* (Qur'an 25:24, context)

> *"...God will make easy for him..."* (Qur'an 65:4, principle)

The particle *ʿala* — upon — indicates burden upon them.

**Kāfir as Coverer**

Root *k-f-r* means to cover — farmer covers seed, night covers world. *Kafir* in Qur'anic usage not simply one who never heard; one who covers over known truth out of arrogance or love of world. This is why Day not easy for him: he spent life covering; on that Day, coverings removed. Qur'an states:

> *"And We have removed from you your covering, so your sight today is sharp."* (Qur'an 50:22)

Difficulty is sudden clarity after lifetime of self-chosen blur. Like person who wore sunglasses indoors for years suddenly forced into bright sun — painful.

Ibn Abbas says *kafir* here means one who denies resurrection, like al-Walid.

**Justice and Mercy in One Phrase**

Phrase preserves both divine justice and mercy: justice in that rejection has consequence; mercy in that consequence not arbitrary but fitting — not easy for those who made truth not easy for themselves. Believer who struggled to make truth easy for others — by teaching, forgiving, giving — finds Day made easy for him. Principle reciprocity of ease. Prophet said:

> *"Whoever makes easy for one in difficulty, God makes easy for him in this world and Hereafter."* (Muslim 2699, from Abu Hurayrah)

So *ghayru yasir* for disbelievers implies *yasir* for those who made easy for others.

**The Two Easinesses**

Qur'an says:

Difficulty of Day for disbelievers contrasts with ease for believers who endured hardship of obedience. Their hardship in this world (patience, purification) becomes ease in next; disbelievers' ease in this world (wealth, sons, smoothed way) becomes difficulty in next. The verse thus inverts worldly accounting.

## Sūrah al-Muddaththir 74:11

> **Leave Me alone with the one whom I created,**

**Expanded Commentary**

**Dharni: Divine Transfer of Case**

Verse *dharni wa man khalaqtu wahidan* — "Leave Me alone with the one whom I created alone" — marks dramatic shift to first-person divine speech. *Dharni* — leave Me — from *w-dh-r*, to leave, abandon. God says to Messenger: leave this opponent to Me. Not call to inaction but transfer of case from human court to divine tribunal. *Wahidan* — alone, solitary — can refer to God (One who created) or human (created alone, without wealth or sons, then given everything). Ibn Abbas says it refers to al-Walid ibn al-Mughirah, who was called *al-wahid* among Quraysh as epithet of eminence — unique one — and also because created alone, without help. Irony sharp: one called unique by people told he alone before truly Unique. Al-Tabari says *wahidan* means alone, without wealth or child, then given.

The Qur'an uses similar *dharni* elsewhere:

> *"So leave Me with those who deny this discourse..."* (Qur'an 68:44)

> *"Leave Me and those who deny, possessors of ease..."* (Qur'an 73:11)

**Asbab al-Nuzul: Al-Walid ibn al-Mughirah**

Majority of mufassirun — Tabari, Ibn Kathir, Qurtubi, Wahidi in Asbab al-Nuzul — narrate verses 11-26 revealed about al-Walid ibn al-Mughirah al-Makhzumi, father of Khalid ibn al-Walid. He among richest and most eloquent of Quraysh, father of many sons, owner of vast gardens and trade. When Prophet recited Qur'an to him, his heart softened. Abu Jahl warned him that his people would collect money for him if he appeared to incline to Muhammad, implying he after money. Al-Walid, to preserve status, gathered Quraysh leaders and deliberated what to call Qur'an, rejecting poet, soothsayer, madman, until settled on "sorcery handed down." Verses chronicle deliberation. Ibn Abbas narration detailed in Tafsir al-Qurtubi and Asbab al-Nuzul al-Wahidi.

This occasion shows peer pressure and status anxiety as drivers of disbelief.

**Universal Type Beyond One Man**

While occasion specific, lesson universal. Qur'an often names no name but describes type: self-made man who forgets he was made. Pattern repeats: wealth, sons, smoothed way, desire for more, opposition to signs when they threaten status. Every generation has its al-Walids — billionaires, influencers, intellectuals who cannot afford to admit truth because social capital invested in denial. God says *dharni* — leave him to Me — because human argument alone cannot pierce status armor; only divine reckoning can. The verse also consoles Messenger: you do not need to defeat him in debate; leave him to Me.

Qur'an says about similar type:

> *"...he was arrogant and was among disbelievers."* (Qur'an 2:34, about Iblis)

**Tawhid in Creation**

Phrase *man khalaqtu* — whom I created — reasserts tawhid al-rububiyyah. Whatever wealth he has, I created him and it. Creator retains right to judge creature. This is Qur'an's consistent move against elite arrogance:

> *"Does man not remember that We created him before, when he was nothing?"* (Qur'an 19:67)

> *"...We created man from drop..."* (Qur'an 76:2)

Reminding of creation alone dismantles arrogance: you were nothing, then you were given everything, yet you oppose Giver. The logic is simple and devastating.

> **and to whom I gave vast wealth,**

**Expanded Commentary**

**Malan Mamdudan: Extended Wealth**

Verse *wa jaʿaltu lahu malan mamdudan* — "and to whom I gave vast wealth" — describes first blessing. *Mamdud* from *m-d-d*, to extend, stretch long — wealth extended in time, amount, reach: continuous income, not one-off. Al-Walid owned large estates in Makkah and Taif, caravans, wealth said to be such that he financed trade caravans alone. Qurtubi says *mamdudan* means abundant and uninterrupted, like مدد — extended supply. Point not condemnation of wealth per se — wealth provision from God — but reminder that extended wealth creates extended accountability. Qur'an says:

> *"...and We have provided them from good things..."* (Qur'an 17:70) — provision itself blessing.

**Wealth as Test, Not Proof**

Qur'an repeatedly corrects fallacy that wealth equals divine approval:

> *"And they say: We are more in wealth and children, and we shall not be punished."* (Qur'an 34:35)

> *"Do they think that We extend to them wealth and sons, We hasten to them with good things? Nay, they perceive not."* (Qur'an 23:55-56)

Wealth extended is test of gratitude and distribution. Prophet said:

> *"If son of Adam had valley of gold, he would wish for second valley, and his mouth will not be filled except with dust."* (al-Bukhari 6439, Muslim 1048)

This hadith directly explains verse 15 coming next.

**Psychology of Abundance and Self-Sufficiency**

Abundance creates illusion of self-sufficiency (*istighna*). Qur'an says:

> *"Nay, indeed man transgresses because he sees himself self-sufficient."* (Qur'an 96:6-7)

When income *mamdud*, person begins to feel tomorrow guaranteed, judgment sound because bank sound. Cure is remembrance of origin — *khalaqtu* — and end — Saqar. The Qur'an says:

> *"...and whatever you have of favor, it is from God."* (Qur'an 16:53)

Gratitude is recognizing extension comes from Extender, not from self. The Prophet said:

> *"Richness is not abundance of goods, but richness of soul."* (al-Bukhari 6446, Muslim 1051, from Abu Hurayrah)

**Modern Mamdud**

Today *malan mamdudan* may be diversified portfolio, passive income, royalties, crypto. The test same: does extended wealth extend gratitude and generosity, or extend arrogance and opposition? The verse does not say wealth evil; says it given — *jaʿaltu* — I gave. Acknowledging Giver is first step to passing test.

Trial remains: does presence of supporters make truth clearer or harder to admit error because many watching? The Qur'an says about those who rely on numbers:

Numbers not proof.

Gratitude for children means raising them to know Giver, not using them as props for ego. ## Sūrah al-Muddaththir 74:14

> **and made smooth the way for him.**

**Expanded Commentary**

**Mahhadtu Tamhidan: Smoothing**

Verse *wa mahhadtu lahu tamhidan* — "and made smooth the way for him" — uses *m-h-d*, to spread, make bed, prepare smoothly — from which *mahd*, cradle, and *tamhid*, paving. Intensive form *mahhadtu tamhidan* indicates thorough, repeated smoothing. Ibn Abbas: made his life easy, his wealth, leadership, affairs without hardship. Qurtubi: expanded for him in provision, leadership, means. It is as if life's road paved for him, no potholes. Qur'an uses similar phrasing for Hell's bed:

> *"...Hell, they will roast therein; and what an evil cradle (mihad)."* (Qur'an 38:56)

But here cradle is worldly ease. The verb *mahhadtu* suggests God Himself smoothed — not luck.

The emphatic cognate *tamhidan* after *mahhadtu* is for emphasis — I smoothed thoroughly.

**Ease That Should Lead to Gratitude**

Logically, smoothed path should lead to gratitude. If someone paves road for you, you thank paver. Yet human psychology often inverts: smoother road, more we think we paved it ourselves. This illusion of self-made success. Qur'an counters:

> *"And whatever you have of favor, it is from God."* (Qur'an 16:53)

> *"...and if you count favors of God, you will not enumerate them..."* (Qur'an 14:34)

The verse lists three blessings — wealth, sons, smoothed way — forming complete picture of worldly success: money, manpower, opportunity. All three given, not earned in ultimate sense. The Qur'an says:

> *"...and He has made subservient to you whatever is in heavens and earth..."* (Qur'an 45:13) — smoothing is subservience.

**Test of Tamhid and Istidraj**

When God smooths path, not always approval; sometimes *istidraj* — gradual luring. Qur'an warns:

> *"So leave Me with those who deny this discourse. We shall lead them on gradually from where they know not."* (Qur'an 68:44)

> *"And We will lead them on from where they know not, and I will give them respite..."* (Qur'an 7:182-183)

Smooth road can lead to cliff if direction wrong. Blessing is in direction, not smoothness. The Prophet said:

> *"When you see God giving a servant what he loves of worldly things while he persists in disobedience, it is only istidraj."* Then he recited 6:44. (Ahmad 17311, from Uqbah ibn Amir, hasan)

Thus *mahhadtu* is test: will he use smoothed way to go forth (37) or stay behind?

**Modern Smoothed Ways**

Today smoothed ways: good education, connections, health, passport, internet. All *tamhid*. Question: does smoothing lead to *shukr* or *ʿunad* (stubborn opposition)? The verse invites audit: list three smoothed ways in your life — wealth, family, opportunity — and ask: am I using them to go forth to God or to stay behind?

## Sūrah al-Muddaththir 74:15

> **Yet he desires that I give more.**

**Expanded Commentary**

**Thumma Yatmaʿ: Insatiable Yet**

Verse *thumma yatmaʿu an azid* — "Yet he desires that I give more" — captures restless *yet*. *Thumma* indicates sequence and distance: after all that — wealth, sons, smoothed way — he still desires more. *Yatmaʿ* from *t-m-ʿ*, greedy hope, covetous desire. Not just wants, but greedily expects. Ibn Kathir links to hadith valley of gold. Tabari: he desired that I increase him in wealth and sons despite disbelief. Verse exposes bottomless appetite.

The *an azid* — that I increase — shows he expects God to increase despite opposition. It is audacity: give me more while I oppose Your signs.

**Why More Never Satisfies: Hedonic Adaptation**

Psychology well-documented: hedonic adaptation. Each increase becomes new baseline, so satisfaction requires next increase. Qur'an diagnoses in Surah al-Takathur:

> *"Rivalry in worldly increase distracts you until you visit graves."* (Qur'an 102:1-2)

Logically, if wealth source of happiness, more wealth proportionally increase happiness. Empirically, plateaus. Spiritually, reveals misdirected worship: heart worshiping increase will never have enough, because increase has no ceiling. Only worship of Infinite satisfies, because Infinite has no ceiling and yet already present. Prophet said:

> *"If son of Adam had valley of gold, he would wish for second valley, and his mouth will not be filled except with dust, and God forgives who repents."* (al-Bukhari 6439, Muslim 1048)

This is direct tafsir of verse.

**Spiritual Danger of Yatmaʿ**

Desire for more from God while opposing God's signs is audacity. Shows he sees God as vending machine: give more regardless of relationship. Qur'an elsewhere:

> *"When We bestow favor upon man, he turns away... but when evil touches him, he is in despair."* (Qur'an 17:83)

> *"...man is ever niggardly."* (Qur'an 17:100)

Modern consumer culture trains *yatmaʿ* as virtue — always want upgrade, next model. Verse calls it disease that blinds to existing blessings. The Qur'an says:

> *"...and you love wealth with immense love."* (Qur'an 89:20)

The cure is gratitude and contentment (*qanaʿah*). Prophet said:

> *"Be content, you will be richest people."* (al-Tirmidhi 2305, from Abu Hurayrah, hasan)

**The More That Matters**

There is praiseworthy *yatmaʿ* — desire for more forgiveness, knowledge, Paradise. Qur'an says:

> *"...and say: My Lord, increase me in knowledge."* (Qur'an 20:114)

So desire for more not evil per se; object matters. Al-Walid desired more wealth while opposing signs; believer desires more signs, more closeness. The verse asks: what do you desire more of? More wealth, or more guidance? The answer reveals heart's direction.

The verse also shows that *ʿanid* is not passive disbelief but active setting self against — *kana li-ayatina ʿanidan* — he positioned himself as opponent, as adversary. This is choice.

## Sūrah al-Muddaththir 74:17

> **I shall constrain him to endure a steep ascent.**

**Expanded Commentary**

**Saʿud: Steep Mountain of Fire**

Verse *sa-urhiquhu saʿudan* — "I shall constrain him to endure a steep ascent" — announces consequence. *Saʿud* from *s-ʿ-d*, to ascend, be high, difficult. Means steep, hard mountain to climb, and by extension, severe, escalating punishment. Ibn Abbas: mountain of fire in Hell that disbeliever made to climb, and when reaches top, thrown down, then made to climb again forever. Tabari reports similar from Mujahid and Qatadah. Qurtubi: burden him with punishment he cannot bear, that keeps increasing. Verb *urhiquhu* from *r-h-q*, to burden, overwhelm, impose with difficulty — as in 18:73, "do not burden me."

The *sa-* prefix indicates future certainty — I shall, emphatically. The pronoun *hu* — him — personal, direct.

**Fitting Punishment: Mirror Logic**

Why steep ascent? Because crime was steep ascent in arrogance — he climbed high in status and looked down. Punishment mirrors crime: he sought high status through opposition; he will be made to climb real high that burns. Qur'an's punishments often fit crimes:

> *"The recompense of evil is evil like it."* (Qur'an 42:40)

> *"...as they forgot meeting of this Day, so We have forgotten them..."* (Qur'an 32:14)

Endless climb also reflects endless greed (*yatmaʿ an azid*) — desire for endless increase meets endless ascent yielding no increase, only exhaustion. Divine irony: you wanted more height; here is height that destroys. Also *saʿud* contrasts with *mahhadtu* — I smoothed way for him in world; now I will make way rugged in Hereafter. Smoothing rejected leads to ruggedness.

**Psychology of Arrogance as Saʿud**

Even in this world, obstinate elite lives *saʿud*: maintaining image, defending past statements, climbing social ladders never ending, anxiety increasing. Verse externalizes inner reality: arrogance exhausting. Humility, by contrast, restful. Prophet said:

> *"Whoever humbles himself for God, God raises him."* (Muslim 2588, from Abu Hurayrah — part of hadith: no one humbles himself for God but God raises him)

> *"...and no one is arrogant except God debases him."* (Ibn Majah 4176, from Abu Hurayrah, hasan)

The *saʿud* in this world is burnout, anxiety, need to keep climbing. In next, literal mountain.

Al-Razi notes *saʿud* also means severe, as in "I will burden him with severe punishment" — severe because his crime severe: calling Qur'an sorcery.

**Warning as Mercy**

Announcing *sa-urhiquhu* before death is mercy: chance to avoid. God does not punish without warning. The verse says I shall — future — giving time to repent. Al-Walid did not. But reader can. The verse thus functions as deterrent: imagine endless climb, then choose humility now. The Prophet described Hell's mountains:

> *"...a rock thrown into Hell falls for seventy years..."* (Muslim 2844, from Abu Hurayrah) — depth and height beyond comprehension.

## Sūrah al-Muddaththir 74:18

> **Truly he reflected and determined.**

**Expanded Commentary**

**Fakkara wa Qaddara: Calculated Deliberation**

Verse *innahu fakkara wa qaddara* — "Truly he reflected and determined" — describes al-Walid's deliberation. *Fakkara* from *f-k-r*, to think, reflect, turn over in mind. *Qaddara* from *q-d-r*, to measure, determine, estimate. Together mean he thought deeply and measured words, crafting calculated response. Tabari: he reflected what to say about Muhammad and Qur'an, and determined his lie. Ibn Kathir: he thought and considered what to invent. Qur'an acknowledges intelligence — he did reflect — but condemns misuse. The *innahu* — indeed he — emphasizes certainty of process.

The two verbs show two-stage process: reflection (internal) then determination (decision). This is not impulsive sin but premeditated.

**Intelligence Without Sincerity**

This verse profound warning about intellect divorced from humility. Al-Walid arguably most literary critic in Makkah; knew poetry, soothsaying, rhetoric. Used expertise not to recognize truth but to label it to preserve status. Modern parallels: experts who use sophisticated reasoning to deny obvious moral truths because truth inconveniences funding, tribe, ego. Qur'an praises reflection (*tafakkur*) when sincere:

> *"Indeed in that are signs for people who reflect."* (Qur'an 45:13)

> *"...and reflect on creation..."* (Qur'an 3:191)

But here reflection for fabrication. Same faculty can be worship or weapon. The Prophet said:

> *"The most intelligent is he who takes account of himself and works for what after death."* (al-Tirmidhi 2459, from Shaddad ibn Aws, hasan)

Al-Walid used intelligence to avoid accounting.

**Slow-Motion Sin: Frame by Frame**

This verse begins slow-motion sequence (18-25) detailing internal process: reflection, determination, looking around, frowning, turning away, arrogance, speech. Qur'an dissects sin frame by frame, showing not sudden but staged. This mercy to reader: you can interrupt sequence at any stage before speech. Al-Walid did not. Each verb is checkpoint: you reflected — did you reflect sincerely? You determined — did you determine truth or lie? The sequence teaches that sin is process, not event. The Prophet said:

> *"...when a servant commits sin, a black dot placed on heart; if he repents, it polished away..."* (al-Tirmidhi 3334, from Abu Hurayrah, hasan)

Each stage adds dot; al-Walid let dots accumulate until speech.

**Modern Fakkara wa Qaddara**

Today *fakkara wa qaddara* happens in PR meetings: how to label truth to discredit it. "Call it extremism," "Call it conspiracy," "Call it outdated." Same process: reflect not on truth of message but on how to neutralize it to preserve status. Verse warns that God sees reflection and determination, not just final statement. Intention matters.

> **May he perish for how he determined;**

**Expanded Commentary**

**Fa-Qutila: Divine Imprecation**

Verse *fa-qutila kayfa qaddara* — "May he perish for how he determined" — is curse or condemnation. *Qutila* literally "he was killed/slain," but Arabs use as "may he be cursed, perish." Ibn Abbas: cursed. Qurtubi: punished. Phrase *kayfa qaddara* — how he determined — expresses amazement at evil nature of determination. How could he determine such lie after knowing truth? Qur'an uses similar imprecation elsewhere:

> *"May hands of Abu Lahab perish..."* (Qur'an 111:1)

> *"May liars perish."* (Qur'an 51:10)

> *"May man perish, how ungrateful he is!"* (Qur'an 80:17)

This not human anger; divine judgment on process that seemed clever but spiritually suicidal. The *fa* indicates consequence: because he reflected and determined evilly, therefore may he perish.

**Cursing Process, Not Just Person**

Note says *kayfa qaddara* — how he determined — cursing method, not just man. Universalizes: any similar determination deserves similar condemnation. Verse teaches intellectual dishonesty — determining to call truth sorcery while knowing not — worthy of divine wrath. Not mistake; moral crime. Prophet said:

> *"Most hated speech to God is when man says: Fear God, and other says: Mind your own business."* (al-Silsilah al-Sahihah, from Ibn Masʿud)

Al-Walid's determination precisely to reject fear of God when truth came. The Qur'an says:

> *"...and woe to those who disbelieve from Day severe..."* (Qur'an 19:37) — woe for determination against truth.

**Rhetorical Kayfa**

The *kayfa* (how) invites listener to imagine: how could man so intelligent, so blessed, so close to softening, decide to call Qur'an sorcery? Answer: arrogance and peer pressure. Question lingers for every reader: how do I determine when truth conflicts with crowd? The *kayfa* also expresses disgust: what an evil determination! Al-Razi says *kayfa* here is for wonder at ugliness of determination. Like saying "How could you?"

The verse also shows God judges intentions: determination itself, before speech, already deserves curse because intention root of action:

> *"Actions are by intentions..."* (al-Bukhari 1, Muslim 1907, from Umar)

**First Curse: Worldly Perishing**

First *qutila* may refer to worldly perishing — spiritual death, loss of reputation, failure of plot. Al-Walid's plot to call Qur'an sorcery failed: Qur'an spread more, his sons eventually became Muslims, his wealth did not save him. His determination perished. The Qur'an says:

His clever determination was out-plotted by divine plan.

## Sūrah al-Muddaththir 74:20

> **then may he perish for how he determined!**

**Expanded Commentary**

**Repetition for Emphasis and Finality**

Verse *thumma qutila kayfa qaddara* — "then may he perish for how he determined!" — repeats verse 19 with *thumma*, then. Repetition in Qur'an never filler; signals confirmation, intensification, finality. Tabari: first curse for determination in this world, second for punishment in Hereafter; or first for thought, second for speech. Ibn Kathir: repetition for emphasis and to show determination utterly evil from start to finish. Qur'an often repeats curses to show layered punishment. The *thumma* indicates time gap: he determined, then persisted, then determined again — second curse after second thought.

The structure *fa-qutila... thumma qutila* mirrors *fakkara wa qaddara* — reflection then determination, then curse then curse — two-stage sin meets two-stage condemnation.

**Two Deaths: Spiritual and Physical**

Classical scholars like Razi note *qutila* twice hints at two perishing: spiritual death in this life — heart dies, reputation as wise man perishes when utters nonsense — and physical punishment in next. He alive physically when verses came, but spiritually slain. Qur'an says about such people:

> *"...they are dead, not alive, and they perceive not when they will be resurrected."* (Qur'an 16:21, about idols, but principle applies)

> *"...is one who was dead and We gave him life... like one in darkness...?"* (Qur'an 6:122)

Spiritual death is worse than physical because it leads to physical eternal death in Saqar.

**Pause for Reflection: Tawqif**

Repetition creates suspense. Between determination and utterance, God inserts double curse, forcing listener to pause and feel gravity before hearing what he actually said. Literary *tawqif* — pause for reflection. Like judge saying "Guilty" twice before reading sentence, letting weight settle. Sequence teaches evil determination, even before speech, already deserves condemnation because intention root of action. The Prophet said:

> *"When servant commits sin, black dot placed on heart; if repents, polished away; if increases, dot increases until heart sealed."* (al-Tirmidhi 3334)

Each stage adds dot; al-Walid let dots accumulate until speech. Double curse corresponds to double dot.

**Theological Balance**

Double curse also shows divine justice not hasty: God gives chance between first and second determination — *thumma* indicates respite. Al-Walid had time to reconsider between first reflection and second; he did not. So second curse confirms persistence. This shows God does not punish first slip but persistent determination. The Qur'an says:

Ibn Kathir: frowned and scowled as if thinking hard to invent lie. The *thumma* shows after beholding faces, he frowned — performing disgust to satisfy audience.

The two verbs together: *ʿabasa* is frowning of forehead, *basar* is scowling of whole face, eyes, mouth — intensification.

**Physiology of Rejection and Faces on Judgment Day**

Qur'an records faces because faces reveal hearts. When truth enters resistant heart, face reacts before tongue. Frown attempt to create physical distance from truth morally close. Qur'an describes disbelievers' faces on Day:

> *"Faces on that Day radiant, looking toward Lord."* (Qur'an 75:22-23)

> *"...and disdain for people is arrogance."* (Muslim 91, part of definition of kibr)

Cheerful face is charity; frowning at truth is sin. The Qur'an says about hypocrites:

> *"...and when they see them, they wink at one another..."* (Qur'an 83:30) — mocking gestures recorded.

So *ʿabasa wa basar* is not trivial; it is part of crime.

**Frown as Performance**

The frown was performance for audience he beheld in previous verse. He beheld faces, then frowned to show he, the expert, found Qur'an distasteful. It is like food critic frowning to show sophistication. The performance reveals insincerity: true intellectual critique does not need frown; it needs argument. Frown substitutes for argument when argument absent. Al-Walid had no argument, so frowned.

**Adbara wa Istakbar: Body and Soul**

Verse *thumma adbara wa istakbar* — "Then he turned his back, and waxed arrogant" — completes physical sequence. *Adbara* from *d-b-r*, to turn back, show back — opposite of *aqbala*, to come forward. *Istakbar* from *k-b-r*, to deem oneself great, act arrogantly — tenth form, seeking greatness. Tabari: turned away from faith and Qur'an, arrogant to follow it. Qurtubi: turned back on truth and became too proud to submit. Two verbs together show body and soul: back turned outwardly, arrogance inwardly. The *thumma* shows after frowning, he turned — frown not enough, need to physically leave.

The Qur'an uses same pair for Iblis:

> *"...he refused and was arrogant and was among disbelievers."* (Qur'an 2:34) — *aba wa istakbar*

**Idbar and Istikbar: Twin Diseases**

Qur'an pairs these often. Iblis's crime was *aba wa istakbar* — refused and arrogant (2:34). Pharaoh's similar. Turning back is abandonment of search; arrogance claim of superiority justifying abandonment. Sequence logical: first frowned, then turned, then deemed self too big to need truth. Ibn Taymiyyah defines *kibr* as rejecting truth and despising people — exactly what al-Walid does next by calling Qur'an sorcery and human speech. Prophet defined:

> *"Kibr is rejecting truth and despising people."* (Muslim 91)

*Adbara* is rejecting truth (turning back), *istakbar* includes despising people (deeming self greater than Muhammad ﷺ and believers). So verse contains full definition of kibr before definition hadith even stated.

**Modern Adbara and Istakbar**

Turning back today may look like unfollowing, blocking, leaving room when reminder comes, switching off lecture that convicts, saying "I don't want to hear." Arrogance today says: I too educated, too modern, too rational to accept ancient book. Verse says posture not new; ancient, has name and consequence. The Qur'an says about those who turn back:

> *"...whoever turns away from My Reminder, indeed he will bear heavy burden..."* (Qur'an 20:100)

> *"...and who is more unjust than one who reminded of verses of his Lord then turns away..."* (Qur'an 32:22)

Turning away after reminder is injustice to self.

**Free Will Affirmed**

Detailed verbs — reflected, determined, beheld, frowned, turned, waxed arrogant, said — all attribute active agency to al-Walid. God did not force him; he chose each step. This affirms human responsibility. Divine decree does not negate human choice; God knows what he will choose, but he chooses. Qur'an says:

> *"Indeed, We guided him to way, be he grateful or ungrateful."* (Qur'an 76:3)

> *"...and We have shown him two ways."* (Qur'an 90:10)

Al-Walid shown way, chose to turn back and be arrogant. Responsibility his.

**Sihrun Yu'thar: Sorcery Handed Down**

Verse *fa-qala in hadha illa sihrun yu'thar* — "and said, 'This is naught but sorcery handed down'" — gives final verdict. *Sihr* from *s-h-r*, to bewitch, delude, make something appear other than it is. *Yu'thar* from *a-th-r*, to transmit, hand down — sorcery inherited from ancients, learned from others, not original. Al-Walid, unable to call Qur'an poetry (he knew poetry), nor soothsaying, nor madness, chose sorcery because sorcery explains influence without admitting truth: effect due to trick, not reality. Accusation collapses on linguistics alone. The word *yu'thar* also suggests old, outdated — not new revelation but recycled old magic. This is attempt to make Qur'an seem unoriginal.

**Ma Adraka: Rhetorical Amplification**

Verse *wa ma adraka ma saqar* — "And what will apprise thee of Saqar?" — uses *ma adraka* formula, which Qur'an uses for things beyond human imagination: *"And what will apprise thee of Night of Decree?"* (97:2), *"What will apprise thee of Striking Calamity?"* (101:2), *"What will apprise thee of steep path?"* (90:12). > *"Fire kindled for thousand years until turned red, then thousand years until turned white, then thousand years until turned black. So it is black and dark."* (al-Tirmidhi 2591, from Abu Hurayrah, hasan)

The *ma* repeated twice — *ma adraka ma saqar* — what will make you know what Saqar is — double *ma* emphasizes double unknowing: you don't know, and you don't know that you don't know.

## Sūrah al-Muddaththir 74:28

> **It spares not, nor leaves behind.**

**Expanded Commentary**

**La Tubqi wa la Tadhar: No Sparing, No Leaving**

Verse *la tubqi wa la tadhar* — "It spares not, nor leaves behind" — terrifying in brevity. *Tubqi* from *b-q-y*, to remain, spare, leave alive. *Tadhar* from *w-dh-r*, to leave, abandon. Tabari offers two interpretations: does not spare anyone — burns all; does not leave anyone — does not leave them dead, but restores them to be burned again. Ibn Abbas: does not leave flesh nor bone nor blood except burns. Qurtubi: does not leave anything of disbeliever unburned, nor spare him death — continuously burned and renewed. Qur'an elsewhere:

> *"Whenever their skins roasted through, We will replace them with other skins that they may taste punishment."* (Qur'an 4:56)

This explains *la tubqi wa la tadhar*: no part left unburned, no relief of annihilation. The double negative covers all possibilities: no sparing, no leaving — total consumption.

**Unending Consumption vs Worldly Fire**

Worldly fire consumes fuel then dies when fuel ends. Saqar consumes but does not end consumption — fuel renewed. Breaks worldly logic, showing Hereafter physics differ. Verse thus refutes idea Hell temporary purification for disbelievers who rejected persistently; for them, abiding. Logic: if sin persistent and arrogant, punishment mirrors persistence. Qur'an says:

> *"...they will not die therein nor live..."* (Qur'an 87:13, 20:74) — neither dead nor alive, but tortured existence.

> *"...every time they wish to exit from it, they returned..."* (Qur'an 32:20)

So *la tubqi wa la tadhar* means no exit, no end.

**Psychological Impact and Irony**

Phrase creates claustrophobia: no sparing, no leaving. No exit, no leftover. For man like al-Walid who wanted more (*azid*), here is more that horrific: more burning, more renewal. Verse teaches actions have momentum beyond death. The *la tubqi* also means it does not spare wealth, sons, status — all things he relied on. His *mal mamdud* will not spare him, his *banun shuhud* will not spare him, his *tamhid* will not spare him. The verse strips all props.

The brevity — four words — after long verses creates punch, like hammer. It is meant to be memorized and shuddered at.

**Mercy in Clarity**

Clarity about Hell's totality is mercy: it prevents minimizing. Some say "Hell not so bad, maybe like hot day." Verse says no: it spares nothing, leaves nothing. So avoid. Prophet said:

> *"...Fear Fire even with half a date..."* (al-Bukhari 1417, Muslim 1016, from Adi ibn Hatim)

Even small charity can protect from Fire that spares nothing, showing God's mercy makes protection easy.

## Sūrah al-Muddaththir 74:29

> **It scorches the human being!**

**Expanded Commentary**

**Lawwahatun lil-Bashar: Scorching**

Verse *lawwahatun lil-bashar* — "It scorches the human being!" — defines Saqar's effect. *Lawwahah* from *l-w-h*, to scorch, blacken, make apparent. Can mean scorches skin until black, or makes clear, makes visible — i.e., Hell becomes apparent. Ibn Abbas: burns skin. Mujahid: blackens skin. Tabari: burns skin and changes color. *Bashar* — human being — from *basharah*, skin, outer appearance — because human's skin apparent. So Saqar scorches very thing humans pride: appearance, skin, face. Al-Walid prided handsome appearance and eloquence; Saqar will scorch that. The form *lawwahah* is intensive, active participle — constantly scorching.

The *li* in *lil-bashar* can mean for, to — it scorches for humans (visible to them) or scorches humans.

**Why Skin?**

Skin largest organ, seat of sensitivity. Qur'an emphasizes skin in punishment because skin where pain felt. Renewal of skins (4:56) shows divine justice: organ of sensation preserved to feel. Not gratuitous cruelty but consequence: skin used to frown and scowl at truth will taste consequence. Qur'an says:

> *"...their faces will be covered with Fire."* (Qur'an 14:50)

> *"...Fire will burn their faces..."* (Qur'an 23:104)

Face honored in this world — prostration on face — if used to scowl, punished on face. The Qur'an says about those who turn away:

> *"...We will drag them on their faces..."* (Qur'an 17:97)

**Apparition Meaning**

Other interpretation: *lawwahah* means making apparent, visible — Saqar made apparent to humans. Supports by:

> *"And Hellfire will be made apparent to errant."* (Qur'an 26:91)

> *"...and Hell brought into view..."* (Qur'an 79:36)

Both meanings true: Saqar scorches humans and is made apparent to humans. It is visible warning now through description, and visible reality later through sight. The verse thus says: this Fire you cannot see now will scorch you and will be made visible to you when too late — so see it now through description.

**Modern Obsession with Skin**

Modern culture obsessed with skin — cosmetics, tanning, surgery, filters. Verse reminds skin's ultimate fate depends on what used for. Skin that prostrated, blushed from modesty, felt tears of repentance — honored. Skin that scowled at truth warned. Verse not shaming body but honoring responsibility of body. Prophet said:

> *"...God does not look at your bodies nor forms, but looks at hearts and deeds."* (Muslim 2564, from Abu Hurayrah) — but bodies used for deeds, so responsible.

The verse also reminds that *bashar* — human — includes all humans; Saqar scorches human being as such, not just al-Walid. Universal warning.

**Expanded Commentary**

**Tisʿata ʿAshar: Number That Tested**

Verse *ʿalayha tisʿata ʿashar* — "Over it are nineteen" — famously concise and famously tested. *ʿAlayha* — over it (Saqar) — nineteen (angels as guardians). Ibn Abbas, Mujahid, Qatadah: nineteen angels as keepers of Hell. Qur'an elsewhere mentions *zabaniyah* (96:18) — stern angels of punishment — and Malik as leader (43:77). Brevity deliberate: just number, no explanation yet, to provoke question. Number 19 fascinated commentators: some note basmalah 19 letters, first revelation 96:1-5 19 words, etc. But majority warn against numerological obsession; point not magic but test, as next verse explains.

The *ʿalayha* indicates over it — authority, supervision — nineteen have authority over Saqar, not merely present.

**Why Nineteen? Precision and Trial**

Why not twenty? Why not thousand? Razi: specifying number shows God's precise ordering; nothing random. Tabari: shows Hell has limited, known guardians, but they are angels, not men, so cannot be overcome. Number being small (19) led Abu Jahl to mock: "Are nineteen enough? I and sons can handle them." Next verse answers: they are angels, not men. So small number tests arrogance: disbeliever thinks quantity equals power, but one angel can destroy nations. One angel destroyed Lut's people, one angel's shout destroyed Thamud. The Qur'an says:

> *"And We have not made keepers of Fire except angels..."* (Qur'an 74:31)

> *"...and none knows hosts of thy Lord but He..."* (same verse) — nineteen leaders, but hosts beyond count.

The number also tests believers: will they accept what God says even if wisdom not immediately apparent?

**Trial of Literalism and Numerology Cults**

This verse became trial because some focused on number, not meaning. Modern groups built entire numerology cults around 19, claiming code, removing verses. Classical scholars like Ibn Kathir warn: verse's own explanation says number is trial for disbelievers. Obsession with number without heeding warning itself falling into trial. Message: don't count guardians; fear Fire they guard. The Qur'an says:

> *"...and those in whose hearts is disease and disbelievers will say: What does God desire by this as parable?"* (Qur'an 74:31)

Asking "what does God desire by this?" mockingly is sign of diseased heart. Proper response: we hear and obey, even if wisdom not fully grasped.

The Prophet said:

> *"...what is lawful clear, what unlawful clear, but between them doubtful..."* (al-Bukhari 52, Muslim 1599) — focus on clear, not doubtful.

**Theological Lesson: Quantity vs Quality**

Nineteen teaches quantity vs quality: nineteen angels stronger than millions of humans. Power not in numbers but in permission from God. This counters modern obsession with numbers — followers, likes, GDP — as measure of power. One angel with God's permission outweighs all. The verse also shows Hell is not chaotic but ordered, guarded, managed — not random torture but precise justice system.

> **And We have appointed none but angels as wardens of the Fire; and We have not appointed their number save as a trial for those who disbelieve, to grant certainty to those who have been given the Book and increase in faith those who believe; and that those who were given the Book and the believers will not doubt; and that those in whose hearts is a disease and the disbelievers will say, "What does God desire by this as a parable?" Thus does God lead astray whomsoever He will and guide whomsoever He will. And none knows the hosts of thy Lord but He. It is but a reminder for the human being.**

**Expanded Commentary**

**Angels, Not Men: Answer to Mockery**

The longest verse in sūrah explains nineteen. First clause: We appointed none but angels as wardens of Fire. Abu Jahl boasted: Ten of you handle one angel, I handle two. Qur'an responds: they are angels — beings of immense power, not humans. One angel destroyed Lut's people (11:82), one angel's shout destroyed Thamud (11:67), one angel rolled back stone of Lut. Hadith: > *"Hell will be brought on that Day with seventy thousand bridles, each bridle held by seventy thousand angels."* (Muslim 2842, from Abdullah ibn Masʿud) If one Hell needs 4.9 billion angels to drag, nineteen leaders command legions beyond count. None knows hosts of thy Lord but He. This shows Hell's management beyond human overcoming.

Al-Tabari: angels are keepers, stern, severe, as described: > *"...over it are angels stern, severe, who do not disobey God..."* (Qur'an 66:6)

**Four Audiences, One Number: Trial, Certainty, Increase, Doubt**

Verse lists four reactions to nineteen: (1) trial for disbelievers — they mock and go further astray; (2) certainty for People of Book — Jews and Christians knew from scriptures Hell's guardians nineteen, so Qur'an's mention confirms prophethood; (3) increase in faith for believers — they see confirmation and trust more; (4) doubt for diseased hearts and disbelievers — they say "What does God desire by this parable?" This divine pedagogy: same information becomes guidance or misguidance depending on heart. As Qur'an says: > *"...and We send down of Qur'an that which is healing and mercy for believers, but it increases not wrongdoers except in loss."* (Qur'an 17:82)

Ibn Kathir: People of Book had in their books number of guardians nineteen, so when they heard Qur'an, they recognized. This is like earlier: > *"...those who were given Book know it is truth from Lord..."* (Qur'an 2:144)

**Divine Will and Human Choice**

Clause *Thus does God lead astray whomsoever He will and guide whomsoever He will* often misunderstood as arbitrary. Ibn Taymiyyah explains: God's will to lead astray follows servant's choice to turn away, as per 6:110: > *"And We turn away their hearts and eyes just as they did not believe first time."* Guidance offered; rejection hardens. The Qur'an says: > *"...God does not change condition of people until they change what in themselves..."* (Qur'an 13:11)

So *yudillu* — leads astray — is judicial consequence of persistent *ʿanad* and *istikbar*. The verse also shows guidance and misguidance not equal: God wills to guide all, but some refuse. The Prophet said: > *"All my ummah will enter Paradise except who refuses..."* (al-Bukhari 7280, from Abu Hurayrah)

**It Is But Reminder**

Verse ends: *It is but a reminder for human being* — not puzzle to obsess over but reminder to act. Number not secret code; alarm. The word *dhikra* — reminder — appears again, linking to 49,54. Purpose of mentioning hosts, numbers, Saqar, is reminder, not trivia. The Qur'an says: > *"...and none knows hosts of thy Lord but He..."* — even angels number unknown; focus on reminder, not counting.

The final phrase *lil-bashar* — for human being — universalizes: reminder for all humans, not just Quraysh. The nineteen is reminder that Hell ordered, guarded, inescapable, so remember and act.

## Sūrah al-Muddaththir 74:34

> **and by the morning as it brightens,**

**Expanded Commentary**

**Subh Idha Asfar: Morning Brightening**

Verse *wa al-subhi idha asfar* — "and by the morning as it brightens" — completes triad. *Subh* — morning, dawn — from *s-b-h*, to be clear, bright. *Asfar* from *s-f-r*, to shine, unveil — also root of *safar*, journey, and *safir*, ambassador — because morning unveils world for journey. Tabari: *asfar* means shines, becomes bright. Morning brightening after night's withdrawal is daily resurrection: darkness dies, light returns. Qur'an swears by this daily miracle to prove greater resurrection possible. The Qur'an says: > *"And by the dawn as it breathes..."* (Qur'an 81:18)

> *"...by the morning brightness..."* (Qur'an 93:1)

The *idha* — when — emphasizes moment of brightening, like sunrise.

**Triad as Witness**

Moon, night, morning — three time markers all humans experience regardless of tribe. They are universal oaths, not local idols. By swearing by them, God calls all humanity as witness: you see moon, you see night withdraw, you see morning brighten — you cannot deny these; so do not deny what they sworn to — that Saqar is one of greatest warnings. Structure: observable cosmic order testifies to unobservable moral order. The triad also covers full cycle: moon (night light), night (darkness), morning (day light) — complete 24-hour witness. The Qur'an says: > *"...We have made night and day as two signs..."* (Qur'an 17:12)

**Spiritual Practice: Fajr as Witness**

Prophet emphasized Fajr prayer as witness: > *"Two rakʿahs of Fajr better than world and what in it."* (Muslim 725, from Aisha)

**Expanded Commentary**

**Ihda al-Kubar: One of Greatest**

Verse *innaha la-ihda al-kubar* — "truly it is one of the greatest" — answer to oaths: Saqar is one of greatest calamities. *Kubar* plural of *kubra*, greatest, from *k-b-r*. Tabari: Saqar is one of greatest afflictions, punishments. Ibn Kathir: one of greatest warnings. Some say pronoun refers to Saqar, some to oaths, some to Qur'an itself — all true: Saqar greatest, its warning greatest, Qur'an that warns greatest. Indefinite "one of" implies there are other great calamities, but this among greatest — leaving room for even greater, like loss of God's mercy. The *inna* and *la* double emphasis: truly, indeed — certainly one of greatest.

The Qur'an says: > *"...and they were warned of greatest punishment..."* (Qur'an 32:21, implied)

**Why Greatest Needs Oaths**

Human mind downplays distant threats. By swearing by moon, night, morning — things we see daily — God says: this distant threat as real as daily sunrise. Greatness not in size but consequence: fire that spares not, scorches, guarded by angels, is greatest because eternal. Worldly greatest — Everest, ocean, empire — all end. Saqar does not. Thus it is *kubra*. The Qur'an says: > *"...and punishment of Hereafter greater if they knew."* (Qur'an 68:33)

> *"...and Hereafter greater in degrees..."* (Qur'an 17:21)

So *kubar* indicates Hereafter's matters greater than worldly.

**Mercy in Calling It Great**

Calling Saqar greatest is mercy, not scare tactic. If doctor says "this disease one of greatest killers," he wants you to screen. Qur'an's hyperbole therapeutic: exaggeration to wake, not depress. Prophet said: > *"If you knew what I know, you would laugh little and weep much."* (al-Bukhari 4621, Muslim 2359)

Knowing greatness of Hereafter reduces greatness of worldly anxieties. The Qur'an says: > *"...so remind by Qur'an whoever fears My threat."* (Qur'an 50:45)

Calling it greatest makes fear appropriate — fear of Saqar greater than fear of poverty, job loss, etc. — so reorders fears correctly. Like driver who fears cliff more than bump, drives safely.

**What Is Greatest? Three Possibilities**

Mufassirun differ what *innaha* refers to: Saqar, or Hell, or Day of Judgment, or Qur'an, or oaths. All valid and all greatest: Saqar greatest punishment, Day greatest day, Qur'an greatest warning. This polysemy is Qur'anic richness: one pronoun carries multiple greatest meanings, all true. Al-Razi says it refers to Saqar, but also to all warnings of Qur'an — all are among greatest. The lesson: take it as greatest, regardless of specific referent, and act.

**Expanded Commentary**

**Yataqaddama aw Yata'akhkhara: Choice Architecture**

Verse *li-man sha'a minkum an yataqaddama aw yata'akhkhara* — "for those of you who wish to go forth or stay behind" — presents choice after warning. *Yataqaddama* from *q-d-m*, to go forward, advance; *yata'akhkhara* from *a-kh-r*, to stay behind, lag. Ibn Abbas: go forth to obedience and Paradise, or stay behind in disobedience and Hell. Tabari: advance to good or remain in evil. Verse says warning for those who have will to choose — respects free will. Does not coerce; presents two directions. The *li-man sha'a* — for whoever among you wishes — attributes will to human.

The *minkum* — among you — addresses audience directly, making choice personal, not abstract.

**Free Will and Divine Will**

This verse key proof for human free will in classical theology. God says *li-man sha'a minkum* — for whoever among you wishes — attributing wish to human. Yet earlier verse 31 said God guides whom He wills. Reconciliation: human will exists within divine will; God wills to give humans will. Ibn Taymiyyah explains: divine will two types — universal will (everything happens by it) and legislative will (what God loves). Human advancing is by both: God allows it and loves it. Staying behind is by universal will (allowed) but not legislative will (not loved). Qur'an says: > *"And say: Truth is from Lord, so whoever wills, let him believe, and whoever wills, let him disbelieve."* (Qur'an 18:29)

> *"...and We have shown him two ways."* (Qur'an 90:10)

**No Neutral: Either Forward or Behind**

Verse says no standing still: you either go forth or stay behind. Inaction is staying behind. In spiritual life, not advancing is regressing, because time moves. Like escalator going down: standing still means going down. You must walk up to stay, run to advance. Powerful for complacent believer who thinks "I'm okay, not sinning big." Okay is staying behind when going forth required. The Prophet said: > *"...take advantage of five before five: youth before old age, health before sickness..."* (al-Hakim, from Ibn Abbas, sahih)

Going forth means using time to advance; staying behind means wasting.

Al-Qurtubi: *yataqaddama* means to Paradise, *yata'akhkhara* to Hell — two destinations, no third.

**Practical Application**

For modern Muslim, going forth may mean advancing to Fajr on time, advancing to charity, advancing to forgiveness, advancing to learning. Staying behind may mean delaying repentance, staying in bad company, staying in heedlessness. Verse says choice yours — *man sha'a*. God wills you have choice. So choose going forth. The Qur'an says: > *"...and hasten to forgiveness from Lord and Garden..."* (Qur'an 3:133) — *sariʿu* — hasten — is going forth.

## Sūrah al-Muddaththir 74:38

> **Every soul is held in pledge for what it has earned,**

**Expanded Commentary**

**Rahinah: Soul as Pledge**

Verse *kullu nafsin bi-ma kasabat rahinah* — "Every soul is held in pledge for what it has earned" — introduces profound legal metaphor. *Rahinah* from *r-h-n*, to pawn, hold as collateral. *Rahn* pledge held until debt paid. Here soul itself pledged for deeds. Tabari: every soul held accountable for deeds, mortgaged. Ibn Kathir: held in pledge, responsible. Qur'an uses similar in 52:21 and 6:70. Idea: you not free until pay debt of deeds; soul collateral. The *bi-ma kasabat* — for what it earned — shows pledge is for its own earning, not other's — individual responsibility.

The *kullu nafsin* — every soul — universal, no exception in rule, though next verse gives exception in mercy.

**Pledge Logic and Redemption**

In Arab custom, if you borrow, you leave pledge — sword, camel — until repayment. If don't pay, pledge lost. Soul pledged for deeds; good deeds redeem it, evil deeds forfeit it. Prophet said: > *"Every person goes out in morning and sells his soul: either he frees it or destroys it."* (Muslim 223, from Abu Malik al-Ashʿari)

This hadith directly explains verse: daily, you sell soul — either free it by obedience or destroy by sin. Pledge metaphor makes abstract accountability tangible: imagine soul in pawnshop, you must buy it back. The Qur'an says: > *"...and every soul will be paid in full what it did..."* (Qur'an 39:70)

Redemption via good deeds, tawbah, God's mercy.

**Psychology of Pledge**

Modern self-help says "you are free." Qur'an says you are pledged — not to society but to own earnings. Empowering: you hold key to own release. No one else can redeem pledge; you must earn. Yet mercy exists: God can forgive and release pledge out of grace, as next verses show companions of right are exception — freed by mercy. The verse also counters idea of intercession without effort: you pledged, you must redeem; intercession only helps those who tried to redeem.

The *kasabat* — earned — from *k-s-b*, to earn, acquire — shows deeds are earning, not random. Soul earns its own pledge status.

**Connection to Previous Choice**

Verse 37 gave choice: go forth or stay behind. Verse 38 gives consequence: every soul pledged for what it earned by that choice. Choice leads to earning, earning leads to pledge status. So freedom of choice does not mean freedom from consequence. Like choosing to borrow — free to borrow, but then pledged. The verse thus links free will and accountability — you choose, you earn, you are pledged for earning.

**Ashal al-Yamin: Companions of Right**

Verse *illa ashab al-yamin* — "save the companions of the right" — provides exception to pledge. *Ashab al-yamin* — companions of right hand — those given book in right hand on Judgment Day, opposite of left. > *"...and companions of right..."* (Qur'an 90:18)

Right in Arab and universal culture symbolizes strength, oath, blessing. Right hand used for giving, eating, shaking. Right side of mosque honored. Giving book in right indicates honor. Being companions of right means being in group honored.

**Why Exception?**

Exception means they not held in pledge same way — good deeds outweigh, or God forgives and releases pledge. Qurtubi: not held because God forgives them, or because their pledge redeemed by faith. Some say they are children of Muslims who died young — not held accountable. Others say angels — not accountable. But majority: believers who believed and did good, so pledge redeemed. The Qur'an says: > *"...every person is pledged for what he earned, except companions of right..."* — implying their earning was faith and good deeds that freed them.

**Identity of Ashab al-Yamin**

Some scholars say *ashab al-yamin* are those who are given book in right hand, as opposed to *ashab al-shimal* (left) and *al-muqarrabun* (nearest). Others say they are less than *muqarrabun* but still saved. In 56, three groups: *muqarrabun*, *ashab al-yamin*, *ashab al-shimal*. The early simplification helps early audience grasp binary: be among right, not guilty.

**Expanded Commentary**

**Fi Jannatin Yatasa'alun: Gardens and Questioning**

Verse *fi jannatin yatasa'alun* — "in Gardens, questioning one another" — places companions of right in Gardens, engaged in questioning. *Jannat* plural of *jannah*, garden, from *j-n-n*, to cover, hide — garden covers ground with trees. *Yatasa'alun* — questioning one another, from *s-a-l*. Scene after judgment, in Paradise, believers conversing, perhaps seeing Hell from distance, inquiring. Qur'an says elsewhere: > *"And they will approach one another, questioning..."* (Qur'an 37:27, 50, 52:25)

**Ma Salakakum fi Saqar: What Inserted You?**

Verse *ma salakakum fi saqar* — "What led you into Saqar?" — records actual question. *Salaka* from *s-l-k*, to insert, make enter, cause to travel along path — as thread through needle, or water through channel. What inserted you into Saqar, what made you travel its path? Tabari: question from believers to disbelievers: what caused you to enter Saqar? Ibn Kathir: companions of right ask guilty. Question implies Saqar not arbitrary; there is cause, path, insertion — actions that led there. Invites guilty to self-diagnose. The *ma* — what — asks for cause, not just what, but what cause.

Confession in Hell too late but useful for us now. The four causes are comprehensive: first two are sins of omission (not praying, not feeding), next two sins of commission (vain talk, denial).

**Diagnostic Question for Modern Soul**

In addiction recovery, first step asking what led to ruin. This verse models that: identify path. Four answers timeless audit. The question also shows that people in Saqar know why they there — no injustice, they confess. The Qur'an says: > *"...and they will confess their sins..."* (Qur'an 67:11)

Confession shows justice.

**Saqar as Destination of Path**

The phrase *fi saqar* — into Saqar — shows Saqar is destination of path *salaka* — to travel. Path to Saqar is not teleportation but travel — gradual insertion. *Khawd* is play, amusement that distracts from purpose.

**Expanded Commentary**

**Nukadhdhibu bi-Yawmi al-Din: Denial of Day**

Fourth confession: *wa kunna nukadhdhibu bi-yawmi al-din* — "and we denied the Day of Judgment." *Nukadhdhibu* from *k-dh-b*, to lie, deny, call lie — intensive form, persistent denial. *Yawm al-din* — Day of Recompense, same phrase as in al-Fatihah 1:4. Tabari: denied Resurrection and Recompense. This root cause of previous three: why pray if no Day? Why feed poor if no account? Why guard tongue if no reckoning? Denial of Day removes motivation for all good. Qur'an says: > *"...and they were deluded by worldly life, and they denied that they would meet God."* (Qur'an 6:130)

> *"...those who deny Day of Judgment..."* (Qur'an 82:9, 83:11)

> *"...Nay, they deny Hour..."* (Qur'an 25:11)

**Logical Consequence of Denial**

If no Day, then hedonism rational: eat, drink, mock, because no consequence. Guilty's logic internally consistent: deny Day, then live accordingly. Qur'an exposes logic leads to Saqar. Believer's logic opposite: affirm Day, live accordingly, find Paradise. Both live by faith — one in no Day, one in Day. Both will see which faith true. The Qur'an says: > *"...and they say: There is nothing but worldly life, we die and live and nothing destroys us except time..."* (Qur'an 45:24) — denial of Hereafter leads to materialism.

The *kunna* — we used to — indicates persistent, habitual denial, not one doubt. They *used to* deny, continuously.

**Modern Denial Forms**

Modern denial of Day takes forms: "You only live once," "No one has come back to tell us," "Afterlife myth," "Science has disproven." Qur'an answers with cosmic signs, moral argument, prophetic testimony. The Prophet said: > *"...if you knew what I know of Hereafter, you would..."* (already cited). Guilty's confession warns denial not intellectual position without consequences; moral choice with destination. The Qur'an says: > *"...and whoever disbelieves in God, angels, Books, messengers, and Last Day has gone far astray."* (Qur'an 4:136) — denial of Last Day is going far astray.

The *bi-yawmi al-din* — denial *with* Day — indicates they denied Day itself, not just details.

**Connection to Earlier Verses**

Denial of Day connects to verse 10 — Day not easy for disbelievers — and verse 8-9 — Trumpet and difficult Day — and verse 37 — choice to go forth or stay behind. Denial of Day is staying behind. The guilty's fourth confession is root of first three: they did not pray because they denied Day when prayer will be asked; they did not feed because they denied Day when feeding will be rewarded; they engaged in vain talk because they denied Day when tongue will be accounted. So *nukadhdhibu bi-yawmi al-din* is foundation of all.

> **until certainty came upon us."**

**Expanded Commentary**

**Hatta Atana al-Yaqin: Until Certainty Came**

Final phrase of confession: *hatta atana al-yaqin* — "until certainty came upon us." *Yaqin* from *y-q-n*, certainty, from *yaqana* — to be certain. Here *yaqin* means death, because death certain, undeniable. Tabari, Ibn Kathir, Qurtubi all say *yaqin* here means death, because death certain and brings certainty of Hereafter. Some say means Resurrection itself. Qur'an uses *yaqin* for death elsewhere: > *"And worship thy Lord until certainty (death) comes to you."* (Qur'an 15:99)

Guilty persisted in four crimes until death interrupted them. No repentance at death accepted. The *hatta* — until — indicates continuity: they continued until death, no break, no repentance.

**Tragedy of Delayed Realization**

Phrase captures tragedy: lived in doubt, then certainty came — but too late. Death moment when doubt ends and certainty begins for everyone. Prophet said: > *"People are asleep; when they die, they awaken."* (attributed to Ali, but meaning authentic)

Qur'an says about Pharaoh's belief at drowning: > *"...until when drowning overtook him, he said: I believe... Now? You had disobeyed before..."* (Qur'an 10:90-91)

> *"...and they will say: Our Lord, we have seen and heard, so return us..."* (Qur'an 32:12) — certainty after deadline not saving; certainty before deadline is.

The *atana* — came upon us — shows certainty came suddenly, not sought, overtook them. Death comes suddenly.

**Lesson: Use Certainty Now**

Verse urges to let certainty come before death — to have *yaqin* in Day of Judgment now, while you can act. Companions of right have *yaqin* now; guilty had it only when no longer helped. Difference timing. Qur'an praises: > *"And who are certain of Hereafter."* (Qur'an 2:4)

> *"...and in Hereafter they have certainty."* (Qur'an 27:3)

Yaqin before death saves; yaqin at death condemns by lateness. The Prophet said: > *"...be frequent in remembering death..."* (al-Tirmidhi 2307)

Remembering death brings yaqin early, which leads to prayer, feeding, guarding tongue, affirming Day — opposite of four crimes.

**Yaqin as Death and Resurrection**

Some mufassirun say *yaqin* here means not just death but Resurrection and seeing Hell — ultimate certainty. Both true: death is first certainty, Resurrection second. The guilty persisted until both came. The verse thus shows that persistence in sin until death leads to persistence in punishment after death. The *hatta* also shows that they had time — from birth to death — to change, but used until for sin, not repentance. The Qur'an says: > *"...and return to your Lord... before punishment comes upon you..."* (Qur'an 39:54) — return before yaqin comes.

> **Thus the intercession of the intercessors will avail them not.**

**Expanded Commentary**

**Shafaʿah: Intercession That Fails**

Verse *fa-ma tanfaʿuhum shafaʿatu al-shafiʿin* — "Thus the intercession of the intercessors will avail them not" — closes guilty's story with finality. *Shafaʿah* from *sh-f-ʿ*, to make even, pair — to add one's influence to another's need, intercede. *Shafiʿin* — intercessors — angels, prophets, righteous, martyrs. Tabari: intercession will not benefit these disbelievers because they died in disbelief. Ibn Kathir: intercession only for believers by God's permission; disbelievers who denied Day have no share. Qur'an says: > *"...who is there that can intercede with Him save by His leave?"* (Qur'an 2:255, Ayat al-Kursi)

> *"...and they cannot intercede except for one whom He approves..."* (Qur'an 21:28)

> *"...and fear Day when no soul will avail another... and no intercession accepted..."* (Qur'an 2:48)

**Why Intercession Fails Here**

Intercession in Islamic theology real but conditional: God must permit intercessor and approve interceded for. For those who denied Day, mocked prayer, neglected poor, indulged vain talk until death, approval withheld. Their own confession shows they never sought intercession through righteous deeds. Prophet said: > *"My intercession will be for those of my ummah who committed major sins."* (al-Tirmidhi 2435, Abu Dawud 4739, from Anas, sahih) — but this for believers who sinned, not for those who denied and mocked until death.

> *"...intercession of angels, prophets, believers... but it will not benefit disbelievers who died in disbelief."* (Ibn Taymiyyah explanation)

Verse thus warns against presumption: "Someone will intercede for me so I can live heedlessly." Intercession not license; mercy for those who tried. The Qur'an says: > *"...and they will not be able to intercede..."* for those who denied.

**Psychological Closure**

Verse ends dialogue with stark closure: no intercession will help them. Prevents listener from thinking "maybe they will be saved after all." No. Their own confession and persistence until certainty closed door. Lesson urgency: seek intercession now through righteous companions, not later through wishful thinking. The *fa* — thus — indicates consequence: because they were as described, thus no intercession benefits.

The plural *al-shafiʿin* — intercessors — indicates many would be willing to intercede, but none can benefit them because condition not met. Even if all angels interceded, not benefit because God does not approve.

**Connection to Verse 42 Question**

Question was *ma salakakum fi saqar* — what led you into Saqar? They answered four causes and certainty came. Now Qur'an adds editorial: thus intercession will not avail them. This editorial from God, not from companions of right, shows finality. Even after confession, no escape. Confession in Hell does not lead to forgiveness; confession in this world does. The Prophet said: > *"...God accepts repentance of servant until throat rattles..."* (al-Tirmidhi 3537, from Ibn Umar, hasan)

After throat rattle — yaqin — no repentance.

**Humurun Mustanfirah: Frightened Asses**

Verse *ka-annahum humurun mustanfirah* — "as if they were frightened asses" — gives vivid simile. *Humur* plural of *himar*, donkey, ass — animal known for stubbornness but also skittishness when frightened. *Mustanfirah* from *n-f-r*, to flee, bolt, be frightened — intensive form, extremely frightened, bolting. Tabari: like wild donkeys frightened and fleeing. Ibn Abbas: wild asses fleeing. Image wild donkeys grazing, then hearing lion and bolting in all directions, panicked, irrational. Qur'an uses animal similes sparingly and powerfully. The *ka-annahum* — as if they — indicates simile, not literal, but accurate.

The word *mustanfirah* also read as *mustanfarah* — frightened by something — both mean fleeing.

**Why Donkeys?**

Donkey in Arab culture symbolizes stubbornness, but wild donkey (*humar wahshiyyah*) symbolizes untamable, skittish, easily panicked. Simile captures both stubbornness against truth and panic away from it — contradictory but true: stubborn in staying in falsehood, panicked when truth approaches. Donkeys fleeing reminder shows irrational fear: they fear Qur'an like donkey fears lion, though Qur'an mercy. Their flight not thoughtful retreat but blind bolt. The Qur'an says about disbelievers: > *"...they are like cattle, nay more astray..."* (Qur'an 7:179)

> *"...like donkey carrying books..."* (Qur'an 62:5) — donkey carrying knowledge but not benefiting.

**Psychology of Aversion**

Verse diagnoses aversion as animal panic, not intellectual critique. When reminder comes, they do not argue calmly; they bolt, mock, change subject, get angry — flight response. This is *fight-or-flight* triggered by truth that threatens ego. Qur'an says elsewhere: > *"...when God alone mentioned, hearts of those who disbelieve in Hereafter shrink..."* (Qur'an 39:45)

> *"...and when Our verses recited to them as clear proofs, you recognize disapproval in faces of disbelievers..."* (Qur'an 22:72)

## Sūrah al-Muddaththir 74:51

> **fleeing from a lion?**

**Expanded Commentary**

**Farrat min Qaswarah: Fleeing from Lion**

Verse *farrat min qaswarah* — "fleeing from a lion" — completes simile. *Farrat* from *f-r-r*, to flee. *Qaswarah* — lion, or hunter, or powerful one — from *q-s-r*, to overpower. Most mufassirun say lion; some say hunters with bows, or strong men. Tabari records both: lion, or archers. Ibn Kathir: lion strongest view. Image: wild asses fleeing from lion that will tear them — yet they flee from Reminder that would save them, as if Reminder were lion. Irony: lion kills, Reminder gives life; they treat life-giver as killer. The *min* — from — indicates fleeing from lion, source of fear.

The word *qaswarah* also means powerful, crushing — from *qasr*, to break, overpower. Lion breaks prey.

**Lion as Truth**

Why lion? Lion apex predator, king of jungle, fearsome. Truth fearsome to false self. Ego experiences truth as lion that will kill constructed identity. So flees. But fleeing from truth does not save ego; starves it. Prophet said: > *"Truth is bitter."* (Ahmad, from Abu Dharr, hasan)

Bitter but healing. Fleeing from bitter medicine leaves disease. The Qur'an says: > *"...and say: Truth has come and falsehood vanished..."* (Qur'an 17:81) — truth like lion that vanquishes falsehood.

The *qaswarah* as hunter also fits: hunters hunt wild asses. Reminder hunts heedless to guide them, but they flee hunter who wants to save, like patient fleeing doctor.

**Literary Power and Warning**

Two-verse simile (50-51) among Qur'an's most vivid. Almost comic — panicked donkeys — but tragic, because donkeys fleeing lion often run toward cliff and die. Those fleeing Reminder flee toward Saqar they fear. Simile thus contains warning: flight path worse than what flee. The question mark in translation — "fleeing from a lion?" — indicates astonishment: are they fleeing from lion? As if to say, what lion? Reminder is not lion; why flee as if it were? Their fleeing is irrational.

The verse also shows that those who flee Reminder think Reminder is predator, but actually predator is Saqar they fleeing toward.

**Connection to Previous Demand**

After simile, verse 52 says they demand pages unfolded — another form of fleeing: instead of heeding Reminder, they demand different Reminder. Fleeing takes two forms: bolting away (donkeys) and demanding alternative (pages). Both avoidance. **Fear as Motivator: Khawf**

Fear of Hereafter in Qur'an not paranoia but healthy caution — *khawf* that leads to *taqwa*. Like fear of failing exam motivates study, fear of Hereafter motivates righteousness. Lack of fear leads to entitlement and mockery. Qur'an says believers are those who fear: > *"...those who fear their Lord in unseen..."* (Qur'an 67:12)

> **So let whosoever will, remember it.**

**Expanded Commentary**

**Fa-man Sha'a Dhakarah: Will to Remember**

Verse *fa-man sha'a dhakarah* — "So let whosoever will, remember it" — grants choice after affirming reminder. *Sha'a* from *sh-y-ʾ*, to will, wish. *Dhakarah* — remember it, from *dh-k-r*. Tabari: whoever wills, let him remember Qur'an and take heed. Ibn Kathir: whoever wills, let him take heed and be guided. Verse respects free will: reminder offered, not forced. Qur'an says similarly: > *"...so whoever wills, let him take path to his Lord."* (Qur'an 73:19, 76:29)

> *"...so whoever wills, let him believe, and whoever wills, let him disbelieve."* (Qur'an 18:29)

> *"...and whoever wills, let him remember..."* (Qur'an 80:12, about Qur'an)

The *fa* — so — indicates consequence: because it is reminder, so whoever wills, let him remember.

**Will and Responsibility**

Verse attributes will to human: *man sha'a* — whoever wills. Evidence for human agency. Yet next verse will attribute ultimate will to God, showing two levels: human will exists and real, but operates within divine will that created it. Ibn Taymiyyah explains: God wills that humans have will; He does not force them, but creates ability. Balance prevents both fatalism (no will) and arrogance (independent will). Practical message: you can remember now; choice yours; no one prevents you except own refusal. The Qur'an says: > *"...God does not change condition of people until they change what in themselves..."* (Qur'an 13:11) — change begins with human will to remember.

The *dhakarah* — remember it — implies not just memorization but taking heed, acting, living by reminder. Remembering is not mental recall but existential response.

**Invitation, Not Coercion**

Verse gentle invitation after stern warnings. After describing Saqar, pledge, donkey simile, says: whoever wills, let him remember. Door open. No compulsion. Reflects Qur'anic principle: > *"There is no compulsion in religion..."* (Qur'an 2:256)

> *"...you are not controller over them..."* (Qur'an 88:22)

Reminder benefits only willing heart; forced remembrance not remembrance. The Prophet said: > *"...make things easy, not difficult; give glad tidings, not drive away..."* (al-Bukhari 69, Muslim 1734, from Anas)

Invitation to remember is easy, open, accessible. No need for *suhuf munashsharah* — just will to remember existing reminder.

**The Time to Will**

The time to will is now, before *yaqin* comes. Verse 47 said guilty persisted until yaqin came; verse 55 says whoever wills, let him remember now, before yaqin. The *man sha'a* — whoever wills — includes everyone reading: you, now, can will to remember. The verse is immediate call to action, not theoretical. The Qur'an says: > *"...and hasten to forgiveness..."* (Qur'an 3:133) — hasten, not delay. Willing to remember now is hastening.

## Sūrah al-Muddaththir 74:56

> **And they remember not, save as God wills. He is most worthy of reverence and most worthy of granting forgiveness.**

**Expanded Commentary**

**Ma Yadhkuruna illa an Yasha'a Allah: Divine Will Overarching**

Final verse *wa ma yadhkuruna illa an yasha'a Allah, huwa ahlu al-taqwa wa ahlu al-maghfirah* — "And they remember not, save as God wills. He is most worthy of reverence and most worthy of granting forgiveness" — closes sūrah with tawhid of will and two divine names. *Ma yadhkuruna illa an yasha'a Allah* — they will not remember unless God wills — shows human will to remember depends on divine will to enable. Tabari: none remembers except God wills it. Ibn Kathir: remembrance is by God's will, not just human. Qur'an says: > *"And you will not will except that God wills..."* (Qur'an 76:30, 81:29)

> *"...and God guides whom He wills..."* (Qur'an 2:213)

This does not cancel verse 55's free will but completes it: human will real but not independent; divine will overarching. Like child wills to walk, but parent wills to let go and support. Both true. Ibn Abbas says: God wills faith for believer, so believer wills faith.

The *wa ma yadhkuruna* — and they will not remember — refers to disbelievers who turn away; they will not remember unless God wills to guide them, and God wills to guide those who go forth (37).

**Ahl al-Taqwa wa Ahl al-Maghfirah: Worthy of Reverence and Forgiveness**

Sūrah ends with two divine attributes: *Ahl al-Taqwa* — People of Taqwa? Actually *Ahl* here means Worthy, Possessor, Deserving — He is worthy to be revered, feared, obeyed. *Ahl al-Maghfirah* — Worthy to forgive, Possessor of forgiveness. Tabari: He deserves that servants fear Him and He worthy to forgive them if repent. Ibn Kathir: worthy to be feared and worthy to forgive those who fear Him. Pairing beautiful: He worthy of reverence, and He worthy to forgive — reverence and forgiveness together. He does not forgive because lenient about reverence; He forgives because worthy of being sought for forgiveness. Prophet reported God says: > *"I am most worthy to be feared, so do not set up another god with Me. Whoever fears to set up another with Me, I am most worthy to forgive him."* (Ahmad 22129, Ibn Majah 4300, from Abu Hurayrah, hasan — cited by Qurtubi for this verse)

> *"...He is worthy of reverence and worthy of forgiveness..."* (Qur'an 74:56, itself)

The *huwa* — He — emphatic: He alone worthy.

**Final Balance: Fear and Hope**

Sūrah began with blanket and fear, moved through warning, case study, Hell, responsibility, and ends with hope: He worthy to forgive. Last word is *maghfirah* — forgiveness — leaving listener with hope after fear. Structure mirrors Qur'anic balance: warn, then give hope; describe Saqar, then Gardens; describe pledge, then exception of right; describe fleeing asses, then invitation to remember; describe human will, then divine will that forgives. Final names invite to return: He worthy of your taqwa — so give it — and worthy of forgiving — so seek it. Sūrah that began with *qum* — arise — ends with *maghfirah* — forgiveness for those who arise.

The Qur'an says: > *"...and whoever fears God, He will make for him way out and provide..."* (Qur'an 65:2-3)

Taqwa leads to maghfirah and provision.

**Living the Seal**

For modern reader, ending with *Ahl al-Taqwa wa Ahl al-Maghfirah* provides daily compass: start day with taqwa — careful driving through dangerous path, aware of God — and end day seeking maghfirah — knowing you swerved. He worthy of both. Sūrah that began with Prophet wrapped in blanket ends with God wrapped in worthiness to be feared and to forgive — blanket of mercy larger than any human blanket of fear. The Prophet taught: > *"...O God, You are Most Forgiving, You love forgiveness, so forgive me."* (al-Tirmidhi 3513, from Aisha, sahih)

Ending on maghfirah invites to say: O Ahl al-Maghfirah, forgive me. The sūrah's final word leaves door open, even after Saqar, even after donkeys fleeing, even after demanding pages — door of maghfirah open for whoever wills to remember (55) and whom God wills to enable (56).

The early Muslims would end recitation of this sūrah with reflection: He worthy to be feared, so fear Him; He worthy to forgive, so seek forgiveness. That is complete religion in two names.

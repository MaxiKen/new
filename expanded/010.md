# Sūrah Yūnus (Chapter 10) — Expanded Verse-by-Verse Commentary

## Introduction to the Sūrah

**Expanded Commentary**

**The Herald of the Alif-Lām-Rā Cycle**

Sūrah Yūnus stands at an important architectural crossroad in the compilation of the Qur'anic muṣḥaf. It is the tenth chapter of the Qur'an and the first in an unbroken sequence of six sūrahs (Sūrahs 10 through 15: Yūnus, Hūd, Yūsuf, al-Raʿd, Ibrāhīm, and al-Ḥijr) that open with the mystical letters *Alif, Lām, Rāʾ* (with the slight variation of *Alif, Lām, Mīm, Rāʾ* in Sūrah 13). After the extensive legal, communal, and constitutional ordinances of the long Madinan sūrahs (al-Baqarah, Āl ʿImrān, al-Nisāʾ, al-Māʾidah, and al-Tawbah), Sūrah Yūnus returns the reader to the fiery crucible of Makkan preaching. It re-anchors human consciousness in the primary questions of existence: the reality of revelation, the signs of cosmic order, the inevitability of the Final Return, the psychology of human complacency, and the absolute sovereignty of the one Creator.

The majority of classical scholars, including Ibn ʿAbbās, al-Ḥasan al-Baṣrī, Mujāhid, and Qatādah, classify Sūrah Yūnus as a Makkan revelation in its entirety, revealed during the middle to late Makkan period before the Hijrah. Some authorities, such as al-Bayḍāwī and al-Qurṭubī, note minor reports suggesting that verses 40 and 94–96 might have been revealed or reiterated in Madinah; however, the thematic continuity, rhythmic majesty, and theological thrust of the chapter demonstrate a unified Makkan atmosphere. It addresses a pagan society steeped in ancestral polytheism, fatalism, and skepticism toward the afterlife, confronting their objections not with coercion, but with inescapable rational proofs drawn from the cosmos, human psychology, and sacred history.

**The Central Argument: The Authenticity of Revelation and the Fallacy of Conjecture**

At the very core of Sūrah Yūnus is the vindication of divine revelation (*al-Waḥy*) and the repudiation of conjecture (*al-ẓann*). The Makkan idolaters expressed profound astonishment that God would communicate with humanity through a mortal man from among themselves (10:2), alleging that the Qur'an was merely poetic invention, ancestral folklore, or manifest sorcery (*siḥr mubīn*). The sūrah methodically dismantles these accusations:

> *"And this Qur'an is not such as could ever be produced by other than God; but it is a confirmation of that which was before it and a detailed explanation of the Book, wherein there is no doubt, from the Lord of the worlds."* (Qur'an 10:37)

The sūrah lays down the famous open challenge to all skeptics: if you believe this Book to be humanly authored, compose a single chapter resembling it and summon every power in creation besides God to aid you (10:38). It demonstrates that human resistance to truth is rarely an intellectual incapacity; rather, it stems from epistemic pride, heedlessness, and an uncritical reliance on unverified assumptions (*ẓann*):

> *"And most of them follow nothing but conjecture. Truly conjecture avails nothing against the truth."* (Qur'an 10:36)

**The Cosmic Rhythm: Origin, Sustenance, and the Final Return**

The theology of Sūrah Yūnus is established upon a grand triad: the Origin (*al-Mabdaʾ*), Divine Governance (*al-Tadbīr*), and the Final Return (*al-Maʿād*). The universe is not an accidental or purposeless machine; it was brought into existence by a purposeful Creator in six cosmic epochs (*ayyām*), after which He established His authority upon the Supreme Throne (*al-ʿArsh*), actively directing every single cosmic and terrestrial affair (*yudabbiru al-amr*). 

The sūrah draws the human gaze to the luminous celestial bodies—the sun as a blazing radiance (*ḍiyāʾ*) and the moon as a measured light (*nūr*) whose orbital stations allow humanity to reckon time and maintain order (10:5). The cyclical variation of night and day, the descent of rain, and the seasonal renewal of the earth are presented as dynamic testimonies to divine oneness (*Tawḥīd*). If God alone originates creation and meticulously sustains it, then returning creation back to life for moral accountability is not only effortless for Him, but a moral necessity of His justice (*bi'l-qisṭ*):

> *"Unto Him is your return all together; the promise of God is true. Verily He originates creation, then He brings it back, that He may recompense with justice those who believe and perform righteous deeds."* (Qur'an 10:4)

**Human Psychology in Prosperity and Peril**

A profound theme running through Sūrah Yūnus is its piercing analysis of human spiritual psychology. The chapter provides vivid case studies of human fickleness. In moments of catastrophe—such as when sailors are engulfed by towering waves on a tempestuous sea—all false gods and secondary dependencies vanish, and human beings spontaneously call upon God alone with absolute sincerity. Yet, the moment divine mercy delivers them safely to dry land, they relapse into arrogance and worldly rebellion (10:12, 22–23).

To shatter this illusion of worldly permanence, the sūrah presents the haunting agricultural parable of worldly life (*mathal al-ḥayāt al-dunyā*): an earth flourishing with lush vegetation, appearing self-sufficient and invincible to its cultivators, suddenly reduced to a barren, mown wasteland overnight by divine decree (10:24). In place of this transient delusion, God invites humanity to the enduring sanctuary of ultimate serenity:

> *"And God calls unto the Abode of Peace (Dār al-Salām), and guides whomsoever He wills unto a straight path."* (Qur'an 10:25)

**Prophetic Narratives and the Singular Mercy of Jonah's People**

The later sections of the sūrah ground these theological and psychological truths in sacred history, focusing upon the steadfastness of Prophet Noah (*Nūḥ*) against his mocking tribe (10:71–73) and the epic confrontation between Moses (*Mūsā*), Aaron (*Hārūn*), and the imperial tyranny of Pharaoh (10:75–93). It documents Pharaoh's desperate, deathbed confession of faith as the waters of the Red Sea collapsed upon him—a repentance rejected because it came only when physical doom was irreversible (10:90–92).

Contrasted against these tragic civilizational ruins is the unique, redemptive history of the people of Prophet Jonah (*Yūnus*), after whom the sūrah is named:

> *"Why was there not a single town that believed and benefited from its faith, except the people of Jonah? When they believed, We removed from them the punishment of disgrace in the life of this world and granted them enjoyment for a time."* (Qur'an 10:98)

This historical exception serves as a monumental message of hope to the Quraysh and to all subsequent generations: historical destiny is not fatalistic. Genuine collective repentance, undertaken before the final threshold of divine retribution, has the power to avert disaster and restore divine favor.

**The Structural Integrity of This Commentary**

In accordance with scholarly rigor and the verse-by-verse methodology, every verse of Sūrah Yūnus is examined individually as a complete, independent discourse. Each section integrates classical Arabic morphology and root linguistics, authoritative Qur'anic cross-references, authentic Hadith literature with precise textual citations, classical exegesis from masters such as al-Ṭabarī, Ibn Kathīr, al-Qurṭubī, and Fakhr al-Dīn al-Rāzī, and deep logical, theological, and practical reflections relevant to the contemporary seeker of truth.

## Sūrah Yūnus 10:1

> **Alif. Lām. Rā. These are the signs of the Wise Book.**

**Expanded Commentary**

**The Luminous Letters and the Threshold of Revelation**

The sūrah opens with three isolated letters of the Arabic alphabet: *Alif, Lām, Rāʾ* (*al-ḥurūf al-muqaṭṭaʿāt*). These disconnected letters appear at the beginning of twenty-nine chapters of the Qur'an, arresting the listener's attention and serving as a profound theological and linguistic threshold. Classical exegetes, led by Ibn ʿAbbās and followed by al-Ṭabarī, al-Zamakhsharī, and Ibn Kathīr, emphasize that these letters represent a direct demonstration of the Qur'an's inimitability (*iʿjāz*). The revelation addresses a culture that took immense pride in verbal eloquence, poetry, and linguistic rhetoric, using the very letters, consonants, and phonetic building blocks of their everyday speech. By framing divine speech with the ordinary alphabet, the text poses a silent, devastating challenge: this Book is composed of the very sounds you master, yet neither you nor the collective genius of creation can produce a single chapter like it.

Al-Qurṭubī records an additional scholarly perspective transmitted from the early generation, wherein these letters are understood as divine secrets whose full reality resides with God (*Allāhu aʿlamu bi-murādihi bi-dhālik*), serving to humble human intellect at the very gateway of scripture. They remind the reader that human comprehension is finite, and that revelation proceeds from the Infinite.

**The Nature of the Signs and the Reality of Divine Wisdom**

Immediately following the letters, God states: *"These are the signs of the Wise Book"* (*Tilka āyātu al-Kitābi al-ḥakīm*). The demonstrative pronoun *tilka* ("these") points to high station and sublime grandeur. The verses of the Qur'an are termed *āyāt* (singular *āyah*), which literally signifies distinct signposts, miraculous proofs, and luminous indicators that point beyond themselves to their Originator. Just as an explorer relies on physical landmarks to navigate an uncharted desert, the moral agent relies on the *āyāt* of the Book to navigate the complex spiritual landscape of earthly life.

The Book is qualified by the majestic attribute *al-Ḥakīm* ("the Wise"). In Arabic morphology, *ḥakīm* is an intensive adjective derived from the root *ḥ-k-m*, which encompasses three interconnected meanings:
1. *Iḥkām* (precision, perfection, and flawless construction): The Qur'an contains no contradiction, incoherence, redundancy, or defect. As Ibn Kathīr notes:
> *"It is decisive, clear, containing no falsehood from before it or behind it, revealed by the One who is All-Wise and Praiseworthy."*
2. *Ḥikmah* (profound wisdom): Every command, prohibition, narrative, and parable is placed in its proper measure to achieve human flourishing in this world and ultimate salvation in the next.
3. *Ḥākim* (judging authority): The Book acts as the supreme criterion that judges between truth and falsehood, justice and oppression.

**The Book as a Living Intellectual Anchor**

In an era characterized by epistemic confusion, shifting moral paradigms, and subjective definitions of reality, the description of the Qur'an as *al-Kitāb al-Ḥakīm* provides an immovable intellectual anchor. It asserts that truth is neither arbitrary nor a human cultural construct. The universe operates under coherent physical laws because its Creator is All-Wise (*al-Ḥakīm*); likewise, human moral life must align with revealed wisdom to achieve equilibrium. When a society divorces its ethical foundations from divine wisdom, it inevitably falls into existential anxiety, social fragmentation, and injustice. The opening verse thus invites the reader to approach the Qur'an not merely as an ancient literary text, but as an ever-relevant reservoir of cosmic wisdom designed to heal and elevate the human condition.

## Sūrah Yūnus 10:2

> **Is it a marvel for mankind that We have sent revelation unto a man from among themselves, [saying], “Warn mankind and give glad tidings to those who believe that they shall have a station of sincerity with their Lord”? The disbelievers say, “Surely this is a manifest sorcerer.”**

**Expanded Commentary**

**The Paradox of Human Skepticism Toward Prophethood**

The verse opens with an interrogative particle of reprimand and astonishment (*istifhām inkārī*): *"Is it a marvel for mankind that We have sent revelation unto a man from among themselves...?"* Classical commentators, including al-Ṭabarī, al-Wāḥidī in his *Asbāb al-Nuzūl*, and Ibn Kathīr, record that when the Prophet Muhammad ﷺ proclaimed his divine mission in Makkah, the chieftains of Quraysh scoffed in disbelief. They argued that if God were to send a messenger, He ought to have dispatched an awe-inspiring angel, or chosen an exceptionally wealthy and politically dominant prince from Makkah or Ṭāʾif, saying: "Could God find no one to send except this orphaned ward of Abū Ṭālib?"

This objection reflects a recurring human cognitive bias: the assumption that divine truth must manifest through sensational, overwhelming, or aristocratic displays of worldly power. The Qur'an exposes the profound irrationality of this stance. It is precisely through God's mercy and pedagogical wisdom that the messenger is chosen from among humanity—a mortal who shares human nature, experiences hunger, grief, joy, and temptation, and walks through the markets. An angelic messenger would be impossible for mortals to emulate; humans would argue that an angel cannot model human moral restraint, familial ethics, or courage in the face of mortal peril. As God states elsewhere:

> *"And if We had made him an angel, We would have made him [in the form of] a man, and We would have covered them with that in which they cover themselves."* (Qur'an 6:9)

**The Dual Apostolic Mission: Warning and Glad Tidings**

The core mandate given to the Messenger comprises two complementary functions: *"Warn mankind and give glad tidings to those who believe."* The warning (*indhār*) is addressed universally to all mankind (*al-nās*), alerting them to the moral accountability awaiting every soul, the destructive consequences of injustice, and the reality of the Final Judgment. The glad tidings (*tabshīr*) are directed specifically to those who respond with faith and moral action, assuring them of divine acceptance and eternal felicity.

In Islamic theological anthropology, these two poles correspond to the psychological drivers of human action: *khawf* (reverent fear of spiritual ruin) and *rajāʾ* (hope for divine grace). A healthy soul requires both. Warning prevents moral complacency and reckless transgression, while glad tidings preserve the soul from despair and cynicism.

**The Meaning of the 'Station of Sincerity' (Qadam Ṣidq)**

The believers are promised that they will possess a *Qadam Ṣidq* ("a station of sincerity" or "a foot of truth") with their Lord. The classical exegetes provide several profound, interrelated dimensions of this unique Qur'anic phrase:

1. **Precedence and Steadfastness in Virtue:** Ibn ʿAbbās, Mujāhid, and Qatādah explained that *qadam* literally denotes a footstep, signifying precedence in good deeds and an established record of righteous action sent forward (*salaf ṣidq*). Just as a firm step anchors a climber on steep terrain, genuine faith anchors the believer in divine favor.
2. **True Reward and High Rank:** Al-Ḥasan al-Baṣrī and al-Bayḍāwī noted that it denotes a noble, unshakeable reward (*thawāb*) guaranteed by God's promise that never fails.
3. **The Prophetic Intercession (*al-Shafāʿah*):** ʿAlī ibn Abī Ṭālib and several early authorities interpreted *Qadam Ṣidq* as the intercession of the Prophet Muhammad ﷺ on the Day of Resurrection, when his established station of praise (*al-Maqām al-Maḥmūd*) will be manifested for the faithful. The Prophet ﷺ said:

> *"I am the master of the children of Adam on the Day of Resurrection, and no boast; in my hand will be the banner of praise, and no boast; and there will be no prophet that day, Adam or otherwise, except under my banner."* (Jāmiʿ al-Tirmidhī 3148, graded ḥasan ṣaḥīḥ)

**The Dismissive Charge of Sorcery**

In the face of these undeniable rational proofs and moral beauty, the disbelievers retreated into the accusation: *"Surely this is a manifest sorcerer"* (*sāḥirun mubīn*). When truth threatens entrenched social privileges and idolized traditions, those in denial invariably resort to ad hominem attacks. By labeling the Prophet ﷺ a sorcerer or his words "manifest sorcery" (*siḥr mubīn*), they attempted to explain away the undeniable, transformative impact of the Qur'an upon human hearts without acknowledging its divine origin. The verse illustrates how psychological defense mechanisms operate across time: when an argument cannot be refuted logically, the skeptic attempts to pathologize the messenger.

## Sūrah Yūnus 10:3

> **Truly your Lord is God, Who created the heavens and the earth in six days, then mounted the Throne, directing the affair. There is no intercessor, save by His Leave. That is God, your Lord; so worship Him! Will you not remember?**

**Expanded Commentary**

**Cosmic Origin and the Six Epochs of Creation**

The verse presents a magnificent summary of Islamic metaphysics and theology: *"Truly your Lord is God, Who created the heavens and the earth in six days..."* (*fī sittati ayyām*). Classical scholars, including al-Ṭabarī, Ibn Kathīr, and Fakhr al-Dīn al-Rāzī, discuss the nature of these "six days." Since the physical sun, moon, and earthly day-night cycles were themselves part of this creative sequence, these "days" are not twenty-four-hour solar intervals, but six vast cosmic epochs or stages of creation known fully to God. As the Qur'an itself clarifies in other contexts:

> *"And indeed, a day with your Lord is like a thousand years of those which you count."* (Qur'an 22:47)

Why did the Omnipotent Creator, Who brings things into existence merely by saying "Be" (*Kun fa-yakūn*), choose to unfold creation across six progressive stages? The scholars explain that this gradual, structured manifestation of the cosmos teaches moral agents the virtues of deliberation, order, and systemic harmony. The universe was designed not as an erratic burst of chaotic force, but as an intricately calibrated home where physical laws and biological life could flourish with mathematical precision.

**The Divine Mounting of the Supreme Throne (al-Istiwāʾ ʿalā al-ʿArsh)**

Following the creation of the cosmos, God *"mounted the Throne"* (*thumma istawā ʿalā al-ʿArsh*). The Supreme Throne (*al-ʿArsh*) is the greatest and highest of all created entities, encompassing the heavens and the earth. The Ahl al-Sunnah wa'l-Jamāʿah approach this divine attribute with reverent adherence to the methodology articulated by Imam Mālik ibn Anas when questioned about the *Istiwāʾ*:

> *"The Istiwāʾ is known (in language), the modality (kayfiyyah) is ungraspable by human intellect, believing in it is obligatory, and asking about its modality is an innovation."*

God is established upon His Throne in a manner that befits His absolute majesty and transcendence, completely free from physical confinement, anthropomorphism (*tashbīh*), or nullification (*taʿṭīl*). As the Qur'an decisively declares:

> *"There is nothing like unto Him, and He is the All-Hearing, the All-Seeing."* (Qur'an 42:11)

**Divine Governance: Directing the Entire Cosmic Affair (Tadbīr al-Amr)**

Crucially, the verse adds: *"directing the affair"* (*yudabbiru al-amr*). God did not create the universe and then abandon it to drift as a self-operating clockwork mechanism—a deistic misconception prevalent in ancient and modern philosophies. Rather, God is actively, continuously, and exhaustively sustaining, governing, and guiding every aspect of creation. The root *d-b-r* signifies looking at the end (*dubur*) of affairs and arranging causes toward their perfect, intended outcomes. 

Every orbit of a galaxy, every drop of falling rain, every heartbeat of an organism, and every human historical turning point is directly encompassed by His eternal knowledge and will. The Prophet Muhammad ﷺ emphasized this perpetual divine governance when reciting:

> *"Every day He is upon some affair."* (Qur'an 55:29)
> The Prophet ﷺ explained: *"Of His affairs is that He forgives a sin, relieves an affliction, elevates a people, and abases another."* (Sunan Ibn Mājah 202)

**The Demolition of Autonomous Intercession**

The verse dismantles the fundamental premise of polytheism: *"There is no intercessor, save by His Leave"* (*mā min shafīʿin illā min baʿdi idhnihi*). The pagan Arabs worshipped idols, angels, and ancestral spirits under the illusion that these entities possessed autonomous leverage with God, able to force His hand or lobby Him independently. The Qur'an establishes that intercession (*al-shafāʿah*) is a created honor granted exclusively by God's permission to those with whom He is well-pleased:

> *"Who is it that can intercede with Him except by His permission?"* (Qur'an 2:255)

No created being possesses independent sovereignty, ontological authority, or private influence over the Divine Will. Intercession is an expression of divine grace, not an infringement upon divine power.

**From Cosmic Lordship (Rubūbiyyah) to Exclusive Worship (Ulūhiyyah)**

The verse constructs an inescapable logical conclusion: *"That is God, your Lord; so worship Him! Will you not remember?"* If God alone created the universe, if He alone mounts the Supreme Throne, if He alone directs every affair, and if no entity can intercede without His leave, then He alone is entitled to human devotion, obedience, love, and submission. 

To direct prayer, sacrifice, or ultimate hope to created beings is an intellectual absurdity and a moral outrage. The closing question, *"Will you not remember?"* (*a-fa-lā tadhakkarūn*), indicates that the reality of God's oneness is already imprinted upon the primordial human disposition (*fiṭrah*); it requires only sincere reflection to awaken it.

## Sūrah Yūnus 10:4

> **Unto Him is your return all together; God’s Promise is true. Verily He originates creation, then He brings it back, that He may recompense with justice those who believe and perform righteous deeds. As for the disbelievers, theirs shall be a drink of boiling liquid and a painful punishment for having disbelieved.**

**Expanded Commentary**

**The Inevitability of the Universal Assembly**

The discourse transitions seamlessly from the origin of the cosmos to its ultimate destination: *"Unto Him is your return all together"* (*Ilayhi marjiʿukum jamīʿan*). The fronting of the prepositional phrase *ilayhi* ("unto Him alone") in Arabic grammar conveys exclusivity (*ḥaṣr*). No soul, tyrant, empire, or creature can escape this universal rendezvous. Every human generation—from the first human being to the last soul breathing at the end of time—will be gathered simultaneously before their Creator.

To reinforce this certainty, God declares: *"God’s Promise is true"* (*waʿda Allāhi ḥaqqan*). In human affairs, promises are frequently broken due to lack of power, unforeseen obstacles, forgetfulness, or deceit. God's promise, however, is grounded in absolute omniscience and omnipotence; it is an ontological certainty that will manifest without fail.

**The Cosmological Logic of Resurrection: Origin (Mabdaʾ) and Return (Maʿād)**

The verse articulates the classic Qur'anic cosmological argument for the physical resurrection: *"Verily He originates creation, then He brings it back"* (*Innahu yabdaʾu al-khalqa thumma yuʿīduhu*). Skeptics throughout history have doubted bodily resurrection, arguing that once bones decay and particles disperse into the soil, reconstituting the individual is impossible. The Qur'an exposes the logical flaw in this skepticism.

Creating something *ex nihilo* (out of absolute nothingness) without a prior model is demonstrably more astonishing than repeating that creation once it has already existed. The One Who possessed the power, knowledge, and wisdom to initiate the universe and bring human consciousness into being from a drop of fluid is self-evidently capable of reconstituting that creation. As God argues elsewhere:

> *"And He it is Who originates the creation, then He brings it back, and it is even easier for Him."* (Qur'an 30:27)

**The Moral Necessity of the Hereafter: Recompense with Absolute Justice (Bi'l-Qisṭ)**

Why must there be a Resurrection? The verse reveals the profound ethical purpose underlying eschatology: *"that He may recompense with justice those who believe and perform righteous deeds"* (*li-yajziya alladhīna āmanū wa-ʿamilū al-ṣāliḥāti bi'l-qisṭ*). 

If earthly existence were the only arena of human life, the moral arc of the universe would be fundamentally broken. Throughout human history, righteous prophets and moral heroes have been persecuted, martyred, and impoverished, while brutal tyrants, exploiters, and mass murderers have lived in opulent luxury and died peacefully in old age. A world without an afterlife would mean that tyranny and righteousness end in the exact same dust—a scenario completely incompatible with the wisdom and justice of a Moral Creator. The Resurrection (*al-Maʿād*) is an absolute moral necessity to vindicate divine justice (*al-qisṭ*), ensuring that every virtuous act is honored and every injustice rectified.

**The Fate of the Obstinate Rejectors**

The verse concludes with a stark description of the retribution awaiting those who willfully rejected divine truth: *"As for the disbelievers, theirs shall be a drink of boiling liquid and a painful punishment for having disbelieved"* (*sharābun min ḥamīm wa-ʿadhābun alīmun bimā kānū yakfurūn*). 

The Arabic word *ḥamīm* refers to water brought to the ultimate extreme of boiling heat, so intense that it lacerates the internal organs. This visceral imagery serves to awaken human beings from the stupor of heedlessness. The punishment is explicitly tied to their own persistent choices: *bimā kānū yakfurūn* ("because of what they used to disbelieve"). In Islamic theology, divine punishment is never arbitrary malice; it is the exact, just manifestation of the soul's own chosen spiritual corruption, returning upon itself as agony.

## Sūrah Yūnus 10:5

> **He it is Who made the sun a radiance, and the moon a light, and determined for it stations, that you might know the number of years and the reckoning [of time]. God did not create these, save in truth. He expounds the signs for a people who know.**

**Expanded Commentary**

**The Precision of Celestial Mechanics: Radiance (Ḍiyāʾ) and Light (Nūr)**

The Qur'an directs human contemplation toward the cosmic clockwork overhead: *"He it is Who made the sun a radiance (ḍiyāʾ), and the moon a light (nūr)..."* Classical scholars and Arabic lexicographers have highlighted the precise, deliberate distinction between the two Arabic terms employed here:
- *Ḍiyāʾ* (radiance/blaze) denotes light that is innate, self-generating, intense, and accompanied by heat, perfectly describing the thermonuclear furnace of the sun.
- *Nūr* (light) signifies luminosity that is cool, gentle, and reflected from an external source, perfectly characterizing the moon's reflective illumination.

As Fakhr al-Dīn al-Rāzī and later commentators noted, the Qur'an consistently maintains this exact terminology across various passages, such as:

> *"Blessed is He Who placed in the sky great stars and placed therein a burning lamp and an illuminating moon."* (Qur'an 25:61)

This distinction reflects the profound harmony between Qur'anic description and the physical realities of the cosmos, illustrating how every celestial body is assigned a precise physical nature suited to its role in the planetary ecology.

**Lunar Stations and the Calculation of Time**

The verse continues: *"and determined for it stations (manāzil), that you might know the number of years and the reckoning [of time]"* (*wa-qaddarahu manāzila li-taʿlamū ʿadada al-sinīna wa'l-ḥisāb*). The moon traverses twenty-eight distinct celestial stations (*manāzil*) across its monthly orbit, waxing from a slender crescent (*hilāl*) to a luminous full moon (*badr*) and waning back to an ancient, curved date-stalk (*al-ʿurjūn al-qadīm*; cf. 36:39).

This mathematical predictability is not accidental; it was deliberately calibrated by the Creator to provide humanity with an accessible, universal calendar. Through lunar cycles, societies across all eras—regardless of technological development—could track months, sacred pilgrimage seasons, commercial contracts, agricultural plantings, and civil transactions. The solar cycle provides the seasonal and daily rhythms, while the lunar cycle structures the calendar months, together forming a dual celestial clock of breathtaking elegance.

**Creation in Truth: The Rejection of Cosmic Absurdity**

God emphasizes: *"God did not create these, save in truth"* (*mā khalaqa Allāhu dhālika illā bi'l-ḥaqq*). In Islamic theology, *al-Ḥaqq* (Truth/Purpose) is the antithesis of *bāṭil* (absurdity, vanity, or sport). The universe is not the byproduct of blind physical accidents or purposeless collisions of matter; it was engineered with deliberate intention, moral seriousness, and structural integrity. As God declares in other verses:

> *"And We did not create the heaven and the earth and that between them in play."* (Qur'an 21:16)

The laws of physics, the precision of gravitational constants, and the delicate balance of the atmospheric conditions are all signposts of divine intentionality.

**The Signs Unveiled for People of Knowledge**

The verse concludes: *"He expounds the signs for a people who know"* (*yufaṣṣilu al-āyāti li-qawmin yaʿlamūn*). The verb *yufaṣṣil* signifies articulating with utmost clarity, breaking complex realities down into understandable, distinct components. 

The signs in nature do not speak to apathy or blind dogmatism; they reveal their secrets to those who cultivate knowledge, scientific observation, and reflective intellect. Islam establishes an inseparable bond between faith and empirical observation: true scientific inquiry into the natural order does not lead away from God, but rather deepens the mind's awe before the boundless intelligence of the Creator.

## Sūrah Yūnus 10:6

> **Surely in the variation of the night and the day and whatsoever God has created in the heavens and on the earth are signs for a people who are reverent.**

**Expanded Commentary**

**The Rhythm of Cosmic Alternation (Ikhtilāf al-Layl wa'l-Nahār)**

The verse turns human attention to the perpetual alternation of darkness and light: *"Surely in the variation of the night and the day and whatsoever God has created in the heavens and on the earth are signs..."* The Arabic term *ikhtilāf* carries a rich dual connotation:
1. **Alternation in Succession:** Night perpetually follows day and day follows night in an unbroken, flawless relay, without one outstripping or colliding with the other.
2. **Variation in Dimensions:** The seasonal lengthening and shortening of daylight and night across different latitudes of the globe, enabling agricultural cycles, ecological balance, and atmospheric regulation.

Classical exegetes such as al-Ṭabarī, al-Qurṭubī, and al-Rāzī note that this cosmic rhythm is a living testimony to the Creator's absolute control. Were the earth to be locked in perpetual daylight, biological life would perish from relentless heat, exhaustion, and the inability of organisms to rest and repair. Were it locked in perpetual night, photosynthetic life would collapse, temperatures would plummet, and existence would freeze into oblivion. As God states elsewhere:

> *"Say: Have you considered: if God should make for you the night continuous until the Day of Resurrection, what deity other than God could bring you light? Will you not then hear?"* (Qur'an 28:71)

**The Universal Sanctuary of Celestial and Terrestrial Wonders**

The verse expands its scope from the diurnal cycle to encompass the entire cosmos: *"and whatsoever God has created in the heavens and on the earth"* (*wa-mā khalaqa Allāhu fī al-samāwāti wa'l-arḍ*). The heavens contain galaxies, stars, gravitational fields, and atmospheric shields; the earth contains oceans, tectonic plates, mineral resources, flora, fauna, and the microscopic realms of cellular life.

Every single entity in this vast expanse possesses finitude, contingency, and dependence, proving that it cannot be self-originating or self-sustaining. The famous early ascetic and poet Abu'l-ʿAtāhiyah captured this insight in his timeless couplet:

> *"In every single thing there is a sign,*
> *Being proof that He is the One."*

**Reverence (Taqwā) as the Cognitive Key to Perception**

Why are these cosmic wonders described specifically as *"signs for a people who are reverent"* (*li-qawmin yattaqūn*)? The concept of *taqwā* (godly reverence, moral mindfulness, and protective consciousness) is the essential spiritual organ through which reality is perceived accurately. 

Just as a traveler walking carefully through a path strewn with thorns steps with vigilant awareness to avoid injury, the person of *taqwā* walks through the cosmos with an awakened, reflective mind, alert to the reality of the Creator and mindful of moral accountability. A person consumed by arrogance, hedonism, or cynicism looks at the sunrise merely as a physical mechanism; the person of *taqwā* perceives in that same sunrise the overwhelming mercy, majesty, and beauty of God. Faith is not an intellectual blindfold, but the very lens that brings the architecture of the cosmos into focus.

## Sūrah Yūnus 10:7

> **Truly those who anticipate not the meeting with Us, and who are content with the life of this world and feel secure therein, and who are heedless of Our signs,**

**Expanded Commentary**

**The Anatomy of Materialistic Complacency**

Verses 7 and 8 present a penetrating psychological and spiritual diagnosis of the soul trapped in secular materialism. Verse 7 outlines the four sequential stages of spiritual degeneration that characterize those who reject divine reality:

1. **Denial of the Ultimate Encounter (*Lā yarjūna liqāʾanā*):** The root *r-j-w* denotes hope, anticipation, and mindful expectation. These individuals live as if death is absolute annihilation, completely disregarding their inevitable meeting with their Lord. When the expectation of divine accountability is removed, the moral compass of the human being is fundamentally compromised.
2. **Contentment with the Lower Life (*Raḍū bi'l-ḥayāti al-dunyā*):** The term *dunyā* literally means the base, proximate, and ephemeral world. Rather than using worldly life as a provisional bridge toward eternity, they make the pursuit of physical pleasure, social status, and material accumulation their ultimate goal and highest purpose.
3. **False Psychological Security (*Wa'ṭmaʾannū bihā*):** They sink into a state of deep complacency, feeling permanent, self-sufficient, and secure in their worldly wealth and health, oblivious to the fragile, transient nature of earthly existence.
4. **Active Heedlessness of Divine Signs (*Wa'lladhīna hum ʿan āyātinā ghāfilūn*):** Having oriented their entire consciousness toward immediate gratification, they become willfully blind and deaf (*ghāfilūn*) to both the cosmic signs (*āyāt kawniyyah*) in nature and the revealed signs (*āyāt qur'āniyyah*) in scripture.

**The Classical Exegetical Diagnosis of Ghawflah**

Imam al-Qurṭubī and Ibn Kathīr elucidate that *ghaflah* (heedlessness) is not merely a passive lack of information; it is a willful refusal to engage the intellect and heart with existential truths. Al-Ṭabarī explains:

> *"They are those who do not believe in the Resurrection, nor do they fear a Reckoning, nor do they hope for a reward from God. They are completely satisfied with the temporal pleasures of this world in place of the bliss of the Hereafter, anchoring their peace in its fleeting comforts."*

This state of spiritual dormancy is precisely what leads to ethical decay. When a human being believes that this fleeting life is all there is, self-interest becomes the supreme law, and justice is sacrificed whenever it conflicts with personal gain.

## Sūrah Yūnus 10:8

> **it is they whose refuge shall be the Fire for that which they used to earn.**

**Expanded Commentary**

**The Law of Moral Reaping and Divine Retribution**

Following the psychological portrait established in the preceding verse, God delivers the inevitable ontological verdict: *"it is they whose refuge shall be the Fire for that which they used to earn"* (*ulāʾika maʾwāhumu al-nāru bimā kānū yaksibūn*). 

The Arabic term *maʾwā* signifies a permanent sanctuary, abode, or final shelter—a place where a wanderer seeks refuge at the end of a journey. The use of *maʾwā* in connection with the Hellfire contains a profound rhetorical irony (*tahakkum*): having rejected the eternal shelter of God's grace and made the fleeting world their temporary refuge, their only ultimate destination is the Fire.

**The Concept of Spiritual Earning (al-Kasb)**

The text emphasizes that this terrifying outcome is entirely the consequence of *"that which they used to earn"* (*bimā kānū yaksibūn*). In Islamic theology (*ʿIlm al-Kalām*), the doctrine of *al-kasb* (moral acquisition/earning) establishes human agency, moral responsibility, and free will within the overarching framework of divine decree. 

God does not cast souls into retribution out of arbitrary cruelty. Rather, human beings actively "earn" their spiritual destinies through their persistent choices, intentions, deeds, and moral trajectory. The Fire is the objective, physical reality of their own spiritual state: a heart that cultivated hatred for truth, burned with arrogance, and rejected the light of God in this world finds itself naturally immersed in an environment of darkness and burning torment in the next. As the Prophet Muhammad ﷺ narrated that God Almighty said:

> *"O My servants, it is only your deeds that I record for you and then recompense you for. So let him who finds good praise God, and let him who finds other than that blame none but himself."* (Ṣaḥīḥ Muslim 2577, from Abū Dharr al-Ghifārī)

**The Modern Crisis of Worldly Fixation**

In the contemporary world, verses 7 and 8 serve as an urgent warning against hyper-consumerism and secular existentialism. Modern culture constantly encourages individuals to be "content with the life of this world and feel secure therein," measuring human worth solely through economic output, material acquisitions, and digital validation. The Qur'an dismantles this illusion, reminding humanity that an existence divorced from eternal accountability leads inevitably to spiritual emptiness in this life and ruin in the next.

## Sūrah Yūnus 10:9

> **Truly those who believe and perform righteous deeds, their Lord guides them by their faith. They shall have rivers running below them in Gardens of bliss.**

**Expanded Commentary**

**The Dialectic of Faith and Guidance (Hidayah bil-Iman)**

In sharp, luminous contrast to the inhabitants of the Fire, the verse introduces the portrait of the righteous: *"Truly those who believe and perform righteous deeds, their Lord guides them by their faith..."* (*yahdīhim Rabbuhum bi-īmānihim*). 

Classical exegetes offer profound insights into the phrase *"guides them by their faith"*:
1. **The Light of Faith on the Day of Resurrection:** Ibn Jurayj, Qatādah, and Ibn Kathīr record that the believer's faith will literally transform on the Day of Judgment into a radiant light (*nūr*) walking before them and to their right, illuminating their path across the narrow Bridge (*al-Ṣirāṭ*) spanning the abyss of Hell into Paradise. As God states in Sūrah al-Ḥadīd:
> *"On the Day you see the believing men and believing women, their light proceeding before them and on their right..."* (Qur'an 57:12)
2. **Guidance in This World:** Al-Ṭabarī and al-Rāzī explain that faith operates dynamically: when a person sincerely embraces faith and puts it into practice through righteous deeds (*al-aʿmāl al-ṣāliḥah*), God rewards that initial step by granting them deeper spiritual discernment (*furqān*), clarity of intellect, and protection against moral corruption. Faith is not static; it acts as an active catalyst for ever-increasing divine guidance.

**The Inseparable Bond Between Faith and Action**

Notice the consistent Qur'anic pairing: *"those who believe and perform righteous deeds"* (*alladhīna āmanū wa-ʿamilū al-ṣāliḥāt*). Faith (*īmān*) in Islam is not merely an intellectual assent or a private emotional feeling; it is an internal seed that must bear the fruit of righteous action. 

Righteous deeds validate the authenticity of faith, while faith infuses deeds with sincere intention (*ikhlāṣ*). Without faith, actions lack spiritual foundation; without action, faith withers into empty rhetoric.

**The Architecture of Eternal Bliss (Jannāt al-Naʿīm)**

The verse concludes with the promise of eternal reward: *"They shall have rivers running below them in Gardens of bliss"* (*tajrī min taḥtihimu al-anhāru fī jannāti al-naʿīm*). 

The plural *Jannāt* (Gardens) denotes multiple tiers and vast dimensions of celestial sanctuaries. The imagery of crystal-clear rivers flowing beneath their palaces and pavilions represents the ultimate fulfillment of peace, perpetual vitality, and abundance. In the harsh, arid climate of Arabia—and indeed for the weary human soul in any era—flowing water in lush gardens represents the ultimate sanctuary of rest, rejuvenation, and divine generosity.

## Sūrah Yūnus 10:10

> **Their supplication therein shall be, “Glory be to Thee, O God!” And therein their greeting shall be, “Peace.” And the conclusion of their supplication shall be, “Praise be to God, Lord of the worlds!”**

**Expanded Commentary**

**The Transformation of Prayer from Petition to Ecstatic Praise**

The verse provides an intimate, breathtaking glimpse into the spiritual life and psychology of the inhabitants of Paradise: *"Their supplication therein shall be, 'Glory be to Thee, O God!' (Daʿwāhum fīhā Subḥānaka Allāhumma)..."*

In this earthly life, human supplication (*duʿāʾ*) is predominantly an act of petition—beseeching God for relief from pain, provision, protection, forgiveness, and guidance. In Paradise, however, all pain, want, fear, and sorrow have been permanently extinguished. Consequently, *duʿāʾ* is transformed entirely into pure, ecstatic contemplation and glorification of the Divine. 

Imam Muslim records in his *Ṣaḥīḥ* a profound hadith transmitted by Jābir ibn ʿAbdillāh, wherein the Prophet Muhammad ﷺ described the nature of celestial devotion:

> *"The people of Paradise will eat and drink therein, but they will not spit, nor urinate, nor defecate, nor blow their noses. Their food will digest through a burp that smells of musk. They will be inspired to glorify and praise God (yulhamūna al-tasbīḥa wa'l-taḥmīd) just as effortlessly as you are inspired to breathe."* (Ṣaḥīḥ Muslim 2835)

Glorifying God in Paradise is not a burdensome ritual or exhausting duty; it becomes as natural, effortless, and delightful to the purified soul as the automatic drawing of breath is to physical lungs.

**The Atmosphere of Universal Peace (Salām)**

The verse continues: *"And therein their greeting shall be, 'Peace' (Wa-taḥiyyatuhum fīhā salām)."* In Paradise, every utterance and interaction is saturated with *Salām*—safety, harmony, mutual reverence, and peace:
- God greets them with peace: *"Peace! A word from a Merciful Lord"* (36:58).
- The angels greet them with peace at every gate: *"Peace be upon you for what you patiently endured; how excellent is the final abode!"* (13:24).
- Believers greet one another with words of peace, completely free from rancor, envy, malice, or superficial posturing.

**The Grand Doxology: Concluding with Universal Praise**

The spiritual cycle of the denizens of Paradise culminates in the grandest doxological formula: *"And the conclusion of their supplication shall be, 'Praise be to God, Lord of the worlds!' (Wa-ākhiru daʿwāhum ani al-ḥamdu lillāhi Rabbi al-ʿālamīn)."*

This doxology mirrors the opening of the Qur'an itself in Sūrah al-Fātiḥah: *"Al-ḥamdu lillāhi Rabbi al-ʿālamīn"* (1:2). The journey of creation, revelation, moral struggle, and ultimate salvation begins with praise and culminates in eternal praise. *Tasbīḥ* (*Subḥānallāh*) purifies the intellect by negating every flaw, deficiency, and limitation from the Creator, while *Taḥmīd* (*Al-ḥamdulillāh*) affirms every perfection, beauty, majesty, and mercy in Him. The dwellers of Paradise find their ultimate, eternal joy not merely in the physical splendors of the Garden, but in the unending, ecstatic realization of the perfection of their Lord.

## Sūrah Yūnus 10:11

> **Were God to hasten evil for mankind, even as they would hasten the good, their appointed term would have been concluded. But We leave those who anticipate not the meeting with Us to wander confused in their rebellion.**

**Expanded Commentary**

**Divine Forbearance (Ḥilm) Against Human Impetuosity**

The verse addresses a profound theological and psychological reality regarding divine patience and human rashness: *"Were God to hasten evil for mankind, even as they would hasten the good, their appointed term would have been concluded..."* (*Wa-law yuʿajjilu Allāhu li'l-nāsi al-sharra istiʿjālahum bi'l-khayri la-quḍiya ilayhim ajaluhum*).

Classical commentators, including Mujāhid, Qatādah, al-Ṭabarī, and Ibn Kathīr, highlight two primary contextual dimensions of this verse:
1. **The Defiant Demands of the Disbelievers:** The pagan Makkans continually challenged the Prophet Muhammad ﷺ to hasten divine retribution upon them to prove his authenticity, declaring: *"O God, if this should be the truth from You, then rain down upon us stones from the sky or bring us a painful punishment"* (8:32). Had God answered their foolish challenges instantly, humanity would have been wiped out immediately without any opportunity for repentance.
2. **Impulsive Invocations in Moments of Anger:** Mujāhid and Ibn ʿAbbās noted that this verse also encompasses ordinary human behavior—when a person in a fit of rage curses themselves, their children, or their wealth. If God responded to human curses and rash invocations with the same immediacy with which He responds to prayers for good, countless lives would be destroyed in moments of emotional instability.

The Prophet Muhammad ﷺ explicitly warned against this reckless human tendency:

> *"Do not pray against yourselves, do not pray against your children, and do not pray against your wealth, lest you coincide with an hour in which God is asked for a grant and He answers you."* (Ṣaḥīḥ Muslim 3009, from Jābir ibn ʿAbdillāh)

**The Mechanics of Divine Respite (Imlāʾ and Mihlah)**

Why does God delay retribution? The verse explains the wisdom of divine respite (*al-imhlāʾ*). God's attribute of *al-Ḥalīm* (The Clement/Forbearing) allows time for hearts to soften, for stubborn skeptics to reconsider, and for future righteous generations to emerge from the loins of disbelievers. If divine judgment were instantaneous, history would end before moral growth could occur.

However, for those who persistently harden their hearts, this respite becomes a trial: *"But We leave those who anticipate not the meeting with Us to wander confused in their rebellion"* (*Fa-nadharu alladhīna lā yarjūna liqāʾanā fī ṭughyānihim yaʿmahūn*). The verb *yaʿmahūn* describes blind, stumbling disorientation in spiritual darkness. When individuals willfully reject truth, God leaves them to the natural psychological consequences of their arrogance—stumbling through life without moral clarity, mistaking temporal success for divine approval.

## Sūrah Yūnus 10:12

> **And when harm afflicts man, he calls upon Us, [lying] on his side, or sitting, or standing. Then, when We remove his affliction from him, he carries on as if he had not called upon Us concerning the harm that had befallen him. Thus is that which they used to do made to seem fair unto the prodigal.**

**Expanded Commentary**

**The Archetypal Portrait of Human Ingratitude and Crisis-Induced Devotion**

The verse offers one of the most penetrating psychological portraits in all of sacred literature: *"And when harm afflicts man, he calls upon Us, [lying] on his side, or sitting, or standing..."* (*Wa-idhā massa al-insāna al-ḍurru daʿānā li-janbihi aw qāʿidan aw qāʾiman*).

The verse captures human posture in all its physical vulnerability:
- *Li-janbihi* (lying on his side): The posture of severe illness, bedridden agony, or utter physical helplessness.
- *Qāʿidan* (sitting): The posture of physical weakness, distress, or anxious contemplation.
- *Qāʾiman* (standing): The posture of active pacing, pacing the floor in panic, or raising hands in desperate pleading.

In moments of existential crisis—a terminal diagnosis, a financial collapse, or a sudden accident—all human pretenses of self-sufficiency (*istighnāʾ*) evaporate. The atheist, the polytheist, and the secular materialist alike find their primordial disposition (*fiṭrah*) instinctively triggered, crying out in desperate, round-the-clock supplication to the Transcendent Creator.

**The Post-Crisis Amnesia: The Return to Heedlessness**

The tragedy of the unrefined human soul unfolds in the second half of the verse: *"Then, when We remove his affliction from him, he carries on as if he had not called upon Us concerning the harm that had befallen him"* (*Fa-lammā kashafnā ʿanhu ḍurrahu marra ka-an lam yadʿunā ilā ḍurrin massahu*).

The verb *marra* ("he passed on" or "he carried on") is masterfully descriptive. It conveys a swift, casual, dismissive walking away—as if the crisis never occurred, as if the desperate tears were never shed, as if medical luck, human skill, or chance alone resolved the predicament. The soul immediately forgets the covenant made in the hospital room, returning straight to old vices, worldly distractions, and arrogant independence.

**The Illusion of Self-Justification (*Zuyyina li'l-Musrifīn*)**

The verse concludes with the diagnostic explanation: *"Thus is that which they used to do made to seem fair unto the prodigal"* (*Kadhālika zuyyina li'l-musrifīna mā kānū yaʿmalūn*). 

The term *musrif* denotes one who commits *isrāf*—exceeding legitimate boundaries, squandering spiritual capital, and living extravagantly in defiance of divine wisdom. Their past sinful habits and rationalizations are embellished (*zuyyina*) in their minds by their own desires and the whispers of Satan, blinding them to their profound spiritual hypocrisy. 

The verse serves as an urgent mirror for every reader: do we remember God only as an emergency rescue service, or do we maintain gratitude, devotion, and humility during the tranquil days of health and prosperity? The Prophet Muhammad ﷺ said:

> *"Remember God in times of ease and prosperity, and He will remember you in times of adversity."* (Musnad Aḥmad 2804; Jāmiʿ al-ʿUlūm wa'l-Ḥikam)

## Sūrah Yūnus 10:13

> **We have indeed destroyed generations before you when they did wrong, and their messengers brought them clear proofs, but they would not believe. Thus do We recompense the guilty people.**

**Expanded Commentary**

**The Historical Iron Law of Civilizational Collapse**

The narrative shifts from individual psychology to civilizational history: *"We have indeed destroyed generations before you when they did wrong..."* (*Wa-laqad ahlaknā al-qurūna min qablikum lammā ẓalamū*).

The Qur'an does not view history as a random succession of disconnected occurrences, but as governed by immutable moral laws (*sunan ilāhiyyah*). The term *al-qurūn* (singular *qarn*) refers to great epochs, nations, and empires—such as the people of ʿĀd, Thamūd, the people of Lot, and Midian. 

These ancient superpowers were not destroyed due to material poverty, technological backwardness, or lack of military might; they possessed monumental architecture and vast wealth. Their destruction was the direct consequence of *ẓulm*—systemic injustice, oppression of the vulnerable, moral decadence, and arrogant rejection of ultimate truth.

**Clear Proofs (al-Bayyināt) and the Nullification of Excuses**

God clarifies: *"and their messengers brought them clear proofs, but they would not believe"* (*wa-jāʾathum rusuluhum bi'l-bayyināt wa-mā kānū li-yuʾminū*).

Divine punishment never falls in the absence of prophetic warning and intellectual clarity. God sends messengers equipped with *al-bayyināt*—undeniable miracles, coherent rational proofs, and elevated moral codes. The phrase *wa-mā kānū li-yuʾminū* indicates a deep, entrenched psychological obstinacy: they had become so constitutionally hostile to truth that no amount of rational or empirical evidence could move them. As God states elsewhere:

> *"And We never punish until We have sent a messenger."* (Qur'an 17:15)

**The Universal Warning: Recompensing the Criminals**

The verse concludes: *"Thus do We recompense the guilty people"* (*Kadhālika najzī al-qawma al-mujrimīn*). The word *kadhālika* ("thus") establishes a universal precedent. 

The destruction of past tyrannies was not an isolated historical anomaly; it is a permanent warning to the Quraysh, to contemporary superpowers, and to every future civilization. When a society institutionalizes oppression, ridicules divine guidance, and glorifies corruption (*ijrām*), it enters the exact same historical trajectory that led prior empires to absolute ruin.

## Sūrah Yūnus 10:14

> **Then We made you vicegerents upon the earth after them, that We might observe how you behave.**

**Expanded Commentary**

**The Stewardship of Succession (Khilāfah ʿalā al-Arḍ)**

Directly addressing the contemporary audience, God states: *"Then We made you vicegerents upon the earth after them..."* (*Thumma jaʿalnākum khalāʾifa fī al-arḍi min baʿdihim*).

The word *khalāʾif* (singular *khalīfah*) signifies successors, inheritors, and custodians. Every rising generation and ascendant civilization stands upon the ruins of those that preceded it. Earthly authority, wealth, political sovereignty, and technological mastery are not permanent possessions or signs of intrinsic racial or national superiority; they are a temporary trust (*amānah*) passed from one community to the next.

**Earthly Life as an Active Moral Examination**

The core purpose of human succession is immediately unveiled: *"that We might observe how you behave"* (*li-nanẓura kayfa taʿmalūn*). 

Power and prosperity are not divine rewards for leisure; they constitute a rigorous moral test. Will the new inheritors learn from the tragic downfall of past empires and establish justice, humility, and faith, or will they repeat the exact same errors of arrogance, greed, and oppression?

Classical exegetes highlight a deeply moving statement transmitted by Imam al-Ṭabarī from the venerable Companion and second Caliph, ʿUmar ibn al-Khaṭṭāb:

> *"Our Lord has spoken the absolute truth: He did not place us as successors upon the earth except that He might observe how we behave. Therefore, let your eyes see nothing from yourselves except righteous deeds, by day and by night, in secret and in public."*

**The Prophetic Warning on the Trials of Worldly Dominion**

The Prophet Muhammad ﷺ reiterated the warning of this verse in an authenticated tradition recorded in *Ṣaḥīḥ Muslim*:

> *"Verily, the world is sweet and lush, and verily God has placed you as successors therein to see how you behave. So beware of the world and beware of [the trials concerning] women; for the first trial of the Children of Israel was concerning women."* (Ṣaḥīḥ Muslim 2742, from Abū Saʿīd al-Khudrī)

For modern humanity, possessing unprecedented technological mastery and environmental dominance, verse 14 resonates with monumental urgency. Humanity is not the sovereign owner of the planet, free to exploit its resources and oppress its inhabitants with impunity; humanity is a steward (*khalīfah*) held accountable by the Supreme Creator for every policy, ecological footprint, and social injustice.

## Sūrah Yūnus 10:15

> **And when Our signs are recited unto them as clear proofs, those who anticipate not the meeting with Us say, “Bring a Quran other than this, or alter it.” Say, “It is not for me to alter it of my own accord. I follow only that which is revealed unto me. Truly I fear, should I disobey my Lord, the punishment of a tremendous day.”**

**Expanded Commentary**

**The Audacious Demand for a Tailored Revelation**

The verse captures the insolent demand of the Makkan aristocracy when confronted with the unyielding moral and theological truths of the Qur'an: *"those who anticipate not the meeting with Us say, 'Bring a Quran other than this, or alter it'"* (*Iʾti bi-qur'ānin ghayri hādhā aw baddilhu*).

Classical commentators, including Ibn ʿAbbās, al-Ṭabarī, and al-Baghawī, explain the historical context: the pagan elites were offended by the Qur'an's uncompromising condemnation of their ancestral idols (such as al-Lāt, al-ʿUzzā, and Manāt), its fierce denunciation of usury (*ribā*), infanticide, and social hierarchy, and its vivid warnings of physical resurrection and eternal reckoning. They approached the Prophet ﷺ with a political compromise: "Bring us a different text that does not attack our deities, or modify these verses to accommodate our customs and social interests."

This attitude represents a perennial human temptation: the desire to reshape divine truth to fit subjective cultural preferences, political ideologies, or personal desires. Rather than conforming human life to divine guidance, the ego demands that divine guidance be rewritten to validate human corruption.

**The Absolute Immutability and Transcendence of Divine Speech**

God instructs the Messenger ﷺ to deliver an uncompromising theological response: *"Say, 'It is not for me to alter it of my own accord. I follow only that which is revealed unto me'"* (*Qul mā yakūnu lī an ubaddilahu min tilqāʾi nafsī in attabiʿu illā mā yūḥā ilayya*).

This statement is foundational to the Islamic theology of revelation (*ʿAqīdat al-Waḥy*):
1. **The Prophet as Custodian, Not Author:** The Prophet Muhammad ﷺ is not the author, editor, or philosophical designer of the Qur'an. He is the faithful messenger (*al-Rasūl al-Amīn*) entrusted with conveying the uncreated speech of God with absolute fidelity. He possesses zero authority to alter a single letter, omit a warning, or soften a command to please worldly powers.
2. **Strict Adherence to Revelation:** The Prophet's own life and teachings are entirely governed by obedience to revelation (*in attabiʿu illā mā yūḥā ilayya*).

**The Requisite Fear of Divine Accountability**

The verse concludes with a powerful expression of prophetic humility and reverence: *"Truly I fear, should I disobey my Lord, the punishment of a tremendous day"* (*Innī akhāfu in ʿaṣaytu Rabbī ʿadhāba yawmin ʿaẓīm*).

If the greatest of all creation, the beloved Messenger of God ﷺ, openly proclaims his reverent fear of divine retribution were he to alter a single aspect of revelation, what possible justification could any scholar, leader, or ordinary mortal have to distort, compromise, or modernize away the immutable moral boundaries established by God? The verse establishes that integrity in religion means submitting our desires to the text, never subordinating the sacred text to our desires.

---

## Sūrah Yūnus 10:16

> **Say, “Had God willed, I would not have recited it unto you; nor would He have made it known to you. Indeed, I tarried among you for a lifetime before it. Do you not understand?”**

**Expanded Commentary**

**The Historical Argument for Prophetic Authenticity: A Lifetime of Unblemished Integrity**

The verse provides one of the most compelling rational and historical proofs for the divine origin of the Qur'an: *"Say, 'Had God willed, I would not have recited it unto you; nor would He have made it known to you. Indeed, I tarried among you for a lifetime before it. Do you not understand?'"* (*Qad labithtu fīkum ʿumuran min qablihi a-fa-lā taʿqilūn*).

The Arabic word *ʿumur* refers to a substantial portion of a lifespan. The Prophet Muhammad ﷺ lived among the Quraysh for forty full years before receiving the first revelation in the cave of Ḥirāʾ. During those four decades, his life was an open book to every citizen of Makkah. He was known universally by the title *al-Ṣādiq al-Amīn* ("the Truthful, the Trustworthy"). He had never composed poetry, delivered grand rhetorical speeches, engaged in political conspiracies, or studied ancient scriptures. He was unlettered (*ummī*), unable to read or write.

The Qur'an presents an inescapable psychological argument: is it rational to believe that a man who never told a lie against a fellow human being for forty years would suddenly, at the age of forty, invent the most monumental lie against the Almighty Creator of the universe? As the Byzantine Emperor Heraclius famously reasoned when interrogating Abū Sufyān (who was then an arch-enemy of Islam) about the Prophet ﷺ:

> *"I asked you whether he was ever accused of lying before he said what he said, and you replied that he was not. So I understood that he who does not tell lies against people would never fabricate a lie against God."* (Ṣaḥīḥ al-Bukhārī 7; Ṣaḥīḥ Muslim 1773)

**The Total Absence of Human Premeditation**

The verse also emphasizes: *"Had God willed, I would not have recited it unto you..."* This underscores that the transmission of revelation was entirely dependent upon the Divine Will, not human initiative. 

Had the Prophet Muhammad ﷺ been the author, he could have produced verses on demand to solve personal crises, defend his beloved wife ʿĀʾishah immediately during the slander (*al-Ifk*), or answer thorny theological questions posed by the rabbis of Yathrib. Instead, there were agonizing periods where revelation paused (*fatrat al-waḥy*) for weeks, leaving the Prophet in deep anxiety. He could neither initiate nor hasten the descent of scripture of his own accord.

**The Appeal to Human Reason (A-fa-lā Taʿqilūn)**

The verse closes with the probing question: *"Do you not understand?"* (*a-fa-lā taʿqilūn*). 

True faith is not a suspension of intellect; it is the ultimate fulfillment of reason. The Qur'an asks the skeptics to look objectively at forty years of character, the linguistic miracle of the text, and the sheer impossibility of an unlettered shepherd producing the literary and legal masterpiece of human history. Sincere rational contemplation (*ʿaql*) inevitably leads to the affirmation of prophethood.

## Sūrah Yūnus 10:17

> **And who does greater wrong than one who fabricates a lie against God or denies His signs? Surely the guilty will not prosper.**

**Expanded Commentary**

**The Ultimate Peak of Injustice: Slandering the Divine**

The verse opens with a rhetorical question of absolute superlative negation (*istifhām inkārī*): *"And who does greater wrong than one who fabricates a lie against God or denies His signs?"* (*Fa-man aẓlamu mimmani iftarā ʿalā Allāhi kadhiban aw kadhdhaba bi-āyātihi*).

In Qur'anic ethics, *ẓulm* (injustice/wrongdoing) is defined as placing something outside its rightful place. Since God is the Supreme Source of existence, truth, and moral order, the greatest conceivable violation of reality is to commit spiritual fraud at the highest level. The verse identifies two reciprocal forms of cosmic injustice:
1. **Fabricating Lies Against God (*Iftarā ʿalā Allāhi kadhiban*):** Claiming that God has partners, children, intermediaries, or that He legislated falsehoods and permitted abominations. This includes claiming false prophethood or asserting that God spoke when He did not.
2. **Denying Divine Signs (*Kadhdhaba bi-āyātihi*):** Rejecting, mocking, or burying the authentic revelations, miracles, and cosmic proofs that God has made manifest.

**The Inescapable Failure of the Corrupters (Lā Yufliḥu al-Mujrimūn)**

God delivers the decisive verdict: *"Surely the guilty will not prosper"* (*Innahu lā yufliḥu al-mujrimūn*). 

The term *falāḥ* in Arabic denotes ultimate, comprehensive success—a farmer harvesting a bountiful crop after prolonged labor, spiritual salvation, peace of heart, and eternal elevation in the Hereafter. The criminals (*al-mujrimūn*) may achieve transient political power, financial wealth, or social dominance in this fleeting life, but their success is a mirage. In the ultimate calculation of existence, anyone whose foundation is built upon slander of the Divine and denial of truth will face absolute bankruptcy.

## Sūrah Yūnus 10:18

> **They worship apart from God that which neither harms them nor benefits them. And they say, “These are our intercessors with God.” Say, “Would you inform God about something in the heavens or on the earth that He does not know? Glory be to Him and exalted is He above the partners they ascribe!”**

**Expanded Commentary**

**The Essential Impotence of False Deities**

The verse exposes the profound intellectual and ontological bankruptcy of polytheism (*shirk*): *"They worship apart from God that which neither harms them nor benefits them..."* (*Wa-yaʿbudūna min dūni Allāhi mā lā yaḍurruhum wa-lā yanfaʿuhum*).

In classical theology, an object of ultimate devotion, prayer, and sacrifice must possess inherent power, autonomous consciousness, and the capacity to benefit or harm. Stone idols, celestial stars, departed saints, political rulers, or modern material idols possess zero autonomous metaphysical power. If an idol cannot protect itself from a fly or grant itself existence, how can it serve as the ultimate refuge for a human soul?

**The Illusion of Autonomous Intercession (Shufaʿāʾunā ʿind Allāh)**

The verse addresses the core theological rationalization offered by polytheists across cultures: *"And they say, 'These are our intercessors with God'"* (*Wa-yaqūlūna hāʾulāʾi shufaʿāʾunā ʿinda Allāh*).

The pagan Arabs rarely believed that their idols (Hubal, Lāt, ʿUzzā) created the universe from nothing; rather, they conceived of them as influential brokers, patrons, or celestial lobbyists who could leverage influence with the Supreme God. The Qur'an dismantles this concept of intercession. True intercession belongs exclusively to God, granted only to whom He wills, and cannot be forced by any created entity.

**The Sarcastic Refutation: Informing Omniscience**

God commands the Prophet ﷺ to deliver a devastating rational critique: *"Say, 'Would you inform God about something in the heavens or on the earth that He does not know?'"* (*Qul a-tunabbiʾūna Allāha bimā lā yaʿlamu fī al-samāwāti wa-lā fī al-arḍ*).

This is a profound logical reduction to absurdity (*reductio ad absurdum*). God is omniscient (*al-ʿAlīm*), encompassing every particle in the heavens and the earth. If God—Who knows all that exists—has no knowledge of these supposed "autonomous intercessors" or "co-deities," it means they simply do not exist in reality; they exist only as empty words and figments of human superstition.

**The Absolute Exaltation (al-Tanzīh)**

The verse culminates in an emphatic formula of transcendence: *"Glory be to Him and exalted is He above the partners they ascribe!"* (*Subḥānahu wa-taʿālā ʿammā yushrikūn*). 

The term *Subḥān* negates every conceivable flaw, limitation, dependence, and partnership from the Divine Essence, while *Taʿālā* affirms His infinite transcendence above all created concepts.

## Sūrah Yūnus 10:19

> **Mankind was but one community, then they differed. And if not for a Word that had preceded from thy Lord, judgment would have been made between them concerning that wherein they differed.**

**Expanded Commentary**

**The Primordial Unity of Humanity (Ummah Wāḥidah)**

The verse presents a profound philosophy of human religious history: *"Mankind was but one community, then they differed..."* (*Wa-mā kāna al-nāsu illā ummatan wāḥidatan fa'khtalafū*).

Secular evolutionary theories often posit that human religion began with crude animism and polytheism, gradually evolving over millennia toward monotheism. The Qur'an reveals the exact opposite: humanity originated in pure monotheism (*Tawḥīd*). Adam, the first human and first prophet, lived in direct relationship with the One Creator. 

Ibn ʿAbbās explained the historical timeline in an authentic report preserved by al-Ṭabarī and al-Ḥākim:

> *"Between Adam and Noah were ten generations, all of them living upon the true religion (al-sharīʿah min al-ḥaqq). Then they differed, so God sent the prophets as bearers of glad tidings and warners."*

Polytheism, sectarian fragmentation, and moral deviation were not the natural starting point of humanity; they were degenerative departures from the original primordial truth, introduced through spiritual corruption, ancestral veneration, and the promptings of Satan.

**The Preceding Divine Word (Kalimah Sabaqat): The Wisdom of Respite**

Why does God not intervene immediately to destroy those who plunge into falsehood and tyranny? The verse reveals: *"And if not for a Word that had preceded from thy Lord, judgment would have been made between them concerning that wherein they differed"* (*Wa-lawlā kalimatun sabaqat min Rabbika la-quḍiya baynahum fīmā fīhi yakhtalifūn*).

The "Preceding Word" refers to God's eternal decree that earthly life is an arena of moral trial (*ibtilāʾ*), freedom of choice, and deferred judgment until the Day of Resurrection. If every theological error and moral crime triggered instantaneous cosmic lightning, human free will would be invalidated, faith would become an involuntary physical reaction to terror, and the moral test of history would collapse. God allows historical drama to unfold so that human souls may freely choose virtue or vice, revealing their true character before the final judgment.

## Sūrah Yūnus 10:20

> **And they say, “Why has no sign been sent down unto him from his Lord?” Say, “Truly the Unseen belongs to God alone. So wait! Truly I am among those waiting along with you.”**

**Expanded Commentary**

**The Insincerity of Demanding Spectacular Miracles**

The verse addresses the persistent skepticism of the disbelievers: *"And they say, 'Why has no sign been sent down unto him from his Lord?'"* (*Wa-yaqūlūna lawlā unzila ʿalayhi āyatun min Rabbihi*).

The pagan Makkans continually demanded sensational, arbitrary miracles: that Mount Ṣafā be turned into solid gold, that angels visibly descend before their eyes, or that rivers gush forth in the desert on demand. The Qur'an exposes the underlying insincerity of these demands. The universe is already teeming with thousands of cosmic signs (*āyāt*), and the Qur'an itself was standing before them as a living linguistic and intellectual miracle. 

When a heart is closed by arrogance, no physical miracle can produce genuine faith. The Pharaoh witnessed nine monumental miracles and remained obstinate; the people of Thamūd received the miraculous She-Camel and slaughtered it. Miracles forced upon obstinate skeptics invariably lead to their immediate civilizational destruction when they continue to disbelieve.

**The Realm of the Unseen (al-Ghayb) Belongs to God Alone**

God commands the Messenger ﷺ to reply: *"Say, 'Truly the Unseen belongs to God alone...'"* (*Fa-qul innamā al-ghaybu lillāh*).

Prophethood is not a carnival act or a magical performance at the beck and call of human spectators. The timing, nature, and descent of cosmic signs belong exclusively to the sovereign knowledge and wisdom of God (*al-Ghayb*). A prophet is a mortal servant entrusted with a message, not an autonomous dispenser of cosmic wonders.

**The Posture of Patient Expectancy (Intiẓār)**

The verse concludes with a powerful proclamation: *"So wait! Truly I am among those waiting along with you"* (*Fa'ntaẓirū innī maʿakum min al-muntazirīn*).

This is a statement of sublime confidence. The Prophet ﷺ does not panic in the face of their mockery. He leaves the outcome to the Lord of history. They are waiting to see if his mission will fail; he is waiting for the inevitable fulfillment of God's promise—the vindication of truth, the opening of hearts, and the ultimate judgment of the Hereafter.

## Sūrah Yūnus 10:21

> **When We make men taste mercy after hardship has befallen them, behold, they have a plot regarding Our signs. Say, “God is swifter in plotting.” Surely Our messengers record whatsoever you plot.**

**Expanded Commentary**

**The Cynical Plotting Against Divine Grace**

The verse diagnoses a common pattern of human ingratitude: *"When We make men taste mercy after hardship has befallen them, behold, they have a plot regarding Our signs"* (*Wa-idhā adhaqnā al-nāsa raḥmatan min baʿdi ḍarrāʾa massathum idhā lahum makrun fī āyātinā*).

Classical commentators, including Mujāhid, Qatādah, and Ibn Kathīr, explain the historical context: when Makkah was gripped by severe famine and drought, the pagan Quraysh suffered immensely. When the Prophet Muhammad ﷺ prayed for them and God sent down life-giving rain and abundant harvest (*raḥmah*), rather than recognizing divine favor and submitting in gratitude, they immediately began scheming (*makr*). They attributed the relief to their ancestral idols, astrologers, or changing weather patterns, plotting new strategies to mock the revelation and suppress the believers.

This illustrates the perversity of an unrepentant ego: adversity produces temporary panic, but prosperity produces renewed cynicism and arrogance.

**The Swiftness of Divine Counter-Planning (Asraʿu Makran)**

God commands the Messenger ﷺ to deliver an immediate warning: *"Say, 'God is swifter in plotting'"* (*Qul Allāhu asraʿu makrā*).

In Arabic rhetoric, *makr* when applied to God is an attribute of justice and wisdom, meaning the precise, inescapable divine arrangement that outmaneuvers, exposes, and nullifies the conspiracies of wrongdoers without them perceiving it until it is too late. Human schemers operate with finite knowledge, vulnerable calculations, and limited power; God's divine planning encompasses all variables, past, present, and future. While the oppressors celebrate their apparent political schemes, God's decree is already tightening the net around them.

**The Incorruptible Record of the Angelic Scribes**

The verse concludes: *"Surely Our messengers record whatsoever you plot"* (*Inna rusulanā yaktubūna mā tamkurūn*).

The "messengers" here refer to the noble recording angels (*al-Karamah al-Kātibīn*) who accompany every human being, meticulously documenting every spoken word, hidden conspiracy, and malicious intention. Nothing is lost, omitted, or altered. On the Day of Resurrection, when the scrolls of deeds are unrolled, every schemer will be confronted with the undeniable, indelible transcript of their own actions:

> *"And the Book will be placed, and you will see the criminals fearful of that which is within it, and they will say, 'Oh, woe to us! What a book this is! It leaves not a small thing nor a great thing but has recorded it!'"* (Qur'an 18:49)

## Sūrah Yūnus 10:22

> **He it is Who carries you over land and sea, even when you are sailing in ships, till, when they sail with them upon a favorable wind, and rejoice therein, there comes upon them a violent gale, and the waves come at them from every side, and they think they shall be encompassed by them. They call upon God, devoting religion entirely to Him: “If Thou savest us from this, we shall surely be among the thankful!”**

**Expanded Commentary**

**The Universal Provider of Terrestrial and Maritime Transit**

The verse opens by reminding humanity of the divine gifts of mobility and transport: *"He it is Who carries you over land and sea..."* (*Huwa alladhī yusayyirukum fī al-barri wa'l-baḥr*). 

God created the earth with accessible plains and paths, domesticated riding beasts for land transport, and calibrated the density of water and the laws of buoyancy so that massive ships can glide across vast oceans. Human travel, global trade, cultural exchange, and migration are made possible solely because God designed the physical ecology to support human transit.

**The Dramatic Anatomy of a Maritime Storm**

The Qur'an then paints one of the most vivid, cinematographic scenes in world literature, depicting human vulnerability at sea:
1. **The Joyful Beginning:** *"even when you are sailing in ships, till, when they sail with them upon a favorable wind, and rejoice therein..."* The journey begins with calm waters, smooth breezes, music, laughter, and high spirits. Passengers feel secure, optimistic, and self-confident.
2. **The Sudden Onset of Chaos:** *"there comes upon them a violent gale (*rīḥun ʿāṣif*), and the waves come at them from every side (*jāʾahumu al-mawju min kulli makān*)..."* The weather changes violently. Howling winds tear the sails, the sky turns pitch black, and towering walls of water crash against the hull from every direction.
3. **The Realization of Inevitable Doom:** *"and they think they shall be encompassed by them (*wa-ẓannū annahum uḥīṭa bihim*)."* The nautical term *uḥīṭa bihim* signifies being completely encircled by destruction, with all physical escape routes severed. The captain's skill, the ship's reinforced timber, and human technology are rendered completely useless.

**The Primordial Cry: Pure Monotheism Triggered by Extremity**

In that ultimate moment of existential terror, human psychology undergoes an immediate, radical purification: *"They call upon God, devoting religion entirely to Him: 'If Thou savest us from this, we shall surely be among the thankful!'"* (*Daʿawū Allāha mukhliṣīna lahu al-dīna la-in anjaytanā min hādhihi la-nakūnanna mina al-shākirīn*).

Notice the psychological transformation: all secondary dependencies, false deities, ancestral idols, and secular arrogance vanish instantaneously. No sailor cries out to Hubal, Lāt, or celestial constellations when drowning in a stormy ocean. The deepest layer of the human soul—the primordial covenant of *Tawḥīd* (*Fiṭrat Allāh*)—shatters through layers of social conditioning, and the soul screams out to the One True Creator with absolute, undivided sincerity (*mukhliṣīn*). They pledge a solemn, binding vow: if God delivers them from this watery grave, their entire remaining lives will be dedicated to active gratitude, worship, and righteousness.

## Sūrah Yūnus 10:23

> **Then when He saves them, behold, they behave tyrannically upon the earth without right. O mankind! Your tyranny is only against yourselves—the enjoyment of the life of this world. Then unto Us shall be your return, whereupon We shall inform you of that which you used to do.**

**Expanded Commentary**

**The Betrayal of the Covenant: The Return to Tyranny**

The verse delivers the heartbreaking sequel to the maritime rescue: *"Then when He saves them, behold, they behave tyrannically upon the earth without right"* (*Fa-lammā anjāhum idhā hum yabghūna fī al-arḍi bi-ghayri al-ḥaqq*).

The particle *idhā* in Arabic syntax denotes suddenness (*al-fujāʾiyyah*). The moment their feet touch solid dry ground, the moment the sun comes out and physical safety is restored, their solemn vows evaporate. Instead of living lives of humble gratitude, they actively engage in *baghy*—tyranny, injustice, exploitation of the weak, and arrogant defiance of divine law. The phrase *bi-ghayri al-ḥaqq* ("without right") emphasizes that injustice has zero legitimate moral justification; it is pure transgression against reality.

**The Boomerang of Injustice: Tyranny Harms Only the Tyrant**

God issues a direct, universal proclamation to all humanity: *"O mankind! Your tyranny is only against yourselves"* (*Yā ayyuhā al-nāsu innamā baghyukum ʿalā anfusikum*).

This is an immutable spiritual and cosmic law: injustice (*al-baghy*) is inherently self-destructive. A tyrant may inflict physical pain, financial ruin, or temporal hardship upon others, but in the ontological ledger of reality, the victim suffers only temporary worldly inconvenience, while the tyrant destroys their own eternal soul. The moral poison a person unleashes into the world inevitably returns to consume them. 

The Prophet Muhammad ﷺ emphasized the rapid retribution that attends tyranny:

> *"There is no sin more deserving that God hasten the punishment for its perpetrator in this world, along with what is stored up for him in the Hereafter, than tyranny (al-baghy) and the severing of family ties."* (Sunan Abī Dāwūd 4902; Jāmiʿ al-Tirmidhī 2511, graded ṣaḥīḥ)

**The Ephemeral Mirage and the Ultimate Reckoning**

The verse places worldly oppression in its true perspective: *"the enjoyment of the life of this world. Then unto Us shall be your return..."* (*Matāʿa al-ḥayāti al-dunyā thumma ilaynā marjiʿukum*).

The term *matāʿ* signifies a fleeting convenience, a temporary provision utilized on a journey and soon discarded. The greatest wealth accumulated through corruption, the grandest palaces built on the tears of the oppressed, and the most intoxicating power last only for a fleeting heartbeat in cosmic time. Death will dismantle every illusion, bringing every soul before the Supreme Judge, Who will expose every concealed crime and render absolute, unyielding justice.

## Sūrah Yūnus 10:24

> **The parable of the life of this world is that of water which We send down from the sky: the earth’s vegetation, from which men and cattle eat, mingles with it till, when the earth takes on its luster and is adorned, and its inhabitants think they have gained mastery over it, Our Command comes upon it by night or by day, whereupon We make it a mown field, as if it had not flourished the day before! Thus do We expound the signs for a people who reflect.**

**Expanded Commentary**

**The Agricultural Parable of Earthly Life**

The verse presents one of the most celebrated and psychologically profound parables (*amthāl*) in the Qur'an: *"The parable of the life of this world is that of water which We send down from the sky..."* (*Innamā mathalu al-ḥayāti al-dunyā ka-māʾin anzalnāhu mina al-samāʾ*).

The parable unfolds across four vivid ecological and psychological phases:
1. **The Influx of Vitality:** Rain descends from the heavens, mingling with the soil, causing vibrant flora of diverse species to burst forth, providing abundant nourishment for humans and livestock.
2. **The Peak of Luster and Adornment (*Zukhruf and Izzayyanat*):** The earth reaches the absolute zenith of beauty. Golden fields of grain, blossoming orchards, and lush green meadows cover the landscape like an exquisite, embroidered bridal garment.
3. **The Illusion of Human Omnipotence (*Ẓanna ahluhā annahum qādirūna ʿalayhā*):** Looking upon their flourishing agricultural empire, the farmers and landowners succumb to the illusion of total mastery, control, and permanent ownership. They calculate their vast profits, assuming their harvest is guaranteed and completely within their power.
4. **The Instantaneous Catastrophe (*Amrunā laylan aw nahāran*):** Suddenly, without warning, the divine decree (*Amrunā*) strikes—a sudden overnight frost, a scorching desert wind, a flash flood, or a pestilence. In a single night or day, the entire lush landscape is transformed into a desolate, mown field (*ḥaṣīdan*), stripped bare as though it had never flourished or existed the day before (*ka-an lam taghna bi'l-ams*).

**The Transience of Human Civilizations and Individual Lives**

Classical commentators, including al-Ṭabarī, Ibn Kathīr, and al-Qurṭubī, explain that this parable operates at multiple levels of human experience:
- **The Lifecycle of the Individual:** Youth, physical beauty, athletic vigor, and career success flourish rapidly, reach an exhilarating peak where one feels invincible, and then swiftly wither through aging, sickness, and sudden death.
- **The Lifecycle of Civilizations:** Great empires and economic systems rise, build breathtaking technological wonders, believe they have conquered nature and mastered the globe, and are then abruptly dismantled by economic collapse, war, or natural catastrophe.

The phrase *ka-an lam taghna bi'l-ams* captures the tragic ephemeral nature of worldly delusion. When death arrives, all the decades of worldly pleasure, status, and vanity feel in retrospect like a single fleeting dream.

**The Call to Deep Contemplation (Li-Qawmin Yatafakkarūn)**

The verse concludes: *"Thus do We expound the signs for a people who reflect"* (*Kadhālika nufaṣṣilu al-āyāti li-qawmin yatafakkarūn*). 

The Qur'an does not forbid enjoying lawful worldly sustenance; rather, it forbids investing ultimate existential hope in a reality that is fundamentally fragile and transitory. The person of reflection (*tafakkur*) enjoys the blessings of the earth with humility, holding the world in their hands without allowing it to conquer their heart, recognizing that their true home lies beyond the horizon of this fleeting world.

## Sūrah Yūnus 10:25

> **And God calls unto the Abode of Peace, and guides whomsoever He will unto a straight path.**

**Expanded Commentary**

**The Divine Invitation to the Abode of Peace (Dār al-Salām)**

Immediately after exposing the fleeting, volatile, and fragile nature of worldly life in the preceding parable, God unveils the eternal, unshakeable alternative: *"And God calls unto the Abode of Peace..."* (*Wa-Allāhu yadʿū ilā Dāri al-Salām*).

Classical exegetes, including Ibn ʿAbbās, al-Ḥasan al-Baṣrī, al-Ṭabarī, and Ibn Kathīr, dwell on the magnificent name *Dār al-Salām* (The Abode of Peace/Safety):
1. **Peace from Every Flaw and Affliction:** Paradise is named *Dār al-Salām* because its inhabitants are permanently safe from death, aging, illness, grief, anxiety, fatigue, poverty, deception, conflict, and loss. Every torment and instability that characterizes earthly life is entirely absent in the Garden.
2. **The Abode of God Himself (*al-Salām*):** *Al-Salām* is one of the sublime Names of God, signifying the One Who is absolute perfection, free from every deficiency. Paradise is *Dār al-Salām* because it is the dwelling place of honor prepared by the King of Peace for His righteous servants.
3. **The Realm of Universal Greeting:** Every interaction in that eternal sanctuary is crowned with peace: the angels greet the believers with peace, believers greet one another with peace, and their Lord greets them with peace.

**The Universal Call vs. The Particular Guidance**

Notice the profound grammatical and theological precision of the verse:
- God's **call** (*yadʿū*) is universal: it is addressed to every human being, without exception. Revelation, prophetic warning, and moral invitations are broadcast to all mankind.
- God's **guidance** (*yahdī*) is granted to *"whomsoever He will unto a straight path"* (*man yashāʾu ilā ṣirāṭin mustaqīm*).

In Islamic theology (*ʿAqīdah*), this reflects the distinction between *Hidāyat al-Dalālah wa'l-Irshād* (the general guidance of revelation and intellectual direction given to everyone) and *Hidāyat al-Tawfīq wa'l-Ilhām* (the special divine grace and inner assistance granted to those who sincerely seek truth and humble their hearts). God invites all humanity to the Abode of Peace, but only those who choose to open their hearts to truth are facilitated along the Straight Path (*al-Ṣirāṭ al-Mustaqīm*) that leads directly into that eternal sanctuary.

The Prophet Muhammad ﷺ illustrated this divine invitation through a celebrated parable recorded in *Ṣaḥīḥ al-Bukhārī*:

> *"The angels came to the Prophet ﷺ while he was sleeping... and they struck a parable for him: The parable of him is like that of a man who built a house, prepared a feast therein, and sent an emissary to invite people. Whoever accepted the emissary entered the house and ate from the feast, and whoever rejected the emissary did not enter the house nor ate from the feast. The house is Paradise, the emissary is Muhammad ﷺ, and whoever obeys Muhammad obeys God, and whoever disobeys Muhammad disobeys God."* (Ṣaḥīḥ al-Bukhārī 2861, 7281)

## Sūrah Yūnus 10:26

> **Unto those who are virtuous shall be that which is most beautiful and more besides. Neither darkness nor abasement shall come over their faces. It is they who are the inhabitants of the Garden; they shall abide therein.**

**Expanded Commentary**

**The Ultimate Crown of Spiritual Excellence: Al-Ḥusnā and the Ziyādah**

The verse establishes the sublime reward awaiting the people of spiritual excellence (*al-Iḥsān*): *"Unto those who are virtuous shall be that which is most beautiful and more besides"* (*Li'lladhīna aḥsanū al-ḥusnā wa-ziyādah*).

The term *aḥsanū* encompasses all who practiced *Iḥsān*—defined by the Prophet Muhammad ﷺ in the famous Ḥadīth of Gabriel as:

> *"To worship God as though you see Him, and if you cannot see Him, then know that He sees you."* (Ṣaḥīḥ al-Bukhārī 50; Ṣaḥīḥ Muslim 8)

Classical exegetes, from the Companions onward, offer an authoritative, unanimous explanation of the two rewards promised:
1. **Al-Ḥusnā ("That Which is Most Beautiful"):** This refers to Paradise itself (*al-Jannah*), the ultimate abode of beauty, delights, palaces, and eternal youth.
2. **Al-Ziyādah ("And More Besides"):** This refers to the supreme, incomparable pinnacle of all celestial existence: gazing upon the Noble Countenance of God Almighty (*al-Naẓar ilā Wajh Allāh al-Karīm*).

**The Beatific Vision: The Zenith of Paradise in Authentic Sunnah**

Imam Muslim records in his *Ṣaḥīḥ* the definitive, authentic prophetic exegesis of this verse transmitted by Ṣuhayb al-Rūmī:

> *"When the people of Paradise enter Paradise, God, the Blessed and Exalted, will say: 'Do you desire anything more that I may grant you?' They will reply: 'Have You not brightened our faces? Have You not admitted us to Paradise and saved us from the Fire?' Then God will lift the Veil, and they will not have been granted anything more beloved to them than looking upon their Lord, the Mighty and Majestic."* Then the Messenger of God ﷺ recited this verse: *"Unto those who are virtuous shall be that which is most beautiful and more besides."* (Ṣaḥīḥ Muslim 181)

In Islamic theology (*ʿAqīdah*), the beatific vision (*Ruʾyat Allāh*) is an established article of faith for the Ahl al-Sunnah wa'l-Jamāʿah. Looking upon the Creator brings a state of overwhelming bliss, joy, and spiritual illumination that dwarfs all the physical delights of rivers, fruits, and gardens combined.

**Faces of Radiant Honor: Free from Darkness and Humiliation**

The verse continues: *"Neither darkness nor abasement shall come over their faces"* (*Wa-lā yarhaqu wujūhahum qatarun wa-lā dhillah*).

The Arabic term *qatar* refers to black dust, soot, gloomy shadows, and dark despair that visibly manifest on the faces of the terrified and guilty. The term *dhillah* signifies the humiliating degradation of defeat and disgrace. The righteous will stand on the Day of Resurrection with faces glowing with luminous serenity, joy, and honored dignity, completely free from the terror that grips the wicked.

The verse concludes by affirming their eternal status: *"It is they who are the inhabitants of the Garden; they shall abide therein"* (*Ulāʾika aṣḥābu al-jannati hum fīhā khālidūn*). Their bliss is not a temporary lease; it is an eternal, unshakeable inheritance that will never diminish, end, or be taken away.

## Sūrah Yūnus 10:27

> **And as for those who commit evil deeds, the recompense of an evil is one like it, and abasement shall overcome them. There will be none to protect them from God. [It will be] as if their faces are covered with dark patches of night. It is they who are the inhabitants of the Fire; they shall abide therein.**

**Expanded Commentary**

**The Strict Mathematical Precision of Divine Justice**

In stark, terrifying contrast to the infinite generosity granted to the righteous, the verse outlines the recompense of the wicked: *"And as for those who commit evil deeds, the recompense of an evil is one like it..."* (*Wa'lladhīna kasabū al-sayyiʾāti jazāʾu sayyiʾatin bi-mithlihā*).

Notice the immense moral and theological beauty of divine justice (*al-ʿAdl*):
- When rewarding good deeds, God multiplies the reward tenfold, seven hundredfold, and adds the boundless gift of the *Ziyādah* (gazing upon His Countenance).
- When punishing evil deeds, God never punishes beyond the exact equivalent of the crime committed: *"the recompense of an evil is one like it"* (*jazāʾu sayyiʾatin bi-mithlihā*). There is zero excess, zero tyranny, and zero injustice in the divine court.

**The Overwhelming Cloak of Abasement (Dhillah)**

The verse describes the spiritual and psychological condition of the sinners: *"and abasement shall overcome them. There will be none to protect them from God"* (*wa-tarhaquhum dhillatun mā lahum mina Allāhi min ʿāṣim*).

The verb *yarhaq* means to envelop, drown, or forcefully submerge. In this world, arrogant oppressors strutted with pride, protected by wealth, bodyguards, and social rank. On the Day of Judgment, every veneer of worldly power is stripped away, submerging them in inescapable, crushing humiliation (*dhillah*). The word *ʿāṣim* refers to a defender, protector, or fortress; no legal loophole, bribe, political ally, or idol can shield them from the just decree of the Almighty.

**Faces Enveloped in the Pitch-Black Night**

The Qur'an employs a haunting visual metaphor to depict their inner spiritual ruin: *"[It will be] as if their faces are covered with dark patches of night"* (*ka-annamā ughshiyat wujūhuhum qiṭaʿan mina al-layli muẓliman*).

The term *qiṭaʿ* is the plural of *qiṭʿah* (a fragment/patch). Their faces are not merely shadowed; they appear as though dense, layered slabs of midnight darkness have been physically draped over their countenances. This external darkness is the physical manifestation of their internal spiritual state: hearts that cultivated the darkness of disbelief, cruelty, and deceit in the world are unveiled in full visible reality upon the Day of Resurrection.

The verse seals their fate: *"It is they who are the inhabitants of the Fire; they shall abide therein"* (*ulāʾika aṣḥābu al-nāri hum fīhā khālidūn*). Having permanently rejected the light of God, they inherit the eternal confinement of the Fire.

## Sūrah Yūnus 10:28

> **On the Day when We shall gather them all together, then We shall say to those who ascribed partners unto God, “Stop there! You and your partners!” Then We shall separate them, and their partners will say, “It was not us whom you worshipped.**

**Expanded Commentary**

**The Cosmic Halt at the Grand Assembly (Makanakum)**

The verse transports the listener to the dramatic courtroom of the Day of Resurrection: *"On the Day when We shall gather them all together, then We shall say to those who ascribed partners unto God, 'Stop there! You and your partners!'"* (*Wa-yawma naḥshuruhum jamīʿan thumma naqūlu li'lladhīna ashrakū makānakum antum wa-shurakāʾukum*).

The phrase *makānakum* is an emphatic Arabic verbal noun of command meaning: "Freeze in your tracks! Stand immovable in your designated positions!" The entire human race, alongside all the false deities, idols, celestial bodies, and glorified mortals that were worshipped throughout history, are gathered in one vast cosmic arena.

In this world, polytheists and oppressors moved freely, escaping accountability through evasion, bribery, and military power. On the Day of Gathering (*Yawm al-Ḥashr*), the divine command freezes them in place for direct confrontation and cross-examination.

**The Absolute Severing of Spiritual Bonds (Fa-Zayyalnā Baynahum)**

The verse states: *"Then We shall separate them"* (*fa-zayyalnā baynahum*). The verb *zayyala* denotes completely severing, fracturing, and dismantling every connection, alliance, and mutual affection that existed between them. 

In this world, cults, pagan tribes, and ideological factions bonded over shared rituals, mutual praise, and collective opposition to prophetic guidance. On the Day of Judgment, that superficial solidarity is shattered instantly into bitter enmity, finger-pointing, and mutual repudiation.

**The Devastating Disavowal by the False Deities**

The climax of the verse occurs when the false partners speak: *"and their partners will say, 'It was not us whom you worshipped'"* (*wa-qāla shurakāʾuhum mā kuntum iyyānā taʿbudūn*).

Classical commentators, including al-Ṭabarī, Ibn Kathīr, and al-Qurṭubī, explain who these "partners" are:
- If they were inanimate idols of stone, wood, or gold, God will grant them miraculous speech on that Day to testify against their worshippers.
- If they were righteous servants of God (such as Jesus, Ezra, or angels) who were falsely elevated to divine status by misguided humans, they will passionately repudiate the idolatrous worship directed toward them, declaring their complete innocence.
- If they were devils (*shayāṭīn*) who deceived humans into polytheism, they will turn on their followers with biting contempt, pointing out that humans were merely worshipping their own lusts, superstitions, and demonic delusions.

The worshippers are left spiritually abandoned, betrayed by the very entities for whom they sacrificed their eternal souls.

## Sūrah Yūnus 10:29

> **God suffices as a Witness between you and us. We were heedless of your worship.”**

**Expanded Commentary**

**The Ultimate Legal Appeal to the Omniscient Witness**

Continuing the dramatic testimony initiated in the previous verse, the false deities and intermediaries conclude their defense by appealing to the Supreme Judge: *"God suffices as a Witness between you and us"* (*Fa-kafā bi'llāhi shahīdan baynanā wa-baynakum*).

In Islamic jurisprudence and theology, when disputes arise between parties, the ultimate resolution rests upon decisive testimony. The false objects of worship declare that they need no defense attorneys, worldly witnesses, or circumstantial evidence: God Almighty Himself—Who encompasses every hidden thought, every secret ritual, and every historical event—is sufficient as the omniscient, incorruptible Witness (*al-Shahīd*). He knows with absolute certainty that these false gods never commanded worship, never consented to polytheism, and never possessed the power to benefit their devotees.

**The Total Heedlessness of the False Objects of Devotion (Ghaflah)**

The false deities state their absolute detachment: *"We were heedless of your worship"* (*in kunnā ʿan ʿibādatikum la-ghāfilīn*).

Classical exegetes analyze the literal and theological dimensions of this statement:
1. **Inanimate Objects:** Stone idols, carved wood, statues, and celestial stars were completely inanimate, devoid of consciousness, hearing, or perception. They had zero awareness of the sacrifices slaughtered at their feet, the incantations chanted before them, or the prostrations offered to them.
2. **Righteous Prophets and Angels:** The angels, Prophet Jesus, and pious saints were completely unaware of the polytheistic cults formed in their names in distant lands or after their earthly departures. They were occupied entirely with their own worship and obedience to God Almighty.
3. **The Psychological Shock to the Polytheist:** The polytheist who spent a lifetime sacrificing wealth, time, and moral integrity for an intermediary is confronted with the horrifying realization that their devotion fell upon absolute deafness, indifference, and unawareness.

**The Futility of Secondary Dependencies**

Verse 29 serves as a timeless theological warning against all forms of shirk—both ancient and modern. When a human being devotes ultimate love, fear, trust, and obedience to anything other than the Living, Eternal Creator—whether an ancient idol, a charismatic guru, a political party, or material wealth—that false master will inevitably abandon, disown, and repudiate them at the moment of ultimate need. Only the Creator is the Ever-Living (*al-Ḥayy*), the All-Hearing (*al-Samīʿ*), Who never forgets a sincere prayer and never abandons those who place their trust in Him.

## Sūrah Yūnus 10:30

> **There and then every soul shall experience that which it did in the past, and they shall be brought back unto God, their true Master, and that which they used to fabricate will forsake them.**

**Expanded Commentary**

**The Direct Encounter with Past Deeds (Tablū Kullu Nafs)**

The verse captures the ultimate moment of existential reckoning: *"There and then every soul shall experience that which it did in the past..."* (*Hunālika tablū kullu nafsin mā aslafat*).

The verb *tablū* in Arabic carries deep layered meanings:
- To taste, experience, and directly confront the full reality and consequences of past actions.
- To thoroughly examine, audit, and inspect the ledger of one's life.
- To be tested and exposed by the revelation of every secret deed, hidden motive, and forgotten transgression.

The phrase *mā aslafat* refers to everything the soul "sent forward" during its brief earthly tenure. In this world, humans easily forget their sins, dismiss their moral failures as insignificant, and bury their compromises. On that Day, the temporal veil is dissolved: every word uttered, every dollar earned or spent, every act of cruelty, and every secret tear of sincere repentance is resurrected in living, tangible reality before the soul's eyes.

**The Return to the True Sovereign (Mawlāhum al-Ḥaqq)**

The verse declares: *"and they shall be brought back unto God, their true Master"* (*wa-ruddū ilā Allāhi Mawlāhumu al-ḥaqq*).

The term *Mawlā* encompasses the ultimate Master, Protector, Sovereign, Owner, and Guardian. In this world, human beings pledged allegiance to false *mawlās*—tyrants, ancestral customs, corrupt corporations, and demonic illusions. On the Day of Resurrection, all artificial claims of sovereignty collapse, and every creature is forcibly returned to the One True Master (*al-Ḥaqq*). His judgment is absolute, unyielding, and pure truth; no appeal can be made to any higher court, because none exists.

**The Total Dissolution of Fabrications (Ḍalla ʿanhum mā kānū yaftarūn)**

The verse concludes with a powerful psychological and ontological finale: *"and that which they used to fabricate will forsake them"* (*wa-ḍalla ʿanhum mā kānū yaftarūn*).

The verb *ḍalla* signifies wandering away, vanishing into thin air, and leaving the person in absolute, desolate abandonment. All the fabricated intercessors, mythical pantheons, theological rationalizations, and secular illusions that people constructed to shield themselves from divine truth will evaporate like smoke. 

The human soul stands entirely alone, stripped of every worldly defense, confronted with the naked reality of its own deeds before the Supreme Creator. The verse invites the reader to experience this spiritual awakening today, before that final Day arrives when realization comes too late.

## Sūrah Yūnus 10:31

> **Say, “Who provides for you from Heaven and earth? Or who possesses hearing and sight? And who brings forth the living from the dead and brings forth the dead from the living? And who directs the affair?” They will say, “God.” Say, “Will you not then be reverent?”**

**Expanded Commentary**

**The Fivefold Interrogation of Polytheistic Inconsistency**

The verse initiates a powerful dialectical interrogation directed at the pagan Makkans and all who associate partners with God: *"Say, 'Who provides for you from Heaven and earth? Or who possesses hearing and sight? And who brings forth the living from the dead and brings forth the dead from the living? And who directs the affair?'"* (*Qul man yarzuqukum mina al-samāʾi wa'l-arḍi amman yamliku al-samʿa wa'l-abṣāra wa-man yukhriju al-ḥayya mina al-mayyiti wa-yukhriju al-mayyita mina al-ḥayyi wa-man yudabbiru al-amr*).

The Qur'an constructs an undeniable fivefold argument based on observable reality:
1. **Cosmic and Terrestrial Sustenance (*Rizq*):** Who sends down rain from the clouds and causes grain, fruits, and livestock to emerge from the earth to feed humanity?
2. **The Gift of Primary Senses (*al-Samʿ wa'l-Abṣār*):** Who designed, engineered, and bestowed the intricate biological faculties of hearing and sight, without which human consciousness would be locked in darkness?
3. **Biological Life from Non-Life (*Yukhriju al-ḥayya mina al-mayyit*):** Who brings living organisms from inanimate soil, water, and minerals, and causes a living chick to emerge from a lifeless egg?
4. **The Cycle of Mortality (*Yukhriju al-mayyita mina al-ḥayy*):** Who extracts lifeless seeds from living plants, and lifeless bone and dust from living organisms?
5. **Universal Cosmic Governance (*Yudabbiru al-amr*):** Who sustains the orbits of stars, regulates the seasons, and governs the destiny of nations?

**The Inescapable Polytheistic Concession: "Allāh!"**

The verse records their immediate, unavoidable answer: *"They will say, 'God'"* (*Fa-sayaqūlūna Allāh*).

Classical commentators, led by Ibn ʿAbbās, al-Ṭabarī, and Ibn Kathīr, emphasize that the pagan Arabs never attributed cosmic creation, rain, sensory biology, or universal governance to their stone idols (Hubal, Lāt, or ʿUzzā). They acknowledged that God alone is the Supreme Creator and Sustainer (*Tawḥīd al-Rubūbiyyah*). 

This exposes the fundamental cognitive dissonance and moral hypocrisy of polytheism: if you openly admit that God alone provides all your sustenance, gave you your eyes and ears, controls life and death, and directs the cosmos, how can you direct your prayers, sacrifices, and ultimate devotions to powerless idols (*Tawḥīd al-Ulūhiyyah*)?

**The Requisite Conclusion: The Call to Reverence (A-fa-lā Tattaqūn)**

The verse closes with the devastating rhetorical challenge: *"Say, 'Will you not then be reverent?'"* (*Fa-qul a-fa-lā tattaqūn*).

If the premises of divine Lordship are conceded by your own intellect, why do you not fear the catastrophic spiritual consequences of associating partners with Him? True rationality demands consistency: acknowledging the One Creator in the intellect must translate into worshipping Him alone in practice.

## Sūrah Yūnus 10:32

> **That is God, your true Lord. What is there beyond the truth except error? How then are you turned away?**

**Expanded Commentary**

**The Ultimate Ontological Identity: Allāh, Your True Lord**

Building upon the admissions extracted in the previous verse, God declares: *"That is God, your true Lord"* (*Fa-dhālikumu Allāhu Rabbukumu al-ḥaqq*).

The demonstrative pronoun *dhālikum* ("That One") points to the Supreme Being Who possesses all the majestic attributes just conceded—the sole Provider, Creator of the senses, Master of life and death, and Governor of the universe. He is your *Rabb al-Ḥaqq*—your True Lord, Whose existence is undeniable, Whose sovereignty is absolute, and Whose right to exclusive worship is ontological truth.

**The Law of Excluded Middle in Sacred Epistemology: Beyond Truth Lies Only Error**

The verse articulates one of the most foundational epistemological principles in Islamic thought: *"What is there beyond the truth except error?"* (*Fa-mādhā baʿda al-ḥaqqi illā al-ḍalāl*).

In classical logic and theology, truth (*al-ḥaqq*) and falsehood/error (*al-ḍalāl*) are contradictory opposites (*naqīḍān*); they cannot both be true simultaneously, nor can reality be empty of both. 
- If monotheism (*Tawḥīd*)—the worship of the One True Creator—is established as absolute truth by reason and revelation, then polytheism (*shirk*), atheism, and idol worship are self-evidently falsehood and delusion.
- There is no neutral, valid third category when it comes to ultimate reality. Any departure from truth is, by definition, a plunge into misguidance.

Imam al-Qurṭubī notes in his exegesis:

> *"This verse establishes that there are only two paths: the path of truth and the path of misguidance. Whoever turns away from the truth inevitably falls into misguidance."*

**The Bafflement of Spiritual Perversion: How Are You Turned Away?**

The verse concludes with an expression of profound astonishment: *"How then are you turned away?"* (*Fa-annā tuṣrafūn*).

The passive verb *tuṣrafūn* ("how are you diverted/turned away?") highlights the irrationality of the polytheistic mindset. When clear rational proof is established before a person's eyes, what mysterious delusion, cultural peer pressure, or psychological arrogance causes them to turn their backs on the radiant light of truth and wander into the darkness of superstition?

## Sūrah Yūnus 10:33

> **Thus the Word of thy Lord came due for those who are defiantly disobedient: that they will not believe.**

**Expanded Commentary**

**The Immutable Divine Decree and Persistent Defiance (Fisq)**

The verse reveals the spiritual mechanics behind persistent disbelief: *"Thus the Word of thy Lord came due for those who are defiantly disobedient: that they will not believe"* (*Kadhālika ḥaqqat kalimatu Rabbika ʿalā alladhīna fasaqū annahum lā yuʾminūn*).

The Arabic root *f-s-q* literally denotes a date slipping out of its protective skin, or a creature breaking out of its natural, healthy boundaries. In Qur'anic terminology, *al-fāsiqūn* are those who intentionally, defiantly, and persistently violate the boundaries of divine truth and moral law.

**The Meaning of the 'Word Coming Due' (Ḥaqqat Kalimah)**

Classical theologians, including al-Ṭabarī, Fakhr al-Dīn al-Rāzī, and Ibn Kathīr, explain the doctrine of divine pre-measurement (*al-Qadar*) in relation to human responsibility:
- God does not arbitrarily compel a soul into disbelief against its will.
- Rather, when an individual repeatedly encounters clear proofs, recognizes the truth in their intellect, and yet stubbornly chooses arrogance, mockery, and corruption (*fisq*), God seals their spiritual state as a just consequence of their own persistent choices.
- The "Word of thy Lord" (*kalimatu Rabbik*) refers to God's eternal, infallible foreknowledge and just decree that those who willfully bury their spiritual faculties will reach a point of irreversible moral calcification, after which they will never believe.

As God declares elsewhere in the Qur'an:

> *"So when they deviated, God caused their hearts to deviate. And God does not guide the defiantly disobedient people."* (Qur'an 61:5)

Spiritual blindness is not an arbitrary curse; it is the natural consequence of persistent moral rebellion against light.

## Sūrah Yūnus 10:34

> **Say, “Is there, among your partners, one who originates creation, then brings it back?” Say, “God originates creation, then He brings it back. How then are you deluded?”**

**Expanded Commentary**

**The Ultimate Challenge: Creation and Resurrection**

The Qur'an continues its relentless theological cross-examination: *"Say, 'Is there, among your partners, one who originates creation, then brings it back?'"* (*Qul hal min shurakāʾikum man yabdaʾu al-khalqa thumma yuʿīduhu*).

The verse challenges the polytheists to evaluate their false deities against the two monumental pillars of cosmic reality:
1. **The Origin (*al-Mabdaʾ*):** Initiating the universe, designing biological life, and bringing galaxies and atoms into existence out of absolute nothingness (*ex nihilo*).
2. **The Return (*al-Maʿād*):** Reconstituting decayed bones, resurrected consciousness, and bringing every soul back to life for eternal accountability.

**The Crushing Silence and the Solemn Proclamation**

Faced with this question, the polytheists have no answer. No rational human being—even among the pagan Quraysh—could claim that a carved statue, an angel, a celestial body, or a mortal king created the universe or possesses the power to resurrect the dead.

God commands the Messenger ﷺ to proclaim the undeniable truth: *"Say, 'God originates creation, then He brings it back'"* (*Qul Allāhu yabdaʾu al-khalqa thumma yuʿīduhu*). The initiation of life and its physical resurrection are the exclusive, sovereign domain of the Almighty Creator.

**The Baffling Delusion: How Are You Turned? (Fa-annā Tu'fakūn)**

The verse closes with the probing question: *"How then are you deluded?"* (*Fa-annā tuʾfakūn*).

The verb *yuʾfak* signifies being violently turned upside down, deceived, or inverted from reality. If you know that God alone created you from nothing and that God alone will resurrect you for judgment, how can your mind be so inverted that you bow down before inanimate stone, worship dead mortals, or live as if the Resurrection will never occur? It is a plea for intellectual integrity and spiritual awakening.

## Sūrah Yūnus 10:35

> **Say, “Is there any among your partners who guides unto the truth?” Say, “God guides unto the truth. Is He then Who guides unto the truth more worthy to be followed, or he who guides not unless he is guided? What is wrong with you? How do you judge?”**

**Expanded Commentary**

**The Epistemological Challenge: Who Guides to Truth?**

The verse shifts from cosmological creation to epistemological and moral guidance: *"Say, 'Is there any among your partners who guides unto the truth?' Say, 'God guides unto the truth'"* (*Qul hal min shurakāʾikum man yahdī ilā al-ḥaqqi qul Allāhu yahdī li'l-ḥaqq*).

Creating physical matter is one aspect of divinity; providing moral, legal, and spiritual guidance (*al-Hidāyah*) to human consciousness is another equally essential aspect. Guidance involves revealing the meaning of existence, establishing justice, illuminating the path of virtue, and rescuing the human soul from existential despair. 

Can any idol, astrological constellation, secular ideology, or self-appointed tyrant provide infallible, universal guidance? Never. God alone sends revelations, commissions prophets, and illuminates human hearts with truth.

**The Rational Criterion: The Autonomous Guide vs. The Helpless Follower**

The Qur'an poses an inescapable logical comparison: *"Is He then Who guides unto the truth more worthy to be followed, or he who guides not unless he is guided?"* (*A-fa-man yahdī ilā al-ḥaqqi aḥaqqu an yuttabaʿa amman lā yahiddī illā an yuhdā*).

Classical scholars highlight the profound linguistic nuance of *lā yahiddī* (originally *yah-tadī*):
- An idol cannot even move from its pedestal unless human beings physically carry it. If knocked over, it cannot right itself.
- A created mortal (even a king or philosopher) is born ignorant, requires guidance, education, and instruction, makes frequent errors, and remains vulnerable to personal biases and passions.
- God Almighty is the Self-Sufficient (*al-Ghanī*), the Source of all wisdom, Who guides the entire cosmos without needing guidance from anyone.

Logic dictates that the Sovereign, Infallible Guide is infinitely more entitled to absolute obedience, love, and emulation than created entities that are themselves helpless, dependent, and flawed.

**The Intellectual Indictment: How Do You Judge? (Mā Lakum Kayfa Taḥkumūn)**

The verse concludes with a stinging reprimand: *"What is wrong with you? How do you judge?"* (*Mā lakum kayfa taḥkumūn*).

What kind of corrupted, distorted reasoning allows an intelligent human being to subordinate their intellect to superstition, preferring the worship of helpless creatures over submission to the Supreme Creator and Guide of the cosmos?

## Sūrah Yūnus 10:36

> **And most of them follow naught but conjecture. Truly conjecture avails nothing against the truth. Surely God is Knower of whatsoever they do.**

**Expanded Commentary**

**The Epistemology of Delusion: The Rule of Conjecture (Al-Ẓann)**

The verse delivers a fundamental critique of the epistemic basis of polytheism, skepticism, and moral relativism: *"And most of them follow naught but conjecture..."* (*Wa-mā yattabiʿu aktharuhum illā ẓannā*).

In Arabic epistemology and Islamic jurisprudence, *al-ẓann* signifies unverified assumption, speculation, subjective intuition, wishful thinking, and blind adherence to ancestral dogma without empirical or rational proof. 

Classical commentators, including al-Ṭabarī, Ibn Kathīr, and Fakhr al-Dīn al-Rāzī, explain that the pagan Makkans possessed zero revelatory scripture, zero rational proof, and zero empirical evidence to support their claim that idols possess intercessory powers or that God has daughters. Their entire religious paradigm was built upon nothing more than unexamined cultural traditions (*taqlīd al-ābāʾ*) and psychological conjecture.

**The Inadequacy of Speculation: Conjecture Avails Nothing Against Truth**

The verse articulates a timeless philosophical axiom: *"Truly conjecture avails nothing against the truth"* (*Inna al-ẓanna lā yughnī mina al-ḥaqqi shayʾā*).

Truth (*al-ḥaqq*) is an objective, unyielding ontological reality that exists independently of human opinions, cultural fashions, or emotional preferences. 
- A thousand people passionately believing a false conjecture does not transform it into truth.
- A million people doubting an undeniable reality does not diminish its truth.
- In matters of ultimate significance—such as the existence of the Creator, the authenticity of revelation, and the reality of the Hereafter—human beings cannot afford to gamble their eternal destiny on mere speculation. Ultimate destiny demands *al-Yaqīn* (unshakeable certainty grounded in decisive proof).

**The Omniscient Witness (Inna Allāha ʿAlīmun bimā Yafʿalūn)**

The verse concludes: *"Surely God is Knower of whatsoever they do"* (*Inna Allāha ʿalīmun bimā yafʿalūn*).

God is fully aware of every hidden motive, every intellectual compromise, and every act of willful self-deception. Those who knowingly trade the clear light of truth for comfortable cultural delusions will face a comprehensive reckoning before the Omniscient Judge.

## Sūrah Yūnus 10:37

> **This Quran could not have been fabricated [by anyone] apart from God; but it is a confirmation of that which was before it and a detailed explanation of the Book, wherein there is no doubt, from the Lord of the worlds.**

**Expanded Commentary**

**The Inherent Impossibility of Human Fabrication (Iʿjāz al-Qurʾān)**

The verse proclaims the absolute divine origin of the Qur'an: *"This Quran could not have been fabricated [by anyone] apart from God..."* (*Wa-mā kāna hādhā al-Qurʾānu an yuftarā min dūni Allāh*).

The grammatical construction *wa-mā kāna... an* expresses structural impossibility (*al-imtināʿ*). The Qur'an possesses an inherent literary, theological, legislative, and prophetic majesty that makes human authorship an ontological impossibility. 
- An unlettered man in 7th-century Arabia could not have authored a text containing such profound legal frameworks, flawless theological coherence, historical narratives of ancient vanished nations, and breathtaking linguistic architecture that remains the gold standard of the Arabic language across fourteen centuries.
- As classical scholars like al-Bāqillānī and al-Khaṭṭābī demonstrated in their treatises on *Iʿjāz*, the Qur'an contains no internal contradictions, emotional volatility, or factual absurdities characteristic of human compositions.

**Confirmation of Past Revelations (Taṣdīq alladhī Bayna Yadayh)**

The verse outlines the harmonious continuity of divine revelation: *"but it is a confirmation of that which was before it..."* (*wa-lākin taṣdīqa alladhī bayna yadayh*).

The Qur'an does not present itself as an isolated, rival theological invention; it is the culmination, verification, and guardian (*muhaymin*) of the original, uncorrupted core of all previous scriptures—the Torah revealed to Moses, the Psalms revealed to David, and the Gospel revealed to Jesus. It confirms their original prophetic message of pure monotheism (*Tawḥīd*) while purifying religious practice from centuries of human alteration (*taḥrīf*).

**Detailed Explanation and Absolute Authority (Tafṣīl al-Kitāb)**

The verse continues: *"and a detailed explanation of the Book, wherein there is no doubt, from the Lord of the worlds"* (*wa-tafṣīla al-Kitābi lā rayba fīhi min Rabbi al-ʿālamīn*).

The Qur'an provides *Tafṣīl*—an elaborate, clear, and comprehensive exposition of creed, moral virtues, legal rulings, and spiritual wisdom necessary for human flourishing. It is stamped with the seal of divine certainty: *lā rayba fīh* ("wherein there is no doubt"). It descends directly from *Rabb al-ʿĀlamīn*—the Sovereign Lord, Sustainer, and Educator of all realms of existence.

## Sūrah Yūnus 10:38

> **Or do they say, “He has fabricated it”? Say, “Then bring a chapter like it, and call upon whom you can, apart from God, if you are truthful!”**

**Expanded Commentary**

**The Historic Challenge (al-Taḥaddī)**

The verse records the skeptics' persistent accusation and counters it with the supreme, open challenge: *"Or do they say, 'He has fabricated it'? Say, 'Then bring a chapter like it, and call upon whom you can, apart from God, if you are truthful!'"* (*Am yaqūlūna iftarāhu qul faʾtū bi-sūratin mithlihi wa'dʿū mani istaṭaʿtum min dūni Allāhi in kuntum ṣādiqīn*).

This is the famous Qur'anic challenge (*al-Taḥaddī*) that stands as an open gauntlet across human history. The Qur'an issued this challenge in progressive stages:
1. Challenge to produce an entire book like the Qur'an (17:88, 52:34).
2. Challenge to produce ten fabricated chapters like it (11:13).
3. Challenge to produce **a single chapter** (*bi-sūratin mithlih*) like it (10:38, 2:23).

The shortest chapter in the Qur'an—Sūrah al-Kawthar—consists of merely three short lines (ten words). The challenge is radical, clear, and totally accessible: if you genuinely believe that a mortal man composed this text, assemble all your greatest poets, rhetoricians, scholars, and supernatural entities (*wa'dʿū mani istaṭaʿtum min dūni Allāh*), and produce a single chapter matching its linguistic eloquence, structural beauty, theological profundity, and moral elevation.

**The Historical Surrender of the Masters of Eloquence**

The pagan Arabs were undisputed masters of Arabic poetry, holding annual competitions at the fair of ʿUkāẓ where poetic odes (*al-Muʿallaqāt*) were suspended upon the walls of the Kaʿbah. They had the strongest possible motivation to defeat the Prophet Muhammad ﷺ, whose message was dismantling their religious traditions, social hierarchies, and economic monopolies.

Had they been capable of producing a single comparable chapter, they would have done so immediately to end his mission, avoiding decades of exhausting warfare, loss of life, and political humiliation. Instead, their greatest masters of rhetoric—such as al-Walīd ibn al-Mughīrah—were reduced to stunned silence, conceding:

> *"By God, I have just heard words from Muhammad which are unlike the speech of man and unlike the speech of jinn. ## Sūrah Yūnus 10:39

The verse provides a profound psychological diagnosis of human skepticism: *"Nay, but they deny that whose knowledge they cannot encompass and whose interpretation has not yet come to them..."* (*Bal kadhdhabū bimā lam yuḥīṭū bi-ʿilmihi wa-lammā yaʾtihim taʾwīluh*).

Human beings frequently suffer from a dangerous intellectual flaw: the instinctive tendency to reject, ridicule, or deny anything that exceeds their current knowledge, cultural conditioning, or immediate sensory experience.

**The Meaning of 'Taʾwīl' in Prophetic Eschatology**

The phrase *wa-lammā yaʾtihim taʾwīluh* ("and whose interpretation has not yet come to them") carries deep eschatological significance. In classical Arabic and Qur'anic usage, the *taʾwīl* of a prophecy or warning is its tangible, physical fulfillment and manifestation in reality.

They deny the Day of Resurrection, the Weighing of Deeds (*al-Mīzān*), and the Hellfire today; but the day will inevitably arrive when the veil of the physical world is torn away, and the *taʾwīl*—the actual reality of those warnings—will unfold before their terrified eyes. **The Historical Continuity of Denial and Its Inevitable End**

The verse warns of historical precedents: *"Thus denied those before them. See then what was the end of the wrongdoers!"* (*Kadhālika kadhdhaba alladhīna min qablihim fa'nẓur kayfa kāna ʿāqibatu al-ẓālimīn*).

The people of Noah, ʿĀd, Thamūd, and Pharaoh exhibited the exact same cognitive arrogance—denying divine warnings simply because they had not yet seen the physical catastrophe with their own eyes. The ruins of their once-mighty civilizations stand as silent, undeniable monuments to the catastrophic cost of intellectual arrogance.

The verse poses a rhetorical question highlighting the limits of human agency: *"But canst thou make the deaf to hear, even though they use not their reason?"* 

A person whose physical ears are damaged can still be reached through sign language or visual cues; but a person who is spiritually deaf—whose internal cognitive faculties (*al-ʿAql*) are locked by arrogance, pride, and vested interests—is impossible to guide through external words alone. 

The Prophet ﷺ is reminded that even the most eloquent, beautiful, and divine speech cannot force an arrogant heart to understand if that heart refuses to engage its God-given intellect (*lā yaʿqilūn*).

## Sūrah Yūnus 10:44

**Expanded Commentary**

**The Absolute Justice of the Divine Essence (Tanzīh Allāh ʿan al-Ẓulm)**

The verse articulates one of the most sublime and foundational theological doctrines in Islam: *"Truly God does not wrong human beings in the least..."* (*Inna Allāha lā yaẓlimu al-nāsa shayʾā*).

The word *shayʾan* is used in the indefinite form within a negative clause, conveying absolute, comprehensive negation: God does not commit even the weight of an atom of injustice (*ẓulm*) against any creature in the heavens or the earth.

- God does not punish anyone for a sin they did not commit.
- God does not blind hearts arbitrarily or force souls into damnation against their will.

> *"O My servants! Verily, I have forbidden injustice (al-ẓulm) for Myself, and I have made it forbidden among you, so do not commit injustice against one another."* (Ṣaḥīḥ Muslim 2577, from Abū Dharr al-Ghifārī)

**The Reality of Self-Inflicted Ruin (Wa-lākinna al-Nāsa Anfusahum Yaẓlimūn)**

The verse reveals the true source of human spiritual suffering: *"but human beings wrong themselves"* (*wa-lākinna al-nāsa anfusahum yaẓlimūn*).

In Islamic theology, sin and disbelief are forms of spiritual self-harm (*ẓulm al-nafs*). When a human being ignores divine guidance, extinguishes their conscience, pursues destructive lusts, and oppresses others, they are not hurting God in the slightest; they are actively destroying their own psychological equilibrium, social peace, and eternal destiny. 

Just as a person who deliberately swallows lethal poison cannot blame the physician for their illness, the soul that chooses spiritual rebellion cannot blame the Creator for the agony of the Fire.

The verse concludes with an unequivocal spiritual audit: *"Lost indeed are those who denied the meeting with God and were not guided"* (*Qad khasira alladhīna kadhdhabū bi-liqāʾi Allāhi wa-mā kānū muhtadīn*).

God commands the Messenger ﷺ to deliver a definitive, unshakeable reply under oath: *"Say, 'Yea, by my Lord! Truly it is the truth...'"* (*Qul ī wa-Rabbī innahu la-ḥaqq*).

This is one of only three places in the entire Qur'an where God commands the Prophet Muhammad ﷺ to swear by His Lord concerning the absolute reality of the Resurrection (the other two being 34:3 and 64:7). The Arabic phrasing is saturated with grammatical emphasis:
- *Ī* ("Yea/Indeed"): An archaic, solemn particle of affirmation.
- *Wa-Rabbī* ("By my Lord!"): A direct oath by the Creator and Sustainer of existence.
- *Inna* ("Truly/Verily"): Particle of absolute certitude.
- *La-ḥaqq* ("Most surely the truth"): The emphatic *lām* attached to the predicate.

**The Absolute Impossibility of Escape (Wa-mā Antum bi-Muʿjizīn)**

The verse concludes: *"and you cannot escape it!"* (*wa-mā antum bi-muʿjizīn*).

The term *muʿjizīn* signifies those who can frustrate, outrun, evade, or overpower an opponent. The disbelievers are reminded of their total ontological impotence. You cannot build a bunker deep enough, run far enough, or assemble an army strong enough to evade the summons of the Angel of Death or escape the universal gathering before the Supreme Judge.

## Sūrah Yūnus 10:54

> **Were it so that each soul that had done wrong possessed all that is in the earth, it would seek to ransom itself therewith. They will harbor regret when they see the punishment. And judgment shall be rendered between them with justice, and they will not be wronged.**

**Expanded Commentary**

**The Infinite Ransom of Desperation**

The verse illustrates the staggering psychological horror and regret of the Day of Judgment: *"Were it so that each soul that had done wrong possessed all that is in the earth, it would seek to ransom itself therewith..."* (*Wa-law anna li-kulli nafsin ẓalamat mā fī al-arḍi la'ftadat bih*).

In this earthly life, wealthy individuals and powerful oligarchs utilize their fortunes, lawyers, and political connections to buy their way out of legal consequences, prison sentences, and scandals. The Qur'an posits a profound thought experiment: if a single guilty soul possessed the entirety of planet earth—all its gold, oil, real estate, technology, and treasures multiplied—that soul would gladly surrender every single atom of it without hesitation just to escape the agony of a single moment in the Hellfire. 

The Prophet Muhammad ﷺ vividly affirmed this in an authenticated tradition:

> *"A person from the people of the Fire will be brought on the Day of Resurrection and it will be said to him: 'O son of Adam, how did you find your dwelling?' He will say: 'The worst dwelling, O Lord!' Then it will be said: 'Would you ransom yourself with the earth full of gold?' He will say: 'Yes, O Lord!' Then God will say: 'You lie! I asked you for something far easier than that: I took a covenant from you in the loins of Adam that you associate no partners with Me, but you insisted on polytheism.'"* (Ṣaḥīḥ al-Bukhārī 6557; Ṣaḥīḥ Muslim 2805, from Anas ibn Mālik)

**The Internalized Agony of Silent Regret (Asarrū al-Nadāmah)**

The verse continues: *"They will harbor regret when they see the punishment"* (*wa-asarrū al-nadāmata lammā raʾawu al-ʿadhāb*).

The verb *asarrū* has two profound interpretations among classical scholars:
1. **Concealing Regret:** They suppress and internalize their sheer anguish in stunned, speechless horror, unable even to articulate words because the reality is so overwhelming.
2. **Manifesting Inward Agony:** The deepest layers of internal regret (*nadāmah*) consume their souls from within, as they realize that eternal bliss was offered to them freely in the world, and they threw it away for transient vanity.

**Judgment Rendered with Absolute Justice (Quḍiya Baynahum bi'l-Qisṭ)**

The verse concludes: *"And judgment shall be rendered between them with justice, and they will not be wronged"* (*wa-quḍiya baynahum bi'l-qisṭi wa-hum lā yuẓlamūn*).

Every single record is verified, every motive is audited, and every soul receives precisely what its deeds deserve. The court of the Almighty is pure justice (*al-qisṭ*).

## Sūrah Yūnus 10:55

> **Truly unto God belongs whatsoever is in the heavens and on the earth. Truly God’s Promise is true, but most of them know not.**

**Expanded Commentary**

**The Absolute Universal Sovereignty of God (Mā fī al-Samāwāti wa'l-Arḍ)**

The verse establishes the absolute metaphysical ownership of the Creator: *"Truly unto God belongs whatsoever is in the heavens and on the earth"* (*A-lā inna lillāhi mā fī al-samāwāti wa'l-arḍ*).

The opening particle *A-lā* is a particle of awakening and alerting (*harf istiftāḥ wa-tanbīh*), demanding full intellectual presence:
- Every galaxy, star, planet, ecosystem, creature, and human soul is the absolute, unshared property (*milk*) of God Almighty.
- Human beings do not truly "own" anything on earth; our worldly possessions, wealth, land, and bodies are merely a temporary loan and a moral trust (*amānah*).
- Since God is the sole Sovereign Owner of all that exists, He has the absolute right to command, prohibit, judge, and hold every creature accountable.

**The Infallibility of the Divine Promise (Waʿd Allāh Ḥaqq)**

The verse affirms: *"Truly God’s Promise is true"* (*a-lā inna waʿda Allāhi ḥaqq*).

Every prophetic promise of resurrection, the gathering of nations, the weighing of deeds upon the Scale (*al-Mīzān*), the crossing of the Bridge (*al-Ṣirāṭ*), and the eternal abodes of Paradise and Hell is an ontological certainty. In human transactions, promises fail due to bankruptcy, death, or deception; God is the Ever-Living, All-Powerful Owner of existence, Whose word is truth.

**The Tragedy of Pervasive Ignorance (Wa-lākinna Aktharahum lā Yaʿlamūn)**

The verse concludes: *"but most of them know not"* (*wa-lākinna aktharahum lā yaʿlamūn*).

Despite the overwhelming cosmic evidence displayed across the heavens and the earth, the majority of humanity remains trapped in superficial sensory perception, living as if worldly wealth is eternal and divine accountability is a myth. The verse invites the believer to break free from the unthinking herd mentality and anchor their life in real, eternal knowledge.

## Sūrah Yūnus 10:56

> **He gives life and causes death, and unto Him shall you be returned.**

**Expanded Commentary**

**The Exclusive Divine Monopoly Over Life and Mortality (Iḥyāʾ and Imātah)**

The verse states with ultimate theological brevity: *"He gives life and causes death..."* (*Huwa yuḥyī wa-yumītu*).

The fronted independent pronoun *Huwa* ("He alone") signifies exclusivity:
- **Bestowing Life (*al-Iḥyāʾ*):** Bringing biological animation, consciousness, and breathing vitality into inanimate matter. No scientist, technological corporation, or collective human intellect can synthesize life *ex nihilo* from lifeless chemicals. God alone sparks life in the womb.
- **Causing Death (*al-Imātah*):** Terminating the appointed biological lifespan (*al-Ajal*), commanding the soul to depart the body. No medical intervention, genetic therapy, or imperial power can prevent death when the decree of God arrives.

These two phenomena—the mystery of birth and the finality of death—are daily observable realities that demonstrate humanity's total dependence upon a transcendent Creator.

**The Inevitable Universal Return (Wa-Ilayhi Turjaʿūn)**

The verse concludes: *"and unto Him shall you be returned"* (*wa-ilayhi turjaʿūn*).

Life is not a circular, meaningless repetition, nor is death absolute extinction. Human existence is a linear, purposeful journey: we originated from God, we live temporarily by His permission, we die at His command, and we are resurrected back unto Him for moral auditing and eternal recompense.

## Sūrah Yūnus 10:57

> **O mankind! There has come unto you an exhortation from your Lord, and a healing for that which is in the breasts, and a guidance and a mercy for the believers.**

**Expanded Commentary**

**The Universal Quadruple Blessing of the Qur'an**

The verse delivers a majestic, universal address to all humanity: *"O mankind! There has come unto you an exhortation from your Lord, and a healing for that which is in the breasts, and a guidance and a mercy for the believers"* (*Yā ayyuhā al-nāsu qad jāʾatkum mawʿiẓatun min Rabbikum wa-shifāʾun limā fī al-ṣudūri wa-hudan wa-raḥmatun li'l-muʾminīn*).

Classical commentators, including Ibn ʿAbbās, al-Ṭabarī, al-Qurṭubī, and Ibn Kathīr, highlight the profound progression of the four descriptions of the Qur'an:

1. **Exhortation (*Mawʿiẓah*):** Inspiring admonition that softens hard hearts, awakens moral conscience, reminds the soul of the consequences of sin, and urges humanity toward righteousness through beautiful wisdom and loving advice.
2. **Healing for the Breasts (*Shifāʾun limā fī al-ṣudūr*):** The ultimate spiritual and psychological therapy. It heals the heart (*al-Qalb*) from the deadly diseases of doubt (*al-shakk*), hypocrisy (*al-nifāq*), malice (*al-ghill*), envy (*al-ḥasad*), arrogance (*al-kibr*), and existential anxiety (*al-qalaq*). It restores inner tranquility and emotional equilibrium.
3. **Guidance (*Hudan*):** An infallible intellectual and practical compass that illuminates the Straight Path, clarifying theology, jurisprudence, ethics, and human relationships, protecting humanity from wandering into philosophical and moral confusion.
4. **Mercy for the Believers (*Raḥmatun li'l-Muʾminīn*):** An overflowing fountain of divine grace, peace, forgiveness, and eternal salvation in the Hereafter for those who open their hearts and embrace its teachings with sincere faith.

**The Holistic Cure for the Modern Soul**

In an era marked by unprecedented mental health crises, existential despair, and moral fragmentation, verse 57 stands as an open invitation from the Creator. The Qur'an is not a burdensome legal code; it is the divine medicine (*shifāʾ*) engineered specifically for the human heart by the One Who fashioned the heart:

> *"And We send down of the Qur'an that which is healing and mercy for the believers."* (Qur'an 17:82)

## Sūrah Yūnus 10:58

> **Say, “In the Bounty of God and His Mercy—in that let them rejoice; it is better than all that they accumulate.”**

**Expanded Commentary**

**The True Object of Human Joy: Divine Bounty and Mercy**

The verse directs the emotional energy and aspirations of humanity: *"Say, 'In the Bounty of God and His Mercy—in that let them rejoice...'"* (*Qul bi-faḍli Allāhi wa-bi-raḥmatihi fa-bi-dhālika fa'l-yafraḥū*).

What is the true meaning of "the Bounty of God" (*Faḍl Allāh*) and "His Mercy" (*Raḥmatuhu*)?
- Ibn ʿAbbās, Ubayy ibn Kaʿb, and Qatādah interpreted *Faḍl Allāh* as the **Qur'an itself**, and *Raḥmatuhu* as **Islam and genuine faith (*al-Īmān*)**.
- Mujāhid and al-Ḥasan al-Baṣrī explained that God's greatest bounty to humanity is guidance, knowledge, and the privilege of knowing the Creator.

The Qur'an does not condemn joy; rather, it elevates and purifies the object of joy. Worldly joy based on wealth, social status, and material acquisitions is fragile, superficial, and easily destroyed by loss or death. Spiritual joy rooted in divine revelation and a living connection with God is an unshakeable inner sanctuary that outlasts the cosmos itself.

**The Spiritual Valuation: Greater Than All Worldly Accumulation**

The verse concludes with a definitive spiritual valuation: *"it is better than all that they accumulate"* (*huwa khayrun mimmā yajmaʿūn*).

The verb *yajmaʿūn* encompasses all that humans spend their lifetimes frantically accumulating: bank accounts, real estate, luxury vehicles, stock portfolios, and social prestige. 

When placed upon the eternal scale:
- A single verse of divine guidance understood and practiced,
- A single moment of sincere prostration to the Creator,
- A single tear shed out of love for God,
is infinitely more valuable than all the combined riches, empires, and technologies accumulated by humanity from the beginning of time to its end.

## Sūrah Yūnus 10:59

> **Say, “Have you seen that which God sent down unto you of provision, and you have made some of it unlawful and some lawful?” Say, “Did God permit you, or do you fabricate lies against God?”**

**Expanded Commentary**

**The Divine Prerogative of Legislation (Tashrīʿ)**

The verse addresses the arbitrary religious innovations and dietary taboos invented by pagan societies: *"Say, 'Have you seen that which God sent down unto you of provision, and you have made some of it unlawful and some lawful?'"* (*Qul a-raʾaytum mā anzala Allāhu lakum min rizqin fa-jaʿaltum minhu ḥarāman wa-ḥalālā*).

Classical exegetes, including Ibn ʿAbbās, al-Ṭabarī, and Ibn Kathīr, explain the historical context: the pagan Quraysh invented superstitious dietary laws without any divine authorization—declaring certain livestock sacred to their idols (such as *al-Baḥīrah*, *al-Sāʾibah*, *al-Waṣīlah*, and *al-Ḥām*; cf. 5:103), prohibiting women from eating certain meats while permitting them to men, and dedicating portions of their agricultural harvest to false gods.

In Islamic theology (*Uṣūl al-Fiqh*), the authority to declare something *Ḥalāl* (lawful) or *Ḥarām* (unlawful) belongs exclusively to God Almighty. When human beings invent arbitrary religious prohibitions or permit what God has forbidden, they usurp the divine prerogative of legislation.

**The Devastating Epistemological Dilemma**

God commands the Prophet ﷺ to confront them with an inescapable dilemma: *"Say, 'Did God permit you, or do you fabricate lies against God?'"* (*Qul Āllāhu adhina lakum am ʿalā Allāhi taftarūn*).

There are only two possible explanations for religious legislation:
1. Either you possess an authentic, verified revelation from God explicitly permitting this ruling (*Āllāhu adhina lakum*),
2. Or you are fabricating a slanderous lie against God out of your own cultural superstitions and desires (*am ʿalā Allāhi taftarūn*).

Since they possessed no scripture, their entire ritual system was exposed as fraudulent human invention. The verse serves as a permanent warning against religious innovation (*bidʿah*) and declaring things forbidden or permitted based on personal whims without decisive textual evidence.

## Sūrah Yūnus 10:60

> **What will those who fabricate lies against God consider on the Day of Resurrection? Surely God is full of Bounty unto mankind, but most of them are not thankful.**

**Expanded Commentary**

**The Terrifying Thought Experiment: Facing the Day of Resurrection**

The verse poses a piercing rhetorical question to all who invent false doctrines, corrupt religious laws, or claim that God has partners: *"What will those who fabricate lies against God consider on the Day of Resurrection?"* (*Wa-mā ẓannu alladhīna yaftarūna ʿalā Allāhi al-kadhiba yawma al-qiyāmah*).

What do these fabricators imagine their reception will be when they stand directly before the Almighty King on the Day of Judgment? 
- Do they think God will overlook their slander?
- Do they imagine their fabricated idols will protect them?
- Do they think their worldly status will save them from justice?

The question creates a chilling pause, forcing the human conscience to confront the catastrophic weight of spiritual fraud before the eternal tribunal.

**The Boundless Divine Bounty (Inna Allāha la-Dhū Faḍlin ʿalā al-Nās)**

The verse continues: *"Surely God is full of Bounty unto mankind..."* (*Inna Allāha la-dhū faḍlin ʿalā al-nās*).

God's infinite bounty is manifested in multiple dimensions:
- He provides abundant sustenance, air, water, and life to all creatures, including those who slander Him and worship others.
- He does not punish sinners immediately; He grants them prolonged life, health, and opportunities for sincere repentance.
- He sent down clear revelations and noble messengers to guide humanity to eternal bliss in Paradise.

**The Pervasiveness of Ingratitude (Wa-lākinna Aktharahum lā Yashkurūn)**

The verse concludes: *"but most of them are not thankful"* (*wa-lākinna aktharahum lā yashkurūn*).

Despite drowning in oceans of divine blessings, the majority of humanity fails to cultivate active gratitude (*shukr*). They attribute their wealth to their own intellect, their health to luck, and their success to secondary causes, while directing their worship to created entities. 

True gratitude involves three essential dimensions: acknowledging the blessing in the heart, praising the Benefactor with the tongue, and utilizing the blessing in obedience to divine command.

## Sūrah Yūnus 10:61

> **Thou art not upon any task, nor dost thou recite any part of the Quran, nor do you do any deed, save that We are witnesses over you when you are engaged in it. And there escapes not from thy Lord the weight of an atom on the earth or in the heaven, nor smaller than that, nor greater, save that it is in a clear Book.**

**Expanded Commentary**

**The Ultimate Paradigm of Divine Omnipresence and All-Encompassing Witnessing (al-Murāqabah)**

The verse presents one of the most awe-inspiring descriptions of divine omniscience and omnipresence in all of revelation: *"Thou art not upon any task, nor dost thou recite any part of the Quran, nor do you do any deed, save that We are witnesses over you when you are engaged in it..."* (*Wa-mā takūnu fī shaʾnin wa-mā tatlū minhu min qurʾānin wa-lā taʿmalūna min ʿamalin illā kunnā ʿalaykum shuhūdan idh tufīḍūna fīh*).

The verse encompasses every dimension of human activity:
- *Fī shaʾn* (any task/affair): Any personal, commercial, domestic, political, or private state you are in.
- *Wa-mā tatlū minhu min qurʾān* (nor recite any part of the Qur'an): The spiritual, liturgical acts of worship, prayer, and recitation of scripture.
- *Wa-lā taʿmalūna min ʿamal* (nor do any deed): Shifted to the plural pronoun (*taʿmalūn*), encompassing every action performed by every human being across all of history.

The phrase *idh tufīḍūna fīh* ("when you are actively engaged/immersed in it") conveys the profound truth that at the exact microsecond an intention forms, a word is spoken, a keystroke is pressed, or a step is taken, God Almighty is directly witnessing (*shuhūd*) the act with exhaustive precision.

This is the spiritual foundation of *al-Murāqabah* (spiritual vigilance) and *al-Iḥsān* (spiritual excellence). When a human being realizes that their Creator is actively witnessing every breath and thought, private hypocrisy becomes impossible, loneliness is transformed into intimate divine communion, and every righteous act is performed with deep sincerity.

**The Sub-Atomic Precision of Divine Knowledge (Mithqāl Dharratin)**

The verse continues with a breathtaking cosmological statement: *"And there escapes not from thy Lord the weight of an atom on the earth or in the heaven, nor smaller than that, nor greater, save that it is in a clear Book"* (*wa-mā yaʿzubu ʿan Rabbika min mithqāli dharratin fī al-arḍi wa-lā fī al-samāʾi wa-lā aṣghara min dhālika wa-lā akbara illā fī kitābin mubīn*).

The verb *yaʿzubu* signifies slipping away, hiding, or remaining concealed. In classical Arabic, *dharratun* referred to the smallest particle conceivable to the human eye—a tiny speck of dust visible in a ray of sunlight, or the weight of a tiny ant. 

Remarkably, the Qur'an explicitly adds: *wa-lā aṣghara min dhālika* ("nor smaller than that"). In an era long before the discovery of sub-atomic physics, quantum particles, quarks, and neutrinos, the Qur'an explicitly affirmed the existence of particles smaller than the *dharrah*. 

Every sub-atomic particle in the deepest vacuum of space, every biochemical interaction inside a living cell, and every microscopic organism in the abyssal trenches of the ocean is exhaustively known, sustained, and registered in the Clear Book (*Kitāb Mubīn*—the Preserved Tablet, *al-Lawḥ al-Maḥfūẓ*).

## Sūrah Yūnus 10:62

> **Behold! Truly the friends of God, no fear shall come over them, nor shall they grieve;**

**Expanded Commentary**

**The Elite Sanctuary of the Allies of God (Awliyāʾ Allāh)**

The verse opens with a grand proclamation of divine favor: *"Behold! Truly the friends of God, no fear shall come over them, nor shall they grieve"* (*A-lā inna awliyāʾa Allāhi lā khawfun ʿalayhim wa-lā hum yaḥzanūn*).

The term *awliyāʾ* is the plural of *walī*, derived from the root *w-l-y*, which signifies nearness, intimacy, loyalty, alliance, and mutual support. The *Walī* of God is one who is close to God through sincere faith, devotion, and obedience, and whom God takes close through His love, protection, and assistance.

In Islamic theology, the concept of *Wilāyah* (divine friendship) is not an exclusive, hereditary caste, nor is it an esoteric title granted to a select few monastic recluses. It is an open spiritual station accessible to every sincere believer who aligns their heart and deeds with divine guidance.

**The Absolute Immunity: Freedom from Fear (Khawf) and Grief (Ḥuzn)**

The verse promises two ultimate psychological and spiritual immunities:
1. **No Fear Shall Come Over Them (*Lā khawfun ʿalayhim*):** Fear is an emotion directed toward the **future**—anxiety regarding impending danger, poverty, sickness, death, or punishment. The friends of God are granted inner peace in this world, and upon facing death and the Day of Judgment, the angels greet them with absolute reassurance that their future is eternally secure in Paradise.
2. **Nor Shall They Grieve (*Wa-lā hum yaḥzanūn*):** Grief is an emotion directed toward the **past**—sorrow over lost opportunities, missed worldly pleasures, departed loved ones, or past mistakes. In Paradise, the friends of God will find their past trials transformed into immense rewards, and their souls will be so overwhelmed with joy that all memory of earthly sorrow will be permanently wiped away.

The Prophet Muhammad ﷺ described these illuminated souls in an authenticated tradition:

> *"Among the servants of God are people who are neither prophets nor martyrs, but whom the prophets and martyrs will envy on the Day of Resurrection because of their station with God Almighty."* They said: "O Messenger of God, tell us who they are!" He said: *"They are people who loved one another for the sake of God, without any kinship between them or wealth exchanged. By God, their faces are light, and they are upon light. They do not fear when people fear, nor do they grieve when people grieve."* Then he recited this verse: *"Behold! Truly the friends of God, no fear shall come over them, nor shall they grieve."* (Sunan Abī Dāwūd 3527; Jāmiʿ al-Tirmidhī)

## Sūrah Yūnus 10:63

> **those who believe and are reverent.**

**Expanded Commentary**

**The Two Inseparable Pillars of Divine Friendship (Wilāyah)**

Following the grand proclamation of the preceding verse, God provides the definitive, universally accessible theological definition of who the *Awliyāʾ Allāh* truly are: *"those who believe and are reverent"* (*Alladhīna āmanū wa-kānū yattaqūn*).

The definition rests upon two clear, inseparable pillars:
1. **Sound, Authentic Faith (*al-Īmān*):** Pure monotheistic conviction in the heart, free from polytheism (*shirk*), hypocrisy (*nifāq*), and destructive theological innovations. It involves believing in God, His angels, His books, His messengers, the Last Day, and the Divine Decree.
2. **Continuous Godly Reverence and Mindfulness (*al-Taqwā*):** Notice the continuous past-tense verbal construction *wa-kānū yattaqūn* ("and they were persistently practicing taqwā"). *Taqwā* is the active translation of faith into daily life: fulfilling obligatory duties (*al-farāʾiḍ*), performing voluntary acts of devotion (*al-nawāfil*), abstaining from all forbidden matters (*al-muḥarramāt*), and guarding the limbs, eyes, tongue, and stomach from spiritual toxins.

**The Demolition of Superstition and Esoteric Elitism**

Shaykh al-Islām Ibn Taymiyyah emphasized in his classic treatise *al-Furqān bayna Awliyāʾ al-Raḥmān wa-Awliyāʾ al-Shayṭān*:

> *"Whoever is a believer possessing taqwā is a walī of God. Divine friendship is not attained through magical tricks, walking on water, or abandoning the Sharīʿah; rather, it is attained strictly through following the Messenger of God ﷺ in inward faith and outward obedience."*

This verse democratizes spiritual greatness. The peasant, the laborer, the mother, the scholar, and the ruler alike can all attain the sublime rank of *Walī Allāh* by cultivating sincere faith and persistent *taqwā*.

## Sūrah Yūnus 10:64

> **For them are glad tidings in the life of this world and in the Hereafter. No alteration can there be in the Words of God. That is the tremendous triumph.**

**Expanded Commentary**

**The Dual Horizon of Glad Tidings (Al-Bushrā)**

The verse outlines the magnificent rewards bestowed upon the friends of God: *"For them are glad tidings in the life of this world and in the Hereafter"* (*Lahumu al-bushrā fī al-ḥayāti al-dunyā wa-fī al-ākhirah*).

Classical commentators and authentic prophetic traditions explain the manifestations of *al-Bushrā* in both realms:
1. **In the Life of This World (*Fī al-Ḥayāt al-Dunyā*):**
   - **The True, Righteous Dream (*al-Ruʾyā al-Ṣāliḥah*):** The Prophet Muhammad ﷺ explicitly explained this phrase when asked by Abū al-Dardāʾ:
     > *"It is the righteous dream that the Muslim sees or is seen for him; it is a portion of the forty-six parts of prophethood."* (Jāmiʿ al-Tirmidhī 2273; Sunan Ibn Mājah 3898)
   - **Serenity of the Heart and Divine Acceptance:** The inner peace, contentment, and the affection that God places in the hearts of the righteous on earth (*al-Wudd*).
   - **The Glad Tidings at Death:** When the angels descend upon the dying believer with sweet aromas and white shrouds, whispering: *"Do not fear and do not grieve, but receive glad tidings of Paradise which you were promised"* (41:30).
2. **In the Hereafter (*Fī al-Ākhirah*):**
   - The immediate greeting of peace in the grave, the illumination of the Bridge (*al-Ṣirāṭ*), safety from the Great Terror (*al-Fazaʿ al-Akbar*), and eternal admission into the Gardens of Bliss.

**The Immutability of the Divine Decree (Lā Tabdīla li-Kalimāt Allāh)**

The verse affirms: *"No alteration can there be in the Words of God"* (*lā tabdīla li-kalimāti Allāh*).

God's promises are immutable ontological realities. No power in creation can revoke, cancel, or modify what God has decreed for His righteous servants. His word is eternal, absolute, and perfectly fulfilled.

**The Supreme Valuation: The Tremendous Triumph (al-Fawz al-ʿAẓīm)**

The verse concludes: *"That is the tremendous triumph"* (*Dhālika huwa al-fawzu al-ʿaẓīm*).

To attain divine friendship, to be liberated from fear and grief, to receive glad tidings in both worlds, and to inherit eternal Paradise is the ultimate, unrivaled victory (*al-Fawz al-ʿAẓīm*) beside which all worldly achievements pale into absolute insignificance.

## Sūrah Yūnus 10:65

> **And let not their speech grieve thee. Truly might belongs to God entirely. He is the All-Hearing, the All-Knowing.**

**Expanded Commentary**

**The Prophetic Heart and the Wound of Slander**

The verse offers divine consolation and psychological healing to the Prophet Muhammad ﷺ: *"And let not their speech grieve thee..."* (*Wa-lā yaḥzunka qawluhum*).

The Prophet ﷺ was a man of profound empathy, emotional sensitivity, and boundless compassion. When the pagan chieftains of Quraysh unleashed relentless campaigns of slander—labeling him a liar, a poet, an insane madman (*majnūn*), and a sorcerer (*sāḥir*)—his noble heart experienced deep sorrow (*ḥuzn*). His grief was not out of wounded personal vanity, but out of profound anguish that their stubborn rejection was sealing their eternal damnation.

God intervenes with tender divine pedagogy, commanding him not to allow their toxic words to penetrate his emotional sanctuary or weaken his spiritual resolve.

**The Absolute Divine Monopoly Over Honor and Power (Al-ʿIzzah)**

The verse provides the rational and theological antidote to social hostility: *"Truly might belongs to God entirely"* (*Inna al-ʿizzata lillāhi jamīʿan*).

The term *al-ʿIzzah* encompasses absolute honor, invincibility, supreme power, and dominion. The disbelievers sought honor through tribal alliances, wealth, and verbal arrogance. God reminds the Messenger ﷺ and all believers:
- All power and honor belong exclusively to God (*jamīʿan*).
- Created beings possess zero autonomous power to bestow true honor or inflict permanent humiliation.
- Whoever seeks honor through falsehood will inevitably be abased; whoever stands with the truth of God will ultimately be elevated. As the Qur'an declares elsewhere:

> *"Whoever desires honor—then to God belongs all honor entirely."* (Qur'an 35:10)

**The Omnipresent Sanctuary: The All-Hearing, The All-Knowing**

The verse closes: *"He is the All-Hearing, the All-Knowing"* (*Huwa al-Samīʿu al-ʿAlīm*).

## Sūrah Yūnus 10:66

**The Universal Metaphysical Domain: All Conscious Beings Belong to God**

**The Epistemic Vacuum of Shirk: Following Pure Delusion**

The verse continues: *"And those who call upon partners, apart from God, follow not [real partners]; they follow naught but conjecture..."* (*wa-mā yattabiʿu alladhīna yadʿūna min dūni Allāhi shurakāʾa in yattabiʿūna illā al-ẓann*).

The Qur'an exposes the ontological non-existence of these "partners." When polytheists pray to their idols, saints, or celestial bodies, they are not interacting with actual divine co-rulers; they are pursuing a complete epistemic vacuum. Their entire theological system is built upon *al-ẓann*—unsubstantiated myths, wishful thinking, and subjective speculation.

The verse concludes: *"and they do but lie"* (*wa-in hum illā yakhruṣūn*).

The verb *yakhruṣūn* is exceptionally descriptive in Arabic lexicography. The verse calls humanity to liberate itself from the shackles of unverified mythology and anchor belief in authentic, verified divine revelation.

## Sūrah Yūnus 10:67

> **He it is Who made the night for you, that you might rest therein, and the day sight-giving. Truly in that are signs for a people who hear.**

**Expanded Commentary**

**The Ecological and Biological Calibration of the Diurnal Cycle**

The verse directs human contemplation toward the daily biological and planetary rhythm: *"He it is Who made the night for you, that you might rest therein, and the day sight-giving..."* (*Huwa alladhī jaʿala lakumu al-layla li-taskunū fīhi wa'l-nahāra mubṣirā*).

The Qur'an highlights two perfectly calibrated cosmic gifts:
1. **The Night as a Sanctuary of Rest (*Li-taskunū fīh*):** The root *s-k-n* signifies tranquility, stillness, peace, and restorative stillness. God designed the dark canopy of night and biological circadian rhythms so that human bodies, nervous systems, and minds can recharge, repair cellular damage, and find psychological refuge from the exhaustion of labor.
2. **The Day as Sight-Giving (*Mubṣirā*):** The day is described by the active participle *mubṣir* ("seeing/sight-giving")—a beautiful Arabic rhetorical device attributing the faculty of vision to the time itself, because sunlight illuminates the physical world, enabling humanity to work, cultivate the earth, engage in commerce, and navigate safely.

Were the earth deprived of either of these two phases, human civilization and biological life would rapidly collapse into physical and mental breakdown.

**Signs for Those Who Truly Listen (Li-Qawmin Yasmaʿūn)**

The verse concludes: *"Truly in that are signs for a people who hear"* (*Inna fī dhālika la-āyātin li-qawmin yasmaʿūn*).

Why does the verse conclude with "a people who hear" (*yasmaʿūn*) rather than those who see? Classical commentators note that hearing (*al-Samʿ*) here signifies receptive listening accompanied by intellectual reflection and obedience (*samʿ tadabbur wa-qabūl*). 

The daily transition from light to darkness and back to light is an eloquent, unspoken sermon broadcast continuously to all humanity; only those who open the ears of their heart can perceive the boundless wisdom, mercy, and oneness of the Creator embedded within it.

## Sūrah Yūnus 10:68

**The Universal Refutation of Divine Filial Claims**

The verse confronts the theological slander attributed to God by various polytheistic and sectarian groups: *"They say, 'God has taken a child'"* (*Qālū ittakhadha Allāhu waladā*).

This encompasses the pagan Arab belief that the angels were the "daughters of God," the Christian doctrine of the divine sonship of Jesus, and any mythological system that projects biological reproduction onto the Creator. 

The Qur'an methodically demolishes this concept through four decisive theological arguments:

1. **Absolute Transcendence (*Subḥānah*):** God is infinitely exalted above all physical limitations, biological needs, and anthropomorphic projections. The term *Subḥān* negates every conceivable defect, contingency, or similarity to created beings.
2. **Absolute Self-Sufficiency (*Huwa al-Ghanī*):** Biological creatures produce offspring because they are mortal, weak, and dependent—seeking lineage, emotional comfort, assistance in old age, and continuity of species after death. God is *al-Ghanī*—the Eternally Self-Sufficient, the Absolute (*al-Ṣamad*), Who has no beginning, no end, no weakness, and no need for assistance from anyone.
3. **Universal Ownership (*Lahu mā fī al-samāwāti wa-mā fī al-arḍ*):** Everything in existence is His created property and servant. A child shares the identical essence and nature of its parent; but everything in the cosmos is created and dependent, while God alone is the Uncreated Creator. It is an ontological absurdity for a Creator to "beget" a created servant.
4. **The Complete Lack of Epistemic Authority (*In ʿindakum min sulṭānin bi-hādhā*):** The term *sulṭān* here means decisive proof, authentic revelation, and rational evidence. You possess zero scriptural or intellectual proof for this monumental claim.

**The Intellectual Indictment: Speaking Without Knowledge**

The verse closes with a piercing rebuke: *"Do you say about God that which you know not?"* (*A-taqūlūna ʿalā Allāhi mā lā taʿlamūn*).

To make dogmatic theological assertions about the nature of the Supreme Creator based on nothing more than human myths and philosophical speculation is the highest form of intellectual recklessness and spiritual crime.

The sequence is mathematically certain:
1. A brief, superficial worldly enjoyment (*matāʿ*),
2. The sudden arrival of death and the universal return to the Divine Court (*marjiʿuhum*),
3. The direct, visceral tasting of severe retribution (*al-ʿadhāb al-shadīd*) for their persistent, unrepentant rejection of truth.

The verse invites every intelligent reader to weigh a few fleeting years of worldly compromise against an eternity of spiritual ruin, urging the soul to choose the enduring over the ephemeral.

Noah addresses his persecutors with breathtaking courage: *"O my people! If my standing [among you] and my reminding you of the signs of God is hard on you, then in God have I put my trust"* (*Yā qawmi in kāna kabura ʿalaykum maqāmī wa-tadhkīrī bi-āyāti Allāhi fa-ʿalā Allāhi tawakkalt*).

God sent prophets like Hūd to ʿĀd, Ṣāliḥ to Thamūd, and Shuʿayb to Midian. Each messenger was equipped with *al-bayyināt*—undeniable miracles, sublime moral character, and clear intellectual proofs designed to awaken human conscience.

**The Inertia of Inherited Skepticism (Mā Kānū li-Yuʾminū bimā Kadhdhabū bihi min Qabl)**

The verse captures the tragic psychological inertia of stubborn disbelief: *"but they were not going to believe in that which they had denied before"* (*fa-mā kānū li-yuʾminū bimā kadhdhabū bihi min qabl*).

Classical commentators, including al-Ṭabarī, al-Baghawī, and Ibn Kathīr, offer two profound insights into this phrase:
1. **Entrenched Ancestral Prejudice:** Each successive generation slavishly inherited the prejudices, cynicism, and mockery of the generations before them. The moment the prophet spoke, their pre-programmed hostility was triggered, preventing them from evaluating the message on its merits.
2. **The Immediate Rejection Syndrome:** The moment they initially denied the message, their hearts hardened; even when subsequent miracles and proofs were shown to them, they refused to retract their initial denial out of sheer pride and stubbornness.

**The Sealing of the Transgressing Hearts (Al-Ṭabʿ ʿalā Qulūb al-Muʿtadīn)**

The verse concludes: *"Thus do We seal the hearts of the transgressors"* (*Kadhālika naṭbaʿu ʿalā qulūbi al-muʿtadīn*).

The term *al-muʿtadūn* refers to those who commit *iʿtidāʾ*—willfully crossing the boundaries of justice, conscience, and truth. The "sealing" (*al-ṭabʿ*) of the heart is not an arbitrary initial act by God; it is the divine consequence of repeated, deliberate choices to suppress truth and embrace transgression. When a person repeatedly extinguishes the light of conscience, God confirms their chosen darkness, leaving their heart permanently desensitized to guidance.

The climax of the confrontation unfolds: *"And when they cast, Moses said, 'That which you have brought is sorcery. Truly God will nullify it...'"* (*Fa-lammā alqaw qāla Mūsā mā jiʾtum bihi al-siḥru inna Allāha sayubṭiluh*).

The sorcerers cast their ropes and wooden rods, having treated them with mercury and optical chemicals, casting a spell upon the eyes of the people and terrifying the crowds so that their rods appeared to slither like countless serpents (cf. 7:116; 20:66).

Moses stands before this dazzling optical storm and utters four immortal words of absolute conviction: *Inna Allāha sayubṭiluh* ("Truly, God will reduce it to nothing!"). 
- Optical illusions, political propaganda, and media deception can create a temporary sensory impression of overwhelming power; but they possess zero ontological reality.
- When Moses cast his staff, it transformed by divine command into a real, biological, towering serpent that physically swallowed up all their fabricated illusions (*talqafu mā yaʾfikūn*).

**The Universal Law: Corrupters Cannot Prosper (Inna Allāha lā Yuṣliḥu ʿAmala al-Mufsidīn)**

Moses proclaims the eternal spiritual law: *"Truly God does not prosper the work of the corrupters"* (*Inna Allāha lā yuṣliḥu ʿamala al-mufsidīn*).

The verb *yuṣliḥ* means to make fruitful, constructive, enduring, and successful. Any project, regime, political campaign, or philosophical system founded upon *fasād*—deception, oppression, and corruption—contains the seeds of its own inevitable destruction. God will never allow falsehood to achieve ultimate victory over truth.

The verb *yuḥiqqu* means to establish, substantiate, manifest, and make triumphant. God validates the truth through His *Kalimāt*—His divine decrees, His revealed scriptures, and His ontological commands that govern reality.

The historic result was instantaneous and profound: the elite sorcerers—who were the foremost experts in illusion and recognized immediately that Moses's staff was not magic but a direct act of the Supreme Creator—fell down in prostration, declaring:

> *"We have believed in the Lord of the worlds, the Lord of Moses and Aaron!"* (Qur'an 7:121–122)

In a single hour, the state's prime ideological champions were transformed into luminous martyrs, willing to face Pharaoh's crucifixion rather than renounce their new faith. Falsehood collapsed publicly, despite the bitter hatred and rage of the Pharaonic establishment (*wa-law kariha al-mujrimūn*).

## Sūrah Yūnus 10:83

> **But none believed in Moses, save some progeny from his people, for fear of Pharaoh and their chiefs, lest he persecute them. For Pharaoh was high in the land, and truly he was of the prodigal.**

**Expanded Commentary**

**The Youth Movement of Early Faith: The Progeny (Dhurriyyah)**

The verse reveals a fascinating sociological and historical reality of Moses's mission: *"But none believed in Moses, save some progeny from his people..."* (*Fa-mā āmana li-Mūsā illā dhurriyyatun min qawmih*).

The term *dhurriyyah* specifically denotes the youth, adolescents, and rising generation. While the elderly and entrenched tribal elders were paralyzed by fear, cynicism, and worldly compromises, it was the courageous youth among the Israelites (and according to some commentators like Ibn ʿAbbās, a few young Egyptians including Pharaoh's wife Asiyah and the Believer of Pharaoh's Family) who had the spiritual audacity to embrace the truth.

This mirrors the early community of Islam in Makkah, where the overwhelming majority of early converts around the Prophet Muhammad ﷺ—such as ʿAlī ibn Abī Ṭālib, al-Zubayr ibn al-ʿAwwām, Ṭalḥah, Muṣʿab ibn ʿUmayr, and al-Arqam—were idealistic youth under the age of thirty.

**The Climate of State Terror (Khawf min Firʿawn)**

The verse explains why widespread public conversion was delayed: *"for fear of Pharaoh and their chiefs, lest he persecute them"* (*ʿalā khawfin min Firʿawna wa-malaʾihim an yaftinahum*).

The verb *yaftinahum* here means to subject them to agonizing physical torture, execution, and economic ruin to force them to renounce their faith. Pharaoh operated a brutal totalitarian police state that specialized in public terror.

**The Anatomy of Tyranny: Haughtiness and Transgression**

The verse diagnoses Pharaoh's character: *"For Pharaoh was high in the land, and truly he was of the prodigal"* (*wa-inna Firʿawna la-ʿālin fī al-arḍi wa-innahu la-mina al-musrifīn*).
- *ʿĀlin fī al-arḍ* (high/haughty in the land): An arrogant tyrant drunk on unchecked political power, elevating himself above all moral laws.
- *Mina al-musrifīn* (of the prodigal/transgressors): One who exceeded all boundaries of decency, shedding innocent blood and claiming divinity for himself.

## Sūrah Yūnus 10:84

> **And Moses said, “O my people! If you believe in God, then in Him put your trust, if you are Muslims.”**

**Expanded Commentary**

**The Spiritual Prescription for Oppressed Believers: Trust Anchored in Faith**

Surrounded by Pharaoh's terror apparatus, Moses delivers the definitive spiritual formula for resilience: *"And Moses said, 'O my people! If you believe in God, then in Him put your trust, if you are Muslims'"* (*Wa-qāla Mūsā yā qawmi in kuntum āmantum bi'llāhi fa-ʿalayhi tawakkalū in kuntum muslimīn*).

Moses connects three foundational concepts in a powerful logical progression:
1. **Authentic Faith (*al-Īmān*):** The cognitive and spiritual conviction that God is the sole Creator, Sustainer, and ultimate Sovereign of the universe.
2. **Absolute Reliance (*al-Tawakkul*):** The active, unshakeable trust of the heart that relies upon God for protection, provision, and victory, while utilizing all available lawful means.
3. **Total Submission (*al-Islām*):** The complete surrender of one's will, life, and destiny to the Divine Decree.

Moses teaches his vulnerable followers that *Tawakkul* is not an optional mystical luxury; it is the vital operational test of genuine faith (*īmān*). If you truly believe that God is greater than Pharaoh, then your reliance must be directed exclusively toward God, not toward human power.

## Sūrah Yūnus 10:85

> **And they said, “In God do we trust. Our Lord! Make us not a trial for the wrongdoing people,**

**Expanded Commentary**

**The Believers' Response: Unconditional Reliance (ʿAlā Allāhi Tawakkalnā)**

The young believers respond with immediate, unhesitating conviction: *"And they said, 'In God do we trust'"* (*Fa-qālū ʿalā Allāhi tawakkalnā*).

Notice the fronting of the prepositional phrase *ʿalā Allāh* ("In God alone"): their trust is exclusive, pure, and undivided. They cast aside their fears and place their collective destiny in the hands of the Almighty.

**The Profound Supplication Against Becoming a Fitnah**

They immediately follow their declaration of trust with an inspired, multi-layered supplication: *"Our Lord! Make us not a trial for the wrongdoing people"* (*Rabbanā lā tajʿalnā fitnatan li'l-qawmi al-ẓālimīn*).

Classical commentators, including Mujāhid, Abū Mijlaz, al-Ṭabarī, and Ibn Kathīr, explain the profound theological depths of this prayer:
1. **Protection from Overpowering Oppression:** Do not allow the tyrants to defeat, crush, and torture us, which would become a severe test (*fitnah*) that might cause weak believers to falter in their faith.
2. **Preventing the Tyrants from Self-Delusion:** If Pharaoh's regime were to triumph over the believers, the oppressors would say: "Had these people been on the truth, God would not have allowed us to conquer them; our dominance proves that our gods and ways are superior!" Thus, the defeat of the believers would become a *fitnah* (a stumbling block of arrogance) that further confirms the tyrants in their disbelief.

## Sūrah Yūnus 10:86

> **And save us through Thy Mercy from the disbelieving people.”**

**Expanded Commentary**

**The Ultimate Petition for Spiritual and Physical Deliverance**

The young believers conclude their heartfelt supplication initiated in the preceding verse: *"And save us through Thy Mercy from the disbelieving people"* (*Wa-najjinā bi-raḥmatika mina al-qawmi al-kāfirīn*).

Notice the theological precision of their prayer:
- They do not demand salvation as an entitlement based upon their own righteousness, piety, or heritage as the Children of Israel.
- Rather, they appeal exclusively to God's unmerited, overflowing grace: *bi-raḥmatika* ("through Your mercy alone").

In Islamic theology (*ʿAqīdah*), no human being enters Paradise or achieves physical and spiritual salvation through the sheer merit of their own deeds; salvation is fundamentally an act of divine grace and mercy (*Raḥmah*). The Prophet Muhammad ﷺ emphasized this core principle to his companions:

> *"None of you will be entered into Paradise by his deeds alone."* They said: "Not even you, O Messenger of God?" He said: *"Not even me, unless God envelops me in His mercy and grace."* (Ṣaḥīḥ al-Bukhārī 5673; Ṣaḥīḥ Muslim 2816, from Abū Hurayrah)

**The Dual Dimensions of Deliverance (al-Najāh)**

Their prayer encompasses two vital dimensions of human flourishing:
1. **Physical Liberation:** Deliverance from the systemic violence, forced labor, and infanticide inflicted by Pharaoh's military state.
2. **Spiritual Liberation:** Deliverance from the corrupting ideological, moral, and cultural pressure of pagan Egypt, enabling them to live in pure monotheism and righteous community.

## Sūrah Yūnus 10:87

> **And We revealed unto Moses and his brother, “Appoint for your people houses in Egypt, and make your houses places of worship, and establish the prayer, and give glad tidings to the believers.”**

**Expanded Commentary**

**The Spiritual Mobilization Under Totalitarian Oppression**

God reveals a strategic spiritual and communal program to Moses and Aaron to preserve the faith of their oppressed community: *"And We revealed unto Moses and his brother, 'Appoint for your people houses in Egypt, and make your houses places of worship...'"* (*Wa-awḥaynā ilā Mūsā wa-akhīhi an tabawwaʾā li-qawmikumā bi-Miṣra buyūtan wa'jʿalū buyūtakum qiblah*).

Classical commentators, including Ibn ʿAbbās, Mujāhid, al-Ṭabarī, and Ibn Kathīr, explain the historical context: Pharaoh had closed down their public places of gathering and banned their religious assemblies, threatening anyone seen praying with execution.

God commands a revolutionary transformation of domestic space:
- *Tabawwaʾā... buyūtan* (appoint houses): Establish clustered, secure domestic quarters for the community in Egypt.
- *Wajʿalū buyūtakum qiblah* (make your houses places of worship/orientation): Transform your private domestic living rooms into sanctuaries of prayer (*masājid*) and spiritual orientation (*qiblah*). When the public sphere is seized by tyranny, the home must become the unassailable fortress of faith.

**Prayer as the Engine of Resilience (Wa-Aqīmū al-Ṣalāh)**

The verse commands: *"and establish the prayer"* (*wa-aqīmū al-ṣalāh*).

In times of extreme persecution, political crisis, and trauma, the primary weapon of the believer is *al-Ṣalāh*. Prayer is the direct lifeline connecting human vulnerability to omnipotent divine power. It infuses the soul with patience, calms panicked nerves, and unites families in shared spiritual solidarity. As God commands elsewhere:

> *"O you who have believed, seek help through patience and prayer; surely God is with the patient."* (Qur'an 2:153)

**The Mandate of Prophetic Optimism (Wa-Bashshir al-Muʾminīn)**

The verse concludes: *"and give glad tidings to the believers"* (*wa-bashshiri al-muʾminīn*).

Despite the darkness of Pharaoh's tyranny, Moses is commanded to radiate *Tabshīr* (glad tidings of victory, liberation, and divine favor). Spiritual leaders must never succumb to defeatism or cynicism; they must instill unshakeable hope in the hearts of the faithful, reminding them that the night of oppression is always followed by the dawn of divine victory.

## Sūrah Yūnus 10:88

> **Moses said, “Our Lord! Truly Thou hast given Pharaoh and his chiefs adornment and wealth in the life of this world, our Lord, that they may lead [men] astray from Thy Path. Our Lord! Obliterate their wealth, and harden their hearts, so that they will not believe until they see the painful punishment.”**

**Expanded Commentary**

**The Prophetic Indictment of Imperial Wealth and Arrogance**

After years of patient preaching, clear miracles, and relentless Pharaonic atrocities, Moses raises his hands in a monumental, historic supplication against the tyrant: *"Moses said, 'Our Lord! Truly Thou hast given Pharaoh and his chiefs adornment and wealth in the life of this world...'"* (*Wa-qāla Mūsā Rabbanā innaka ātayta Firʿawna wa-malaʾahu zīnatan wa-amwālan fī al-ḥayāti al-dunyā*).

Moses identifies the two idols that fueled the Egyptian regime's corruption:
- *Zīnah* (adornment): Grand palaces, golden chariots, exquisite jewelry, and imperial pomp that dazzled and seduced the masses.
- *Amwāl* (wealth): Boundless financial resources, agricultural monopolies, and treasury reserves used to purchase military hardware, bribe officials, and maintain political control.

Instead of utilizing this wealth in gratitude to feed the poor and establish justice, Pharaoh's court used it for one purpose: *li-yuḍillū ʿan sabīlika* ("to lead men astray from Your Path").

**The Supplication for Economic and Spiritual Retribution**

Moses utters two profound imprecations:
1. **Obliteration of Economic Power (*Rabbanā iṭmis ʿalā amwālihim*):** The root *ṭ-m-s* signifies effacing, petrifying, or wiping out something completely. Ibn ʿAbbās, Mujāhid, and Qatādah record that God answered this prayer by turning their gold, silver, crops, and treasures into petrified stone.
2. **Sealing of Their Hardened Hearts (*Wa'shdud ʿalā qulūbihim*):** Moses prayed that because they had exhausted every opportunity for repentance and used their respite solely to torture the innocent, God should seal their arrogant hearts so they remain blind until they are submerged in the physical agony of divine retribution (*ḥattā yarawu al-ʿadhāba al-alīm*).

This was not personal vengeance; it was the sacred invocation of an authorized prophet of God after centuries of systemic evil had crossed the absolute threshold of divine justice.

## Sūrah Yūnus 10:89

> **He said, “Your supplication has been answered. So stand upright, and follow not the path of those who know not.”**

**Expanded Commentary**

**The Divine Acceptance of the Prophetic Invocation (Qad Ujībat Daʿwatukumā)**

God responds directly to the prayer: *"He said, 'Your supplication has been answered...'"* (*Qāla qad ujībat daʿwatukumā*).

Notice the dual verbal form *daʿwatukumā* ("the supplication of you two"):
- Classical commentators, including Ibn ʿAbbās, al-Ṭabarī, and Ibn Kathīr, explain that while **Moses** was articulating the words of the supplication, **Aaron** was standing beside him saying *"Āmīn"* ("O God, accept!").
- In Islamic spiritual jurisprudence, the one who sincerely says *Āmīn* to a righteous supplication is an equal partner in that prayer and shares in its divine response.

**The Command of Steadfast Perseverance (Istaqīmā)**

God pairs the promise of answered prayer with an essential behavioral command: *"So stand upright"* (*fa'staqīmā*).

The verb *istaqīmā* (addressed to both Moses and Aaron) denotes maintaining unyielding steadfastness (*Istiqāmah*), moral integrity, continuous worship, and patient perseverance upon the Straight Path. 

The commentators note that between the divine decree *"Your prayer has been answered"* and the actual physical drowning of Pharaoh in the Red Sea, a period of **forty years** elapsed according to reports from Ibn Jurayj and others. 

This teaches an invaluable spiritual lesson: the fact that God has accepted a supplication does not mean its physical manifestation will occur instantly according to human impatience. Believers must remain steadfast, disciplined, and active in good works while divine wisdom unfolds historical events in its perfect timing.

**The Warning Against Following the Ignorant (Wa-lā Tattabiʿānni Sabīla alladhīna lā Yaʿlamūn)**

The verse concludes: *"and follow not the path of those who know not"* (*wa-lā tattabiʿānni sabīla alladhīna lā yaʿlamūn*).

Do not adopt the reckless haste, emotional volatility, or faithless despair of those who lack knowledge of God's wisdom and cosmic timing.

## Sūrah Yūnus 10:90

> **And We carried the Children of Israel across the sea, and Pharaoh and his hosts pursued them in rebellion and enmity, till, when drowning overtook him, he said, “I believe that there is no God except Him in Whom the Children of Israel believe, and I am of the Muslims.”**

**Expanded Commentary**

**The Miraculous Maritime Exodus: Parting of the Red Sea**

The narrative reaches its dramatic climax: *"And We carried the Children of Israel across the sea..."* (*Wa-jāwaznā bi-Banī Isrāʾīla al-baḥr*).

God commanded Moses to lead the Children of Israel out of Egypt by night. Trapped between the waters of the Red Sea in front of them and the advancing chariots of Pharaoh behind them, Moses struck the sea with his staff by divine command. The waters parted into twelve massive walls of liquid towering like mountains (*ka'l-ṭawdi al-ʿaẓīm*; 26:63), revealing dry seabed paths through which the Israelites crossed safely.

**The Insatiable Arrogance of the Tyrant: Pursuit in Rebellion and Enmity**

Driven by blind rage and imperial pride, Pharaoh and his elite army plunged into the seabed corridor: *"and Pharaoh and his hosts pursued them in rebellion and enmity"* (*fa-atbaʿahum Firʿawnu wa-junūduhu baghyan wa-ʿadwā*).

The terms *baghy* (tyranny/injustice) and *ʿadw* (transgression/hostility) characterize their motives. Even when witnessing the physical sea miraculously suspended before his eyes, Pharaoh's arrogance blinded him to the obvious reality of divine power.

**The Deathbed Confession in the Surge of Drowning**

When the last Israelite reached the opposite shore and the entire Egyptian army was in the center of the seabed, God commanded the suspended waters to collapse: *"till, when drowning overtook him, he said, 'I believe that there is no God except Him in Whom the Children of Israel believe, and I am of the Muslims'"* (*ḥattā idhā adrakahu al-gharaqu qāla āmantu annahu lā ilāha illā alladhī āmanat bihi Banū Isrāʾīla wa-anā mina al-muslimīn*).

As the roaring waves crushed his chariot and the water filled his lungs, the supreme megalomaniac—who once declared *"I am your highest Lord"*—was reduced to utter, pathetic helplessness. He uttered a desperate, triple confession of monotheism:
1. Affirming pure Tawḥīd (*lā ilāha illā...*),
2. Identifying with the faith of the oppressed Israelites (*alladhī āmanat bihi Banū Isrāʾīl*),
3. Explicitly proclaiming his submission as a Muslim (*wa-anā mina al-muslimīn*).

## Sūrah Yūnus 10:91

> **Now? Though previously you disobeyed and were among the corrupters!**

**Expanded Commentary**

**The Divine Rejection of Deathbed Panic (Al-Īmān al-Yaʾūs)**

The divine response cuts through the roaring waters with devastating finality: *"Now? Though previously you disobeyed and were among the corrupters!"* (*Ālʾāna wa-qad ʿaṣayta qablu wa-kunta mina al-mufsidīn*).

The single interrogative word *Ālʾān* ("Now?!") embodies the absolute rejection of forced, retroactive repentance. In Islamic theology (*ʿAqīdah* and *Fiqh*), the door of repentance (*al-Tawbah*) is wide open throughout human life, with one absolute cosmic deadline:
- Repentance is accepted as long as the soul has not reached the final physical death rattle (*mā lam yugharghir*), and as long as the sun has not risen from the West.

The Prophet Muhammad ﷺ explicitly stated:

> *"Verily, God accepts the repentance of a servant as long as the death-rattle has not reached his throat (mā lam yugharghir)."* (Jāmiʿ al-Tirmidhī 3537; Sunan Ibn Mājah 4253, graded ḥasan)

**The Anatomy of Disobedience (Maʿṣiyah) and Corruption (Fasād)**

The verse reminds Pharaoh of his true historical legacy:
- *Wa-qad ʿaṣayta qabl* (though previously you disobeyed): Decades of murdering infants, enslaving nations, defying prophetic signs, and claiming divinity.
- *Wa-kunta mina al-mufsidīn* (and you were among the corrupters): Institutionalizing tyranny, economic exploitation, and state terror.

Declaring belief only when the physical angel of death is standing over you and physical survival is impossible is not faith; it is a useless reflex of terror.

## Sūrah Yūnus 10:92

> **Today We shall save you in your body that you might be a sign for those after you. And truly most of mankind are heedless of Our signs.**

**Expanded Commentary**

**The Preservation of the Imperial Corpse (Nunajjīka bi-Badanika)**

God delivers an astonishing, historically unprecedented prophecy: *"Today We shall save you in your body that you might be a sign for those after you..."* (*Fa'l-yawma nunajjīka bi-badanika li-takūna liman khalfaka āyah*).

Classical commentators, including Ibn ʿAbbās, Mujāhid, and Ibn Kathīr, record the historical background:
- When the Red Sea collapsed upon Pharaoh's army, some of the Israelites—deeply traumatized by centuries of Pharaonic terror—could hardly believe that their mighty oppressor was truly dead. Some whispered: "Pharaoh is immortal; he cannot die!"
- To shatter this psychological trauma, God commanded the surging waves to cast Pharaoh's lifeless, armored corpse onto an elevated mound of the shore (*najwah*) for all the Children of Israel to see with their own eyes.

**The Archaeological and Modern Miracle of the Preserved Body**

Beyond the immediate ancient audience, the phrase *li-takūna liman khalfaka āyah* ("that you may be a sign for those after you") contains a stunning prophecy that transcends millennia. 

While countless drowned soldiers across history were consumed by marine life or decomposed into dust, Pharaoh's body was uniquely preserved, mummified by the Egyptians, and eventually rediscovered in modern archaeological excavations in the late 19th century. Today, his preserved physical corpse lies displayed in museum glass cases in Cairo—a literal, tangible, preserved sign (*āyah*) for modern humanity, witnessing the exact fate of a mortal who claimed to be God.

**The Pervasive Human Heedlessness (Wa-inna Kathīran mina al-Nāsi ʿan Āyātinā la-Ghāfilūn)**

The verse concludes: *"And truly most of mankind are heedless of Our signs"* (*wa-inna kathīran mina al-nāsi ʿan āyātinā la-ghāfilūn*).

Millions of tourists and students walk past the preserved corpse of Pharaoh, viewing it merely as an ancient archaeological artifact, completely heedless of the terrifying moral and theological sermon written in its dried flesh: that all human tyranny ends in humiliation before the Almighty.

## Sūrah Yūnus 10:93

> **Certainly We settled the Children of Israel in a settlement of truth, and We provided them with good things; and they differed not till the knowledge came unto them. Truly thy Lord will judge between them on the Day of Resurrection concerning that wherein they differed.**

**Expanded Commentary**

**The Settlement of Truth (Mubawwaʾa Ṣidq) and Pure Provision**

The narrative outlines the fulfillment of God's promise to the liberated Israelites: *"Certainly We settled the Children of Israel in a settlement of truth, and We provided them with good things..."* (*Wa-laqad bawwaʾnā Banī Isrāʾīla mubawwaʾa ṣidq wa-razaqnāhum mina al-ṭayyibāt*).

- *Mubawwaʾa Ṣidq* (a settlement of truth/honor): Classical exegetes, including Qatādah and al-Ṭabarī, identify this as the fertile, blessed lands of the Levant and Palestine (the Holy Land, *al-Arḍ al-Muqaddasah*), as well as secure settlements in Egypt after Pharaoh's downfall. It was a dwelling place characterized by stability, honor, and freedom.
- *Mina al-Ṭayyibāt* (of good things): Pure, wholesome sustenance, agricultural abundance, manna, quails, fresh fruits, and lawful wealth.

**The Tragedy of Post-Knowledge Sectarian Fragmentation**

The verse diagnoses the tragedy that befell later generations: *"and they differed not till the knowledge came unto them"* (*fa-mā ikhtalafū ḥattā jāʾahumu al-ʿilm*).

Human disputes are often excused by ignorance; but the most tragic form of schism occurs when a community possesses clear revelation, prophetic scripture, and scholarly knowledge (*al-ʿIlm*), and yet fractures into hostile sectarian factions due to *baghy*—envy, arrogance, political rivalries, and personal ambitions. Knowledge without spiritual humility becomes a fuel for sectarian warfare.

**The Final Eschatological Arbitration (Inna Rabbaka Yaqḍī Baynahum)**

The verse concludes: *"Truly thy Lord will judge between them on the Day of Resurrection concerning that wherein they differed"* (*Inna Rabbaka yaqḍī baynahum yawma al-qiyāmati fīmā kānū fīhi yakhtalifūn*).

Worldly theological polemics and sectarian claims of monopoly over truth will find their final, absolute resolution in the court of the Omniscient Judge.

## Sūrah Yūnus 10:94

> **So if thou art in doubt concerning that which We have sent down unto thee, ask those who have been reading the Scripture before thee. Truly the Truth has come unto thee from thy Lord, so be not thou of the doubters.**

**Expanded Commentary**

**The Rhetorical Formulation of Absolute Certitude (Shart Imtināʿī)**

The verse presents a profound rhetorical and theological address: *"So if thou art in doubt concerning that which We have sent down unto thee, ask those who have been reading the Scripture before thee..."* (*Fa-in kunta fī shakkin mimmā anzalnā ilayka fa'sʾal alladhīna yaqraʾūna al-Kitāba min qablik*).

Classical scholars, led by Ibn ʿAbbās, Qatādah, Saʿīd ibn Jubayr, and al-Ṭabarī, emphasize that the conditional clause *Fa-in kunta* ("If you are...") is a standard Arabic rhetorical device (*uslūb al-farḍ wa'l-taqdīr*) used to establish undeniable certainty, not an indication that the Prophet Muhammad ﷺ ever experienced doubt. 

The Prophet ﷺ never doubted his revelation for a single second. It is recorded by al-Ṭabarī and Ibn Kathīr that upon the revelation of this verse, the Messenger of God ﷺ declared:

> *"I do not doubt, nor do I ask!"*

**The Corroboration of Historical Scripture**

The purpose of the verse is pedagogical and directed at the wider human audience:
- The narratives revealed in the Qur'an concerning Moses, Pharaoh, Noah, and ancient prophets were not known to the pagan Arabs of Makkah, who were unlettered.
- The Qur'an challenges anyone seeking historical verification to consult the learned scholars among the People of the Scripture (such as ʿAbdullāh ibn Salām), who possessed ancient texts confirming these exact historical accounts and predicting the advent of the final Prophet.

**The Prohibition of Wavering: Be Not of the Doubters (Fa-lā Takūnanna mina al-Mumtarīn)**

The verse concludes: *"Truly the Truth has come unto thee from thy Lord, so be not thou of the doubters"* (*La-qad jāʾaka al-ḥaqqu min Rabbika fa-lā takūnanna mina al-mumtarīn*).

Truth (*al-Ḥaqq*) has descended with overwhelming intellectual, linguistic, and historical clarity from your Lord. The command to avoid doubt (*al-imtirāʾ*) is a universal directive to every believer: anchor your heart in the immovable rock of revelation, immune to the passing winds of skepticism.

## Sūrah Yūnus 10:95

> **And be not thou among those who deny the signs of God, lest thou be among the losers.**

**Expanded Commentary**

**The Absolute Prohibition of Spiritual Compromise**

The divine warning continues with uncompromising moral clarity: *"And be not thou among those who deny the signs of God, lest thou be among the losers"* (*Wa-lā takūnanna mina alladhīna kadhdhabū bi-āyāti Allāhi fa-takūna mina al-khāsirīn*).

Although addressed grammatically in the singular to the Prophet Muhammad ﷺ, the prohibition is universal to all of humanity (*khiṭāb li'l-nabī wa'l-murād bihi al-ummah*):
- If the greatest, most beloved Messenger of God ﷺ would be counted among the "losers" (*al-khāsirīn*) were he to deny divine signs, what possible hope could any ordinary mortal have if they turn away from revelation?
- The verse establishes that spiritual status is not an unconditional shield: closeness to God requires continuous, humble obedience to His signs.

**The Ultimate Nature of Loss (Al-Khusrān)**

The term *al-khāsirīn* denotes ultimate existential bankruptcy:
- The person who rejects divine guidance trades eternal joy in Paradise for transient worldly delusions.
- When death arrives, all their accumulated worldly wealth, prestige, and influence evaporate, leaving them spiritually destitute on the Day of Reckoning.

## Sūrah Yūnus 10:96

> **Truly those for whom the Word of thy Lord has come due will not believe,**

**Expanded Commentary**

**The Irreversible Threshold of Moral Calcification**

The verse explains the deep spiritual mechanics behind persistent human obstinacy: *"Truly those for whom the Word of thy Lord has come due will not believe"* (*Inna alladhīna ḥaqqat ʿalayhim kalimatu Rabbika lā yuʾminūn*).

Classical theologians, including al-Ṭabarī, Fakhr al-Dīn al-Rāzī, and Ibn Kathīr, elucidate the relationship between divine decree (*al-Qadar*) and human agency:
- The "Word of thy Lord" (*kalimatu Rabbik*) refers to God's eternal, infallible foreknowledge and judicial decree that certain individuals—who have repeatedly, willfully, and arrogantly extinguished their own conscience—have reached a state of complete spiritual death.
- God does not force them into disbelief; rather, their own persistent choices to mock truth, persecute the righteous, and bury their intellect have sealed their moral destiny.

This provides profound emotional consolation to the Prophet ﷺ: your mission has not failed because people like Abū Jahl and Abū Lahab refuse to believe; rather, their rejection is the tragic fulfillment of divine foreknowledge concerning souls that have permanently chosen darkness.

## Sūrah Yūnus 10:97

> **though every sign should come unto them, till they see the painful punishment.**

**Expanded Commentary**

**The Total Immunity of Arrogance to Empirical Evidence**

Following the declaration of the preceding verse, God reveals the complete futility of presenting further signs to obstinate hearts: *"though every sign should come unto them, till they see the painful punishment"* (*Wa-law jāʾathum kullu āyatin ḥattā yarawu al-ʿadhāba al-alīm*).

This verse exposes the fundamental myth of skeptical rationalism—the claim that "if only God showed me an undeniable miracle, I would believe." 
- When a human heart is locked by arrogance and moral corruption, no amount of empirical, cosmic, or miraculous evidence can produce genuine faith.
- If the sky were split before their eyes, if the dead were raised to speak with them, or if mountains turned into gold, the obstinate skeptic would simply dismiss it as optical illusion, psychological hallucination, or advanced technology (*La-qālū innamā sukkirat abṣārunā bal naḥnu qawmun masḥūrūn*; 15:15).

**The Final Threshold of Involuntary Sight (Ḥattā Yarawu al-ʿAdhāb al-Alīm)**

The only event that finally shatters their denial is the direct, physical arrival of the painful punishment (*al-ʿadhāb al-alīm*)—whether the drowning sea of Pharaoh or the agony of the Hellfire. But as established throughout the sūrah, faith forced by physical agony is useless for salvation.

## Sūrah Yūnus 10:98

> **Why has not a single town believed and benefitted from its faith, except the people of Jonah? When they believed, We removed from them the punishment of disgrace in the life of this world, and We granted them enjoyment for a time.**

**Expanded Commentary**

**The Singular Historical Miracle: The People of Jonah (Qawmu Yūnus)**

The verse unveils the profound historical event from which the entire sūrah takes its name: *"Why has not a single town believed and benefitted from its faith, except the people of Jonah?"* (*Fa-lawlā kānat qaryatun āmanat fa-nafaʿahā īmānuhā illā qawma Yūnus*).

Across sacred history, every ancient civilization that rejected its prophet (the people of Noah, ʿĀd, Thamūd, Sodom, and Pharaoh) persisted in arrogance until divine punishment physically descended, at which point their late repentance was rejected and they were annihilated.

There is exactly **one magnificent exception** in human history: the people of Prophet Jonah (*Yūnus*) in the ancient city of Nineveh (near modern Mosul, Iraq), numbering over one hundred thousand people (cf. 37:148).

**The Anatomy of Collective Repentance in the Shadow of Doom**

Classical commentators, including Ibn ʿAbbās, Saʿīd ibn Jubayr, al-Ṭabarī, and Ibn Kathīr, detail this extraordinary historical drama:
- When Jonah warned his people of impending divine destruction within three days and departed their city in frustration, the people observed dark, ominous clouds of divine retribution gathering over their horizon.
- Unlike Pharaoh who hardened his heart, the people of Nineveh experienced a collective spiritual awakening. They recognized the truth of Jonah's warning before the final destructive strike occurred.
- The entire population—from the king on his throne to the poorest peasant—tore off their royal robes, donned coarse sackcloth, took their children and livestock out into the open desert, separated nursing mothers from their infants, and wept collectively in desperate, sincere prostration, begging the One True Creator for mercy and forgiveness.

**The Lifting of the Disgraceful Punishment (Kashafnā ʿAdhāb al-Khizy)**

God looked upon their genuine, collective brokenness and answered: *"When they believed, We removed from them the punishment of disgrace in the life of this world, and We granted them enjoyment for a time"* (*Lammā āmanū kashafnā ʿanhum ʿadhāba al-khizyi fī al-ḥayāti al-dunyā wa-mattaʿnāhum ilā ḥīn*).

This historical exception contains a monumental theological message of hope for all humanity:
- Human destiny is not fatalistic.
- Sincere collective repentance (*Tawbah Naṣūḥ*), undertaken with genuine humility before the final physical destruction, has the power to shatter historical precedents, avert catastrophe, and unleash divine mercy.
- The verse stood as a loving, urgent invitation to the pagan Quraysh of Makkah: it is not too late; follow the path of the people of Jonah, turn back to God, and avert the impending doom.

## Sūrah Yūnus 10:99

> **And had thy Lord willed, all those who are on the earth would have believed together. Wouldst thou then compel people until they become believers?**

**Expanded Commentary**

**The Divine Philosophy of Free Will and Moral Agency**

The verse articulates one of the most fundamental theological and philosophical principles governing human existence: *"And had thy Lord willed, all those who are on the earth would have believed together..."* (*Wa-law shāʾa Rabbuka la-āmana man fī al-arḍi kulluhum jamīʿā*).

God possessed the absolute omnipotence to create humanity without the capacity for disbelief or sin—just as He created the angels, who obey divine commands continuously without rebellion. 
- However, divine wisdom decreed that earthly life should be an arena of moral trial (*al-Ibtilāʾ*), intentional choice, and free agency.
- Genuine faith (*īmān*) and moral virtue have spiritual value and deserve eternal reward precisely because they are chosen voluntarily in the presence of temptation, doubt, and conflicting desires.
- Forced faith is an ontological contradiction: love, devotion, and virtue cannot exist without the freedom to choose otherwise.

**The Absolute Prohibition of Religious Coercion (A-fa-anta Tukrihu al-Nās)**

The verse poses a direct rhetorical question to the Prophet Muhammad ﷺ: *"Wouldst thou then compel people until they become believers?"* (*A-fa-anta tukrihu al-nāsa ḥattā yakūnū muʾminīn*).

This establishes the foundational Islamic doctrine of religious freedom and the prohibition of coerced conversion, reaffirmed in Sūrah al-Baqarah:

> *"There is no compulsion in religion; truth has become clear from error."* (Qur'an 2:256)

The Prophet Muhammad ﷺ longed with every fiber of his being for every human soul to accept guidance and be saved from the Fire. God gently comforts and guides his prophetic heart: your duty is *al-Balāgh*—to articulate truth with sublime beauty, rational clarity, and compassion. You cannot, and must not, attempt to force human consciences into belief. Faith is an internal reality of the heart that only God can illuminate.

## Sūrah Yūnus 10:100

> **It is not for a soul to believe, save by God’s Leave. And He places defilement upon those who use not their reason.**

**Expanded Commentary**

**The Doctrine of Divine Permission (Bi-Idhn Allāh)**

The verse reveals the metaphysical foundation of faith: *"It is not for a soul to believe, save by God’s Leave..."* (*Wa-mā kāna li-nafsin an tuʾmina illā bi-idhni Allāh*).

In Islamic theology (*ʿIlm al-Kalām*), no event occurs in the universe outside the sovereign will (*al-Mashīʾah*) and permission (*al-Idhn*) of God Almighty. 
- A human being possesses free choice (*al-ikhtiyār*) to seek truth, reflect upon signs, and humble their heart.
- However, the actual transformation of that sincere seeking into living faith (*al-Īmān*) is a gift of divine grace (*Tawfīq*) bestowed by God.

This prevents intellectual arrogance: the believer does not boast that they arrived at faith through their own superior intellect, but acknowledges with deep humility that faith is a divine favor.

**The Defilement of the Unused Intellect (Al-Rijs ʿalā alladhīna Lā Yaʿqilūn)**

The second half of the verse establishes an indispensable epistemological link between reason and divine grace: *"And He places defilement upon those who use not their reason"* (*wa-yajʿalu al-rijsa ʿalā alladhīna lā yaʿqilūn*).

The term *al-rijs* in Arabic denotes spiritual filth, abomination, cognitive darkness, confusion, and divine wrath. 

Upon whom does God place this spiritual defilement? Upon *alladhīna lā yaʿqilūn*—those who refuse to use their God-given intellect (*al-ʿAql*). 
- Islam does not pit faith against reason; rather, it views the abandonment of rational reflection as the very cause of spiritual damnation.
- When human beings suspend their critical faculties, blindly follow ancestral dogmas, indulge in superstition, and ignore the clear rational signs of the cosmos, they invite spiritual darkness upon their own souls. Genuine faith is the highest fruition of an awakened, disciplined intellect.

## Sūrah Yūnus 10:101

> **Say, “Observe that which is in the heavens and on the earth.” But signs and warners do not avail a people who believe not.**

**Expanded Commentary**

**The Universal Mandate for Empirical and Cosmic Contemplation**

The verse issues a sweeping divine command to all humanity: *"Say, 'Observe that which is in the heavens and on the earth'"* (*Qul unẓurū mādhā fī al-samāwāti wa'l-arḍ*).

The verb *unẓurū* (observe/contemplate) is an active command:
- **In the Heavens:** Contemplate the cosmic architecture—the expansion of space, the thermonuclear fusion of stars, the gravitational balance of planetary orbits, and the atmospheric protection shielding terrestrial life.
- **On the Earth:** Contemplate geological strata, the water cycle, the diversity of plant and animal species, the genetic coding of cellular DNA, and the delicate ecological harmony that sustains human existence.

Islam transforms the entire physical universe into an open laboratory of divine discovery. Scientific observation and deep ecological mindfulness are sacred acts of worship that deepen human awe before the infinite wisdom of the Creator.

**The Limit of External Evidence (Mā Tughnī al-Āyāt wa'l-Nudhur)**

The verse concludes with a piercing diagnostic reality: *"But signs and warners do not avail a people who believe not"* (*wa-mā tughnī al-āyātu wa'l-nudhuru ʿan qawmin lā yuʾminūn*).

External evidence (*al-āyāt*) and prophetic warnings (*al-nudhur*) are completely powerless when presented to a soul that has already made a dogmatic, predetermined commitment to disbelief (*qawm lā yuʾminūn*). 
- The problem is never a deficiency in the evidence provided by God; the problem is the internal intellectual dishonesty and pride of the observer.
- Just as the most brilliant sunlight cannot illuminate an eye that is deliberately clamped shut, the most magnificent cosmic signs cannot guide a heart that refuses to seek truth.

## Sūrah Yūnus 10:102

> **Do they wait for aught save the like of the days of those who passed away before them? Say, “Wait then; truly I am with you among those waiting.”**

**Expanded Commentary**

**The Historical Delusion of Immunity (Ayyām alladhīna Khalaw)**

The verse addresses the complacent arrogance of the skeptics: *"Do they wait for aught save the like of the days of those who passed away before them?"* (*Fa-hal yantaẓirūna illā mithla ayyāmi alladhīna khalaw min qablihim*).

The phrase *Ayyām Allāh* ("the Days of God") in Qur'anic and classical Arabic terminology refers to those decisive historical moments when divine justice descended to annihilate ancient corrupt empires—the Flood of Noah, the shrieking blast that destroyed Thamūd, the violent windstorm that uprooted ʿĀd, and the sea that drowned Pharaoh.

The Qur'an asks the Quraysh: Do you imagine that you are metaphysically immune to the universal moral laws of history? If you imitate the exact same arrogance, tyranny, and denial of ancient vanished nations, what possible reason do you have to expect a different historical outcome?

**The Confident Stance of Prophetic Patience (Intiẓār)**

God commands the Prophet ﷺ to deliver a serene, confident reply: *"Say, 'Wait then; truly I am with you among those waiting'"* (*Qul fa'ntaẓirū innī maʿakum mina al-muntazirīn*).

This is not a statement of doubt; it is an expression of supreme theological certitude:
- You are waiting to see if prophetic truth will be extinguished by your political intrigues;
- I am waiting for the inevitable fulfillment of God's promise—the vindication of righteousness, the defeat of tyranny, and the supreme reckoning of the Hereafter.

## Sūrah Yūnus 10:103

> **Then We save Our messengers and those who believe. Thus it is a duty incumbent upon Us: We save the believers.**

**Expanded Commentary**

**The Inviolable Divine Law of Prophetic and Believing Deliverance**

The verse articulates the ultimate conclusion of historical drama: *"Then We save Our messengers and those who believe"* (*Thumma nunajjī rusulanā wa'lladhīna āmanū*).

Whenever historical civilizations reached the point of terminal moral decay and divine retribution was unleashed, God established an absolute, unbroken pattern:
- The messengers and the sincere believers who stood with them were systematically, miraculously preserved and delivered (*nunajjī*).
- Noah was saved in the Ark; Hūd was saved from the wind; Ṣāliḥ was saved from the blast; Lot was led out before Sodom collapsed; Moses was carried safely across the Red Sea; and Muhammad ﷺ was granted total victory and triumph over pagan Arabia.

**The Self-Imposed Divine Covenant: A Duty Incumbent Upon Us (Ḥaqqan ʿAlaynā)**

The verse culminates in an extraordinary theological declaration: *"Thus it is a duty incumbent upon Us: We save the believers"* (*Kadhālika ḥaqqan ʿalaynā nunji al-muʾminīn*).

Classical scholars, including al-Ṭabarī, al-Qurṭubī, and Ibn Kathīr, emphasize the profound significance of the phrase *Ḥaqqan ʿalaynā* ("A binding right upon Us"):
- God Almighty is the Supreme Sovereign; no created entity can impose a duty upon Him.
- However, out of His infinite grace, justice, and mercy, God has voluntarily bound Himself by an immutable promise (*kataba ʿalā nafsihi al-raḥmah*): that He will never abandon, forsake, or allow the final destruction of the true believers.

This promise stands as an eternal fortress of hope for every oppressed, struggling believer across all generations: temporary trials and persecution may occur, but ultimate spiritual and eternal victory is guaranteed by the Creator of the universe.

## Sūrah Yūnus 10:104

> **Say, “O mankind! If you are in doubt concerning my religion, I worship not those whom you worship apart from God, but I worship God Who causes you to die; and I have been commanded to be of the believers,”**

**Expanded Commentary**

**The Ultimate Apostolic Manifesto of Monotheistic Clarity**

The sūrah builds toward its grand conclusion with an uncompromising, universal proclamation: *"Say, 'O mankind! If you are in doubt concerning my religion, I worship not those whom you worship apart from God...'"* (*Qul yā ayyuhā al-nāsu in kuntum fī shakkin min dīnī fa-lā aʿbudu alladhīna taʿbudūna min dūni Allāh*).

The Prophet Muhammad ﷺ draws a sharp, immovable boundary between pure monotheism (*Tawḥīd*) and polytheistic compromise. If humanity harbors doubt regarding his mission, his principles, or his resolve, let all doubt be permanently extinguished: he will never bow down before idols, flatter political tyrants, or compromise divine truth for worldly peace.

**The Transcendent Master of Mortality (Alladhī Yatawaffākum)**

Notice the sublime attribute chosen to identify the One True God: *"but I worship God Who causes you to die"* (*wa-lākin aʿbudu Allāha alladhī yatawaffākum*).

Why did the Prophet ﷺ specifically highlight divine power over death (*al-Wafāh*)?
- The pagan Makkans boasted of their worldly wealth, military power, and vitality, feeling invincible.
- The Prophet ﷺ reminds them of their most undeniable, inescapable vulnerability: every single one of you will have your soul seized by the One True God. Your idols cannot prevent death; your wealth cannot delay death. 
- Is it not the height of madness to worship powerless dust, while ignoring the Living Sovereign into Whose hands your breathing soul will be delivered?

**The Divine Commission: To Be of the Believers (Min al-Muʾminīn)**

The verse concludes: *"and I have been commanded to be of the believers"* (*wa-umirtu an akūna mina al-muʾminīn*).

The Messenger's faith is not a personal philosophical preference; it is an absolute divine mandate from the Lord of the worlds.

## Sūrah Yūnus 10:105

> **and “Set thy face toward the religion as a ḥanīf, and be not thou of the polytheists,”**

**Expanded Commentary**

**The Total Reorientation of Human Consciousness (Aqim Wajhaka)**

The divine instruction continues: *"and 'Set thy face toward the religion as a ḥanīf...'"* (*Wa-an aqim wajhaka li'l-dīni ḥanīfā*).

In Arabic rhetoric and psychology, "setting one's face" (*iqāmat al-wajh*) signifies total, undivided focus, wholehearted dedication, and unswerving commitment. The face is the mirror of human intention and identity. 

To set one's face toward the religion means to align one's entire mind, heart, moral choices, and life trajectory in direct, uncompromising devotion to the way of God, without glancing left or right toward worldly compromises.

**The Meaning of the Primordial Monotheist (Ḥanīf)**

The term *ḥanīf* (plural *ḥunafāʾ*) is one of the most evocative terms in Qur'anic theology. Derived from the root *ḥ-n-f*, which literally means to incline away from crookedness toward uprightness:
- A *ḥanīf* is one who deliberately turns away from all forms of polytheism, idolatry, moral degradation, and philosophical confusion, inclining with absolute purity toward the original, unblemished monotheism (*Tawḥīd*) modeled by Prophet Abraham (*Ibrāhīm*).
- It is the state of natural spiritual equilibrium that aligns with the uncorrupted human disposition (*al-Fiṭrah*).

**The Absolute Prohibition of Polytheistic Association (Wa-lā Takūnanna mina al-Mushrikīn)**

The verse concludes: *"and be not thou of the polytheists"* (*wa-lā takūnanna mina al-mushrikīn*).

Polytheism (*shirk*) is the supreme spiritual poison that destroys the soul. The verse commands complete, total purification of heart and action from every subtle form of idolatry—including the worship of wealth, power, ego, and social approval.

## Sūrah Yūnus 10:106

> **and “Call not, apart from God, upon that which neither benefits thee nor harms thee. For if thou doest [so], truly thou wilt then be among the wrongdoers.”**

**Expanded Commentary**

**The Prohibition of Invocations Directed to Helpless Entities**

The verse establishes the absolute boundary of Islamic prayer (*Duʿāʾ*): *"and 'Call not, apart from God, upon that which neither benefits thee nor harms thee...'"* (*Wa-lā tadʿu min dūni Allāhi mā lā yanfaʿuka wa-lā yaḍurruk*).

In Islamic theology, supplication (*al-Duʿāʾ*) is the very core and marrow of worship (*al-Duʿāʾu huwa al-ʿIbādah*). Directing prayer, desperate cries for help, ultimate trust, and spiritual reliance to anything other than the Almighty Creator—whether dead saints, celestial bodies, angels, political rulers, or material idols—is fundamentally irrational and spiritually destructive.

No created entity possesses autonomous capacity to bestow a single blessing (*nafʿ*) or inflict or remove a single harm (*ḍarr*). To pray to created things is to appeal to poverty for wealth, and to helplessness for power.

**The Verdict on Spiritual Compromise: Among the Wrongdoers (Mina al-Ẓālimīn)**

The verse warns: *"For if thou doest [so], truly thou wilt then be among the wrongdoers"* (*fa-in faʿalta fa-innaka idhan mina al-ẓālimīn*).

In Qur'anic terminology, *al-ẓālimūn* here specifically denotes the polytheists (*al-mushrikūn*), because *shirk* is the greatest conceivable injustice (*Inna al-shirka la-ẓulmun ʿaẓīm*; 31:13). 

If even the noble Prophet Muhammad ﷺ—the most perfected soul in existence—would be counted among the wrongdoers were he to direct a single prayer to other than God, how could any ordinary human being imagine they have a license to engage in polytheistic practices? The verse establishes the absolute, non-negotiable purity of monotheistic worship.

## Sūrah Yūnus 10:107

> **And if God should touch thee with affliction, none can remove it save Him; and if He intends good for thee, none can repel His Bounty. He causes it to reach whomsoever He wills of His servants; and He is the Forgiving, the Merciful.**

**Expanded Commentary**

**The Absolute Monotheistic Sanctuary: Divine Control Over Tribulation and Bounty**

The verse articulates one of the most liberating, anxiety-shattering theological truths in all of sacred scripture: *"And if God should touch thee with affliction, none can remove it save Him..."* (*Wa-in yamsaska Allāhu bi-ḍurrin fa-lā kāshifa lahu illā Huw*).

The verse establishes two absolute metaphysical axioms:
1. **The Removal of Harm (*Kashf al-Ḍurr*):** If God decrees that an affliction (*ḍurr*)—illness, financial loss, grief, persecution, or hardship—touches you, all the medical specialists, political leaders, armed forces, and astrologers in the cosmos combined cannot lift that trial by a single millimeter without His permission. God alone is *al-Kāshif* (The Ultimate Reliever).
2. **The Unstoppable Inflow of Divine Grace (*Faḍl Allāh*):** *"and if He intends good for thee, none can repel His Bounty"* (*wa-in yuridka bi-khayrin fa-lā rādda li-faḍlih*). If God has decreed a blessing, healing, elevation, success, or guidance for you, the entire world conspiring together cannot prevent, block, or delay that blessing from reaching you.

The Prophet Muhammad ﷺ taught this profound reality to his young cousin ʿAbdullāh ibn ʿAbbās:

> *"Know that if the entire nation were to gather together to benefit you with something, they could not benefit you except with something that God had already written for you; and if they were to gather together to harm you with something, they could not harm you except with something that God had already written against you. The pens have been lifted and the pages have dried."* (Jāmiʿ al-Tirmidhī 2516, graded ḥasan ṣaḥīḥ)

**The Universal Distribution of Grace and the Twin Names of Comfort**

The verse continues: *"He causes it to reach whomsoever He wills of His servants"* (*yuṣību bihi man yashāʾu min ʿibādih*).

God distributes His trials and blessings according to infinite, perfect wisdom, educating and elevating human souls.

The verse closes with two sublime divine attributes: *"and He is the Forgiving, the Merciful"* (*wa-Huwa al-Ghafūru al-Raḥīm*). 
- *Al-Ghafūr* (The All-Forgiving): He covers and forgives human shortcomings, sins, and moments of panic.
- *Al-Raḥīm* (The Ever-Merciful): Even the afflictions He touches His servants with are wrapped in hidden mercy, designed to purify their hearts, detach them from worldly illusions, and draw them closer to His eternal presence.

## Sūrah Yūnus 10:108

> **Say, “O mankind! The Truth has come unto you from your Lord. So whosoever is guided is guided only for his own soul, and whosoever strays only strays against it; and I am not a keeper over you.”**

**Expanded Commentary**

**The Final Apostolic Summons to All Humanity (Al-Ḥaqqu min Rabbikum)**

The sūrah delivers its penultimate, universal manifesto: *"Say, 'O mankind! The Truth has come unto you from your Lord...'"* (*Qul yā ayyuhā al-nāsu qad jāʾakumu al-ḥaqqu min Rabbikum*).

The address is universal (*Yā ayyuhā al-nās*): it transcends tribal boundaries, racial categories, and historical epochs. The Qur'an and the Prophetic mission constitute *al-Ḥaqq*—the Objective Truth—originating directly from your Creator, Sustainer, and Lord.

**The Absolute Autonomy and Consequence of Moral Choice**

The verse outlines the ultimate law of spiritual responsibility:
1. **Guidance Benefits Only the Guided (*Fa-mani ihtadā fa-innamā yahtadī li-nafsih*):** When you embrace faith, establish justice, pray, and purify your character, your righteousness does not increase God's kingdom or benefit the Messenger in the slightest; you are benefiting and saving your own eternal soul.
2. **Misguidance Harms Only the Straying (*Wa-man ḍalla fa-innamā yaḍillu ʿalayhā*):** When you choose arrogance, disbelief, hedonism, and cynicism, your rebellion does not diminish God's sovereignty or harm the truth; you are committing spiritual suicide, harming only yourself.

**The Liberation of the Prophet: I Am Not a Keeper Over You (Wa-mā Anā ʿAlaykum bi-Wakīl)**

The verse concludes: *"and I am not a keeper over you"* (*wa-mā anā ʿalaykum bi-wakīl*).

The term *wakīl* denotes a guardian, custodian, or guarantor of behavior. The Prophet Muhammad ﷺ is a compassionate warner and bearer of glad tidings, not an authoritarian warden or cosmic policeman forced to compel human obedience. Every moral agent stands directly and individually accountable before their Creator.

## Sūrah Yūnus 10:109

> **And follow that which has been revealed unto thee, and be patient till God judges; and He is the Best of Judges.**

**Expanded Commentary**

**The Final Prophetic Mandate: Adherence, Patience, and Divine Justice**

The magnificent sūrah concludes with a sublime, comprehensive command that encapsulates the entire methodology of prophetic mission and believer resilience: *"And follow that which has been revealed unto thee, and be patient till God judges; and He is the Best of Judges"* (*Wa'ttabiʿ mā yūḥā ilayka wa'ṣbir ḥattā yaḥkuma Allāhu wa-Huwa Khayru al-ḥākimīn*).

The closing verse establishes the three foundational pillars of the spiritual life:

1. **Uncompromising Adherence to Revelation (*Wa'ttabiʿ mā yūḥā ilayk*):** The believer's daily life, creed, ethics, and choices must be strictly anchored in the guidance of divine revelation (*al-Waḥy*). When worldly cultures drift into moral confusion and philosophical fads, the Book of God remains the immovable compass of truth.
2. **Active, Enduring Patience (*Wa'ṣbir*):** Patience (*al-Ṣabr*) in Islam is not passive resignation; it is active spiritual perseverance:
   - Patience in steadfastly fulfilling divine commands and moral duties (*ṣabr ʿalā al-ṭāʿah*);
   - Patience in restraining the soul from temptations and forbidden desires (*ṣabr ʿan al-maʿṣiyah*);
   - Patience in enduring the persecutions of critics and the hardships of worldly decrees (*ṣabr ʿalā aqdār Allāh*).
3. **Unshakeable Trust in Divine Arbitration (*Ḥattā yaḥkuma Allāh*):** Continue walking the path of righteousness with tranquility, leaving the final outcome to the Lord of history.

**The Grand Theological Seal: The Best of Judges (Khayru al-Ḥākimīn)**

The entire chapter of Sūrah Yūnus—which opened with the Wise Book (*al-Kitāb al-Ḥakīm*)—concludes with the declaration of the Supreme Judge: *wa-Huwa Khayru al-ḥākimīn* ("and He is the Best of Judges").

God is *Khayru al-Ḥākimīn* because:
- His knowledge is absolute, encompassing every hidden intention and historical variable;
- His justice is pure, containing zero prejudice, bias, or error;
- His judgment is final, unappealable, and eternally vindicated.

With this sublime seal, the servant rests in total peace, knowing that the struggles of this fleeting life are held in the hands of the All-Wise, All-Just, and Most Merciful Creator of the universe.
